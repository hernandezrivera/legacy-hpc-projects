Project Summary

Timely life-saving Shelter and NFI assistance is essential to prevent physical hazard and ensure safety, security, and dignity of IDPs and vulnerable residents. The purpose of the project is to contribute to improving protection, safety & security, and dignity of new IDPs through provision of life-saving assistance, and extremely vulnerable people as well as PWSNs living in protracted displacement with more sustainable, emergency-recovery type of interventions. The project targets localities in Khartoum, Al Jazeera, East and
South Darfur states that experienced several displacements in 2023 and are still hotspot areas. The project basically targets two groups; first new crises-affected people will be provided with emergency shelter making materials & NFI assistance. Second, people living in protracted displacement will be supported with replenishment of S/NFI and shelter upgrading solutions. Based on the experience, in this project we planned to support 79,765 people (about 15,953 HHs) by ES/NFIs. To achieve the above purposes Alight will monitor situations, preparing hotspot areas and identifying affected & vulnerable communities, and raising awareness of the communities on hazards. We regularly coordinate with HAC and UNHCR to exchange information on existing humanitarian situations. Whenever responses are required (suppose conditions
worsen or new humanitarian conditions emerge such as displacement of people due to conflict or extreme hazard (drought/flood)) then Alight will trigger joint need assessment (with state level stakeholders), conduct joint verification exercises and response plans. Any response prioritization will be done through cluster conventional processes. Activities that are in line with cluster objectives and relevant to the locality culture and traditions are carefully selected and will be implemented to deliver effective responses.
Wherever and whenever possible Cash Base intervention (CBI) (up to 20% of total budget) will be applied to help households fulfill their S/NFI needs. Alight will engage local authorities, COR, HAC, UNHCR and other key partners and jointly monitor and Supervise distributions and conduct Post distribution evaluation and document learnings.
To make this project a success, Alight uses its leverage of cumulated knowledge and experience. During implementation, Alight will work with the communities and local administrations, mobilize its potential suppliers and transporters, which will help to timely responses. Also, in case NFI gaps occur in the market Alight will utilize the Core Pipeline to respond to emergencies. As much as possible uniformity of responses will be respected by applying standardized NFI packages and Standard Operating Procedures
(SOPs) developed by the ES/NFI cluster. Wherever necessary to deliver holistic response,
Alight will combine WASH and protection responses with the S/NFI distribution. As much as possible Alight will respect and take care of the environment, culture, traditions and local businesses. We will use environmentally friendly and culturally appropriate shelter materials, most of the procurements will be locally sourced, which will help to activate local economies. Alight also provides on-the-job training in building techniques of shelters that will be useful for developing self-reliance and resilience. To improve accountability, transparency in programming, and adherence to “Do no harm principles,” Alight will strengthen the capacity of staff (men, women, and youth) and local national NGOs through training in the complete package of PSET for emergency response. Additionally, Alight will implement the project by considering protection and accountability to the community and to the donor. Our MEAL system will gather important data and produce information. Communities will be provided with information about our
complaints and feedback systems and Alight document complaints, refer and take corrective measures.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Alight’s design of services ensures their accessibility across the gender, age and diversity (GAD) categories and vulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for at high-risk categories of beneficiaries such as female headed households, young girls, older and severely ill and people with disabilities. Alight ensures all persons with special needs, and all persons assessed to be a higher protection risk are prioritized. We shall ensure their active participation through routine analysis of protection risks,individual and group assessments, and, where possible, encourage their participation and representation in all affairs of their communities. This will help Alight to develop project designs that are adapted to mitigate the protection risks of the users based on improving Safety, Access and Dignity (SAD) of the users of services. Alight will work with other members of the community to strengthen the social protection for people with specific needs through mobilizing community support. Alight is committed to the principle of `do no harm' in humanitarian actions. The interventions are designed in away to mainstream the four main protection principles in project interventions, ie avoiding exposing people to further harm; ensuring access to impartial assistance; and protecting people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion while assisting people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse. The project services will be accessible to avoid exposing the beneficiaries, particularly women, to any risks. Necessary measures willbe taken to preserve the privacy and dignity of the target beneficiaries. To ensure dignity and rights of beneficiaries to health and nutrition services, the project will ensure that the provided interventions are acceptable to the community, and they take part in it through involvement of community leaders and community volunteers. Services are provided with impartiality regardless of sex, gender, ethnicity or religious affiliation.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In regard to Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), Alight will employ the AGD policy that is implemented through community based, participatory approaches that are already well established in the IDPs camp and host community areas. The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and through the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, and project activities will be tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities. The Project will ensure that all persons of concern have equal and non- discriminatory access to protection, assistance and solutions, this will build on their resilience and recovery. Activities like awareness raising, dialogues, will be conducted in different WASH, Shelter and NFIs trainings and in the communities, specific needs and vulnerabilities identification and culturally sensitive issues will be considered and discussed separately. Alight will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions and complaints box) using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non- sensitive feedback e.g. use of complaints boxes, toll free hot lines.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Alight’s commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on implementing systems and methods that reveal client experience of organizational services so that we can align a response and re-establish trust between Alight and the IDPs, host communities and refugees who are our clients. Thus, Alight believes that trust works in both directions and therefore it must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. It is this belief that defines our work in enhancing greater involvement of these clients in designing, developing and implementing programs, defining their quality outcomes, and solving problems alongside them. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneficiaries. With deep and well-established links with the communities in the target localities, we have built capacity of community structures that support day-to-day planning and implementation of activities that include community nutrition screening, community water quality monitoring, community clean up campaigns, community household visits, and health education. Through these relationships, we have ongoing conversations with these community members about their ideas, concerns, and needs. These ongoing discussions help shape our program designs. To further demonstrate our commitment to AAP, we will continue ensuring strong accountability in our humanitarian responses toward affected communities and individuals directly not only through the hitherto mentioned involvement, but also by encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. This will be done via periodic focus group discussions (FGDs), community forums, community meetings, community awareness and outreach campaigns, branding and marking, and ongoing feedback/complaint response mechanism (F-CRM) throughout this project. Alight has also started producing briefs and a monthly newsletter which will be translated into Arabic and that will be accessible to targeted communities.
