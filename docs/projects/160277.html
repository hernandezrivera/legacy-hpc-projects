Project Summary

The goal of this intervention is to contribute to the collective effort to reduce the GAM rate to less than 10% in Greater Bagari through the provision of quality lifesaving CMAM interventions to children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating
Women in all 14 Bomas of Greater Bagari (Baggari, Bazia, and Besselia).
Through this project, Johanniter will screen over 90% of children under five years 10,946 (5,691 girls and boys 5,254) and also over 90% of pregnant and lactating women 4,170 for malnutrition in the 14 Bomas of Bagari and Besselia areas. Additionally, a total of 7,531 women and 796 men will directly be reached through MIYCN messaging and counseling at the community level. The project will reach at least 80% of SAM and MAM in girls and boys 6-59 months; 80% of PLW. The estimated number of SAM cases is 1,174 and MAM in children under five years 3,577 and MAM in PLW 978. Indirect beneficiaries of this project will include 790 males who are caretakers of children under five, County health department staff, health workers and Community nutrition volunteers (CNVs).
The project will be a continuation of the current emergency nutrition response in Bagari, Bazia and Besselia by Johanniter under the SSHF 2018. The project will be complemented by on-going UNICEF and WFP projects in Wau and Jur River, and a GBV project privately funded under Johanniter which will support with protection services.
In an effort to address the prevailing humanitarian situation in the Baggari, Bazia, and Besselia area, Johanniter is requesting SSHF funds to continue the current emergency nutrition intervention which has already seen nutrition centers established, staff trained, increased outreach activities, the formation of mother to mother groups, reporting, and referral system set up. This will also complement the on-going UNICEF and WFP projects to provide integrated nutrition services to the affected and vulnerable populations. At the moment, Johanniter is in the final stage of development of the PCA with UNICEF and FLA with WFP to cover the OTPs and TSFP activities in Baggari for the period of 2020.
The 187,500 USD that is being requested will ensure that there are no gaps or interruption in services to the population that has been greatly affected by prolonged insecurity, limited access to basic services and displaced. Under this funding, Johanniter will scale up outreach activities, train health workers on CMAM and MIYCN, recruit more nutrition staff to meet the minimum standards in the nutrition sites, integrate WASH interventions particularly messaging and WASH supplies at facilities so as to reduce on WASH-related diseases, setup feedback mechanisms at the facilities, referral system for both complicated medical cases and GBV cases. The amount being requested will not be able to cover all the planned activities however it will be complemented by UNICEF and WFP budgets.

1 - Needs

Greater Bagari in Wau County (WBeG) has witnessed armed conflict between government and opposition forces since 2016 and although there is no active fighting at the moment, the effect of the conflict has continued to date especially in terms of accessibility and displacement. According to IPC September 2019 report, the population in Wau and Jur River are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and is projected to remain that way in October - December 2019. The continued general stability, delivery of humanitarian food assistance due to improved security and access has led to the county being classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), with fewer populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) than the same time in 2018. The GAM rates for September and projection until end of 2019 are at phase 3. The rates of malnutrition are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. Poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and the high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). In June 2019, about 1,617 children from 6-59 months were screened using MUAC and height measuring board of which 73 had SAM (4.5%) and 165 MAM (10.2%). MAM among 1,492 PLW screened was 228 (15.3%). This, therefore, means that malnutrition in the most vulnerable groups (CU5 and PLWs) needs to be prioritized. Johanniter is currently providing nutrition services to the host and displaced communities in 18 OTP/TSFP sites of Greater Bagari under SSHF with supplies from UNICEF and WFP. There are still plans to open 3 more sites based on needs assessment. Out-reach activities are on-going in other areas. In an effort to address the prevailing humanitarian situation in Greater Bagari, Johanniter is requesting funds to build on the SSHF 2019 gains and to complement the running UNICEF and WFP projects.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

As part of accountability, Johanniter will hold itself accountable to the beneficiary communities (that their needs for assistance and protection are met, with dignity) and the donor by ensuring that assistance is provided for the proposed purpose. At the onset of the project, SMOH and CHD and any other stakeholders will be oriented about the project, expectations, and targets. The beneficiaries will be involved in the project cycle from the beginning. They will be involved in the selection of the Community Nutrition Workers/Volunteers and also in the feedback mechanism. Together with the CNVs and mother to mother support groups messaging will be discussed that will be tailored to the local context, gender and age. The groups involved will have representation from the different age groups and gender including adolescents and males to ensure comprehensive information on key areas. A feedback mechanism will be set up in all the operation sites (static and mobile). The beneficiaries will be made aware of the reporting channels at each of the operation sites. Any complaint raised by the beneficiaries will be discussed at the community level, at field office level or Juba level and solutions provided. For our beneficiaries/clients to receive the quality care that they deserve, any form of harassment or abuse or negligence by our staff reported will be investigated and once confirmed will face necessary actions taken by Johanniter/CHD and RRC. As part of the “do no harm” policy, Johanniter will ensure security of its staff (CNVs, MIYCN counselors and project staff) and also the beneficiaries with emphasis on women who are prone to being abducted and raped by adhering to strict security policies/guidelines and getting regular updates from UNOCHA, local authorities and the beneficiaries themselves so that the staff and beneficiaries have safe access to services. The beneficiaries and local leaders will also be asked to provide us the safest locations for outreaches.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will include offering the much-needed services to the IDPs. All project activities will be carried out with impartially and neutrality in the areas where Johanniter will implement this project regardless of whether the selected beneficiaries are IDPs or the host community. This will mitigate the potential for conflict over access to emergency assistance meant for the vulnerable groups.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Johanniter has been working closely with the county health department (CHD), community leaders, health facility staff and the mother to mother groups that also include some men. These consultations are through community sensitization during outreaches, in mother to mother support groups and through the feedback mechanisms set at the health facilities. The project will work with the community nutrition volunteers who are identified by the community and share concerns and complaints from the community for example about days of nutrition activities, location of services and use of nutrition supplies. This information will guide Johanniter in reassessing the implementation of activities so as to ensure beneficiaries get quality services as is expected. The monitoring activities for this project will be together with SMOH. Johanniter will continue to play an active role in the Nutrition Cluster coordination meetings at state and national levels. The implementation strategy and approach for this nutrition project will follow the national guideline of MICYN, CMAM and basic packages of health and nutrition Services (BPHNS) for South Sudan. The integration of the nutrition services into the existing health system of government is based on the national guideline including strengthening the capacity of county health department ((CHD) to enable them to continue with the project even when the planned project funding comes to an end.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Johanniter will engage the CHD, target beneficiaries and all relevant community leaders in decision making. The community will guide us on identifying locations for outreaches and distribution days and time to minimize time-wasting. Safe access and distance to the outreach sites will be assessed to take into consideration all potential risks to beneficiaries. The nutrition sites will have adequate facilities such as toilets, water, and shades available to protect people especially women’s well-being and dignity. Johanniter is a recipient of UN funds (UNICEF, WFP, and SSHF) and therefore complies with minimum standards included in the agreements which we must demonstrate to maintain compliance. It provides clarity on the minimum requirements for protection, gender, Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) and PSEA. There will be awareness-raising for staff and related personnel (whether collectively or by the agency); concepts of humanitarian workers, sexual abuse and sexual exploitation; produce a plan of action suggesting actions to prevent and respond to sexual abuse and a complaint feedback reporting mechanism. Johanniter commits to Interagency Standing Committee (IASC) Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian particularly through risk mitigation e.g. familiarizing staff with the guidelines, training, community awareness and advocacy, regular reporting on GBV in cluster meetings, working with partners and early referrals of victims. All project activities will be carried out impartially and neutrality in the areas where Johanniter will implement this project regardless of whether the selected beneficiaries are IDPs or the host community. This will mitigate the potential for conflict over access to emergency assistance meant for the vulnerable groups.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The proposed project approach for nutrition programming will be through the integration of CMAM into the existing health system of government within the Counties to ensure comprehensive care. Training, mentoring and supervision will be provided to existing health workers on CMAM according to the new MOH guideline so that when this project exits, the health facility, and the county health department will continue implementing the treatment of children based on current evidence. Johanniter will organize joint planning on nutrition programming together with the SMOH and CHD. The SMOH/CHD will be actively involved in quarterly supportive supervision of nutrition sites, provision of organized and on-the-job training and ensuring quality of the nutrition service as part of capacity building and sustainability. The implementation strategy and approach for this nutrition project will follow the national guideline of MICYN, CMAM and basic packages of health and nutrition Services (BPHNS) for South Sudan. Therefore, the approach for addressing malnutrition through integrating nutrition services into the existing health system of government following the national guideline, emphasis on strengthening the capacity of health facilities and county health department during the implementation phase to enable them to continue with the project even when funding comes to an end is to ensure sustainability and continuity.
