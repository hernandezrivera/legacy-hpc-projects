Project Summary

In 2022, 3.6 million people, 97 per cent of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. Following COVID-19, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. In addition, about 139,000 refugee children also need education support. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures further impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children.
An estimated 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. In addition, some 139,000 refugee children will need education support in 2022. Unity state where the project is going to be implemented is one of the most affected states.
South Sudan has been prone to various emergencies related both to the consequences of decades of wars and environmental calamities such as floods and drought resulting in inability of the government to provide access to quality education that serves the needs of marginalized and at-risk children. Flooding in Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties led to displacement of communities which has affected the learning opportunities of school age going children with girls becoming more vulnerable as compare to boys.
In order to inform the intervention, UNIDOR did undertake out of school mapping with funding from Global Partners in Education (GPE) in partnership with Ministry Education in Mayendit, Leer and Panyijiar Counties. The findings of the mapping include: schools being located very far away from residential areas for children aged between 3 – 10 years, lack of feeding programs in schools resulting in school dropouts, inadequate/lack of teaching and learning materials, some schools lack sources of clean drinking water and lack of training for teachers on general pedagogy and other immerging needs such as Covid -19 protocols as provided by UNICEF and the Ministry of Health guidelines.
In the light of the above and with recommendation from the communities, UNIDOR will intervene to improve access to quality education by leveraging on its current projects by carrying out the following activities:
 Establish 16 temporary learning (4 in each counties) space in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019.
 Rehabilitate 8 temporary learning spaces or schools (2 in each county) that have been damaged by the flood.
 Rehabilitated/construct 12 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls).
 Provide teaching and learning materials to children (5-17) and teachers provided with
 Provide recreational material supplies to children and youth (6-17 years).
 Train teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies.
 Train 40 community education committee members and local education officials (5 per each Payam) in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR).
 Train 120 PTA members provided with appropriate IEC prevention and mitigation information and materials.
 Organize 8 school campaigns on back to school/learning (1 for each Payam).

1 - Needs

Currently UNIDOR are implementing Girls’ Education South Sudan (GESS) project in the selected counties that targets increase in enrollment and retention of girls in schools through provision of cash to the girls and also provision of capitation grants. The implementation has enabled UNIDOR to collect data on the school needs on daily basis. Assessment carried out by IRNA in Panyijar county in September 2021, Inter-agency assessment carried out in Mayendit in August 2021, multisector flood assessment carried out in Leer in September 2021 and initial rapid assessments carried out in Koch in July 2021reported dire needs on education which are all summarized below:  Despite the fact that schools reopened in May 2021 many children are still remaining at home due to various factors. Therefore, carrying out a campaign drive will help in mobilizing the communities to send their children back to school.According toEducation Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding in 2021 and was mentioned as impediment for learner's access to school in the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021. Many people in the four counties have been displaced as a result of the flood resulting in overcrowding in the schools where they are being hosted. Many parents who are settled far away from the existing schools opted to keep their children at home. Across the four counties many schools and WASH facilities have been damaged by the flood.The only option is to rehabilitate/construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls). Children and teachers lack teaching and learning materials. Many teachers are untrained and have problem understanding basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies. The status of the School Management committee/PTAs is also wanting. Most of them do not know their roles and hence there is minimal community engagement in the schools. Lack of and delayed salaries and incentives result in teacher absenteeism, apathy, high turnover and poor-quality teaching. It is also worth mentioning that the proposed intervention directly responds to the priorities of the GESP 2017-2022 that seeks to address issues of access and equity, quality of education from early childhood development, primary, secondary and AES.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNIDOR will use the beneficiary feedback throughout the programming cycle to make adjustments and adaptations to activities and services. This will be done with consultation with the donors especially where the adaptation will deviate so much from the agreed deliverables.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During implementation the M&E team shall hold focus group discussions with individuals in in each category separately and where language barriers exists, a local staff shall be present to ensure robust collection of feedback is undertaken to facilitate in addressing any challenges during the project cycle. Deliberate efforts will be made to invite people with disabilities to participate in the discussions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR has elaborate PSEA policies which are shared by key staff in project locations appointed by the PSEA focal person in field location who reports to the contact Manager in charge of protection department. All new staff are oriented on the PSEA guidelines and sign adherence to the same. At the field level in this project the Field contact staff will during the various trainings undertake community sensitization of the SEA guidelines and reporting mechanisms established.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There will be periodic meetings with the key project stakeholders in order to share information on the project and solicit their views. There will also be deliberate focus group discussions with the beneficiaries from which their views will be gathered. During the focus group discussions information about the project will also be provided to the beneficiaries. During trainings there will be a session (first session) for providing the beneficiaries information about the project. It will be made very clear for them that their feedback or complaint about the project will be highly appreciated.In event resources employed in the project are not adequate UNIDOR will en-devour to bring onboard other funding to provide additional support to gaps highlighted by beneficiaries where possible. Gathering feedback will be inclusive enough. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Due to the fact that some areas schools are a distant from where children stay, those in ECD would have their parents restrict them from going to school hence this risk of violence due to the issues of distance and at times violence shall be mitigated by ensuring PTAs and Education authorities provide alternate sites for ECD classes so that they do not miss out on attendance.The risks of PSEA shall be mitigated by ensuring the beneficiary community being fully sensitized on the Zero tolerance and understanding the field contact person together with location of complaint boxes where issues relating to SEA are to be reported. Lack of awareness on GBV referral pathways shall be mitigated by incorporating the UNIDOR protection department staff conducting sensitization to the PTAs on available mechanisms in the county.The risks of persons with disabilities being not consulted or being excluded are mitigated by ensuring the county education authorities together with the PTAs being sensitized on the need to mobilize and collect data on children with special needs within their jurisdiction that are out of school enabled to enroll via consultations with the parents involved.
