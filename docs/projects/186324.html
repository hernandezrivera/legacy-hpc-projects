Project Summary

The August 2021 MSNA by IOM/OCHA in the North West, South West, West and Littoral regions, reports more than 500,000 persons displaced by the conflict. The HNO reports some 435,000 persons in need of protection. The protection situation in the crisis affected regions is marked by violence inflicted upon the most vulnerable by the belligerent State forces and non-State armed groups (NSAGs) including increased gender-based violence, attacks, and violations of the right to property illegal or arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment of children. As of 31 October, UNHCR in North West and South West Regions documented some 4,700 protection incidents mainly GBV, killings, destruction of properties. In 2021, the security situation in the NWSW regions remained extremely volatile with armed conflict, terrorism and crimes beside the imposed lockdowns, and roadblocks. The COVID-19 pandemic and the limited humanitarian access further exacerbated the protection context.
UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2022 and beyond to contribute to the protection response and play its role as last resort provider and the Protection Cluster lead: 1) strengthen the centrality of protection, 2) coordination of lead clusters, 3) emergency preparedness and response and 4) securing solutions. UNHCR will cover the North West and South West Regions and Wouri Division of Littoral region.
Through a community-based approach, UNHCR intends to secure protection and provide solutions through reinforcing protection monitoring and empowering communities to contribute to their own protection, respond to protection incidents and search for solutions, and ensure peaceful coexistence among their members. The intervention will respond to critical GBV cases including legal assistance, support for civil documentation and psychosocial services. In-kind support, cash-based interventions, and referrals to available services will be an integral part of the response.
An area-based approach will consist of selecting specific areas in the regions affected by the displacement and work with all stakeholders in the priority locations including displaced, host community, IDPs and refugees’ returnees, local authorities, civil society, agencies working in the areas to increase protection outreach and support to the most vulnerable; mitigate tensions, empower local authorities; increase local coordination. The approach will contribute to build sustainability of key services and prevent displacement as well as guarantee a greater accountability to affected populations (AAP). UNHCR will also use the existing joint UNHCR-WFP hotline for AAP and PSEA. UNHCR will targets 38 communities in 6 divisions 3 (Fako, Meme and Manyu) in the South West Region and 3 (Mezam, Boyo and Menchum) in the North West Region and 1 (Wouri) in the Littoral with an estimated reach of 350,000 persons and specific targets include:
• 38 Community-Based Protection Committees supported to contribute to solutions and GBV prevention and response in the targeted areas.
• 10 community empowerment initiatives supported approximately 1400 individuals.
• 2000 GBV survivors will receive psychosocial services (PSS)
• 800 victims of protection incidents and GBV survivors will receive legal services
• Provision of material assistance (dignity and mama kits) to 2000 GBV survivors
• Provision of multi-purpose cash grant support to 2000 GBV survivors and persons with specific needs to mitigate protection risks, establish small business and agriculture/livestock and restore dignity.
• 4000 persons of concern including children will be supported to obtain civil documentation.
The project is in line with Cameroon HRP 2022 Protection Framework, 2019 HC engagement in IDPs situation document, UNHCR priorities and Strategic Vision 2022 – 2026 and is UNSDCF and considers the global commitment to the SDGs in particular SDG n.5 and n.10.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Protection: Protection monitoring and community engagement mechanism will be used as essential mechanisms to identify, prevent and mitigate conflict and violence, and their consequences, including forced displacement. These monitoring mechanisms and systems will be regularly used for advocacy, interventions, programming, resource mobilization and communications. In addition to monitoring, protection responses will involve protection services for IDPs including activities that prevent, respond to, and mitigate protection risks, including protection against GBV, child protection, family separation, case management and referrals for persons with specific needs or victims of protection incidents, and mental health and psychosocial support. UNHCR will establish protection monitoring mechanism in 38 communities in 6 divisions of the North West and South West regions (Fako, Meme, Manyu divisions in SW and Mezam, Menchum, Boyo divisions in NW), with an estimated reach of 174,000 persons in need covered by protection monitoring monthly. Civil documentation capacity building for the support for the establishment 4000 civil documents of children and adults identified through the protection monitoring or referred by others will be provided. UNHCR intervention will include legal assistance to 160 individuals whose rights were violated, and 13,200 crisis-affected people will be reached out during sensitization sessions on their rights and obligations. Targeted protection assistance will be provided to identified 2,500 most vulnerable people and 38 community-based structures will be trained on protection standards and policies.Communities will be empowered through 38 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) for the prevention and response to GBV. 10 community empowerment initiatives towards protection solutions will be supported with direct support to approximately 1000 individuals. Both the IDPs and host communities will be increasingly involved in the protection monitoring and their capacity to address and prevent the protection incidents occurring in their areas will be boosted. The capacity building of community-based protection committees will also contribute to build peaceful coexistence and a sense of ownership and responsibility of their own protection, Respond to GBV incidents. UNHCR will provide support to GBV survivors (women, girls, boys and men) and will strengthen the community-based approach while ensuring that interventions focus on the protection of survivors, especially women and girls. 2,000 survivors of SGBV will be provided with case management, psychosocial and medical support and multi-purpose cash-based interventions to mitigate protection risks and respond to incidents, 2000 GBV survivors will benefit from material support (dignity kits and mama kits). Legal assistance will be provided to GBV survivors with a Law firm cabinet as per need and their consent. 25.000 people will be reached out during sensitization activities on GBV prevention conducted at the community level. In addition, 200 selected women and girls will benefit from vocational training and income -generating activities. Communities will be involved in all the programming process (from needs assessment to monitoring & evaluation of the protection intervention, including planning and implementation). UNHCR will strengthen referral mechanism to available services in the identified communities and increase collaboration to cater for existing operational gaps. All identified persons with specific needs that are not covered by UNHCR support will be referred to existing structures and programs for the access to basic social services for their inclusion to increase their socio-economic well-being,
