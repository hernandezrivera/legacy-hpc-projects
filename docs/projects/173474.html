Project Summary

In 2021, DRC GBV will retain case management for women and girls, male survivors and child survivors in Camp 6, Rajapalong wards 6 and 9 and Palongkhali wards 5 and 6. DRC will establish 2 safe spaces through using existing infrastructures in Camps 2W and 10 for case management as well provide outreach sessions and psychosocial support for women and girls in Camp 6 and Rajapalong wards 6 and 9 and Palongkhali wards 5 and 6. Men and boy engagement activities will scale up in Camp 6 and Rajapalong wards 6 and 9. These activities will be introduced in Camp 8E and 8W. In Palongkhali, the biweekly hijra safe space will continue and one hijra safe space will be established in Camp 6. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms. In all locations, outreach will be designed to include the disabled, and third gender with specialized outreach and participation modules by focusing protection mainstreaming in each stage. DRC GBV technical expertise will be available to build the capacity of local GBV actors, including local authorities and local NGOs based on tailored needs assessments, and PSEA will also be shared through trainings. Also, DRC will continue to develop and map a partnership strategy through linking government, local NGOs, and private organisations and align the ISCG sector strategies for mainstreaming the activities. DRC will diversify the service accessibility fund in place for contingency needs. A hotline for case management support will be available throughout the year. The selection of locations is based on analysis of GBV-SS mapping and complements DRC’s other sectoral programme. DRC will set up CFRM in the host locations and direct to DRC CoC or CFRM as appropriate in refugee camps for AAP.

1 - Identified Needs

The inter-agency report by UNHCR, CARE and ActionAid titled Intersectional Analysis of Gender Amongst Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh highlights the role of the policy restrictions on freedom of movement, income generation and livelihoods opportunities for Rohingya refugees leading to negative coping strategies and exacerbating risks to refugee women and girls including survival sex, child marriage, trafficking and reduced access to assistance items. According to the report, refugee men and boys are at risk of perpetrating IPV and sexual harassment and engaging in polygamy in this environment. Hijras remain underserved and unrepresented across sectors. These patterns entrench the consequences of systemic denial of rights faced by the refugees when in Myanmar, following experiences of acute conflict related sexual violence during flight. For displacement-affected host communities, where patriarchy and the needs of a development context already caused GBV risks, the influx threatened social cohesion, income generation and livelihood opportunities. DRC GBV’s implementation experience shows this intimately impacts the lives of women and girls, whose movement may be restricted as a measure to protect against strangers or who may self-impose restrictions (over and above the traditional restrictions on movement already present); who may have partners opting for polygamy as a coping method or who may be deprioritised for the very limited livelihoods opportunities available for the few women and girls able to work outside of home. In both the refugee and host locations, the COVID pandemic has deteriorated the protection environment. Two IRC reports (What Happened? How the COVID-19 Response Failed Women and Girls; and In the Shadows of the Pandemic: The Gendered Impact of COVID 19 on Refugee and Host Communities) complementarily illustrate this: the first says GBV accounted for only 0.48% of the Global Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 (as of August 2020) and the second concludes that safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for everyone, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. Also, GBV is increasing, including IPV, polygamy, transphobic violence, while access to support is more restricted. The JSMNA 2020 shows that SGBV services were identified by host community respondents as not going well since the pandemic. While refugee respondents on the JSMNA 2020 rated SGBV services highly both prior to and after the pandemic, the analysis of health, safety, legal and PSS services in the JSMNA 2020, as well as the IRC’s report findings, suggest that in fact the components of case management are not sufficiently available. The IRC report shows that according to 42% of the refugee respondents, it is more unsafe for women and girls inside the house since the COVID-19 pandemic. For host communities, according to the JSMNA 2020, information itself is a gap. This is also indicated by DRC GBV’s case management supervision experience. Thus, the relevance of DRC’s full package of GBV response and prevention services for host and refugee communities remains for 2021. This includes engagement of men and boys, and dedicated programming for child survivors and male survivors. DRC’s unique contribution in having run a safe space for hijras since 2019 anticipated gaps being flagged in the recent reports and is proposed for scale-up. In the interest of sustainability, DRC technical expertise is made available to partners and stakeholders in GBV through training, sensitisation and mentoring. Strategies to aid recovery from the interruptions caused by COVID-19 are incorporated. This proposal is people centered approach that builds capacity of local actors. All services will be provided using mobile delivery or be based in DRC’s existing camp and community structures constructed according to engineering standards, and the environmental impact of the project implementation will be minimum.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Establishment of 2 women and girls’ safe (GBV service points) space which will prioritize the safety and dignity of the target population in Camps 2W and 10, and address COVID-19 health issues and awareness raising, DRR and preparedness related themes. Activity 2: Maintenance of women and girls’ safe spaces and additional mobile service points in Palongkhali wards 5 and 6, Rajapalong wards 6 and 9 and Camp 6. Activity 3: GBV case management support including direct provision of PSS services for survivors (12 years and above) in refugee and host locations with capacity building to support child survivors and male survivors. Activity 4: Capacity building and develop partnership strategies for local protection actors, including protection CBOs and volunteers (non GBV actors and GBV actors) on introductory packages specifically designed to ensure ethical referral and cover existing technical capacity gaps respectively with a commitment to follow ups with government and national partners, in addition to, maintaining the coordination and linkage with DRC inter sectoral programme and ISCG regularly. Activity 5: Capacity building for local CBOs, staff and volunteers on PSEA. Activity 6: Prevention of specific GBV issues through engaging men and boys in Palongkhali wards 5 and 6, Rajapalong wards 6 and 9, Camps 6, 8E and 8W. Activity 7: Retention of biweekly hijra safe space in Palongkhali. Activity 8: Establishment of hijra safe space in Camp 6. Activity 9: Outreach and Mobilize at risk GBV service accessibility fund per location as well during emergency to ensure services to those who unable to access easily. Activity 10. Establish hotline support for GBV Case management service.
