Project Summary

An estimated 8.3 million people, including IDPs, host communities and returnees are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May – July). This represents a 7 percent increase from the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The main drivers are (1) covariate climatic, conflict, and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence); and (2) household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as the death of a breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks.
As per the main driver 1. Communities in Jonglei continue to face adverse effects of displacement, economic collapse, food insecurity, inflation, and rising of food prices, and continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence for communal, and tribal conflict within Jonglei State. This had a great cumulative effect of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods due to the conflict of 2013, and 2016 after the country independent in 2011.
The agro-pastoralist nature of populations in the counties targeted has exposed the population to severe food insecurity and malnutrition due to low food availability, diversity, and access. Loss of livestock which is the major source of livelihood due to low vaccination and treatment reach, loss due to insecurity, as well as the compounding effects of foods. Medicair seeks to integrate her present nutrition programming with FSL activities, aimed at improving the well-being of households
Enhancing household and communal production and value addition will enhance communal resilience and enable communities to withstand future shocks.
A total of 31,250 persons (6,250HHs) are targeted throughout 2021 in the counties of Nyirol Jonglei State

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, the precarious situation will be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas, and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. Food assistance is expected to decrease by 10 to 20 percent from the already reduced levels in 2021. It is expected that the projected resources available for 2022 will only be sufficient to cover eight months of needs. Livelihood's support was funded at less than 35 percent in 2021. For 2022, urgent pre-positioning is necessary ahead of the main farming season. The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sectoral analysis identified an increase in the severity and extent of food insecurity through the use of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System Plus (FSNMS+) outcome indicator data, FSL assessment reports (including over 100 pieces of evidence), and other information and analysis by colleagues from WFP, FAO, FEWSNET, REACH, and the Food Security Cluster.The seasonal deterioration of road infrastructure during the rainy season will also affect market functionality by disrupting the timely restocking of markets. The effects of insecurity in parts of the country will also lead to displacement, depletion or loss of assets, and disruption of livelihoods, further contributing to reduced income for purchasing food and essential needs. Food utilization is affected mainly amongst women, children, and the elderly because of the chronic nature of waterborne diseases, low use of latrines, poor personal hygiene, and living environments, and limited access to hygienic materials. In total, 19 counties have seen significant or minor improvements, while 22 counties have remained stable, and 37 counties have experienced significant or minor deteriorations in their food security level. In terms of food insecurity severity (refer to FSL HNO severity map), 31 counties are described as being of extreme or severe concern, 36 counties are described as of very high or high concern and 11 counties are of moderate concern. Five urban locations were assessed as being of very high concern. The most severely affected counties are in locations where the compounded shocks are exceptionally amplified due to intensified sub-national violence, three consecutive years of widespread flooding (across 33 counties in 2021) and/ or localized dry spells, the continued indirect effects of COVID-19 on production and supply chain, a protracted macro-economic crisis, and persistently high food prices.Of great concern is the target Counties of Duk and Nyirol of Jonglei State, where food security is rapidly deteriorating, predominantly and are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4). According to the (January 2020 IPC acute analysis, the acutely food insecure population is expected to reach 6.01 million people in early 2020 even in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. This number includes 20,000 people who are likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Akobo and Duk counties of Jonglei state (FEWS NET, 21 Mar 2020). The unprecedented levels of inter-communal conflict, the macroeconomic crisises recurrent flooding, and the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and preventive measures have worsened the situation especially in the Counties of Jongolei State including Duk and Nyirol. The main hazards to livelihoods in Duk and Nyirol include conflict (both intercommunal and more organized local conflict) and cattle raiding, flooding, livestock diseases, crop pests, and drought that have led to a decrease in the number of livestock in the two Counties and has also decreased agricultural productivity (WFP/FAO 2020). The proportion of Nyirol County residents facing inadequate access to food increased from 27% to 55% from March to June 2020, and 15% of assessed settlements reported that they relied on humanitarian aid as their primary source of food (WFP 2020)

2 - Project activities

• Provision of Emergency food & livelihood support through the distribution of crop kits, fishing kits, and vegetable kits; • Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment.• Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality.• Support value chain addition e.g. through agriculture, fishery, and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing.• Training & capacity building for both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training, etc.

3 - Project indicators

• Number of HHs (female, child & male-headed) receiving crop kits, vegetable & fishing kits.• Number of HHs receiving cash/ vouchers (ITF/ nutrition)• Quantity & variety of seeds distributed• Quantity & variety of crops produced• Number of animals vaccinated & treated; • Number of livestock vaccine cold chain established/ maintained animal disease surveillance reports• Number of CAHWs trained/ supported through cost recovery• % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish);• Number of food security-related reports prepared and shared with stakeholders in a timely manner• Number of SAD people trained by (e.g. improved nutrition/ land conservation/ reducing post-harvest losses/ improved farming practices);• Number of national, state, and County level food security and livelihoods coordination meetings.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project design will be greatly influenced by input from beneficiaries and key stakeholders. Feedback will be documented and utilized in project review meetings to inform design and implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Focus group discussions will be utilized to obtain maximum feedback from the most vulnerable members; as well as house-to-house interviews, interviews with key informants. Medicair will assign key staff to follow up households with persons with special needs, in order to ensure maximum input from all.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information will be provided through maximum utilization of all information materials, such as flyers, banners, T-Shirts, workshops, focus group gatherings, interviews as well as assessments. Key pieces of training will be organized for all stakeholders directly involved in the project which will have a key focus on PSEA protocols according to humanitarian standards.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Clear feedback pathways have been designed by Medicair, through which dissemination and input of information from key stakeholders will be done. All stakeholders will be trained/briefed on the importance and mechanisms of feedback. Information on key achievements against the targets will be provided through public dissemination using local community publicity forums, community gatherings, and use of IEC materials. Focus group discussions will be organized, suggestion boxes fixed at key field sites, interviews with key informants, as well as house to house surveys. Toll-free hotlines will be set up; to enable maximum input from beneficiaries and key stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Safety audits will be carried out to mitigate risks associated with the delivery of humanitarian aid. Services will be brought closest to the beneficiaries; avoiding women and children traveling long distances to access aid. Beneficiary selection will be done in consultation with all stakeholders, with the priority of response given to the most vulnerable based on gender. Clear feedback mechanisms and pathways will be emphasized to all stakeholders while working with local authorities, other humanitarian actors to mitigate incidences of bureaucratic impediments. Coordination will be done with the mine action working group to ensure a reduced risk of harm from UXOs.
