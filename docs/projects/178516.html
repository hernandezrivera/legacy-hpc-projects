Project Summary

CARE proposes to support 3,050 HHs living in Old and New Zoghara Camps located in Jarablus with WASH services in this project. The camps were built by AFAD in 2017 to host IDPs fleeing conflict areas in NW Syria. Through the funds from the first standard allocation 2020, CARE extended the new camp to host 1,200 HHs in addition to the 1,850 HHs living in the old camp and improved lifesaving conditions by replacing damaged tents with new ones, insulating tents and implementing graveling and ground site preparation in addition to securing safe access to WASH facilities in consideration of COVID-19 precaution measures.
The camps are among locations with high reported cases of COVID-19. 104 cases have been recorded in the camp. CARE in coordination with the stakeholders has completed the establishment and set up process of a quarantine zone at new Zoghra camp. The quarantine zone consists of 50 tents facilitated with NFIs. Public WASH facilities have been prepared with equipment to serve the quarantine area. CARE has ensured that the quarantine zone is isolated from the camp’s remaining sectors by selecting the appropriate location and having it separated with a surrounding fence. Local authorities hold responsibility of providing necessary medical equipment and staff.
Access to water is essential for effective handwashing, which is one of the most important measures to prevent COVID-19 infection. CARE will work on securing safe access to adequate drinking water by operating water stations, providing the camp with water (35 L/P/D), provide water storage tanks for IDPs and to reduce crowdedness around current distribution points. CARE will conduct full water quality analyses on water sources in advance. Trucked water will be chlorinated and FRC levels will be checked daily throughout the entire supply chain.
Essential emergency services such as desludging, latrines, soap provision will be provided. To reduce overcrowding, improve hygiene behaviors and increase access to handwashing facilities, CARE will install new shared family latrines where Sphere standards are not met (one toilet for every 20 people), installing door locks for safety and privacy and providing appropriate lighting. CARE will also undertake minor repairs to existing latrines including quick fixes of handwashing facilities in particular and cleaning of latrines, including disinfecting communally used surfaces to mitigate COVID-19 infection risk.
For the prevention and control of infections, CARE will apply intensive hygiene promotion sessions. CARE’s hygiene volunteers will be in charge of disinfection measures to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 in camps. Disinfection kits including soap and PPE will be distributed along with one-off hygiene kits to all families in the camp. Volunteers will be assigned to sterilizing latrines, water distribution points and WASH equipment such as water tankers using the provided cleaning materials and disinfectant liquid.

4 - Needs

As of April 20, the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN) in Syria has started conducting tests for people who showed symptoms and were referred by health facilities in the NWS. The cases started increasing dramatically by October 2020 and more positive cases have been reported over time. The Turkish government is taking measures by screening the temperature of everyone crossing the borders and there has been minimal movement from the government-controlled areas to NWS. The COVID-19 outbreak in NWS added an additional burden to the fragile health system and additional pressure on the provision of WASH services to a population of 4,336,920 living in NW Syria (2,670,184 in Idleb, 1,666,736 in Aleppo), including 2,681,273 IDPs. Health and WASH services have been already overwhelmed due to the huge influx of more than 961,000 newly displaced IDPs in northwest Syria (NWS) since December 2019. CARE conducted a needs assessment to capture the situation of the IDPs in two camps in Jarablus, Aleppo governorate from 21st until 23rd of August 2020 and conducted 4 fucus group discussions on 24th August 2020.49% of the interviewed KIIs mentioned that women can’t access clean and safe water, while 51% mentioned that for men. The main obstacles for accessing water for both women and men are no access to water sources (30%), the distance to the water sources (25%), lack of household water storage capacity (25%), the price of water (10%), or the water had a bad look, smell or color (7%). 60% of interviewees mentioned that they have access to private latrines, 24% mentioned that the toilets are public or shared, while 16% mentioned that the toilets are makeshift made.

5 - Implementation of activities

By mid of Oct 2020, in partnership with OCHA through the first standard allocation for 2020, CARE completed the rehabilitation and extension project of new Zoghra camp to make it capable of hosting 1,200 HHs. CARE is currently investing its resources to support IDPs living in Zoghara camps “Old and New”, by providing multi-sectoral services through different sources and funds, such as cash for food, protection, WASH, and shelter different services in a coordination with the camp management and Turkish authorities. Through the proposed project, CARE through direct implementation will be working to apply the minimum humanitarian standards to serve the camp's residents with the proposed WASH activities. This will secure the IDP’s daily adequate needs for drinking water as well as playing a key factor in fighting against the spread of Covid-19. For the time being, CARE is able to provide for all WASH needs in both Zoghara old and new camps. However, current project activities are due to end by the end of January 2021. The proposed SCHF grant would be a great opportunity to continue build on what has already been achieved and will ensure WASH services are adequately covered in both proposed camps.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

With respect to this particular project, the activities will place priority for beneficiaries that are members of vulnerable groups living in the camps where COVID-19 pandemic is highly expected to spread including single heads of households, families of individuals with special needs, and people with disabilities. The delivery of project services and assistance is designed to be safe and accessible by women, men, girls and boys, and members of marginalized groups taking into account the social and cultural dynamics in the communities. GBV mitigation measures will be considered in the implementation of the intervention in line with the IASC Guidelines for integrating GBV in humanitarian action, and all the precautions will be taken to reduce the risk of exposure to GBV including SEA in the project locations. Increasing the availability and accessibility of water for women, girls, and other at-risk groups, and involving these groups in decision making processes around water management, design and monitoring of WASH facilities, and hygiene promotion activities will constitute some of the areas that will be looked at very closely to improve the safety and security of the response. CARE will act in coordination with protection specialists and actors in other humanitarian sectors in its efforts to prevent and mitigate GBV.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risks to CARE staff as a result of armed conflict: CARE maintains strong security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. In spite of close monitoring and strong systems and protocols, it is impossible to fully mitigate this risk. Risks of aid diversion from both armed groups and civil governance entities: CARE works on developing strong systems, identifies growing risks trending in the context, and continues to work with UN and donors to strengthen the community-wide response to this risk. CARE also continues to directly monitor project activities at several layers at the area and country office levels. The risk of aid diversion does however continue to be a high risk that is difficult to fully mitigate. The spread of COVID-19 does not undermine CARE's ability to conduct field activities. CARE has developed contingency plans to continue to implement during a significant outbreak in NWS while also mitigating risks to field staff. Access – lack of access to affected population in targeted camp– mitigate with a strong ongoing engagement with the Turkish authorities, local communities and when needed push to overcome with and strengthening the field team, ambitious use of all access points. CARE will be ensuring they have staff trained and systems established to operate safely in this environment. CARE further insists on making risk management a collective effort across the humanitarian community and has worked closely to advocate the UN to taker a greater responsibility in this regard. Safeguarding/do no harm: CARE is working to improve safeguarding measures and specifically improve the collective systems to improve Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
