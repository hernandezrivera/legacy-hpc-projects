Project Summary

In Mozambique, armed conflict, climate crises [including an increase in cyclones], and an increase in
cholera cases are causing an influx of displacees in and into Cabo Delgado, Nampula, and Zambezia
[UNICEF 2024]. Anticipatory action is critical to assure children have access to life-saving, life-sustaining
learning, safe, secure, and supportive spaces, and sanitation services and supplies at school in and through
crises; however, armed actors continue to affect access to schools in Cabo Delgado and Nampula
[UNICEF 2024]. 2M children and 25,000 educators need essential education in emergencies, with gendered
gaps exaggerating exposure to risks for girls [OCHA 2023].
The proposed project targets 15,000 out-of-school children [aged 6 to 13], who have missed out on education or experienced disruption or dropout due to displacement and its distinctive, differentiated effects. [UNICEF and UIS 2016].
This includes (i) children who aren't able to access school; (ii) children who aren't able to attend school
[where attendance refers to being recorded as present >90% of a month]; and (iii) children who have
experienced disruption or dropout [where dropout refers to being recorded as absent >one month and
adverse coping strategies by children and caregivers, concerns for safety and security in, to, and from
schools, and closure of schools, have affected their participation in education programmes. These
categories intersect with (i) children associated with armed groups, (ii) children who are victims of, or vulnerable to, sexual violence.
These children are exposed to extreme risks that endanger their engagement in education, arising from
and aggravated by –
• Adoption of adverse coping strategies, including early marriage and motherhood; forced labor and
recruitment into armed groups; and forced migration [UNHCR 2022];
• Alienation arising from exposure to extreme violence or violations including attacks, abductions,
kidnapping, and killing [children are angry, aggressive, and/or struggle to adopt appropriate codes of
conduct and to strengthen social connections in school [Street Child 2024]];
• An absence of accessible, safe, secure, and sufficient learning spaces and schools as a consequence
of attacks on schools, closure of schools, concerns for safety and security, congestion, and
overcrowding [classrooms contain >200 children in the districts of Ancuabe, Macomia, Palma and Chiure [IOM 2023]].
• An absence of appropriate support for teachers to assure lifesaving, life-sustaining learning for children,
including children associated with armed groups, children who are separated or street-connected,
and children who are victims of, or vulnerable to, sexual violence – and appropriate strategies for
addressing teacher shortages as a result of attacks, displacement, and/or death of teachers [UNICEF
2023]; and
• An absence of coordinated strategies that support children to stay in school in spite of armed conflict
and/or crisis across schools and systems.
The proposed project will –
• Provide 30,000 children and their caregivers with advice, awareness-raising activities, and resources
to promote awareness of rights and risks, including recruitment into armed groups; prevent the
adoption of adverse coping strategies; prevent separation from caregivers, street-connectedness,
and/or sexual violence; and promote the prioritisation schooling.
• Mobilization of children and teachers in coordination with DPEDH where applicable
• 3000 learners in IDP camps and host communities (disaggregated by gender, age and disability status) affected by the disruptions in target schools/communities
• Training of primary teachers on EiE teaching techniques (TiCC), inclusive pedagogy, Psycho-social Support (PSS), positive discipline, interactive teaching methodology.
• Establishment of community reading clubs

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project proposes a critical contribution to commitments to increase access and integrate out of school children into schools: the Government of Mozambique and the CSO articulates a commitment to ensuring equitable access, attendance, and retention for children affected by climate change in its 2020-2029 Plano Estratégico da Educação [ESP]. These policies and plans articulate ambitious reforms; however, clusters assert the absence of current capacities and call for contributions to enhancing and expanding education in emergencies, enshrined in the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plans [OCHA 2023]. Street Child assures close coordination with the cluster coordination architectures and the Ministério de Educação e Desenvolvimento Humano in Mozambique. The key targets for this project include:Component 1:The proposed project will – • Establish 90 ptovisionslearning space (TLS); • Empower 150 tutors from the targeted communities to teach in these learning spaces. • Enroll 8,858 children into stable, stimulating, and supportive learning spaces; and • Enable 6000 children to transition into state schools through targeted, tailored support. - 8,858 children who are out of school or falling behind (4,429 girls and 4,429 boys) will benefit from alternative education activities, with enrolment of 6000 children back to school (disaggregated by age, gender and disability).• 620 adolescents and youth (310 girls and 310 boys) will be provided with life skills vocational training for reintegration and prevention purposes.• 287 children receive School based MHPSS specialized services to overcome trauma and social reintegration.- 4, 832 children will be provided with Teaching and Learning Supplies- 1,919 Children will be provided with safe and adequate temporary learning spaces. This outcome addresses – • An absence of accessible, safe, secure, and sufficient learning spaces and schools as a consequence of attacks on schools, closure of schools, concerns for safety and security, congestion, and overcrowding [classrooms contain >200 children in the North of Mozambique [IOM 2023]]; • An absence of appropriate strategies for addressing teacher shortages as a result of attacks, displacement, and/or death of teachers [UNICEF 2023]; and • Alienation arising from exposure to extreme violence or violations including attacks, abductions, kidnapping, and killing [children are angry, aggressive, and/or struggle to adopt appropriate codes of conduct and to strengthen social connections in school [Street Child 2024]]. Component 2 (Alternative education and VT)• 620 adolescents and youth will be part of either on the job traineeship program or start-up business program. This will mostly target over 13- 17 youth, should any minors participate, a strict set of rules (voluntary basis, very limited hours, not in school hours etc) will be followed to protect the child.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project aims to reach a unique total of 8,858 out of school children and children in school. The project will be implemented in Palma, Ancuabe and Mecufi districts. The major approach is through provision of Accelerated Education (Accelerated Learning, Catch-up and Remedial) focus on children aged 6-12 for reintegration into government led formal education, while Alternative Education (Life skills, basic literacy and numeracy) target adolescents aged 13-17, who have been out of school for long periods or have never been enrolled in formal education face greater risks of not returning to/ entering the school system. Learning supplies, hygiene kits, and menstrual hygiene materials will be distributed to support school enrollment and attendance, particularly for adolescent girls, and prevent school dropout. School feeding programmes encourage attendance and provide essential nutrients to support cognitive development and learning in emergencies.To enhance the quality of education, approximately 150 teachers and education staff will be trained in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) framework, Inclusive Education, and strategies to prevent Violence Against Children (VAC), including gender-based violence (GBV). This training ensures teachers are equipped to provide inclusive, supportive environments that address both boys' and girls' overall needs, including psychosocial well-being. Gender-responsive and inclusive education activities, including life skills and basic literacy and numeracy, will further support adolescents who may not re-enter formal schooling.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All staff will be trained in Psychological First Aid (PFA) and Child Protection (CP) principles. Community mapping of perceived risks and activity planning will take age and gender into consideration, and all assessments will be sensitive to age and gender, so as to encourage the collection of a wide variety of feedback on gender sensitive programme design. Equitable access to all services and activities will be sought through i) ensuring the recruitment of both male and female staff, ii) conducting specific gender and age-appropriate activities. The latter is specifically responding to different risks posed to children based on their gender. There is a higher risk on girls in early marriage and teen pregnancy, greater risk of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) due to presence of armed groups and frequent movement, domestic labour, and deprioritisation for education. On the other hand, boys are exposed to risks such as recruitment into armed groups and subsequent social stigma, becoming unaccompanied or separated, and child labour. Gender will also be a consideration when training alternative carers, ensuring girls could be placed with female carers. As the pre-existing risk of GBV, including exploitation, is compounded by the conflict, all staff will be trained on how to spot GBV warning signs, support survivors, referral systems, and GBV survivors are a case management priority. Sustainability of this intervention will come firstly from the capacity building of national actors, allowing them to form new partnerships and bringing them into the fore of the humanitarian response of Cabo Delgado. Frequent training on child rights and child protection for Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) members and local authorities, coupled with awareness raising and sensitization around Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and grave violations against children, ensure increased community’s understanding of the CP issues and provide the space for them to be the main agent of change in the community. Key risks:• Security – security SOPs will be adopted when opening offices and starting field activities due to the heightened risk in Palma and Ancuabe, and coordination will be sought with INGO security structures. Security of the staff is a consideration given they will be engaging with sensitive topics and working directly with Children Associated with Armed Forces or Armed Groups (CAAFAG), so community engagement and sensitization will be prioritized throughout. CAAFAG could be at risk if they are recognized or ostracized by the community, so all work with CAAFAG will be done under the do not harm principle, to ensuring their safety. • Population movements – population movements make continuous support such as completing the education cycle a challenge, therefore all community engagement will stress the importance of communicating planned and unplanned movements and report to the pedagogical director.• Community acceptance – in particular due to engagement with CAAFAG, potentially with regards to messaging on GBV and early marriage, and crucial to the overall buy in and success of the project. In order to mitigate the risk that the project is not accepted, and therefore does not have the desired impact, all initial engagement will be done with key stakeholders from all relevant beneficiary groups, as well as leadership. • Creating tension between peoples – to avoid the risk of creating tension between host and IDP communities, beneficiary criteria will be vulnerability, not status, based, activities will target those from host and IDP communities, and selection criteria and rationale will be explained by field staff every step of the way.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Women in general, women with disabilities and elderly peopled are often left out and marginalized due to stigma and discrimination, access to services is very limited due to several barriers that include communication, non-inclusive infrastructure, lack of information and many others, women who have internally displaced are as well marginalized in most instances in IDP camps, this is because of different ethnic groups that meet each other at a particular place and one wants to appear superior than the other and as a topping factor language barriers also come into place. Women and elderly in zones of return are sometimes rejected by their community and family as they return. Women who are heads of households and widows feel pressured by the need to sustain their families leading them to indulge in harmful practices. The project recognizes the special needs these women have in terms of access to information and services as well as means of subsistence, therefore the program will closely work with the government multi-sectorial structures, men, community leaders, community radios, women forums to end violence against women to ensure that integrated services are being offered, and everyone is moving towards the same agenda. This program aims in providing inclusive services respecting the Golden principle of “LNOB”- Leave No One Behind. Learning cycles will as well be conducted with other partners or rather and assessment will be done in order to identify the potential gaps and what can be done in order to respond to them. Deep coordination with partners implementing similar interventions and moving towards the same agenda will be guaranteed as a way of reducing duplication of efforts and supporting each other on existing gaps as a way of offering a coordinated integrated GBV response.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will mainstream disability into all the activities and ensure barriers to inclusion are removed to enhance effective and active participation in the project activities by people with disabilities. People with disabilities are often left out and marginalized due to stigma and discrimination, access to services is very limited due to several barriers that include communication, non-inclusive infrastructure, lack of information and many locally available services. Lack of access to safe and inclusive livelihood options for people with disability is increasing multiple risks of GBV including sexual exploitation, child marriage, denial of resources, and intimate partner violence. Displaced women and girls with disability are compelled to resort to negative coping mechanisms i.e, the sale and exchange of sex to meet their basic needs. Livelihood programmes that engage displaced women and girls with disabilities at risk in safe, dignified, and sustainable livelihood options linked to GBV response are urgently needed in settings across Cabo Delgado. It is a set up were literacy levels are very low for PwD and overly it is a patriarchal society where women in general are not allowed to voice their concerns, express their sentiments, make decisions on their own and have access to services available in their communities due to lack of knowledge, lack of decision power and in many instances, lack of available legal support services dedicated for women, worst still with disabilities..

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The constraints are aggravated by climate change, which compounds with conflict and crises to (i) exacerbate dehydration and diseases amongst children as a consequence of changes in climatic controls; (ii) endanger educational access and achievement as a consequence of acute malnutrition and adverse mental health and hygiene; (iii) endanger educational attainment as a consequence of crop failures and food scarcities and shortages subsequent to droughts, floods, and landslides; and (iv) enforce exclusion from education amongst affected children. Climate change also has differentiated and disproportionate impact on girls, who are exposed to early marriage and motherhood, malnutrition, and risks of abuse and violence - and are often the first to be forced to disengage and dropout [Plan International 2024]. Drought is estimated to have effected over 500,000 early marriages amongst girls in Mozambique [Care International 2016].
