Project Summary

The proposed project is designed in response to the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2025 and seeks to break the barriers preventing access to quality basic education for Returnees in Borno and Adamawa States.
The HRP and HNO 2024 indicated that out of the 4.4m persons in need of humanitarian assistance, 1.5m persons require education and vocational services. It further showed that host community members and returnees face severe needs, As a result, an estimated 630,000 children (327,600 girls) have lost access to learning while 42 percent of children in camps do not attend school. 60 percent of girls of primary school age are out of school in Borno State - the highest percentage in the country Of those who do attend, 72 per cent are unable to read upon completion of primary six, while Borno State has the lowest literacy rates at only 35 percent of female and 46 per cent of male adolescents.
Following the needs identified at Konduga, Borno State, Madagali and Michika LGAs of Adamawa state, J-HF proposes to implement activities that will ensure conflict affected children have access to quality and inclusive education in safe learning environments. Furthermore, J-HF will provide accelerated basic education for the children who have never been in schools or dropped out at early grades, provide learning materials and train teachers.
Furthermore, This project also seeks to Support State and local authorities/structures to build resilience for the crisis-affected communities. Therefore, community structures such as School Based Management Committee (SBMC) will be strengthened to perform their functionality and to support management of education, monitoring, maintenance, resource mobilization, and safety for quality education.
J-HF will strengthen the capacity of teachers to adopt Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) and the use of experiential learning which is a great teaching method because it encourages creativity, helps children learn from mistakes, fosters reflective thinking, and prepares pupils for future experiences. J-HF will also provide tablets, smart boards and other modern teaching and learning materials to boost children’s engagement in the classroom which allows them to be more alert and participate in active classroom learning.
To enhance access to education for children, J-HF will form and strengthen Girls 4 Girls Clubs and He 4 She Clubs (safe spaces) within the temporary centers; this will provide a platform to address barriers to education and gender inequality.
The project will improve access to quality education in safe learning environments where children feel safe while communities build resilience to sustain programs. This intervention will also adopt a community-inclusive, culturally sensitive approach involving community institutions and structures.
The project will include persons living with disability (PLWD) across all age and gender groups. Persons living with disability will be supported to benefit from the intervention through a tailored support in line with the type of disability, in Konduga, Borno State, Madagali and Michika LGAs of Adamawa state.
Consequently, the project aims to ensure the continued delivery of robust Humanitarian intervention, the integration of learners into formal education, the strengthening of educators' and school leaders' capacities, and the enhancement of local leadership to fully take ownership of the project, clearly linked with long-term solutions.

1 - Needs

The HRP and HNO 2024 indicated out of the 4.4m persons are in need, 1.5m persons requires education and vocational services, access to education for girls and boys of school age (3-17 years) has been impacted by a range of complex challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, poor/no WASH facilities, insecurity, attacks on educational facilities, a shortage of teachers, lack of premises adapted for children with disabilities and other protection concerns.These challenges have varying effects on different population subsets. Girls are likely to face the risk ofearly marriage, while boys may be forcedly recruited by armed groups. The crisis has exacerbated thesituation for children with disabilities, who constitute 15 per cent of the population (8 per cent of whomare girls), depriving them of essential, needs-based educational services. 56 per cent of all public primaryclassrooms are deemed inadequate, and nearly 1,400 schools have been destroyed since the onset ofthe crisis . Flooding in 2022 and 2023 affected 132 schools, depriving about 50,000 girls, 48,000 boys and9,000 children (4,860 of them girls) with disabilities of access to educational facilities. in Konduga, Borno State, Madagali and Michika LGA Adamawa State poverty fuels child labor to support family income, preventing children from attending schools. It further promotes early marriage and early pregnancy. At the same time, there is a loss of confidence in public schools to provide safe learning environments and quality services, further reducing demand for public education. Consequently,, dropout rates witnessed a 6% increase in primary, and a significant surge of 27% at the secondary level. This trend is largely attributed to the unmet children’s needs, often stemming from overcrowded classes and insufficient education facilities. Furthermore, the need for essential education support is projected to remain considerably high, primarily due to the protracted security challenges, climate crises, gaps in services and the adverse economic impact on IDPs, returnees, and host communities. The extended period during which displaced children have been denied access to education services in the Konduga, Madagali and Michika LGA;s has resulted in noticeable learning gaps. Children in these locations have a pronounced need for education opportunities. Also, there is a need to continue promoting gender equality while also empowering both boys and girls to reach their full potential.Following the needs identified by J-HF at Konduga LGA of Borno State, Madagali and Michika LGA of Adamawa state, J-HF proposes to implement activities that will ensure conflict affected children have access to quality and inclusive education in safe learning environments. J-HF is currently implementing an education project in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States on girl child education therefore has experience in implementing education based interventions.

2 - Activities

Activities Activity 1.1.1. Inception workshop/Advocacy/community engagement Activity 1.1.2 Learning outcome assessment for learners A baseline assessment will be carried out at the beginning of the project to determine the knowledge and grade placement of learners. While the end line will be carried out towards the end of the project. Then, an evaluation will be conducted to determine the impact of the project on the knowledge gained by the learners. Activity 1.1.3 J-HF will Collaborate with the Local Education Authority to assess the levels of damage for classrooms across project locations . Activity 1.1.4 Provision of learning spaces and rehabilitation for children to access education. Activity 1.1.5 Formation of Girl 4 Girls Clubs and He 4 She Clubs The G4G and H4S Clubs is a peer learning clubs (Age 6-17)with a membership 25 members per group. J-HF will form and strengthen the Clubs on right to education, the importance of Education, gender equality, GBV, good hygiene practices, leadership, and life skills; this is to bring a behavioral change of their mindset, conceive core value of education, determining and willing to adapt on situation that surrounds the flow of the system to retain, complete and transit from level to levels. Activity 1.2.1 Engagement of Community Volunteer Teachers (CVTs) to provide Accelerated Education Program (AEP) Qualified CVTs will be engaged and provided with monthly incentives for a period of 9 months. The CVTs will be recruited from the community following a recruitment process. Subsequently, successful candidates will be chosen to provide lessons at the alternative learning centers. Activity 1.2.2 Training of CVTs on the delivery of Teaching at the Right Level (how to ensure pupils read and write with the use of a simple testing tool to assess and group children according to their learning level rather than their age or grade)and the AEP curriculum . Activity 1.2.3 Provision of teaching and learning materials The project will provide teaching and learning materials including those of Teaching at the Right Level to teachers and learners, respectively, in order to improve teachers' pedagogical skills and increase student participation in the classroom. Learners will be provided with numeracy and literacy workbooks, school bags, and writing materials (including exercise books, pencils, erasers, and sharpeners). While teaching materials will consist of facilitators manuals, chalk, tablets, virtual boards and classroom registers. Activity 1.2.4 Conduct supervision and monitoring visits for schools and other educational facilities. J-HF will organize a joint monitoring visit to schools and educational facilities. A monitoring checklist will be developed by the M&E department to ensure focused and effective monitoring and supervision during school visits. A joint monitoring team will be formed with individuals from SUBEB, the Ministry of Education (MoEs), LGEAs, and J-HF staff. Regular monitoring and supervision of schools and project sites will serve as quality assurance for effective teaching and learning. Activity 1.2.5 Mapping and Capacity Building for SBMCs J-HF will use the OCA tool to assess the needs of identified SBMCs in order to determine their capacity. The tool will provide them with a set of criteria to assess their capacity to carry out their mandate. A capacity-building improvement plan will be developed in collaboration with the Committees based on the needs assessment findings. J-HF will train SBMC members and other community-based groups on how to effectively carry out monitoring of education services, budgeting, advocacy, and how to efficiently manage data and information through the development of tools and processes. Activity 1.2.6 Project close out workshop.
