Project Summary

The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non Food items targeting newly displaced people, returnees and host communities affected by conflict, natural disaster and disease outbreak. The project will focus on providing these assistance taking into consideration do no harm, protection mainstreaming and Covid-19 guidelines. The project will focus on the lifesaving shelter/NFI needs as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks. The project will target all states of South Sudan through mobile emergency response as per the need as well as using its static teams based in Kajo Kiji, Yei, Nimule, Torit, Mayom, Mankien, Leer, Koch, Wau, Alek, Aweil, Bor, Akobo, Twic and Duk. However, major focus areas will be Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahrul Ghazal, Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria states.
Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible to the community. When blanket targeting is not applicable, the action will focus on lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, people with disabilities and households with members who have certain health complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no community linkages will also be targeted.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2021 HRP's. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and to start the NFIs and emergency shelters distribution and other activities within 14 days from the assessment. The project will also support returnees in settlement as a part of their local integration or returns to their areas of origin. The project will directly benefit 120,000 individuals.
The SNFI interventions will be implemented in close coordination with the Emergency
Rapid Response Mechanism (ERRM). SNFI actors where implementation is based on the multi-sector assessments and team involving WASH, Shelter, Food security and emergency Education with protection as a cross cutting theme. NRC will also ensure coordination with clusters leads, SFPs and partners on the ground.
NRC team will ensure doing Covid-19 awareness raising campaign throughout the project activities (Assessment, registration and distribution) while ensuringthe Covid-19 guidelines are followed by providing the staff and all enumerators with PPEs. More distribution locations will be considered to avoid any crowd and house to house registrations will be considered.

1 - Needs

According the HNO 2021, the need of emergency shelter and non-food items will affect the living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 2.1 million people. Around 1.65 million women, men, girls, boys and older persons live with inadequate shelter and NFIs. Without shelter or having a strong social support network, women and children are especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and their health, wellbeing, safety and security are at risk. People identified with emergency shelter and NFIs needs in 2021 include displaced people, returnees and 2.5 per cent of the host communities. It is estimated that more than 70 per cent of newly displaced people outside PoC sites and collective centres are unable to meet their basic household needs and will require a humanitarian intervention. More than 50 per cent of returnees also need emergency shelter and NFI support. Insecurity in areas affected by sub-national and localized violence have led people to being newly displaced and have created new vulnerabilities. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) mobility’s tracking report mapped a total of 1,615,765 IDPs (5% previously displaced abroad) and 1,674,668 returnees (32% per cent from abroad) in 2,854 locations across South Sudan. According to the SNFI cluster targeted breakdown document, the HRP 2021 targeted beneficiaries will be 1,130,200 people. This figure includes the internally displaced person (IDPs), returnees and the Host Communities (HC). In cases of returnees, where the areas of returns are stable, the returnees are assumed to stay, rebuild their lives and able to cope with the existing/available resources. The Cluster will also look into alternative support modalities, such as cash vouchers, training, and other engagement with communities. People’s S/NFIs needs are primarily linked to conflict induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. Households in Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites and collective sites do not have access to naturally-grown shelter materials, including for the replacement of temporary shelters. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall Shelter and NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. Any new outbreaks of violence are expected to discourage or prevent people from moving out of camp-like settings in large numbers. Most of the affected population especially those in hard to reach areas have limited access to basic humanitarian assistance. The rainy season has strained humanitarian efforts as well as exposed the IDPs to greater vulnerability, leading to further displacement and hindering accessibility to some areas. The climatic extremes experienced both the rainy and dry season create high need for NFIs to strengthen the populations copying mechanism. The emergency shelter kits provided are not adequate enough and will soon need to be replaced / changed. Most adults still sleep in the open, and occasionally congested in one shelter, while the other available shelters are used to accommodate children, the sick and the older during the night. According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Seasonal Flood Analysis (2020) report, Severe flooding across South Sudan has caused temporary displacement and disruption in service provision to more than 1 million individuals in 2020 (OCHA). DTM mapped more than 100 locations (villages and sites) receiving flood affected IDPs (linked to dataset) through a network of field focal points and key informants, in coordination with other humanitarian partners and local authorities.

2 - Project activities

1-Coordination and Desk work: Coordination with Cluster coordinators, SFP and partners on the ground to gather more information, to ensure not to overlap and to engage partners with all the intervention. Coordination with local authorities will be done to ensure that they are aware of the team deployment and to ensure their support.2-Community Mobilization:- Meeting local authorities and partners on ground to ensure proper coordination, and explaining the project objectives.- Form Community Committee which will be engaged during all the project phases.- Recruiting enumerators for community mobilization in consultation with the community committee. They will be trained on how to contact the community and what messages to be delivered.- AAP training for community leader, local authorities, youth, handicap representatives, etc.- Protection mainstreaming training for the community will involve awareness training on HLP and CDR methodologies.- Raise awareness on GBV through protection mainstreaming.- Establish Community Feedback Mechanism 3-Need Assessment:-Identify beneficiaries in need (focusing on particularly on the needs of women, men, girls and boys), taking in consideration the protection mainstreaming, do no harm, AAP approach in consultation with the community committee. -Setting up CFM system. Communities will be trained on how to communicate their feedback and then how the response will be provided from NRC. - Mixed approach will be followed as using Household Questionnaires, Key informer interviews (KII), focus group discussion (FGDs) and team observations to verify beneficiaries need. Covid -19 SOP will be followed and key messages will be disseminated on symptoms, physical distancing of 2 meter, practice of hand washing more frequently and in case of any symptoms, how to contact MOH.- HLP: Incorporate the shelter due diligence checklists in the needs assessments to ensure HLP considerations in shelter interventions are addressed. General information will be captured based on the available time and context of each location on land rights, clarification of tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership. HLP issues will be mitigated through multiple activities, I.e. having an HLP training on access rights, HLP cases referral, distribution of flyers on HLP rights, working with authorities and community leader to ensure no beneficiaries evection will be done. -Sharing the assessment report with the Cluster for endorsement, along with the verified numbers of beneficiaries.4-PLR: Based on the need, assessment report will be shared along with the PLR with the SFP, Cluster coordinators, Pipeline team and the Logistic cluster team. As soon as the PLR is endorsed by the SFP and cluster coordinators a plan for response will be prepared accordingly. Coordination with pipeline will be done along with the Logistic cluster, to ensure having the items transported on time.5-Beneficiaries Registration: Explain the criteria for selection to the community committee, then registration will be done for distribution of NFIs to the identified beneficiaries. Registered beneficiaries will be issued tokens on which the distribution date and location will be mentioned and they will be made aware of their entitlements. Beneficiaries selection criteria will focus on the most vulnerable population such as:- IDPs, Returnees and the most vulnerable HC (2%)- Women, child and Elderly headed households - Families hosting disabled persons- Pregnant and Lactating WomenBeneficiaries to be targeted will depend on the needs and the actual data on the ground. Distribution will be organized in consultation with the Community Committee. Kits will be distributed on the verification of tokens received from each beneficiary. Distribution location will be agreed on after consultation with the community and local partners, taking in consideration all the protection risks.At all stages, there will be a Complaint ad Feedback mechanism.

3 - Project indicators

NRC will measure the success of the project using various indicators, i.e. number of people reached with in-kind NFIs assistance, number of people reached with in-kind shelter assistance, % of affected population served with S/NFI items reporting that assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, and participatory manner, % of complaints registered through CFM desk addressed, % of distributions followed with rapid monitoring, number of people reached with cash and voucher for essential emergency shelter upgrades and NFI, number of people reached with in-kind lifesaving NFIs and number of people reached with in-kind emergency shelter. NRC will also measure how fast the program responded once the alert is received. These indicators will be measured using different channels, including Intervention reports, Pipeline requests and Post distribution monitoring. NRC will ensure regular reporting to the cluster through attending the weekly reports and submission of the bi-weekly operational timeline report. Coordination with the Cluster coordinators, SFPs, partners on ground and other stakeholders will be done to ensure the proper accomplishment of targets and avoid any overlap with other partners.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project will look into market functionality, as market assessment is designed to be conducted in locations of intervention to identify functional markets. NRC has its own market assessment tools in addition to the SNFI cluster tools available. The outcome will inform the methodology which will be applied.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash will be used only in locations where functional markets are available. SNFI teams will conduct market assessment to identify the availability and variety of SNFI materials. The outcomes of the market assessment, will guide NRC which methodology to use; Cash or In-kind distribution. Market assessment and SNFI assessment reports will be shared with the cluster and other stakeholders.In areas of intervention, and based on the available funds and after consultation with the community, Cash support will be considered where markets are available and functional. NRC will carry out need assessment for beneficiaries and market assessment to find the capacity of market, the availability of needed items as well as to capture the NFIs and materials prices.NRC will come up with the cash amount to be distributed for each household based on the household size, market assessment and need assessment outcomes and it will be coordinate it with the FSP and other stakeholders as well. House to house registration will be done when possible in order to comply with Covid-19 regulations. NRC will distribute the cash with the support of NRC’s cash agent taking in consideration the protection of staff and beneficiaries all the times to maintain the do no harm principle. Distribution locations will be chosen based on a protection analysis and with consultation with the community groups and local authorities, multiple distribution locations might be considered based on the need. Beneficiaries’ registration and distribution sheets will be kept with NRC staff only to ensure data protection. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) will follow to capture the success of the intervention.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All SNFI intervention phases will be carried out by a SNFI team that consists of both SNFI and Protection Officers. NRC Protection officer will conduct PSEA training for different groups of the community including Chiefs, Local authorities, IDPs, Returnees and HC. NRC team will also ensure to engage both males and females from different age groups and people with disabilities as well. PSEA training will also be done during the AAP training since AAP training covers different topics in which PSEA is one of them.NRC protection officer will also do community awareness raising in all locations of interventions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Every SNFI team is composed of SNFI officers and Protection officers during every SNFI assessment. They will visit the communities and will identify the most vulnerable community members in assessment, registration and distribution process. House to house visits will be done in most locations when applicable to ensure covering all the community groups and to ensure having their feedback as well. Feedback and Complaint desk will be sat up during all project phases. Community complains will be recorded and responded during all the phases. Efforts will be made that no vulnerable community member is left out. Community committee will also be formed and trained on accountability to affected population (AAP). Community will be made aware that what support is to be provided by the project. Roles and responsibilities will be clearly explained. Community will be trained on AAP.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability lies at the core of this project. Prior to activity implementation, the protection team will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders are involved in all stages of project development and informed of the feedback mechanisms available to them. Beneficiaries will be able to make confidential suggestions for improving the project, lodge complaints where they disagree with implementation. NRC will ensure that the project will systematically engage with all beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, including the most marginalised, to deliver information about assistance to be provided in such a way as to maximise meaningful participation and consultation. Information shared will include the scope of the proposed interventions, entitlements of those involved, clarify any targeting of vulnerable groups, and information essential to accessing companion services, as well as other relevant information. NRC will consult with communities about the optimal way to share information, and provide information in forms accessible to those with limited literacy and in local languages, to the extent that it is possible. Taking precautions to make the project as transparent and inclusive as possible will both service to make the program more inclusive and contribute to a sense of local ownership, as well as mitigate possible negative consequences of targeting or prioritising vulnerable persons and minority groups. NRC has utilised a variety of approaches to provide beneficiaries with information, including holding community awareness raising sessions, both general and targeted at specific groups according to gender, age, and other considerations. IEC materials used and messages shared will be tailored to meet the needs of these unique groups. NRC has also training community mobilisers to go door-to-door in targeted communities and share information, as well as sharing information over the radio. These and other approaches would be used in the current project, following consultation with communities about the best way to reach affected groups, and depending on the duration of the intervention (i.e. static versus mobile).The NRC response approach strengthens multi-sectoral planning and delivery, mainstreams protection activities across the strategic objectives and focuses on strengthening AAP. A robust inter-sectoral approach has been put in place to ensure that targeted populations and beneficiaries feel informed and consulted throughout the entire humanitarian programme cycle. Through a targeted community communication and engagement plan, it aims to protect vulnerable communities in high-risk areas from sexual exploitation and abuse. Population groups targeted to receive assistance should know and use established complaint mechanisms throughout the entire programme cycle. In addition, project grant opening/ project inception meeting with staff, key stakeholders, government and the donors. The NRC team during the assessment phase will conduct meetings with all the community groups, and will ensure the diversity in all those meetings, those meeting will be done in different ways such as KIIs, FGDs and HH interviews. NRC team will conduct an AAP training for different groups which represents all the community groups from HC, IDPs, returnees, Chiefs and local authorities. During the AAP training and the other meetings, NRC team will provide all the project information including the complaint and feedback mechanism. During distribution NRC will make sure to have a complaint desk which visible and accessible by all beneficiaries. The complaint desk will be managed by one NRC Protection officer, a translator (If needed) and one of the community leaders/Chiefs if the beneficiaries requested to. Before the distribution start, NRC team will do a brief on how people will be served and will also mention the complaint desk and its location for all beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will use the feedback for Learning, improvement, decision making and adaptive programming. NRC will engage the community in decision making during all the project phases. During assessments, NRC team will conduct FGDs, KIIs and HH interviews in order to find out the beneficiaries needs and also to consult them on what type of assistant they need and they are looking for. In addition to that NRC team will also consult the beneficiaries on what is the best way to conduct distribution and all these information will shape the intervention modality after verifying the need and ensuring do no harm principle. Distribution locations and methodology will be also decided on, based on the feedback of the community during the FGDs, KIIs and HH interviews and based on the protection and security team recommendations as well. NRC team will ensure sensitize the community on Covid-19 and will ensure to explain well to them the Covid-19 guidelines and how it can be mitigated during the intervention phases.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.1: In order to avoid this risk, registration and distribution sites will be carefully selected in collaboration with beneficiaries. For registration, efforts will be made for the project team to visit the beneficiaries at their homes to assess potential security risks and as a prevention measure to the Covid-19. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.2: NRC staff has a strict code of conduct on beneficiaries for exploitation and sexual abuse. Action will be taken immediately by the management if such case is reported and confirmed. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.4: Community awareness sessions will be conducted focusing on GBV, PSEA, Child protection and other protection issues guiding the community on what to do in a particular case. In addition, the service providers and referral path ways will be mapped out. Mitigation Measured for Risk 8.5: Mobilization of the community leaders on how to include the minority and marginalized groups during the need assessment and the response. Mitigation Measure for Risk 8.6: Complaint and feedback desk will be arranged at the distribution/registration site where complaints are received at the spot and also responded. NRC team will ensure making the complaint desk visible and accessible by all beneficiaries. Mitigation Measure for Risk 8.9: NRC team will engage the community leaders, Chiefs and local authorities from day 1, and they will be trained on AAP. These stakeholders in addition to the host community will be consulted on the best locations for the displaced people to settle in and will ensure that there will be no land dispute between the community groups. NRC also has HLP team which will be engaged based on the need and the SNFI team recommendations.
