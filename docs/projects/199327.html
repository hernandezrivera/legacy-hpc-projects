Project Summary

The "Cash for Education Initiatives in Conflict-affected Communities of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States" project is a pilot project initiated by the Danuwa Empowerment Foundation (DANUWA) which aims to tackle the challenge of low school attendance in conflict-affected regions by providing cash incentives to students. In these areas, parents and teachers often prioritize sending children to work on farms over attending classes due to economic pressures. The intervention seeks to incentivize regular school attendance by offering cash rewards to students, thereby encouraging their participation in education despite the prevailing circumstances. This initiative aims to increase school attendance and improve educational outcomes in these vulnerable communities by addressing financial barriers and aligning incentives.

1 - Needs

The "Cash for Education Initiatives in Conflict-affected Communities of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States" project is strategically crafted to address multifaceted needs prevalent in these regions. Foremost is the pressing issue of low school attendance of girls stemming from economic hardships. According 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 68 per cent of households reported financial constraints as a major barrier to education causing families, struggling amid conflict to often prioritize immediate financial gains over children's education, leading to diminished school enrollment. Moreover, the upheavals caused by conflict have eroded educational infrastructures and created barriers to quality learning. Recognizing the misalignment between incentives and educational priorities, the project seeks to bridge this gap by introducing cash incentives to encourage families to send their children especially girls to school. By doing so, it aims not only to mitigate immediate economic challenges but also to lay the groundwork for long-term development by ensuring access to education, fostering stability, and empowering the youth in these vulnerable communities.

2 - Activities

In selecting the activities and targets for our "Cash for Education Initiatives in Conflict-affected Communities of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States," we've meticulously aligned our approach with the pressing needs and the most effective strategies to address them. Our primary activity involves the implementation of a cash incentive program and school supplies aimed at increasing school attendance. We chose this approach after thorough assessments revealed that economic pressures significantly impact children especially girl's access to education in these conflict-affected areas. By providing cash incentives directly to families conditional upon regular school attendance, we aim to tackle the immediate economic barriers that hinder girls from attending classes.Our modalities and technologies include a robust data collection system leveraging mobile and web-based platforms. Through these technologies, we'll track attendance, disburse cash incentives, and gather essential data for impact assessment. Additionally, we're partnering with local educational authorities, community leaders, and NGOs to ensure the effective implementation of the program and to garner community support.The selected targets are strategically set based on the assessed needs and the potential for impact. For instance, our target to increase school attendance by 30% within the year directly aligns with the identified issue of low attendance rates. Furthermore, our aim to reach 7,500 enrolled students in the program ensures a substantial impact within the targeted communities. Overall, our chosen activities, modalities, and targets are intricately designed to address the root causes of low school attendance, leverage technology for efficient implementation, and achieve tangible, measurable results that will positively impact the educational landscape in these conflict-affected regions.
