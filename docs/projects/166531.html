Project Summary

In December 2019, a severe type of Coronavirus that caused pneumonia-like sickness was detected in the Chinese city of Wuhan. Since its discovery, its geographic distribution has continued to evolve. As of 20 April 2020, 4.9million confirmed cases and 323 000 deaths have been recorded globally and the virus has continued to spread fast. Zimbabwe recorded its first positive case on the 19th March 2020 and as at 17 May 2020, the country had recorded 46 confirmed cases, 4 deaths and 18 recoveries. Although, the current number of detected cases in Zimbabwe are relatively low, the capacity of the Zimbabwe health system to cope with an expected increase in COVID-19 cases is severely impaired as it is currently characterised by longstanding medical supply challenges that include strike by medical staff, shortage of PPE and life supporting equipment such as respirators. The COVID pandemic comes at a time when Zimbabwe is grappling with recurring WASH related disease outbreaks, acute foreign currency shortages in the health sector, stock outs of essential medicines and increasing food insecurity. The result is that the mandatory government lockdown has affected the most vulnerable and poor citizens who and struggle for a living daily and survive hand to mouth. Social distancing, a key instrument of slowing down the spread, is proving difficult in the country as most of the citizens are scrounging and hunting around for basic commodities like bread and mealie meal.
Women and girls face heightened protection risks in emergencies, including Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR), sexual and gender-based violence as well as forced marriages and harmful practices. The project will address threats to the protection of women and girls in emergencies. This includes problems that existed before the emergency which were aggravated by the crisis including domestic violence, and early child marriages. The project will also address indirect threats like women being put in unsafe positions when they walk long distances to access basic service such as health and water. Action Aid Zimbabwe is receiving distress calls from women in its project areas, hence the need to strengthen women led committees in communities on referral pathways for GBV. In Zimbabwe 1 in 3 women aged 15-49 have experienced physical violence, whilst about 1 in 5 women have experienced sexual violence since the age of 15, usually by their intimate partners. Since the lockdown, there has been a worrying spike in domestic violence across the country, hence the need to enhance women’s protection in this response. Women also make up the majority of the primary healthcare workforce rendering them more at risk of contracting COVID-19 in their line of duty if adequate PPE are not supplied.
The project will target adolescent girls between the ages of 10-19 and women between the ages 20-59 residing in Manicaland Province; in Buhera, Makoni and Nyanga districts, Harare Province, Matabeleland North province; Binga district. The selection of these areas was based on two criteria; the fact that they were identified as vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance as indicated in the Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment report of 2019 and also in order to build on the already existing ActionAid interventions. The target population will be 46992; 36992 women and girls and 10000 will be men and boys including traditional leaders.

1 - Activities

1. Minimize the risk of Covid19 to spread in the communities.Zimbabwe is facing challenges that need special consideration in the aim to minimize the spread of Covid-19. Both in rural and urban areas, are faced with serious water and sanitation challenges, overcrowding, food insecurity and dilapidated health infrastructure. ActionAid will consider those aspects to effectively adapt and implement measures aimed at preventing and controlling the Covid-19 outbreak. Risk assessment specific to settings will be conducted, an adapted plan developed for different setting and risk communication & community engagement promoted.ActionAid will also engage communities through social analysis, to assess risks of Covid-19 spread for appropriate response, empower women community leaders & local authorities to develop the plan; map resources; train community groups in responses actions and work with local stakeholders to focus on organic responses. ActionAid will maintain its humanitarian signature of ensuring women leadership throughout this response. We will maintain close relationships and collaboration with community leaders, local Community based NGOs, as well as local media outlets around effective and correct messaging. 2. Modification of existing Humanitarian and development ProgrammesActionAid will implement the new ways of working in already existing works that fit to the Covid19 context. Required standards such as the Core Humanitarian Standards, SPHERE as well as Gender and Inclusion and Governance Framework will be prioritized to address the needs of diverse groups including People Living with Disability, different age groups (children, older people), as well as to gender equality, particularly to account for women’s and girls’ specific needs, risks and roles in the response to develop and deliver a people-centered humanitarian response. The core principles of the AAZ humanitarian signature including women leadership, accountability and shifting of power to local’s, will be at the center of this response. In formulating and implementing Covid-19 prevention, mitigation and response measures, attention will be taken to assess their possible impact on the most vulnerable among populations to avoid discriminatory impacts.3. Mitigate the social impacts of Covid19 on womenThe pandemic has compounded existing gender inequalities and increased risks of gender-based violence. ActionAid will continue to lobby and advocate for prioritization of protection and promotion of the rights of women and girls. ActionAid will also lobby and advocate for the Government of Zimbabwe (GoZ) to set up a toll-free line to cater for GBV cases and provide referrals to survivors seeking immediate assistance. Provision of an adequate supply of clean water for citizens is also another area of advocacy for AAZ, as this will reduce the burden of unpaid care work for women. Key Activities• Equip established Safe Spaces for women and girls with Covid19 protective measures such as disinfection products, sanitizers, soap, face masks, buckets and containers. • Conduct snap survey regarding the increased figures on GBV cases. • Training of established Women-led committees on GBV referral patterns and information dissemination in their communities. • Dissemination of Short Message Services (SMS) and radio shows regarding Covid19 prevention measures. • Development of IEC material on Covid19prevention measures in local languages. • Distribution of dignity kits. • Advocacy on promoting the continuity of essential health services for survivors of GBV during COVID-19. • Advocacy campaigns on the integration of GRPS in relation to COVID 19
