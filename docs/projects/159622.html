Project Summary

The “Provision of multi-sectoral services to protect the dignity of female head of households in the Gaza Strip" project is a roll-over of an existing project implemented in 2019 by AISHA and fully funded for one year by Global affairs of Canada-GAC approved as HRP project 2019 for three year aims to contribute to mitigate the impact of conflict on the psycho social, mental health and wellbeing of affected women, men, boys and girls in the marginalized localities in the Gaza Strip (middle area, khanyounis and Rafah). The main outcomes of the project are: 1) provision of GBV & mental health services for women affected by conflict in Gaza.2) Build the capacities and awareness of duty bearers (a) Men and Women in the community and family of neglected women (b) community leaders and service providers through raising their awareness on GBV, and Women legal rights. Aisha will work to achieve these outcomes through various activities starting from coordination with main stakeholder to outreach the affected women, and networking with partner CBOs placed in the targeted localities and according to specific criteria receive GBV survivors and to host the project activities. Then, providing structured psycho-therapy and specialized mental health, integrated security, and empowerment training activities for Female Headed of households (neglected women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, GBV, cancer and suicide survivors, and detect the severely affected cases in need for advanced psychological, mental health service to Aisha community Center for mental health and substance abuse through 3 mobile clinics. On other hand, Aisha and the protection cluster partner will apply the principle of do no harm in all activities especially during the period of providing transitional shelter assistance for six months where it will provide these services as transitional phase until the targeted women will be able to manage their own issues which will be through the case management services that provided to them. As well as, AISHA and the protection cluster partner will adopt the inclusion in this project through targeting different groups of women, harmonize the sessions for targets groups and WWDs. Moreover, AISHA will apply the previously developed manual Hawwa Zahra developed according to IASC guidelines, that was revised to adopt inclusion principles for WWDs. In addition, in 2017 Aisha established Aisha community center for mental health, substance abuse, and GBV that was license from the Ministry of Interior and the Ministry of Health. With UNFPA support Aisha developed the protocol of Aisha community center including 5 parts, management of common mental health disorders, case management for GBV, Case management for substance abuse. Also, AISHA has already a referral mechanism in which all referrals and needed information are followed up and traced. Aisha in partnership with the protection cluster partner and Shelter cluster Partner (PHC) Palestinian Housing Center will be responsible to provide the psychosocial, mental health, case management activities, and transitional shelter assistance by provision of rental fees to most vulnerable families in close coordination with shelter cluster. AISHA will be committed to coordinate with protection cluster and GBV SC, report on indicators to HRP periodic reporting and Financial Tracking System; GBV,CPWG, MHPSS working groups through IMS and 5W-Matrix

1 - Needs

By 2020, the UN projects that Gaza will be “unlivable.” Of 1.9 million people 64 % are estimated to be in need. The territory is facing a crisis of hyper-unemployment, food insecurity, electricity blackouts, sanitation disasters, and a failing medical sector. These stresses have been linked to increased incidence of gender-based violence (GBV), including early marriage, while shelters and other service providers struggle to meet the needs with increasingly limited resources. UNW Gender Alert 2019 stated that FHHs, WWDs, survivors of Breast cancer one of the most vulnerable groups that face gender-based vulnerabilities. According to the RNA conducted by AISHA in July 2019 with women victims/survivors of GBV showed that the needs prominent women are need to Support to have a shelter, health Services, Financial and economic, psychosocial and Emotional needs Informational, Support to have job opportunities. According to the GBV sub-cluster IMS dash board (2018), in the identified project localities in Gaza City and South of the Gaza Strip, 2299 women suffered from physical violence and sought services; 2661 suffered from psychological violence and sought services, 894 suffered from forced marriage and sought services; 343 suffered from sexual violence and sought services and 1982 suffered from resource denial and economic violence. According to MOSA sources (2018), there are 250-300 evicted families who reflected their need for safe, accessible and healthy shelter. There are 576 GBV survivors who need MHPSS, 158 cases who need psycho-therapy and 85 cases who need transitional shelter assistance (TSA). Furthermore, according to FGDS conducted with (45 women) 18-60 years beneficiaries of ongoing HRP project funded by Global Affairs Canada,30 women highlighted their needs to have psycho-social support sessions, mental health services, case management and cash assistance. 15 WWDs participants of FGDS needs psycho-social support, MHPSS, legal services, psycho-therapy, and dignity kits following SOPs including case management and assistive tools. Also, AISHA's services are mostly demanded by the wives of governmental employees. That is due to the deterioration of humanitarian situation, salaries cut, legal status and individuals with financial liabilities and loans upon employees which get them locked up based on financial debts, makes women looking for income sources to depend on it as they became the breadwinner, where according to the feedback from currently targeted women, the economic enablement intervention supported women to be self-reliant and sustain income resources a . According to Aisha’s data base, there are specific humanitarian needs under the current crisis in Gaza where more than 1000 women>18 applicant reflect their need for MHPSS services, equal access to services, transportation fees to facilitate their access to medication and other services, recreational activities, and financial support where most of them were the breadwinner for their families and now there is no income resource for food security or shelter or basic health services. To continue its efforts in supporting vulnerable women victims/ survivors of GBV and WWDs in the Gaza Strip, Aisha has designed the intervention of this project to cover other uncovered localities based on the experience and lessons learnt from its current and previous crisis intervention projects implemented in response to the deteriorating situation in the Gaza Strip to improve women GBV victims’ and survivors’ accessibility to multi-sectoral and gender responsive services including GBV case management, psychosocial support, legal counselling, referral, economic enablement, and temporary sheltering services. In addition to enhance community awareness on GBV and Protection mechanisms and pathways and develop service providers’ skills in dealing with GBV and WWDs Activities

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

All the project actions prioritize safety and dignity avoiding causing harm and paying special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and service. All the activities assess and identify gender needs (practical and strategic needs of women GBV survivors). All project staff have long experience working with GBV cases in humanitarian context utilizing the case management approach and referral pathways, and will convey their knowledge to other services providers who will offer a coordinated, comprehensive, confidential and safe attention for the GBV (FHHs and WWDs). Moreover, Aisha will apply Rights Based Approach as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders and empowering them to protect and claim their rights. Follow Code of Conduct ensuring that affected women and girls are treated with respect and dignity, have their welfare promoted and receive support in an environment in which every effort is made to promote welfare and to prevent abuse.-Use complaint system so that affected women>18 years and without disabilities and survivors of breast cancer subjected to GBV can give feedback on quality of services as well as their ways of treatment.- The project is announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities for affected women>18 years to benefit and join the project activities- Select direct efficacies based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities. Their selection should fulfill the global prioritization criteria for the project to be lifesaving, reducing suffering, maintaining human dignity, time critical or critically enabling as well as the oPt context-specific criteria, such as focus on communities at imminent risk of displacement, or particularly vulnerable demographic groups.- Include gender analysis to capture the gender differences including different situations, problems, priorities need of affected women>18 years and girls<8 years with and without disabilities and survivors of breast cancer subjected to GBV, men and boys during the project cycle management phases.- Follow IASC GBV guidelines and enable vulnerable affected women>18 years and girls<8 years with and without disabilities and survivors of breast cancer subjected to GBV to access multi-sectoral specialized GBV responsive, remedial and environment building activities towards reducing risk of GBV, promoting resilience, and aiding recovery of communities to create lasting solutions to the problem of GBV.- Apply GBV approaches like: 1. Rights based Approach that assesses the capacity of vulnerable women and girls (rights-holders) to claim their rights and to participate in the development of solutions that affect their lives in a sustainable way and assesses the capacities and limitations of service providers (duty-bearers) to fulfill their obligations, 2. Survivor-Centred Approach so that that the survivor’s rights, needs and wants are prioritized when designing GBV-related interventions, 3. Community-Based Approach so that affected populations are key partners in developing strategies related to their protection, and 4. Systems Approach that improves humanitarian actors’ knowledge, attitudes and skills related to gender equality and GBV through media and advocacy initiatives. In addition, Aisha will maintain linkage to child protection referral networks for meeting the needs of girls in need for child protection interventions not provided by your organization and committed to establish a follow up/tracking system of referrals and cases received and put in place feedback mechanism to referring organizations and report on that for PC and its working groups

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AISHA will address the difficulties of targeting communities in marginalized areas e.g. ARA in targeted localities including Middle area, Khanyounis, Rafah. In this regard, AISHA follow the guidelines in its "Risk management plan including risk analysis and risk mitigation" and "Security Manual" to protect the lives of their staff during humanitarian work in Gaza Strip. However, AISHA will involve CBOs and local communities in the targeted localities to facilitate vulnerable women (FHHs, WWDs to be engaged in the project activities, besides the project will offer transportation fees so all beneficiaries can approach the close CBOs in dignify manner. All CBO will be identified according to specific criteria that assure safety and accessibility of beneficiaries where AISHA will ensure the inclusive access to GBV services for vulnerable women> 18 years and girls<18 years, particularly with physical disability using adapted buildings/places and with speech and hearing disabilities using the sign language. Media tools and information sharing about project activities will be announced in the targeted localities to facilitate beneficiaries’ access. Finally, fast action responding to any complaints received in terms of access to project implementing localities.
