Project Summary

Extreme levels of acute food insecurity are projected to persist in Central Equatoria and Jonglei due to bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints attributed to insecurity and cases of Gender Based Violence along major roads; with humanitarian agencies facing challenges to reach displaced and vulnerable people. Extreme food insecurity is projected to continue through 2020 with IPC Phase 3 or worse projections (IPC Sept 2019; FSNMS, 2019). A large number of returnees including displaced populations reported lack of food, seeds, tools, shelter among other basic life support amenities (IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, April 2019; OCHA South Sudan Situation Reports, 2019).
Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) aims to provide and support emergency food security and livelihoods via emergency provision of crop, fishing and vegetable livelihood kits that will provide immediate and short terms access to food and incomes among targeted households.
SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in Greater Equatoria and Jonglei and has in addition currently implementing SSHF and UNICEF funded projects in these regions. SPOCI is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and Accountability to Affected Populations Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has for the past five years supported over 55,000 vulnerable populations with WASH/ Education /FSL interventions through UNICEF, SSHF among other partners support in Jonglei and Equatorias. Through these, the organization has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries of Livestock and Fisheries, Agriculture. This is in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.
Proposed activities will enable children (boys and girls), youth, men and women including people with special needs recover have improved and diversified access to food, incomes and improved livelihoods in addition to strengthening community capacities to coping with threats. Targeted beneficiaries will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches and do no harm ethos.
Activities will ensure delivering a package of integrated activities through linkages with other sectors and complementing programs in WASH, Education, Health, Protection to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, by targeting SAM/MAM children and their families; coordinate Relief and Rehabilitation Commission other NGO partners. SPOCI will further integrate the project with other Health-related priorities such as Ebola Task force, WASH, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health, and ultimately resulting in high malnutrition. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

Recent armed skirmishes in Central Equatoria and its environs between various armed actors have led to multiple displacements of an estimated 26,454 IDPs who have been displaced from villages such as Lasu and Mugwo; in addition, there are reported new returnees arrivals from the Democratic Republic of Congo with majority staying in Libogo, Nyori, Asole, Jabara, Alero, Payawa areas, which are makeshift settlements (IOM DTM, 2019). This is in addition to protection concerns which include destruction and looting of property and grave human rights violations (Protection Trends, 2019). This has been exacerbated by additional bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints attributed to insecurity and increased cases of GBV along major roads; further fuelled by destruction of bridges in Lainya and access constraints along major roads such as Juba-Yei, Yei-Maridi, Yei-Ladu, Yei-Tori and Yei-Koyoki roads including mines presence. Pibor County has in addition been compounded by recent waves of active conflict which started in April, 2019, a revenge attack and cattle raiding mounted by a rival community in Nauyapuru, Boma leaving a trail of destruction including looting of nutrition supplies, with at least 20 dead, 74000 livestock looted among other grave human rights violations. This is in addition to subsequent raids later in May/ June 2019 with resultant displacement of at least 8,500 people in Nauyapuru, Rumit, Khoradep and Naokodou and an additional 25,000 displaced on the border of Upper Boma and neighbouring Kapoeta (IRNA Report, Boma April 2019). These events have been further coupled with increasing bureaucratic impediments in Pibor, with latest issuance of threatening letters from youth groups in Pibor and local leadership interference (NGO Forum Updates, May 2019). Despite relative current calm in Yei, Kajokeji and Pibor recent assessments conducted indicate significant population returns of at least 32,000 civilians. However, large numbers of returnees including IDPs reported lack of food, seeds, tools, shelter among other basic life support amenities (IOM DTM, 2019; OCHA South Sudan Situation Reports, 2019). January-April 2020 IPC September 2019 update indicate a deterioration in food insecurity and malnutrition levels in the Equatoria and Jonglei with a projected decline in food availability and access; further compounded by reduced purchasing power, destruction of crops due to fall army worms in 2018 and 2019, looting and destruction of farms due to recent clashes. Conflict in 2019 led to displacements and majority of populations unable to cultivate with an estimated 11000 and 41000 people in IPC 4 in Yei and Pibor respectively (IPC, September 2019 Update). Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) is currently implementing an SSHF funded project in Yei and SSHF/ UNICEF funded WASH interventions in Pibor and proposes to scale up emergency food security and livelihoods via emergency provision of crop, fishing and vegetable livelihood kits including trainings in 2020. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in Greater Equatoria and Jonglei and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs. Activities will ensure integrated programming with WASH, Education, Protection etc to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, coordinate with RRC among other partners. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Targeted beneficiaries, men, women, girls and boys, who include people with special needs and the elderly, will be supported whilst taking into account of their rights and considerations on how each of these groups are affected by conflict and ensuring the project respects their rights and dignities. SPOCI will in addition involve key stakeholder groups and beneficiary representatives to ensure accountability and beneficiary voices are heard and incorporated to inform high quality programming/ response.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SPOCI’s consultative and feedback approach will ensure targeted beneficiaries, mainly displaced people, their views and perspectives on implementation are taken into consideration to inform an two-way appropriate response. These will as well be registered and targeted to benefit from the various support packages during implementation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

SPOCI’s project is linked to the government line ministry priorities in supporting the agricultural sector and tapping into the fisheries resources, in line with CAMP-Consolidated Agricultural Master Plan unveiled by the government of South Sudan. Key community leadership structures and RRC were in addition engaged and consulted including various assessments conducted which key recommendations highlighted included but not limited to provision of inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits); whose benefits can be immediate to mid-term provision of access to nutritious foods and income. SPOCI will hence prioritize these interventions by closely working with line ministries, local stakeholders and beneficiaries including capacity building initiatives that will ensure active participation and sustainability.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project is aligned with the 2020 Food Security and Livelihoods Humanitarian Response Plan, responding to the second and third FSL Cluster Objective; Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing support (in IPC 5, 4 and 3) and Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (in IPC 5, 4 and 3). SPOCI’s proposed projects will work and preposition inputs (crops, vegetables and fishing kits) through FAO Pipeline, coupled with relevant trainings. In addition, inter-cluster synergies will be explored in targeted by close working with partners in nutrition, health, FSL, Protection. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

SPOCI’s target locations are fragile with an unstable context that is likely to warrant insecurity and instability that could negatively impact the project. However, SPOCI has a dedicated Monitoring and Evaluation department that has in the past and will in the proposed project; carry our regular analyses, thorough analysis of conflict dynamics and risks with appropriate mitigation measures via Do No Harm principles to assess the necessity and feasibility of implementation without causing further harm among target beneficiaries. This is in addition to risks associated with Ebola with a recent confirmed case of Ebola in DRC, some 70 KMs from South Sudan border, and Yei being a key transit point and SPOCI will closely coordinate and work with health partners especially while dealing with returnees from DRC.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SPOCI’s sustainability and handover strategy will involve strengthening service delivery through agricultural and fisheries extension workers in the targeted counties/ payams/ Bomas. These will be supported and trained during implementation to take over activities, with strengthened line ministry linkages and local leadership structures to support implementation, even upon project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will mitigate key risks and protection concerns including congregation during implementation, by prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’ including advocacy and prevention of Gender Based Violence and Sexual Exploitation and abuse. SPOCI will ensure non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men. These will also in addition liaise with protection partners including putting in place appropriate referrals. SPOCI will focus on mainstreaming and also fully engage with the community among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities including advocacy on retrogressive cultural practices such as cattle raiding, abduction of children/ women and GBV; and coordinating with Mine Action Group in activities near/ environs of suspect mines/ UXOs/ ordinances.Project beneficiaries will be identified as host/ IDP/ returnee communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided. Household heads providing care to children below 5 years, and hosting pregnant and lactating mothers, the disabled and the elderly will specifically be identified as primary beneficiaries of inputs to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

SPOCI will ensure a two way communication and information sharing with all relevant groups and stakeholders. These will include men, women, girls, boys, youth, people with special needs and the elderly. These will be involved across all stages of planning, consultation, monitoring, implementation. Activities will be conducted in presence of beneficiaries trustees such as local leaders, RRC officials including various youth/ women groups. Areas of implementation and distribution lists will be shared and endorsed by community representatives; in coordination with counterpart agencies. Interventions will be done timely including during safe periods to avoid risks posed during late hours. Active two way feedback mechanisms will be employed including sharing staff contacts and beneficiary feedback desks at various locations in the targeted counties. Conflict sensitive programming will be ensured to ensure do no harm ethos.
