Project Summary

The emergency nutrition response is targeting children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers from host communities and internally displaced persons in food insecure, flood and conflict affected Tonj South County (Warrap state) with chronic emergency nutrition needs.
The planned nutrition activities will as well support health, WASH and GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies with the health, GBV and WASH clusters through Health, GBV and WASH implementing partners so as to reduce nutrition related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in Tonj South County, Warrap state). Therefore, the proposed project activities and targets include early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of 720 girls and boys between 6-59 months for severe acute malnutrition, treatment of 1880 boys and girls for moderate acute malnutrition and treatment of 1100 pregnant and lactating women for moderate acute malnutrition through establishment of four (4) nutrition sites, micronutrient supplementation to 7450 under five children with vitamin A, provide behavioral change messages to 2590 care takers with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to improve nutrition at household level, conduct 48 cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity and nutrition status at household level, carry out deworming for 5810 children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation, conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered as per the sphere standards, conduct testing of malaria for severe acute malnourished children 6-59 months so as to reduce the prevalence of malaria among malnourished children, provide WASH kits to the caregivers of 720 severe acute malnourished (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children, establish a kitchen garden at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity, stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play materials at every nutrition site, conduct Gender based violence safety audits at every nutrition site to make sure child protection and gender-based violence mitigation measures are guaranteed among others. The project intends to support nutrition needs of 18,085 beneficiaries from most vulnerable food insecure and conflict affected host communities and internally displaced persons (IDPs) beneficiaries of Tonj South County of Warrap state, mostly children under five years with acute malnutrition and malnourished pregnant and lactating mothers. This project comes at a time when Tonj South County has been affected by floods and intercommunal conflicts worsening the vulnerability evidenced by high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) due food insecurity caused by limited time for cultivation due to displacements and high food prices due to poor access to markets and inflation. The situation has impacted negatively on the already limited basic health and nutrition services in these locations. Therefore, this project will address the needs of the most vulnerable women, boys and girls affected by the sub national conflict, food insecurity and malnutrition integrated with GBV risks mitigation. TADO will achieve these targets with strong coordination with other Implementing partners and county health departments among others.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report 2022, intermittent communal conflicts and natural disasters like floods coupled with the resent COVID-19 pandemic in South Sudan has deepened the country into humanitarian crisis with 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) with estimated 2 million people including 1.3 million children under the age 5 and another 675,548 pregnant and lactating women expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. The communities of Tonj South County of Warrap state have experienced unprecedented humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent sub national conflict and heavy floods that has caused heavy displacements, loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged this county into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. According to population in need data by the nutrition cluster, Tonj South County’s GAM rate is 16.9% which is above the WHO thresholds of more than 15% calling for nutrition intervention in this county to curb the alarming rates of acute malnutrition. According to the UNOCHA led IRNA assessment report conducted in March 2021 of which TADO contributed, there was evident influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) from Tonj North in Payams of Jak and Tonj and Thiet which worsened the already evident nutrition needs of the communities in Tonj South County. These recurrent displacements have continued to occur from the neighboring Tonj East and Tonj North Counties hence increasing vulnerability in terms of food insecurity and malnutrition. According to the functionality reports (DHIS2) from the health cluster, some of the health facilities in Tonj South County lack nutrition sites leading to mothers/caretakers to travel long distances for one day to collect nutrition supplies which makes it risky for these populations to travel in already insecure communities due to communal conflicts in the region.TADO proposes to bridge this gap by responding to nutrition needs of 18,085 beneficiaries in Tonj South County (Warrap State) by establishing four (4) nutrition sites in these locations mostly in the Payams Manyang-ngok, Wanhrel, Jak and Thiet with high population of IDPs and inadequate health and nutrition services where people walk long distances to receive nutrition services. TADO will utilize the operational and static presence in Tonj South County with field offices where its implementing health project to make sure equitable access to quality preventive and curative nutrition services to children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women are offered. The synergy with other existing health, Nutrition, WASH and protection interventions by other implementing partners in these counties will easily contribute to improve cluster objectives by reducing acute suffering and prevalence of global acute malnutrition in this county. The health facilities and mobile clinics operated by TADO with no active nutrition services will easily integrate nutrition services including other Payams which urgently need nutrition intervention.

2 - Project activities

TADO will address the nutritional challenges of the vulnerable population in Tonj South County though the following activities: 1. Conduct early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition though establishment of four nutrition sites. 2. Provide micronutrient supplementation to the under five children (Vitamin A) at every nutrition site during nutrition visits and growth monitoring. 3. Provide behavioral change messages to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to improve the nutrition practices at household level. 4. Conduct cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity and nutrition status at household level. 5. Conduct deworming for children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation. 6. Conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered are of quality following the ministry of nutrition guidelines. 7. Conduct testing and treatment of malaria for acute malnourished children under five years so as to reduce the prevalence of malaria among malnourished children. 8. Distribute WASH kits to the caregivers of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children. 9. Establish kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity. 10. Stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play materials at every nutrition site. 11. Conduct Gender based violence safety audits at every nutrition site to make sure child protection and gender-based violence mitigation measures are guaranteed.12. Conduct spot exit interviews to assess the level of satisfaction of nutrition service delivery13. Prevent malnutrition among vulnerable children (6-23 months) and pregnant and lactating mothers through blanket supplementary feeding program.14. Support CNVs to undertake home visits.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

TADO shall constantly collect the information from the suggestion boxes at the nutrition sites , community feed back meetings and local authorities and analyze carefully and the build on the complements to provide better quality nutrition services and work on the gaps challenges reported to improve on the project for better service delivery. This shall also be adopted in the future to serve the beneficiaries better in the future programming.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

TADO shall make sure that feed back from most vulnerable members like people living with disabilities, women and girls are considered at every level of the project implementation. The community shall be sensitized on the gender equality issues, women leaders shall be involved at every level of project implementation so that they can front the women and girls' concerns to the relevant authorities. The people living with disability shall be sensitized on the avenues of feed back so that their opinions can count at every level of implementation. TADO shall involve women, girls and people with disability at every level of the project implementation and this will help on improving the avenues of feedback from these vulnerable groups. TADO shall make sure the posters with information on feedback mechanisms are translated in the local languages and pictures that can carry information for these people to easily interpret.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO has protection officer that will constantly sensitize the beneficiaries on the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse from the inception of the project and during project implementation. The local authorities shall be oriented on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse so that they can sensitize the beneficiaries in their respective meetings. TADO shall provide information communication and education materials containing protection against sexual protection and abuse messages in the local language and pin them at the nutrition sites and public places to make sure the beneficiaries are sensitized. TADO shall request the beneficiaries to nominate a focal person from the community who shall constantly spearhead the PSEA activities in the community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TADO always provides information to the beneficiaries about the projects and creates feed back mechanisms for the beneficiaries. In this particular project TADO will print IEC materials in the local languages to inform the beneficiaries on who to approach in case of any concern about the services offered. TADO shall orient and keep sensitizing the community on the avenues of community feedback through community meetings during project inception and implementation. TADO will also engage the local leaders at every level of the project implementation and constantly orient them on the feedback mechanisms available. TADO shall establish suggestion boxes at the project sites where beneficiary concerns and feedback on the project will be put. TADO shall form accountability to affected population committees that will routinely gather feedback from the community and address the concerns through relevant stakeholders. TADO shall orient the local and women leaders to gather feedback from social gatherings and churches so that all the community members have equal chances to provide feedback to the relevant stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. This is a medium risk and therefore TADO shall make sure safety audits are conducted at every nutrition site for women and girls so that protection risks are identified and dealt with. TADO shall also involve the local authorities to make sure women and girls are protected from violence along the way. 2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. This a low risk and TADO shall make sure that the beneficiaries especially women and girls are sensitized from the inception of the project on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse and their rights. Women leaders shall be involved at every stage of the project implementation so that they are sensitized on PSEA and continue to sensitize the women and girls. A PSEA focal person shall be appointed and introduced to the community so that any early warning signs of sexual exploitation and abuse can be reported to the responsible authorities. 4. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). This is a medium risk since the protection services at community level are limited. TADO will include protection awareness in our routine programming so that vulnerable beneficiaries especially women, girls and children shall be sensitized on the possible protection issues and prevention measures. TADO shall work with the protection partners in the project locations so that the referral pathway is established and active to support the survivors of GBV and child protection issues among others. 5. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. This is a medium risk and TADO shall make sure that marginalized groups like persons with disability, older persons and women are involved in the decision making process at every level of project implementation.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

TADO shall make sure that through enrollment of maternal infant and young child feeding nutrition practices at community level and the nutrition site, nutrition prevention practices will be instilled in the communities. Through conducting of safety audits at nutrition sites and identification of GBV cases and referral to for protection services, response to GBV will be rolled out. TADO will make sure psychosocial counselling and capacity building of the nutrition staff and the community volunteers will be done.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

This nutrition project shall make sure the flood related displacements are reduced through engaging the community to develop durable and sustainable solutions to floods by relocating to safer grounds where they will be supported and oriented to improve food security through establishment of kitchen gardens. Through collaboration with NFI, food security and livelihood implementing partners, these communities can be supported to make channels and dikes to reduce flooding in their areas

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Our project activities are designed to reduce the number of people in IPC 4 and 5 through active case finding at community level so that the malnourished women and children are identified at moderate states and managed before worsening to the severe states. TADO will routinely conduct maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to the beneficiaries and this will improve nutrition practices at house hold level, dietary diversity, food hygiene and sanitation which will in the long run improve the nutrition status of the beneficiaries and ultimately reduce the prevalence of malnutrition. TADO will establish cooking demonstration sessions at the nutrition sites so that the beneficiaries will improve on their cooking habits at household level. TADO shall improve establish cooking kitchen garden demonstrations for each nutrition site and this will ultimately improve the nutrition and food security at household level in the priotised locations. When there is reduced food insecurity, the households will have reduced gender based violence since the resources that would be spent on the management of malnutrition is saved to improve on the livelihood status of the household. The demonstration of kitchen gardens at the nutrition sites will improve food security at household level which will ultimately reduce the number of people in IPC 4 and IPC 5.
