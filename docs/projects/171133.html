Project Summary

The project will target affected population groups (newly displaced populations, IDPs living in a protracted situation, refugee returnees and affected host communities) with shelter and NFI interventions. The interventions include provision of NFI kit, emergency shelter and transitional shelter.
According to JMCNA 2020, Shelter is the first priority need of more than half of the population. 3.15 million people are in need of shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to inadequate shelter conditions, overcrowding, economic hardship and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include IDPs, poor host communities, refugees and asylum seekers and refugee returnees. Factors that have been considered for calculating the severity and people in need include shelter conditions, type of shelter, overcrowding, availability of NFIs at household level and security of tenure.
UNHCR plans to distribute NFI kits to 90,000 households (HH) in kind/cash, provision of emergency shelters to 25,000 HH and transitional shelters to 500 HH. The project will target 540,000 individuals with NFI kits, 150,000 individuals with emergency shelters and 3,000 individuals with transitional shelters. NFI, emergency shelters and transitional shelters assistance will be provided in cash or in kind and the response modality will depend on the availability of items in the local market, user preference and associated protection risks.
UNHCR will carry out Post Distribution Monitoring and will establish Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanism for each project. The Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted 4-6 weeks after NFIs and ESKs distribution to analyze the impact of the project, the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction and establish the lessons learnt for future programming
UNHCR will target vulnerable HH including women headed households, child headed households, households with persons with disabilities and households with the elderly.
UNHCR will undertake infrastructure support targeting 8,250 vulnerable households (49,500 individuals). Site decongestion through expanding the site using available adjacent land and through basic site planning will be a priority and will be implemented where feasible under infrastructure support. Most of the IDPs live in the sites that are overcrowded and are not planned, leading to the increased risk of fire outbreak, spread of diseases including COVID 19, GBV related incidences and flooding due to the absence of or poor drainage system.
UNHCR will target a total of 543,000 individuals of the 2.5 million individuals targeted by the Shelter Cluster with assistance. Of the targeted individuals, 83,803 women, 81,616 men, 170,927 girls, 184,794 boys, 21,860 elderly and 81,450 disabled individuals. The project targets 108,600 vulnerable individuals from the host community and 434,400 vulnerable IDPs. The targeting will occur based on UNHCR and Shelter Cluster assessments for those most in need and highly vulnerable. The project will target Bari, Banadir, Bay, Lower Juba, Gedo, Mudug, Hiraan, Nugaal, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle and Woqooyi Galbeed regions.
The activities will be implemented through partners. UNHCR will monitor the activities through the Implementing Partners. Settlement leaders and local authorities will participate in different phases of the interventions. Monthly project tracking will be carried out and 4W matrix inputs shared on monthly basis with Somalia National Shelter Cluster.

Project Activities

1. Provision of 90,000 NFI kits.NFI kits will be provided to affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities and refugee returnees so that they have basic household items at the household level to protect themselves from weather elements using the blankets and mosquito net and to live in dignity. Each household will get NFI kit comprising of 1 plastic sheet, 3 blankets, 2 jerrycans, 1 solar lamp, 2 sleeping mats, 1 mosquito net, 1 kitchen set (consist of: 7 litre cooking pot, 5 litre cooking pot, kettle, steel cup, steel plate, steel bowl, tablespoons, serving spoon and kitchen knife) and 0.75kg of soap. In case the assistance is provided in cash, each household will receive $100 through mobile transfer to procure NFI items from the local markets. Out of 90,000 NFI kits, UNHCR will maintain a stock of 5,000 to respond to new displacement 2. Distribution of 25,000 Shelter kits.Shelter kits will be provided to affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities and refugee returnees. 150,000 vulnerable individuals will receive Emergency Shelter Kit (ESK) in kind or where cash assistance is feasible, UNHCR will provide cash which they will utilize to procure the Shelter Kits from the local markets. Each HHs will be provided with $237 cash. Partner technical staff will ensure that shelters are built according to the agreed standards through regular inspection visits to the project sites. 3. Construction of 500 transitional shelters.Construction of 500 transitional shelters to support reintegration of returnees, and for affected population such as vulnerable and disaster affected IDPs and host communities who have long term security of tenure. The transitional shelters will be constructed using the owner driven approach. 4. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) and Market Assessment. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) and Market Assessments will done for all activities.A Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted 4-6 weeks for all activities to analyze the impact of the project, the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction, feasibility of cash based response and establish the lessons learnt for future programming.Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) will be established for all activities. The beneficiaries will be fully aware of the scope of the project through community mobilization and sensitization which will be undertaken at the onset of the project. The beneficiaries and community committee will be informed about the beneficiaries’ selection process. During distribution, multiple sites will be established to prevent congestion and thus minimize the risk of COVID-19 infection. Social distancing will also be applied at the distribution sites. 5. Infrastructure support: Decongestion of existing sites will be done by undertaking basic site planning and expanding the sites. This will also help in minimizing the spread of diseases including COVID 19, mitigate risk of fire outbreak, reduce GBV related incidences and flooding due to the absence of or poor drainage system. UNHCR will use age, gender and diversity approach to address the needs of the affected population. Different population groups including men, women, children, older people and disabled will be consulted and their specific needs will be considered in planning and implementation of the planned interventions. UNHCR will target a total of 543,000 individuals of the 2.5 million targeted individuals with target assistance. The targeting will occur based on UNHCR and Cluster's assessments for those most in need and highly vulnerable. UNHCR will coordinate with other partners especially CCCM, WASH, Protection for site decongestion and provision of transitional shelters.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In locations where markets function and materials are available, beneficiaries prefer cash. UNHCR will assess the protection risk and will use cash where the protection risks are minimal. According to PDM reports carried out by UNHCR Partners on NFI distribution in Qardho, when asked about their preferred modality of assistance, the majority (75%) of the respondents indicated cash assistance. Only 5.1% were in favour of in-kind support. PDM report on NFI distribution in Bardheere, when asked about their preferred modality of assistance, majority (88%) of the respondents preferred cash to be put in form of mobile money for buying the non-food items as opposed to receiving the items directly from UNHCR. Only 1% preferred in-kind distribution. UNHCR uses Cash-based interventions to provide protection, assistance and services to the most vulnerable. UNHCR has strong experience and existing mechanism, operational standard operating procedures and strategies in planning, designing, implementing and monitoring Cash-Based Intervention. Cash-based interventions as modality of protection delivery, provides dignity, flexibility and choice for persons of concern, as they allow people to decide and address their own needs. Beneficiaries are able to purchase items of their choice, size and quantity. If a house needs of repair, the beneficiary can purchase the required material and undertake the repair. Cash-based interventions helps to increase protection outcomes by enhancing; protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safe and dignified delivery and contributing to protection benefits, in areas such as child protection and SGBV and reducing negative coping mechanisms by meeting basic needs. Moreover, cash-based interventions improve cost efficiency in programming. Particularly in the current situation of COVID-19 pandemic, Cash-based interventions enables and enhances the efficiency of program delivery. In addition to this, cash-based interventions have multiplier effects, directly benefiting the local economy as affected populations purchase goods and services in the local market but also promote peaceful coexistence and social cohesion with host communities.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

UNHCR Somalia has framework agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) since 2014 to implement CBI interventions which is executed through Al Amal Bank inside Somalia. The project will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI SOPs. UNHCR and partners have successfully implemented cash based response in recently in Baidoa, Dollow, Kismayo etc. as markets are functional. This is further strengthened and confirmed by the Joint Market Monitoring (JMM) of Shelter Cluster in August in twelve urban locations across the country, indicating that markets are functional. Shelter Cluster with WASH Cluster and REACH conduct joint market monitoring every 3 months and that considers market functionality.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Impact: beneficiaries will be exposed to protection risks and will fail to benefit from protection and assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is mainstreamed across the whole project cycles and that partners and community leadership structures are also aware of their obligation in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and to meet the policy of zero tolerance. 3. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Impact: those in need might not receive assistance and protection Mitigation measure: UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries. All interventions will be guided by age gender and diversity mainstreaming approach that ensures that all groups in the community are consulted and their capacities and needs are identified. UNHCR will existence of a zero-tolerance policy to fraud with the framework of the project and across all stakeholder of the project. Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms will be strengthened, and communities will be made fully aware of these mechanisms. 4. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. Impact: Lack of existing and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms will result beneficiaries not able to report diversion of assistance/fraud and beneficiaries not able to access assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will strengthen and establish multiple complaint and feedback mechanisms including, a hot line or toll-free phone number that can be available 24/7, suggestion boxes, community structures that allows for community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms, designated emails, UNHCR field and partner staff. In addition to this, regular beneficiary contact monitoring through post distribution monitoring through third partner will further strengthen the complaint and feedback mechanism process. UNHCR will distribute IEC materials with information on existing complaint and feedback mechanisms. 5. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Impact: competition over scarce resources and limited assistance available will negatively affect inter-communal dynamics and may lead to deterioration of protection environment for persons of concern. Mitigation: continuous engagement of relevant actors, including communities and community leadership structures to inform on the targeting and vulnerability criteria used for assistance provision. 6. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area. Impact: tenure insecurity and evictions are one of the key protection risks affecting IDPs in Somalia. Mitigation: UNHCR and partners will carefully access and weight tenure security issues before implementation of any shelter intervention.
