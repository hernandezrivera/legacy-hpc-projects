Project Summary

HLSS With the proposed intervention HLSS intends to respond to the humanitarian needs identified in the HRP prioritized locations of Bor south and Twic East counties of Jonglei state and Torit and Magwi counties of Eastern Equatoria state. The Humanitarian needs continues to grow in the proposed locations with continue to indicate minimal responses from humanitarian actors covering the whole affected population in the prioritized counties coupled with the state of emergency declared in the affected counties and those in high risk level for possible flooding, HLSS intends to reach to 32,000 individuals with GBV responses and prevention intervention of which (13,000 Women, 9,000 Girls, 6,000 Men and 4,000 Boys) Therefore with the proposed intervention HLSS will focus on the Tier one activities which are basically response activities and some of the activities on Tire 2 more of prevention activities, and these shall include the following activities:
1. Provision of sex and age appropriate comprehensive case management services to survivors of GBV. This shall be conducted by the cases workers, 2. HLSS shall conduct service mapping to ensure referral pathway is established/updated to support multi-sectoral services for survivors of GBV. 3. Through this intervention HLSS shall ensure survivors of GBV through their consent shall access other specialised services through referral made by the case workers in line with the established/updated referral pathway. 4. A response through the provision of dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age shall be a knee activity under this intervention, this shall help in maintaining the dignity of women and girls of reproductive age. 5. HLSS shall conduct awareness raising/information dissemination to the community about existing services for women and girls in the proposed project intervention sites, this shall promote timely response to GBV cases and also coping mechanisms for women and girls who have undergone any form of abuse by accessing the WGFS established. 6. HLSS through the proposed intervention shall establish WGFS which shall support women and girls through psychosocial support activities, PFA and other skills building activities. 7. HLSS shall ensure to work with other partners such as Child protection, WASH, Health and general protection to mitigate the risks women and girls face in access the mentioned sector services. 8. HLSS shall ensure training of the services/front line service provider is conducted.
By providing the mentioned services HLSS looks to promoting the dignity of women and girls from the different forms of GBV they have gone through.

1 - Needs

Five years of the most recent conflict has forced nearly 4.2 million people to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. While the intensity of conflict may have reduced recently, and clashes contained to certain regions, vulnerable people continue to experience the impacts of the conflict through 2019. UN reports indicate that all parties to the conflict have repeatedly violated international humanitarian law and perpetrated serious human rights abuses, including gang rape, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls, and recruitment of children, both girls and boys. People continue to be affected by the conflict, including the more than 300,000 refugees in South Sudan, repeatedly identify security among their primary needs according to the HNO 2019.Even with the advent of the revitalized peace agreement in late 2018 and the promise of better times to come, the cumulative effects of the conflict have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than 7 million people in South Sudan. This is particularly the case in the Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity, where drivers and multipliers of crisis have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal conflicts, ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, economic instability and insecure access to food and livelihoods. Yet, prospects for peace and development may improve and begin to generate some confidence for durable solutions, including returns, relocations or local integrations, although their scope, scale and flows remain difficult to anticipate.In Bor South County in particular the most humanitarian partners have concentrated services within the Bor town leaving the outskirts of Bor South county where there is dare humanitarian needs identified. According to the proofing and scoping of the population in need to services by HLSS CCCM program in Sept 2019 in Bor South county, the findings indicated that women, girls, children and elderly have been most affected in the 5 Payams and inadequate and lack of services ranged from general Protection, GBV services, schools, health services, nearby markets, water among others. These findings clearly posse protection risks to women and girls. Twic East with the current flooding which has made the Government to declare state of emergency has got most of the population affected and displaced. According to the OCHA updates on the flooding situation Twic East remains a high risk and most of the affected people are Women, girls, children and the elderly. HLSS currently is implementing GBV prevention and Response program in Twic East however not covering all the 6 Payams, thus GBV services in the further payams continues to remain a gap since most partners are implementing within Panyagor, Kongor and pakeer. The lack of GBV services coupled with the flooding situation puts women and girls at high risk of in the GBV incidences alongside other services such as housing and shelter, NFI etc. However, Torit continues to be relatively claim with access to basic services like health care, GBV services, nutrition services, WASH services, there is still inadequate service provision as the is high number of returnees from the refugee camp in Uganda, still coupled with the cultural norms and beliefs women continue to be looked as property and abuse of women physically continues to be high in Torit according to HLSS GBVIMS data for quarter 3. HLSS apparently is running a one stop centre GBV services in Torit state hospital but this does not cover the service gaps in all the payams in Torit county thus the risks of GBV to women and girls continue to remain high in Torit county. Same to Magwi county HLSS is running Primary Health Care services under HPF through the CHD, BBC and GESI have identified GBV services gap for Women and girls.

The proposed intervention shall mitigated risks selected in a various activities proposed in the intervention, conducting awareness raising on GBV key concerns in the community, disseminating information on avaliable services for GBV survivors and accessing other services through the existing referral pathway shall knee throughout the project implementation secondly establishing complain and feedback mechanism through conducting client's satisfactory survey about the services, conducting monthly AAP meeting with women and girls at the established WGFS as well as involving women and girls to agree on PSS activities to be conducted at the WGFS shall be key throughout the project implementation. These findings shall be shared with partners and advocacy made to them in implementing the recommendation jointly to mitigate the risk exposures for women and girls.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HLSS has employed different mechanisms for feedback in its various field of operation, thus with the proposed intervention HLSS shall compile the same feedback mechanisms, HLSS understands very well the challenged in incorporating complains and feedback of beneficiaries and it is key in project implementation. Therefore the following systems shall be put in place throughout the project implementation, involving of the beneficiaries right from the project design by conducting rapid needs assessments so that the needs are informing and aligned with the proposed project activities. HLSS understands very well that beneficiaries in the proposed locations (women, men, boys and girls) are both illiterate and literate therefore complain and feedback mechanism shall be set in two ways both written through an information desk at the WGFS and through FGDS and monthly meetings and feedback documented by the staffs and feedback given back to the beneficiaries. Involvement of the community structured leaders throughout the project implementation shall be one key thing looked at as this shall be one of the strongest ways of their involvement in monitoring the activities. As well as the different humanitarian actors on ground shall be engaged to ensure multi-sectoral complaints raised by the beneficiaries during project implementation are communicated with them and feedback given back to the beneficiaries through the various strategies set in place. HLSS shall also set follow of communication between the beneficiaries, community leaders and the implementing partners which shall allow easy follow up of complaints for feedback throughout the project implementation period.
