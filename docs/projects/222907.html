Project Summary

This project will provide Integrated, lifesaving, emergency health interventions for populations affected by conflict, floods, disease outbreak and displacement in the 5 counties of Akobo, Pibor, Pariang, Abiemnhom and Mayom. This intervention will target a total of 168,057 people (82,348 Males and 85,709 Females). Out of these, 35,292 will be under the age of 5 years (17,293 Males and 17,999 Females) and 132,765 above the age of 5 years (65,055 Males and 67,710 Females). 6,722 will be Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). 2,689 of those targeted will be people living with disability. 21,087 of those targeted will be IDPs (under-5 – 5,063 and above 5 – 16,024), and 36,581 of those targeted will be Returnees (under-5 – 9,056 and above 5 – 27,525). The project will focus on children, women and men in the 5 target counties above. The project will ensure improved access to an integrated package of emergency lifesaving health services focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected, flood-affected, and vulnerable IDPs, host community and returnee households in Pariang, Akobo, Pibor, Mayom and Abiemnhom Counties.
Based on the 2025 health cluster strategy and objectives, the project will provide a package of health services focused on the following:
- Providing and improving vaccination for children under the age of 5 years, targeting returnees, internally displaced populations, and populations under IPC 3, 4 and 5.
- Improving access to crisis affected populations and those who live in remote and insecure locations who would otherwise not have access to health services.
- Capacity building and training of health personnel to enable them provide a wide range of health services per the BPHNS.
CARE will focus on:
1. Returnees in Pariang and Abiemnhom that have continue to arrive, particularly through the Jau returnees and refugees from Sudan through the Jau border point. Though the numbers of refugees and returnees have been fluctuating between weeks and months, CARE, through its programs in the two counties, will continue to provide health services to returnees and refugees.
2. Displaced populations, driven by inter- and intracommunal conflict and cattle raiding, also contribute significantly to the high levels of morbidity and mortality. During such conflicts, livelihoods are destroyed, particularly in Pibor and Akobo, where over 50,000 people are estimated to be displaced, heightening the dire health situation.
3. People in IPC 3, 4 and 5 that are affected by severe food insecurity: the projected severe acute food insecurity and the malnutrition, will continue to drive high morbidity and mortality, particularly in IPC 3, 4 and 5 counties.
The ongoing economic crisis, currency devaluation, and reduced market access and functionality are also expected to play a detrimental role in the deterioration of the food security situation in 2025, and the resultant health status of the population.
To do this, CARE will support static health facilities as well as mobile health units in Pariang, Akobo, Pibor, Abiemnhom and Mayom based on need. At the community level, CARE will use Community Health Workers (Boma Health Workers) to provide community based treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea, as well as screening and referral for malnutrition and immunization for children under the age of 5 years. Boma Health Workers will also deliver other services including health education, surveillance and risk communication and community engagement.

1. Needs

The health system in South Sudan remains weakened by multiple shocks experienced in the past years and continues to deteriorate further with the influx of refugees and returnees from the Sudan crisis and the impacts of floods that have affected over 1.4 million people, with over 379,000 people displaced and health facilities submerged and/or cut off from the population as of November 15, 2024 (OCHA, 2024), in addition to the ongoing cholera outbreak with suspect cases reaching over 400 and 7 fatalities (WHO & MOH, 2024). This project will provide integrated, lifesaving, emergency health interventions for populations affected by conflict, flood, disease outbreak, and displacement, targeting a total of 168,057 people (82,348 males and 85,709 females). Out of these, 35,292 will be under the age of 5 years (17,293 males and 17,999 females) and 132,765 above the age of 5 years (65,055 males and 67,710 females). 6,722 will be pregnant and lactating women (PLW). 2689 of those targeted will be people living with disabilities. 21,087 of those targeted will be IDPs (under-5 - 5,063 and above 5 -16,024), and 36,581 of those targeted will be returnees (under-5 - 9,056 and above 5 - 27,525).The triple impact of Flooding, Conflict and Food Insecurity have continued to negatively impact many of the locations where CARE operates (Pariang, Mayom, Abiemnhom, Pibor and Akobo). This has resulted in the displacement of the population and destruction of health facilities and properties, significantly affecting the capacity and functionality of donor-dependent health systems, affecting over 102,474 people in Mayom, over 157,649 in Pibor, over 108,042 in Akobo, and over 34,634 in Pariang and 20,342 in Abiemnhom according to Health Cluster Persons in Need (PIN) estimates for 2025. The current flooding has destroyed health facilities and WASH facilities (boreholes, water points, and latrines), leading to widespread contamination of water sources and loss of water storage containers as well as long-lasting insecticide-treated nets (LLINs). As a result, malaria and acute watery diarrhea (AWD)-related morbidity and mortality trends in the second-last quarter of 2024 were above those of 2020 within the affected counties, particularly in Mayom and Pariang. In addition, and more worryingly, flooding has hugely impaired access to health services in Pariang and Mayom and the counties are amongst the high risk for cholera outbreak, (WHO, 2024; MOH, 2024).Roads have been closed. Transport is available, but it is expensive and not easily accessible, which further hinders access to critical maternal and child health services at health facilities. The lack of access to health services, with only 28.6% of the population living within 5 km of a health facility (Macharia et al., 2017), results in only 2.6% of the population achieving the recommended 9 vaccination doses (L. Belaid, 2022, ISNA, 2023). This, when combined with population displacement due to flooding, creates an environment that is conducive to disease outbreaks. Moreover, outbreaks of cholera, measles, meningitis, and Hepatitis E have been declared in the CARE areas of work by the Ministry of Health and WHO in 2024 due to low immunization coverage of 27.5% in states like Jonglei State (Jok. P. M. J. Jil, 2022) due to frequent population displacement by flood and other factors. In response, vaccination and risk communication efforts for measles, meningitis, and cholera are ongoing in various counties. Low immunization coverage within the counties where CARE operates continues to be an issue of concern, with Penta 3 coverage at 58.4% and measles at 56.6% coverage at the state levels. Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 64% of all morbidities and 69% of all mortalities in 2023 (WHO, 2024; MOH, 2023; ISNA, 2024). The ongoing crisis in Sudan is driving up the humanitarian needs, with the few available resources being diverted to cater to returnees.

2. Project activities

CARE will provide Emergency Life-saving Health services in a total of 10 Static and Mobile Health Facilities across 5 Counties of Akobo, Pibor, Pariang, Abiemnhom and Mayom in line with the South Sudan’s Ministry of Health Standards and Guidelines as well as the WHO and SPHERE Standards and guidelines. The response activities implemented by CARE will be based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden and seasonality. CARE will work with other clusters and partners to ensure integrated response across the field locations. These Clusters include Nutrition, Protection and FSL. CARE will also put in place a robust system of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints from beneficiaries. This will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence based decision making in order to improve program quality and performance. In the 5 Counties where CARE proposes to work, it is also the partner that is implementing development related health funding from UNICEF through the HSTP Project. CARE will therefore ensure that the activities carried out under the HNRP will be linked to the Development /longer term funding. CARE will also work closely and coordinate with HSTP partners, CHD, SMOH as well as UNICEF and WHO to ensure smooth delivery of health services within the above counties. To address the identified health needs within the identified counties, CARE will: 1. Improve access to lifesaving essential health services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, provision of GBV and MPHSS services to the vulnerable population including those living with disability. 2. Reduce mortality and morbidity by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases 3.Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. CARE will train staff on emergency preparedness and response for diseases of outbreak potential. This will ensure early detection and response. In addition, CARE will strengthen its existing surveillance systems through the IDSR and EWARS. CARE will provide health services that are adapted to the prevailing conditions and context. This will be in addition to ensuring infection prevention measures are taken into account while continuing to deliver health services. Specifically, in response to the 2025 HNRP, CARE will carry out the following activities. - Ensuring epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response across all its health projects. - Providing immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities for the health programmes. - Provision of BeMONC/ CeMONC services including procurement of essential RH commodities and building the capacity of skilled birth attendants - Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non- communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. - Integrating MHPSS and SGBV services in health facilities and provision of supplies for MHPSS services. - IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the delivery of health services. There will also be supervisory visits to ensure quality provision of health services. - Community health service provision linking with facility- based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives - Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices for the for disabled.
