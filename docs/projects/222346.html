Project Summary

UNIDOR will support 7,000 HHs (42,000 people) with Food security and livelihood interventions across the 2 counties. The proposed support will focus on communities to transition off humanitarian assistance through sustainable and long-term systemic interventions. UNIDOR has identified that flood displaced and other conflict-affected households in Koch, and Panyijar are facing multiple shocks that has severely limited livelihoods, which has come on top of economic erosion, failed harvests that are stretching the lean season flooding, communal violence, and displacement.
UNIDOR will be ensure that the project activities are in line with HNRP Cluster Objectives such as to(i) reducing the morbidity and mortality Vulnerable crises-affected people through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and FSL Cluster specific objectives on providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 ) This will be achieved through provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations, Distribution of Unconditional cash transfer to the most vulnerable households - Distribution of inputs (fishing kits & quick maturing vegetable seeds), Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support, Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock through provision of emergency food & livelihood support, fishing kits and vegetable kits to help them meet their food needs from own production and improve livelihood condition, Conducting extension trainings for most vulnerable farmers on modern agricultural techniques, Livelihood support to returnee households through provision of multipurpose cash, Establish kitchen gardens near nutrition sites, Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes through Conducting awareness for most vulnerable populations both female and male on environment protection, gender involvement and equality, Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. Training and supporting agricultural extension workers to provide sessions and technical support to agricultural beneficiaries, Establish and support VSLA groups for vulnerable households to improve savings, Establishment of Farmers Field Schools (FFSs), Training the beneficiaries in post harvest handling techniques Establishment and training of project management and ensure accountability measures.

1. Needs

As per the IPC analysis of October 2023, approximately 7.1 million people of the total population of 12.4 million will face acute food insecurity at crisis levels. Reports from HNRP 2023-2024, according to Report from (Initial Rapid Flood Needs Assessment Report Mirmir and pakur payams, Koch County 20th September 2024, Unity State) over 36,000 people in Koch County are affected by flooding, with many homes and gardens submerged, seasonal flooding further complicates the situation, resulting in crop and livestock losses and exacerbating food insecurity. Access to basic services like healthcare, education, and clean drinking water is limited, while humanitarian efforts are often hampered by poor infrastructure and ongoing insecurity. The local economy relies heavily on subsistence farming and livestock herding, but poor market access due to flooding and conflict restricts economic opportunities. The south sudan Joint Market Monitoring Initiative 1-7 August 2024 indicate that accessibility (25%) physical and social access to markets, availability (30%) ability of markets to consistently supply core commodities, affordability (15%), financial access to markets and price volatility, resilience (20%),vulnerability of supply chains and ease of restocking, infrastructure (10%), state of markets physical and financial infrastructure. The combination of inadequate sanitation, nutrition, and health risks poses serious challenges for the population, highlighting the need for humanitarian assistance in improving farming produce to families whose farms are damaged and those that experienced death of livestock because of flooding, support low milk production from livestock because of no grazing spaces, introduce resilience coping strategies that improve livelihood, improve access to market where trading is affected. UNIDOR propose to ensure immediate provision of multi-purpose cash assistance to the affected population, Food distribution to the IDPs and the most vulnerable returnees and IDPs, Provision of livelihood kits (FK, VK,) to the IDPs and most vulnerable host communities, increase cash-based services where feasible and support local markets and community empowerment, Capacity-building and creation of community infrastructure aimed at promoting sustainability, aligning with long-term development and peace building goals in collaboration with relevant stakeholders.

2. Project activities

UNIDOR tend to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) - Distribution of Unconditional cash transfer to the most vulnerable households - Distribution of inputs (fishing kits & quick maturing vegetable seeds), Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines), Provision of Emergency food & livelihood support, fishing kits and vegetable kits to help them meet their food needs from own production and improve livelihood condition, Conducting extension trainings for most vulnerable farmers on modern agricultural techniques, Livelihood support to returnee households through provision of multipurpose cash, Establish kitchen gardens near nutrition sites, Conducting awareness for most vulnerable populations both female and male on environment protection, gender involvement and equality, Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19,Training and supporting agricultural extension workers to provide sessions and technical support to agricultural beneficiaries, Establish and support VSLA groups for vulnerable households to improve savings, Establishment of Farmers Field Schools (FFSs),Training the beneficiaries in postharvest handling techniques - Establishment and training of project management and AAP committees made up of 60% women and 40% men. Sharing COVID-19 prevention measures messages
