Project Summary

According to FSNAU/fewsnet post gu reales 2021 in Middle Shabelle 47,960 people are expected to be in emergency (IPC class 4) and 182,220 in ipc 3 (crisis). This is caused by a combination of floods and drought. People are in need of safe food of the right nutritional quality and quantity. The project will use a wide variety of tools to improve household access to food, these include:
(1). Direct food transfers through Food vouchers which entails the provision of coupons to receive a fixed quantity of specified commodities to households in crisis - these will be targeting the most vulnerable drought affected population with major concerns regarding food
(2). Unconditional cash grants or provision of cash for the purpose of increasing access to food - also these are the most vulnerable people
(3) livelihoods assistance to protect and restore livelihoods related to food and income sources primarily through provision of agricultural inputs as well as through conditional cash transfer or cash for work for farmers targeting IPC 3 and 4 areas. The people selected are families with work able people.
The food vouchers will be designed to meet the total minimum nutritional requirements i.e. 2,100 kcals per person per day, 10% of total energy provided by protein, 17% of total energy provided by fat, and adequate micronutrient intake. It will include 50kg of Rice, 5kgs of sugar, 3.5kg of oil, and 6kgs of pulses per household per month. Agricultural input provision will help to reestablish a ‘self-help’ mode within communities affected by emergencies: once families have seed and farming tools, they can start the process of producing their own food and/or making money from selling crops, and thereby reducing their dependence on external sources for their livelihoods.
Cash for Work interventions will provide employment to unskilled and semi-skilled workers on labor-intensive projects to rehabilitate irrigation systems. it will have dual objectives of Providing income support to poor, vulnerable men and women through short term and unskilled labor so they can meet their essential needs and to rehabilitate vital community infrastructure.
For accountability purposes, WOCCA will Communicate project objectives, our expectations of workers, the payment amount, and process to the community prior to implementation proper. Activities will be gender-sensitive and will promote the participation of women without prejudice to their capacity to perform the work. The wage rate is set to meet the basic needs of the targeted population. The minimum consumption basket(s) - an estimated amount of cash, or the number of basic commodities expressed in cash equivalent, needed by a household to survive for a month (estimated at 2,100
kcal/person/day) - will be used. We will cross-refer information on prevailing wages from Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping Units such as FSNAU/FEWSNET and the cash working group. Disposition of the tools such as a shovel and wheelbarrow will be done at the end of the project preferably to beneficiary groups or to the local government unit with a well-documented handover process.
Given that payment is a key feature of CFW programs, various control instruments must be put in place including attendance sheets, payroll sheets, and identification details. We will run unconditional cash transfer schemes alongside CFW programs in order to extend assistance to people affected by a suddenonset disaster who are physically unable to work. (e.g., PLWHIV, the elderly, the disabled, vulnerable children, and families with high dependency ratios). Targeting will be carried out according to clear and transparent targeting criteria. It will involve the community and local authority and use the triangulation of information to minimize inclusion and exclusion errors. Systematic respect of the «do no harm» principle so as to make sure that a response to one crisis does not increase beneficiaries’ risk-exposure and vulnerability to other crises.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

It is expeccted that vouchers minimizes money carrying risk of WOCCA. Apart from that personnel cost will be reduced because a lot of the logistics is carried by flood suppliers whose business it is to do this on a regular bases and will therefore be more cost efficient.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Before the food vouchers and cash for work, a market analysis will be done ensuring food is available in the market. Suppliers will aslo be contacted and a maximum price will be agreed upon based on market prices and quotations or tender. If the market functions in the area more or less as normal, local suppliers will be directly contracted as indicated. In case that market functionality is not as normal and prices are structurally higher than usual, the project is at risk of affecting prices for people that are not beneficiaries. If this is the case, food may be sourced from other places, even from outside the country in order to ensure that local prices are not hiked by the project.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

WOCCA has a SEA policy in place and this policy will also be explained to the people there will also be a hotline shared with the people so they can direct complains to someone of WOCCA outside the project. To ensure that members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are includedin consultations and/or are not excluded from humanitarian services / assistance and that Women, girls and boys are included in consultations and decision-making processes, Wocca will employ staff representative of all groups in the community by gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group and disability. Gender balance of distribution teams and implementation committees will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community.During distributions, we will ensure that the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk putting in mind that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking rations home. Distributions will take place giving sufficient time to travel home before nightfall while also ensuring that the location, distance and routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable. Separate male and female queues will be designated with female staff present at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. special needs of pregnant and lactating women, under 5 year olds and older persons will be responded to and the quality of their assistance made equitable to that of other groups. ration cards to female-headed households, childheaded households and unaccompanied children will be issued in their own name. Livelihoods programmes will be developed in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities and promotes family unity. They will be developed in a manner that ensures that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities (including cash/food-for-work schemes), micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work. Clear information will be provided to the affected population explaining that they do not have to pay or provide favors to anyone in exchange forreceiving food assistance or being selected for livelihood projects. Deliberate efforts will be made to ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls. To ensure that delivery of humanitarian assistance does not affect peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources and Exposure of womenand girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in elation to access to humanitarian assistance does not occur Wocca will promote equitable and impartial access to all; we will Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities and make decisions. We will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure thedisplaced as well as the mobile populations are not excluded, lack of documentation will not necessarily exclude individuals from accessing services Wocca will employ staff who are representative of all clans and groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and ensure all staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training, they will be sensitized sign and adhere to a Codeof Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. This will ensure that our staff play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voices of marginalized groups are represented.
