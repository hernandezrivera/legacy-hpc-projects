Project Summary

This project will contribute to comprehensive child protection assistance to children in Rohingya refugee camps 2E, 2W, 6 and 7, building on DRC CP intervention in these camps during 2018, and focusing on strengthening community-based child protection networks/systems, mobile and static PSS services, holistic case management and awareness-raising and outreach activities, incorporating social cohesion and DRR elements, and reaching a total of 30,572 beneficiaries.

Needs

The Education and Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) Joint Needs Assessment (JNA 2017) highlighted significant child protection risks in refugee camps. The top risk for girls was identified by camp residents in Bangladesh as child marriage. Risk of trafficking, sexual harassment and physical and sexual violence also scored high, most significantly for girls. Regarding boys, sexual harassment and violence were to a lesser degree highlighted as risks by the respondents, however there is clear perception amongst the respondents, that more boys than girls are being subjected to trafficking practices and forced labour. Most of the respondents also stated that children in the camps are living under high psychological distress. Children are reported to have witnessed horrific violence, including their loved ones getting killed or burned alive. Moreover, there is a significant number of children who lost their parents, family separation is particular risk in the overcrowded camps, which is exacerbated during monsoon season and high likelihood of natural disasters. These factors can leave children more prone to violence and abuse and affect their mental and physical growth in process. DRC CPiE programming in refugee camps during 2018 highlighted that the risks for children identified in the JNA continue to be present and some are increasing. Domestic violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect of boys and girls, sexual abuse and exploitation of girls, psychosocial distress, and risks in the physical environment are among the prevailing CP concerns identified through DRC CP interventions. Furthermore, lack of services for disabled (physically and mentally) children and adolescent boys and girls further exacerbate already existing child protection risks and places them in further harm. It is widely documented that children with a disability (physical or mental) are more vulnerable to harassment, bullying, humiliation, physical, sexual abuse, whereby limited access to services only marginalizes this vulnerable group further. Additionally, adolescents remain the most underserved group in the refugee camp, creating a strong push factor for families caring for adolescents to resort to negative coping mechanisms, encouraging child mariage, trafficking and forced child labor. Despite the needs, the ISCG estimates that only about 50% of the needs for survivor-centred case management and psychosocial support services are being met. Moreover, most of the facility-based services are not accessible to vulnerable people due to lack of mobility due to either socio-cultural constraints or physical ability. This includes adolescent girls who report being more socially isolated than they were in Myanmar. Direct service provision is required to address immediate needs, while embedding them in community mechanisms will enable access and a transition to more sustainable approaches.

Activities or outputs

The proposed project will support CBP activities in the camps adjacent to the host community (HC) and support joint social cohesion initiatives between the two communities. AGD approach will be used for targeting (targets calculated based on this year experience and available data). HC intervention will seek to engage with the relevant ministries and government service providers (e.g. DSS/MoWCA) to support exchanges on good practices, joint consultations and capacity-building at the field-level.1. CBP (HC, camp): Creation of community groups (with M and F representation, incl. different age groups) to strengthen understanding and referrals of protection concerns, identification and capacity-building of community outreach volunteers and supporting groups with specific (women and girls, persons with disabilities, elderly) in strengthening their access to services2. Community-driven safety & protection plans to foster prevention and social cohesion, facilitate CGs to identify protection risks/threats, and support implementation of community-designed priority actions with technical, material, or financial support from DRC. This activity will be supported through technical guidance from DRC’s environmental advisor to assist communities in identifying and responding to environmental risks. 3. Community centres in 4 HC locations, which will serve as a multi-functional site for community-based awareness-raising, outreach, and protection groups, as well as a protection desk providing individual counselling and CM. DRC will coordinate this activity with actors on the ground in the camps and HC to avoid any duplication. In the targeted camps, CPB will complement other DRC protection activities implemented through other grants.4. Protection monitoring (in HC) to identify trends in vulnerability in HC locations and inform programming. Approx. 200 monitoring visits planned for the year (target 2400 M/2400 F from HC). Note: PM in camps is fully covered under UNHCR funding and not included in this JRP submission by DRC. 5. Case management (CM) for the most vulnerable persons in HC locations, through counselling, referrals or individual assistance, and provision of PSS (AGD disaggregation). CM target estimated at 450 based on this year’s work in the targeted HCs and CM in camps. Protection referral pathways will be rolled out jointly with the Protection Sector, the protection camp focal points, inclusively of all the protection actors on the ground.6. Awareness-raising and outreach to the community (HC/camp; women/men of different age groups) to improve understanding of protection, rights, and vulnerabilities, and support community-driven protection responses. This activity will be implemented through CBP groups, IEC/outreach and is estimated to reach roughly 30% of HC and 10% of camp population. 7. Capacity-building of government counterparts and humanitarian actors to support protection mainstreaming across the responseCOMPLAINTS AND FEEDBACK RESPONSE MECHANISM (rolled out in camps by DRC SMS with a link to Protection) will also be available in HC through CCs; consultation and feedback mechanisms are part of the methodology of all DRC protection activities. DNH principle is at the core of all DRC Protection approaches, is reflected in activity SOPs and tools. Social cohesion work will be based on careful analysis of communities’ dynamics and community-led process to identify opportunities for joint initiatives which would contribute to improved relationships. All DRC staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct with specific PSEA provisions, and CoC trainings are provided to all joining staff and volunteers; DRC has a robust CoC response mechanism with links to CFRM and protection referral. DRC is a member of PWG (and CPSS, GBVSS, PSEA Network) and will coordinate within these fora to ensure that protection activities are complementary and holistic.

Key project/performance indicators

1) 800 children (400 girls, 400 boys), including adolescents, identified in need who are receiving structured PSS services2) 900 children at risk (450 girls, 450 boys) identified who received individual case management services to meet his/her unique needs3) 288 adolescents (115 girls, 173 boys) receiving services, including life skills, and VT, self-reliance activities4) 1000 community awareness sessions conducted on family separation and prevention, child marriage, child labor, MHPSS 5) 200 refugees (100 women, 100 men) with improved knowlege and skills on child protection response and prevention
