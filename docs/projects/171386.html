Project Summary

Access to food in Gaza remains a challenge for a majority of refugees struggling with deteriorating socio-economic condition compounded with the COVID-19 emergency. Soaring rates of unemployment and limited income are key drivers to poverty and food insecurity among the population. Through the Cash for Work intervention, UNRWA aims to support Palestine refugees in Gaza coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees. These will be equal to more than 1,430,000 work days, and will inject over US$ 17.6 million into households, generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short term job opportunities will include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions. Amidst COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA developed a Standard Operation Procedures to ensure that beneficiaries under cash for work programming have access to enhanced occupational health and safety norms for the work they agree to undertake. It identifies the functions and manner in which the Cash for Work modality can be deployed for UNRWA activities in a pandemic environment safely and in a dignified manner in order to continue providing income generation opportunities to refugees to achieve livelihood objectives.

1 - Needs

The on-going blockade and intermittent hostilities as well as the intra-Palestinian conflict compounded with COVID-19 emergency are continuously deteriorating all aspects of life in the Gaza Strip. While the limited economic access to food is the main cause of food insecurity driven by unemployment and poverty, both indicators are in the rise due to the overall slowdown of the economy following the restrictions imposed to contain the COVID-19 pandemic (HNO 2021). According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment in Gaza reached 49.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2020. Palestine refugees are facing even higher unemployment rate of 49.9 per cent compared to 47.7 per cent for non-refugees. PCBS also identified 17 per cent decrease in the number of employed persons in the local market between the first and second quarter of 2020 in Gaza confirming the impact of COVID-19. Furthermore, the current situation has a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups, including women and youth. According to the Gender Alert by UNWOMEN (September 2020), women’s participation in the labor market in Gaza is dramatically lower than among men standing at less than 15 per cent in the second quarter of 2020, of whom, 78 per cent, are unemployed (HNO 2021). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped more than half of them into food insecurity. According to the findings of the Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SEFSec) carried out in 2018, which is the latest SEFSec data as of October 2020, about 68 per cent of households in Gaza experience severe or moderate levels of food insecurity. This is despite the fact that 69 per cent of households in Gaza reported that they receive some form of food assistance or social transfers from Palestinian governmental bodies or international organizations. People cope with food insecurity mainly through borrowing food and/or money. The situation has gotten even worse in the past two years as reflected in HNO 2021. UNRWA has been experiencing the influx of people desperately seeking for jobs, even for a temporary employment such as Cash for Work opportunities. Currently, an average waiting time for a family to receive such opportunity is more than four years.

2 - Activities

Through its Job Creation Programme (JCP), UNRWA will support Palestine refugees coping with protracted threats and shocks and mitigate food insecurity through offering short-term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees, meaning that would directly and indirectly benefit around 77,000 people considering the average family size of 5.5 (Food Cluster, 2020). It equals to more than 1,430,000 work days generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents, and injects over US$ 17.6 million into local economy. The short-term job opportunities would include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions, which would fluctuate depending on the situation of the COVID-19 pandemic in Gaza. Priority will be given to applicants from households who have assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$ 3.87 per person per day. As a part of the Agency-wide Gender Mainstreaming Strategy, UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. The salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneﬁciaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of ﬂuctuations in the exchange rate. The JCP ﬁeld monitoring team will ensure appropriate placement of refugees, and will monitor work attendance and performance. In order to continue to provide income generation opportunities to refugees to achieve livelihood objectives amidst COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA developed SOPs identifying the functions and manners in which the Cash for Work opportunities can be deployed in a pandemic environment safely and in a dignified manner. In order to minimize risks during the work placement, the beneficiaries will receive the same work protections (PPE) and training afforded to staff, and beneficiaries with pre-existing medical conditions are not exposed to “front line” functions. Furthermore, beneficiaries are afforded the opportunity to complete their employment opportunities assigned in the event they must quarantine or isolated or due to other factors.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations with a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestine refugees' rights. An integrated humanitarian response, with the inclusion of Cash for Work, is directly related to a growing focus on resilience to withstand and recover from crisis. The cash support reduces current risks by providing relief to strained families and enhancing refugees’ sense of dignity and self-reliance. Gauging the impact of such very-short temporary assistance is impossible; especially in a volatile and chronic-emergency Gaza context; however, without such cash assistance, the vulnerability of poor families will be worsened and hence this entire project will contribute – among other factors and interventions- towards “resilience.”The salary provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneﬁciaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of ﬂuctuations in the exchange rate. All staff are required to take mandatory courses on prevention of SEA, and all beneficiaries and staff can report SEA concerns directly to the investigation office. The job placement is taking consideration of the age and gender of the beneficiaries to mitigate possible risks associated with the Cash for Work opportunities. Amidst COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA developed specific SOPs to ensure the health and safety of Cash for Work beneficiaries. In order to minimize risks during the work placement, the beneficiaries will receive the same work protections (PPE) and training afforded to staff, and beneficiaries with pre-existing medical conditions are not exposed to “front line” functions. Furthermore, beneficiaries are afforded the opportunity to complete their employment opportunities assigned in the event they must quarantine or isolated or due to other factors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all Palestine refugees who are seeking for assistance. However, in terms of the Cash for Work opportunities, the priority will be given to applicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$3.87 per person per day, considered as food insecure. Targeting criteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). Offering CfW opportunities to unskilled women in Gaza remains one of the main challenges for this intervention, mainly due to cultural constraints. To overcome these challenges, some of the available CfW opportunities specifically targeted women. Moreover, new job opportunities were created to help absorb more vulnerable unskilled women in other working fields, such as school attendants, guards, cleaners, and food packers at UNRWA distribution centres. According to PCBS report (Labor Force Survey, Q2-2020), while the unemployment rate of women in Gaza remains high (66.8 per cent), the unemployment rate of refugee women (64.7 per cent) is lower than non-refugee women (72.7 per cent). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. With the limited prospects of employment opportunities in Gaza, the Agency through its Job Creation Programme, managing the Cash for Work, addresses these economic hardships and ensures that families are self-reliant. The provision of CfW opportunities are expected to allow families to cover some of their basic expenses, to improve living conditions and contribute to their copying mechanisms and resilience.
