Project Summary

Ensuring the sustainability and expansion of essential, high-quality healthcare services across community, primary, and secondary care levels is paramount for meeting the health needs of the affected population, particularly among shifting lines of control that may disrupt healthcare services and impact humanitarian access.
Areas facing limited accessibility, underserved regions, and those recently subjected to changes in control demand a revitalization of the healthcare system. This is especially crucial in areas like Der-Ez-Zor, characterized by its remoteness, lack of active partners, and the presence of displaced populations, which further strain the already fragile healthcare infrastructure.
The project will prioritize the provision of primary healthcare services through the operation of two primary healthcare centres. These centres will offer medical consultations, diagnostics, and treatments across various specialities, including first-aid, general medicine, paediatrics, and obstetrics and gynaecology (OBGYN) clinics, in addition, to providing basic diagnostic services by running a basic laboratory and providing needed medication, through a dedicated pharmacy. The healthcare centres will implement IMCI, and MISP programs, and will have NCD and CD management.
Moreover, each centre will deploy a mobile clinic to reach communities in the nearby communities, providing essential healthcare services and referring complex cases to the centres or nearby hospitals as necessary.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to the 2024 HNO, 14.9 million people in Syria require life-saving primary and secondary health assistance in 2024, approximately 2.2 million reside in NES, confronting weakened and disrupted health systems exacerbated by ongoing public health crises. Recurrent disease outbreaks, waterborne illnesses, a prolonged drought, vaccine-preventable diseases, and food insecurity have collectively contributed to a surge in mortality and morbidity rates. Meanwhile, high inflation, currency depreciation, and surging commodity prices have further exacerbated humanitarian needs, leading to increased poverty and dependence on aid. The 2024 SMART survey, conducted by NES partners, revealed significant health challenges across Governorates in North East Syria, for instance, measles immunization coverage fell below the recommended ≥80% in all areas surveyed, with Al-Hasakeh achieving the highest coverage at 76.1% and Ar-Raqqa the lowest at 52.7%. Similarly, vitamin A coverage was inadequate across all Governorates, with notable disparities; Al-Hasakeh reported the highest coverage at 61.6%, while Deir-ez-Zor had the lowest at 12.7%. IPV aims to continue running two Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCCs) in Abu Kamal districts: Al-Salam PHC in Alkeshkia community, Hajin Sub-district, and Alshafa PHC in Shafa community, Susat Sub-district. Each PHC will be supported by a Mobile Medical Unit (MMU) for community coverage after careful mapping with health partners. Operating 8 hours daily, PHCCs will offer essential departments it will include pediatric, general medicine clinics, pharmacy that will provide NCD medication. In addition to a 24/7 first-aid and gynecology clinic that will provide normal delivery in addition to consultation, ANC, and family planning. Each PHC will provide MHPSS and nutrition services "detailed in nutrition proposal", through CHW, and GP trained on MHgap, Each PHC will be linked with one ambulance to refer needed cases and deliveries. The MMU will provide health promotion, nutrition services, medical consultations, ANC through a midwife, and referral. Protection Risk Analysis: 1)The risk from SEA. Mitigation: -Community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance, awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel, Provide information to communities about the NGO principles to which NGO adheres to. 2. GBV risk. Mitigation: health outreach services are the first to contact GBV survivors seeking assistance, and survivals' safe access to facilities. Train the staff on PSS and support the presence of female staff in all teams. 3)risk of violence against Healthcare workers. Mitigation: Set up centers in safe and accessible locations, Relocate centers in relatively safe areas, Reporting on attacks against healthcare. Addressing PSEA: Through PSEA training, Code of Conduct, reporting channels, community awareness, and ensures staff training and commitment to safeguarding women and children.
