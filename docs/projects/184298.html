Project Summary

The project overall objects is to address the humanitarian challenges of a classified affected hard to reach populations in El-Afwein of Sanaag Region. Sanaag have the highest infant mortality rate, 51 deaths per one thousand live births and U5 mortality rate is 76 per thousand live births. The situation is worse in El-Afwein, Erigavo and Las’anod because of a fewer health facilities, recurrent climate-related shocks and political instability, risks to crop production and food access are key drivers of food insecurity and malnutrition. Lower than expected and delayed Deyr and Gu’ rainfall and desert locust swamps severed crop production and pasture worsened by significant increase of food prices and 10-20 percent decline of remittance all contributed to food insecurity of local people. A recent assessment carried out by HEAL, shows that most rural health facilities are not with enough staff, supplies and equipment.
Malnutrition is widespread driven by the above multiple factors. GAM prevalence is Alert in El-Afwein and Erigavo (9.9% and 8.6% respectively). Approximately 10,000 U5 children and 3,600 PLW are likely to be moderately malnourished, including 1,400 to be severely malnourished.
To avoid deterioration, this proposal calls for durable solutions to prevent and treat malnutrition, including focus on gender inequality, addressing of poor hygiene and anti-discriminatory measures, immunisation coverage, accessibility and accountability of services to direct beneficiaries.
This will be achieved through recruiting and training Emergency nutrition teams on standard Somalia IMAM and IYCF tools with the purpose of early detection and treatment of under 5 children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition. Thereafter, the project will help and give integrated services including screening, identifying, and treating and referring children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition. The pregnant/lactating women will receive multiple micro-nutrient supplementation, while benefiting Infant Young Child Feeding (IYCF) counselling services. Breastfeeding promotion is provided throughout the course of the project. Children and mothers by referring to the nearest Health facility. In protection mainstreaming, HEAL fully involves IDPs, including from marginalised communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects.
1. The project will support 4 mobile teams in El-Afwein District with linkage to existing health facilities of El-Afwein MCH, Garadag MCH, Huluul MCH. 2. The identification, referral and treatment of 5,605(6-59 months) children suffering from acute malnutrition through OTP/TSFP and community based approaches. 3. The project will strengthen local technical capacity for integration of Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) into the Primary Health Care system through utilisation of Basic Nutrition Services Package (BNSP). 3. Strengthening supply chain management, well-trained health workers, financing, and use of existing community based nutrition programs in order to scale-up prevention, early identification and treatment of acute malnutrition of children and Pregnant and Lactating (PLW). 4. Service delivered will be Maternal and child nutrition components including IMAM, IYCF, NHHP, referral system, Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) supplies, mitigation of COVID-19, nutrition surveillance and as well as mobilising, educating and creating demand for service uptake. 5. Preventive measures are, de-worming, counselling and promotion on infant and young child feeding including Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) in emergencies. Community mobilisation, screening and referral, micro-nutrient supplementation for PLW (multiple micro-nutrients and Iron Folate), Expanded Program on Immunisation (EPI) and U5 (Vitamin A supplements) for children reinforced with Communication for development (C4D). Also Personal protection of staff using PPE. 6. Prevention services will include COVID-19.
