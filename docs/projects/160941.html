Project Summary

The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non Food items to the most vulnerable persons newly displaced/host communities and returnees. The project will focus on the life saving shelter/NFIs needs as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks targeting all states of South Sudan through mobile response as per need as well as using its static teams based in kajo Kiji, mayom, Leer Koch, Wau, Aweil, Bor, Akobo, twic and Duk. However major focus areas will be Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahrul Ghazal, Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria states. Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible to the community. When blanket targeting is not applicable, the action will target mainly lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, and households with members who have certain health complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no community linkages will also be targeted. The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2020 HRP's. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 14 days for full-edged delivery of NFIs and emergency shelters and other activities per location. The project will also support returnees in settlement as a part of their local integration or returns to their areas of origin. The project will benefit directly 60,400 individuals.

1 - Needs

UN OCHA South Sudan new IDPs baseline report shows the number of internally displaced persons in South Sudan for 2020 to be used as 1.8 million while UN OCHA and the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) are still in the process of finalizing the exact number of IDPs. While the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview reported that the number of internaly displacced people was 2 million. So for 2020 it shows a reduced number which makes some sense. S/NFI cluster need analysis shows that an estimated 2,442,716 individuals require S/NFIs assistance in 2020. This figure includes the internally displaced person (IDPs), returnees and the Host Communities (HC). The cluster differentiates the context into three different contexts; inside of formal and informal sites, including PoCs, collective sites and other spontaneous sites; outside of formal and informal sites; and relatively stable locations where people are returning to their former habitual residence. Inside PoCs and collective sites where the displaced populations is sheltered for safety and security, the cluster targets 100 per cent of the population. Outside the PoCs and Collective Sites, the cluster assumes 70 per cent of the displaced population are in need of S/NFIs assistance. Outside of displacement sites, the targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and often times unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other resources. In cases of returnees, where the areas of returns are stable, the returnees assumed to stay, rebuild their lives and able to cope with the existing/available resources. NRC will look into alternative support modalities, such as cash vouchers, training, and other engagement with communities People’s S/NFIs needs are primarily linked to conflict induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. Households in Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites and collective sites do not have access to naturally-grown shelter materials, including for the replacement of temporary shelters. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall Shelter and NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. Any new outbreaks of violence are expected to discourage or prevent people from moving out of camp-like settings in large numbers. The 2019 HRP estimated that 7.1 Million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Most of the affected population especially those in hard to reach areas have limited access to basic humanitarian assistance. The limited assistance provided initially was intended to sustain the IDPs for a short period of time, with the anticipation that the conflicts would cease and the IDPs return to their villages of origin. However, the renewed conflicts in various states points to a protracted conflict situation which will continue to displace populations. Additionally, the rainy season has strained humanitarian efforts as well as exposing the IDPs to greater vulnerability as most of the land in South Sudan is prone to flooding, leading to further displacement and also hindering accessibility to some areas. The climatic extremes experienced both the rainy and dry season create high need for NFIs to strengthen the populations copying mechanism. The emergency shelter kits provided are not adequate enough and will soon need to be replaced / changed. Most adults still sleep in the open, and occasionally congesting in one shelter to cover against the night rains, while the other available shelters are used to accommodate children, the sick and the old during the night.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Mitigation Measured for Risk 8.1Mobilization of the community leaders on how to include the minority and marginalized groups during the need assessment and also during the response so that their views are captured and their needs are identified and they are aware on what support they will receive. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.2:This risk will be mitigated through organizing separate focus group discussion with women and girls. in the meetings consultation will be made with them on the needs as well as the proposed response. Their views will be recorded and their concerns will be noted and a proper gender friendly response will be organized. Mitigation for Risk 8.4:To arrange community awareness sessions focusing of GBV, Child protection and other protection issues guiding the community on what to do in a particular case. Also map out the service providers and let the community know where to refer in case of any protection case. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.7:In order to avoid this risk, selection of the registration and distribution site will be carefully done, so that people feel safe to reach there. For registration efforts will be made that the project team visit the beneficiaries at their homes in case there is security risk for them to gather at an insecure place. Mitigation Measures of Risk 8.8:Sometimes the local authorities pressurizing the partners to pay extra illegal tax. For this case the issue will be referred to the country director and then share with NGO forum to advocate with the Government relevant department/RRC to avoid such things. Mitigation Measure for Risk 8.10:NRC staff has a strict code of conduct on beneficiaries for exploitation and sexual abuse. Action will be taken immediately by the management if such case is reported and confirmed. Mitigation Measure for Risk 8.15:Complaint and feedback desk is arranged at the distribution/registration site where complaints and received at the spot and also responded.
