Project Summary

This project targets out-of-school children (OOSC) in conflict-affected communities by providing accessible, quality non-formal education and facilitating the successful transfer of NFE students to formal schools, which will be rehabilitated. The project tackles barriers to gender and inclusion, and provides an integrated response, offering psychosocial support for students, caregivers and teachers, and referral to protection and livelihoods projects under the same organisational response for vulnerable students and families. It thereby contributes to the Education Sector’s HRP objectives of increasing education enrolment, inclusion, equity and attainment.
The sub-districts to be prioritised for targeting will follow the severity of 4, except Ar-Raqqa, Ath-Thawrah and Mansura, with 3 (2023 HNO). Locations are chosen based on the number of OOSC determined in collaboration with the local Education Committees (ECs) and the Education Working Group on the severity of need, and the NGO's own door-to-door assessments. 15% of people targeted in this project are estimated to be persons with disabilities.
The project will provide non-formal education to build the knowledge, confidence and skills (literacy, numeracy and social and emotional learning) required to (re)integrate successfully into formal education. Homework support clubs in formal schools will support students’ transition to primary or secondary school, and promote the retention of students attending these schools but at risk of dropping out due to learning difficulties or being over-age. Where formal education is not appropriate, older adolescent NFE graduates will be supported to join vocational training in the organisation's Livelihoods project or other locally available vocational opportunities. The NFE class-based activities will be monitored through structured formative and summative assessments including baseline and endline assessments per cycle using EGRA/EGMA and SERAIS tools to review impact and identify areas for improvement in activities and instruction.
Additionally, due to gaps in school infrastructure and the safety risks posed by damaged, non-climate-proofed buildings and limited access to sanitation facilities and clean water, the project will provide public school rehabilitation (windows, doors, holes, fences). Existing WASH facilities will be fixed and additional facilities will be added adhering to SPHERE standards on the number of latrines as well as gender segregated latrines, latrines for teachers and at least one accessible latrine per school.
In coordination with the WG, the project will also promote quality and education by providing basic materials for teachers to support differentiated teaching and learning including basic school furniture, hygiene supplies and some recreational and learning material. The project will contribute to protective educational services by providing Teachers in Crisis Contexts training to teachers and other education personnel to foster a safe and positive learning environment. It will also offer caregivers the opportunity to increase engagement in children’s academic and socio-emotional learning by attending positive parenting sessions.
In Tabqa, Ar-Raqqa, this project will support a local partner to deliver NFE to children with disabilities focusing on children with hearing, speaking and visual impairments and delivered by specialised staff in a dedicated centre. We provide funding, teaching and learning material (including PSS manuals) and capacity building, and will facilitate the wider application of the learning gained from the centre by working the EC, and communities to make a nearby pilot school more inclusive.
These activities are designed to be flexible, allowing adaptation to the rapidly evolving needs in NES and ability to respond to a wide range of scenarios. This also allows the project to provide a more tailored response in each district in accordance with different needs and authorities’ priorities.

3 - Needs

The cumulative effect of conflict, displacement, severe economic downturn, drought, Covid-19, Cholera and other factors continues to negatively impact an overstretched education system. Access remains severely infringed across NES. The number of OOSC remains high, particularly in Ar-Raqqa (22%), Deir-Ez-Zor (19%) and Aleppo (16%) (HNO, 2023). The primary causes are economic, lack of facilities for children with disabilities, and children not wanting to go to school because of poor quality, duties at home, or psychosocial reasons (HNO, 2023).In 2021, the NGO assessed over 2,700 students in Deir ez-Zor and Raqqa. It found less than 10% of students attended formal education with many out of school for over 3 years. Nearly 60% were not attending due to lack of available schools, and 20% due to school quality and safety. Nationally more than half of students complain about the lack of WASH facilities (2023 HNO).Poor teaching practices result in low learning outcomes; corporal punishment negatively impacts children’s wellbeing. The perceived poor quality of education, along with its high opportunity costs, contribute to high dropout; 44% of households perceive the need to enhance teacher capacity as key to improving education services (2023 HNO).The Self Administration lacks the resources/capacity to provide support to OOSC or for school retention (less than 2% of children have access to NFE services, 2023 HNO) while education remains one of the most underfunded sectors in NES.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Literacy, numeracy and SEL instruction to current or former out-of-school children monitored by EGRA/EGMA/SERAIS.1) Accelerated education for OOSC aged 8-14.2) Homework support for former OOSC and children at risk of drop-out in formal school.3) Catch-up programme, reinforced protection, and support for school reintegration or transition to vocational training for OOSC aged 14-17.4) Cash for education to support adolescents to obtain school certificates to continue formal education (currently at pilot stage).Activity 2: Caregiver Sessions to promote children’s wellbeing through stress management, positive discipline, psychosocial activities and awareness about referral mechanisms to formal education.Activity 3: Capacity building for EiE local partners/NGOs etc on TiCC and SEL and support for collaboration among the NES EWG partners.Activity 4: Repairs to school classrooms and infrastructure, including to WASH facilities, retrofitting, winterization and accessibility to ensure safe, accessible and conducive learning environments according to INEE and SPHERE Minimum Standards.Activity 5: Support to formal schools through provision of teaching, learning and safety materials and COVID-19 and Cholera prevention materials, and awareness training.Activity 6: Provision of quality and safe education support to formal schools through TiCC to teaching staff focused on teacher/student wellbeing and child protection/PSS.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Many students continue to learn in unconducive/unsafe learning environments. Many schools in NES are in dire need of intervention, since they were not rehabilitated after the conflict and remain a substantial risk to children’s safety and health.Unsafe learning facilities – which are structurally unsafe, lacking protection from the elements, with no or insufficient WASH facilities (and heightened COVID/Cholera risk – contribute to low enrolment and high dropout. The lack of windows and heating in classrooms exposes children to freezing temperatures in winter. These risks will be mitigated through rehabilitation and improvement of learning facilities so they are safe and conducive for learning.Child safeguarding measures are to be put in place in all activities.Corporal punishment and cognitively undemanding pedagogies negatively impact children’s wellbeing and learning. To counter this, TiCC training will be provided to teachers on positive discipline methods; prevention and reporting of violence; sensitisation to code of conduct; basic self-care and PSS.The project will ensure that NFE staff, facilitators and contractors sign and abide by the child safeguarding policy. The NGO will ensure all classes are free from any kind of risk including physical, sexual and psychosocial risks.The project has a Community Response Mechanism; participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about programmes, staff and systems via WhatsApp, email, telephone and complaints boxes.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. These include PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices who are trained with support from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes PSEA network-developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in activities.This project is the Education component of a multi-sectoral project that includes Protection separately. The Protection component has been submitted separately.Baseline and endline assessments will be administered using EGRA, EGMA and SERAIS tools for all NFE students. MEAL will continuously monitor attendance as well as conduct FGDs with students, caregivers and facilitators. All teachers trained will undertake pre- and post-tests to assess how their knowledge has changed, and lesson observations through teacher learning circles established by the project will assess classroom implementation of training. A pre- and post- questionnaire will also be developed to assess changes in attitudes and practices of caregivers attending the caregivers sessions. All data are disaggregated by gender, age (8-13, 14-17), residence (IDP, returnee and resident), disability, and location.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RisksEscalation of conflict, or deterioration in security, as a result of:-escalation in the conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation;-IS resurgence in Deir ez Zor/extension of attacks to surrounding areas;-tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict, jeopardising access to project areas.Markets affected by currency devaluation, border closures or conflict escalation, leading to supply shortages and inflation.Drought increasing hunger and negative coping strategies, including child labour, early marriage and recruitment by armed groups.-COVID-19 and/or Cholera resurges; new lockdowns restrict access.AssumptionsThe project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their needs and implement the programme.There will be no significant shifts in control over key locations.Markets continue to function; internal and regional borders remain open; goods are available locally.Mitigating measuresStrengthen operational resilience. In increased insecurity, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. Flexibly shift modalities between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of goods. Full engagement in continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area. COVID-19/Cholera SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
