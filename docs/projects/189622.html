Project Summary

Despite gaining independence ten years ago, and three years after signing the revitalized peace agreement, South Sudan continues to face deteriorating numerous challenges in achieving sustainable peace, social, political, and economic stability as a result of recurring endemic violence, and multiple shocks including extraordinary flooding, localized drought, exacerbated by direct and indirect effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.
Conflicts, intercommunal violence and flooding have forced up to 1.64m persons to relocate and live in displacement sites across the country as of 2022 with 1.4 million requiring urgent CCCM support. The wide spread violence and limited protection services across the country,, and cycle of displacements faced the returnees, have created fear among the displaced populations to return to their home of origin for settlement. Such traumatized population continue to live in dire humanitarian situation that require meaningful community engagement and sustainable strong community driven camp leadership.
The displaced population in Lianya, Budi, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Ikotos live in destitution without much support from partners to meet the most needed live saving interventions such as food, NFI, shelter, health/nutrition, protection. Cases of Sexual Gender based violence (SGBV) have been reported among the displaced community. HLSS shall coordinate response to induvial SGBV Case management including provision of i (CMR) with cluster partners while ensuring robust humanitarian partners’ coordination and management mechanisms.
Highly trained and experienced CCCM roving and mobile teams shall be deployed in areas with onset emergencies, to rapidly roll out need based key CCCM activities such as registration, service mapping, sensitization and mobilization of the displaced community. Identified community needs shall be prioritized at all the 06 IDP sites(Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Ikotos, Budi & Lianya) to guide rapid roll out of key activities aimed at improving the living conditions by facilitating safe, equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian services.
The displaced population shall be mobilized to actively participate in the inclusive community driven camp governance structures with equal participation of women, men, boys and girls, elderly, and persons with specific needs in decision-making processes. The community shall also be mobilized to engage in cash for work on essential sites maintenance activities, hygiene promotion and coordination of shelter improvements to impart knowledge and skills as part of long term strategy to attain self-reliance, while promoting peaceful coexistence.
Humanitarian service delivery shall be coordinated and monitored to avoid duplication of activities with continuous advocacy to ensure an equitable and needs-based response across the 6 sites.
Strong feedback mechanisms, such as setting up complaints desks, conducting satisfaction surveys among the displaced community to assess their levels of satisfaction and experiences to inform partners on the need to readjust humanitarian service delivery.
The CCCM site management teas shall conduct household verification and bio-metric registration exercises and set up camp data base clearly disaggregated into male, female, boys, and girl, elderly which shall be shared with stakeholders on Monthly basis.
Capacity building training shall be provided to partners, site management committees and leadership including government counterparts, the RRC, to improve overall understanding as well as capacity to carry out camp coordination activities and skill on identification of services gaps and coordination with humanitarian partners.
HLSS shall carefully plan and implement phase out and exit strategy with involvement of the sites governance structures and leadership, to ensure smooth exit at the end of the project.

1 - Needs

The Republic of South Sudan marked its tenth anniversary as an independent state on 9 July 2021. However, ten years after independence and three years after signing of the revitalized peace agreement, the South Sudanese population continue to face deteriorating numerous challenges in achieving sustainable peace, social, political, and economic stability as a result of recurring endemic violence, and multiple shocks including extraordinary flooding, localized drought, exacerbated by direct and indirect effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. Conflicts and flooding have forced hundreds of thousands of people to relocate and remain in displacement sites across the country. CCCM cluster report of January 2022, indicates a total 1.64m internally displaced person with 1.4 million requiring urgent CCCM support in 2022 compared to 1.3m in 2021. The recent eruption of armed clashes between the cattle herders and the indigenous community in Magwi County led to massive population displacement of 17,560 individuals (IRNA report, 1st March 2022, RRC briefing) and self-settled in a camp like settings at Magwi Payam headquarters. They survive without much support from the agencies to meet the most needed lifesaving items such as food, NFI, health/nutrition, protection, WASH and general humanitarian coordination and services monitoring services. These identified Humanitarian gaps require site infrastructural improvements, with robust advocacy and Humanitarian partners coordination, to streamline response. The current political situation and the lack of political will to implement the signed revitalized peace agreement, localized conflict between non-signatories, and recurring endemic Intercommunal violence resulting from Cattle rustling, territorial inversion of farmlands by armed cattle herders, extra judicial killings coupled with limited protection services have created fear among the displaced populations to return to their home of origin for settlement. Women and girls continue to face threats to their safety and security arising from early marriage and sexual exploitation/Gender Based Violence (GBV) as they lose livelihoods and access to education, which is not prioritized by their families. Majority of vulnerable groups(Women, elderly, orphans, persons living with disabilities) living in the camp like settings are often isolated and left out of decision-making circles and assistance. Such circumstances require guided formation and re-organization of representative camp committees, with equitable representation of vulnerable groups, with effective participatory camp coordination and management mechanisms. This requires meaningful community engagement, partners coordination and robust care and maintenance activities, to empower the community and lead to community self-management and long-term resilience. The displaced population in Bor South, Twic East , Magwi, Kapoeta, ikotos and Lianya are still without much support from the agencies to meet the most needed live saving interventions such as food, NFI, shelter, health/nutrition, protection and general humanitarian coordination and services monitoring. In addition, due to inter-communal violence in Jonglei, Cases of Sexual Gender base violence (SGBV)and child abductions, are being reported. This requires meaningful engagement and sensitization campaigns of the local authorities with robust sensitization among the community. At the same time, relative security improvements in some areas have prompted spontaneous return of approximated 387,000(UNHCR October 2021) refugee population from the neighboring Countries. The government, with humanitarian support facilitated returns to certain areas. However, the returnees populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. However, considerable number of them have lost access to their properties(land, houses) due to illegal occupation and grabbing. This requires continuous meaningful engagements.

2 - Project activities

Health Link South Sudan intends to respond to these challenges by rolling out key CCCM activities at 06 IDP sites(Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Ikotos, Budi, & Lianya).These activities are tailored to the specific needs of the IDPs and will seek to improve their living conditions by facilitating safe, equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian services, including CCCM.This project shall focus on mobilization of the IDPs to actively participate in the community driven camp governance structures with particular emphasis on rational inclusion of women, men, boys and girls, elderly, and persons with specific needs into decision-making processes.These site governance committees shall identify community needs and priorities to be addressed. HLSS shall coordinate provision of induvial Case management with cluster partners and coordinate site level satisfaction survey.A team of highly trained CCM technical roving and mobile teams, shall be deployed to areas with ongoing emergencies and areas with onset emergencies, to facilitate rapid roll out of the CCCM activities.HLSS shall set up coordination mechanisms in the identified IDP sites and camp-like settings to facilitate coordination of humanitarian response, monitor service provision at site level and advocate on needs; and work with service providers to ensure an equitable and needs-based response.HLSS shall introduce and engage IDP community(men, women, youth and PSNs) into cash for work activities at all the 6 IDP sites. The activities shall focus on essential sites maintenance activities, hygiene promotion and coordination of shelter improvements such as support and renovation of community centers, PSNs shelters and pit latrines, bathing shelter, cleaning of water points, drainages, and road to support minimum living standards and protection.HLSS shall conduct service awareness sensitizations to orient the IDPs on access and existing services such as distribution points and distribution mechanism, complaint desks, GBV/SEA reporting mechanism, referral pathways, Health facilities, etc taking into consideration groups with specific needs. HLSS shall set up complaint registration desks, sensitize the IDP community and leadership to facilitate recording of complaints and to provide feedbacks.HLSS shall work in collaboration with the cluster leads to conduct household verification especially at the new sites like Magwi, and bio-metric registration exercises, segregated into male, female, boys, and girl, elderly. The CCCM site management team shall as well set up and maintain a camp information management system and share with stakeholders on Monthly basis. HLSS in coordination with the RRCs and County authorities shall conduct monthly peaceful coexistence meetings between IPDs and host communities and establish camp population self-help and social support networks to promote peaceful coexistence.CCCM field team shall provide capacity building training for partners, site management committees and leadership including government counterparts, the RRC, to improve overall understanding as well as capacity to carry out camp coordination activities and skill on identification of services gaps and coordination with humanitarian partners.HLSS shall carefully plan and implement phase out and exit strategy with involvement of the sites governance structures and leadership.

3 - Project indicators

By the end of the project period, Health Link South Sudan shall ensure the below CCCM objectives and activities are accomplished, through active site participatory leadership and inclusive community led-governance structures. Foremost, the project shall ensure that Community led-governance structures are established or strengthened at all the 8 selected sites through free and fair elections of leaders and committees, with full participation of all groups especially the interest groups especially women and PSNs. Community engagements meetings such as peaceful coexistence meetings, information campaigns e.g COVID-19 preventive measures, conducted with full participation of the camp and host community leaderships. sites management committees, representatives of women, youth PSNs including minority groups such as persons living with disabilities, to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian services, by the 40,000 IDPs at the selected sites.Capacity building trainings provided for partners, site management committees, including RRC officials, with roles clearly defined, agreed, understood, and widely disseminated to the camp population.Functional partners’ coordination mechanism structures are established in all the 6 IDP sites and monthly meetings attended by government counterparts and stakeholders conducted, with key information such as 3Ws, service advocacy shared.Community-based cash for work care and maintenance interventions activities introduced and community-based infrastructure rehabilitated at the 6 IDP sites through active community participation and effective community governance structures with a specific focus on PSNs. Functional referral GBV, CP and protection pathways, including information desks established and strengthened at the 6 IDP sites to ensure that people with specific needs receive the assistance and protection they require. HLSS shall coordinate this with the already existing protection department and other cluster partners.Satisfactory survey conducted at all the 6 IDP sites and percentage of site population satisfied with overall levels of service delivery including those who consider the complaints response mechanisms accessible, effective, confidential, and safe are established. Such feedbacks shall enhance advocacy and service delivery improvement. FGD guide developed, and safety audits conducted, in coordination with site level protection actors, in line with CCCM cluster guidelines with sex and age specific dis-aggregated data on services gaps established and shared with partners during monthly partners’ coordination meetings. Recommended mitigation actions directly integrated into the 8 sites maintenance plan and addressed with site maintenance activities.Community participatory phase-out structures established at all the 6 IDP sites with full participation of interest groups(Men, Women, girls, boys, PSNs,) and supported with self-management tools, taking into considerations female views during decision-making processes. HLSS CCCM site management team shall ensure all the 6 sites phased out and handed over to the community and RRC for self-management by the end of the project period.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Health Link South Sudan is aware of the Socio-political and geo-political environments including inter communal violence across the Republic of South Sudan and also understands that applying effective and appropriate risk mitigation measures to avert predictable environmental and social impacts to ensure project success is paramount. The project designs and activities are informed by the “DO NO HARM principles and tailored to specific needs of the IDPs and shall seek to improve their living conditions by facilitating safe, equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian services. This project shall focus on mobilization of the displaced comunities to actively participate in the community driven camp governance structures with particular emphasis on rational inclusion of women, men, boys and girls, elderly, PSNs and persons living with disabilities into decision-making processes. They shall actively engage in needs identification and inclusion into project activities.Site surveys, Community engagements meetings and information campaigns shall be conducted with full participation of the camp and host community leaders. sites management committees, representatives of women, youth PSNs including minority groups such as persons living with disabilities, to identify and decide on safe locations for services points(food distribution centers, complain desks, schools etc). This shall be done in consulting and coordinating with partners and cluster, to ensure protection mainstreaming, safe and protective environment, providing equitable, non-dissimilatory and dignified access to services to social cohesion among the beneficiaries.The roving and mobile teams shall conduct service awareness sensitizations meetings to orient the displaced populations on their rights to access unconditional humanitarian assistance, actions that constitute SEA, and behaviors that may lead to SEA, existing reporting mechanism(hotlines, suggestion boxes etc), referral pathways, and HLSS SEA zero tolerance policy. Community Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCM), and focal points shall be established. Age and sex contexts specific protection, GBV, SEA, CP and explosive ordinances IEC materials and reporting mechanisms translated into local languages, shall be displayed at appropriate locations such as food distribution centers, community centers, markets, schools and through appropriate media like Radio talk shows or spot messages. HLSS project staff shall be vetted against SEA during recruitment reference checks, trained on PSEA with emphasis on HLSS Zero tolerance policy, made to sign Code of conduct, SEA clauses inserted in employment contract, provided safeguarding policy(Child protection & PSEA). Female staff shall be embedded among the service provision teams especially those involved in food distribution. Humanitarian services shall be provided at the designated sites as agreed.Regular oversight visits shall be made to the project sites by the head office team to ensure compliance to the PSEA standards.Suppliers shall be oriented on HLSS PSEA standard with PSEA standard clauses embedded in engagement/service contracts.
