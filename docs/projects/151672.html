Project Summary

Characterized by entrenched intertwining complexities and having far-reaching consequences on general protection and recovery efforts, i.e. durable solutions initiatives, evictions remain unarguably one of the most pressing protection challenges in Somalia. As a country that is still recovering from the legacy of prolonged state collapse, and grappling with persistent insecurity and cyclic natural disasters, evictions further exacerbate the protection vulnerabilities of displaced communities. Albeit important strides are being made, the scale, scope and prevalence of the problem have proportionally outmatched ongoing eviction response initiatives, thus necessitating a more robust, integrated and coordinated program response. It is within this broader strategic framework that the current project, which seeks to facilitate enhanced protection of displaced communities from evictions, has been conceptualized. Project services and customized assistance packages will benefit 71,400 individuals in 21 districts across 15 regions. In terms of delivery modalities and outputs, the overarching objectives of the project will be achieved through a combination of complementarily reinforcing set of actions that are framed around the following eight core pillars.
1. Strengthening ongoing eviction monitoring systems to enhance analysis, data availability and usability. 2. Scaling up and consolidating eviction risk mapping to inform advocacy and response. 3. Supporting proactive engagements to negotiate improved tenure arrangements and/or facilitating dignified relocations for populations of concern. 4. Mitigating protection risks exacerbated by evictions by providing different forms of tenure security support to displaced populations, including facilitating issuance of documentations at both household and group levels. Documentation will also seek to reduce risks to HLP assets on which local livelihood initiatives are dependent. 5. Supporting extremely vulnerable victims of evictions cope with post-eviction stress (PES) through the provision of multi-sectoral cash assistance, specialized counselling and legal assistance services. 6. Mobilizing and supporting government’s effort to address evictions, including robust engagement with landlords, gatekeepers and community leaders. 7. Facilitating access to information designed to assist IDPs access services, navigate complex procedures, and utilize existing remedies in areas of displacement to enhance their protection. 8. Strengthen coordination as well as the functional and operational capacity of the HLP sub cluster.

Needs

Forced or otherwise, evictions have enormous impact on the victims’ dignity, physical security, livelihoods, and most importantly, their search for durable solutions to displacement. Widespread tenure insecurity and general inadequate legal and policy frameworks remain the primary underpinning factors, but development-induced evictions, inability to pay rent, and dispute over ownership between competing land owners were among the reasons most cited for evictions in 2018. Evictions continue to occur at different levels, and while mass evictions at group levels are commonplace and engender greater impact, household-level evictions are the most ubiquitous, difficult to pre-empt and/or prevent, and disproportionately accorded minimal strategic attention. Between January and September 2018, the HLP Sub Cluster documented an average 15,000 individuals being evicted across Somalia on a monthly basis, and nearly 70% of all evictions occurring on private properties. Even in situations where tenure is undisputed, evictions do not adhere to acceptable standards for the most part. With the conditions favorable for continuous evictions still thriving across Somalia, the phenomenon is expected to persist for the foreseeable future, which also means that at the current rate of prevalence, a quarter million people could potentially be evicted by the end of the year. A recent eviction risk mapping exercise piloted in the six most affected accessible urban cities found that the proportion of IDP sites at high and extreme risk of eviction are more than those at low, medium and negligible risk. While the Cluster is monitoring the statuses of these settlements and engaging through negotiations and advocacies at different levels, these response efforts are comparably inadequate. Efforts to strengthen protection and contribute to durable solutions to displacement in Somalia will be incomplete and/or undermined without addressing the issue of eviction concurrently and systematically.

Activities

While contributing toward realization of the principal objectives set out under this project, activities will also aim, as part of the overall conceptual and technical design, to consolidate ongoing humanitarian efforts by expanding critically essential protection services and strengthening operational synergies between mainstream Protection and other sector areas in ways that promote integration. In the case of this specific intervention, the following activities have been prioritized: 1. Eviction monitoring, documentation and reporting; 2. Eviction risk mapping and reporting; 3. Negotiating improved tenure arrangements, access to alternative lands, and/or facilitating dignified relocations; 4. Provision of tenure security documents; 5. Provision of cash assistance and specialized services to victims of evictions; 6. Mobilizing and supporting engagement with government, landlords, gatekeepers and community leaders; 7. Facilitating access to information necessary to navigate administrative and procedural obstacles linked to the enjoyment and exercise of HLP rights; 8. Facilitating the resolution of HLP disputes through community engagement processes; 9. Development of HLP capacity through tailored trainings for duty bearers and right holders; 10. Supporting the organization and facilitation of HLP forums and learning events.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The conditions necessary to facilitate eviction prevention and response initiatives in ways that meaningfully contribute to durable solutions and resilience vary vastly from region to region, with some regions having more favorable conditions than others. In general, however, regional states are stabilizing and gradually beginning to exert and expand formal authority, albeit challenges persist. With the exception of the wider Banadir region where land issues tend to be more complex and problematic due in part to the lack of public land, inadequacy around legal and policy frameworks, and concentration of multiple forces exerting authority and power over land matters, conditions in other regions are more suitable even with the absence of legal and policy frameworks. Government’s response such regions, primarily through the municipalities, is more robust and usually decisive as compare to the Banadir area. Nonetheless, efforts are underway to possibly ameliorate this dynamic and this project is designed to contribute to that initiative. Technically speaking, preventing eviction is the responsibility of the government through relevant agencies and institutions. Government initiatives intended to address the issue of evictions will be supported as part of this project intervention, which is anchored on a two-prong approach of direct assistance to beneficiaries and support to state institutions with responsibilities for the administration and management of land and land resources. In locations where regional administrations do not have specific ongoing initiatives or plans, the project will seek to stimulate such initiatives through ongoing advocacies and structured technical support. Regions that are being targeted for pilot initiatives within the framework of supporting durable solutions and resilience include Banadir Regional Administration, and the municipalities of Baidoa, Kismayo, Bossaso and Galkayo.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs are the stratum of the population disproportionately affected by evictions, and as such, they constitute the main target population of this action. Therefore, the risk of exclusion based on displacement status is inherently extremely insignificant. On the other hand, it is possible that sub groups within the broader IDP population could be at risk of exclusion based on ethnicity and/or other minority-specific characteristics and variables. As a result, specific actions have been mainstreamed into the project’s design in anticipation of such possibilities. Generally speaking, assistance packages and services available through this project are normally provided to entire groups as oppose to families/households or individuals, making the risk of exclusion slightly minimized. However, for activities such as cash assistance to cope with eviction stress, and tenure security documents, affected communities will be involved in the process of defining selection criteria. Plus, the composition of beneficiary selection committees will be sufficiently diverse and balanced, consisting members of the affected populations and local authorities, and specific groups identified as minority or perceived to be at high risk of discrimination.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

A number of project services are provided to entire groups without preconditions. Such services, i.e. negotiations to facilitate access to land, prevent eviction, information, legal assistance, etc., are not predicated on gender or age statuses. However, similar to the prevention of risk of exclusion based on displacement status, for individual type services, an established set of vulnerability criteria which has been used over the last few years will be adopted for the implementation of this project. This will be in addition to having the affected communities being involved in the process of defining selection criteria and ensuring that the composition of beneficiary selection committees is diverse, balanced, and composed of specific groups identified as minority or at high risk of discrimination. Being cognizant of the fact that women within the Somali society face different forms of discrimination and exclusion due to cultural and structural biases, and as an affirmation action, females shall constitute 60% of the total beneficiary population. Children are not targeted directly as a result of the nature of the project and the type of services. However, the number of children with elevated vulnerabilities within a family to the list of vulnerability criteria to entire that children are not entirely excluded.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The issue of land sits at the heart of most armed conflict around the world. While there is no evidence that eviction is contributing to or influencing armed conflict in Somalia, there is evidence of forced eviction processes resulting in violence, injuries and deaths. Secondly, competition over scarce resources tend to heightened during displacement ensuing an eviction incident, and such competition often foment tensions which then degenerate into violence of varying proportions. By addressing the issue of eviction systematically and constructively through this project, i.e. averting forced evictions, facilitating dignified relocations, etc., the impact of violence on target beneficiaries will be substantially mitigated.
