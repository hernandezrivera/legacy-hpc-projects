Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, the post independent South Sudan remains in serious humanitarian conditions even after signing of the revitalized peace agreement to resolve conflict among the warring parties. As reported by HNO, "their situation is worsened by endemic [sub-]national violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighbouring countries."
Alight has operational presence; in Aweil West, Aweil Centre, Kajo Keji, Ulang, and Kapoeta South and Budi Counties, South Sudan. Alight runs GBV response and prevention interventions in all but one proposed locations. It is only in Aweil West County where we currently don't have active GBV services but have on-going nutrition interventions. HRP funds will be used to complement ongoing BHA/USAID support in; Kapoeta South, Budi, Kajo Keji and Ulang and integrate GBV into our nutrition activities in Aweil West. In Aweil Centre, GBV activities are funded by UNICEF which will end in September 2022.
To ensure that Alight and other partners do not duplicate services, Alight will undertake GBV service mapping on ‘’who is doing what and where’’ in all the locations targted. This will allow Alight to only target areas not served by humanitarian interventions.
Through our GBV programming in the aforementioned locations, gaps are emerging in nearby payams of the counties we are operating and these new vulnerabilities to GBV are being identified due to evolving context as a result of floods and related displacements of IDPs, returnees and host communities against a background of limited funding for GBV. The cumulative effects of these emerging gaps therefore calls for scaling up GBV service provisions to locations and populations that have not been benefiting from our current GBV programs. In Aweil West, GBV services have been non-existent as identified by our nutrition staff providing Nutrition services in Aweil West.
To ensure inclusivity of interventions especially for persons with disability, Alight has a culture of conducting assessment prior to launch of project to ensure that disability information are captured for planning purposes. This information usually help Alight to put in place deliberate measures such as; procurement of white cane for the blind, placing ramps to all constructions and employing skilled disabled persons to form part of our staffing structure. Alight also structures home visits for both GBV messaging and services provision to cater for the interest of the disabled persons.

1 - Needs

Kajo Keji: ARC (Alight) participated in UNOCHA led inter-agency assessments carried out from 30th September to 7th October 2021 in Kajo Keji to assess the needs of displaced persons caused by influx of returns, IDPs and large herds of cattle in the farming areas of Kajo-Keji County. Findings from these assessments highlighted the need for lifesaving services for returnees, IDPs and host community. GBV response and prevention, WASH and Shelter, food and nutrition and NFIs are the most needed interventions. Hot spots with protection concerns are; Lori, Rodo, Gederu in Nyepo Payam, Ajio, Sokare and Mangalotore in Liwolo Payam with reported cases of rape and sexual violence exacerbated by the presence of cattle keepers in Kajo-Keji County. The assessment put the number of; IDPs at 56,370, returnees at 46,131 and 5,685 host community. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 57% of IDPs expressed greater concern/fear about GBV, specifically rape and forced marriage. HRP 2023 funding will broaden the scope of GBV services to those who were displaced persons and returnees in Kajo Keji. Kapoeta South (KS) and Budi in Eastern Equatoria also receive returnees from the neighbouring countries of Uganda and Kenya. According to REACH-South Sudan Cross-Border population movement factsheet for Eastern Equatoria region released in December 2021, 38% of the movement are refugees/returnees (inbound) while 48% are internal movements (IDPs). For instance, during the formulation of the 2022 HNO, focus group discussions (FGDs) and Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) conducted in host communities, IDPs, and returnees indicated that their community members did not feel safe from various forms of gender-based violence. Of the reported GBV incidences, physical assault accounted for 36 percent, sexual violence for 24 per cent, and emotional abuse for 23 per cent. The 2021 FSNMS+ qualitative assessment identified rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, forced and early marriage as GBV concerns in more than two-thirds of the interviews held with both male and female participants. Protection risks associated with firewood collection among women are common in this region highlighting the need for fuel efficient stoves to mitigate GBV risks. Cases of early child marriage are rampant among nomadic Topossa and Didinga communities. Aweil Centre and Aweil West: The South Sudan 2022 protection severity mapping for 2023 showed, Aweil Centre and Aweil West falls under GBV severity level 3 (severe) and therefore, if no GBV services are provided, it will increase GBV risks to the affected population of Aweil Centre and West and this will relapse of the county to GBV severity 4 with aggravating factor such as flooding that become routine natural disaster hotspot. The HRP 2022 will aim to address the gaps as well as expanding GBV services to Aweil West County where GBV services are unavailable and partners have to refer GBV survivors to Aweil centre that is over 65KM to access services. The lack of GBV partners to provide GBV services is creating situation of impunity for violence against women and girls which Alight wishes to address through the support of the HRP 2022. Ulang County: Ulang County is one of the many counties in Upper Nile State affected by flooding and inter-communal violence (ICV) which led to the increased pressure on the few services available in the area. The flooding and ICV has led to increased displacement and thus protection risks for women and girls due to underlying factors that place women and girls at disadvantaged position. According to the IRNA assessment conducted between 4th -5th November 2021, 9,244 households (46,220 individuals) have been affected by the flooding alone. The HNO 2022 has put Ulang in severity level 4 (extreme) meaning relapse of situation to catastrophic if no interventions are put in place. The HRP 2023 funding will be used to upscale services to other Payams such as; Yomding, Doma and Kewer among others.

2 - Project activities

Cluster Objective 1: Reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability challenged person through the provision of multisectoral specialized GBV response services. Activity 1.1 Setting up systems and procedures for survivor-centered case management in Aweil West. Alight has on-going GBV response and prevention activities in all locations except in Aweil West where GBV project will be established. System set up will include; recruitment and training of Caseworkers, establishing private rooms for case management, procurement of necessary supplies printing tools needed for documenting survivor’s stories. Activity 1.2: Comprehensive case management and referrals. Survivor-centered and confidential case management services to GBV survivors will be undertaken as well as consent-based referrals. To this end, Alight will work with partners to localize and update referral pathways regularly. Activity 1.3 Staff and community frontline trained in case management. Participants will be GBV staff and other frontline service providers in the referral pathways. Activity 1.4: Establish, equip and operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West. Alight will construct, equip and operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West. WGFS activities will conform to UNICEF’s WGFS guidelines for South Sudan. Activity 1.5: Provide PSS for women and girls at the WGFS. WGFS will be an entry for women and girls to access other services. Alight will identify a fully dedicated WGFS facilitator from the beneficiaries to coordinate skill building and PSS activities so that caseworkers can fully focus on case management. This will also foster sustainability and ownership of WGFS activities by the community. Cluster objective 2: Vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys, including ability challenged persons, have improved access to the provision of GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions. Activity 2.1: Community sensitization and awareness raising on GBV. GBV prevention programming will involve awareness raising on services available and referrals. Alight will adopt different strategies in passing GBV key messages to the community such as house-to-house visits, use of posters and community conversations. 15,000 women, girls, boys and men will be reached with GBV messages through awareness raising. Activity 2.2: Distribution of dignity kits. Alight will procure, transport and distribute dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age as a GBV risk mitigation measure. 1,000 dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls. The dignity kits contents will be guided by the GBV Sub Cluster. Activity 2.3: Conduct safety audit. Alight will lead joint safety audits with other sectors to strengthen integration and develop a holistic approach in responding to GBV risks. Safety audit findings will be shared with all partners with key actions for each sector. Activity 2.4: Training on fuel efficient stoves as a risk mitigation measure. 50 women and girls will be trained to make energy-saving stoves. Activity 2.5: Alight will provide livelihood opportunities for 150 of girls and women at the WGFS to support women economic empowerment and reduce GBV risk related to denial of resources and opportunities. Cluster objective 3: Prevent and mitigate GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Activity 3.1. Train 60 Community Mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Human-centred designing, person-centred approaches, and service user involvement are integral part of Alight's project cycle mechanism. Alight values special interest groups as an integral part of its beneficiaries and takes proactive measure to ensure that they are involved in all stages of its project; from planning, designing, implementations, monitoring and evaluations. By so doing, we are continuously learning to serve them better and get feedback on how best they wish to be served. The continuous engagements with the most vulnerable members has made it possible for them to approach Alight staff and register either complaints or vote of appreciations. We will thus build on experience we had in different locations to continually improve and make our services accessible to all categories. Alight also placed special attention to capacity building of local staff to ensure that beneficiaries are served by staff both male and females known to them, connected to them by belonging and language so that they can easily interact and register feedback in their native language. This facilitates feedback from the most vulnerable community. Equally so, Alight's policy of equal opportunity in terms of employment allow them to employ the special interest groups with skills to be part of their staff and this has given them ability to understand and respond to specific vulnerability issues brought forward at planning table.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Alight's commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on the effective implementation of three long-held principles: 1) our customer is the IDP/host community/returnees, 2) IDPs/host communities/returnees define quality and 3) we collect feedback including complaints and solve them alongside IDPs/host communities/returnees. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneficiaries. To demonstrate Alight's commitment to AAP in practice, Alightwill ensure strong accountability in its responses toward affected communities and individuals directly affected by disasters by involving them directly in program design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. Alight will use periodic focus group discussions (FGDs) and an ongoing feedback/complaint collections and response mechanism throughout this project to obtain feedback from beneficiaries. Feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed from regularly and Alight will coordinate appropriate action internally and bilaterally with respective partners. Feedback/Compliant Response Mechanism: Alight places a strong emphasis on receiving feedback from the individuals and communities that we serve in order to improve ownership and the quality of our programs. A feedback/complaints response mechanism, which both receives inputs from beneficiaries and regularly provides feedback, will be utilized during this project. Where the need to adapt programming to suit the beneficiaries' interest arises, Alight normally incorporate such feedback and adjusts programming so that the real needs of the beneficiaries are met.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA: Alight staff have signed a code of conduct which includes zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. Alight commits to continuously educating beneficiaries on PSEA and other violations and to inform them on reporting mechanisms in place when such cases occur. WGFS are used as entry points for reporting PSEA cases for women and girls while County coordinators are another opportunities for boys and girls to report the same. Alight believes that PSEA cases may not only affect women and girls but equally men and boys, for this case, an alternate reporting through the County Coordinators will help address single line reporting. Alight will support survivors of PSEA to access support in a manner that does not compromise their dignity, privacy and confidentiality. Alight has a graphic display of its reporting mechanism in its WGFS and other structures that outlines how one can report cases of SEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback supports real-time adaptation of projects to the needs of their target groups and contexts, and accountability of programmes and service providers. Alight believes that the process of giving feedback empowers beneficiaries and make partners respond to the needs of the beneficiaries. Context continually changes and therefore the modality to deliver time specific and relevant responses are informed by use of the feedback mechanism. Alight therefore uses these beneficiaries' feedback to make their interventions relevant to the beneficiaries' needs and adapt services that suit the needs of the beneficiaries. Alight believes in symbiotic relations between beneficiaries and Alight. For this relationship to be nurtured well, Alight has committed to delivering services that respond to the real needs of the beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To mitigate the risks of external interference, ARC will ensure that projects are officially launched involving all the stakeholders so that they are sensitized about the project targeting modality that is based on needs and not political affliations. Women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities, older person and other marginalized groups will also be involved in the design, implementation and monitoring and evaluating the project to respond to their needs at every stage of the project cycle. This will include ensuring that the needs and priorities of vulnerable and minority such as elderly, people living with disabilities, women, adolescent girls are taken into consideration and they are included as beneciaries of this project. ARC will continually enage in advocacy with stakeholder to enlist their support in the project instead of working against the project through interference. ARC will sensitize beneciaries on services available, where and how they can be accessed. To do this effectively, ARC will ensure an updated and localized referral pathways in place and will be disseminated widely among the service providers and to the community. Ethical considerations in regard to handling of survivor’s information will be upheld. Survivor’s information will be safely stored in lockable cabinets and case management rooms will only be accessed by key staff. GBVIMS tool will be used to safely collect, store, analyze and share GBV data in an ethical manner. GBV data will not be shared as a whole but rather statistical extract related to trends will be shared for advocacy.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

A GBV project as a protection intervention has much to do with reducing community vulnerability to natural disasters like floods as well as building resilience to cope. Through the GBV safety audit and hotspot mapping, a GBV project can contribute to identification of harm-ways such as areas proned to flooding that causes displacements. Once the project carry out such mapping and flooding issues are identified as key protection concerns, we develop joint mitigation measures with partners in the project catchment area, community members, and their leaders. This intervention includes sensitization on the need to relocate to higher land or areas less affected by floods and mobilizing the community to build dykes to prevent flowing water from breaking in residential areas where human settlements are found. Alight’s GBV project will contribute in flood preparedness plans and our project will provide for pre-disaster mitigation, early warnings and emergency operations. We will integrate disaster trainings in our training programs as well as including early warning as part of awareness campaign messaging. Training at all levels, public information activities, and readiness evaluations are all tools available within disaster preparedness which can easily be integrated in GBV project activities. Coordination of local and state disaster preparedness plans and programs is essential. Success is closely associated with the degree to which individuals, local governments, and states protect themselves by taking appropriate hazard mitigation measures and obtaining flood mitigation measures to reduce community vulnerability and displacement due to flooding. By so doing, GBV project will contribute in reducing flood related displacements.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The quality, timely and inclusive protection (GBV) services is enshrined in Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Quality Assurance Tool that offers health care providers, facilities, and program planners and people who work with children and women a straightforward way to start, strengthen or expand post-GBV health, mental health, psychosocial services through the use of evidence-based standards. Alight adopts these standards while providing GBV services to ensure that its services are quality, timely and inclusive to the affected population. We attribute quality to include responding to real needs of the affected population. Given that we do not provide all the services that people might need, we transcend quality into other partners by training them on the quality assurance tool so that their service delivery is guided by adhering to the standards. Special attention will be paid to the affected population and priority locations given the vulnerable situation the population finds themselves in. The quality of case management and psychosocial services are premised on; 1. staff capacity and 2. institutional capacity to adhere to standards. These two premises will be used to ensure quality delivery of minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV services.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

GBV interventions will contribute towards reducing or decreasing proportion of population facing food insecurity by making women, girls, boys and men contribute towards agricultural activities and decision-making process of how harvests at household levels are managed. Power is one of the most abused decision tools in South Sudan. This transcends to the household level since the patriarchal system is so much embedded in society management. Alight's GBV project places importance in awareness raising to influence attitude change towards women and girls. By so doing, it will increase population participation in food production and decision-making on harvest management to improve household level of nutrient intake. This is important as it helps to decreased population facing IPC 4 and 5 as more people will participate in food production. It must be realized that food production in South Sudan is left in the hands of women and children while men and boys are involved in wars and in some communities, cattle rustling. By involving these traditionally inactive populations (men and boys) in agriculture, it will increase food production and reverse progression towards food insecurity in the Country to severity level 4 and 5 by 2023.
