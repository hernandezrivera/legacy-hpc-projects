Project Summary

Providing safe and decent living conditions to RRs and HC to reduce exposure to physical and protection-related risks is the primary object of the project. Interventions are designed to construct 400 new MTS, 100 MTS repair and maintenance for the RRs and 50 shelters for the HC. New MTS in camp 1E and 1W will be built based on the Shelter/NFI Sector assessment to target only completely damaged shelters and if it may not reach the planned target and in this case GUSS will coordinate with the Sector to include other camp based on needs and discussion with the Focal Point organizations to reach the target. These activities will be implemented at camp-1 (E&W) for the RR and Jaliapalong and Palongkhali unions for the HCs. It is expected that, 2% of PWD will get assistance prior selection on the basis of Washington group on disability.
Beneficiary selection will be done by participatory community consultation process that will involve community people, majhis, local administration, community leaders and UNHCR. Needs will be assessed by conducting technical assessment and in coordination with SNFI focal point at camp level. To ensure gender and inclusion activities, adult men and women will be engaged in site management, lightweight works, and bamboo mat production etc. In addition, elder people, PWD and children will engage to identify safe, accessible shelter location, map potential risks, and environmental disasters. Men, women ratio will be 70:30, payment will be equal and will have equal rights to express their opinion. Prior that, vulnerable shelters will be identified through coordinating by the partners, sectors followed by the 4W dash board by sector.
SADDD and AGDM will be helpful to ensure meaningful participation, using a participatory, rights and community-based approach, in design, implementation, M&E. The level of engagement in gender and inclusion activities will be identified by age groups.
We constructed 896 MTS and distributed solar lamps to 776 HHs in Camp 1 (E and W) since 2022. No major safety and security concerns arises so far. However, COVID-19, treated bamboo, decision’s change in shelter design and global recession may obstruct project activities. To address protection issues
- Selected shelters location will not be vulnerable from disasters like land sliding
- Distance will be maintained between shelters for proper drainage
- Coordinate with other actors for protection issues
- Passage over the drainage, ramps, and handrails for PWDs
- Strictly prohibited child labor
To address PSEA issues, we will follow the principles of IASC, UN regulations and its existing policy when and where required. Staffs will be trained on PSEA in coordination with PSEA networks and sectors. Moreover, GUSS will coordinate with SNFI sector preparing 4W reports, attending meeting, training and workshop. Besides, communication will be made with sector focal in the camp and other relevant partners of the camp.
J-MSNA reveals, CwC has always been denied for both RR and HC. To avoid misinformation, beneficiaries will be involved in implementing process, IEC materials will be produced addressing the assistance received, guidance of using assistance, branded tarpaulin and a functional CRM will be there. Feedback receiving phone number will be displayed and an officer will be assigned to record complaints and feedback into the register. Other activities are
- Feedback through written texts, phone call or SMS including face to face discussion.
- Identity of feedback senders will be kept confidential.
Capacity building of the beneficiaries will be tailored focusing on shelter and protection which will build a sense of ownership and empower community people. In addition, involvement of govt. stakeholders during the project will strengthen rapport with NGOs. We have a functional Environment Policy which enables us to comply high consumption of Day Light, recycling resources, discourages deforestation, and reducing CO2 emissions etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The total population is as per UNHCR Operational Data Portal (as of 19th October’22) is 943,529 across the 33 camps. While this region is the most poverty stricken with very limited livelihood opportunities, hilly & uncultivable lands due to salinity and proneness to numerous natural disasters. Given the context, this massive influx also created additional social, economic and environmental burden on the HC as well.J-MSNA 2021 shows, the unmet needs for shelter is 74%, highest among the other sectors and the refugee population in camps remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance, and almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to shelter/NFIs and food security/livelihoods, roughly three quarters of households reporting issues with their shelters, as well as roughly one in ten households reportedly having to make rent payments, and large proportions reporting having insufficient NFIs. In addition, specific types of households, such as female-headed households, households without access to self-reliance, 36% households reported not having made improvements to their shelter in the 6 months prior to data collection despite having reported issues. In July 2022, 4W data from the shelter/NFI sector showed that around 30% of the refugee households hadn’t receive any non-emergency shelter assistance since 2020 due to COVID-19 restrictions. Another research shows of 21,232 MTS built in 2021 and 2022, only 9,435 used treated bamboo and good quality tarpaulin. As such, 11,797 shelters (56% of all MTS) will require replacement of materials in 2023.J-MSNA 2021 dictates, 54% of Populations of HC lives in kutcha house whereas 32% in semi pucca, 9% in jhuprie, and only 4% in pucca house. The people living in kutcha and jhuprie house are the most vulnerable. 71% of the households reported shelter issues and 30% did not notice any improvements to their shelter. Moreover, 53% of the respondents from the HC reported issues regarding up gradation of shelter materials which has made among their top 3 priority needs was notably higher this year compared to previous years (with 85% of the host community data collection having been completed prior to the flood event at the start of August). In case of HCs appear to have been no recovery from the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak and its secondary impacts on livelihoods, with a potential risk of a long-term deterioration of coping capacities and living standards. Besides, Large proportions of households were reportedly not able to access sufficient information on the types of assistance available to them, face challenges providing feedback or complaints, and do not feel consulted or heard by humanitarian actors. In response to CwC, 74% of households in RR reported that they were unable to access enough clear information on the types of assistance available to them, 9% of households reported having faced challenges when providing feedback or complaints in the 6 months prior to data collection whereas the percent is 99% and 36% respectively for the HC. Hence, there will have strong CFRM for the targeted beneficiaries to minimize misinformation, appropriate IECs containing risk information messages in relation to support provided and organizational visibility. Although many of the organizations have been responding to the overall needs but a significant gap especially for Shelter and NFI are still persistent both in RR sites and HCs due to the limited availability of funds compared to the needs.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

We partly agree the concept of one implementing partner per sector per camp but we are afraid that as the humanitarian assistance for RR and HC is 100% dependent on foreign donors, it will be a huge challenge for us and other NGOs to ensure funding continuously round the year. GUSS has strong presence in camp 1 (E and W) having a health post, significant number of shelters, NFI, Food Security and WASH interventions whereas some other camps are also covered through shelter, Food and WASH interventions. We will coordinate with SNFI sector focal points by sending regular updates through in –person and virtual meetings and send updated 4W report regularly. Although it is desirable for the NGOs to ensure multi-year funding which to create stability in refugee operation and improve rapport with beneficiaries, the govt. and other partners which is also depends on the funding availability. We also believe handover of project facilities must be done in a responsible manner. In addition, we try to comply other principles as much as possible. For example, GUSS always consult with sectors/working groups before engaging in an activity for the RR and HC, comply with the technical standards and policies set by relevant sector, member of PSEA network with nominated focal points. We usually respond programmes based on our technical knowhow and proven geographical presence. Although we often implement programmes at different camps based on the need assessment and prior necessary approval from RRRC and the sector. We regularly take part capacity building sessions organized by NGO platforms, attend and participate coordination meeting with the sectors and shares 4W report to the relevant sectors.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The shelters provided will be structurally safe, constructed from materials resistant to rapid deterioration, located on sites protected from disasters, accessible to water points, local markets and other basic services which prioritizes safety and dignity and avoid causing harm. We ensure free access of all age groups in shelter construction, implementation and monitoring. We engaged beneficiaries in need assessment, site development, lightweight works, and shelter construction where possible. In case of PWD people, we engage them to identify map potential risks, and environmental disasters.

3.2 - Gender

Gender refers to the characteristics of women, men, girls and boys that are socially constructed. Those characteristics varies society to society and change over time. The characteristics of gender among the RR and HC in cox’s Bazar are more or less similar. The power structure of RR and HC families is male dominated. Most of the cases, families are dependent by men which is due to the lesser opportunity of women in income generating activities, lack of women friendly livelihood options and social barriers bordered with superstition and social stigma. Considering the bitter reality, we will include women headed families, widowed, elder women and single mother families as the beneficiary. Moreover, male headed vulnerable families will also be included. While implementing activities, we will engage women in project design, implementation and monitoring of the project. One to one sessions will be conducted with family members to discuss gender issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project encourages engagement of beneficiaries of all age groups including men, women, children, elder, PWD and LGBTI etc. They involve in need assessment, shelter construction, lightweight works and site development. We will have FGD with men, women, children, elderly, and PWD and LGBTI group to address their need accordingly. The shelter location will be selected as if it can be safe, accessible and close to the playgrounds, local markets and child-friendly spaces as if people of all ages can avail services. Involving children as a labor is strictly prohibited according to our child safeguarding policy. Our old staffs are trained in child-safeguarding policy whereas newly appointed staffs are orientated. Our position in child safeguarding ensures no children is abused by the project staffs, vendors and other relevant stakeholders. On the other hand, festoons are always hanged on our project site to aware people on safeguarding issues.

3.4 - Diversity

People of all age groups will be involved in project design, shelter construction, site development and monitoring etc. Sex, Age and Diversity Disaggregated (SADDD) policy was maintained for targeted beneficiary under this project to address the specific needs of each beneficiary group. In line with the beneficiary targeting criteria with special needs such as the households with pregnant & lactating women, Children, households headed by widow or separated woman, households with disabled, chronically sick and LGBTI people will be given priority. On the other hand, member of religious or ethnic group will get priority in receiving assistance, assessing their needs and engaging them where necessary. Moreover, we will have FGD with PWD to assess the needs where we will also discuss how they will be able to contribute in project interventions. Based on their ability, we will engage PWD people at our project interventions.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Ensuring AAP is one of the prerequisites for GUSS. Once we are accountable to the beneficiaries they will own the project which will improve the quality of the programme. To address the compliant and feedback from the beneficiaries, we have strong CFM. They are visible at project sites and have access to express their concerns by face to face interaction, phone calls, SMS and written texts to the project staffs and designated CFRM/Monitoring Focal. That person will be involved in preparing monitoring tools to assess the programme quality. Information of the assistance will be shared through IEC materials based on our Information Disclosure Policy. Participation will be ensured by the affected people in project design, implementation and monitoring based on inclusion principles. They will be engaged in need assessment, shelter construction, site development and lightweight works etc. Moreover, satisfaction survey will be conducted at the different stages of the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will be implemented without causing any harm to the environment. No tree and plant will be cut down and no vegetation will be destroyed for the project initiatives. Impact assessment will be done considering the social, cultural and environmental aspects. Good quality treated bamboo, tarpaulin, and metal footing and other shelter materials will be used as if the structure can be safe and reinforced from the effects of disasters like cyclone and earthquake. The project will have activity of site development which will reduce the vulnerability of land sliding, earthquake and other natural disasters. We have a functional Environment Policy which enables us to comply high consumption of Day Light, recycling resources, discourages deforestation, reducing CO2 emissions and raising awareness of environmental issues.
