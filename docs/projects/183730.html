Project Summary

The action, based on the WC vulnerability map, the WC comprehensive assessment in HCFs that involved CESVI and ACTED, consultations carried out with key stakeholders (WC, PWA, MoE, North Gaza JSC), targets the HRP2022 WASH specific objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians” and focuses on:
-WASH and Protection support in HHs
The project targets 800 vulnerable HHs (4,600 individuals) in North Gaza, Deir Al Balah, Khan Younis, Rafah Governorates.
Thanks to the consolidated experience from recent projects, ACTED and CESVI in collaboration with YEC and Beit Lahiya Development Association are proposing to scale up activities for vulnerable HHs and enlarge the access to water, by ensuring increased water storage capacity and improving sanitation facilities. Improving access to safe water for drinking and hygienic use prevents further deterioration of HHs’ living and health conditions, enhances protection against inadequate WASH facilities and exposure to nutrition risks, limits negative coping mechanisms (e.g. water purchase from unreliable vendors, using open spaces as toilets). CESVI has developed strong expertise in WASH (previous/ongoing projects in Hebron and Gaza in partnership with ACTED for adequate access to water). YEC has proven experience in implementing WASH programs (access to services for fragile families, human and protection assistance, hygiene kits distribution, awareness raising). The project design has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders (WC, PWA, MoSD) and simultaneously addresses urgent needs of vulnerable populations while endowing them with proper WASH systems.
-WASH support in public institutions (HCFs and schools)
CESVI and ACTED have direct knowledge about WASH-related risks and needs affecting these institutions and thanks to previous projects in Hebron in partnership with UNICEF, gained relevant experience in improving WASH facilities in HCFs and schools.
The project proposes rehabilitation interventions of WASH facilities in 11 schools (in Gaza, Deir Al Balah and Khan Younis Governorates) and 3 HCFs (in Deir Al Balah and Rafah Governorates).
This component is deemed extremely important in view of the worsened impact due to lack of improved WASH facilities and related health risks in the frame of the Covid19 pandemic. Public places like HCFs and schools are strongly exposed to the risk of contamination, due to the high turnover of users and visitors, if not equipped with proper WASH and sanitation facilities, also in consideration that they serve brackets of population already vulnerable per se (sick people, children).
The activity is implemented directly by CESVI and ACTED with the approach adopted for interventions at HH level. The specific expertise combined with close relationships with relevant authorities (MoE and MoH), results in assessments pre-identifying necessary and specific interventions in each facility.
-Support for Waste service providers
The project supports service providers in 8 Municipalities in North Gaza Governorate, supplying 200 containers to improve the capacity and the environmental situation of existing collection points, and 300 kits with PPE and working tools for the workers to improve working conditions.The project is supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA) with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly. Gender principles are mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBPCs are trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus on women and children. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian consequences of the longstanding illegal Israeli blockade on Gaza Strip for 15 years, are enormous: it has devastated Gaza’s economy, left most people in need for assistance, have no access to food, water, essential services such as healthcare and education, and cut Palestinians off from each other and unable to leave or enter Gaza Strip.The destruction of WASH infrastructure, weak resource management, and rapid population growth contribute to the increasingly dire shortages of safe drinking water and access to sanitation. According to PWA, 97%the coastal aquifer water is unfit for human consumption due to unsafe over-pumping and wastewater contamination. Moreover, about 40% of the domestic water supply is lost on the way to consumers because of Gaza’s outdated infrastructure.Access to safe water is also dependent on constant availability of electricity. However, the chronic energy crisis in Gaza results in just few hours of electricity per day. Due to irregularities in supply, people have to stock water in containers on their rooftops to use on days when the supply is cut off. This limits people’s ability to operate pumps to fill up the containers. Families that do not get a chance to pump water to the roof before the power supply runs out find themselves with no running water. This crisis affects all aspects of life, including ordinary HH activities like cooking, bathing and laundry, and also critical needs for which safe water is necessary daily (such as personal hygiene practises, female hygiene, infants feeding with bottle-feeding using water, rather than exclusive breastfeeding) and that increases the exposure to contamination, infections and waterborne diseases).Safe disposal of wastewater is affected as well. The low quality and limited supply of water force the Gazans to purchase desalinated water from private vendors, mostly for drinking and cooking, but also for other purposes. Much of the purchased water is polluted, too.Women and girls are disproportionally impacted by water quantity and quality limitations affecting substandard sanitation facilities, for their own health and for bearing the burden of HH duties for all the family. Therefore inadequate WASH facilities mean gender inequality.Adequate access to safe WASH facilities and practices are prerequisites also for public institutions like healthcare facilities and schools, given their dedicated purpose to serve vulnerable categories of population (sick people, children). More, in both cases unsanitary and inadequate facilities for women and girls may discourage them from going to hospitals to receive treatments, and girls from attending school. The overall status of WASH facilities in schools does not meet international guidelines, with a less than adequate ratio of toilets to students. For such public institutions the lack of proper WASH facilities was observed during the recent assessments conducted by the Wash Cluster and, as regards schools, was confirmed by the MoE during meetings conducted by Cesvi.Gaza Strip is a disaster and conflict-prone area due to severe flooding and frequent conflict. These recurrent events not only cause damage and losses to the infrastructures but also result in huge volumes of piled waste and debris in the aftermath. In post-conflict situations, affected communities produce large volumes of waste, often overwhelming the existing, albeit weak, solid waste management systems in Gaza. This results in an accumulation of waste, which often aggravates the affected communities and runs the risk of disease outbreaks and exacerbating flooding. Delivering efficient waste management services during emergency and post-conflict situations, however, is very challenging in Gaza largely due to the following factors: power disruption and shortage of electricity supply to operate landfills and transfer stations; difficulties in collecting and transferring normal waste and overloaded debris from damaged buildings and constructions; inability to access landfills due to restriction in mobility and road blockage from debris of damaged infrastructure and/or security measures; shortage of fuel and replacement of spare parts for garbage trucks; damage to truck repair facilities, community trash collection containers, and bulldozers; and lack of effective communication platform/system.Conflicts in Gaza revealed that municipalities are poorly equipped to respond to emergency situations and are often caught off guard to provide crucial SWM services in times of urgency. SWM institutions in Gaza have challenges in identifying and adopting appropriate contingency waste management response systems due to the lack of resources such as infrastructure, knowledge and skills, and capacity in planning for waste management services. Given the frequency and imminent nature of conflict-related emergencies, municipalities in collaboration with local authorities, emergency management personnel and solid waste managers in Gaza need to pre-emptively develop contingency plans and associated procedures to effectively respond to urgent situations.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Strengthening and capacity-building of WASH committees. 3 Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPCs) are reinforced. They are involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level and to guarantee better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. CBWPCs provide support in the distribution of hygiene kits, upgrade of basic water and sanitation facilities. ACTED provides capacity-building to the committee members on WASH, SPHERE and protection standards including key PSEA messages. They also raise awareness of the project’s complaint response mechanism amongst beneficiaries.Activity 2 – Supporting WASH needs for vulnerable HHs: PE tanks, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, hygiene kits. The project proposes a combination of different interventions in order to address specific needs by way of tailored interventions. In details:2.1 CESVI and YEC provides 300 HHs in Beit Lahiya (North Gaza) with 250L-PE water tanks, to maximize the water storage capacity of drinking water for vulnerable people. HHs are selected based on an assessment recently conducted by CESVI in September 2021 on water storage capacity. The water tanks distribution aims at ensuring the targeted HHs adequate access to safe water for drinking and hygienic use.2.2 Following the lists of HHs received by the related authorities, 500 HHs are visited to assess needs for rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities, and tailored BOQs are developed. CESVI handles the rehabilitation of 300 HHs in North Gaza Governorate, providing also 300 1,000L tanks and ACTED supports rehabilitation works of 200 HHs in Rafah, Khan Yunis, and Deir al-Balah Governorates. During this step, beneficiaries will be involved in the design of the response and the specific needs of PWD, as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns will be included. A self-repair approach will be used with the provision of restricted cash in 3 instalments. Work implementation is monitored through visits conducted by CESVI and ACTED to each HH to review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel instalments. Monitoring visits are also used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could face and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance.2.3 CESVI and ACTED also provide cash assistance to the 500 HHs supported under Activity 2.2 for the purchase of hygiene kits to complement the rehabilitation of HH sanitation facilities, therefore CESVI follows up the 300 HHs targeted in North Gaza Governorate, while ACTED conducts the same activity with the 200 HHs targeted in Rafah, Khan Yunis, and Deir al-Balah Governorates. CESVI and ACTED monitor the activity by visiting each HH in parallel with assistance provided for Activity 2.2.Activity 3 – WASH in schools and Health Care Facilities (HCFs) Response framework for WASH in HCFs and Schools Vulnerability Mapping conducted in 2021 by the WC, with the active support of ACTED and CESVI, highlighted a pressing need to enhance WASH facilities in such institutions in Gaza. Following the technical assessment of selected schools and HCFs, in collaboration with MoE and MoH and with specific focus on needs of people with disabilities (PWD), as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, Bills of Quantities (BOQs) are developed for 14 institutions, outlining rehabilitation and maintenance WASH needs and related action plans. CESVI is in charge of rehabilitating 7 schools while ACTED is for 4 schools and 3 HCFs in Deir al-Balah, Rafah, Khan Younis and Gaza Governorates. Contractors are selected through a transparent tender process. ACTED, CESVI and YEC conduct regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure respect of the plan and of the WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as to provide corrective actions where needed.Activity 4 – Support to Joint Service Council for SWM. In coordination with the JSC of North Gaza, CESVI addresses 8 municipalities’ urgent needs for the improvement of the waste collection service, by way of community containers to improve capacity and environmental conditions of waste collection points, and working tools and PPE kits to improve sanitation workers’ conditions. CESVI provides 200 1m3 containers and 300 kits for sanitation workers, including protective suits, rain safety suits, safety gloves, boots, and working tools like hand-brooms, shovels, and brakes. The activity includes the procurement process and quality control to ensure meeting the service provider’s standards, and thus ensuring the population of the target municipalities the access to safe waste disposal system, and the sanitation workers proper protection from contamination.

In addition, the Protection Officer, with the support of the CBWPC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. Were any issues to be identified, the Protection officer will offer guidance and complimentary training to beneficiaries.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the project Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM), implemented in all CESVI projects. It will be adapted to ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and considers the harmonization with PSEA complaints and whistle-blower. The system is overseen by MEAL and AMU officer, which is independent of the delivery of activities. All beneficiaries will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will be provided with small pamphlets summarizing the process and the different avenues/channels available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CFM and CRM rely on existing and solid ToRs, SoPs, field paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (<15 days) to the complainants. This CFM/CRM system will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits by staff to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is a key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of projects, and is reinforced through the CBWPCs. Beneficiaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored through regular visits and interviews regarding ongoing activities. The MEAL officer and AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. CESVI and ACTED have been implementing projects in the targeted Governorates since 2010 and thus benefit from a high level of acceptance in local communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with CESVI and ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

A community-based protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at the community level. The CBWPCs (Community-based WASH Protection Committees) of the project target areas will be engaged in order to guarantee compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities).Do No Harm: members of CBWPCs will also be actively involved in the activity of distribution of hygiene kits, in order that all beneficiaries are informed on their correct usage, in particular on items which could expose beneficiaries to risks if misused. All team members involved in the project implementation will undergo extensive training in PSEA, Protection and WASH Sphere standards to ensure they engage with the beneficiaries and take into account the specific needs of all household members. The Protection Officer together with CBWPCs’ members, will conduct additional visits to ensure that the responses to specific needs, regarding gender, age and disability, are integrated and the needs of female members are prioritized in the rehabilitation plans. Inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance (for example items to be included in the kits), and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. A self-help approach will be adopted to implement rehabilitation and improvement of sanitation facilities at HH level (planned through cash modality) having several added values as it assigns an active role to the beneficiary in carrying on rehabilitation works of their own house, thus increasing ownership and personal satisfaction, and tends to support economic recovery in the Gaza Strip by involving the local market contractors which is more cost-efficient.Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by CBPWC members on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practices. CBWPCs will be responsible to refer to the project team any acutely vulnerable households that could have been neglected, or who would present significant vulnerabilities despite not fitting the criteria mentioned before for further investigation.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs. CBWPCs will receive training on the specific needs of various groups, in order to ensure they factor them in throughout project implementation and that assistance can be adapted to beneficiaries’ needs. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, using and maintaining WASH facilities at the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanisms such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Protection Committees and ACTED Protection Officer will ensure that the rehabilitation work of the WASH facilities will include adequate lighting, internal lock and privacy fencing in order to ensure women safety, security and privacy inside their house. Women will be highly encouraged to be part of the Committees, follow all the implementation and join partners team during the field visits to the households. During the awareness sessions ACTED, CESVI and YEC teams will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as “women issues” In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actively engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between females and males of proper hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. Specific sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers and children.In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected populations, partners are engaging beneficiaries in the intervention design for construction or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, both in households (families) and in public institutions (staff managing HCFs and schools). Prior to the procurement of assistance, each household and public institution will be informed on the planned assistance in order to determine if needs have significantly changed since the original needs assessment. If outstanding needs are identified through this process, the material assistance will be adapted to meet those needs, as long as it fits within the budgeted amount for the location. Community-based WASH Protection Committees will represent communities’ interests throughout the implementation process. Indeed, they will ensure that the repair to be undertaken by the household is decided in collaboration with the household members (women and male) and is in line with their priorities and needs. They will also ensure that all protection and privacy concerns faced by the household due to the substandard WASH facilities (toilets and kitchens) are addressed in priority. A similar approach will be adopted while implementing activities in public institutions (HCFs and schools), according to the needs represented by the authorities in charge (MoH and MoE) and assessed during the development of the proposal, that will be assessed again at the time of the project start.
