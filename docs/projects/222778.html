Project Summary

To address the identified needs for Rohingya refugees mostly required the WASH infrastructure repair and maintenance, and rehabilitation of existing infrastructure. For promoting their hygiene practices including behavioural changes with gender-sensitive hygiene promotion round off the year of the NRC WASH program and contingency stock is available to support in emergency response.
NRC worked closely with WASH Sector in last couple of weeks on rationalization exercise and agreed to continue WASH intervention one block (F) in Camp 13 and 3 blocks (A, B and C) of Camp 19. NRC will provide support with water supply, sanitation and Hygiene promotion and awareness campaign for community people's behavioural changes. Awareness sessions will be conducted through local staff or trained volunteers and WASH sector guidelines, modality, and approach NRC will follow. WASH sector-developed IEC materials will be use among the project participants. In the host community NRC will do close collaborations with UNO, DPHE, PIO, chairman and host WASH focal and other stakeholders. NRC have a CFM team for receiving community complaints and feedback for program quality improvement and quality service delivery. A PDM will be conducted for comparing the stratus with the baseline. Outcome monitoring will let NRC the impactful result and project participant satisfaction service from NRC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

To date, the considerable efforts made by the Government of Bangladesh, together with WASH partners, have resulted in 89% of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs reporting having enough water to meet their household needs. 96% of sanitation facilities are also reported as functional. Challenges remain however, especially in hygiene (due to the high cost of supply and inappropriate hygiene practices) and solid waste management. NRC is closely working with WASH sector to support the gaps and need in Cox's bazar response. Based on that, NRC is planning to scale up its operation in F block of Camp 19 from January 01,2025. Furthermore, NRC will to continue regular WASH intervention in Camp 19 ( A, B and C block) in 2025 as like 2024. In line with sectoral strategy, NRC will focus on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost efficient solutions. NRC will target the provision of quality water and sanitation facilities, as well as solid waste management provided through infrastructure support and community engagement.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The context presents specific risks for women, adolescence girls, persons with special needs, and gender-diverse populations, who continue to encounter heightened barriers in accessing WASH services. To address this issue, meaningful participation of project participants is crucial. The project aims to enhance the involvement of these diverse groups and NRC is committed to ensure inclusivity throughout the project. NRC recognizes the gender impacts, distinct needs, and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys, and girls . The organization acknowledges that women and adolescence girls, face socio-cultural barriers limiting their access to service provided for WASH intervention. Additionally, special attention is given to the elderly and persons with special needs, ensuring their specific vulnerabilities are taken into account through tailored assistance and accessibility measures. This holistic approach acknowledges that different age groups have distinct requirements, and the program is structured to cater to the specific needs of each demographic, fostering inclusivity and resilience within the community. To mitigate the risks of delivering inequitable assistance or reinforcing existing inequalities, NRC will implement community-led creative interventions, fostering meaningful participation of beneficiaries in decision-making processes. During distributions and training, NRC will create designated area for different age and gender group. Volunteers comprehensive training in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and a code of conduct to minimize risks. The project aims to improve access to services for persons with special needs through activities and referrals, with coordination maintained through meetings and capacity building.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC prioritizes the safety and wellbeing of project participants by ensuring that its staff and volunteers undergo training on child safeguarding. NRC strictly adhere to organizational policies designed to protect project participants and stakeholders from sexual abuse and uphold the principles of the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The organization has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with the goal of encouraging beneficiary feedback on interventions and methodologies employed. The CFM not only addresses general feedback but also handles reported cases of sexual abuse and PSEA. To support these efforts, a protection referral pathway is in place, ensuring that reported cases receive appropriate and timely case management. In order to effectively mainstream protection, NRC is committed to fostering community engagement. Communities will be empowered and equipped to actively participate in ensuring that they have access to services provided by NRC. This collaborative approach underscores NRC's dedication to creating an environment that is not only protective but actively involves the community in the safeguarding process. NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff and is engaged with the PSEA Network for regular training for the staff and new camp volunteers at the beginning of each project. PSEA, is one of the key elements for NRC way training that regular staffs, volunteers and partners are sensitized about.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Climate change has an immense negative impact on WASH services. People affected by displacement can be impacted through water in many ways as it is the primary medium through which we feel the effects of climate change. Water scarcity can affect health, food security, economic activity, environmental wellbeing, and create conflict. Managing water resources effectively is necessary for ensuring the supply of water on which people’s health and livelihoods depend – now and in the longer term. Below are described some of the climate change mitigation and adaptation measures and initiative that NRC will implement at country office level: - NRC will prioritise solar-powered water supply systems, where technically and economically feasible- NRC will improve its environmental performance through the application of Environmental Minimum Standards (EMS) and the ‘NEAT+’ tool. - Advocacy will support communities to have access to and influence with higher-level institutions and decision-makers. Global initiatives will be developed to support country offices to achieve this including capacity building.- Reduce NRC WASH CC carbon print and other negative environmental impact of our responses.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, The project has number of DRM elements to address the needs of population affected by disasters which includes, emergency WASH/hygiene kits distribution, affected infrastructure rehabilitation, raised awareness through session and campaign and will create community leadership for key messages sensitization. NRC also will do some early action with the information that will help the community to be prepared for monsoon or disaster through the community volunteers. Also, a number of capacity building training will be conducted for emergency response roster and community volunteers as a partner organisational readiness to respond to an emergency.
