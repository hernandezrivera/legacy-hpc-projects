Project Summary

The proposed project is intended to improve access to immediate food needs resilience building of the target population and expand economic opportunities among the vulnerable urban, peri-urban and rural IDPs, and host communities affected by prolonged droughts, recurrent floods, evictions as well as other environmental disasters in Somalia.
The proposed project is intended to improve access to immediate food needs of the target population and expand economic opportunities among the vulnerable urban-rural populations and host communities affected by prolonged droughts, recurrent floods, evictions as well as other environmental disasters in Gedo, Galgaduud and Lower Juba regions of South-Central Somalia.
The proposed intervention is set to benefit as much as 30,144 beneficiaries through improved access to food availability through support to investments in productive community infrastructures/assets such as water catchments, feeder roads through Cash for Work approach. The intervention also seeks to enhance the local community’s livelihoods through diversification, fodder production during drought periods as well as appropriate actions aimed at ensuring improvement in productive community infrastructures/assets such as water catchments and feeder roads through Cash for Work approach. Through the above approaches, the project is expected to build the resilience of pastoral, agro-pastoral livelihoods in the target locations.
The targeted project beneficiaries are drought stricken poor pastoral households/families headed by women who have disproportionately lost their livestock and moved to urban centers, children, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), families with elderly and disabled among others.
The project is in conformity with the FSC Objectives, Somalia 2019 HRP and CaLP and Somalia Cash Working Group.
The targeted project beneficiaries will be given inputs (Seeds, Fertilizers and other farm equipment) to improve their farm yield and improve their food security status. Farmers will also be capacity built together with the respective line Ministries such as Agriculture, Livestock of the Federal Government of Somalia as well as of the Regional Governments. ADA will also strive to ensure access to early warning systems to ensure faster dissemination of information in times of shock to enhance early preparedness and mitigation measures.
Recent climatic improvements and large-scale humanitarian assistance has led to the improvements in the food security situation of Somalis. Still, acute food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihood needs remain. The residual impact from the 2016/17 drought, combined with the recent floods, displacement, evictions etc., continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable Somalis in South Central Somalia. Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3).
Poor households in the crop-dependent parts of southern Somalia (mainly riverine) currently have little to no food stocks and limited farm labor opportunities, due to localized below-average production, flooding, and the extended lean season. Beneficiaries facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse urgently need humanitarian assistance and livelihood support to prevent food security deterioration. Livelihood support is also required for people categorized as Stressed (IPC Phase 2). Routine field monitoring and assessment by ADA indicate that acute food insecurity remains prevalent among vulnerable populations affected by drought and riverine flooding and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, hence the need to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.

Project Activities

The Proposed project activities include: 1. Through Multi-Purpose Cash Transfer, 500 HH will receive $85 each per month for 3 months. This will be transferred through TAAJ money transfer which has a reliable network coverage.2. Through Cash for Work Approach ADA will reach 820 HH spread across the project locations using Mobile Money Transfer which is more efficient and reliable. This will be done through identifying productive community infrastructures/assets and rehabilitation/construction in all the targeted locations. Beneficiaries will be paid a sum of $6 per head per day through mobile money transfer. Beneficiaries will work for 24 days per month over 3 consecutive months of Cash for Work activities. 3. Strengthen crop production through distribution of drought resistant inputs, provision of tools and training of 1200 farmers (benefiting 7200 people in post-harvest management) 4. Pilot of fodder production and preservation-This will increase livestock access to biomass, thereby minimizing the risk of livestock death associated with scarcity of food. 5. Facilitate income generation and vocational skills training (in entrepreneurship, financial literacy and basic marketing) for 1200 youths (700 females and 500 males).6. Pilot mobile early warning system on dissemination of national early warning information to people so as to enhance community disaster preparedness mechanisms.7. Supervision of atleast 5 the rehabilitation of the productive infrastructures/assets, reporting, monitoring and evaluation.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed response plan is addressing household immediate food needs, resilience building and recovery of the target community by improving the adaptive capacity to shocks, drought and displacement in host and IDP settlements, offering different approaches based on tne preferences, vulnerabilities and needs identified by the communities. ADA will work with IDPs and host community to define food security and livelihood interventions that consider people's choices, for example vocational skills training for those in IDP camps and agricultural investments for host communities in rural areas of the project for those who have access to land.ADA will further explore working with IDPs on how to tackle the problem of access to land when it comes to livestock rearing and agricultural livelihoods. This response plan will bridge short term humanitarian response to medium and long-term livelihoods diversification. Disaster risk reduction and climate change adoption activities are part of the commitments of ADA. The Federal Government of Somalia priority under the NDPII (National Development Plan II) includes resilience of the local communities to withstand shocks and stresses. Somalia government is focusing on resilience and sustainable food for it's communities to avert and tackle effects of recurrent drought on Somali people. This project therefore contributes to meeting government's efforts on saving lives of the people facing food insecurity situation primarily and promote resilience endeavors of the government.
