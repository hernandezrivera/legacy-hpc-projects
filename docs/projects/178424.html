Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen livelihoods, support resilience and facilitate socio-economic empowerment among vulnerable young Syrians in Rural Damascus. To address the negative consequences and pressures of protracted crisis and internal displacement on vulnerable Syrian youth, the project seeks to strengthen access to sustainable and safe livelihoods for conflict-affected populations, recognising them as economic actors.
The project will assist the transition to self-employment to stabilize household level income and contribute to livelihood security through a tailored package of life skills training, entrepreneurship capacity building, financial micro-business start-up grants, and mentoring and specialised business development services.

4 - Needs

With the sustained erosion of people’s resilience and at the same time increasing returns to destroyed and impoverished areas, the needs relating to basic services and livelihood opportunities are ever-more growing. The HRP 2019 highlights that rebuilding livelihoods and economic opportunities are among the primary concerns of people, and sets the increase of resilience through the restoration of livelihoods as one of its three main strategic objectives. Syrians’ food security and livelihoods have been particularly affected, in a country where 80% of the population already live below the international poverty line of $1.90 per day. Syria faces generic unemployment and significant deficiency in the labour market as a result of extensive displacement and disruption to the education infrastructure. Out of a workforce of 4.9 million people (between 18 and 65 years of age), more than 50% are unemployed according to the HNO 2019. Young people are disproportionately affected, in particular females. The most of the factories in Rural Damascus that accommodated large part of the population were destroyed during the years of war and were never rebuilt thus limiting opportunities for jobs in the governorate. At the same time there are high number of women headed households who are in a dire need of livelihood for them and their families as they have lost their husbands during the war. A 2019 UNDP assessment report on employment and livelihood support highlights that the most immediate need for job creation is in technical and vocational occupations, especially in textile, engineering and food industries, as well as in construction, management and marketing.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Participants selectionIn total 50 beneficiaries will be selected at the first stage – 25 with start-up idea and 25 with existing micro/small business.3. Trainings on life skills using Aflatoun methodology, including decision-making and problem-solving skills, critical thinking, understanding and managing the self, managing feelings and stress, communication and negotiation skills, and teamwork skills.4. Entrepreneurship training and development of business plans The course takes the trainees through the process of establishing a small business or HBB step by step, providing them with the basic knowledge of inventing a business idea (Ideation), managing business and finance, marketing, legal framework in Syria and finally developing a simple business plan.5. Distribution of the start-up grantThe average size of grant will be 1340 euro, while the actual size of the grants may vary from 700 to 2000 to fit to the different needs of the small businesses. 6. Business coaching and BDSQuality business coaching is an essential part of the business development process. Business coaching will be offered for the duration of 4-5 months by FCA. 7. Women support Since the project will focus 50% of women who are normally underrepresented in the businesses due to several reasons, an additional support will be offered for women entrepreneurs participating in the project. Out of 2 business coaches selected for this project, 1 will be a woman and 1 man.8. Monitoring and lessons learnedThis is the first livelihood project for FCA in Syria and so far not many organizations are doing similar recovery program in Syria. To this end it is extremely important to capture and document learnings both in terms of process and results.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will not pose serious risks for the beneficiaries or Humanitarian workers. One risk is associated with the distribution of the cash grants to the beneficiaries for their business startups. To minimize the risk, the following measures will be included: 1) providing cash grants in smaller instalments (if business plan allows it); 2) providing grants close to the beneficences business so he/she doest not have to travel with large amount in his/her pocket. Project teams will be visiting beneficiaries in their homes in pairs (man and women coach) to make sure that no gender related (mis)treatment is being exercised and that coaches are not subject to GBV.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1. The GoS restricts activities of the non-governmental sector, including those of Church-affiliated actors, and interferes in project implementation such as selection of beneficiaries, or restricts visits of foreigners and does not issue their entrance visas; national staff is arrested or conscripted. M1: FCA maintains transparent communication with the GoS; this will be continued and also initiated for this project in the project inception phase to ensure that sudden restrictions are not applied. R2. Escalation of conflict results in disruption or suspension of activities. M2: In case the security situation deteriorates and access to the project location is not possible, activities will temporarily be suspended. The project will continue to maintain its staff (where possible) throughout the suspension of activities to ensure that operations can be resumed as soon as it is safe to do so. In case of substantial impact on the action, a modification process will be triggered.R3. Failure to overcome barriers to achieve participants’ economic inclusion and empowerment. M3:The proposed action aims to reduce poverty and foster financial exclusion to address issues both on the demand and supply side of labour. Specific care will be taken to adapt targeting methodologies, engagement strategies for enrolment, proposed livelihood options and training content and methodologies to identify gaps in the local value chain as well as the target groups’ capacities.
