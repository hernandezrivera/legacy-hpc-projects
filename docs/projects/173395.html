Project Summary

The IRC’s proposed project, Community Outreach Mobilization Empowerment (COME- MT), aims to provide community-based protection response to vulnerable people with specific protection needs and to strengthen the protection environment for these people with information dissemination and awareness raising with broader communities. The IRC will add an individual protection assistant (in-kind) component to complement the immediate material needs of the most vulnerable to support their socio-economic well-being and to prevent them from adapting a harmful mechanism to cope with their specific needs. COME will target the people not based on the status but on their needs and protection risks. The specific prioritization and the focus will be given on the people in need of mental health and psycho-social support, missing documentation, people with disabilities, and elderly among the communities targeted with this action.
The IRC has successfully utilized the community volunteer system for implementing the community-based protection interventions in Libya to address the protection and health needs of the targeted communities (IDPs, returnees, non-displaced, migrants and refugees) since 2018. For the proposed action, IRC will leverage its community outreach volunteers in Tripoli and Misrata (including, medina, Bani Walid and Tawerga) to identify the people in need of protection assistance, information and access to the required services. The COVs will be trained on psychological first aid, identifying the MHPSS and protection needs, and administering the safe service referrals to the specialized services. The IRC will ensure that the COVs are selected among their respective communities and representative for different age and gender groups.
To strengthen the protection environment and for its sustainability, the IRC will provide capacity building trainings on Protection and Humanitarian Principles, Protection Mainstreaming, and International Legal Framework and Refugee Protection for the local humanitarian and civil society organizations, local authorities (Ministry of Health, Ministry of Social Affairs, and relevant Municipality staff) (75), and community committee leaders and members (80).
The IRC has supported the establishment of five migrant and refugee community committees in Tripoli and will target to add five more with this action. The IRC aims improving the function and participation of the committees in responding the needs of their respective communities by supporting them with guidance to create and implement their community based protection action plans and with small funds for the identified urgent action plans (in-kind support). (10)
The action will be complementing the IRC’s ongoing protection, health and governance programs in the target areas, which will help for the timely and safe referrals between health, mental health, and protection (IPA, GBV, and CP) support services provided by the IRC. This will also help the IRC to leverage the exiting CBP capacity and the already established relations with and the ongoing support to the local CSOs and stakeholders.

1 - Needs

The situation in Libya remained volatile due to the conflict and now the already conflict affected dire humanitarian needs and protection risks has been exacerbated by COVID-19 in 2020. Protection sector estimates around 463,000 people (24% women, 36% children and 15% disabled) with protection risks and assistance in 2020; including, 123,000 displaced people, 104,000 returnees, 88,000 non-displaced Libyans, 102,000 migrants and 46,000 refugees. In the western Libya, the highest number of people in need of protection assistance are located in Tripoli and Misrata. The service capacity in Tripoli and Misrata has been extremely stretched by the increased number of the IDPs, returnees, migrants and refugees in the city. The issues around the access to essential services and financial opportunities has been deteriorated further by the Covid-19 and the related restrictions (lockdowns, and closure or partial closure of the public services) for prevention and control of the spread of the disease. This has created another layer of barrier of access to the essential services for those who have already movement related risks and restrictions and face discrimination (women and girls, people with mental health issues, with disabilities, and older people from all population groups, ethnic minorities, and migrants and refugees especially from the African countries). Coupled with the broken law and order and social networks, the deteriorating financial situation has impacted the housing, the job market, and the purchase power of these groups for obtaining the essential goods and basic items, which draws them to adapt harmful coping mechanisms. In such context, the vulnerable groups require safe and non-discriminatory spaces and networks to receive protection support and access to information on available services for them. People with disabilities and mental health are the chronically neglected fields with longstanding problems that predate Libya’s 2011 revolution, including underdeveloped specialized services, a shortage of qualified workers, lack of facilities, social stigma, and funding marginalization. The predominant number of mental health consultations is reported for Tripoli area while absent in 80% of other districts in the country. COVID-19 led to the closure of the main psychiatric hospital in Tripoli and suspension of outpatient services. The situation is more dreadful for the migrants and refugees with disabilities or mental health problems, especially if they are unaccompanied or supported by a caregiver. They are the most vulnerable group against the discrimination and abuse.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.4. The staff (including COVs) will follow the Covid-19 IPC measures (wearing masks, keeping distance, and self-isolation if infected or suspected). The project implementation will adapt alternative service delivery modalities to ensure not risking the safety of the staff and the beneficiaries - which includes remote phone-based service delivery (information, counseling, and psychosocial support). The COVs and the staff will be provided with extra phone credits to cover their and the beneficiaries' communication expenses related to the phone based service provision. 4.10. The COVs will be selected from diverse groups, to be representative for the all segments from their respective communities to ensure the consultation done with different gender, age, ethnic, religious and national groups. In addition, the project aims to establish and empower the community committees by including them in the identification of the needs and the decision making process of addressing these needs. 4.11. and 4.12. The project will target the people with disabilities and mental health issıes as well as their caregivers. COVs will be trained on the aspects of the disabilities and raise awareness among the community, and link the caregivers and families to social-emotional learning and awareness raising sessions. If the person cannot access to the services provided bye the IRC, the service will be mobilized to them (one-to-one or remotely depending on the safety and Covid-19 situation).4.15. The IRC will have multiple channels of feedback and complaint from the clients. the information will be shared with the beneficiaries. The IRC will refer any direct or secondary complaint related to SEA to its Ethics and Compliant Unit for further investigation. The staff and the COVs will be trained on the IRC Way (standards of conduct) and on PSEA and how to report about the SEA issues safely and confidential manner. 4.18. The IRC will connect the people who lack of documentation needed for the access to the needed services with the relevant actors and advocate for their access to the required services. 4.22. The essential role of the COVs will be disseminating service information. They will be also trained on safely processing the referrals of sensitive case to the relevant internal and external services. They will guide and accompany the clients to the services they consent and need.5.23. To prevent the aid dependency, the IRC deploys a community based approach. The IRC will conduct capacity building trainings and mentoring support to the community committees and all local stakeholders relevant to the beneficiaries.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The IRC maintains a conflict-sensitive approach and provides services across statuses, including refugees, migrants, displaced Libyans, and returnees. The IRC’s 2021 strategy also prioritizes a needs-based, client-informed humanitarian response. This approach includes soliciting feedback from clients, and adapting projects to meet client-identified and evidence-based needs. At the onset of the Action, the IRC will conduct a series of KIIs and FGD to gather primary data about needs and access constraints for the general population and for acutely vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, migrants, and ethnic minorities. IRC will continue to use the feedback/complaint boxes, and feedback forms, and establish a feedback and complaint line (phone, email, WhatsApp, viber, and Facebook). Feedback will be analyzed and course-corrective measures taken. All new staff involved will receive training on confidentiality and information protection.
