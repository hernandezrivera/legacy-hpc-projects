Project Summary

This concept note is developed as a project for Counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States. The project seeks to improve access to integrated emergency food and nutrition security programmes including management of Sexual Gender-Based Violence (SGBV), mainstreaming of gender and protection programs. The project will target 35,000 (20000 women: 15000 men) people in need. The target beneficiaries will be distributed as follows: 18000 (Ulang, 6000; Nasir, 8000; Longechuk, 4000); 12000 (Magwi, 3000; Torit, 4000 and Kapoeta South, 5000); and 5000 (Akobo) for Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei States respectively. Of the overall target population of 35,000 people; according to residence category, the number of IDPs, returnees and host communities will be estimated at 12,000; 8000 and 15,000 people respectively.
Through integration of nutrition, WASH, Protection and gender mainstreaming, the project will support numerous activities including support to public assets through the provision of Basic Packages of nutrition and WASH facilities; unconditional cash to improve markets and households food and nutritional security and livelihoods; cash for work as a mode to improve public assets and cub natural hazards such as floods and maintenance of public assets through building dykes, market installs, conducive child and women space, and clearing, etc. The Project interventions will focus on engaging youths by providing employment opportunities; capacity building of staffs through on-job and classroom training; and routine monitoring of FSL field activities.
The project aims at; improving access and responsiveness to integrated essential FNSL needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on sustainable households’ food and nutritional security and livelihoods; increasing access to essential social protection among vulnerable populations targeting SGBV and improving resilience among vulnerable populations by increasing access to mental and psycho-social support services.
This project will mobilize farmers, fisher-folks, support established farmers groups, training of farmers and fisher-folks’ groups and distribution of farming and fishing inputs (seeds, seedlings, fishing gears, tools, etc) to them. Through greening practices and environmental conservations, proper food and nutrition security and livelihoods analysis, agricultural commercialization for income generation and awareness creation through social media, local announcements, in schools, markets, churches, public information sharing, etc; agricultural sector will be improved.
The expanded overgrazing and natural hazards in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria, such as flooding, drought and cattle raiding has placed the states on frontlines of food insecurity and climate change situations. Because of limited inputs distribution, institutional, technical and financial capacity, implementation of interventions become unlikely supported for food security including climate change adaptation for improved livelihoods. Therefore, local production of vegetables and economic fruits system has the potentiality to create sustainable employment opportunities especially among youths and women. Yet, provision of improved farming and fishing technology, management and utilization has the potential of improving the food and income security and nutrition of the rural residents, including returnees. Less skilled and knowledgeable producers will not be able to access the high-value market segments, however by being able to develop their capacities to meet the required standards and competency would change their status and capacities to access various market segments.
This project is developed as a result of community needs assessments, cluster prioritization strategy, and consultations with the communities in the targeted locations that indicate a dire need for emergency food and nutritional security and livelihoods.

1 - Needs

Worldwide everyone has absolute right to life and high standard of living. However, the aspects of growth and development can only be attained through having adequate food, decent housing, and a clean environment. In this instance, the reverse is true in the case in South Sudan where an estimated 3.3 million people who still need life-saving. This is further exacerbated by the cumulative effects of continued civil wars/conflicts over the years and massive flooding that creates misery, violence and destroyed people’s lives and properties including their household livelihoods (Humanitarian needs overview 2019). According to the recent FSL situation assessment reports involving UNKEA, food and nutritional security in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria state have drastically deteriorated amongst the community members. Following the ceasefire, Upper Nile has received overwhelming numbers of returnees from neighboring countries such as Ethiopia and Sudan. An estimated 64,966 individuals returned to Nasir County alone in March 2019 (ROSS report)-increasing the total number of the population that needs humanitarian assistance to 288,704 (ROSS Sobat report, 2019). Similarly, Ulang County continues to host large numbers of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and a large number of returnees because of its proximity to Ethiopia. The returnees are further putting a strain on the already fragile situation that is characterized by persistent high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) exceeding the WHO emergency thresholds (FSNMS report, Round 22). Again, Akobo West and Longechuk are among the counties with acute food insecurity (IPC 3) yet they are routes for returnees. Although relative calm has helped reduce the number of people facing extreme food insecurity, this notwithstanding, food insecurity persists and an estimated 691,000 people (67% of the population) are likely to be facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in May-July 2019. For instance, Longechuk in Uppernile is projected to face Catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity during the same period because of their high levels of vulnerability compounded by the effects of the lean season, as well as humanitarian assistance delivery constraints. Among all of the counties of Uppernile State in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) including Ulang, Nasir, etc with the exception, Longechuk County, has the highest Emergency status with the highest proportion of population–estimated at 85%–facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. During the lean season, majority of households in Longechuk mainly rely on livestock products (for those households who have access to them), fish and wild foods, with very limited amount of food, especially cereals, purchased from the market due to high prices and long distances to functioning markets. The proportion of food insecure people as projected for May-July 2019 is the highest ever and the food insecurity situation is driven by household food shortages that are typical of a lean season, but have been exacerbated by delayed rainfall, the persistent macro-economic crisis, population displacements, additional needs from returnees, prolonged years of asset depletion, and the generally eroded livelihoods due to continued years of conflict–all of which continue to compromise majority of the households’ capacity to access enough food during the ongoing lean season. High food prices occasioned by depleted harvests as well as rain-induced deterioration of road networks that disrupts market supplies and functionality are also contributing to the high levels of acute food insecurity indicating a dire need for emergency response. It is because of this that there is need to increase the farmers and fisher-folks capacity development and support through provision of inputs for sustainable food security and livelihoods.

2 - Project activities

UNKEA will support provision of farming and fishing inputs to its vulnerable beneficiaries. The identified vulnerable beneficiaries will also be provided with agricultural tools such as Maloda, and other land clearing equipments including irrigation equipments (watering cans, water pumps, etc) either in groups or as individuals. 23 Demonstration farms will be supported and established as a demonstration sites for vegetable farmers in the target locations as follows (4 Nasir, 5 Ulang, 4 Longechuk, 5 Akobo, 4 Magwi, 2 Torit, 3 Kapoeta South). There will be a supplementary kitchen gardens established and households trained on them. A number of packages and scope of services will be offered including; (i) inception workshops will be conducted in all the target locations (Counties) to introduce the project that emphasis much on the implementing partner and donor organization and the activities and services to offer communities; (ii) Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) to ascertain the safety and protection of the targeted vulnerable beneficiaries; (iii) Lobby for farming and fishing inputs (seeds, seedlings, tools, and fishing kits, etc); (iv) Distribution of the inputs to the vulnerably registered beneficiaries; (v) A number of trainings will be conducted to both farmers and fisher-folks that includes: Farmers to be trained on vegetable production techniques, importance of crop and vegetable production, integrated pest management (IPM); Fisher-folks will be trained on good fish handling, modern methods of fishing, fish preservation techniques, fish storage and fish marketing; (vi) Cross-cutting issues: The project will work closely with the nutrition department. All the farmers and fisher-folks will be trained on dietary diversification and nutritional security of their household members; the farmers and fisher-folks will be trained as lead farmers to train others in future; the project will also Strengthen WASH programs by providing support to the construction of gender-disaggregated pit latrines in markets and communities and provision of waste segregation buckets/rubbish pits and dustbins; and will ensure the establishment of a robust follow up mechanism; protection and gender will be mainstreamed in the activities by strengthening sexual and gender-based violence services through training in NO HARM-Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: UNKEA will train and equip the community workers and its staff to identify, counsel, and manage victims of SGBV; there will emergency preparedness and response training on food security (especially failure in harvest that cause famine) and climate change and natural hazards such as floods, drought, etc. This activity will be targeting the key stakeholders including UNKEA project team, traders, local authorities, chiefs, and other beneficiaries. UNKEA will train, equip and facilitate FSL officers and extension workers for proper supervisory work of the individual or group farmers and fisher-folks. After the success of the project, previous beneficiaries are expected to share their success stories on the activity implemented as they are deemed to achieve from it such as use of demo plots and harvest from their garden and how it helped their various households and lifted them.

3 - Project indicators

M&E will carry out surveys (baselines and endline surveys) and to ascertain whether its targets are reached. As an indicator to success of this project, it can be anticipated that atleast 80% beneficiaries will be served with FSL kits including agricultural seeds, seedlings, tools and fishing gears. In addition, gender and protection will be mainstreamed in the programmes for equitable participation of the communities targeted.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SGBV, including forced marriage, rape, assaults and cases of snake bites, have been regular in this payams, subsequently this plan will help address some of the protection concerns in making good use of the resources available. This plan is vital for both the community and the partners as the plan will aim at addressing the already identified community protection concerns.This plan will help both payam protection committees, protection officers and local authorities on dividing tasks, roles and responsibilities in addressing the above protection concerns in the community. Furthermore, the plan will help to integrate more protection concerns and to upscale protection in future project projects. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project will adopt multi-communication and response channels that will include both sensitive and non-sensitive feedbacks from beneficiaries and other stakeholders, and will ensure that those selected channels are relevant and inclusive to even the most vulnerable populations such as women, girls, and people living with disabilities, illiterate people, and chronically-ill persons. UNKEA possess a proper laid down feedback and complaints mechanisms and handling system that will be implemented in all project sites in consultation with project participants. These feedbacks will be implemented at three levels: First level is the Area Managers who will be independent of any on-going project activities and reports directly to the Executive Director. Through the Area Manager–critical information is filtered through to the senior program managers without sharing with other project staff (FSL officers and extension workers).The second focal point person is the national-level manager who will interact directly with the Feedback Committees established at Payam levels. This includes the complaints desk set up at all distribution points to receive complaints and concerns raised through the input or cash distribution committees and/or Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) that will be established. Through Feedback Committee– the project in collaboration with the beneﬁciaries will also identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison person who presents beneficiaries complaints on behalf of the communities. Arrangements to directly meet the persons with disability will be done by paying visits to their various households. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the ﬁeld and also at the organization headquarters.The Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems, interpersonal communication through the selected trusted liaison persons. The project M&E system will be designed too to capture beneficiaries’ feedbacks through the scheduled exit interviews and PDM analysis. Regular feedback/complaint mechanisms will be incorporated through the project life span; either monthly or quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various community members. All complaints shall be handled in a systematic manner ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The recent revitalized peace agreement deal between the warring parties of national government herein referred to as “incumbent government” and government in opposition referred to as “IO” has resulted in some safety and stability within most counties in South Sudan. This has provided opportunities for people to free movement to return to their homes. Movement options for inhabitants have been widening to enable access to affected populations who are in most needs. For example, large numbers of returnees from neighboring Ethiopia have been registered in Nasir County and some parts of Ulang County. In Eastern Equatoria, some households have started to come back to the original residence from refugee camps in Uganda. Engagements between opposition and government actors have paved way to allow unhindered access and movement of humanitarian workers and items. The work of local institutions of police and community leaders are gaining traction. Although there still exist prevalence of revenge killing by communities, the buy-in of different protection measures by armed groups even in volatile conditions are conducive for delivery of humanitarian assistance. The project has integrated gender and protection within its activities to ensure that no harm is done to the individuals and communities served considering gender sensitivity. As a feedback mechanisms when accountability to affected population is put in place at all times, interventions are likely to heed the best interest of the people and ensuring that the people are served with dignity. In regards to Uppernile state, Ulang and Longechuk, UNKEA has already supported development of four (4) protection plans as means of creating an enabling environment for interventions in 2020. Therefore, this project will also implement UNKEA's integrated protection approach that mainstreams protection across their regular activities. Similar initiatives will be rolled out in other project target locations proposed for this intervention.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Distribution of food security and livelihoods inputs to beneficiaries and trainings. Establishment of demonstration sites for the community farmers to use for practices. Most will be trained as lead-farmers for the upcoming farmers.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will ensure beneficiaries are aware of their rights routinely through training and community dialogue meetings. Gender sensitivity is one right that the project focused on ensuring that gender equality and equity (women and men) are both considered for inclusive beneficiaries’ participation. In addition, the entirely engaged FSL project staff including extension workers are required to sign up to a code of conduct, and are expected to do no harm during provision of services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The high rates of malnutrition are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. Poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in counties also contribute to acute malnutrition. Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrain service delivery to areas already afflicted by high malnutrition levels. Therefore by diversifying households dietary meals through distribution of plum nuts to children, other nutrition-dense foods such as fruits, dairy, vegetables, etc that have sufficient food values such as proteins, carbohydrates, fats, calcium, and folates will also be given to such such households with malnourished children.
