Project Summary

This project aims to support 2500 farmers and 3500 livestock breeders in Al-Bab, Jarablus, Afrin, A’zaz, Idleb, and Harim districts as the following:
1- Support 1,000 local farmers who can plant one dunum of agricultural land but they are in need of agricultural inputs through supporting them with seeds, fertilizer, pesticide, fungicide, modern irrigation network, and cash for irrigation to plant Autumn, winter vegetables, and potato.
2- Support 500 olive farmers with agriculture services regarding pre-harvesting practices (supplementary irrigation) harvesting process, the extraction process, marketing such as branding and labelling) and post-harvesting process (post-harvesting tillage). Here we should mention that harvesting will be conducted as CFW modality (500 workers). Olive farmers will be targeted in an average of 3-4 dunum for each which means almost 50 olive trees.
3- Support 1,000 wheat and barley farmers with seed and fertilizers to plant one Hectare for each farmer.
4- Rehabilitation of the irrigation canals of Jarablus and Al-Ra’ee by recruiting 200 workers as Cash for Work (CFW) to clean and maintain the canal edges.
5- IYD will support 1000 livestock breeders (500 cows -one cow per breeder) and 500 sheep breeders (10 sheep per breeder). These breeders will receive veterinary services and fodder; each cow will be provided with 10 kg of pellet fodder daily for five months and each sheep will receive 1 KG fodder daily for 5 months. Also, IYD will support the targeted breeders with technical training.
6- IYD will support 500 vulnerable HHs with 5,000 chicks of a Turkish type. These chicks will be fed, vaccinated, and bred for about four months.
7- IYD will run 8 mobile livestock clinics to provide health care follow up for the livestock of all breeders (host community and IDPs) within Al-Bab and Jarablus, and Harim districts. These mobile clinics aim to target only cows, sheep, and goats and provide daily health care follow-up by treating the diseases and supporting the breeders with technical advice for dealing with their livestock with no vaccinations. It is worth mentioning that the estimated numbers to benefit from this activity will reach approximately 2,000 participants during the project life as there will be no fixed target groups. The livestock breeders might receive the service once or more during the project life based on the needs.
The fund for this project has not been secured yet.

4 - Needs

Since 2011, livestock breeding and agriculture have decreased throughout the area, which has had a negative impact on the local economy. The decrease appears to be driven by the absence of extension services, fodder rations, cultivation of pasture seeds and seedlings, digging of wells to provide water for the population and livestock, grading and improving activities, artificial insemination, vaccination, livestock treatments, veterinary drugs, improved breeds, and research services, and provision of agricultural inputs.IYD Needs Assessment conducted during March 2021 reflects the following facts:Agriculture component: All targeted communities have a cultivated area, and farmers depend on the wells (surface, artesian) and irrigation channels to irrigate their land. The average cost to irrigate one dunum is (800 TL, about $ 100 per donum).The available crops’ types are (wheat, barley, legumes, and summer, winter vegetables).The available fruit trees are (olive, pomegranate, figs, nuts, and apple).All the targeted communities have enough daily workers in harvesting with a daily average wage (17 TL per day).The farmers' main obstacles are the lack of the capital to purchase the inputs such as (seeds, fertiliser, irrigation) in addition to the farmer's need for technical follow-up to deal with climate change and its effect on their crops.Livestock component:There are breeders for (sheep and cows) in the targeted communities. Although the availability of good-quality fodder, health services, and vaccines, the breeders' main obstacle is the lack of money to provide these services for their cattle.Most breeders do not depend on green fodder to feed their cattle because of its unavailability in the local markets

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD, in coordination with the local communities, will select the BNFs according to selection criteria.-1,000 targeted farmers will be supported to plant one dunum with vegetables and potato by providing agriculture inputs (seeds, fertilizers, fungicide, pesticide, fuel, and drip irrigation networks).-500 olive farmers (50 trees per farmer) will be supported as follow: supplementary irrigation, Post-harvesting tillage, and hiring 500 workers will harvest olive trees.-Support 1,000 wheat and barley farmers with seed and fertilizers to plant one Hectare for each farmer.-Rehabilitation of the irrigation canals of Jarablus by recruiting 100 workers as Cash for Work to clean and maintain the edges of the canal. Vehicles’ costs to implement this activity will be covered.-500 cows and 5000 sheep will receive veterinary services as follow: applying artificial insemination for the targeted cows, and hormone program for the targeted sheep, conducting reproductive and health following up for sheep and cows and distributing pellet fodder which will contribute to improving and increasing the productivity of cows and sheep.- 500 targeted HHs will be supported with ASF intake which will lead to improving the health status of these HHs’ members. IYD will create the ideal conditions which will lead to the success of procuring and breeding 5,000 chicks.- IYD will run eight mobile livestock clinics to provide health care follow up for the livestock of all breeders (host community and IDPs). These mobile clinics aim to target only cows, sheep, and goats and provide daily health care follow-up and supporting the breeders with technical advice. During the project implementation, PDM, endline, baseline, and MEAL tools will be applied.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Exclusion and discrimination to benefit from the services provided if the service is provided to groups that do not meet the selection criteria and others are excluded. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: Printed guiding instructions that clarify the selection criteria. Active complaint mechanisms to get feedback from people who haven't benefited from services. Risk 2: Beneficiaries may face problems at distribution sites,(theft, intimidation, extortion).Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: The fodder will be distributed door to door with the presence of female staff in the distribution team.Risk 3: Sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by the project staff against female beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: Providing training on PSEA to all staff and establish complaint mechanisms and addressing these complaints. All employees will sign the Code of Conduct. Risk 4: Increased risk of intercommunity tension between community residents and host communities due in part to the unequal level of assistance given to community residents in comparison to host communities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: IYD will mitigate this by ensuring that the community is all needs-based and that community residents are amongst the most vulnerable.Risk 5: The backlash from family or community on women participating in project activities and prevent them from traveling to other areas and access services.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: 1. Community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase, and throughout all project cycles. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders.

Also, IYD utilizes the PSEA Network tools ( PSEA prevention and mitigation field checklist - organizational checklist) as it is recommended.5) IYD has a SEA investigation committee that received a lot of training about conducting any investigation related to the SEA allegations in the best way.6) The PSEA focal point will maintain and follow PSEA & Child Safeguarding Minimum Standards over the different areas of the organization starting from developing policies, the safety of people, safe programs, safe operations, and safe response.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge • Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation. IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety • Risk Management Strategy To effectively manage the risks that may arise during the implementation of the project, key stakeholders will be capacitated and empowered. COVID 19 spread among the BNFs and staff. IYD will apply social distancing in the distribution location, using hand sanitisers, and all measures recommended by the health cluster will be applied.
