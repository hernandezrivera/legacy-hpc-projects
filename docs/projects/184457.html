Project Summary

According to the latest FSNAU findings the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at the national level has remained at a serious level of 11.1% weight-for-height z-score. The nutrition cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of 5 years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently. 295,515 children under the age of 5 are likely to be severely malnourished and an estimated 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM). Further, an estimated 245,909 PLWs will be at risk of acute malnutrition.
Malnutrition in Jubaland is driven by low immunization, low Vit A Supplementation, food insecurity, sub-optimal IYCF practices, poor hygiene and sanitation services. This has adverse consequence on child survival and well being of pregnant and Lactating mothers.
SFH will Strengthen Community component of IMAM and community care practices in lower juba through regular community education; screening of children under five; including referrals and follow-up; promotion of appropriate IYCF practices; and dissemination of key health and hygiene messages. Community mobilization will enhance access to and coverage of nutrition services, contributing to reducing mortality by early detection of acute malnutrition. Key strategies include capacity-building of Community health workers and regular education sessions to equip the target communities with key skills and knowledge on the prevention and promotion of child and maternal health and nutrition practices. This intervention will specifically target 24000 gender disaggregated beneficiaries (Boys, girls, men and women) under the age of 6-59 months and Women of Child-Bearing Age including persons living with disability. Due to the complex operational environment in Somalia and the level of humanitarian crisis in the areas of operation, the primary focus of this intervention is responding to the immediate nutrition needs thus helping to prevent further morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition. Nevertheless, it is designed to also build local capacities, enabling communities to better respond to future crisis, which also serves to improve the sustainability of this action.
Additionally, the proposed action aimed to provide prevention and management of Acute Malnutrition, provide Vitamin A supplementation, immunization, and de-worming treatment for all <5 children admitted in the programme, including health promotion; promote and support optimal IYCF practices at both facility- and community-level; train health workers in delivering IMAM, IYCF and the Baby-Friendly Initiative; and conduct regular monitoring and surveillance.
OTP distributions will take place on a daily basis. Education sessions for all care takers will include OTP feeding methods and other key messages concerning health and hygiene (including that of water, sanitation and hygiene). Upon admission and following every subsequent distribution, all children included in the programme will undergo a medical check by a nurse, receive routine treatment (e.g. antibiotics, mebendazole, vitamin A, anti-malaria, and measles and other EPI vaccinations) and any additional treatment as necessary (e.g. Resomal to treat mild dehydration).
The immunization status of all children admitted to the nutrition programme will be checked and referrals will be done as necessary to the immunization programme run at the MCH clinics. Micronutrient supplementation and de-worming treatment will be provided to all eligible beneficiaries admitted in the IMAM programme. Severely malnourished children with medical complications will be referred to the nearest Stabilization Centre at Dobley Hospital.
Further, awareness creation interventions will focus on supporting, promoting and protecting breastfeeding practices at the facility level to encourage the majority of caregivers to practice optimum feeding of their infants and young children.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed intervention aimed to provide enhanced access to integrated promotives, preventive and curative nutrition services that includes:1) Regular/Daily screening and identification of acutely malnourished child and PLW's for six days in a week. 2) Provision of therapeutic and supplementary support3) Establishing strong referral pathways for those requiring in-patient stabilization care.4) Promotion of optimal child feeding and care practices 5) Provision of essential Vitamins and Micro-nutrient supplements to both children-boys and girls and PLWS's.6) Promotion of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices7) Increased uptake of immunization services in both the static and the mobile centers to reduce the high morbidity and mortality trends emanated from vaccine-preventable diseases as well as poor hygiene and sanitation practices.SFH will provide treatment services for acute malnutrition, focusing on quality and coverage and using the existing protocols enshrined in Somali Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition guidelines. In addition, SFH will ensure that the underlying causes of acute and chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies are addressed through the provision of a High impact intervention/BNSP as outlined in the national guidelines. OTP activities will be carried out through 4 fixed centers and 4 outreach team (Bulagaduud-HC, Yontoy, Wirkoy, Barsaanguni, Gobweyn, Kulbiyow, Hosingo and Waraq-in Kismayo and Badhadhe) in Lower juba to ensure maximum coverage. All SFP/OTP activities at the facility and outreach sites will use an integrated approach and will ensure that all the Basic Nutrition Services Package components are incorporated in the intervention. SFH will engage 24 Community Health Workers and 20 volunteers in follow-ups as well as active case finding and referrals.Plumpy Nut distribution will be carried out for the severely malnourished children who have been registered in the OTP programme on a daily basis. Bi- weekly Health and nutrition promotion sessions will be provided mostly for mothers and caretakers of infants and young children (Boys & girls) to compliment the daily promotion sessions. The severely malnourished children with medical complications who cannot be managed at the mobile sites will be referred and transported to the in-patient facilities/stabilization center within the district.Vitamin A supplementation targeting all children 6-59 months will be carried out at both the fixed centers and outreach sites and indicated into their child health cards. Children will be supplemented with Vitamin A twice yearly and other mass campaign strategies such as Child Health Days will be used to increase Vitamin A supplementation. Postpartum women will also receive vitamin A supplementation within six 6 weeks after delivery.Multiple Micro Nutrients will be administered to pregnant women during the antenatal care clinics to boost their immunity and also to ensure proper growth and development of their unborn babies.Indicators:1. # children 6-59 months MUAC screened2. # PLW in project sites are screened3. # children 6-59 months presenting with AM (SAM / MAM)4. # children 6-59 months admitted to OTP5. # children 6-59 months admitted to SFP6. # PLW admitted to TSFP7. # of severely malnourished children with medical complications identified in OTPs are referred8. # under five children admitted in OTP/SFP who receive de-worming and Vitamin A supplementation9. # PLW with infants 0-12 months screened for breastfeeding support10. # of IYCF lactating and support groups active and supported by IYCF peer counsellors11. # of Community Nutrition Volunteers trained on IYCF12. # of mother/infant pairs benefiting from weekly IYCF promotion sessions13. # of baby friendly centers established

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance:. Mitigation measure: SFH considers exclusion based on disability, gender or social status as strong protection concern and will be mitigated through the following approach: a) In the mobilization phase of the program, SFH will ensure by informing the community that persons affected with disability and those marginalized are by default the prioritized groups. b) SFH will undertake direct implementation of the program without any intermediary or third party contractor to ensure inclusive service delivery with the support of the district Health and Nutrition committees and AAP committees representing persons with disabilities, marginalized groups and women. c) Through the mobile teams SFH will ensure person mobility impairment are reached. d) SFH will facilitate installation of appropriate hand bars and disability friendly removable seat at the Nutrition facility latrines. e) To accommodate persons with impaired hearing, vision or mental health, SFH will provide information about the availability of services through both verbal or written forms. f) SFH will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the Nutrition Centers and services are accessible to all members of the community, including persons with disability. g) SFH will collect gender, age and diversity sensitive data to ensure all age groups, disability and genders access inclusive and quality nutrition services. h) Where applicable SFH will coordinate with other actors in Lower Juba such as Humanity & Inclusion (Handicap International) who are specialized in working with persons with impaired mobility and disability to provide data of such individual to improve service delivery4.2: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. : Mitigation: a) SFH has strict policy on Prevention of Sexual exploitation and abuse that guides our humanitarian programming and assistance. In this policy, every new staff upon recruitment is required to sign PSEA policy, organizational code of conducts and Child protection statement to ensure strict adherence/compliance and its violation will lead to disciplinary actions including termination of employment contract. b) No 1 to 1 engagement will children and both fixed and mobile staff are gender dis-aggregated and women with special cases attended by female staff. c) Waiting areas will be separate for male and female at the Nutrition centers. d) Separate examination rooms with female nurse or doctors will be established.
