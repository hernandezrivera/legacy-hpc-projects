Project Summary

The proposed action aims to reduce the morbidity and mortality of vulnerable populations affected by the crisis in the Batibo health district in the NW region by providing them with humanitarian assistance.
Concretely, the project aims at guaranteeing access to health care and nutrition and quality medical care through the provision of fixed medical care in the Saint Joseph Catholic Health Centre, Widikum. The project will thus support the pediatric and maternity services and the Intensive Therapeutic Feeding Centre (ITFC) of Saint Joseph Catholic Health Centre. In addition, the project also aims at ensuring the provision of primary health care, through the establishment of mobile clinics, to people who are displaced and/or far from health centers while ensuring referrals in case of complications to St. Joseph's Catholic Health Centre. They will be deployed in the health areas of Bifang, Laringi, Eka and Batibo urban and will offer a complete package by providing curative and preventive care.
On the other hand, the project aims at overall capacity building through human resources (support and training), the supply of medicines and medical consumables, and the upgrading of the structures. Finally, the project aims at providing support to the national health system, particularly to the Health District of Batibo, in the response to COVID-19.
The project will provide access to health care for 46,497 people among whom are 9,299 children aged 0-59 months and 10,964 women of childbearing age; and will be carried out in three areas:
Axis 1: Mobile strategy (Mobile Clinic)
- Consultations and management of simple cases: primary health care, management of severe acute malnutrition without complications;
- Reproductive health promotion: prenatal consultation, post-natal consultation;
- Supply of drugs and therapeutic inputs;
- Implementation of infection prevention and control measures;
- Referral system for complicated cases between the mobile clinic and the management structures.
Axis 2: Fixed strategy (Saint Joseph's Catholic Health Centre)
- Training of health personnel;
- Management of complicated cases of children with severe acute malnutrition, and pediatric complications;
- Providing skilled delivery services for pregnant women and management of pregnancy complications including Cesarean sections;
- Provision of mental health consultations;
- Support in additional human resources;
- Supply of drugs and biomedical equipment;
- Implementation of a COVID-19 sorting circuit
- Provision of personal protective equipment for health personnel;
- Implementation of infection prevention and control measures.
Axis 3: Prevention
- Raising awareness of the population on essential family practices
- Raising public awareness on barrier measures against COVID-19
- Training of mothers and women of childbearing age in the Mother MUAC screening and Infant Young Child Feeding method
The direct beneficiaries of care will be selected on the basis of medical criteria and humanitarian needs. No discrimination will be made on the basis of gender, place of residence, ethnic, religious or political affiliation.
The primary health care delivery project cannot be gender-specific. It is the state of health that will be at the center of any action. However, the objective of each training will be to integrate a large number of women. In this sense, the PB-mothers training is itself a module reserved primarily for women, since their mission is to detect malnutrition in their children.
The project was designed with the local health authorities, which made it possible to identify and propose interventions targeted at the most urgent needs. Regular meetings with the health authorities will be organized to discuss the progress of the project and, if necessary, the reorientations to be made. The mobile clinics will serve as a channel for feedback and perception of the beneficiaries in order to allow the readjustment of messages and activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the Batibo health district, the persistence of insecurity and recurrent attacks on health structures limit the supply and access of the population to quality health care. This situation has several consequences. (1) Population displacements put significant pressure on basic social services. At the end of october 2020, 705 800 internally displaced persons, 360 500 returnees and 61 300 refugees in neighboring Nigeria had been registered (2) Access to health care, including emergency care, is made difficult by the destruction and non-functionality of health centers. At least 7 health centers are closed and some have been damaged (burned or destroyed). Inaccessibility is accentuated for IDP, 45% live more than 5 km from a health facility. Human resources left the premises (3) Access to health care is limited due to the cost recovery system for communities that have lost income (4) The NWSW regions are leading into a situation of acute food insecurity (Phase 3 of the IPC) according to the November 2020 FEWS (5) The nutritional situation is also a problem. The nutrition Cluster estimates that 8,000 boys and girls under the age of 5 are likely to develop severe acute malnutrition in NWSW regions by January 2021(6) The resurgence of epidemics adds to the burden of inaccessibility to care. A cholera epidemic declared since February 16, 2020 is still present in the Southwest with 73 cases notified for 1 hospital death as of July 3, 2020. (7) Cameroon is hit by the COVID-19 epidemic. As of 2 december 2020, the epidemiological situation reports 24 560 confirmed cases and 441 deaths, and the NW regions registers 959 cases with 37 deaths. ALIMA proposes to act on 4 axes of intervention:1) The provision of medical and nutritional assistance to people affected by the conflict by supporting the Saint Joseph Hospital for medical care. ALIMA will support, in terms of human and material resources, the pediatric service (for the treatment of serious pathologies), emergency and maternity services and will make available the essential equipment and the inputs. ALIMA will setting up a treatment unit for inpatient management of SAM children with medical complications. GBV cases will be taken into account with the pre-positioning of PEP kits, and ALIMA will ensure the medical care of victims. Caregivers of under-5 children will receive training in the early detection malnutrition through the Mother-MUAC approach, as well as awareness sessions on essential family practices.2) The strengthening of access to health care for remote and displaced populations through the establishment of mobile clinics. In the health areas of Bifang, Laringi, Eka and Batibo urban, where the health centers are not functional, mobile clinics will be set up to reach and provide primary health care to populations who no longer have access to basic health services. They will offer preventive and curative outpatient care, as well as the reproductive health package. The mobile clinics are composed of 3 nurses, a midwife and 10 community health workers. They will also ensure the referral of complicated cases to St. Joseph's: SAM cases with complications and pediatric and obstetric emergencies. They will conduct community-based health promotion activities and linkage with health care. 3) Supporting the response to the COVID-19 epidemic. This will focus on the implementation of barrier measures, support in the establishment of a triage system in Saint Joseph Hospital , staff training, provision of PPE and hydroalcoholic solution; provision of IPC equipment (hand washing devices, hygiene products...); sensitization of the population. On the mobile clinics, the teams will be equipped with PPE; hand washing and disinfection points will be set up, social distancing measures will be observed and the teams will conduct awareness sessions on barrier measures. 4) Evacuation of surgical emergencies to the Widikum hospital to the hospitals with an adapted technical platform in Bamenda.
