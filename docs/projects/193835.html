1 - Needs

Diabetic Foot Ulcers are significant health and socioeconomic problem, having negative effects on the quality of life and imposing a heavy economic burden on the patient and the community. By 2030, the global estimate of diabetic patients is expected to rise to over 552 million. In Palestine, few studies indicated that the prevalence of DM in Palestine was 9.6% in females and 10.0% in males. In Palestine, the percentage of diabetic patients varies between 14-18% of which 18% are in the green line, 15-17% are in the West Bank, and 14-16% are in the Gaza Strip.The number of formally registered Diabetic patients in the Gaza Strip is approximately 142 thousand whereas, the actual number exceeds 180 thousand patients. Left untreated, diabetes can cause major health complications, including a higher risk for bone and joint disorders, retinopathy, cardiovascular diseases, and peripheral artery disease. As the Gaza Health care system has been struggling for more than 15 years because of the protracted crises that increase mortality and morbidity rates, non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are one of the leading causes of death in the Gaza Strip. Thus, one of the highest priority areas in the NDC is complications such as Diabetic Foot (DF).As per Al Shifa hospital statistics for the year 2021, the number of diabetic foot patients in Gaza was between 6000-7000 patients of which 35% are female and 65% are male. Most of the patients were in the age range of 40-60 years old (65%).Between 80-85% of diabetic foot patients need to be hospitalized for a period between 20 to 30 days 2 -3 times per year.It is worth mentioning that yearly 3000-4000 new diabetic foot cases are discovered.Unfortunately, 6-10% of the cases face amputation whereas more than 70% suffer from cardiac disease.Diabetic Foot patients among Diabetes Mellitus (DM) patients is around 6% which is around 3,306. Adding to that; Men who have complications of DF are 2 times higher than women while age disaggregation in DF patients is as follows: 65% of DF ages are between 40-60 years old while 13% ages between 30-40, and only 5% ages are between 20-30 years old.According to the Diabetic Foot Unit (DFU), report 2021, DF patients have high morbidity and high disability rates as amputations for lower limbs are 6.8% while Toes amputations are 34.6%.Half of DF patients have Ulcers in their forefoot and toes while 35% have ulcers in the midfoot and 15 % have ulcers in the hindfoot (Heel foot).According to the round table of the NCD group during the HNO workshop, DF complications don’t only require multidisciplinary medical services, but also such patients need MHPSS, PT and nutrition services to cope positively and have better life quality. 50% out of the DF people need follow-up where they suffer from accessibility and affordability of services.In coordination with MOH, the Diabetic Foot Unit (DFU), Ahli Arab Hospital has conducted 2 FGDs with men and women who have DF where women were 12 and men were 18. Unfortunately, all of them were unemployed, depending on people's kindness and sickness is a burden not only to the patients but also to their families. While women with DF are a third of the total number of patients, they looked like they were having a more difficult life. They expressed their need to have antibiotics and medicine when they have infections as well as having proper dressings, hospitalization, nutrition awareness and transportation. An 18-year-old girl said, “I missed my childhood as a diabetic patient. I was not allowed to walk outside the home alone as I have short sight, impaired vision, because of the DM”. She also mentioned that the family doesn’t have enough money to cover her expenses, DF dressings and transportation. While she showed tears in her eyes, she said my father can barely provide food for my 6 little brothers and sisters. She also said that my mother doesn’t have time to stay overnight with me in the hospital and I can’t go on my own to areas where men only provide services, and I don’t feel comfortable when men touch me.Men were not much luckier as many expressed their need for crushes and aid devices after amputations. A 45-year-old man said that he can’t afford transportation to Shifa hospital where the DFU stays. He compromised doing dressings at home or pharmacies with lesser quality than what the unit provides because he can’t always afford transportation. Another 52- year man said that public transportation drivers refuse to transport him because of the wheelchair, and he can’t afford private cars most of the time. He mentioned that I make the dressing myself then the doctor blames me. He also indicated “I thought to end my life many times to stop such suffering, but I remembered that God is there for help”.In a meeting, Dr Taneh, the head of DFU at the Shifa hospital, said that 80-85 % of the DF patients need hospitalization. He also mentioned, “I feel sad when I discharge patients who need hospitalization because there are not enough beds to accommodate them”. He also indicated that there is a big shortage of medicine and medical supplies to appropriately cover the need. He designated the need for MDT for DF cases that includes orthopaedic, surgical, vascular, nursing care and PT to deal with such cases. He ensured that there is no national protocol to deal with DF cases and that doctors depend on their experiences. In another meeting with Dr Ismail Jadbeh, a retired vascular surgeon who used to work with such cases, he said the DFU in Shifa is a part of the surgical vascular unit; it must be a separate place to minimize infections. He also said “I prefer to see it at one of the Gaza hospitals that can accommodate not just surgical teams, but also other services like nutritionist which is crucial for DF patients.Lastly, the MOH, cooperative international department and the NGO coordination unit have proven the need for an NGO hospital that can carry part of the load for DF patients.
