Project Summary

This project responds to critical humanitarian and early recovery needs in the Um Dukuhn Locality in Central Darfur, Kornoi in North Darfur and El Genina Locality in West Darfur States. The overall objective of the project is to enhance the sustainable and socio-economic reintegration of returnees by creating conducive environment for their peaceful co-existence with other community members, strengthening community-based protection structures and enhancing opportunities for sustainable livelihoods. It will strengthen the self-reliance of returnees and host community members alike and facilitate durable solutions for displaced populations therefore ensuring sustained peace and stability in the target areas. The project will also promote environmentally sustainable natural resource management whilst resolving natural resource conflicts through the strengthening of local collective institutions as well as community-based conflict resolution mechanisms.

Needs

The project will target Four (4) communities in Umdukhun locality; three (3) communities in Beiada locality; and three (3) communities in Kornoi locality. According to recent inter agency assessments conducted, Um Dukuhn has witnessed one of the highest numbers of returnees in Sudan since 2013 and reaching its peak in 2017, with 72,445 people reported. Since 2015, over 80,387 people have returned to the locality, many into already devastated communities. The influx puts extra pressure to an already tense conflict environment where access to land and other natural resources continue to spark local conflicts. UNDP, under the SHF is currently piloting activities within three communities in the locality. However, the needs are high whilst several other communities that are at the flash point of relapse into conflict have not been targeted. This project will consolidate the work already done and expand into other communities.UN joint assessments in the locality indicate that over thousands of people have returned to the Genina locality. The majority of people have returned from Chad but some IDP returnees have also been registered. The influx has been influenced by a number of factors including improvements in the general security situation and food assistance being phased out at the sites of displacement in Chad. The continued influx of returnees and other displaced people into these localities is gradually stretching the carrying capacity of the area resulting in mounting tensions that are likely to result in full blown conflicts if not intervened. For this reason, the need to intervene in this locality is now critical. To date, response to the situation remains scant based on information from assessments and our ground understanding of the context.The Kornoi Locality continue to receive increasing number of returnees into already desiccated areas with water scarcity and difficult agro-ecological regime; food insecurity, limited access to basic services, therefore creating limited opportunities for livelihood recovery. To date, over 17,000 people have returned to Kornoi locality bringing additional pressure and competition to the already scant socio-economic opportunities, basic services and natural resources. UNDP has been working in 5 communities the Kornoi Locality where the organization has supported livelihoods activities, rehabilitation of infrastructure and the promotion of security and peace-building activities. However, several locations remain unsupported. This project will build on the on-going work on ground and expand to the other locations where early recovery support has remained critical. This project therefore responds to the above critical humanitarian and early recovery needs in the Um Dukuhn Locality in Central Darfur, Kornoi in North Darfur and El Genina Locality in West Darfur States. It aims to strengthen the self-reliance of returnees and host community members and facilitate durable solutions for displaced populations.
