Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide food assistance and livelihood support to 1,500,000 vulnerable populations in the former states of Greater Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Greater Bhar el Ghazal. The project will implement activities that will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies of target beneficiaries in IPC 5, 4 and 3. The project will also seek to enhance emergency food production and contribute to the reduction of dependency to food and agricultural inputs. World Vision will adopt a mix of intervention approaches including but not limited to unconditional and GFD, cash transfers, complementary livelihood support, capacity building (e.g. on agronomic practices), and resilience building at the household level. The proposed project will also be implemented in synergy with other sectors implemented by World Vision in the target locations such as health, nutrition, WASH, gender and protection related programming.

1 - Needs

An estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population), in August 2019, are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). In the projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve as seasonal harvests become available. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity.Through the 2020 humanitarian response plan, World Vision intends to reach the conflict affected and other vulnerable populations in the former states of Greater Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Greater Bhar el Ghazal with food assistance, emergency food production and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. The food security situation in the targeted counties are very critical in terms of food consumption, dietary diversity, reduced coping strategies, level of hunger and livelihood coping strategies. Overall, an estimated 655,000 people, representing 47.8 percent of the population in the former Upper Nile state will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) during the harvest period. The IDPs, Refugee, returnees, and host communities are particularly vulnerable and many of them reported having switched to negative coping strategies which include both selling their assets and decreasing the number of meals per day per person.Despite the recent stability allowing the beginning of return movement, the situation remains critical for the population of the Greater Bahr El Ghazal State. The last IPC released in September 2019 classified GBG in crisis (IPC 3) with an estimation of 440,000 persons, representing 68% of the State population, expected to suffer from food insecurity in the coming months, and clearly identified as “likely be at least one phase worse without current or programmed humanitarian assistance”. In addition, even if markets start to be resupplied, the lack of productive asset especially for the women head of HH, in charge of the livelihood of their family, remains a barrier for the poorest.World Vision has been implementing Conditional Food Distribution together with nutrition supplement in the target locations and still there are needs to continue supporting to meet their immediate food needs. Cash Based Interventions (CBI) are also on going on some of these areas to create short term employment and income. However, there is a need of restoration of livelihood and there may be a risk of returnee’s influx which require appropriate recovery support. World Vision will also initiate community cohesion and mobilization, empowerment of women and youth development as priority in addressing tensions between refugees and host communities that influences refugee-host community relations, limiting the protection space and opportunities for interdependence in the area of operation. Such tensions result from use of land, natural resources, firewood collection, access to medical, educational and infrastructure facilities, family disputes, overstretched absorption capacity of conflict affected communities. World Vision will also address the emerging needs for resilience and development assistance to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks.
