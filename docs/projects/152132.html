Project Summary

WFP response will support a preventative nutrition approach that delivers both nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive interventions to prevent and manage malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations with a focus on children under five years and women of reproductive age, and emphasizing the critical 1,000 Days ‘window of opportunity’.
WFP’s prevention activities, through blanket supplementary feeding (BSFP), and treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) through TSFP will be integrated and aligned with WFP’s food assistance, including social behavior change communication (SBCC) activities.
WFP will also collaborate with federal and state-level institutions, partners and communities to deliver a multi-sectoral preventative package that targets children 6-23 months, PLW and mothers/caregivers with fortified complementary foods and cash transfers through nutrition-sensitive and specific activities integrated with health, education, WASH, food security and livelihood responses.
Joint multi-sectoral assessments and Emergency Food Security Analysis will be conducted regularly to monitor the nutrition, food security, and markets situation in crisis-affected states. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. A gender-transformative approach that reflects the needs of women, men, girls and boys will be adopted and affected populations (women and men) will be consulted throughout the programme cycle.

Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education and basic services. Of the 1.76 million people that remain displaced in the three most affected states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe), nearly 80 percent are women and children. In recent months, thousands of people in dire need of assistance have newly arrived at areas of relative safety, placing increasing pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their most basic food, nutrition and other needs. The prevalence of malnutrition in Nigeria is unequally distributed across the country with the Northern states disproportionately affected. Micronutrient deficiencies are highly prevalent, particularly in women of reproductive age (15-49 years) and young children, and chronic malnutrition rates are amongst the highest globally. Preliminary findings of the April/May 2018 Nutrition Surveillance Round V showed global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates for children 6 to 59 months reflecting a poor situation in Adamawa State (8.2 percent), and serious situations in Borno (10.1 percent) and Yobe States (12.2 percent). These state figures, however, mask higher rates at regional levels where, for example, the GAM prevalence in Central Yobe is 14.1 percent and in Central Borno is 14.9 percent. The nutrition situation for women of reproductive age (15 to 49 years) remains a concern, as the most currently available data shows acute malnutrition rates of 10 percent in Borno State and 12 percent in Yobe State; only 42 percent and 31 percent of these women met the minimum dietary diversity in Borno and Yobe states, respectively. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production, disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and nutrition needs.

Activities

(1) Provide preventative nutrition activities (BSFP) for children 6-23 months, MAM children 24-59 months and PLW through specialised nutritious foods and SBCC, including community screening, referral and follow-up [contributes to Nutrition sector Objective 1]. In 2019, under this activity it is planned to reach with BSFP up to 127,000 children and 78,000 pregnant and lactating women in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States. Nutrition support will be maximized from the beginning of the year up to the lean season and will decrease to during the final quarter of the year (post-lean season and harvest period). (2) Support facility-based management of MAM (TSFP) for 10,600 MAM children 6-59 months through CMAM continuum of care, specialised nutritious foods and SBCC, including community screening, referral and follow-up [contributes to Nutrition sector Objective 2]. (3) Support a multi-sectoral prevention package for up to 48,400 PLW, adolescent girls, children 6-23 months and other nutritionally vulnerable persons through access to nutritious foods, quality maternal, child and infant care, SBCC, capacity strengthening and income generating activities [contributes to Nutrition sector Objective 1].
