Project Summary

This project aims at increasing chances for women and adolescent girls' access to second-chance education and learning opportunities. The goal of UN Women SCE Project in both Rohingya and Host Community is to ensure that marginalized and displaced young and adult women have access to and achieve quality learning and employment outcomes. UN Women programming is inclusive and aligns to the AGD. The education project considers people living with disability (PWD0, our learning facilities and teaching methodology have been designed to accommodate PWD needs in terms of mobility and learning convenience.

Under JRP 2022, UN Women will continue to provide SCE to women and girls aged 14-25 years old through its five Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs) in Camps 3, 4, 4 extensions, 5, and 18 and through home-based approach, and through has 3 more MPWCs in the Host Communities in Ukhiya, Teknaf and Cox’s Bazar Sadar. MPWCs have been providing safe spaces for women and adolescent girls for accessing learning opportunities and skill building trainings and livelihood activities, as well as psycho-social services, counseling, and referral services for GBV cases. Empowerment through learning and education opportunities are the key components of this project.
UN Women has adapted its global Second Chance Education Programme approach and content to Cox’s Bazar context and harmonized with the learning framework for the Rohingya adolescents. The proposed project builds upon and expands UN Women’s established expertise to address the urgent needs of women and young women in crisis through UN Women’s Leadership, Empowerment, Access & Protection in Crisis Response (LEAP) model to promotes resilience, restore dignity, and provide durable solutions to refugees and displaced women, their families, and communities, including SCE for marginalized and displaced women.
The learning pathways that will be provided include basic literacy (in Burmese and/or English); numeracy; lessons on life skills such as sexual and reproductive health and menstrual hygiene; awareness raising sessions on gender-based violence, negative consequences of early and child marriages, and human trafficking; as well as basic financial literacy, digital literacy (Basic Computer Literacy) and management skills. In the aftermath of the COVID-19 closures on education, there will be a focus on the back to learning campaign for both out-of-school/drop out women and adolescent girls through catch-up learning courses and new learners will be enrolled. Under this project, more learning opportunities in computer literacy will be added to equip adolescent boys and girls with more marketable skills in modern society.
Forecast outcomes include: i) More marginalized women and young women access and benefit from high quality educational content, material and learning pathways; ii) More marginalized women and young women benefit from increased employment, livelihood and entrepreneurial opportunities; iii) Fewer marginalized women and young women are disadvantaged and denied education opportunities due to harmful and discriminatory social norms; iv) More marginalized women and young women have improved access to education and employment pathways through enhanced multi-sectoral collaboration.
UN Women follows participatory, and people centered approaches, it is in this regards that UN Women will replicate its Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM), as part of AAP, to collect feedback from the people of concern and revert through designed interventions, follow up and proper referrals depending on raised issues.

1 - Identified Needs

There is a very high level of illiteracy among Rohingya women – particularly among adolescents who did not have access to education in Myanmar, however, they share same partners as women and adolescent girls from Host Community. The Rohingya community is highly conservative community. Social and culture norms, and restrictions on movement of women and girls limit their access to education and learning opportunities, access to information, assistance, and other services. Amid Covid-19 the existing inequalities have been exacerbating, and the Covid-19 crisis have been disproportionately affecting women and girls, as highlighted in the Rapid Gender Analysis on the Impact of COVID-19 in Cox’s Bazar (conducted by the Gender Hub, UN Women, OXFAM, and Care). Second chance education has been one of the most powerful tools for empowerment of women and girls facing above mentioned hurdles. Education and life skills to marginalized women and girls open livelihood opportunities to them. With education, women and girls change their families’ lives for better, increase self-confidence, and self-respect, increase their status in the community, and take their communities to a higher level. While the education response in the Rohingya crisis response has made significant progress providing safe and systemic access to education opportunities, particularly to children aged 6-14, the Education Sector has identified over 500,000 Rohingya children, adolescents, and youth still in need of education services . Even more alarmingly, 83% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old do not have access to any educational activities. In all age categories (6-14, 15-18, and 19-24), the prevalence of males is more than female in terms of attendance, which further stresses the lack of female education in the Rohingya community. The lack of support for at-home learning will likely lead to many adolescent boys and girls dropping out. Equally, the increase in unpaid care work and the low valued placed on girls’ education will put adolescent girls in a worse situation. Lastly, for adolescents over 15, particularly girls, there are very few genders and age-appropriate learning centers and educational facilities for them.According to UN Women education needs assessment (DCA, April 2020), access to education was widely perceived as a positive, essential need for women and girls foremost because they said that education enhance their ability to adjust to and navigate daily life in the refugee camps. Furthermore, women and girls’ respondents said that educational opportunities increase their agency because it allowed them to gain control over daily functions and contribute to economic opportunities. Caregivers also focused on the role education could play in child rearing (ability to teach their children themselves) while adolescents emphasized the role education could play in securing a positive future (employment, economic opportunity, career). Therefore, UN Women Second Chance Education project aims at contributing to the enhancement of empowerment and resilience of Rohingya women and adolescent girls in the refugee camps and host community through development and delivery of a learning programme to marginalized women and girls aged between 14-25 years old. The learning programme will offer basic literacy (in Burmese and/or English); numeracy; lessons on life skills and basic financial literacy, digital literacy, and business skills. UN Women SCE project’s goal is to ensure that marginalized women and girls have access to and achieve quality learning and employment outcomes. UN Women builds on its knowledge and expertise on gender equality and women’s empowerment and gender analysis proven gaps to provide second chance education from safe space with tailored timetable that enable women and girls to attend to other gender roles.
