Project Summary

For more than a decade, the protracted conflict in the three BAY states of northeast Nigeria has significantly jeopardized education and exposed children and their caregivers to a myriad of physical and psychosocial threats. The 2022 Humanitarian Need Overview underscores the gravity of the situation, revealing that 8.4 million people urgently require assistance, with an alarming 58% projected to be children. The imperative for immediate aid is particularly pronounced for displaced, separated, and unaccompanied children, who face heightened risks of abuse, exploitation, and neglect due to the critical nature of their protection and education needs.
The consequences of the conflict are evident as children, whether excluded from educational opportunities or left to navigate life without adult guidance, increasingly become pawns in various nefarious activities. Exploiting this vulnerability, non-state armed groups utilize children as carriers of explosive devices, leading to a notable surge in the recruitment of children for attacks and acts of terrorism. These children are also exposed to remnants of war, posing additional threats, and encounter rejection, inter-communal tensions, and various stresses upon reintegration.

In response to this multifaceted crisis, the FRAD Foundation is steadfastly engaged in implementing integrated programming in Borno and Yobe states through its Child Protection and Education departments. Embracing a holistic approach, the foundation aims to address the diverse protection concerns confronting children, ensuring their access to education. Through integrated Protection and Education programming, the FRAD Foundation seeks to empower children, addressing their various protection needs and fostering resilience to cultivate a sense of security in their homes and communities.
This integrated project aims to develop a comprehensive framework for addressing the multifaceted challenges faced by conflict-affected populations in the Northeast, with a focus on integrated protection, education, and explosive ordnance risk education (EORE). The project seeks to enhance the resilience of communities by promoting a holistic understanding of protection, encompassing physical safety, psychosocial well-being, and educational empowerment.

1 - Needs

The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) reveals a dire situation in the northeast, where 8.3 million people urgently require humanitarian assistance. Notably, 83% of those in need are women and children, rendering them susceptible to abuse and unforeseen vulnerabilities. Recent flooding during the rainy season has compounded the challenges, displacing families and repurposing schools as shelters for flood-affected households, thereby hindering children's access to learning. Children are confronted with various protection risks, encompassing child labor, sexual abuse, exploitation, resource deprivation, and inadequate access to health care. These threats exacerbate the challenges faced by boys and girls in their homes and communities. Given these complex needs, it is evident that these children require protection from violence, abuse, exploitation, and rights violations. Additionally, they need access to quality education to facilitate continuous learning, along with protection from the risks of landmines both on the way to school and within educational settings. Moreover, children are facing increased threats from explosive remnants of war, posing risks such as limb amputations and displacement, heightening protection concerns. The ongoing impact of climate change, conflict, and persistent displacements and resettlements will likely lead to a further increase in out-of-school children, potentially intensifying child separations. This underscores the urgent need for immediate, integrated case management and psychosocial support to enhance protective factors for these children. The risk of recruitment and kidnapping for adolescent boys and girls remains elevated due to the breakdown of family and community protective mechanisms. Conflict-affected children and their caregivers have endured brutal violence, loss of family members, friends, and property, resulting in accumulated stress, deteriorating living conditions, increased military presence, social disruption, and restricted access to vital social services like schools and healthcare. These experiences significantly impact the development of children and the psychosocial well-being of adolescents and caregivers, potentially leading to reluctance to attend school. To address these multifaceted challenges, the proposed project aims to support vulnerable children and those residing in conflict-affected locations and newly returned areas. Key interventions include providing critical education services, offering support to caregivers to manage stress, employ positive coping mechanisms, and practice positive parenting. Additionally, the project will deliver case management services to children with identified protection concerns, ensuring their safety in schools and communities. Furthermore, mine risk education will be provided to children in schools and safe spaces, equipping them to identify and avoid landmines. Assistance will also be extended to children and women who are victims of explosive ordinances. Through these comprehensive initiatives, the project seeks to alleviate the immediate and long-term impacts of conflict, displacement, and environmental challenges on the well-being of children and their caregivers in the northeast.

2 - Activities

Child Protection Case Management: FRAD aims to deliver comprehensive case management services for vulnerable children, encompassing Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), those at risk, and those who have faced abuse, neglect, or exploitation. SAFE/SHLS Establishment: FRAD will create four Safe and Healing Learning Spaces (SHLS) for children in two communities of return, while also rehabilitating safe spaces within communities. These areas will be utilized for SHLS activities, including Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE). SAFE Adolescent Children Support: Following the completion of the SAFE Curriculum and life skills program, FRAD Foundation will provide support to adolescent children through small grants. These grants will enable boys and girls to continue their education while generating modest income to sustain their schooling. SAFE Caregivers CAFAAG Support: FRAD will engage caregivers of adolescent girls involved in SAFE sessions in a caregivers' program. Caregivers of CAAFAG will undergo the Growing Stronger curriculum, specifically designed for caregivers of CAAFAG and other vulnerable communities. Education Back to School Support to Children: FRAD will assist out-of-school children from its case management caseload to re-enter school and address any challenges that may hinder their continued education, with a specific focus on children formerly associated with armed groups. Teachers Training: FRAD Foundation will target school teachers for training in digital skills. This initiative will equip teachers with modern skills, enabling them to access resources and use modern technologies to support and facilitate learning. The training will include practical simulations of EdTech software, fostering emergency learning and inclusive education for persons with disabilities. Scholastic Materials Support: FRAD will provide enrolled children with essential school materials to enhance their learning experience, foster interest in education, and encourage ongoing school attendance. Community Learning Support: FRAD will develop a model to support children's learning within their communities. Trained Community Volunteers will facilitate small group learning for both out-of-school and in-school children, fostering interest in education and encouraging enrollment. Advocacy Visits: FRAD will regularly conduct advocacy visits to influential traditional leaders, ministry heads, and religious leaders. The goal is to advocate for increased enrollment of children in schools. Mine Action EORE Sessions: FRAD will conduct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) sessions with children in schools and safe spaces to ensure they are aware of and protected from explosive ordinances. These sessions will extend to communities, training beneficiaries on EORE. Support to Victims of Explosive Ordinances: FRAD will provide livelihood support to victims of explosive ordinances and offer equipment assistance to those who have become disabled due to such incidents. Training of Trainers on EORE: FRAD will conduct Training of Trainers targeting key stakeholders who will continue to administer EORE training to communities and schools, ensuring long-term sustainability.

Goal of the HDP Package: The overarching goal of the HDP package is to strengthen the resilience of conflict-affected populations and advance towards voluntary and safe durable solutions. This comprehensive approach seeks to address immediate humanitarian needs, support sustainable development, and contribute to peace-building efforts, fostering lasting positive effects on the affected people. Components of the HDP Package: Humanitarian Component:Goal: Provide immediate relief and protection to conflict-affected populations.Key Actors: Humanitarian organizations, NGOs, and UN agencies engaged in delivering emergency assistance.Interaction with the Project: The project aligns with the humanitarian component by addressing urgent child protection needs, ensuring access to education, and mitigating the immediate impact of explosive remnants of war.Development Component:Goal: Support long-term development initiatives that enhance livelihoods and well-being.Key Actors: Development agencies, governmental bodies, and community-based organizations focused on sustainable development.Interaction with the Project: The project contributes to the development component by providing education support, livelihood assistance, and fostering community learning. By enhancing education and psychosocial support, the project contributes to creating a more stable and harmonious environment.Key Actors in the HDP Nexus: UN Agencies: Coordination with UN agencies such as UNICEF for child protection and education initiatives.Governmental Bodies: Collaboration with government ministries responsible for education, social services, and peace-building efforts.NGOs and Humanitarian Organizations: Partnership with NGOs for humanitarian assistance and development-focused organizations for sustainable initiatives.Community Leaders and Volunteers: Engagement with local leaders and volunteers for community-based initiatives, including learning support and awareness campaigns.Project's Interaction with the HDP Nexus: The project serves as a critical component within the HDP package by addressing immediate child protection needs, providing education support, and integrating mine risk education. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure effective protection mainstreaming in the project, several key modalities will be implemented: Child Protection Case Management:Identification and Prioritization: The project will employ a robust child protection case management system to identify and prioritize vulnerable children, including those who are unaccompanied, separated, or at risk of abuse and exploitation.Tailored Support: Individualized plans will be developed to address the specific protection needs of each child, ensuring a holistic and tailored approach to support.Safe and Healing Learning Spaces (SHLS):Protection-Focused Curriculum: The SHLS activities will incorporate a protection-focused curriculum, addressing psychosocial needs, life skills, and resilience-building elements to create a safe and supportive learning environment.Regular Monitoring: Ongoing monitoring will be conducted to assess the well-being of children in SHLS, with a specific focus on protection indicators.Mine Action and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE):Targeted Awareness: EORE sessions will be conducted to raise awareness among children and communities about the risks associated with explosive remnants of war. This includes educating them on how to identify and avoid landmines, reducing the risk of accidents.Support for Victims: The project will provide support, including livelihood assistance, to victims of explosive ordinances, with a specific focus on protecting and assisting those who have become disabled as a result.Community Learning Support:Inclusive Learning Environment: The community learning support model will be designed to create an inclusive learning environment that considers the diverse needs of both out-of-school and in-school children, ensuring protection considerations are integrated.Community Volunteer Training: Community volunteers will receive training on protection principles and child safeguarding to enhance their ability to create a protective and supportive learning space.Advocacy and Stakeholder Engagement:Sensitization and Advocacy: FRAD will conduct advocacy visits to engage traditional leaders, ministry heads, and religious leaders. The focus will be on advocating for increased enrollment and creating an environment that promotes the protection and education of children.Collaborative Approaches: Collaborative efforts with key stakeholders, including government bodies and NGOs, will ensure protection considerations are mainstreamed into broader initiatives.Monitoring and Feedback Mechanisms:Accountability Framework: FRAD will establish an accountability and feedback mechanism that incorporates community participation. Regular monitoring will include protection-specific indicators to track the well-being of children and the effectiveness of protection measures.Consultative Approach: The project will employ a consultative approach to gather feedback from the community, ensuring that protection concerns are heard and addressed promptly.These modalities collectively reinforce the project's commitment to protection mainstreaming, embedding protection considerations into all aspects of the project to safeguard the well-being of children and vulnerable populations in conflict-affected areas.
