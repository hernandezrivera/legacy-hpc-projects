Project Summary

The goal of the proposed project is to enhance the protection environment and access to GBV services for conflict-affected women and adolescent girls with a focus on the most vulnerable individuals (e.g. children, elderly and single-headed households and people with disabilities (PWDs)) through supporting a women and girls safe space in Orm Aljouz in idleb, and jendares in ifreen, Syria. The proposed project is based on supporting a safe environment for women and adolescent girls, where they feel physical and psychological safe (absence of trauma, excessive pressure, violence and fear) and is free to express themselves without fear of discrimination or any kind of harm. The safe space aims at targeting both: Big Orm and jendares, and its neighbouring communities, and the activities will include IDPs and people from the host communities.
The conceived outcomes are the following:
1. Conflict-affected women and girls receive preventive and responsive specialized GBV services.
2. The most vulnerable and crisis-affected women and girls are empowered with the skills required to survive and take care of themselves (and their families) in times of conflict.
The project contributes to the providing of quality services and measures for GBV, and develops appropriate services to prevent and reduce risks related to GBV.
This project provides specialized GBV services, both preventive and responsive services for women and girls survivors -or at risk- of GBV.
The technical staff will be trained on: PFA, PSS, basic concepts of GBV, SOPs, case management, referral techniques, and life skills.
The Center will include a specialist in dealing with cases of GBv and GBV case management. There will be also outreach team of psychosocial workers and referral workers.
The safe space consists of a well-constructed and structurally secure building, the building is not a military target, and it is uninhabited by men. The building consists of six rooms with a meeting room and a garden surrounded by a suitable wall, and it is not exposed to the main road.
The safe space is divided into the following sections:
1. Two large activity rooms to accommodate 20 girls and women;
2. Special room to provide case management services and individual extension services;
3. room dedicated to the care of children accompanying their mothers;
4. Room for emergency services;
5. A meeting room to conduct the staff meeting and beneficiaries’ workshops.
In addition, the centre will conduct awareness activities for girls and women on risks of GBV within the centre, to prevent and mitigate the risks of GBV.
Furthermore, the outreach team will provide preventive and responsive services for women and girls outside the centre activities.
The Center will be provided with a special treasury to assure confidentiality of information by saving the cases files, and training of case workers and specialists, while maintaining strict confidentiality and privacy measures of the situation and the placement of a special code for each case and its preservation in the treasury.
The organization will also establish a committee of beneficiaries selected by direct election. This committee will contribute to leading the center and selecting appropriate activities according to the wishes and ages of the beneficiaries. The committee will also have a role to lead the center at the end of the project period and has the right to communicate with the interested parties working in the area to sustain the project activities even by transferring the project to another organization or by extending the project activities.

Needs

Assessments have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress and limits their movements. Additionally, various means of technology—e.g. texting and social media—are used to sexually harass and facilitate early/forced marriages. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtails their rights, with widows who have experienced divorce and widowhood and adolescent girls disproportionately impacted. The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence means that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Girls are reportedly being married younger, with early/forced marriage- sometimes involving girls of 10 or 11 years old - framed as a protective coping strategy. The socio-economic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty are ultimately leading to women and girls to be at higher risks of all forms of violence including sexual exploitation and abuse. with women and girls’ safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training).Amal has made a Rapid Assessment of the current situation in “Orm Aljouz in idleb, and jendares in ifreen” area.Focused groups discussions were held in a number of residential communities’ area, where the questions were related to the needs of women in the current conditions in the area.The most important problems facing women and girls were as follows:Most women and girls are deprived of basic rights such as education, work opportunities and access to other available services, where men prevent their daughters from getting education under the pretext of fear. In addition to the deprivation of inheritance, which was relatively existed in Syria, has remarkable increased in conditions of war because of many reasons as the absence of law, which can be a main one of them.Women also referred to their fears of sexual harassment and abduction, this is limiting their mobility and affecting their freedom of movement. In addition, there is the problem of early marriage, which also included forced marriage where parents seek their daughters to get married at an early age because of lack of awareness and poverty.Under these circumstances, the problem of violence is increasing, as women have pointed out in FGDs, and the majority of violence cases are classified domestic.Women indicated that the most vulnerable were "people with special needs" who had been subjected to physical violence and emotional abuse and had no mobility or access to services.Followed by a group of "widows and divorcees" who do not have a breadwinner and have no career to help them work and women who lead families. This underscores the importance of safe spaces for women and girls to help them engage in the activities and facilitate access safe services.

Implementation of activities

1- Provide specialised GBV services to survivors:- GBV Case Management and PSS Counselling within the supported safe spaces- awareness raising sessions will have a structured curriculum which aims to lead women and girls through self-confidence improvement and self-awareness raising sessions, which will include topics on GBV, Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), and psychosocial distress. Basic courses will also be provided to guide women out of potentially dangerous situations. After these sessions, women and girls can approach a counsellor to confidentiality disclose and receive, non-identifying case management for Gender-Based Violence and Sexual Gender-Based Violence.- Provide referral services if needed, for women & girls (Host community and IDPs) in the safe space.- Conduct Life skills sessions for women and girls, and GBV prevention activities (Host community and IDPs)2- Provide empowerment and awareness raising activities for women and girls using the GBV SC Toolkit- The empowerment activities include life-skills and recreational activities for a number of the most vulnerable/at risk women identified3 - Provide GBV programming with focus activities for adolescent girlsthe methodology we will be using is my safety my wellbeing curriculum for adolescent girls, as special times will be set for adolescent activities within the WGSS (Timing and dates will be selected after consulting the adolescent girls and considering that if any of them are attending schools this will not effect that). from our previous experience we didn't had a lot of married adolescent girls for that we are not planning for a specific activities but if we were able to encourage them to attend we will specify a specific time for them or refer them to an organization with a specific program for married adolescent girls.Conduct PSS sessions and GBV services for women & girls (Host community and IDPs) in the safe space.- Conduct structured group sessions that address elements of Psychological first aid (PFA), self-protection, GBV prevention, Early Marriage, and domestic violence, in the Safe Space.- Open-day events with local community (Women workshops, recreational activities, discussions, competitions, self-confidence and leadership-enhancement activities).- Provide support for children accompanying women through setting up a safe space for children.- Conduct structured Outreach visits that address elements of Psychological first aid (PFA), self-protection, GBV prevention, Early Marriage, and domestic violence, through mobile team.4- Monitoring and evaluation team at Amal will undertake the following:Data will be installed by the data entry officer and analyzed by MEAL officer in Turkey, then results will be included in the final MEAL report- Evaluation: it is proposed that project evaluation will be conducted in the last month of implementation and should be carried out jointly by all stakeholders, including Amal and beneficiaries. The evaluation process will ensure completion of project implementation and adherence to the time frame set by AmalThe evaluation will include an evaluation at the beginning of the project to ensure safe space readiness to start the project and a mid-project evaluation to ensure that the work is proceeding according to the time and financial plan by Amal and the final and post-final evaluation to ensure the impact of the project on the beneficiaries.The M & E officer will prepare a model to measure the satisfaction of the project beneficiaries and the employee satisfaction modelAMAL M&E team is also in charge of the complaint mechanism that will be set up at the initial stage of the project. The WhatsApp phone number will be widely disseminated in the areas identified for the implementation of the activities. Complaints will be received by AMAL. AMAL will mention the quantitative number and type of complaints received regarding this specific project in reports.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services.2. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging 2. Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 2. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities3. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High- Mitigating Measure: Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point

Humanitarian actors suspend their funding to those locations: Likelihood: Medium Impact: highMitigation Measure: Train the committee of women to lead and being able to map the resources available in the community to run the basic group activity and donate any equipment available to this committee to support them doing this role
