Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen a rights-based protection response for IDPs and non-IDP communities, as well as refugee returnees, in particular those most vulnerable through stronger advocacy, life-saving protection and assistance activities, evidence-based programming with the focus on developing and building the capacity of affected communities to cope and recover and better position them for the attainment of durable solutions. Improving coordination and quality of assessments and monitoring will also be key components of the project. The project also foresees benefiting the entire displaced population through continuous advocacy and support in the area of Law and Policy, fostering the successful engagement with relevant authorities and partners with a focus on the approval and implementation of the new IDP Law. This activity will be built upon efforts by UNHCR that in 2020 culminated in the Somalia accession to Kampala Convention and development of draft IDP Law that is aimed to domesticate it.
UNHCR will continue to make the best of its key membership of the protection cluster and ensure regular reporting to the clusters’ 5Ws and strong participation and contribution in the different clusters’ working groups to ensure coordination and complementarity of interventions, avoiding duplication with other members and ensuring regular sharing of protection reports with the cluster.
Many IDPs in protracted displacement remain in situations with no progress towards durable solutions and suffer from systemic marginalization. Access to life-saving assistance and basic services, such as education and health, exclusion from services provided by government are other factors that contribute to heightened protection risks faced by displaced and other conflict-affected populations. Sexual and gender-based violence also remains a critical protection concern for IDPs. A significant proportion of IDPs are members of minority groups lacking the protection of major clans. COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated pre-existing risks and vulnerabilities. In Somalia internally displaced persons (IDPs) have been facing despicable acts of man-made violence including forced evictions and natural hazards which have led to multiple displacement and limited access to durable solution opportunities. Many newly displaced IDPs still remain in need of assistance. Many of the IDPs reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. The basic needs of IDPs are often not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. Forced evictions remain one of the most severe and prevalent protection threats, particularly for IDPs and poor households, due to an underdeveloped regulatory framework, hampered access to justice, low awareness of and respect for HLP rights, and a weak protective environment. It is anticipated that opening of the borders and lifting some of the COVID-19 travel restrictions can lead to an increase of number of returns as compared to 2020 that will bring overall number of returnees in the Somalia that will be in need of variety of protection services to 109,989. Many returnees choose not to return to their areas of origin, preferring to settle in urban areas. Many of these contribute to and suffer the results of pressure on basic services, further compounded by recurrent influxes of IDPs. Any intervention will be vulnerability and needs based. While there is increased effort to promote sustainable return and re-integration, a substantial number of returnees have limited access to basic services, precarious or no physical security and are constantly exposed to myriad protection risks that proposed project will seek to address. The project includes a sustainability/handover component where feasible, particularly in the areas of Law and Policy, as well as community mobilization and NNGOs engagement.

Project Activities

UNHCR protection intervention will be guided by HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy and relevant frameworks for the protection, assistance and solutions to IDPs. The project will contribute all HRP Strategic Objectives as it aims to meet immediate lifesaving needs of vulnerable persons through the provision specialized assistance services (SO1 and Cluster Objective 1); ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to essential services (SO2 and Cluster Objectives 2 and 3); strengthen protection environment with due consideration to protection mainstreaming (SO3 and Custer Objective 4). In particular, UNHCR will implement the following key activities that will contribute to the achievement of the aforementioned HRP Strategic Objectives and cluster specific objectives: (i) protection assessments and monitoring to assess protection risks and vulnerabilities, needs and capacities of different groups in line with age, gender and diversity mainstreaming approach (SO1,2,3; CO1,2,3,4); (ii) UNHCR will continue working with partners for its Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), which has a country-wide coverage and is one of the key sources of displacement trends in Somalia, protection risks, vulnerabilities, human rights violations, including evictions monitoring and those related to COVID-19 situation, of the displaced communities. PRMN will continue to contribute to Protection Cluster Somalia Protection Monitoring System (SPMS). UNHCR will ensure that PRMN results are widely used for advocacy and design of the response interventions by humanitarian community. PRMN will also continue to be used as a protection monitoring tool on refugee returnee movements (SO1,2,3; CO1,2,3,4); (iii) UNHCR will strengthen the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites and ensure equitable access to services and assistance to persons in need also provided at the individual/household level with a clear focus of moving towards attaining durable solutions. This will also contribute to peaceful co-existence between different groups in the community, ensure community engagement, ownership and sustainability of intervention (SO1,2,3; CO1,2,3,4); (iv) UNHCR will provide the support to community management structures on protection principles and promotion of age, gender, diversity mainstreaming approaches in the management structures, assessment and service delivery to the affected communities (SO1,2,3; CO1,2,3,4). (v) prevention and response services to SGBV survivors through provision of targeted assistance and support, including through referrals with relevant service providers (SO1,2; CO1,2,3); (vi) provision of specialized services and support to the most vulnerable individuals according to their identified needs, including through direct assistance provision, referrals to service providers, as well as cash interventions to address needs identified (SO1,2; CO1,2,3); (vii) capacity building of the government authorities, sector partners and the community management structures on IDP rights and guiding principles on internal displacement as well as coordination/sector system(SO3; CO4); (viii) advocacy for domestication of Kampala Convention and development of national IDP legislation that is in line with international standards. To ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights, UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws and advocacy with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters (SO3; CO4). Identification of people with disabilities and disability inclusion will be pursued throughout the project. Qualitative and disaggregated data on disability among the project target population will be collected to have an accurate assessment of disability inclusion and address needs and heightened risks faced by people with disabilities.

Project Indicators

The project will target 1.2 million IDPs with its protection and return monitoring activities. The most vulnerable will benefit from the targeted protection and assistance provided at the individual level and community level (as referred to in the specific cluster objectives and indicators section of this project proposal). It is anticipated that 18,050 refugees will be assisted with return to Somalia in the course of 2021 that will bring the total number of refugee returnees targeted by this project to 109,989 refugee returnees. Such an increase will necessitate the scale up of the area-based projects that should inter alia cover education, WASH, health as well as livelihood specifically in the areas of high return. Such projects to be pursued in addition to the individual and family level support, including in the form of cash-based interventions. All IDP population in Somalia will benefit from UNHCR advocacy and technical support to the Government for domestication of Kampala Convention and development of national IDP legislation that is in line with international standards. Relevant indicators for selected cluster objectives and activities are selected from the menu of pre-set indicators and provided for in the Cluster / Sector section below. Through UNHCR age, gender and diversity mainstreaming approach the persons with disabilities will be consulted and included in all assessments and evaluations, so that that ensure their meaningful participation in the decision-making process on the design of the response programs according to their needs and capacities. UNHCR will also promote participation and representation of persons with disabilities in the community leadership structures. UNHCR will ensure meaningful participation of persons with disabilities in all their diversity, the promotion and mainstreaming of their rights into the work of the Organization, the development of disability-specific programmes and the consideration of disability-related perspectives, in line with UN Disability Inclusion Strategy and Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. This will be done through the consistent and systematic approach to disability inclusion in all areas of programming and intervention.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

UNHCR uses cash-based interventions to provide protection, assistance and services to the most vulnerable. UNHCR has strong experience and existing mechanism, operational standard operating procedures and strategies in planning, designing, implementing and monitoring cash-based interventions. Cash-based interventions as modality of protection delivery, provide dignity, flexibility and choice for persons of concern, as they allow people to decide and address their own needs. Cash-based interventions help to increase protection outcomes by enhancing protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safe and dignified delivery and contributing to protection benefits, in areas such as child protection and SGBV and reducing negative coping mechanisms by meeting basic needs. Moreover, cash-based interventions improve cost efficiency in programming. Particularly in the current situation of COVID-19 pandemic, cash-based interventions enable and enhance the efficiency of program delivery. In addition to this, cash-based interventions have multiplier effects, directly benefiting the local economy as affected populations purchase goods and services in the local market but also promote peaceful coexistence and social cohesion with host communities.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

UNHCR Somalia has framework agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) since 2014 to implement cash-based interventions (CBIs), which is executed through Al Amal Bank inside Somalia. The project will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). COVID19 mitigation measures will be adhered to during the CBI program implementation, including in relation to determination of delivery mechanism.As a member the CWG UNHCR uses the cash and market analysis by the CWG to design and implement CBIs. The cash market analysis consists of food pricing and supply by Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and World Food Programme (WFP), which is issued on quarterly to inform CBI programming in Somalia. Through this assessments, minimum expenditure basket (MEB) and Transfer Values are established which will inform this project.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Impact: beneficiaries will be exposed to protection risks and will fail to benefit from protection and assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is mainstreamed across the whole project cycles and that partners and community leadership structures are also aware of their obligation in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and to meet the policy of zero tolerance.Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Impact: those in need might not receive assistance and protection. Mitigation measure: UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries. All interventions will be guided by age gender and diversity mainstreaming approach that ensures that all groups in the community are consulted and their capacities and needs are identified. UNHCR will exercise a zero-tolerance policy to fraud with the framework of the project and across all stakeholder of the project. Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms will be strengthened, and communities will be made fully aware of these mechanisms. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. Impact: disclosure of personal data of beneficiaries may have negative impact on beneficiaries and increase their existing protection risks. Mitigation: any confidential and personalized data (particularly those collected through PRMN and for the purposes of service provision/ referrals) will be restricted to authorized protection and community services staff and will be shared only on needs to know basis and adhering to UNHCR data protection policies. Any use of data by UNHCR and its implementing partners is regulated by the general principle of confidentiality and provisions in project agreements with the partners. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Impact: competition over scarce resources and conflict will negatively affect inter-communal dynamics and may lead to deterioration of protection environment for persons of concern. Mitigation: UHNCR will pursue projects that promote peaceful co-existence and also benefit vulnerable host communities’ members. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area. Impact: tenure insecurity and evictions are one of the key protection risks affecting IDPs in Somalia. Mitigation: protection monitoring and continuous advocacy with relevant stakeholders to mitigate and address those risks.

Please provide further explanation

In addition to the challenges related to the safety and security and the overall development of the country, there are specific issues which represent relevant and immediate obstacles to ensure the durable solutions for population targeted by this project. These remain a challenge due to several factors, including land, access to basic services and lack of livelihood opportunities, continuous and secondary displacement caused by conflict and natural disasters / factors. UNCHR will work closely with relevant Government authorities for domestication of Kampala Convention/ adoption and implementation of national legislation on internal displacement. The project will build upon and leverage on existing structures and systems with a clear benefit for the acceleration of process of durable solutions. This inter alia will be achieved through peaceful co-existence initiatives implemented at the community level that will aim to promote sense of ownership and sustainability of intervention.
