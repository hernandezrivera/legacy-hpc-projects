Project Summary

UNICEF’s response will serve children and teachers in conflict affected areas of eastern Ukraine with essential interventions that will ensure continuation of education in the time of COVID-19 pandemic and address immediate and cumulative impacts of the protracted armed conflict. 75,000 boys and girls will benefit from a range of activities that ensure their access to safe, inclusive and enabling learning environment and improve quality of teaching and learning. Over 60% of these boys and girls live and learn in Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) where humanitarian needs are most severe. 500 schools on both side of the Line of Contact (LoC) will be supported with implementation of COVID-19 infection prevention and control protocols. 450 education professionals in NGCA and GCA will be trained on identified priority topics that enable them to deliver conflict-sensitive, risk-informed and life-skills-based education. UNICEF will work with the Ukraine Education Cluster partners to support the Government in implementation of the Safe Schools Declaration commitments to protect schools during the armed conflict.

1 - Needs

Education in eastern Ukraine is facing an unprecedented dual challenge of coping with the cumulative impact of armed conflict in its seventh year and the COVID-19 pandemic. According to the draft HNO 2021, over 400,000 girls, boys and their teachers remain in need of humanitarian assistance related to education. Needs vary depending on the proximity to the Line of Contact (LoC), security situation in a particular area, investments of authorities and de-facto authorities in education, humanitarian access and ability of families to cover education related costs.In areas close to the LoC children and teachers in both GCA and NGCA live and learn in a highly unsecure environment characterised by military presence around many schools, mine contamination of areas near routes children use to commute to school, shelling and shooting that occasionally result in damages to schools. In 2020 (January-October) the Ukraine Education Cluster reported 11 conflict-related incidents resulting in physical damages to school infrastructure; and, according to the draft HNO 2021, some 65 percent of the education facilities within 20km of the LoC in GCA have reported that the conflict has impacted their students’ ability to learn as well as their well-being. UNICEF’s study on impact of the conflict on adolescent development outcomes has confirmed that prolonged conflict exposure was associated with increased behaviour problems (e.g. delinquency, substance use, and risky sexual behaviour), internalising symptomatology (e.g. anxiety, depression, and self harm), and an overall reduction in well-being.Further away from the LoC the conflict continues to have an adverse impact on income of many households and their ability to meet education related expenses. According to the 2019 REACH trend analysis, 27 per cent of households in GCA are unable to afford school materials for their children. The situation is most acute in NGCA where access to quality education in an enabling learning environment for tens of thousands of children remains under threat, also due to constrained humanitarian access and underfunded education in emergencies response in previous years. Monitoring visits conducted in 2020 revealed that the vast majority of educational facilities are unable to cover expenses for essential supplies such as school furniture and sports equipment. COVID-19 pandemic generated an additional layer of needs in safe school operation through implementation of infection prevention and control (IPC) guidelines issued by the Ministry of Health, Ministry of Education and Science and de-facto authorities in Donetska and Luhanska NGCAs. Educational facilities require hygiene and IPC supplies for safe operation and dedicated IT equipment to deliver distance learning. Teachers require support with acquiring new skills to continue distance and home-based learning due to quarantine restrictions. The assessment conducted by UNICEF jointly with the Institute of Educational Analytics revealed that 18% of surveyed children in Donetska and Luhanska GCAs were not participating in distance education offered by their schools during the lockdown in spring 2020. Among the top barriers to distance education school principals named the following: lack of digital learning devices among children (76%), lack of internet access at children’s and teacher’s homes (74% and 47% respectively) and lack of skills required for distance education among children and teachers (29% and 21% respectively). The school year in conflict affected areas have started on the 1st of September 2020, however already by the end of October most of 660,000 school age children in conflict affected areas were homebound due to COVID-19 related school closures. Yet many of these children – particularly those in poorer households and rural areas along the line of contact – do not have necessary equipment at home amplifying the effects of existing learning inequalities.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

UNICEF will conduct a range of activities to respond to the humanitarian needs in Education aimed at improving access to safe, child-friendly, protective and well-equipped learning environment for conflict affected boys and girls and mainstreaming implementation of safe school protocol during COVID-19 pandemic. The response activities will be focused on NGCA and in 0-20 km area along the contact line in GCA.In both GCA and NGCA will be supporting schools in implementation of infection prevention and control (IPC) guidelines issued by the Ministry of Health, Ministry of Education and Science and de-facto authorities in Donetska and Luhanska NGCAs to ensure that face to face education can continue and the risks associated with transmission of COVID-19 within schools are minimised. These will be done through provision of required hygiene and IPC supplies to schools. In GCA UNICEF will consider using Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality for this intervention that’s proved to be successful in 2020. UNICEF will also consider provision of equipment to schools that is required to support home-based/distance learning (e.g. personal computers, printers, digital learning devices, etc.) and complement these activities by raising awareness on Infection Prevention and Control for children, parents and educators. Implementation of the capacity building program that consists of Life Skills Education (LSE), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Conflict Sensitive Education (CSE) will continue and will enable teachers and community workers to deliver quality conflict-sensitive, risk-informed and life-skills-based education. In GCA the program will target teachers that were not reached in the previous years of response and in NGCA will compliment rehabilitation of education facilities and delivery of supplies whenever appropriate. Dialogue with de-facto authorities in NGCA will continue to enhance outreach with capacity building activities and ensure holistic approach to addressing needs in education. Capacity building activities will be implemented in line with the Guiding Principles on Integrating Conflict Sensitivity in Education Programming that promote gender mainstreaming at all levels including support to the policies which promote protection from abuse of girls and boys, and young women and men, equitable distribution of resources, and curriculum which is free of gender and social prejudices.Rehabilitation of education facilities is planned only for NGCA for the 2021 where the needs are most severe due to limited presence of humanitarian actors and limited capacity of communities and authorities to upkeep proper maintenance of aging buildings. The rehabilitation of education facilities will be inclusive of water and sanitation facilities that is critical in the times of COVID-19 pandemic and will include gender-segregated toilets to ensure girls and boys have access to safe toilets and water for drinking and hands washing.To support families that experience economic hardship in covering expenses associated with education that are often deprioritized in conflict affected areas UNICEF will distribute individual education kits and Early Childhood Development (ECD) kits in both GCA and NGCA. UNICEF will also conduct pprocurement and distribution of educational supplies and equipment (school furniture and sports equipment) for schools to ensure children have access to safe, child-friendly, protective and well-equipped learning environments.To address the protection risks children, teachers and schools face during the armed conflict UNICEF will provide technical assistance to the Government in implementation of the Safe Schools Declaration commitments. A special focus will be put on schools that experience military presence nearby in GCA. UNICEF will also continue monitoring the right of children and youth in NGCA to continue higher education in their preferred geographical location and advocate on this with the relevant stakeholders.

3 - Indicators & targets

500 schools with an estimated 75,000 will be supported with implementation of COVID-19 prevention and control protocols through provision of related hygiene and IPC supplies;450 teachers will be trained on identified priority topics and 12,000 boys and girls will benefit from conflict sensitive, risk-informed and life skills-based education delivered by trained teachers;10,750 children will benefit from education and early learning supplies;18,000 boys and girls will benefit from procurement and distribution of equipment to conflict-affected education facilities including equipment to support home-based/distance learning;35 education facilities in NGCA will be rehabilitated and 5,250 children will benefit from improved learning environment;At least one of the Safe Schools Declaration commitments will be fulfilled by the Government of Ukraine with the support from UNICEF.
