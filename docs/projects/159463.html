Project Summary

Adequate infant and young child nutrition (IYCN) practices are crucial for the growth and development of children. These, include early initiation of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months of life, and beyond six months, timely and age-appropriate (in terms of quality and quantity) complementary feeding of children, with continued breastfeeding up to two years of age.
Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) programming is an intervention which can have a significant impact on reducing mortality and morbidity in young children( boys and girls). In fact, broad coverage of breastfeeding and complementary feeding interventions can reduce up to 20% of deaths in young children. In Somalia, the 2016 Infant and Young Child Nutrition (IYCN) Assessment found that only 33% of infants below six months are exclusively breastfed, and while 46% of mothers continue to breastfeed their infants up to 12 months, only 15% continue to breastfeed the child up to 24 months despite being dictated in the Koran. Complementary feeding also appears compromised, with only 15% of children 6-23 months receiving the minimum dietary diversity, and only 9% consuming the minimum acceptable diet. Bottle-feeding is prevalent, with 50% of infants 0-23 months bottle-fed. These trends are associated with recurring infections among malnourished children and household food insecurity, resulting in poor or non-response to treatment, or mortality if left untreated
Children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWG’s) are among the most vulnerable people in the event of humanitarian crises, being particularly susceptible to micronutrient deficiencies and risk of malnutrition in the first 1,000 days of life. Although data collection for the 2019 micronutrient survey is still ongoing, previous surveys undertaken in 2009 indicated a high level of micronutrient deficiencies among women (41.5% iron deficiency) and children below five years (59.3% anaemia). Women do not consume a variety diet in pregnancy, with their foods being low in iron, vitamin A, vitamin C among others. There are reports of women fasting in their third trimester in order to avoid having to deliver a large baby. This can lead to poor outcomes for both mother and child. Few women deliver at health facilities or access ante- or post-natal care from a qualified health worker. In addition, women are not encouraged by their family or by their communities to practice optimal infant feeding. Women are not given the space, time or support to practice exclusive breastfeeding.

Project Activities

• Recruitment of IYCF counselors in all the 17 districts of Mogadishu. 100% of staffs should be females• Recruitment of community based IYCF Workers in all 17 Districts of Banadir. 10 pers District. 80% of community workers shall be Female.• Provision of Preventive Nutrition Intervention to Pregnant and Lactating women and children under Five years. i.e Multiple Micronutrient Powder, Multiple Micro- nutrient Tablets, VITAMIN A, IRON-FOLATE etc in all major IDP camps within Banadir Region.• Provision of theoretical IYCF training for skilled health workers i.e All IYCF counselors selected. 95% of the staff to be trained will be female.• Provision of community IYCF training for Community IYCF Workers. A total of 170 Community workers will be targeted of which 80% will be female. • Selection and formation of Mother to Mother Support Groups in All the target districts. A total of 6 Mother to Mother Support per district comprising of 20 mothers per group will be targeted, a total of 102 groups in total.• Provision of Training to Mother to Mother Support Group formed. • Participate in the 2020 World Breastfeeding week celebration Involving all MtMSG graduants & Participant, Community Workers and IYCF Counselors.• Issuance of certificate to the participants of Mother to Mother Support group who successfully graduates.
