Project Summary

UNICEF will support improved access to learning, early childhood and skills development opportunities for 296,065 (50% girls) refugee crisis affected children, adolescents and youth aged 3-24 years, including 260,043 in the camps and 36,022 in Teknaf and Ukhyia upazilas of Cox’s Bazar District.
In the camps, UNICEF will sustain operational cost of 2,645 learning centres and support capacity building of teachers, technical officers and programme officers to enable them effectively support the delivery of improved learning for 226,035 (50% girls) children and adolescents aged 3-14 years using the learning competency framework and approach (LCFA) as well as the Myanmar curriculum for students in Grades 6 to 9 and Grades 1 and 2. UNICEF will upgrade 115 existing LCs to a double-storey, medium-hazard structures, contributing to increased access to emergency shelters for the refugee population in case of disaster and providing safe and protective learning environment for children. In addition, 34,008 refugee adolescents and youth (15-24) will be supported with secondary education (10,000) based on the Myanmar curriculum and vocational skills development (24,008) programmes. UNICEF also support capacity building of partners on inclusive education, learning outcomes assessment and the use of education technology solutions to enhance the delivery of education services as part of the COVID19 response and recovery.
In the host communities in Teknaf and Ukhyia, UNICEF will support 36,022 children, adolescents and youth to access improved quality preprimary and primary education (28,818 children) and vocational skills training (7,204) programmes for out of school adolescents and youth. The support to host community will include system strengthening component aimed at enhancing the capacity of the district primary office on evidence based planning to enhance acceleration of access to equitable quality education. Schools will be supported to safely reopen and provide continuity of learning with appropriate safety measures in place. UNICEF will provide grants to schools to support implementation of school improvement plans in collaboration with school management committees.

1 - Identified Needs

The October 2020 joint Government of Bangladesh-UNHCR population estimates indicates that 862,000 Rohingya refugees live in camps in Bangladesh’s Cox’s Bazar District .Just over half (52 per cent) of the refugees are children under 18 years and one per cent are people with disability. For decades, Rohingya children have faced systematic discrimination including denial of the right to education and repeated violence in their home country that forced many of them into exile in Bangladesh and other countries. While Bangladesh has offered the refugees relative safety, many children are still deprived of some of their most fundamental rights, including the right to education based on an internationally recognized curriculum. Since their arrival in Cox’s Bazar, UNICEF and partners have relentlessly advocated for Rohingya children’s right to education and in parallel supported expansion of access to education across the camps. According to the 2020 Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), among children aged 4-14 years, 76 per cent of girls and 80 per cent of boys reported attending learning centres. By age 15 -18 years, attendance drop to 3 per cent of girls and 28 per cent of boys. For adolescents and youth aged 19-24 years, access to education and skills development opportunities is even further diminished, with only one per cent of girls and four per cent of boys reporting access to learning or skills training in the camps. Besides the absence of secondary education in the camps, the other key barriers that continue to deny the right to education, especially adolescent girls, are socio-cultural norms such as lack of value for the girl-child and practices. Among Rohingya communities, girls face additional socio-cultural barriers combined with safety concerns and supply-related issues such as a lack of trained female educators or gender-segregated latrines. A complex combination of these factors - also violence against women, heightened security issues and traditional beliefs – continue to affect girls’ participation in education. Many Rohingya parents prefer that older girls attend single-sex classes, making it more difficult for them to access education in the camps. Most LCs operate as single classroom units and only 22 per cent separate boys from girls . Improvements in gender mainstreaming and targeted interventions are needed to ensure that adolescent and young girls in the refugee and host communities have access to quality and inclusive education. The COVID-19 pandemic also presents children, adolescents, families and communities with significant mental health and psychosocial concerns including fear, anxiety, worry, depression and misinformation. Faced with psychological stress, parents, and caregivers themselves are unable to adequately provide attentive care to their children. Reduced parental supervision at home due to this distress leaves children and adolescents susceptible to additional risks. The economic vulnerabilities experienced by families increases the risks of child labour and other child protection issues. Children and adolescents with COVID-19 symptoms are at risk of abandonment for fear of transmission to family members, the social stigma associated with the disease, or because they are unable to afford the cost of treatment.

2 - Activities or outputs

-Activity 1 : Repair/maintain 2500 LCs and WASH facilities and supplies for COVID 19 risk mitigation -Activity 2 : Construct double storey learning centres based on approved design -Activity 3 : Provide teaching and learning materials teachers and students including LCFA and Myanmar Curricula (MC) textbooks-Activity 4 : Provide specialized support for children with disabilities including identification, enrolment and provision of assistive devices-Activity 5 : Provide vocational training and post training programmes for Rohingya adolescents and youth-Activity 6 : Rehabilitate/construct classrooms in host community schools-Activity 7 : Provide access to integrated ECD services in collaboration with nutrition, health and WASH sections- Activity 8 : Train teacher trainers, technical officers, teachers and facilitators (50% female) on sector approved Teacher's Professional development programme (TPD)-Activity 9 : Support professional development programmes for teachers in host community schools focusing on early learning and through PTI-Activity 10 : Support Myanmar Curricula pilot for Grades 6- 9 and scale up to Grades 1-2-Activity 11 : Support effective management of the Education Sector coordination mechanisms Support capacity building of district education officials on evidence based planning and coordination"-Activity 12 : Support implementation of education technology solutions, including radio programme to learning for children -Activity 13 : Support back to school/ back to learning campaign in host community and camps along with COVID messages -Activity 14 : Provide school grants to support implementation of improvement plans including safe reopening and operation of schools in response to COVID 19 -Activity 15 : Support training and engagement of school and learning center management committees including in promoting girls education, DRR and environmental protection initiatives
