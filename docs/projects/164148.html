Project Summary

Ar-Raqqa and Deir Ez Zor are Governorate in the east of Syria. Areas is currently controlled by Syrian Democratic Forces since 2017 after kicking out Islamic State from area after intensive military conflicts. Areas, even before start of the crisis, in 2011, was neglected by the government of Syria, and public health and service indicators were relatively low comparing to other regions and provinces in Syria.
HNO 2019 for Protraction sector indicates lack of safety and security. respond to humanitarian crisis over there, multiple humanitarian organizations started to deliver aid in the area including Hajin sub-district. Nevertheless, there is remarkable needs in multiple areas. WHOSD has recently conducted a needs assessment report to better understand the current response and existing gaps and needs. The assessment was conducted in December 2019 in 4 communities in Deir Ez Zor Governorate,Hajin SD; Hajin, Bahra, Abu Hasan, Abu Alkhatir communities and in in Tabqqa city and in Ar-Raqqa Governorate, Tabqqa SD; and Al-Thawrah SD and the availability of protection services in these areas was investigated. key informants were community key persons like teachers, local authorities, and some female leaders of women-supporting entities. Data collected about vulnerable people were separately analyzed to make sure that the project design will take their demands in consideration.
WHOSD will open a new center and mobile team in Tabqqa city, Al-Thawrah and Hajin. Project will cover 3 cities and 5 communities.
WHOSD will implement a project within 12 months to increase opportunities for effective access to justice for survivors of sexual and GBV in Syria and in displaced persons camps to limit incidences of SGBV and to increase possibilities of fully insertion of SGBV survivors in their families and communities.
WHOSD will carry out following activities to meet the project goal.
1. Awareness raising activities will consist of following:
􀁸 Advocacy campaigns and provision of psychosocial, social and legal support, as well as providing consultations.
2. Training activity: 8 training activities distributed in all of the project regions will be carried out. These activities include training package containing standards for prevention of GBV and how to strategically engage UN mechanisms.
Review of gender-based violence cases (SGBV): SGBV survivors in need of assistance will be encouraged/facilitated to come forward through information provided as part of the activities under awareness raising by providing them all information about available assistance, and modalities so that they can make an informed choice to seek assistance. This will include, first, the presentation of the consultation services provided by the project. Second, providing psychological counseling to the female applicants at the project main office and mobile team.
4. Monitoring domestic violence and recording the statements made by women in light of the framework used by the international humanitarian law and the international human rights law, and in accordance with a feminism vision that establishes the principles of equality and social justice. In general, project activities will provide psychological support to women and girls who have been subjected to sexual violence, and to members of vulnerable and traumatized groups – the most affected by gender-based violence and sexual violence. They also empower women by providing them with the knowledge and skills that enable them to know their rights identified by the international agreements and conventions. It is worth highlighting that the project activities will contribute directly to building a healthy society through empowering of women and eliminating all forms of discrimination and violence against women. Consequently, this will lead to establishing a social legal framework that ensures the concept of gender equality which enhances the process of rebuilding the society within the chosen regions.

4 - Needs

Displaced people living in last resort sites and in open areas face severe deprivation and survival needs that cut across all sectors of intervention. They have typically exhausted all other accommodation options and available resources, and are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic survival and protection needs. In 2020, the number of IDPs seeking shelter in last resort site s has increased by 42 percent compared to 2019. As at end-February 2020, over 1.2 million people of all IDPs reside in over 1,249 last resort sites, with 80% or 974,000 of IDPs concentrated in 603 last resort sites in the north-westWomen and children represent 80 percent of the population in these sites. Similarly, women and children make up the vast majority in the north-east IDP sites and makeshift settlements in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa Governorates. The gender and age composition of IDPs in last resort sites raises specific needs related to child protection (family separation/reunification, psychological support), education and child clothing. IDPs generally require specialized protection services related to GBV prevention and civil documentation, especially in sites where they are subjected to rigorous security screening procedures and are deprived of civil documents. People with disabilities living in last resort sites are in particular need of tailored assistance and specialized servicesWomen of reproductive age (15 – 49 years-of-age) and children under age five make up more than 35 percent of the affected population and require routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health services, which constitute the first-line support for GBVAccording to HNO 2020 that is not officially published, Findings suggest that sexual exploitation and abuse is an issue of concern for communities, with small numbers of respondents indicating that they have been exposed to exploitation and abuse when accessing humanitarian assistance.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lack of quality services and referrals expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and a lack of trust in services. 2. GBV service providers' safety and security because of their role and PTS for dealing with violence. 3. Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor-centered services 2. Promotion of SOPs rolls out for all staff of organizations involved in referral 3. Traditional legal systems put women more at risk, message to be mainstreamed 4. Self-care strategies as part of programs and organization HR policy 5. Include supervisory structures in response services 6. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.
