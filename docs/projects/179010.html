Project Summary

IYD proposes this project to improve the shelter status for those are living in damaged and unfinished houses to meet the minimum standard of shelter provided by Shelter/NFI cluster and to enhance the daily living for new IDPs by distributing the requirements items, also support them in winter to face the harsh weather by providing commodity electronic vouchers (e-vouchers) to IDPs in camps to enable them to purchase a defined quantity of either diesel or coal to fuel heating stoves to heat their homes during the winter months, IYD will focus on a more durable solution, focused on shelter rehabilitation, to reduce protection risks for the most vulnerable, This project will target 400 damaged houses, and 200 unfinished houses(3500 individuals, 750 men, 878 women, 800 boys,1072 girls) and 1000 new IDPs individuals in two governorates Idlib and Aleppo, Idlib, Jsr Alshoghur, Dier Hassan located in Idlib governorate, Azaz, and Aghtarin located in Aleppo governorate, These communities are managed by local councils where the local councils responsible for providing all services to the people and maintain and implement the infrastructure in the Areas. IYD has a complete and complementary plan in the shelter sector to rehabilitate both damaged houses and unfinished houses, this plan consists of 6 steps: Project Preparation, Coordination & Community Selection, Beneficiaries Selection & Technical Need Assessment, Tendering & Contracting, Implementation & Quality Management, and Documentation. These haphazard shelter solutions increase protection risks to women, young girls, and children because they lack privacy and utility services like water and electricity often do not function. NW Syria has the highest for HHS reporting shelter inadequacy issues the most significant barrier to making shelter repairs was the expense; either that repairs required a professional, which households could not afford, or that households are able to make repairs, but the materials are too expensive- this was more commonly reported than a general lack of shelter materials, Shelter/NFI cluster data. clearly demonstrates that shelter units and more adequate shelter space are one of the most critical gaps with a need for increased support directed towards host communities to safeguard coping mechanisms and improve the standard and availability of shelter. Through this project IYD is planning to intervene in two locations at the district level, Aleppo governorate, Azaz, and Albab district, those two locations have many damaged houses. Under this project, IYD will rehabilitate 400 partially damaged houses between 40 % – 70 %, 100 houses in Jsr Alshohgur, and 300 in Idlib, Aleppo governorate, and 200 unfinished houses in Azaz, by reconstructing the destroyed walls, install new doors, and windows, and rehabilitate the water services, the estimated total number of targeted beneficiaries 3,000 individuals, also, IYD under this project will construct a wall to form separate rooms and provide new materials doors, windows, and lighting systems, in addition, the rehabilitation of the water services will be done by providing water tanks, toilets, and baths. IYD finds that residents are willing to contribute to improvement projects; specifically, to provide labor to support the repair and rebuilding of the accommodations. During conducting technical need assessment for shelter rehabilitation, IYD team will be focusing on the basic needs for the most vulnerable families, this is to identify the most required NFIs kits to be distributed for those families in the priority for new arrival families which they need, IYD planning to distribute new arrival kits, kitchen kit, and comprehensive kit, the distribution process will be through registration of the families and doing the verifications for the beneficiaries, the program team will prepare the distribution lists in coordination with the local council, each family will distribute NFI standard kit.

4 - Needs

The number of people in need of shelter assistance has increased in overall Syria especially in Idleb, and Aleppo governorates.There has also been a significant worsening in overall shelter arrangements in Syria, with 36 percent of sub-districts reporting an increase in the proportion of communities living in damaged or temporary shelters. The acute humanitarian situation in the Northwest is driven by the high proportion of IDPs, overcrowding and the lack of shelter capacity, high rental prices and unaffordable items, and poor access to markets. A considerable number of people live in shelters with at least one adequacy issue, including lack of heat and insulation, lighting, necessary bathing or cooking facilities, inadequate space, lack of privacy, and the inability to securely lock their home. The shelter adequacy issues mentioned by different age According to the NHO report for 2021, 5.9 million people across Syria are in need of shelter assistance and a large number of households cannot carry out necessary repairs to their homes, most frequently because they cannot afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation, and due to a lack of skilled labor. As in previous years, the primary providers of shelter assistance and support are host households with households hosting others.HLP issues remain a challenge on many fronts. Repair and rehabilitation of housing are only pursued following confirmation of HLP rights; however, many displaced people do not have sufficient documentation (many never had proper or formal documentation or lost them during displacement), and the processes and infrastructure to formalize or recuperate documents are not uniformly functional or accessible.

Sector Objective.2: Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure, IYD determined 400 damaged houses to be rehabilitated in Idlib and Jsr Alshoghur communities, the beneficiaries from this activity will be 2,400 individuals, the methodology of implementing these activities will be as following: IYD will hire the required staff to implement the project.IYD team in coordination with the local authorities will publish an announcement in the targeted locations to allow the beneficiaries to register to benefit from the project, this registration will be through criteria after the registration process completed, the verification list will be checked by the MEAL team. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

IYD has a good reputation as a non-profit organization and works side by side with the community to empower and encourage the community ownership of facilities. Accordingly. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough to conduct the aid or distribution project. IYD will coordinate together in the implementing process and both of the teams will work together to ensure the safety and security of the team in the field. Protection risk:1- Military conflict might happen around the targeted locations Likelihood: High, Impact: medium Mitigating measures: The qualified staff of IYD used to follow the safety and security procedures.2- the families with disability person Likelihood: High, Impact: low Mitigation: the project’s design must include this situation and not exclude them from the benefit of the project, for that IYD will consider the special need for those who have disabilities in the targeted locations by providing the items they need such as toilets, baths.3- child labor Likelihood: High, Impact: high Mitigation: IYD policy prevents child labor from implementing its project, and we declare in all our contract about this issue, IYD will not accept under any circumstances child labor.4- housing, land, and property issues, Likelihood: High, Impact: high Mitigation: the common issue in HLP is more than 57% of the owners (houses, lands) lost their documentation about the property, This requires working more, reaching key people in the community, and conducting a community investigation of the correctness of the ownership of homes and lands.
