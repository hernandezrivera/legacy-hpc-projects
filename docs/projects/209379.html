Project Summary

The conflict that erupted in April 2023 in Sudan has decimated the country’s public infrastructure, including the health system. The health care system is suffering from an acute lack of staff, funding and medical supplies in addition to repeated attacks, looting and occupation of medical facilities and hospitals. More than 70% health facilities in conflict-affected regions of Sudan are inoperable or closed.
The displacement of civilians has placed an additional strain on already overwhelmed health care resources, as well as water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services. An outbreak of measles in the second half of 2023 has claimed the lives of more than 1000 children across Sudan. The country is also grappling with a severe cholera outbreak, with the number of suspected cases surpassing 8,500 in December 2023, having increased by 94% over the past month. With high rates of malnutrition, a debilitated health system and low levels of immunization, disease outbreaks will continue to have catastrophic impacts, particularly for children.
The remote areas that affected by the current conflict in Darfur and Kordofan states are inaccessible and suffer much from lack of medical assistance. The difficulty of transporting patients from rural areas to urban centers and from the remote state capitals to Khartoum for advanced diagnosis and medication is now extremely not possible mainly due to security risks along the main roads and because the health system in Khartoum itself and other big cities like Medani have collapsed due to fighting.
All the above mentioned challenges and difficulties place huge responsibilities on the shoulders of the humanitarian agencies to endeavor tom outreach the vulnerable people who need lifesaving health services and need prevention from expected epidemics resulting from fighting, negligence of environmental health, living in crowdedness conditions and lack of enough medical services.
United Peace Organization (UPO) is aiming to contribute in saving lives and alleviate the suffering of the people being affected by conflict and suffer from the magnitude of the epidemics which are driven by the conflict that triple doubled their impacts on the far majority of the communities. The intervention aims to support essential public health assistance with focus on primary health care services (including emergency trauma response). The project will address the needs of vulnerable groups who are disproportionally affected by health emergencies exacerbated by conflict and epidemics. UPO endeavors to access the remote areas that are currently disconnected from health care services due to the conflict, and that will be achieved in close coordination with the governmental departments and local communities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project is designed in a protection-sensitive modality, is essentially based on the gender roles framework and the gender matrix framework, and will consider all protection and gender standards and approaches throughout the humanitarian program cycle, project implementation, and exit strategy while promoting the same approaches and standards in the targeted communities to ensure the sustainability of the project's long-term outcomes. Using UPO protection, SEA, SGBV, accountability policies, gender-sensitive policies, modalities, and tools. The project ensures that safety, dignity, and anonymity are prioritized while avoiding causing harm. Specifically, the SGBV cases will be handled with high care and will referred in a way to avoid putting the victims into further risks and social pressures.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in homes) through aid workers

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

UPO recognizes the critical role of community feedback in enhancing its responsiveness and effectiveness. The utilization of community feedback will be embedded in various aspects of the project through the following strategies: 1/ Community Consultations and Participatory Approaches: UPO will conduct regular community consultations, meetings, and focus group discussions to actively engage with community members. In addition to utilizing participatory approaches that involve community members in decision-making processes related to the project activities. 2/Inclusive Representation: UPO will ensure representation from diverse community groups, including women, men, boys, and girls, in feedback sessions to capture a broad range of perspectives. UPO will also consider the inclusion of marginalized and vulnerable individuals who may face additional challenges in expressing their needs. 3/ Capacity Building for Community Engagement: UPO will build the capacity of local community leaders and representatives to effectively articulate community needs and feedback, by fostering a sense of ownership and empowerment among community members to actively participate in shaping and improving project activities.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UPO regularly informs the inter-agency coordination mechanism through information sharing, including best practices and lessons learned. Quarterly reports that include activities conducted, concerns raised, issues identified, and mechanisms established to address the issues will be prepared and submitted to the sector coordinators both at the field and HQ in Port Sudan. At the field level, the organization will establish a committee to meet on a monthly basis to review the complaints and feedback received from the communities. The reports will be shared widely with other agencies working in the same areas
