Project Summary

This project aims to improve 32,900 households’ immediate access to food through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoods; and to protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources of 32,900 households through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings in Awdal and Banadir Region of Somalia. It will provide life-saving and life-sustaining integrated, multi-sectoral assistance to reduce acute humanitarian needs and excess mortality among the most vulnerable people. The recent floods, cyclone, displacement, evictions etc., combined with the impact of recurrent droughts since 2016, continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable Somalis. The food security outlook remains particularly worrying among rural populations in the Awdal (Guban pastoral livelihood zone) of the country, which received below-average rainfall during the last Gu rainy season. Moreover, most of the IDPs from last few years have moved to urban centres including Mogadishu and its outskirts in search of better livelihood opportunities and humanitarian assistance. The situation of IDPs continues to be worsened by an increase in frequent forced evictions, mainly in urban areas, with those who are most aﬀected regularly identiﬁed as the poorest and most vulnerable people in the country. Guban Pastoral livelihood zone, where poor households have very few sale-able animals to purchase food and will likely remain in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and IDP settlements in Banadir are areas of greatest concern most of which are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). This is a 12 months project meant to address the needs of target beneficiaries in Awdal and Banadir IDPs.

Needs

Although the food security is generally improving, most areas of the country are currently Stressed (IPC Phase 2), though Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes exist in some areas and among IDP populations. A recent assessment conducted by Islamic Relief reveals that cyclone affected areas the malnutrition rates are exacerbated by inappropriate IYCF practices with about 60% of the children feeding on less than 4 food groups. Breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth at 49.3%; 55% of the mothers provided pre-lacteal feeds to young infants during the first 3 days after delivery, thus increasing the risks of diarrhea. Most HH consume less than 3 meals a day with adults eating fewer times than children. IRS nutrition survey 2018. 91% are female headed households and engage in search of water, sale of charcoal and firewood which is unsustainable and risky thus exposing them to GBV. In addition, 50% of poor agro pastoralists HHs have little access to veterinary drugs and services leading to 25% average loss of livestock especially cattle. High cost of tools has been cited as a hindrance to food production. (IRS Participatory needs assessment August 2018). Rehabilitated productive infrastructure- water pans to improve access to water for mainly livestock use. Therefore, in the absence of continued humanitarian assistance, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are likely in riverine livelihood zones and northern and central Somalia. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is likely in Guban Pastoral livelihood zone. Islamic Relief is proposing to provide access to food for populations aﬀected by ﬂoods, cyclones, drought, conﬂict and diseases to address acute food insecurity. It is of paramount importance that we support to people living in these area to protect their livelihoods, enable local food production and prevent adoption of “crisis” strategies.

Activities

Activities include implement Cash for Work scheme; provide refresher training and drug kits for 200 CAHWs; restock 5000 households; provide livestock treatment for identified vulnerable households using voucher system; provide fodder for livestock to support 400 milking and breeding stocks for 200 vulnerable households during the stress periods. IR will assist up to 5000 people (at least one person per destitute household), through cash for work programs. The primary objective of CFW is to provide immediate cash needs to destitute households thereby providing income for food and other needs. The cash for work activities will create durable community assets in their areas including rehabilitation/construction of rangeland rehabilitation, community infrastructures such as access roads, and natural resource management such as gullies, etc. This intervention will have an immediate impact as beneficiaries are paid much needed cash income to be used for the purchase of food and non-food items. Chronically sick, disabled and pregnant women will be provided unconditional cash transfer for two months. Moreover, we will provide small grant to 60 women to set up petty trade to supply food items and other basic items. Community members prioritised restocking as it helps individual target pastoral households or communities to build up lost herds and flocks in a sustainable manner. Pastoral systems had indigenous mechanisms for assisting destitute families by restocking their own members gradually through existing arrangements. The project will provide livestock aimed at "restocking" destitute pastoralists to 2000 households (12,000 people) in the project areas. Through this project IR Somalia will supply each recipient household with a nucleus herd of small stock. Animal health care service will be provided to 7000 heads of animals to maintain the productivity, marketability and survival of animals. Emergency animal health services are a proven strategy.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The programme approach and strategy are designed in a way that is intended to mainstream sustainability in all activities and to build the capacity of institutions at all levels including community-level so that the benefits can continue after the defined programme lifespan. Capacity development and training activities are specifically designed to increase individual and collective self-sufficiency in a way that will drive local ownership. This will be backed up through systems strengthening to develop the management information and management capacities of state and community institutions, so that they can function effectively and independently of the programme. Furthermore, accountability mechanisms that are created will help to ensure that the delivery of basic social services continues to be accountable to communities and that there is effective engagement between community management structures and government organisations at all levels. Care will be given that information, training and knowledge can be passed down through organisations and government structures to mitigate the risk of outcomes being lost through turnover of staff. Central to the sustainability strategy is the support to the development and training of community water management committees to act as representatives of communities to government bodies. Training of these will ensure that they are able to operate effectively and independently following the programme and will have an on-going effect on communities’ ability to engage in basic social service delivery. Specific programming activities in this workstream are also designed to encourage sustainability and enable a smooth transition. The development of effective management information systems across sectors will facilitate greater ownership of basic social service delivery institutions and training will be designed to ensure that this approach is followed. The programme will plan and hold meetings at an agreed point with stakeholders from the government and communities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main target population for the project are pastoralists and IDPs and the surrounding host communities. Pastoralists in Guban pastoral zone lost their livelihoods assets to the recurrent droughts and subsequent impact of cyclone, and IDPs, most notable those in camps have lost their livelihood assets. IR will identify groups at risk of unsafe livelihoods practices and priorities provision of alternatives for these groups and develop the intervention in such a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities and promotes family unity. Minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, gender-based violence, tracking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, IR will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis that seeks to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. IR will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable groups including women, girls, boys, men and people with disabilities, and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. For instance, IR will ensure that lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. IR will ensure that committees are representative of all groups within the community such as gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability. IR will provide all staff have received protection mainstreaming trainings and have ample experience in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination to ensure the voice of marginalized groups are considered. IR will set up accessible, & confidential feedback mechanisms.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

A large segment of population is mainly children and women, yet they have the burden of struggling to earn for their family, the project will include them as exclusion further exposes them to SGBV, abuse, poverty and marginalization. IR will respond to the special needs of pregnant and lactating women, under 5 year olds and older persons and that the quality of their assistance and services is equitable to that of other groups. Issue ration cards to female-headed households, child-headed households and unaccompanied children in their own name. Through strengthening or establishing community-based organisations in consultation with local authorities, deliberate efforts will be made to ensure inclusion of all sections of the beneficiaries (men, women, youth, disadvantaged persons), and different clans/groups. Also, IR will consider the gender balance of distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. Establish mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women. In order to address the identified challenges vouchers will be given directly to women which will promote a greater role for women in decision-making within targeted households. This will allow enough time for women to engage in other roles and will promote gender empowerment as well as reduced women and girls’ exposure to related SGBV risks. IR ensures that staffs and community committees are representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability). All IR staffs received protection mainstreaming training which help them play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

IR works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include risk theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their rations. IR will ensure minimize the time between distributions the larger to ensure that the quantities distributed are manageable to avoid risk of attacks and pillage. Doing so will aid being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary. IR applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. IR local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. IR will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.
