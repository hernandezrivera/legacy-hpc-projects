Project Summary

The project has 3 main objectives:
1) Ensure access to a comprehensive package of services (psychological first aid, case management and medical care) for women and girls survivors of GBV,
2) Support GBV prevention by raising awareness through sensitization campaigns (outreach activities, radio broadcast, communication tools, Advocacy through faith based organizations)
3) Strengthen the GBV coordination through sub cluster in the North West and South West Regions
II. ACTIVITIES OR OUTPUTS
1) To restore/improve access to medical care and psychosocial support for survivors of GBV
2) To provide gender and culturally-appropriate psychosocial support to the worst affected communities, especially survivors of gender based violence.
3) To increase awareness of individuals and communities, including through the use of a network of media houses, in high risk communities on the prevention of GBV, especially sexual violence, and management of its consequences.
4) To develop a functioning referral system that is community-based for GBV and sexual and reproductive health.
5) To protect/restore the dignity of affected and vulnerable women and adolescent girls through the provision of culturally appropriate dignity (hygiene) kits.
6) To build capacities for improved and well-coordinated GBV response

Needs

The conflict in the South West and North West regions of Cameroon goes into the third year with escalating violence and destruction of social services affecting the lives of displaced people and host communities. 959,000 people area affected by the crisis while an estimated 594,000 people are in need of life saving health and other humanitarian services in the region. The largest proportion of the People in Need (PIN) are IDPs.The crisis in the North West and South West regions has resulted in a rapid deterioration of living conditions and an upsurge of abuse affecting the entire population, especially school-age children, women and children. In this context, women, girls and boys are the most vulnerable to gender-based violence that was already very high in both regions and was exacerbated by the crisis.Access to services for survivors of GBV is limited in IDPS sites and host communities. The prevalence of sexual violence remains under-reported due to fear of reprisals by perpetrators, stigmatization of their own communities, and lack of trust in existing justice systems.GBV needs in both crises relate to access to a comprehensive service (medical, psychosocial, security, legal, socio-economic integration, etc.) and well-coordinated for survivors, as well as the improvement of prevention systems. GBV, including Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, through the mitigation of risk factors and the strengthening of community protection strategies.
