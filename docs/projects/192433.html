Project Summary

The play-Based methodology is an inherent part of RTPs psychosocial programmes. Our methodology is grounded in an RCA (reflect, connect, and apply) technique, based on Kolb's learning cycle, and forms a basis upon which children and adults will utilise the learning from activities and adapt it to their circumstances, environment and aspirations. Additionally, many of the RTP PSS resources and pieces of training are grounded in trauma-based approaches, which take advantage of Cognitive Behavioural Therapy and narrative play methodologies to equip PSS providers and children to recognise and de-pathologise signs of distress and trauma in themselves and their community.
For this project, RTP aims to support 1,000 beneficiaries including 800 children and adolescents and 200 caregivers. The intervention will take place in Beirut, North and South and will allow target groups access to community-based PSS. Additionally, the project will entail having community members and children plan and implement initiatives serving to mitigate against identified CP risks.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

I) Individuals receiving prevention services:- Peer to peer support: this activity will utilize RTP's Play and Care Together (PACT) resource to allow for community members, particularly youth, to be trained on providing PSS support for other children. The approach takes careful steps into ensuring youth trainers from the community are identified, trained, and supported throughout the process. RTP staff and partners will be co-leading the sessions in the initial phases, and then providing coaching and tips around the sessions. - Music for social change: This activity entails providing children and youth with PSS through music utilizing RTP's Music for Social Change (M4SC) resource. These activities will allow target groups to have a unique and artistic platform to express their concerns, learn the basics of music, and potentially embark on music-related advocacy activities. II) Child Protection Initiatives:- RTP PSS activities mentioned above will allow participants to build better resilience to potential shocks and to bring the process full circle, they will be engaged in planning and implementing community initiatives to address CP risks that they identify within their communities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All staff at Right To Play receive Child Safeguarding training at the time of on boarding and undertake a refresher training each year. Additionally, Child Safeguarding training for Country, HQ and National staff is scheduled and conducted at least every two years. Safeguarding training is provided to RTP staff during onboarding and staff undertake refresher trainings each year. As outlined in Right To Play’s Child Safeguarding Policy, children and parents/guardians are informed of the Child Safeguarding policies and procedures, and means of contacting Right To Play directly for concerns about violence against children, abuse and/or exploitation. Throughout the life of the project, all involved stakeholders including partners will receive trainings and refreshers on Child Safeguarding. Additionally, all RTP resources are gender sensitive and takes into consideration different capacities and abilities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RTP provides children with information on where to go locally for confidential help and advice in relation to violence, abuse, exploitation, harassment and bullying. Throughout the program, quarterly Focus Group Discussions will also be held with girls and boys participating in the program to understand their experiences of the program and support in identifying any concerns. Additionally, RTP will utilize it's referral mechanisms under the umbrella of the inter-agency and will ensure timely referrals and follow-up as need be.
