Project Summary

IOM will contribute to community revitalization and resilience through supporting local economic recovery, improving access to basic/social services, and promoting community engagement and social cohesion. The project will strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and improving access to production and market infrastructure to foster local economy recovery. This comes in line with both Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector 2021 objectives.
IOM will improve access to basic services by rehabilitating essential infrastructures identified by the communities, through a process of community engagement and consultation, as being most needed. IOM will also provide vocational training and business development support (BDS), including training and financial or in-kind support, to vulnerable IDPs for them to establish or expand their own business. Prior to implementation, IOM will identify the local market gaps in coordination with the communities.
Furthermore, IOM will provide income generating activity through Cash for Work (CfW) programmes to enable vulnerable beneficiaries to cover their households’ most urgent needs. CfW wages will be based on local average wages so as not to distort the local labour market, and activities will be identified in consultation with local community representatives and business owners. CfW activities will be implemented in line with the CWG’s minimum recommended actions. For all cash assistance, IOM will coordinate with the ER Cluster and the Cash Working Group to harmonize transfer values and avoid overlap.
Lastly, IOM will improve the livelihood opportunities and community engagement for IDPs and vulnerable communities through the continuation of the Community Enhancement Center (CEC) and establishment of Community Engagement Teams (CETs) in Afrin, Aleppo, A’zaz, Harim, and Idleb sub-districts.

3 - Needs

After 12 years of conflict compounding its high poverty and unemployment rates, livelihoods in NWS remain highly disrupted. Only 53 per cent of men and 4 per cent of women in NWS have regular income (regular employment or business owners). Only 21 per cent of households have more than one source of income, and IDP households are more likely to face unemployment. About one third of employed individuals work in the agricultural sector, which is regularly affected by droughts and high production costs. In addition, the median salary for employed individuals is USD 71, which is far below the cost of the current survival minimum expenditure basket of USD 111.51. According to community-level assessments conducted by IOM’s IPs, IDPs mention the lack of job opportunities and of required skills to qualify for jobs as the primary reason for lacking a stable income. Women and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) reported facing additional barriers to access jobs, primarily cultural norms, household obligations, inadequate community infrastructure, and movement restrictions.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. IOM activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions due to beneﬁciary selection for livelihood activities or limited resources and response capacities. 2. Conflicts and tensions about the communities can arise during community consultation sessions where community members debate their most urgent needs and priorities. 3. Pressure on the implementing partners from the local authorities on the selection of beneficiaries or contractors. 4. Vulnerable beneficiaries including women, the elderly, and PWD experiencing abuse or violence, including intra-household conflict, as a result of receiving assistance. Mitigation measures: 1. Clearly communicate the selection and vulnerability criteria to the communities prior to selection, as well as the project’s limited resources.2. Train IP staff and CETs on conducting effective and respectful discussions with the communities and how to manage any tensions.3. Raise awareness with IPs and CETs on principled humanitarian assistance, work with CETs to mitigate undue influence from local actors, and train IPs on reporting any incidents or interference to IOM’s Programme and Access teams as early as possible to ensure the safety of the field teams and the integrity of the project.4. Communication and messaging designed by protection/GBV and CVA partners addressing potential tensions.5. Communicate on the existence of feedback and referral mechanisms to address protection concerns for the most vulnerable.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:- Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by IP staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes.- Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA.- Lack of availability of and information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries.- Lack of female IP staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures:- Code of conduct includes PSEA language.- All IP staff are trained on PSEA.- Internal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization. - PSEA awareness raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. - All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks.- Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complaint mechanisms and hotlines.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. Security situation in NWS allows IOM to implement response activities.2. Activities can be implemented in coordination with local councils.3. Beneficiaries start their business after receiving cash or in-kind support.4. Vendors in targeted areas have sufficient capacity.5. Active community participation and engagement. Risks:1. Security situation in NWS does not allow IOM response activities to be undertaken.2. Local authorities are not in favor of IOM intervention or influence on beneficiary selection/aid diversion 3. Support packages are sold by beneficiaries instead of being used as planned.4. Vendors lack the capacity to provide required items.5. The beneficiary selection process leads to abuse or harassment of female beneficiaries. IOM and its IP will explain the selection criteria and concerns will be flagged through the AAP mechanisms.6. GBV takes place during livelihood activities, which is mitigated by separating male and female beneficiaries, training IP staff, and ensuring the presence of TPM.
