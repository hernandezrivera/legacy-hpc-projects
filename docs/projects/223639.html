Project Summary

Action aims to improve food security situation among vulnerable population in Monguno and Gwoza facing poor food security outcomes due to conflict, soaring food and fuel prices, eroded purchasing power and deteriorated food production. 1, 552 households (7,760 individuals) will receive four (4) cycles of unconditional cash grants for Food Minimum Expenditure Basket (FMEB) during the lean season (June-September). Households will purchase food from local markets enabling them to meet their immediate food needs. Agriculture inputs kits support for 300 households will allow them to engage in wet season agriculture under the short-term livelihoods. This will contribute directly to food consumption and couple with improved purchasing power, positive food security outcomes will be achieved while reducing excessive coping and preserving lives.

1 - Needs

Impact of conflict, insecurity, soaring fuel and food prices, and effects of climate change, has caused unprecedented levels of hunger in Nigeria. October 2024 results projection that that about 1,589,340 people and 456,544 for IDPs in Borno State will face poor food security outcomes during lean period between June and September August 2025. These will be in “Crisis” and above, IPC/CH Phases 3-5 population. Currently households’ food consumption is under “Stress” in IPC/CH Phases 2 and with populations in Phase 3. Joint MSNA findings in Borno State suggests that households are currently not able to meet the minimum food requirements where 58% of the survey population displayed “Poor” Food Consumption Score (FCS), 28% at “Borderline” while a small proportion, 13% presented better food security outcome “Acceptable” FCS. Findings also demonstrates elevated levels of coping among survey households reported by 63% of In Borno State. High Food inflation estimated at 75%, fuel price increases and poor harvests in 2024 wet agriculture immensely contribute to households’ failure to meet their immediate food needs. Prohibitive costs of agriculture inputs may further exacerbate consumption patterns and outcomes compounded with insecurity discouraging participation of agriculture livelihoods as farmers occasionally attacked at their farmers. Continued effects of climate change manifesting themselves as delayed on set of rainfall, short seasons, reduced annual rainfall amount, dry spells and flooding further compromises food productivity.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of unconditional cash grants for Food Minimum Expenditure Basket (FMEB)Targeted acute food insecure households in Monguno and Gwoza will be provided with unconditional cash grants to purchase diversified and nutritious food items from the local markets enabling to meet their immediate food needs. All the 300HH for short-term livelihoods participants will be included for lean season assistance. A total of 1,552 households (7,760 individuals), 776 households each from Monguno and Gwoza will be supported. Each household will receive NGN 59,160 (€ 35,5) per month for four months as lean season assistance between June to September 2025.The cash grant is meant to cover 70% of the households’ monthly minimum food needs inclusive of cooking fuel. Proposed transfer value is guided by the Food Security Sector (FSS) standards embedded as per the eighth edition guidance note issued in May 2024. During implementation, if revised transfer values are announced by FSS, the project will be adopted. To cater for food inflation, a 5% contingency has been included and the budgeted transfer value is NGN70,992 (€42,60). To ensure that cash grants provided are used for the intended purpose, awareness sessions will be conducted before disbursement to ensure that resources are used for the intended purpose of purchasing diversified and nutritious food items for household consumption.2. Provision of emergency food production kitsNRC will support a total 300 households (HH), 150 HH each from Monguno and Gwoza with short term livelihood support. These households will also benefit from lean season assistance. Short-term livelihood support will directly contribute to food consumption. Participants will grow cereals and legumes improving food availability contributing to better food security outcomes. Agriculture inputs kits will be provided to enable them to participate in wet season agriculture. Each household will receive one staple cereal seeds of their choice, legumes, organic fertiliser, hand tools and protective gear. Participants will select one staple cereal seed from 10 kgs of maize seed, 8kgs of sorghum seed and 8kgs of millet seed. Legumes will be 8kgs of cowpea seeds to be provided to all and they will be consulted for preferences. Quantity of staple seeds provided will be adequate to plant cover one hectare (1ha) except for maize see which will cover half a hectare (0,5ha). Cowpeas will cover third of a hectare (0,3ha) and to be intercropped with a staple cereal.
