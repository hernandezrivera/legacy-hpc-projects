Project Summary

The proposed WASH intervention will deliver life-saving emergency water services, restore access to sustainable water sources, improve access to faecal management services and sanitation infrastructure, including the construction and rehabilitation of latrines, and promote improved hygiene practices through mass media campaigns and distribution of water purification and hygiene kits in vulnerable communities across Gedo, Hiraan and Banadir.
As a leading cause of acute diarrheal diseases (ADD) and malnutrition, integrated WASH service delivery with health and nutrition intervention improves health and nutrition outcomes. There is a correlation between districts accounting for over 50% of the total incidence of ADD and poor nutrition outcomes, including Banadir (Dharkeynley and Heliwa), and Hiraan (Beletweyne), and areas where there is poor access to WASH services. The prolonged dry conditions for much of 2019 have exacerbated competition for scarce water resources for human and animal consumption which has driven conflict and displacement. In 2019, RI health centres in Banadir, Hiraan and Gedo have treated over 7,000 cases of ADD and treated/admitted/referred 4,688 under 5 children for malnutrition. In an effort to stem the spread of communicable diseases and increase access to water points, RI constructed a 13km long pipeline. This pipeline now reaches 40,000 people with water for domestic use, including the two RI health facilities in Belet Hawa and the Malkeriyeey. Complementing the restoration of sustainable access to water, RI trucked water and distributed hygiene kits to distressed populations. This approach exemplifies RI’s integrated emergency and durable solutions approach to WASH challenges.
This project will address the immediate needs of households with emergency water trucking and hygiene activities while laying the foundation for durable WASH solutions with the construction and rehabilitation of latrines and water points which will serve communities for the foreseeable future. RI will establish simplified Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) systems to restore/maintain sanitation systems. Additionally, to maximize impact, RI will reinforce improved sanitation and hygiene practices at household level by increasing access to water and sanitation services, and hygiene promotion. To ensure protection and inclusion of the most vulnerable groups, RI will ensure barriers are removed which inhibit access and safety, particularly to women and children, ethnic minorities, displaced people, and people with disabilities.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities in alignment with the WASH cluster strategic objectives:- Truck water to communities without safe access to clean water;- Promote improved hygiene practices through mass media campaigns, community dialogues, and household visits;- Construct and rehabilitate strategic water points;- Distribute hygiene, water purification, and menstrual management kits;- Construct and rehabilitate latrines; - Establish simple faecal management systems;- Establish/support water management committees;- Train water management committees (WMC) on maintenance of WASH infrastructure, including cost recovery; - Train community hygiene promoters;- Develop emergency preparedness plans with WMCs to build resilience.

Mitigation Measures

1b:RI will mitigate protection risks for beneficiaries accessing key WASH components in the following ways:- distribute commodities in areas accessible to to all groups and at times conducive to the most vulnerable beneficiaries, including IDPs, ethnic minorities, and people with disabilities. - establish water points in safe areas that all groups can access- site water points and conduct distributions in places that the limit time travelled. This approach limits exposure to potential protection concerns- maintain WASH facilities to ensure that beneficiaries, after travel, are able to use and safely benefit from the infrastructure. For example, public latrines that are gender segregated with lockable, secure doors with handwashing facilities.- 3a:Ri will ensure external interference is limited by:- Ensuring clan dynamics are considered when consulting communities- Ensure voices from IDP and host communities are heard and certain settlements aren't excluded- 3c:- Determine safe and secure locations for water points and distributions when conducting activities involving IDPs- consult households and communities when selecting sites for water points and distributions- Ensure latrines are well lit, secure, gender segregated and have lockable doors- Distribute items with female health staff present to create a sense of safety and confidence in beneficiaries- Ensure people with disabilities are included and considered
