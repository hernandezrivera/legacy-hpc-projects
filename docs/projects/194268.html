Project Summary

South Sudan Education sector faces significant challenges that originated in years of conflict, displacement, lack of infrastructure, teachers and investments. More than 3,6 million children need assistance. PAH is an experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use its expertise in education, protection, WASH and other sectors, team of specialists and well-established cooperation with various stakeholders to further provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country.
Recognizing the vast needs in Education across South Sudan, PAH will target mainly areas of highest needs, basing on the regularly updated cluster priority lists. Secondly PAH will focus on communities affected by conflicts and floods displacements, with high percentage of displaced members that over-stretch the community resources, which may lead to nonfunctional schools, drop outs and negative coping strategies (inc. child labour and marriage).
PAH plans to ensure access to WASH facilities. As mentioned in the donors report (UN OCHA SSHF SSD-19/HSS10/SA2/E/INGO/13446) PAH provides high standard facility, namely latrines and hand washing models.
Thus, basing on the needs, PAH may implement broad variety of activities:
• Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces, construction of temporary learning spaces (TLS) and provision of educational equipment and other materials
• Establishment of child/youth-friendly spaces: organizing recreational activities, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities.
• Provision of psycho-social support and encouraging learning for the pre-school age children through provision of ECD materials.
• Establishment of youth-friendly spaces, and recreational activities for adolescents and youth.
• Establishment or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, including latrines (gender-segregated), water points and soap provision for hand-washing, and provision of dry chlorine.
• Health and hygiene promotion for children, youth and care-givers, hygiene messaging on Covid-19, cholera symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention by school-based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth); menstrual hygiene education and menstrual hygiene kits provision
• Teacher professional development including training on Education in Emergencies-EiE, training of PTA and SMCs on school management and other cross-cutting issues
• Creating a school environment clubs to maintain the school environment through the planting of trees and vegetables. This will empower students to promote good environmental behavior in the schools and the community.
• MHM education and menstrual hygiene kits provision
• Psycho-social support and peaceful coexistence promotion activities in schools, learning spaces and friendly spaces.
PAH has designed and construct flood resilient WASH facilities, including institutional level latrines. These will be implemented in the flood prone locations where feasible.
As PAH implements multi – sector responses we provide access to multiple services for the beneficiaries. In case of the needs children and youth will be covered with Protection ( mainly AoR Child Protection) activities. Provision of WASH infrastructure will be implemented in the close cooperation with PAH WASH unit. For the sectors of Health and Nutrition PAH will closely cooperate with other partners.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.
Taking into consideration the Ebola preparedness plans due to the cases in neighboring Uganda, in case of outbreak in South Sudan PAH will follow the preparedness plans and procedures of respective authorities (MoH, WASH cluster, Health cluster etc.)

1 - Needs

According to 2023 Intercluster Severity Scales for Humanitarian Needs Overview vast majority of the country (67 out of 78 counties) was classified as extreme severity plus 1 county was classified as catastrophe. 22 couties were clasified as serevity 5 level in terms of Education needs (Lainya, Morobo, Yei, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Magwi, Akobo, Bor South, Nyirol, Uror, Awerial, Leer, Rubkona, Luakpiny/Nasir, Melut, Panyikang, Renk, Tonj East, Tonj North, Twic, Mundri East and Mvolo). Another 21 are level 4.According to the latest HNO data from for 2022, in year 2021, 5862 schools were operating in South Sudan, however, they only reopened in May following the 14-months closure caused by COVID-19 pandemic. Out of those schools, 2404 were either destroyed due to floods (560) or conflict (535), used as temporary shelters (17) or closed again for other reasons (1292). At least 30% of the remaining schools have no access to safe water source and over 50% reported no access to clean water and soap. During COVID-19 the schools were closed for 14 months (March 2020 – May 2021), causing a range of issues, related to education and protection, among others. According to the 2021 UNICEF Needs Assessment, less than half of learners were able to access the national radio distance learning programs during the school closures causing a significant gap in the learning process. Many children never came back to school after that period.Distance to schools, security incidents, population displacement, killings of teachers and scarce financial resources were limiting access for children to schools. Climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict are further exacerbating this barriers. Girls face additional challenges, including harmful social norms, lack of MHM kits and sanitation facilities, among others. It is estimated that 2.8 million children is out of school, with insecurity indicated as a major reason. Menstruating girls were said to have high drop-out or low attendance rates in school as they missed lessons while menstruating and found it hard to catch up. Many of the drop-out girls may never go back to school due to teenage pregnancy or early marriage. Boys on the other hand, often drop out of schools due to the risk of revenge killings and engage in child labour, including recruitment by armed groups and community-based militias. Additionally, lack of- or inadequate infrastructure, costs of accessing educations as well as lack of trained personnel are hampering the access the education. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout further deepening gender inequalities as girls are more severely affected. Hunger is a key barrier to education, resulting in poor academic performance, inconsistent attendance and eventual dropout. Most learners do not get a meal before school and over 60 % of schools assessed in the Education Needs Assessment do not provide food for learners. Lack of and delayed teacher salaries and incentives result in teacher apathy, high turnover and poor-quality teaching. Teachers’ salaries in South Sudan have not been adjusted for inflation—salaries that were around $100 per month in 2011 are currently worth less than $5.Children with disabilities are among the most affected as over half of schools report their latrines are inaccessible for children with disabilities. Teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for children with disabilities.The number of child protection cases raised significantly during the schools closure related to COVID-19, with the high rise in learner pregnancy. Schools play a key role in identification of child protection cases, however, referral mechanisms in over 30% of schools are not functional. There is a strong need for integration of the two sectoral interventions.

2 - Project activities

● Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces. Repair and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows locks. ● Construction and/or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLS) ● Establishment or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, including latrines (gender-segregated) using the Sani Tweak approach of consult, modify and consult approach, water points and soap provision for hand-washing, and provision of dry chlorine. ● Distrubution of hygiene materials e.g. soap, sanitisers, masks for students and teachers. ● Health and hygiene promotion for children, youth and care-givers ● Chlorination, hygiene messaging on Covid-19, cholera symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention by school-based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth). ● Creating school hygiene clubs to support messaging, provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines. ● Provision of furniture, equipment, textbooks, learning and teaching materials ● Provision of school materials support ● Organizing recreational activities to emergency-affected children, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities. ● Establishment of child/youth-friendly spaces, and recreational activities for adolescents and youth. ● Porviding ECD materials for schools, ● Teacher professional development including training on Education in Emergencies-EiE (child-centred approaches to pedagogy, classroom management, lesson planning and cross-cutting issues), child well-being (physical protection and PSS) and life skills. This will also include in-classroom coaching. ● Training of PTA and SMCs on school management and other cross-cutting issues including protection issues, psycho-social support, the inclusion of people with disabilities. ● Menstrual hygiene education and menstrual hygiene kits provision, aiming to increase girls’ attendance. Educational sessions on menstrual hygiene target both boys and girls to raise awareness and limit the stigma. ● Creating a school environment clubs to maintain the school environment through the planting of trees and vegetables. This will empower students to promote good environmental behaviour in the schools and the community.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to define the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # of children and youth (3-17 years old) accessed and attended schools in affected areas. # of temporary learning spaces established (in line with Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies- Minimum Standard for Education in Emergencies (INEE-MSEE) # of temporary learning spaces rehabilitated # of school children got access to gender-sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards) # of school children benefited from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards) # of learners received sanitation and hygiene materials # of children and youth (6-17 years) benefited from recreational material supplies. # of teachers trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies. # of teachers learners trained and updated on PSS and on the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms # of teachers and education personnel trained on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health and nutrition. # of community education committee members and Local Education officials trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assitance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, inc. clusters.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In the case of children with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH officers will visit them directly to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate parents, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism and the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. People can also share feedback and complaints through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who participate in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through: - institutions and HH surveys, - focus group discussions with representatives of various groups (teachers, parents, students disaggregated by gender and age), people with disabilities, - interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. PAH will give special attention to women and girls' voices to ensure the team's protection issues are well-known. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the community's caseworkers. They will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage adequate psychosocial support. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities; plans will be discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based caseworkers in GBV referrals, translators, enumerators. PAH will organise regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process and suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and used to adjust project activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project – Parents, Teachers, authorities, health club members and volunteers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback are kept and regularly analised by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. Under the supervision of Senior Management, the implementing programme team conducts regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback. PAH also take into consideration the feedback from the stakeholders, eg. from the previous Education project under SSHF the feedback was to produce the information materials in the local language, what was done in the following activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with precise AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Children from minority groups will be encouraged to attend child/youth friendly-spaces and enrol on school, where inclusion messaging will be part of the activities. PAH will target all vulnerable groups with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to find out about any gaps and address them. PAH will consult women and girls separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer to open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation, PAH can avoid aid diversion and ensure ownership of project results and community involvement in monitoring and controlling the outcomes. The project has precise risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated, and the security situation is monitored continuously. PAH coordinate and communicate with the log cluster to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, and local regulations that may include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

PAH has designed and construct flood resilient WASH facilities, including institutional level latrines. These will be implemented in the flood prone locations where feasible. Such solution will provide people access to water in the flooded areas and decrease the need of displacement.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Access to education for girls gives them better start at the labour market. Also provided access to knowledge and different support network at schools give them opportunity to broaden the horizon and change the traditional gender roles and their place in the society. Attending school is an efficient preventive measeure for different forms of GBV against girls, e.g. early merriage. Protection will be part of the project, as will be one of the main topics of training for parents, teachers and community members. Psycho-social support is a way of assistance to children who suffer from psychological stress or hold symptoms of traumas. PAH experienced Protection officers will assess the context and will conduct stakeholders mapping to ensure clear referral paths. They will provide this information to parents and teachers and encourage seeking psycho-social, legal and health support if needed. PAH provides multi-sector response including GBV and Child Portection subsectors, what guarantees access to needed services and comprehensive response within the project.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

In case of establishment of temporary learning spaces, rebuilding and equipping schools, PAH will encourage school feeding programs. Those programs will ensure at least one meal per day for each student, which will significantly improve the nutrition status of them. The parents and teachers will receive training about nutrition and food security good practices and schools will have vegetable gardens established. They will act as examples and encouragement for parents and other community members for gardening practices. This comprehensive actions may result in decreasing number of population facing IPC high levels.
