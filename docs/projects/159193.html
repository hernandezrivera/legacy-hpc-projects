Project Summary

Although there has been a slight improvement in the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate in Somalia from Post GU 2018 (15.5%) , to the 13.8% noted following the FSNAU Post GU 2019 assessment, Amongst the total burden of the 1,008,500 children who are likely to be malnourished, 178,400 are likely to be SAM in 2019 . The malnutrition situation is likely to worsen in pastoral areas as the impact of the drought on livestock has led to a lack of access to milk, and many poor households have accumulated large debts while struggling to feed their families. Although the 2019 Deyr (October-December) rains are forecasted to be average to above average, the positive impact on food security will not be realized until late 2020. IDPs and pastoral communities are vulnerable to high food insecurity across the regions and this is mainly due to low purchasing power, lack of diversified income sources, leading to food scarcity, irregular meals and this also contributes to high diseases incidence resulting from lack of appetite and poor feeding practice and poor child spacing. Childhood morbidity is also leading to malnutrition as a result of poor hygiene and access to clean water. The water scarcity in the northern regions has triggered high water prices which are further expected to worsen during the dry seasons. CARE conducted and IYCF KAP survey in Sep 2018 in Sool and Sanag region and found sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices. There are a lot of myths surrounding pregnancy and breastfeeding in the community and this needs to be tackled through tailored IYCF counselling at community level through mother to mother support group structures. There is high rate of bottle-feeding in Sool and Sanag regions (46.8% ) and poor diet diversity (4.6%) as well as sub-otpimal exclusive breastfeeing rates (68%). In light of this and to continue building on the gains made in 2019 CARE will continue to implement an integrated nutrition and health intervention which will also be linked with other CARE wash and food security programs. This integrated nutrition and health program will consist of several components and cover both curative and preventative interventions targeting 23,500 children U5 years (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women living in Bari, Sool and Sanag regions covering 9 districts (Bosaso, Badhan. Dhahar, Lasqoray, Ceelafweyn, Erigavo, Ainabo, Lascanod and Talex), . The intervention will provide access to basic and emergency nutrition and health services through support to key MOH staff at mobile and fixed clinics to enable delivery of outreach services. Nutrition activities will include identification and screening of acute malnutrition at both the community level and facility level; admissions into OTP, TSFP and SC services; referral of complicate cases; micronutrient supplementations including Vitamin A and IYCF counselling and promotions services at community and facility level. In addition to beneficiaries will also receive basic health services including outpatient disease consultations and treatments; referral of complicated cases for inpatient care; EPI immunization; antenatal and postnatal case service; referral to EmoC services and delivery by skilled birth attendants.

Project Activities

The integrated nutrition and health program of CARE Somalia targets the most vulnerable in the community namely PLW and children under 5 suffering from acute malnutrition. The services are integrated with health and will be both curative and preventative in nature and including identification and treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM), community mobilisations and referral of complicated cases of malnutrition as well as children diagnosed with severe form of childhood morbidity for instabilization services. The PLW and Caregivers will also receive IYCF promotions and counselling services helping them to learn more about the optimal child feeding practices to improve the rate of exclusive breastfeeding among children under 6 months and dietary diversity and continued breastfeeding support for upto 2 years. The caregivers will also be targeted for Health and hygiene promotions to prevent childhood morbidity prevalent in the area including ARIs and diarrhoea which may lead to malnutrition amongst the vulnerable children. Handwashing with soap during critical times, importance of immunization, family planning methods will be addressed to support them mother and to enable her to make an informed decision regarding her child's health. The Caregivers will also be targeted for hygiene kit distributions and the program will be linked to CARE WASH services ongoing in the area so that they can purify their drinking water at home and use soap for handwashing as well as receive appropriate water storage buckets to avoid contamination of their water. Care givers and children will receive multiple micronutrient supplementations including Vitamin A to promote better health. They will also receive basic health services including immunizations, antenatal and prenatal services, referral of complication including transport support and food. CARE Somalia is also implementing CASH plus support project in Sool and Sanag and this will also ensure that a good number of nutrition beneficiaries will also benefit from unconditional cash support uring the dry season when food insecurity spikes to protect the gains made and to avoid relapse and further deteriorations of nutritional status amongst the most vulnerable households. Therefore will this in mind CARE Somalia is going to target Bari, Sool, Sanag regions for an integrated and nutrition sensitive programming to treat and prevent malnutrition amongst 18,000 women and children under 5 affected by drought and displacement in 2020.

Mitigation Measures

2a) Marginalised communities may feel excluded from the services provided and to tackle this CARE will 1) ensure they are identified during the design stage and mapped out; 2) consult with them during the design of the project to ensure they feel safe to access the sevrices provided by CARE: 3) CARE will ensure a representative of the marginalised group is always present during screening and identification process in ongoing as a community volunteer; 4) follow up on any case of concern throught the FCRM mechanism in place in case there is any marginalisation and address it; 5) Inform communities and beneficiaries about CAREs policies regarding discrimination and impartiality of assistance to improve awareness raising and show zero tolerance to discrimination based on creed and race. 4b) CARE recognizes this could be a risk as some beneficaries will be excluded from the nutrition admission criteria due to their nutritional status and age as the programs only target malnourished boys and girls aged 6-59 months and PLW. This may therefore expose them to risk of violence during registration/ ration distributions by those not in the program. CARE will mitigate against this risk by: consulting the village elders and involving them in community mobilisations sessions including screening process; to use them for crowd control and to inform them about the admission criteria. 2) CARE will follow security measures thouroughly so as to not expose them to violence by operating appropriate hours; 3) ensure the centre is close as possible; 4) centre is gated; 5)adequate number of project staff are present to prevent long waiting hours. 4C- Care recognises there is risk of extortion or theft of the nutrition supplies and to mitigate against this we will do the following. 1) Provide a safe storage for all nutrition supplies in the health centre with a lock and key system to avoid theft by the community or staff; 2) Conduct physcial stock count after each distributions to identify theft of supplies or misuse; 3) Provide biweekly rations of the supply so as to not expose beneficiaries to risk of extortion or theft by carrying a large sum of supplies; 4) ensure that the community elders are present during distributions and community volunteers to provide awareness raising about the use and intention of the supplies; 5) to investigate any mis-use and encourage caregivers to come forward about any case of extortion or harassment during the consultations. 4d- Although CARE has complaint mechanisms there may be barriers preventing beneficaries to report concerns and CARE will mitigate against this risks by1 1) always ensuring there are multiple and confidential ways of reporting; 2) make sure that beneficiaries feel protected and that their concerns will not reach a thrid party without their approval; 3) ensure there are alwasy a female and a male staff present at the centres if the persons feels safer to report concerns face to face; 4) to respond to concerns in a timely and efficient manner so as to encourage beneficairies to come forward; 5) to always be impartial and make clear to the beneficiaries that CARE will not tolerate anyone harming their beneficaries for seeking services and appropriate referral GBV pathways are identified and used.
