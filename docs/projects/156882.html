Project Summary

AHA will provide shelter support through rehabilitating of 300 households living in Afrin sub-district and in Jenderis subdistrict in Aleppo governorate.
In order to improve the humanitarian response and alleviate suffering of IDPs and host communities and vulnerable populations in conflict-afflicted areas, AHA will support the rehabilitation of 300 Households.
AHA will target HHs that have been partially destroyed due to continued damage as a result of aerial/land attacks in addition to general dilapidation from poor living conditions (AHA will verify that by Local Councils and members of the host community that reside in those homes). In addition to the repair of small damages in walls, doors, broken windows, locks and deteriorated plumbing, AHA will install one solar panel system in each targeted HHs to provide an alternative source of lighting in an energy scarce environment. All these actions and activities will be in line and guided strictly by the ''Shelter repair and rehab guidelines'' issued by the shelter cluster. Mainly, shelter type grade 2 as Residential housing (single or multiple-story buildings), partially damaged or unfinished. Also, this activity will be implemented through two types of contract modalities, the first is through contractor for the for procurement and transportation of materials, coordination of works, including hiring of staff, health and safety practices as well as a commitment that the work is completed on time in addition to the mass works, while the other type is through CFW in which those workers will work in areas that do not needs technical expertise and will consider the age and gender related consideration ensuring that women and men and if applicable people with disabilities are included in CFW matching their expertise witht the required functions.
The methodology of Cash-for-Work programming is embedded in the action as a short-term intervention to provide temporary employment in this specific shelter project, workers will be deployed in works (such as measurements taking, clearing debris or re-building infrastructure) to the most vulnerable segments of a population.

Needs

Till 2019, 870,343 IDPs are residing in Aleppo governorate with total population of 1,3 million are in need for shelter and NFI assistance in only Aleppo rather than bigger number if we considered Syria as a whole, in September 2018 more than 84k people were displaced newly in Syria, where almost have of them reside in Aleppo Afrin district due after the security situation changed in Afrin. Both targeted subdistrict Afrin and Jandairis are marked 3 and 4 on severity level. The systematic targeting of social infrastructure including civilian homes has been a major driving force behind mass internal displacement. The failure to rehabilitate deteriorated homes and secure stable shelters for families has perpetuated the challenges of internal displacement and for host communities rather than deescalating the crisis in a time when the overall security situation has stabilized as a result of the cessation of hostilities. Families deprived of adequate shelter provision suffer from a wide spectrum of fundamental issues that erode their quality of life, continue to deprive them of essential resources, diminish their physical and mental protection and reduce their overall resilience/ability to respond to conflict-related events. In addition to poor sanitation, exposure to hazardous environmental conditions and lack of safety, families endure psychological instability that arises from lack of privacy within degraded homes. In December 2018 and January 2019, AHA had conducted a full needs assessment for most of the sub-districts in Afrin, where we received many appeals from LCs to see the bad condition of the houses, therefore, our out-reach team headed to the location and accomplish the rapid assessment (document with narrative report will be issued in Feb) where some findings of the surveys were:It showed a great and urgent need for:- Construction of walls and enclosed and fully functioning latrines with doors will maximize privacy and decrease overcrowding.- The lack of separation between men, women, children and members of different families residing in the same shelters, as well as the need for women and children to travel to latrines at night unaccompanied due to degraded conditions exacerbate the risks of sexual and domestic violence and child abuse. - IDPs residing in unfinished building (especially the communities of AFRIN and JANDAIRIS) with no windows and doors nor even locks or lights which puts a great danger from protection perspective on the IDPs. To address this problem, each targeted HH will receive a solar panel system (300 total) to provide beneficiaries with an alternative source of energy to promote independence and sustainability in an energy scarce environment. rapid needs assessments which will be done by AHA shelters’ crew to identify households and community members through collaboration with Local Councils within the two locations to aggregate data on the most vulnerable individuals within these sub districts before the implementation of the acitvities. AHA will identify the following in the targeted communities: the resident of the house, is he/she the owner of the house, is the family IDPs or locals, what is the income, is the property rented or owned, how many families in the house, how many indvs, Based on the greatest beneficiary needs, AHA will focus on:• / Rehabilitation or construction of concrete structures and blocks for walls (Plastering, maintenance, repairs, painting) due to major structural damage from aerial strikes and shelling of civilian areas• / Repair of plumbing/piping of homes with damaged latrines• / Installation of electrical wiring for solar panel installation• / Latrine installation to homes without functional latrines• / Replacement of damaged or destroyed doors and addition of locks• / Replacement of broken windows• / Repair of damaged walls and replacement of destroyed walls• / Repair or replacement of ceilings according to level of damage

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Armed opposition groups and government-controlled areas continue promoting an enabling environment for aid delivery and information gathering in Syria, and aid delivery continues to be accepted and possible in Syria despite of the presence of various armed groups. Risk a: There is a risk that NGOs may be accused of supporting the political aims of one group or another and lose neutrality. There is a risk that armed groups may respond by taking a harder line, including restricting access of humanitarian groups in specific areas. Mitigation strategy: since AHA is a well-known local organization in Syria, AHA ensures that if certain actors are no longer accepted by opposition groups, the activities can be transferred to other center which may still be able to implement. Risk b: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/ stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping. Mitigation strategy: AHA will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/ violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/ violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented to mitigate against the possibility of an armed group seizing control of all of AHA stock inside Syria, or consider alternative areas/ activities under the planned intervention with the approval from the donor. Assumption 2 – Access to Syria remains possible and safe from the neighboring countries. Risks: A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the borders or and/or negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria Mitigation strategy: As a registered NGO in Turkey, AHA is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change. Assumption 3 – Markets are functioning properly in the area identified Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/ or supplies. Mitigation strategy: AHA's teams have confirmed the market is functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. Assumption 4: The project does not create distortions in the local economy Risk: As consequence of the project, there is a shortage of materials in the area and the prices increase. Mitigation strategy: contractors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information. AHA also designed this project to correspond to the needs of most vulnerable groups and considered them in all project phases. and will ensure to correspond to the 4Ws in coordination with shelter cluster respectively.
