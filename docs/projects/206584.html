Project Summary

South Sudan faces an increased risk of explosive hazards where over 18 million m² land is suspected of being contaminated with landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW). With the increased numbers of Returnees from Uganda, Ethiopia and Sudan to South Sudan anticipated in 2024 due to the coming general election. More people will be at risk of explosive ordnances because of the lack of knowledge on the hazards prevalent and what to do when they are discovered. Explosive ordnances inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, and receiving health care, among other essential services. They further prevent humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas and emergencies.
South Sudan Mine Action Area of Responsibility has planned to reach 507,000 people with risk education messages, 402,000 among them returnees and IDPs in 2024 with life-saving mine action services before they reach their intended destinations. For this reason, CINA’s EORE project will target returnees, IDPs and host communities who had no and/or little knowledge on the risky behaviors. These EORE activities will seeks to reduce the risk of injury from mines and Explosive Ordnance by raising awareness and promoting behavioral change, including public information dissemination, education and training at community level. It will be the role of Explosive Ordnances Risk Educators to move from community to community, schools, churches, door to door etc. sensitizing and raising the awareness on safety and prevention messages about the EO. CINA EORE project will employ preventive measures to deal with injuries which come as lack of knowledge especially to the returnees and IDPs who may not be well conversant with the local marking signs. CINA will reach 123, 392 people (43,190 returnees, 43,200 IDPs and 37,002 Host communities including people with disability to be reached with EORE messaging in Jonglei state (Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Ayod and Akobo) and Upper Nile State (Panyikang, Malakal, Fashoda and Melut).
This EORE project will ensure safety of all beneficiaries and encourage them to provide feedback on the relevant of EORE materials, messages and other concerns about EORE activities as means of Accountability to the Affected Population and coordinate with Mine Action Area of Responsibility in all activities. CINA will deploy EORE teams to both counties. From experience, this approach is more sustainable because CINA normally strives to work with local structures as much as possible. Previous CINA EORE Teams will be engaged in implementing the EORE activities to continue with the project.

1. Needs

The Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) data for June 2023 reported about 447 hazards identified. This is an increased from last year identification due to multiple factors such as new arrivals from Refugees camps and Internally Displaced people. Actors implementing EORE activities recognize that estimating the true extent of landmines and EO contamination within South Sudan is a real challenge specifically within counties with high number of returnees like Jonglei and Upper Nile in particular. There are existing testimonies where CINA has been implementing EORE activities through SSHF and UNICEF funds. Despite several years of interventions by different humanitarian actors in regards to EORE activities in South Sudan, many people including host community, IDPs and Returnees are still engaged in practicing risky behaviors that continue to cause injuries and death of civilians. This is evident also by the fact that although a significant number of UXO incidents are reported to EORE survey123, the exact number of EO casualties in the Country is still unknown and is considered to be more higher than the number of the cases reported. It has been realized that explosive hazards prohibit civilians from a safe and secure environment in which they can access life-saving services, collect water, firewood or other daily subsistence needs such as cultivation of land and/or grazing of animals. This is more practical in deep field where landmines and unexploded ordinance (UXO) inhibit the ability of women, men, girls, and boys to meet their basic needs and access opportunities to survive. The targeted beneficiaries need more EORE messages to be in the know of the dangers encompassing their lives when they approach unknown substances/UXOs that can be harmful to their lives or to those who live among them in the same locality. Such unknown objects may include anti-personnel mines, anti-tank mines, unexploded ordinances among others. CINA has experienced EORE Teams who have been trained through funds by UNICEF and SSHF. These competent staff will be used as well as the existing EORE banners printed by UNICEF and NMAA to scale up education in schools, markets and other public places where there are systematic gathering on weekly basis. This project will conduct need assessments to inform risk education for above mentioned Counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile States.

2. Project activities

To address the needs of the crises-affected vulnerable people’s, CINA will provide the following protection risks mitigation activities to uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action and informed by communities’ priorities, and in particular these HPC Specific Objectives of Women, men, girls and boys, people with disabilities and the elderly are protected from life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation and injury, as well as have access to specialized life-saving services. CINA plans to reach 123,392 people with EORE education awareness raising sessions to people (including those with disabilities) on key EORE messages. Every EORE Session in school and community will take approximately 45 mins each. Due to large number of children in school, arrangements will be made with teachers so that it is done per class; there will be two sessions in a particular school. The schools normally accept 3 days visit in a week and the attendance per class is usually between 30 - 50 . However, the introduction sessions might have the whole school parade to benefit from public information dissemination for more pupils to be informed on EORE messages. The team will use the EOR printed banners provided by UNICEF, NMAA and UNMAS since they are generally accepted. Conduct EORE awareness sessions for humanitarian actors from GBV, child protection, health, FSL working groups/actors to improve coordination and/or referral as well as reporting in service delivery for mine/ERW survivors and PWDs. Having an integrated approach across all sectors will help CINA actors receive information related to EO fast and help in effective decision making as well as protection mainstreaming. Equipping every sector with key EORE messages will help the humanitarian actors take care of their own safety together with others as they respond in their project locations. Conduct community-based EORE training to caregivers of mine/ERWs victims, community leaders, local authority leader, women and youth groups. These topics to be covered will include but not limited to education on dangers of EO, learning about local markings around suspected UXOs, how and who to report any spotted mine/ERW, what to do when one comes across an UXO, informing others about which areas are safe/unsafe, information on small arms and light weapons (SALW), etc. Convene one-day training to teachers/PTAs/SMCs on EORE messages to impact knowledge to the children at school and their families. Members of Parents and Teachers Association (PTAs), School Management Committees (SMCs) who will be trained on key EORE messages will act as EORE Mentors in schools, homes and places of residence and in their communities. Provide training to adolescent peer educators on EORE messaging to support in delivery of risk education safety messages in schools and community. Most of the at-risk populations are often children and women. Training and support to peer educators will not only help in disseminating EORE messages to large number of people but will help in project sustainability. CINA will therefore train adolescent peer educators on RE per targeted location for a period of 3 days on the key EORE messages. They will then be tasked to replicate the same messages in schools and community. Establish/strengthen and train Disabled People's Organizations to advocate for the rights of mine/ERWs survivors, PWDs among other minority groups. After establishment/strengthening of DPOs, training of the DPOs members will help in the sustainability of the project after the project closure. The DPO members will be empowered to act as agents of change and keep informing the communities of the dangers of mine/ERWs and importance of risk education to the community members, IDPs and Returnees. In each county, there will be established 1 DPO. Provide training for CINA EORE team, accreditation, internal quality assurance and IMSMA reporting including on EORE survey 123 software.
