Project Summary

FSA 2024 Syria HNO indicates worsening food security, impacting 15.5 million people (65% of the population), based on a country-wide assessment of 42,000 HHs. This marks an increase from the previous year, with 12.9 million needing food assistance & 2.6 million at risk. Concentrated in areas like Idleb (73%), Al-Hasakeh (74%), Quneitra (65%), Hama (59%), Ar-Raqqa (59%), Aleppo (58%), & Deir-ez-Zor (50%), food insecurity is exacerbated by displacement, especially in NWS & NES hubs, with 73.4% of IDPs affected. Recurring disease outbreaks, drought, & economic instability escalate mortality, morbidity, & humanitarian needs. Economic challenges, including currency depreciation, spur displacements. Agricultural constraints, such as climate change & limited inputs, strain vulnerable farmers. In response, IRC's 2024 ERD strategy enhances livelihood opportunities, climate-smart agriculture, & market systems reinforcement. IRC aligns interventions with ERL & FSL objectives. In ERL, IRC enhances and increases access to LVDs & job opportunities in NWS. In FSL, support self-reliance, & enhance resilience through irrigation rehabilitation, food processing & women's agribusiness. Interventions target financially vulnerable beneficiaries to reduce negative coping strategies. Livelihood projects offer vocational training & start-up support, prioritizing economically vulnerable groups. IRC focuses on climate-smart interventions for food security & income generation. Training in climate-smart agriculture is provided, followed by support for input & irrigation systems. CFW initiatives create temporary employment & promote economic inclusion. Agribusiness support empowers women, promoting gender equality, climate adaptation, & agricultural resilience. Gender-specific needs are addressed, SADD information is collected. Community tension due to perceived inequality & discrimination poses risks for staff. Mitigation includes establishing area-based coordination groups, ongoing engagement with community leaders, & communication. Selection criteria based on vulnerabilities & collaboration with protection service providers ensure targeted assistance. Continuous M&E with feedback mechanisms, clear complaint channels, community participation, & staff training on PSEA are crucial. The medium likelihood & high impact of beneficiary safety risks & exploitation are mitigated through collaborative planning, coordination, & adjusting project plans. Integrating protection priorities, staff adherence to IRC policies, conflict sensitivity, & risk assessment tools ensure safety & accessibility. GBV mitigation measures aligned with international guidelines reduce risks in project locations.
FSL & livelihoods team, supported by the protection team, will promote "Do No Harm" principles & PSEA. Working with community leaders, representatives, & elders, IRC raises stakeholder awareness about rights & responsibilities. Clients are informed of entitlements & support procedures. Stakeholders & staff are sensitized to uphold humanitarian principles. Confidential reporting mechanisms are available to staff & clients, encouraging use of IRC's feedback system. IRC will integrate program activities with the protection sector to assist survivors & ensure referral pathways during beneficiary selection, data sharing, & follow-up. IRC ensures marginalized groups review selection criteria, conducts FGDs, & engages authorities to clarify services & expectations. Pre-project security assessments address conflict risks, & clear FARM mitigate impartiality risks. Protection Risk Analysis identifies tension in the community due to perceived inequality, addressed through robust selection criteria mechanisms & ongoing engagement with community leaders. Medium likelihood risks, like physical safety of clients, taxation, & exploitation/abuse, are mitigated through collaborative implementation plans, amendments to project plans, & awareness of FARM among clients.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

IRC's interventions are strategically aligned with the goals of the ERL and FSL sectors. Within the ERL framework, IRC focuses on enhancing livelihood access in NWS. In the FSL sector, IRC's activities support self-reliance by providing urgent agricultural inputs and enhancing community resilience through value chain enhancement and irrigation infrastructure rehabilitation. Additionally, IRC empowers women in agribusiness to promote gender equality.Based on market assessments in NWS, IRC targets beneficiaries facing financial shocks, offering vocational training/apprenticeships followed by start-up support and enterprise assistance. Priority is given to economically vulnerable households, including women, the elderly, PWDs, and youth. PWDs are identified through WGQs, with tailored programs to meet their specific needs.In agricultural support, IRC focuses on farmers for food security and income generation through climate-smart interventions. Training on climate-smart agriculture is followed by support for environment-friendly inputs and irrigation system rehabilitation, creating employment through Cash for Work initiatives. IRC also supports women in Climate-Smart Agriculture Initiatives, providing training and start-up assistance.For the FSL proposed interventions; business assets and materials duration is one month, for the new business startup assets duration is 2 months, and for the CFW is 20 days. Protection Risk Analysis identifies high tension due to inequality and discrimination, mitigated through coordination, engagement, and monitoring. The PSEA strategy emphasizes staff training, collaboration, and community awareness, with measures including reporting and referrals, and engagement with local authorities.The food processing intervention targets 150 households, providing them with electrical appliances, pots, and packaging materials to start or enhance small-scale food production businesses. Each household receives an average grant of $500, with one month of training and support.For irrigation support, 80 households (20 wells serving four households each) will receive pumps, solar energy systems, irrigation networks, and concrete tank building materials to boost agricultural production. The intervention, carried out by the IRC team, lasts three months at an average cost of $20,000 per well. Agricultural input support aims to benefit 550 households with pesticides, fertilizers, and seeds to increase crop yields and overall agricultural production. Each farmer receives an average grant of $350, with support provided once over the course of one month.In summary, the interventions consist of food processing support for 150 households, irrigation support for 80 households through the installation of wells and equipment, and agricultural input support for 550 households to enhance crop yields.
