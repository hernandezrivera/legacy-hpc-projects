Project Summary

The devastating earthquakes at the beginning of the year combined with the ongoing, protracted conflict have uprooted hundreds of thousands of families , needing humanitarian assistance for their survival. As a result of these challenges, According to HNO 2024, 4.832.758 M people will be in need to ER interventions in Aleppo and Idleb governorates , and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs.
The proposed project will deliver a main three interventions that will contribute to enhancing the resilience of the most vulnerable households in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, which have a high a number of affected people in high severity needs locations, through:
1. Providing 890 workers with cash for work activities as follow:
• Distribution of free bread in the camps 30 workers/156 day/5 $
• Olive picking for / 400 hectare/ in Jandairis and Idleb 750 workers/22 D/5$
• Rehabilitation work for irrigation channels 80 workers 20 D /6 $
• Participation in activities of the municipality; (30 Workers/60D/5$) The selection of these 30 workers will be in cooperation with local council in the targeted communities These activities include cleaning up dust, opening road drains, and clearing snow during the winter. The workers will cooperate with local councils to ensure that these activities are carried out efficiently and
effectively.
2. Provide vocational, skills training, and SME trainings based on market mapping to 500 beneficiaries (males and females including PWDs).they will be selected according to the selection criteria agreed with the Protection Cluster, the project will identify the most vulnerable households who face challenges to access to livelihoods opportunities and provide the following trainings: (tailoring, wool knitting, handcrafts, hairdressing, food consumption, technical business training, babysitting, Manufacture of home-made detergents ,and electronic literacy). 30 trainers will be contracted with, and the trainees will be supervised by the project's M&E team as well as by the GBV/Protection team during the cash recipients as per the SOPs of the GBV/CVA task force. The M&E team will conduct the pre/posttest for the BNFs as well taking the attendance for each training session.
3. Providing 300 small grants (of 1500 $) to 300 beneficiaries who are selected from the previous activity to start small business entrepreneurship initiative based on the evaluation scores provided by the trainers during the vocational and skill training with the vulnerability scores resulted during the registration and verification phase. the results will be shared with the relevant clusters and SCHF as means of verification. The trainers (coaches) will provide coaching and mentoring to help the grant receivers correct any mistakes and resolve appearing issues before entering the labor market.
This activity will ensure that business coaching and business support is conducting in a timely and adequate manner. The project team will coordinate the monitoring activities to ensure that business coaching and business support is conducting in a timely and adequate manner.
The risks of SEA or GBV can be faced during implementation, Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: -Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by integrating PSEA in Sadad policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting accidents are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Risk: The principle of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objectives based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process. Mitigation measures:Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community board.PSEA strategy:Risks: -Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes. -Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA. -Lack of availability of information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries. -Lack of female staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures: -Code of conduct includes PSEA language. - All staff are trained on PSEA. - nternal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization. - PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. -All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks. -Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complaint mechanisms and hotlines. -All activities will be implemented in safe and secure environment with clear and applicable contingency plan. -Do-No harm approach is adopted while supporting MSMEs so the local vendors will not negatively affected by the intervention. Sadad will ensure CfW daily wage rates do not distort local labour market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities, therefore harming local producers. Risk :CFW participants do not always work the full day Mitigation : 1.Set up a system for payment for partial days. Risk : CFW may cause disruption to local labour markets. Mitigation : 1. Do not set wage rates above the average level. 2. Limit the number of days that people can work with the project. Risk : Weather conditions and annual events delay CFW timelines Mitigation : Consult weather forecasts and annual calendar events when planning the timetable for CFW activities.
