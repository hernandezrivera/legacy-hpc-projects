Project Summary

The purpose of this project is improve access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities via Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) methodology for returnee populations and existing communities in the villages of Korma Administration Unit, El Fasher Locality, North Darfur. In targeting these services (water and sanitation) VCOD's overall objective is to improve the health of the target population by reducing waterborne disease and disease spread by vectors such as flies. In addition VCOD will complement the activities by conducting hygiene promotion activities such as household visits and distribution of soap at household level. VCOD plans to improve access to safe water by rehabilitating 25 hand pumps and VCOD will conduct training for 50 community members in hand pump mechanics with minimum 50% female participation. VCOD will also provide training in water safety planning for 75 community members, again with minimum 50% female participation and assist communities in drawing up their own Water Safety Plan. VCOD, supported by UNICEF, has triggered 29 communities in Korma AU to be Open Defecation Free (ODF). In order to assist Sudan in implementing the roadmap to ODF by 2022, VCOD proposes using CLTS methodology to further trigger 30 communities in this project to become ODF by digging and constructing their own pit latrines at household level. VCOD also proposes to construct VIP latrines at institutional level, in this project 2 school latrines and 1 at health centre level with water distribution system. Given the current pandemic of COVID 19 and one of the cornerstones of infection prevention and control being hand-washing VCOD Hygiene Promotion activities will focus on hand-washing at critical times and correct hand-washing technique and complement with soap distribution. The project also envisions a contribution to durable peace by providing increased access to these services and thereby reducing competition for resources.

1 - Needs

VCOD is currently working in Korma Administration Unit, El Fasher Locality with support from UNICEF.During project implementation many needs have been identified which include but are not limited to lack of access to safe drinking water, lack of sanitation facilities at both household and institution level and a need to adopt improved hygiene practices with regard to water and food safety, hand-washing and sanitation . With the new peace agreement signed in Juba there is an expectation that some Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) may return to their villages of origin increasing the population in those areas and thereby the pressure on existing resources. It has also been cited that while some IDPs wish to return to their villages of origin, a lack of services in these areas is discouraging them from doing so. Community members are accessing water from open dug wells due to hand pumps not working and an inability to repair same. Open dug wells are a source of contaminated water due to their very nature, thereby increasing the incidence of water borne diseases such as diarrhoea. Lack of access to sanitation facilities and open defecation also contribute to the spread of disease and in following the ODF roadmap VCOD proposes using the CLTS methodology to address the issue of open defecation by triggering communities to feel disgust and shame at their current behaviours and compel them to provide their own sanitation facilities. Schools and Health centres are also lacking sanitation facilities in Korma Administration Unit. VCOD, during its WASH project, supported by UNICEF, in the IDP camps of Abu Shok and Al Salam, identified during this recent rainy season that a number of pits of communal/shared latrines collapsed resulting in damage to the superstructure and slabs. These communal latrines continue to serve approximately 25 households each and are necessary to maintaining ODF status in the IDP camps.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Project Activities include:1. Rehabilitation of 25 hand pumps in selected villages. This work will be conducted by VCOD's WASH officer with assistance from community members 2. Training of 50 community members in Hand pump mechanics. This is a 3day training course that will be conducted by VCOD WASH Officer. Provision of tools to village councils to ensure community members can maintain the hand-pumps. 3. Training of 125 community members in Water Safety Plan and assistance in devising a plan for each Community. This is a 3day training course that will be conducted by VCOD WASH Officer. 4. Triggering of 30 villages using CLTS methodology to become ODF. The 30 villages will be selected based on the needs assessment, that is no latrines at Household Level and Open Defecation present. Pre- triggering, 4 community members will be selected from each village and attend a 2 day training in CLTS methodology.Triggering will be conducted by VCOD Public Health Officer by the end of which community members should provide a date at which time they will have dug and constructed their household latrines and ceased from open defecation. Subsequent follow up visits by VCOD staff will record progress towards achieving ODF and assist the community in declaring themselves ODF. Post self declaration State Ministry of Health will be requested to conduct verification that the community is ODF and if certified ODF the community will celebrate their ODF status and be presented with certificates and signboards. 5. Construction of school latrines in 2 location with water distribution system. Construction tenders will be advertised and awarded as per VCOD procurement policies. There will be on-going liaison between VCOD structural engineer, locality engineer and contractor to ensure construction is of rigorous standard with frequent inspection by locality engineer. On completion of construction the buildings will be handed over to the Ministry of Education 6. Construction of health centre latrine in 1 location with water distribution system. Construction tenders will be advertised and awarded as per VCOD procurement policies. There will be on-going liaison between VCOD structural engineer, locality engineer and contractor to ensure construction is of rigorous standard with frequent inspection by locality engineer. On completion of construction the buildings will be handed over to the Ministry of Health. 7. Rehabilitation of 10 communal latrines in the IDP settlements of Abu Shok and Al Salam, El Fasher. Rehabilitation will involve reconstruction of pit and slabs. In line with VCOD's current strategy in the camp the community will be requested to provide the superstructure from local materials. 8. Training of 120 community members in hygiene promotion activities, with focus on behaviour change communication, health and hygiene promotion and hygiene messages relating to COVID 19 prevention. This 3 day training course will be conducted by VCOD Public Health staff with related IEC materials provided to assist in conducting health/hygiene promotion sessions. 9. Household visits and health awareness sessions. These activities will be conducted by the 120 community members trained (Community Hygiene Promoters, CHPs) over a period of 6 months. 10. Distribution of soap at household level on a monthly basis. Distribution of soap by 120 CHPs in order to encourage handwashing with soap and at critical times. 11. Provision of menstrual hygiene materials to older girls via schools in Korma AU. The materials will be distributed to girls in years 7 and 8 following active participation by the girls in discussions surrounding menstrual health and hygiene by VCOD Health staff.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

By rehabilitating existing hand pumps there will be less need for communities to resort to digging shallow wells in order to access water. The project should improve the surrounding environment from a public health point of view as the aim is to be Open Defecation Free.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

By triggering beneficiaries to stop open defecation and to dig pits at household level there is increased dignity for beneficiaries due to increased privacy. This should also reduce the need for women and children to travel distances from their homes in order to defecate with privacy. All institutional latrines will be fitted with locks from inside to ensure privacy and dignity for the user. These latrines will also include ramps for ease of access for older community members and those with disabilities. The latrines will be separated based on gender with clear signs in pictorial form indicating male and female. Rehabilitation of hand pumps will decrease distances for women and children to travel for water and any inherent risks there maybe in travelling outside their villages, such as risk of attack and gender based violence .
