Project Summary

The proposed project responds directly to the first and second objectives of the WASH Cluster, in addition to PWA’s national strategy to ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services with dignity for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians, as well as strengthening WASH preparedness and response capacity to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks in northern the Gaza Strip. Human Appeal in close coordination with CMWU and PWA aims to upgrade five public desalination plants by installing solar PV systems and providing maintenance to the five desalination plants in order to improve access of 28,500 women, men, girls and boys, of the most vulnerable, to safe drinking water in north of the Gaza Strip. The project will increase the access to drinking water of target groups from 2 liters per person per day up to 6 liters per person per day.
The project also aims at raising public awareness of community on water conservation and the safe practices for transporting and storing the water to minimize public health risks and contamination. This will be achieved by disseminating leaflets, brochures and organising public awareness-raising meetings with the local community.
The project also contributes to two Sustainable Development Goals No. 6 and 7 by improving availability of safe drinking water and reducing the critical gaps in accessing water in the target areas, and also ensuring access to affordable reliable and modern energy for all.

Needs

While all the population of the Gaza Strip is negatively affected by the current ongoing needs in WASH sector, Gaza Northern Governorate is severely affected by lack of drinking water. Out of 364,188 total population of Gaza Northern Governorate, only %14.2 of the population (26,316 men, including 12,829 boys and 25,399 women including 12,227 girls) have had access to safe drinking water in 2017 according to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics in 2017. This leaves approximately 159,009 men, including 77,513 boys and 153464 women, including 73,877 girls without access to safe drinking water. There are 7 public desalination plants across Gaza Northern Governorate (3 in Jabalya, 3 in Beit Lahiya and 1 in Beit Hanoun). However, none of these public plants has worked at its full capacity since they were established due to chronic shortage of electricity supply. The current access rate to safe drinking water in northern Gaza is 2 liters per capita per day due to the fact that the public desalination plants only operate for 4hrs daily. Chronic shortage of electricity threatens the sustainability of the operation of desalination plants and exposes desalination plants to frequent repair needs. The severe deterioration of WASH services in the Gaza Strip has led to sharp decline of the average water quota supplied to the people liter/capita /day down to 45-53 liters/Capita/day which is way below WHO’s standards of 100 liters per capita per day, affecting harmfully the level of public health in Gaza strip and exposing men, women, girls and boys to public health risks such as waterborne diseases and infections. Human Appeal has conducted a series of group discussions and semi-structured household interviews with women and men from various localities in north of Gaza where men and women expressed serious concerns about limited access to safe drinking water and high price of purchasing water given that so many families have no sources of income to afford purchasing water. According to a mapping exercise conducted by Human Appeal’s strategic partner Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), Gaza Northern Governorate was selected as a priority area for this proposed project based on several criteria including who is doing what where, and what is in the pipeline and what the current gaps are. While all women, men, girls and boys are equally exposed to the various WASH risks in the community, women and children seem to be differently affected by the consequences. For example, in Jabalya and Beit Lahiya, where the project will be carried out, young boys and girls and women play roles in collecting water from stations and desalination plants, exposing themselves to protection or/and public health risks. Lack of general awareness of the community has led to various contamination of drinking water due to malpractices in transportation and storage of drinking water. Previous WASH studies showed the importance of awareness raising activities to clarify some misconceptions about water desalination. There is also a lack of general awareness on hygiene practices when it comes to water storage at home.

Activities

1. Technical capacity assessment of five Public Desalination Plants in Beit Lahiya and Jabalya:CMWU has already conducted an initial assessment of the public desalination plants in northern Gaza and identified the key information such as current production capacity, operating hours, PV power consumption, and area availability for installation of solar panels. A detailed assessment will focus on the gaps in capacity and technical specifications needed to upgrade the capacity of units to reach out to more households for longer time. 2. Provide maintenance and upgrade public desalination plants:This activity will improve water quality of the five desalination plants by 146m3 per day instead of 36.5m3 per day (increase plants’ capacity from 18% to 71.5%), in order to provide 28,500 men, women, boys and girls with 6 liters of drinking water per person per day instead of only 2l/c/d northern Gaza.A specific technical assessment will be conducted for the five public desalination plants to identify key areas for upgrade and maintenance to reach the targeted production capacity. Upgrade and maintenance works will include: replacement of some parts such as membranes, primary pumps, high pressure pumps, etc.3. Installation of Solar PV Systems for five public desalination plants:To ensure uninterrupted water supply, solar PV systems will be installed at the 5 pre-selected public desalination plants northern Gaza. Each plant will have a 10kw PV power consumption capacity. Installed solar panels will increase the production of desalinated water from 4hrs a day only to up to 16hrs per day, leading to much improved access to safe drinking water by the community.Human Appeal and its implementing partner will follow the financial and procurement standard operating procedures to develop tenders and identify contractors and technical experts to carry out this task.To ensure maximum impact and sustainability of the project, a maintenance agreement will be in place to ensure the quality of the installed solar PV systems during and beyond the project duration.Although the used technology requires low maintenance on the long term, CMWU has a plan in place for running and maintaining these plants after the completion of the project.There would be no environmental, economic or social negative impact of the project. In fact, this activity will be a support to environmental preservation.4. Raising Public Awareness on Best Practices for Transporting and Storing Water (3000 direct beneficiaries):To prevent public health risks, a targeted awareness raising campaign/activities will be carried out, targeting women, men, boys and girls in Jabalya and Beit Lahiya where the project will be implemented. Printed materials with visual illustrations will be disseminated to the wider community in northern Gaza to minimize public health risks at all lifecycle of the water production, transportation and storage. 10 public meetings will be held in targeted localities to raise awareness of the public on water conservation and safe water practices. Such awareness activities will be coordinated with other active WASH cluster members in the targeted areas. 5. Monitoring methodology:Human Appeal has a dedicated M&E team headed by Programmes Manager. Project M&E system will take into account the project work plan, objectives, outputs and intended end-of-project impact. The frequent project monitoring visits during the project cycle will be conducted by the monitoring team. The project successes, lessons learned and observation will be documented and the gaps will be identified and shared with the donor.The local community is involved in informing the needs of their localities for water access through participation in preliminary needs assessment. The community will participate in monitoring the implementation of the project by providing frequent quarterly feedback on changes in levels of access to water in terms of availability, affordability and quality.
