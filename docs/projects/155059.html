Needs

More than 900,000 Rohingya refugees live in Bangladesh after they fled violence and persecution in Myanmar. Now the second year into the crisis, the scale, severity and complexity of needs across the hazardous conditions in the camps remain overwhelming and services continue to be insufficient or of poor quality. The refugees are highly vulnerable with many being traumatized and facing continuous exposure to a multitude of protection risks. According to the FSL sector needs assessment, 100% of the Rohingya refugees remain dependent on food assistance. The influx has put an immense strain on the District Government and the Bangladeshi host communities who live in the area. Host community members report declined income due to the results of the recent influx as a cause of lost work opportunities to a cheaper refugee labour market. The dire circumstances of the Rohingya communities are reinforced by a lack of access to income and livelihoods options including lack of opportunity for own food production. Women and girls lack basic life skills and being restricted to their shelters, they do not engage in livelihoods activities. Particularly female-headed and child-headed households are exceptionally resource-poor, which impacts food security and dietary diversity, nutrition and health for women and children. People need cash to buy food and to cover other basic needs such as health, clothes, fuel, debt repayment and shelter. Hence, there is an urgent need to work with livelihoods and resilience to minimize the use of negative and damaging coping strategies. The local host community was already among Bangladesh’s poorest population before the influx with high malnutrition and food insecurity rates at 38%. The increase in population has heavily impacted food security, livelihoods access, the environment and the labour market for the host community. Labour wages have been driven down while food prices have increased. Food production in the districts is scare, leading to increased household expenditures on food and overall economic vulnerability for host community.

Activities or outputs

Based on the identified needs, this project targets both refugee and host community members as well as women and men and aims at providing tailored livelihoods support. DCA’s cash and livelihoods project focus on integrating with protection and GBV and therefore targets a majority of female households, while idle young men will also be targeted. The project supports self-reliance and socio-economic activities by enhancing livelihoods opportunities and home production of more nutritious food items. *Assessment and analysis of markets to enable local procurement and distribution of cash vouchers to beneficiaries to access inputs locally. By using the local markets as much as possible, the project expects to have economic recovery/support effects on these and local vendors from the areas near the camps. *Distribution of cash vouchers or direct cash for inputs for multi-storey, home gardening (vegetable seeds, saplings, tools, equipment). The type of vegetable seeds will be determined together with the targeted group and aligned to the natural environment. Targeted rights holders will be trained in natural methods for vegetable production (agro-ecology) by DCA staff and the Department of Agriculture. * Support individuals and groups to establish income generating activities (IGAs). The project targets women who are referred from GBV and Education activities implemented by DCA. The focus for IGAs will be home-based activities with marketing opportunities in the camps and local markets. DCA continuously assess protection issues for women in accessing markets and will support them to sell their products locally. DCA delivers skills training to IGA beneficiaries under Education Sector activities. * Distribution of Cash for Work (CfW) grants. DCA implements site improvement activities (tree planting, slope stabilisation, improvement of roads, construction of bridges) in two camps through the engagement of unskilled and skilled labourers. CfW rates follow set standards of the cash working group based on the minimum expenditure basket. DCA actively promotes the inclusion of women in cash for work activities and identifies suitable tasks that can be carried out by female workers. *Market monitoring and post-distribution monitoring. DCA will closely monitor prices, quality and availability of the inputs assessed to be needed for the intervention as well as basic food commodities that are regularly accessed by refugees and host community members. Protection and access will be regularly monitored. PDM is conducted for all components to ensure that needs are met in the most effective, timely and sustainable manner.
