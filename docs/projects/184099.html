Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) some 14.6 million people – 31 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2022. This is a 1.2 million increase compared to 2021. Overall, the number of people in need in Sudan in 2022 is the highest in the past decade. The transitional government is prioritizing peace and ending the economic crisis, issues closely intertwined with the drivers of humanitarian need in the country.
While incidents of fighting have reduced considerably in recent years, the situation of people displaced due to decades of conflict remains unresolved. Some 2.9 million IDPs, 1.2 million refugees, 0.9 million returnees and 9.6 vulnerable residents continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection support, both in camps and host communities. Pockets of armed conflict continue in Darfur, and sporadic inter-communal conflicts also continue.
Across Sudan, basic services are lacking, and natural disasters, like floods, affect people each year. But it is a deepening economic crisis, following years of stagnation and little investment in already-weak public services, that is driving worsening food insecurity, deteriorating healthcare, and other needs across Sudan.
In 2021, Sudan faced a worsening economic crisis that will continue to drive humanitarian needs into 2022, even as the transitional government works toward economic reforms. While the transitional government is committed to stemming the economic crisis, reforms have already involved subsidy cuts which have a further impact on families who are already struggling to cope. Basic service provision, already weak and underdeveloped, has been dangerously degraded by the economic crisis. This has affected people directly, impacting households’ ability to buy food and cover basic needs. It has also deepened already existing needs – including among existing vulnerable groups like displaced people, returnees, and refugees – and may cause new people to fall into humanitarian need

1 - Needs

With the economic crisis aggravating and economic hardship affecting new segments of the Sudanese society that were not classified as vulnerable previously, thousands of families are struggling to meet their basic needs, including food and medicine, as well as access to basic services. Moreover, the severity of needs of those who had been vulnerable prior to the economic crisis and the levels of deprivation are increasing further. This is manifesting itself with the record 14.6 million people requiring humanitarian assistance in 2022. About 12.8 million of them will need emergency assistance for life-threatening needs, a 75 per cent increase compared to 2021. Humanitarian needs persist for vulnerable people who have been living in protracted displacement for over a decade; people affected by recent localized clashes in Darfur’s Jebel Marra area; people affected by food insecurity, malnutrition and protection risks; as well as people displaced due to inter-communal violence and conflict. An estimated 14.6 million people in Sudan need some form of humanitarian assistance. Further the situation is exacerbated by the reduced household purchasing power caused by increasing prices of key commodities and medicines. At the same time, the country continues to be a major host of over one million refugees and asylum seekers, the majority of whom are largely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Food insecurity, Malnutrition, Displacement and refugees, and Protection concerns are the key drivers of needs in Sudan. Increasing food prices, and poor WASH systems and health services, continue to drive malnutrition across the country. Children under five with severe acute malnutrition (SAM)and stunted is estimated to be 12 times more likely to die than their well-nourished peers, for those with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) the rate is 4 times higher in countries with relatively similar nutrition situation like Sudan. Within those severely malnourished, one in five children will die if not treated, the risk increases to 9 out of 10 for SAM with complications. According to the latest IPC report on the status of food security in Sudan, more than half of the acutely food insecure people in the country (about 53 per cent) are in central, eastern and northern Sudan. These are non-conflict areas, which seem to be bearing the brunt of the economic crisis and lack of long-term development investment, especially in the case of eastern Sudan. Tackling food insecurity in these areas necessitates response beyond the traditional emergency and relief pattern observed in Darfur and the Two Areas and closer coordination and synergies between humanitarian and development actors. Sudan remains prone to disease outbreaks with decline in service provision by the public sector is forcing the population to seek health services at the private sector that is dominating the scene with 69.3 per cent of current health expenditure (National health recovery and reform policy and strategic plan NHRRP 2020-2022). According to the 2021 multi-sectorial needs assessment MSNA 72.3 per cent of the population had to pay in full for their health services despite a reported coverage by the national health insurance fund of 67 per cent. During 2021 the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43 per cent compared to 57 per cent during 2020. In 2021, some 900,000 people living in 18 states were affected by flooding, the capacity of existing health systems has been stretched as water has been contaminated, exposing people to water and vector borne diseases. Durable solutions have not been achieved and the root causes of their displacement remain unaddressed. In addition to living in dire conditions for years with limited basic services, many IDPs - particularly women and girls - still face high protection risks and continue to be harassed, and in some cases killed or raped. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Provide support to the HC and HCT to enable effective leadership in strategic coordination, including the integration of key cross cutting issues such as gender, environment, and accountability to affected people in humanitarian actions 2. Lead the inter sector coordination mechanisms at national and sub national levels in accordance with IASC guidance and support sectors in the preparation of sector response plans and their strategic monitoring indicators including those that directly fall under CCS sector 3. Establish and maintain dialogue with affected people, including in IDP settlements in Darfur, to ensure provision of basic services to vulnerable people. This includes evidence-based liaison with sector coordinators at state and Khartoum level to address any gaps reported. 4. Support the HCT in developing the Humanitarian Response Plan informed by evidence-based analysis of assessed needs with clear measurable objectives 5. Ensure that inter agency risk analysis is undertaken and minimum preparedness actions and advanced prepared actions are routinely carried out in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness Framework 6. Develop and publish on a regular basis communication and information products using a standardized and integrated approach. This includes the coordination of humanitarian public information and advocacy efforts 7. Analyze and provide access reports to guide and inform decision making by the HCT and advocacy and negotiate for access 8. Promote the adherence to humanitarian principles by humanitarian actors to ensure principled interventions and in line with the HCT Minimum Operating Standards
