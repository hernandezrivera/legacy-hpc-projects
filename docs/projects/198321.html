Project Summary

The project seeks to sustain, strengthen and initiate rapid responses in emerging emergencies like flood disasters and secondary displacements due to government IDP camp closures and relocation, to ensure access to quality integrated GBV prevention and response services in the protracted insurgency-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY), Nigeria. This will include the provision of lifesaving multi-sectorial services that will address the medical, psychosocial, legal, livelihood and protection/safety needs of GBV survivors in and out-of-camp IDP, returnees and host communities as well as communities of return to strengthen community protection mechanisms to mitigate and prevent GBV. The project will focus on expanding and maintaining services in safe spaces/shelters and one-stop-centre (OSC)/SARC centres, improving referrals to medical services including clinical management of rape (CMR), strengthening case management, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) as well as legal and protection services, procurement and distribution of
menstrual, dignity kits, cultural appropriate clothing, and strengthening field coordination, referral pathways and ethical data management. Other complementary activities will focus on strengthening community resilience, tackling harmful practices like child/early/forced marriage and restorative justice through increased engagement of men and boys and addressing GBV in and around schools (SRGBV) as well as promoting GBV/SEA risk mitigation mechanism/AAP in a humanitarian setting. Our target is to inclusively reach 30% (395,394 - including 3,194 PLWDs) of the sub-sector target (1,317,980 individuals -32% IDPs, 25% returnees, 43% host communities) who require GBV intervention across the BAY state in 2023, spread across 11 LGAs identified as having the highest severity of needs. A similar number of Host community LGAs will also be targeted with GBV response services as well as 5 LGAs that are hosting returnees. Borno state has the bulk (48%) of those in need followed by Adamawa at 34% and 18% in Yobe. Special emphasis will be put on strengthening joint inter-agency coordination, M&E, capacity building, assessment, monitoring and partnership that will translate to better outcomes.

1 - Needs

There continues to be a glaring gap in the provision of GBV response services in a number of LGAs across the BAY states, especially in the hard-to-reach areas. In Yobe state, for example, there are access challenges in the provision of health and legal services for GBV survivors with only one functional Sexual Assault and Referral Centre (SARC centre) located in Damaturu. There is also a lack of safe spaces for women and girls across a number of LGAs. MHPSS and livelihood opportunities have also been highlighted as being inadequate in a number of hard-to-reach areas. The absence of civil authorities, especially court sessions, in several LGAs outside Maiduguri, hinders access to justice and other legal services for affected people including survivors of GBV. In addition, the increased flooding and its impact on settlements especially IDP camps as well as the secondary displacement created by the government's camp closure and resettlement activities further heighten the essential needs of the vulnerable populations. An estimated 1,829,002 million individuals (32% IDPs, 25% returnees, 43% host communities) are in need of GBV services across the BAY states with Borno state having the bulk (48) of those in need followed by Adamawa at 34% and 18% in Yobe. The hosting areas (11 LGAs) are identified as having the highest severity of needs. GBV incidences especially intimate partner violence (IPV) including sexual violence continue to be reported across the BAY states with women and girls disproportionately affected. According to 2022 GBVIMS (Jan-June), 99% of the reported incidents affected mostly women and girls. IPV was reported by 65% of survivors who sought help at various points of service. In terms of spread across the states, Borno had the highest percentage of reported incidents (83%), followed by Adamawa (13%) and Yobe state (4%).The GBV Sub-sector will seek to address the highlighted gaps/needs by scaling up GBV response services including MHPSS in these locations.
