Project Summary

Project Name: SCEC(Supporting communities to ensure education)
The following project is done by Delta association in order to sustain and ensure education to the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant school aged children and youth who are in and out of public schools. Delta aims to help around 1200 school aged boys and girls. Delta will persuade dropout students to get back to school, and will help enrolled students to retain themselves in school.
At the beginning of this project, a needs assessment will be done to identify the students who left their schools in districts of Aaley, and Chouf. Students who left their schools will be contacted, and reasons for their dropouts will be discussed with their caregivers. After knowing the reasons of all the caregivers, households will be divided into clusters, each having its own reason that caused the dropout.
One of the project's objective's is to return 960 students back to school. The outreach team will ask for a list of all registered students in school year 2020-2021, to compare it with registered students of 2021-2022, in order to convince these students to return back to school.
SCEC will provide assistive technologies for 160 students with disabilities. Delta has a partnership with NRDC (National Rehabilitation and Developmental Center) which will provide us with all the needed information regarding people with disabilities.
Households of students that dropped out of education, and those who are still in education will be given a certain amount of cash to persuade students return back to school. The project aims to provide cash for assistance for around 405 households following certain vulnerability criteria. Children with disabilities, distance from student to school, number of persons in households, renting cost of guardian, health problems in households, number of students registered in school per household, and sources of income, are criteria to be followed in order to choose the most vulnerable household.
SCEC aims to provide 135 caregivers with basic and digital learning support, which helps them in teaching their children, and helps them find jobs. Two volunteer teachers will be hired to provide learning remotely.
Delta's PSS and protection team will perform multiple awareness sessions to 770 learners, through certain qualified protection staff discussing different topics such as harassment, risky behaviors, violence, bullying, SEA (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), and SGBV risk.
Delta will perform its procurement global policy in order to insure low cost solutions (low tech and high tech) and food packaging. Low tech solutions will be provided in forms of flash-drives, and routers. High tech solutions will be provided in forms of tablets, and cell phones. Every household will be provided with low cost solutions according to their needs. SCEC is aiming to provide low cost solutions to 540 households and food packages to 337 households.
The project will also support vulnerable students through a community based learning program which addresses their learning losses. Teaching and learning materials will be provided to 40 teachers and 425 learners. These learners will be divided into groups of ten, where volunteer Syrian and Lebanese teachers will be hired to implement this activity in two phases. 1. Empower students education in summer. 2. Homework support after schools start.
SCEC will also provide inclusive and adapted catch-up learning support programs for 50 vulnerable students. Ten qualified Lebanese teachers will tutor the students in groups of two. These students will be selected from public schools, according to the educational needs. This will happen in coordination with school directors and teachers.
SCEC will provide cash assistance for 18 schools who have faced huge influxes of transferees from private to public schools. The schools will be selected according to certain criteria, mainly those who contain a great number of Lebanese and Syrian students.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Our protection department will make a plan to ensure safety and protection to all children who are in and out of schools. This plan will consider the role of caregivers and school directors and teachers as well, who will be given awareness and training sessions to ensure no SEA incidents will occur. In addition, the protection team will be available on a daily basis in all remote learning groups online, and will treat any referrals confidentially.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

There are many key elements of accountability, which includes participation and inclusion, where boys and girls of diverse backgrounds engage meaningfully, and avoiding any kind of discrimination against new students who came from private schools or any refugee student. Another key element is communication and transparency, where communication is facilitated between partners and people of concern. Sharing information and media, as well as financial reports that will be audited externally.
