Project Summary

The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Newly Merged Districts (KPNMDs) have remained underdeveloped due to governance issues, lack of ownership by the federal government, close association of the natives with Afghanistan followed by militancy and insurgency which aftermath the internal displacement of the local population. Since 2008, over 5 million individuals were displaced from NMD of KP due to a volatile security situation and ongoing military operations to clean up the districts from militants.
The Government of Pakistan (GoP), with the support of humanitarian partners has facilitated 438,657 (94%) of the displaced families over the period and ensured their return to KPMDs, still 16,064 off camp families and approximately 2,000 in-camp families from KPMDs are still to return. In addition, there are approx. 6,000-8,000 families who are yet to repatriate from Khost, Afghanistan. These exiting resources and infrastructure cannot cater the needs of the returnees as the barren agriculture which full of debris; demolished water channels which were being used for drinking and irrigation purposes are fully or partially damaged; which required external support to be able to bring into utilization.
SIF is present in Orakzai newly merged district since November 2017 and has also implemented emergency food assistance interventions in Mohmand district in 2020. hasSIF implemented various Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) projects to support the families returning specifically to Upper Orakzai which was severely affected by conflicts during FATA insurgency. Under these projects, SIF has conducted a study on local dynamic and prevailing needs in selected villages of Orakzai and project activities were designed as per community needs identified.
Regarding the food security and livelihoods situation, the findings of the most recent assessments conducted by SIF are described below:
Food insecurity: It has been observed that retuned families are adopting different coping strategies for survival. 88% of the assessed households are relying on less preferred and less expensive food, 73% purchase food on credit, 68% borrow food or rely on help from friends or relatives and 62% send household members to eat elsewhere. A recent detailed study on food security and livelihoods by WFP in 2019 in all newly merged districts also identified Mohmand, Orakzai, Kurrum and North Waziristan as the most vulnerable districts.
Livelihoods: 59% people are doing labor work or getting non-monetary in-kind support to meet their family ends. The remaining 41% are not doing any work. Before displacement, the majority of the households were dependent on agriculture and livestock whereas now most of the households are dependent on daily wage labor work. The majority of the houses own agriculture land but none of them are using it for agriculture production. This is fundamentally due to lack of financial and technical resources and inputs. The significant reduction of the livestock population in the region occurred as a significant number of livestock was sold at extremely low price to vendors during displacement, and some of them have lost their livestock due to death, injury, lost, etc.
This proposed project will be implemented tosupport the returned families in Orakzai and Mohmand newly merged districts. Based on the proposed interventions, immediate relief support will be delivered to the returning families in a way that will ultimately support FATA transition framework that aims at introducing and implementing durable solutions for ensuring sustainable return in affected districts.

1- Project Activities

The proposed project aims to address the FSL needs of the families returning to different parts of Orakzai and Mohmand newly merged districts but having not yet received any assistance by any humanitarian organizations. 1.1. Provision of un-conditional cash grants to returned families:Unconditional cash grants will be given to 3,000 return families as monthly food assistance for 3 months “4,000 PKR per month in total 12,000 PKR. Thus, the returned families will be able to access sufficient food in terms of quantity and quality there from the nearest market. These cash grants will also strengthen the local market economy in Orakzai and Mohmand through the circulation of cash among local communities (returned families). The devised unconditional cash grant is based and calculated after consultations with district administration and authorities. SIF has also distributed emergency food baskets of the same amount in the area in its previous interventions.1.2. Provision of un-conditional cash grant to women headed households and households with orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. At least 100 extremely vulnerable women headed households and households with orphans, elders and persons with disabilities will be prioritized for the provision of cash grants. Cash will be transferred in three equal tranches of PKR 3000 per month and PKR 9,000 in total.2.1. Provision of agriculture support:The majority of the returned families have their agriculture land that they have been cultivating before displacement but not currently due to lack of inputs (equipment, seeds, knowledge) and due to financial constraints. Agriculture support will be provided to 200 returned families who own cultivable agriculture land. The farmer families will be assisted with agricultural inputs as per identified needs including but not limited to seeds, tools, land preparation, etc. The agriculture package will be of PKR 18,000 per targeted family. That activity will remove the major cropping barriers which the returned families are currently facing in their villages. After the land rehabilitation and leveling, the agriculture package along with tools will be given to the targeted farmers to sustain their livelihood through crops cultivation into their agriculture land. 2.2. Provision of kitchen garden support:A kitchen garden package will be provided to 300 vulnerable women. This kitchen gardening support will include both training and tools kits. The activity will also include rehabilitation and preparation of the land for kitchen gardening. This activity will provide an opportunity to women to work inside their houses and contribute to the food security and economy of the family. The fresh vegetables produced will be consumed at household level which will contribute to the improved nutrition as well as savings of the family2.3. Provision of poultry birds:Poultry birds of local breed will be distributed among returned families, with a focus on women headed households. Each package will include 10 chicken birds, feed and vaccination. 300 vulnerable families will be targeted under this activity. The beneficiaries will be trained on basic poultry management skills, identification of diseases and prompt vaccination/medication to treat bird diseases. Similarly, cash grants will be given to beneficiaries for poultry cages reconstruction/construction.3.1. Provision of economic support:Livelihoods grants of PKR 50,000 each will be given to 50 youth for developing small enterprises in their respective villages. Trainings on enterprise development will be given to these youths for improving business development skills. 3.2. Carry out a tree plantation campaign:Afforestation campaigns will be carried out with the support of farmers, women and the agriculture department to discourage deforestation and promote forestation. Local fruits/trees plantation will be promoted to plant 4,000 fruit/local trees by engaging 100 beneficiaries in the targeted area.
