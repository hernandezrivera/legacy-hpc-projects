Project Summary

In Borena Zone, the last two consecutive rainy seasons have failed resulting failure in crops, pasture and critical water shortage both for human and livestock consumption and displacement as well as disrupted socio-economic status of the pastoralist community. Due to this, food insecurity occurred where price of staples food increased while decrease in livestock price. According to the recent Zonal iCCG meeting held on 4th January at Yabello, the number of drought-affected people alarmingly increasing. Accordingly, more than 602,289 individuals are identified to be affected by the severe drought in the zone .
In Dhas and Wachile Woredas alone, about 78,403 individuals from 26 kebeles are severely affected by the drought. More than half a million-pastoralist people are significantly affected by the drought with their livestock1. More than 106,992 livestock deaths reported so far where they belong to 38,438 drought affected HHs1. Pasture for livestock and water for both human and livestock is at critical stage now. The drought impacted the local livestock production and productivity as well as crop production and local markets for both livestock and grains. This resulted the decrease in livestock market. It also hindered the life and livelihood of the community. There are thousands of drought induced IDPs in Borena who are in critical condition. In three affected woredas alone (Guchi, Dhas and Wachile, there are more than 76,496 drought induced IDPs who are suffering from basic services including from shortage of basic water supply services and sanitation facilities. Malnutrition rates are increasing alarmingly where all the woredas in the zone are a priority-1 hotspot woredas. SAM and MAM cases among both under 5 children and >5 as well as pregnant and lactating mothers are increasing, especially since July 2021. For example, MAM cases among pregnant and lactating mothers in July was only 6,467. However, it increased to 7,849 and 9,078 in August and September respectively. In December, it’s reported that, there are 426 SAM cases among pregnant and lactating mothers. In Dhas and Wachile alone, there are eleven water schemes are non-functional mainly due to over usage by drought affected community and increase in water table depth Majority of the affected people currently using unprotected pond and ella water where they are exposed to higher risk of communicable diseases. Coupling with existing water stress, this resulted more than 78,000 individuals to be at higher risk of critical water shortage. Moreover, existing 52 schools in in this two woredas are in dire need for water supply and needs water rationing.
To mitigate the impact and consequence of the severe drought, ANE proposes to respond with emergency water, sanitation, and hygiene intervention at Dhas and Wachile Woredas. The proposed WASH activities will result in reduction of WASH related communicable diseases and ease associated disease morbidity and mortality through increased access to WASH services to 42,900 drought affected communities. The project will also mitigate protection risks of the most vulnerable affected women and girls through increasing access to water schemes and sanitation facilities. The proposed targets and identified needs are a priority by the zonal authority and OCHA as well as WASH Cluster. ANE is strategically well-placed national NGO in the zone where we have a project office at Mega town which is fully equipped and well-staffed. ANE has been working in the zone since its establishment in 2012 to alleviate the suffering of the community.

1. - Projects activities

Detail Project Activities1. Water schemes sites feasibility study. ANE will conduct quick feasibility study on the sites of non-functioning water schemes to figure out the base line data and to identify the current damage status after the need assessment period. 2. Rehabilitation of non-functional water schemes. ANE will fully rehabilitate 5 shallow wells and 1 Borhole. the rehabilitation work includes well site clearing, head work maintenance and drainage work, removing non-functional hand-pump, supply and installation of new hand pump and fencing.3. Water trucking4. Procure HTH chlorine for ensuring water quality purpose. ANE will procure 3 drums of HTH chlorine and support woreda water offices.5. Water management Committee establishment and training. The project will facilitate 6 water management committee establishment and training for each water supply schemes to be rehabilitated. The training will be based the government guideline and facilitated by the government officials. 6. Provision of maintenance tool box for the water management committee. The project will purchase and distribute maintenance tool boxes for the trained water management committee to enable them to do simple maintenance.6. Distribution of WASH NFIs and HHWT chemicals. The distribution will be based on the government guideline for COVID-19 prevention. So that ANE will ensure all beneficiaries coming to the distribution site will have face mask and access to hand washing device and or hand rub sanitizers. The physical distancing will be also ensured during the distribution process.7. Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring. ANE will conduct a PDM survey (with sex/age disaggregated data) after conducting the WASH NFI and HHWT chemical distribution. The survey will assess how the distribution generally goes and feedbacks from beneficiaries on the kit content, quality and quantity as well as the use, timeliness, effectiveness and impact of the project. The findings will be used to improve future interventions.8. Construction of gender sensitive semi-permanent communal and institutional latrine blocks. ANE will construct sex segregated 22 blocks of semi-permanent communal latrine at IDP collection sites and 6 blocks of semi-permanent institutional latrine at schools in target towns. The latrine blocks will have one room with ramp dedicated for disable people and has inside lockable door to ensure privacy for all users.11.
