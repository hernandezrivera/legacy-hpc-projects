Project Summary

Acted will implement this project under the SCCCM sector titled ""Providing Disaster Preparedness and Response to Rohingya Refugees in Kutupalong-Balukhali and Teknaf Sub-district, Cox’s Bazar."" The project aims to strengthen response mechanisms in the camps to address recurring fire hazards effectively.
The continuation of fire safety awareness and firefighting training initiatives in 2024 has shown significant positive impacts. SMSV/DMU volunteers refreshed and enhanced their firefighting skills through practical exercises like live shelter burning demonstrations, improving their knowledge and preparedness for the fire season. Household fire awareness sessions further empowered women, who manage cooking—a common source of fire incidents in the camps. These sessions provided critical knowledge on fire hazards, prevention, and response strategies, helping women better mitigate risks in their households. These integrated efforts have built a more resilient and fire-prepared community.
In 2025, the project will focus on training, awareness-raising, and maintenance support to enhance fire safety and response capacities among SMSV/DMU Volunteers, camp residents, and teachers. Over 18,455 refugees and host community members will directly benefit, equipping them to prevent and respond to fire and water-related risks. Acted will adopt a ‘One Camp Approach’ to ensure all firefighting units function as a cohesive unit, enabling a unified and rapid response. This approach fosters collaboration and resource-sharing among volunteers to ensure effective prevention and control.
Project Activities 2025
Activity 1: Training Initiatives
a) Firefighting Refresher Training: For 3,300 SMSV/DMU Volunteers (100 per camp across 33 camps), including live fire demonstrations.
b) Household Fire Prevention Training: For 20,130 individuals (610 per camp across 33 camps), focusing on safe cooking practices, fuel usage, and female-friendly cooking spaces. The remaining 23 camps will be covered in 2025.
c) Water & Flood Safety Training: For 1,700 DMU volunteers in 17 IOM AoR camps.
d) MFFU Repair & Maintenance Training: For 1,320 volunteers (40 per camp across 33 camps) to enhance equipment familiarity and ensure 24/7 functionality.
e) GIS Mapping Training: To familiarize volunteers with real-time mapping tools for fire response. This pilot activity is pending for donor approval.
Activity 2: Repair and Maintenance of MFFUs
Acted will provide repair and maintenance services for MFFUs (59 UN-funded three-wheeler, 585 portable water pumps). Maintenance will be conducted through Acted’s workshop near Camps 9 and 7, equipped with spare parts, tools, and engineering machines. Volunteers will receive training to identify issues and conduct regular checks, ensuring functionality.
Coordination and Implementation
Acted will actively engage SMS partners across 33 camps. As an SCCCM member, Acted will collaborate with the RRRC office (CIC, ACIC), AOR focal points (UNHCR, IOM), SMS agencies, and education and health sector actors. Coordination will occur through SCCCM sector and EPR WG meetings in Cox’s Bazar and at the camp level.
A dedicated hotline will support reporting and resolution of MFFU equipment issues, ensuring operational readiness for emergency responses.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Needs Assessments conducted by Acted and other agencies in the camp show frequent fires have emerged as a major hazard in the congested camps in Cox's Bazar, causing loss of lives and properties due to gaps in coordination, awareness, training, fire safety knowledge and functional equipment to tackle fire accidents. Needs Assessments that are relevant to the interventions are:• Union Civil Protection Mechanism, Final report on Fire Safety Strategy, Rohingya Refugee Camps, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, 2022.• Technical Advisor of MOAS Dan Graham, Fire Investigation: Camp 4, Block F4, November 2022. • 2023 Joint Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment. ISNA 2024.• Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2023, 2024. • Fire incident report of camp 5, 8W, 9 & 11 where health facilities were affected due to fire. • Acted, ECHO Needs Assessment Disaster Preparedness (Fire response), 2023.Needs Assessment conducted by Acted in February 2023 demonstrates that the majority of the respondents (59.3%) reported that fires occur ""sometimes"" with 67.2% reporting having personally experienced a fire. These findings show a high level of sporadic fire accidents occur in the camps. More than one-third (72.78%) reportedly participated in fire awareness sessions conducted by various partners in the camps; however, One-third (66.8%) interviewees reported women of their family didn’t receive any awareness sessions under any partner. Similarly, about two-thirds of camp children (64.7%) never received any awareness training. Acted assessments also show while a significant number of fire accidents occur due to unsafe cooking practices as only 28.3% of the camp residents reportedly received fire safety training so far. About 40.7% lacked any training regarding cooking.According to ISCG/NPM Daily Incident Reports, fire and flood were among the most frequently reported incidents in 2023 and 2024. In 2023, a total of 529 fire incidents were reported, damaging 546 shelters and affecting 2,547 households, with no deaths recorded. Additionally, there were 24 reported drowning cases, affecting 4 households and resulting in 12 fatalities. In 2024, 483 fire incidents were reported, damaging 1,521 shelters and affecting 1,556 households, with 3 deaths recorded. There were also 24 reported drowning cases in 2024, affecting 16 households and resulting in 14 fatalities. In the camps shelter materials are easily flammable: plastic sheets and bamboo. Thus, the shelters are more prone to accidental fire incidents.Camp residents expressed the need of more knowledge regarding the matter. Most of the interviewees also expressed the need for more fire safety equipment’s, fire awareness sessions, to effectively tackle fire hazards. However, the community lacks the understanding of SMSV/DMU responsibility and mechanisms in terms of fire preparedness and response. The Joint Response Plan 2023, 2024 also identifies fire as one major issue to tackle regarding the frequency of fire accidents in the camps. In the ISNA 2024 report it is highlighted that the coordinated DRM training is one of the key focus for next year. The new arrivals will also be included as they also need to be familiar with the fire hazard and response mechanism within the camp .

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Acted places great importance on mainstreaming protection in all of its programming. For the proposed disaster preparedness and response project, protection principles and approaches will be integrated throughout the project cycle and across key activities.Community participation and feedback mechanisms will be prioritized to ensure the unique needs and rights of vulnerable groups are addressed. During the initial needs assessment, interviews with refugees members were conducted to understand fire-related protection risks. This analysis helped identify those requiring targeted support, such as female and persons with disabilities who may face barriers to accessing information or assistance. To promote safety and reduce vulnerabilities, fire safety trainings will be conducted separately for SMS volunteers, different age groups and gender to account for cultural sensitivities. Individuals like women will be trained on operating firefighting equipment to build self-reliance. Acted’s CFM along with the SMS CFM desks and UNHCR and UNWFP hotline numbers will allow anonymous and non-anonymous feedback to continually improve the response.Community volunteering opportunities under the project empower refugees by leveraging their existing social structures for outreach and awareness activities. Campaign materials will be developed with refugee input and in local languages to ensure equitable access to information about rights and available services.Through this integrated approach, Acted aims to mainstream protection considerations into all aspects of project cycle from design to implementation to monitoring through active community participation and collaborative partnerships. Ensuring a coherent approach to addressing safety, dignity and rights of fire-affected populations is a key priority.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will mainstream age and disability inclusion through a thorough needs assessment process and inclusive project design. The needs assessment will identify the specific protection risks, vulnerabilities and needs of different groups within the target population through consultations with community members representing a diversity of ages, genders and abilities. This will provide important insights into how to best design activities and target services.Relevant beneficiary data captured through monitoring tools will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability, with the information fed into an interactive dashboard. This will help project staff evaluate whether any gaps exist in achieving an equitable response and adjust their approach accordingly.Activities like fire safety awareness sessions and training will be carefully planned and implemented using participatory methods respectful of cultural dynamics, to promote inclusion of all ages, genders and abilities. Accessibility measures will further support participation by persons with disabilities.Selection prioritization for any assistance provided will factor in vulnerability levels determined through a scoring system that considers age, gender and disability.Feedback from the refugee community itself will be continually sought at each phase of the project cycle to ensure their diverse needs, views and strategies for coping with risks are fully reflected.Moreover, learning center teachers trained in fire safety and water safety by Acted will impart this knowledge to their students as part of regular class time. This expands outreach, especially among children as a vulnerable group.Through applying these inclusion mainstreaming efforts systematically and comprehensively, the project aims to equitably deliver fire risk reduction activities and services benefiting all segments of the refugee population.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project ensures compliance with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments by promoting meaningful access, participation, and involvement of beneficiaries throughout the project cycle.The design is based on a needs assessment conducted with camp residents and Safety Unit Volunteers to capture their priorities and views. Men and women were consulted through focus group discussions and confidential interviews using Kobo tools for data collection.Beneficiaries will participate in implementation through community awareness sessions, fire safety training, and drills. Engaging local volunteers, including Safety Unit Volunteers who are Rohingya refugees, fosters cooperation with camp leadership and strengthens community ownership of fire response activities.Feedback and complaint mechanisms will operate at central and camp levels. ACTED’s MEAL system adheres to AAP principles, using baseline, midline, and endline surveys to assess effectiveness from beneficiaries’ perspectives. Disaggregated data will track outcomes, while lessons learned will inform future improvements.Enhanced focus on specific needs groups includes:• Persons with Disabilities (PWDs): Accessible venues, support persons, tailored sessions, and outreach via SMS.• Older Persons: Priority seating, adjusted session durations, large-print materials, and family support.Data collection includes pre/post-training assessments and real-time monitoring by MEAL teams. Weekly data analysis and monthly reviews ensure adaptive management. This inclusive approach ensures accountable, equitable, and effective service delivery for all beneficiaries.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Acted has established robust policies addressing the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment (PSEAH), as well as Child Protection and gender policies. All Acted staff and partners are required to endorse these policies, and their underlying principles will be integrated into all project activities. Specific training on PSEAH and complaints feedback and response mechanisms are conducted by designated PSEAH and AAP focal points at Acted.Communities will be provided with awareness about the complaint feedback and response mechanisms, enabling them to voice concerns about any misconduct or harm. Focal points are designated to address complaints promptly and activate response protocols. Special efforts will be made to encourage reporting, particularly from women and children.In a recent UNHCR assessment, Acted scored 8 out of 8. The organization is actively participating in the PSEAH network of ISCG. Acted employs both online and in-person training methods to ensure its staff is well-versed in PSEAH, with mandatory in-person training for all staff annually, refreshing themselves with online courses.Acted's referral process is comprehensive, featuring a hotline at the field level accessible to anyone. The organization maintains a dedicated email and transparency line to manage cases related to PSEAH. In responding to cases, Acted adopts a survivor-centric approach to ensure the best possible outcome for the survivor.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project includes embedded Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. A team of ten fire and rescue instructors is on standby to provide support in-case of large scale of floods or fires. Additionally, the project maintains a sufficient contingency of spare parts for the firefighting three-wheeler units, ensuring the 24/7 functionality of Mobile Fire Fighting Units (MFFUs) across 33 camps.

Yes, the project activities include youth in all training sessions. Some of them serve as SMS volunteers, household community members, and LC teachers. These youth participants will receive training in various Disaster Risk Management (DRM)trainings.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this Disaster Risk Management (DRM) focused intervention aims to enhance the capacity of camp residents and first responders (volunteers). By building these skills, the project strengthens the resilience of refugee communities, enabling them to be better prepared for and respond to disaster events within the camp setting.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, similar to the camp setting, members of the host community living in or near the camps will receive training in Disaster Risk Management (DRM). This training will enhance their preparedness and response to natural and man-made disasters, such as floods, cyclones, landslides, and fires, which frequently affect these areas. By raising awareness of hazards and preventive measures, the project supports Cox’s Bazar’s district-level development priorities, helping to reduce damage and improve community resilience.
