Project Summary

The humanitarian consequences for people suffering the crisis in Syria are wide-ranging and profound. Overall, 14.2 million people need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population, based on a countrywide assessment, this includes at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure, significant increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020.
The proposed project will contribute to achieve the SO1:To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through lifesaving and life-sustaining food assistance via swiftly addressing food insecurity of 15,000 households (90,000 individuals), where 10,000 households will be supported via three rounds of food distribution, After informing details of food distribution 3-7 days prior to the event, 10,000 households will receive food basket for 3 months through small and multiple distribution points at each village to avoid a high number of beneficiaries at the same place, all of the contents of the food basket will be selected based on the FSL recommendations and beneficiaries feedbacks on previous similar interventions, in addition to distribute a fresh bread on daily basis to 5,000 HHs (30,000 individuals) whom living in the camps in North of Syria for continues 6 months, where each family will receive 1 K.G of bread for 6 days a week for 6 months, the bread will be from wheat flour baked in local bakeries selected after a public tender taking in consideration that the quality of the bread should be inline with the global standards and inline with Syrian flavor, the bread will be delivered to each camp on the morning, many distribution points in each camp will be to avoid the crowded lines.
Ensuring the beneficiaries feedbacks and recommendations to inform the project implementation, a leaflet on feedback mechanism will be circulated during distribution in addition to PSEA leaflets.
Through setting a selection criteria based on vulnerability analysis, the most vulnerable groups among the IDP will be supported and they can reduce their likeliness of engaging negative coping mechanism, and their life-risks related to food shortage to be mitigated which will be verified through post distribution monitoring activities.
the project is considered as a planned

4 - Needs

The humanitarian consequences for people suffering the crisis in Syria are wide-ranging and profound. Overall, 14.2 million people need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population, based on a countrywide assessment, this includes at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure, significant increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020, where protracted crisis in Syria has intensified needs over the past year through a complex and deepening socio-economic crisis, degradation of livelihoods, knock-on effects of COVID-19, the ongoing conflict in the North of the country and major losses in the food production sector (due to conflict and climate change). These have combined to severely compromise the national food security situation, prices of bread have also tripled for a bundle of bread and only 58 per cent of the population’s minimum bread needs were met in 2020 (HNO 2021).The similar trends in food insecurity were confirmed in HIHFAD specific needs assessment, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infrastructure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers income. Many people were obliged to discontinue agricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by HIHFAD staff and will provide 10,000 HHs from the IDPs and Vulnerable Returnees with food baskets for 3 months, HIHFAD will conduct the distribution through many small distribution points at each village. also HIHFAD team will distribute wheat bread to 5000 HHs from the IDPs whom living in the camps on daily basis for 6 days a week along of 6 months of distribution. the project implementation will be through 5 main activities: 1. HIHFAD field team will start to collect the updated new IDPs beneficiaries list. 2. HIHFAD logistic team will announce for 3 public tenders for procurement of Food baskets and the wheat flour, and the 3rd one will be to bake the bread inside Syria. 3. while the head office in Turkey is working on the procurement of the food baskets and wheat flour the HIHFAD field team will conduct home visits for verification of the Beneficiaries. 4. HIHFAD field team will conduct kick-off workshops with all of the local councils and local authorities and camps managements in all of the targeted villages to sign a MOUs. 5. HIHFAD distribution team will conduct the distribution of the food baskets for the selected beneficiaries for three rounds, and will conduct the daily bread distribution in the selected camps. 6. HIHFAD field MEAL team will conduct the PDM after each round of food baskets distribution and on monthly basis for the bread's one to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction regarding the quality of the distributed food baskets and the quality of the bread, the field team behaviors and the timing of the response.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to specific Sub-districts remains open. The Food baskets arrived to our warehouses on time. No drastic changes on the Bab-Alhawa border regulations. Risks: Risk 1: escalation the airstrikes in targeted areas. Mitigation 1: Conducting the distribution via many micro distribution points meaning don't have any high number of collected people in the same place in addition to cancel the day of distribution when mentioning any fighter jet hovering in the sky. Risk 2: Turkish Government decided to close the Bab- Alhawa border for urgent issue. Mitigation 2: 2.1. put this issue on the contract with the supplier and put a condition that after signing the contract if Bab-Alhawa closed for any issue, the supplier will provide HIHFAD with the same quantities of agreed items in Side Syria with the same specification from local resources. 2.2. Immediate contracting with multi suppliers inside Syria to provide the same food items with the same quantities. Risk 3: Delay from the supplier on delivering the Food Baskets to HIHFAD warehouse. Mitigation 3: Put a high penalty for the delay in delivering on the supplier in the signed contract, so the supplier will be more focus on his commitment with the delivery date.
