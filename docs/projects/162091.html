Project Summary

To mitigate the suffering in the camps due to the monsoon and respond to the overall problem HEKS/EPER propose response that will reduce the risk of disaster by reinforcing the camp infrastructure.
In the absence of sustainable livelihoods, Rohingya need to be supported with food assistance. Food security interventions have changed over time. Whereas in the early response only in-kind emergency food (rice, lentils, and vegetable oil) was distributed, the system under the lead of WFP is gradually shifting towards e-vouchers, which provide people a selection of 20 food items including 8 seasonal fresh items in outlets. Since September, already half of the population is covered by the e-voucher system , which is good progress. Food diversity scores are increasing, but still, only 56% of households reached an acceptable food diversity score mid of 2019.
Transforming the topography and the vegetation cover of the hills has completely changed the water discharge regimes. The monsoon season brings extreme precipitation within a short time. If water cannot discharge safely, it can enter shelters, erode plots, destroy infrastructure and trigger landslides. If water is not efficiently channeled to the rivers, households in the lower areas can be affected by flood events. The quality of drainage systems is insufficient, and existing systems are often blocked with waste due to missing functional solid waste management systems in the camps. During the 2019 monsoon, more than 3,200 households have been affected by landslide events and 5,700 households by flooding events all over the camps.
HEKS/EPER confirms the relevance of infrastructural needs for drainage, access, and slope protection as WFP has outlined in the DRR RFP. Addressing these needs is in line with the JRP 2019. Targeted food assistance by CfW is a highly valuable intervention that complements the in-kind and voucher distributions implemented in WFP’s general food assistance component. Improving settlement infrastructure increases safety and accessibility especially during the monsoon season and planting trees creates a better environment for the Rohingya and Rehabilitates an important asset of the host community.

Objectives

Holistically upgrade and improve the settlement areas and their immediate surroundings, through community-participatory and mid-term macro planning, linked to construction and maintenance works that enable safe, dignified and resilient living conditions ensuring access is for women, girls and children. Ensuring the centrality of community led decision making.

Needs

The Rohingya refugee crisis has resulted in a large influx of refugees to Cox’s Bazar District in Bangladesh increasing the refugee population to more than 910,000 in 2017. 91% of the refugees live in highly congested camps. Despite the Bangladesh government’s interest to repatriate Rohingya to Myanmar and agreements between the two countries, Rohingya are unwilling to return without guarantees that their security and rights will be protected. The international community is insisting that a return can only take place on a voluntary basis. With this constellation, it is very likely that the big majority of Rohingya will continue staying in the camps in Cox’s Bazar for several years, even though the two governments started a new attempt to repatriate Rohingyas in August 2019. The Bangladesh government formally manages the camps but UN Organizations in cooperation ensure basic needs within the government framework. More than two years after the last big wave of Rohingyas, the emergency is changing from priority short term needs to mid-and long-term needs of Rohingyas and stronger incorporating the needs of the host community.

Activities or outputs

5,000 meters drainage systems construction, 2-3 feet width: Currently, rainwater within the camp mostly drains on the footpaths between the shelters. The existing paths will be replaced by covered drainages with a width of 1 ft, making them serve both as drainage as well as a stabilized path. The water from the small drainages will be collected in larger drainages (2 or 3 ft) which remain open. Concrete slabs will cover the drainages on sections crossing footpaths. 5,000 meters pathways construction, 4 feet width: Long-lasting brick pathways will be constructed. Mostly a single brick layer will be used with exception of heavily used pathways, where a double layer of bricks can be installed. The ground will be well compacted before laying bricks and jute bag with sand-cement will be used to protect edging bricks to increase durability. 5,000 meters stairways construction, 4 feet width: Damaged jute-bag and rotten bamboo stairway will be replaced with longer-lasting brick masonry stairways. A brick riser of single brick thickness will be laid in alternating courses, to make it durable and stable under the foot. On heavily utilized stairways further improved designs might be used. To increase safety, a score renders to create a rough surface for grip as well as Bamboo railings for steep and long stairs will be installed. 1,500 meters slopes stabilization, 4 feet height: Steep slopes will be stabilized by geo-bags especially in locations where steep slopes create a risk for shelters or infrastructure. Plants, embankment weep holes, and reinforcement bar hammered through bags into the ground will ensure durability. Cash for Work: A total of 455 families (2,139 people) will be benefited directly through the CfW interventions and 12,208 people will be benefited indirectly from the project support.
