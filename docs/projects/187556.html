Project Summary

The project is aligned with the objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2021 “Enhance access to market-based livelihood opportunities and production”.
DRC will support vulnerable conflict-affected populations toward self-sufficiency. DRC will aim to achieve the below outcome: “To improve the adaptive capacities and socio-economic well-being of people, civic actors and communities” through:
-Increasing households’ income, assets, and consumption of needed goods and services.
DRC believes that households’ income, assets, and consumption of needed goods (including food) and services will increase if people participate in functioning markets which will be restored if people have appropriate skills for income generation and employment; and if people have safe and equal access to capital, markets, and employment; and if business norms enable people’s equitable economic participation.
- Creating more cohesive, inclusive, and equitable communities in Syria.
For people to achieve cohesive, inclusive, and equitable communities, marginalized groups must be able to exercise social and economic influence equal to those of other dominant groups and participate in decision-making.
A total of 11,000 will be targeted through DRC intervention in Daraa, Homs, Hama, Rural Damascus, and Aleppo. DRC will deliver Early Recovery assistance to persons of concern (PoCs) targeting conflict-affected youth and adults, men and women, including people with disabilities, to develop skills and increase income-generating opportunities with an aim to strengthen their access to livelihoods through microgrants for business development (start-up or scale-up), business development skills training, vocational training, distribution of toolkits in addition to the rehabilitation of community infrastructure restore through rehabilitation of Irrigation water canals and provide the beneficiaries with the needed assets or MGs or improve access to spaces in support of livelihoods. PoCs with protection-related concerns and vulnerabilities will be particularly targeted, and internal and external referrals will be made to ensure that protection services are provided in parallel based on identified needs.
The intervention will be coordinated within the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector meetings led by UNDP, and the Protection Sector led by UNHCR, where DRC is an active member to ensure coherence with other livelihoods and protection interventions. DRC will also coordinate closely with SARC, the Chamber of Industry of Aleppo, and The Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour (MoSAL) at both field and country levels. Initial needs assessments will be conducted jointly with MoE, MoSAL, and SARC field staff, when applicable. EcRec team will firstly conduct a Labour Market Assessment (LMA) to identify the employment sectors for both women and men, with more potential to grow, and which creates diversified jobs suitable for rural and urban areas. DRC will develop linkages with the private sector based on the LMA, where DRC will provide opportunities to vulnerable individuals through the internship placement programme. the intervention will target and support vulnerable individuals (at least 35% women) with financial and business development skill trainings. The beneficiaries will be selected based on a standardized vulnerability and suitability assessment of the proposed activities. Selected beneficiaries’ technical experience and eligibility are tested through a supervised technical test. Individuals will be trained on business and financial management, and how to develop and run a business). DRC will invite all participants to submit business proposals based on their skills, capacities, and assets needed. The business grant could be allocated either for start-ups businesses or scale-ups, allowing growth of existing enterprises, or the creation of new scalable ones.

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria is now entering its second decade, and the number of people relying on humanitarian aid for survival continues to grow, a decade of conflict in Syria has displaced a total of 6,845,888 individuals, averaging around 1.4 million displaced per year inside and outside of the country. People in Need is 14 million and People in Food Crisis/Emergency is 12400000. The impact of the prolonged crisis on people, services, and access during the course of 2021 has led to a 15 percent increase in the number of Syrians in need of humanitarian assistance, from 13.4 million in 2021 to 14 million in 2022. The proposed intervention in Syria responds to the Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, highlighting the 11.6 million people in need of early recovery and livelihood support. 51% are estimated to be women, 45% chiLdren, and 26% PWDs, specifically the livelihoods assistance needed by IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents across the country. Syria’s economy – already ravaged by war, COVID-19, sanctions, and a banking collapse in neighboring Lebanon – has been deteriorating. With currency going down, food prices rising, and a fuel crisis, it's estimated that more than 80 percent of Syrians now live below the poverty line and, a record 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure. Persons with Disabilities (PwD) face even greater barriers (both social and physical) to accessing livelihood opportunities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Security situation deteriorates, and humanitarian support (whether directly DRC or contracted vendors) cannot reach the areas of intervention. Likelihood: High, Impact: High. Mitigation: Assess area's situation before intervention and constant communication with donors to have certain levels of flexibility linked to the selected areas of intervention in case it requires shifting due to the lack of access - Selection of beneficiaries and lack of community ownership can create community tensions. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Clear communication plan to be transparent with the people of concern, as well as the mechanism of complaints in place. Close coordination with local authorities, communities, and SARC.- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to inequality of services provided. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Conduct conflict analysis before the implementation of activities and involve communities at all the stages of the intervention.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

COVID 19 pandemic will affect every aspect of the lives of men, women, boys, and girls. Increased prevalence of violence in the home or family, including intimate partner violence (IPV), the loss of employment, restricted access to essential services, and restrictions on freedom of movement can place significant added stress on a family unit and result in the use of negative coping mechanisms. Mitigation: support family members to ensure that COVID-19 prevention and control measures do not exacerbate harmful norms, provide them with psychosocial support, prepare to respond to possible surges in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse; and ensure that vulnerable populations are included in national surveillance, preparedness, and response plans and activities. DRC at the country level is regularly verifying that critical elements of prevention and response plans to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) are in place and prepare for contingencies where they are significantly impacted by the pandemic through training (including refresher) on Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and PSEA policies; transparent response to SEA complaints; GBV mainstreaming in operational spaces/activities; Feedback and Complaints Reporting Mechanism.
