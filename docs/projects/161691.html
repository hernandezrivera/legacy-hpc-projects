Project Summary

Nigeria’s North-East continues to experience intricate and multi-faceted humanitarian crisis in the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) sector, as a result of the ongoing protracted conflict, unpredictable and continuous large-scale population displacements, recurrent large-scale cholera epidemics and seasonal flooding, and fierce storms and winds.
The main drivers of vulnerabilities related to WASH are closely linked to the conflict, including recurrent, unpredictable and continuous population displacements. They are also a result of pre-existing low levels of WASH services.
In response to the needs identified and in contribution to strategic Sector objective 1, 2 and 3 of the WASH sector, IOM will scale-up its provision of WASH services targeting internally displaced people, returnees and host community in camp and camp-like settings.
To this end, IOM will (A) Ensure safe access to safe water facilities and services through the drilling, installation, maintenance and rehabilitation of boreholes; (B) Ensure safe access to sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) through the operation and maintenance of existing facilities and the construction of additional gender segregated facilities; (C) Promote improved water and sanitation outcomes in targeted settings through the systematic promotion of hygiene messages and the support for increased capacity of established community-based committees..; (D) Ensure environmental sanitation (solid waste management) in operation area through establishment of WASH Committee including- male and female community members, procurement and distribution of tools and equipment; (E) Distribution of WASH non-food items (NFI); and (F) Capacity building and partnership with WASH partners and stakeholders

The IOM WASH project will be implemented in three States of North East Nigeria: Borno (8 LGAs), Adamawa (5 LGAs) and Yobe State (4 LGAs), targeting 150,047 individuals.
IOM ensures that all WASH activities take into consideration the age, gender as well as disability -specific needs of project beneficiaries. Through this project, IOM will guarantee the participation and special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and age group, and specifically address women and girls’ specific needs, such as Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). All activities related to this project will equally benefit women and men, girls and boys and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. In regard to assessments, monitoring and evaluation of gender, age and disability specific needs, and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels where possible. IOM will ensure that “Do No Harm” principles are adhered to throughout the WASH interventions, particularly in WASH activities pertaining to latrine and water point locations and construction.

1 - Needs

The increased number of displacements and new arrivals has put existing emergency services under strain and continued pressure, particularly with regard to services provided to displaced populations and has significantly increased the need for WASH services and facilities in camp and camp-like settings. According to Nigeria WASH Sector gap analysis, as of 13th October 2019, considering overall WASH services across BAY states, more than 278,079 people do not have any water source to get minimum sphere standard water supply (15 lit/person/day); 1,215,626 people leaving in these three states do not have latrines as per Sphere standard (20 people per drop hole). 1,874,964 people haven’t gotten any hygiene promotion services including hygiene kits.The need for WASH services and facilities has been exacerbated by heavy rainfall during the recent rainy season from June to September 2019, which has resulted in damages to and destruction of WASH facilities. Furthermore, the general lack of hygiene kits and hygiene awareness among affected populations is impeding safe water collection and storage and other good hygiene practices. With congested populations in displacement sites and lack of basic and adequate WASH services, water-borne diseases spread rapidly. Spiraling cholera outbreaks have taken hold each year. As of 31st October 2019, a cholera outbreak has affected 4 LGAs with a total of 808 cases and 4 deaths in Adamawa State. In addition, flooding, fierce winds and sandstorms have resulted in substantial damages of WASH infrastructures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the community-based WASH committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
