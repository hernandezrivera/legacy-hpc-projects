Project Summary

This project aims to reduce the vulnerability of conflict-affected populations in the Western, Eastern and Southern regions of Libya through the provision of unconditional multi-sectoral cash assistance to internally displaced, non-displaced, and returnee households and improve their access to essential goods and services of their choice. This project will provide three installments of unconditional multi-sectoral cash assistance to approximately 400 vulnerable internally displaced, non-displaced, and returnee households in conflict-affected areas in the West, East, and South of the country.
In cooperation with coordination structures, notably the Cash and Market Working Group (CMWG), REACH proposes to conducting market monitoring and analysis in 2020 through its Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI). Through the monthly monitoring and analysis of prices and market systems, the initiatives improves the understanding among humanitarian agencies and organizations of how markets in Libya are functioning and reacting to the crisis. The JMMI also supports and facilitates the planning, implementation, and evaluation of cash-based interventions (CBI) in Libya by giving accurate information on the prices and availability of key Food and Non-Food Items.

1 - Needs

As a result of the protracted conflict in Libya and the constant population movement that is a characteristic of it, many populations in Libya – including host community members, returnees, and displaced persons – face heightened vulnerability.Prolonged displacement situations have been draining the financial resources of IDPs, host community households and the hosting cities, particularly in the case of multiple displacements. According to the 2019 HNO, vulnerable populations in Libya are forced to resort to negative cash coping mechanisms, including limiting portion sizes (38%), reducing the number of meals per day (31%) and restricting adult food consumption so children can eat (21%). MSNA findings in 2019 also indicate that 10% of households reduced expenses on health or medicine, 11% sold productive HH assets or means of transportation, 25% were forced to borrow money, and 3% asked strangers for money or food. Women and female-headed households in Libya are also particularly at risk of food insecurity and malnutrition, and therefore more liable to resort to emergency level negative coping mechanisms.In addition, the monitoring of market prices in a disrupted economy is crucial to ensure the design and implementation of humanitarian programming is adapted to the local economy. Access to basic services and commodities depends to a significant extent on the availability and functionality of markets as well as the capacity of consumers to actually buy those commodities. While the latter have proven to be generally functional with most basic items generally available, the worsening liquidity crisis and increasing prices, as well as a fragile banking system have placed markets in a precarious situation. Continued monitoring of markets through the JMMI are needed to ensure the availability of data and analysis to continually reassess the viability and impact of market-based programming (including cash) as well as define response modalities.

2 - Activities

In order to support socio-economically vulnerable conflict-affected populations to meet their basic needs, ACTED proposes to provide multi-month unconditional multi-sectoral cash assistance to internally displaced, non-displaced, and returnee households in the West, East, and South of Libya. This project will provide unconditional multi-sectoral cash assistance to 400 internally displaced, non-displaced, and returnee households (240 IDP HH, 160 returnee HH) through the delivery of three instalments of the CTV as defined by the CWG for 2020 (which is scheduled to be revised through JMMI inputs every quarter). ACTED will use the e-card cash modality through a Financial Service Provider, which has been identified as a successful means of MPCA in Libya under ACTED’s ongoing UNHCR project. This e-card modality allows ACTED to ensure strict control mechanisms, with full capacity to track the flow of funds from end to end.Additionally, through support to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI), REACH proposes to strengthen and expand its existing price and market monitoring system, thereby improving the understanding among humanitarian agencies of how markets in Libya are functioning and reacting to the crisis, whilst facilitating cash-based interventions. This initiative will support cash-based response activities by giving accurate information on the prices and availability of basic Food and Non-Food Items on a monthly basis.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Risk 1: E-card distribution sites: security & accessibility for beneficiaries: Beneficiaries may face risks relating to transportation and access to the distribution sites where e-cards are being delivered, including female-headed households, individuals living with disabilities, and the elderly.Risk 2 -Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (SEA): The involvement of humanitarian workers in acts of SEA is a serious violation of the ‘do no harm’ principle, and humanitarian organizations’ obligation to protect populations affected by crises. Sexual exploitation is defined as ‘any actual or attempted abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, or trust, for sexual purposes, including, but not limited to, threatening or profiting monetarily, socially or politically from the sexual exploitation of another.’ Sexual abuse is defined as ‘the actual or threatened physical intrusion of a sexual nature, whether by force or under unequal or coercive conditions.’Risk 3 - Security and safety of JMMI enumerators: Data collection activities for the JMMI may put enumerators at risk of security incidents, when they visit stores in key urban areas to collect information on the prices of key food and non-food items, notably in conflict-affected areas.

5 - Mitigation measures

ACTED will mainstream protection into its cash-based interventions, taking into account lessons learned from previous CBI programming, to mitigate protection risks in accessing CBI programmes/ services, as well as barriers for various individuals to do so based on a variety of diversity factors, including age, gender, and disability status.R1: Distributions of e-cards will take place in day light hours, with security present, to mitigate the risk that women and children are vulnerable to exploitation, violence or sexual harassment while attending distributions. ACTED will take into consideration the restricted mobility facing vulnerable individuals including the elderly by locating distribution sites conveniently as to minimise the distance travelled and would perform a household visit to distribute the card in the case that an individual was unable to attend a distribution for security or mobility reasons. R2: ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism provides a mechanism for beneficiaries or community members to report any instances of SEA on the part of ACTED staff or partners. Where possible, ACTED will ensure a gender-balanced group of staff at its cash assessments and distributions for all program field teams. Additionally, selection criteria for program activities and the principles of humanitarian action, including the fact that humanitarian is free and equal to all without expectations of reciprocal favors, will be explained to beneficiaries of ACTED programming. ACTED also has a prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy that all staff have to abide by, and any violation of this policy results in immediate termination of staff.R3: Security and safety of JMMI enumerators: Given the volatility of the security context in Libya, enumerators may not be able to target all field locations at all times. All enumerators working on data collections are advised to refocus or suspend their data collection efforts if this safety cannot be guaranteed.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) is in place and active and serves as a core means to ensure accountability towards beneficiaries – including those benefitting from MPCA and protection assistance. The CRM is managed by Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation (AME) staff to ensure independence from programme teams and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM phoneline, Whatsapp, email and in the field by AME and programme staff. All complaints are registered and recorded by the CRM Focal Point and categorised based on the level of sensitivity. The CRM Focal Point leads to resolve complaints raised, with the involvement of programme, AME or senior management staff, depending on the case. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint. Within this project, ACTED will ensure that all beneficiaries are made aware of the CRM process and that they are able to raise any issues. In this way, the CRM serves as a feedback and response mechanism that both gathers the views of POCs and incorporates them into project changes, as well as provide a mechanism for beneficiaries or community members to report cases of mismanagement, misconduct, or sexual exploitation.During the screening process, all interviewees are communicated with an ACTED CRM number in case they have any complaint or feedback on the selection process. Information regarding the CRM (what it is, how it works, how to make a complaint, how ACTED processes and responds to complaints) will be systematically communicated to all beneficiaries in this project by the AME unit in partnership with programme teams. ACTED's CRM will connect to the Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) that the Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) will implement in 2020.
