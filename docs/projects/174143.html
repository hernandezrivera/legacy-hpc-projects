Project Summary

COVID-19 has now engulfed almost the entire World, affecting around 220 countries, areas or territories. According to World Health Organization (WHO) globally, as of 10 December 2020, 08:46 am GMT-8, there has been 68,165,877 confirmed cases of COVID-19, including 1,557,385 deaths. COVID-19 is a highly contagious pathogen, transmitting via both contact and droplet as per the latest available data.
February 2020 saw the first case of COVID-19 cases in Pakistan. Most initial cases were those who had traveled into the country from countries already known to be suffering from the pandemic. According to WHO, Pakistan reported 3,138 new cases of COVID-19 on 09 December 2020. The total number of cases notified since the beginning of the outbreak is 429,280 with 8,603 deaths (CFR: 2.00%). Out of the total 46,376 currently active cases 3,170 cases are hospitalized.
In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa as of 9th December, 2020 a total of 655,641 people are tested for COVID-19. Total positive cases are 50,762 with Lab test Positivity rate as 7.7%. A total 1,439 deaths are reported so far with Case Fatality Rate (CFR) 2.8%. Out of total 50,762 positive cases, 656 are admitted (231 on low flow oxygen, 379 on high flow oxygen and 46 on ventilators), 4080 are at home or elsewhere isolation and 44,633 are recovered.
*(Source: Ministry of national health Services, regulation and coordination, National emergency operation center).
The Pakistani government has activated all departments and personnel towards efforts to control transmission and ensure isolation of those tested positive for COVID-19. Quarantine and isolation facilities are established throughout the country to timely support and facilitate suspected & positive cases of COVID-19. According WHO so far 15,000 Health Care Workers (HCWs) are trained on COVID-19 Infection Prevention and Control versus target of 100,000. The number of cases and deaths are increasing with each passing day. The current situation requires training of health professionals in both Government and private sector including private practitioners, quacks and traditional healers on Infection prevention and control, case management, referral, Interpersonal Communication and establishment of linkages with telemedicine, if available. Providing accurate information for prevention of the disease, management of mild cases and referral of serious cases, development of linkages of private practitioners with District Health Administration and reporting of data to the District Health Information system, as well as mitigating public panic is a key to the management of this emergency. As such a well thought out capacity building plan, provision of SOPs and information material including standees, banners and IEC material to the public and private practitioners and social mobilization approach is required to support the Government in the response. The efforts are in progress as far as disseminating correct information and encouraging social distancing practices is concerned, however more efforts are required to combat this menace. Government capacity need to be enhanced, particularly in engaging the private health sector, social mobilization, risk communication, capacity building of frontline workers and implementation of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) with respect to COVID-19, in this regard NMDs deserve particular attention as the access to information there is not at par with rest of KP, therefore additional support is required particularly in high risk districts i-e Khyber, South Waziristan, North Waziristan, Bajaur and Mohmand in KP (erstwhile FATA).
