Project Summary

Improving Access To Quality Health Services Focusing on Major Causes of Morbidity in Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut, Akobo, Longechuk, Tonj North and Gogrial East project seeks to improve access to integrated emergency primary and secondary health care including management of victims of Sexual Gender-Based Violence (SGBV). The project targets 25,000 (12,950 women:12,050 men) people in need with a total cash value of $1.5 million. Of the 25,000 people; 8,000, 7000, and 10,000 people will be IDPs, returnees and host communities respectively.
The project will support the provision of Basic Package of Health and Nutrition services through a network of 12 PHCUs, 7 PHCCs, 1 Hospital, and 8 mobile clinics in hard to reach locations. The Project interventions will focus on prevention and case management of epidemic-prone diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and measles through both static and outreach services; provision of essential medicines and medical supplies; capacity building of staffs through on-job and classroom training; disease surveillance, and routine monitoring of health information through strengthening the DHIS2 system.
The clinical package will include; Health education and promotion, curative outpatient and inpatient services, routine immunization including supplementary immunization activities focusing on measles and polio, Maternal Newborn and Child Health Services (MNCH), and HIV/TB services including e-MTCT. The project will integrate Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) at PHCCs and Hospital, management of severe cases of malnutrition with medical complication at the stabilization centers, identiﬁcation, and referral for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), and Water Hygiene and Sanitation (WASH) at the health facilities.
The project aims at; Improving access and scaling-up responsiveness to integrated essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality; Preventing, detecting and responding to epidemic-prone disease outbreaks; Increasing access to essential clinical health services among vulnerable populations targeting SGBV, and SAM with Medical Complication; and Improving resilience among vulnerable populations by increasing access to mental health and psychosocial support services.
This project is a result of community needs assessments, cluster prioritization strategy, and consultations with the communities in the targeted locations that indicate a dire need for emergency health services for women, girls, men, and boys.

1 - Needs

Everyone has the right to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health that includes all medical services, sanitation, adequate food, decent housing, and a clean environment (WHO). However, this is not the case in South Sudan where an estimated 3.3 million people need life-saving critical care (Health cluster HRP 2020 strategy document). The drivers for this need include the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods (Humanitarian needs overview 2019). According to the various situation assessment reports including the UNKEA health assessment reports, health services are among the frequently requested services by the community members. Averagely, less than 2% of the national budget of South Sudan is apportioned to health which is a very minimal contribution to the health sector while 56% of the general populations are not within a 5km radius of a health facility and 51% of children remain susceptible to vaccine-preventable diseases. NGOs are managing 4 out of 5 health facilities (HPF report). As of 2019, the basic package of health and nutrition services is implementable at only 10% of the health facilities, indicating a dire need for emergency response. The recurrent disease outbreaks coupled with threatening Ebola virus disease outbreak resulting from the outbreak in the neighboring DRC has further concretized the need for emergency response with lifesaving interventions. Following the ceasefire, Upper Nile has received overwhelming numbers of returnees from neighboring countries such as Ethiopia and Sudan. An estimated 64,966 individuals returned to Nasir County alone in March 2019 (ROSS report)-increasing the total number of the population that needs humanitarian assistance to 288,704 (ROSS Sobat report, 2019). Similarly, Ulang County continues to host large numbers of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and a large number of returnees because of its proximity to Ethiopia. The returnees are further putting a strain on the already fragile situation that is characterized by persistent high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) exceeding the WHO emergency thresholds (FSNMS report, Round 22). Maiwut, Akobo West and Longechuk are among the counties with the lowest vaccine coverage for measles and Penta3 in the country (MOH EPI data, August 2019) yet they are a route for returnees. Despite the sporadic inter-clan clashes and frequent ﬂoods that lead to the seasonal displacement of people, Gogrial East has registered high numbers of IDP and refugee returnees (Greater Warrap IRNA 2019) thus requiring humanitarian support. The situation is not any better in Tonj North that has equally received overwhelming numbers of returnees and still continues to register inter-communal clashes. Due to persistent health service delivery challenges, Tonj North registered a massive measles outbreak early this year that affected all payams. In all the targeted project areas, preventable diseases such as Malaria, Acute Respiratory Infections, measles and Acute Watery Diarrhea are among the top five causes of morbidity and generate the most alerts (week 38, 2019 IDSR). Children aged 0-12 months remain susceptible to vaccine-preventable diseases with only 23% and 19% receiving DPT 3 and measles-containing vaccines respectively (MOH EPI data August 2019). The situation is not any better for pregnant women. Deliveries under skilled birth attendants remain very low with less than 10% of the targeted population utilizing services (DHIS 2 service utilization at 21 UNKEA supported health facilities). This project will contribute to a reduction in morbidity and mortality in the implementation areas.

2 - Activities

UNKEA will support delivery of BPHN through primary health care and secondary health care in the above-earmarked locations through a network of 38 PHCUs (9 Nair, 1 Ulang, and 12 Gogrial East), 9 PHCCs (4 Nasir, 1 Ulang, 4 Gogrial East), 1 hospital (Nasir), and 7 mobile clinics (1 in each of the locations) . The package and scope of services that will be offered will include;(a) Conduct Emergency preparedness and response: UNKEA will build train and mentor health workers to effectively implement infection prevention and control in the facilities, perform early reporting of suspected cases, collect samples and facilitate preliminary isolation of suspected highly contagious cases like EVD.(b) Provide case management for common causes of morbidity. UNKEA will train, equip and facilitate health workers to provide static and mobile curative outpatient and inpatient services focusing on common causes of morbidity such as malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea, and measles. Support will include provision of incentives, medical equipment, HMIS tools, and job aids.(c) Provide routine and supplementary immunization activities. UNKEA will support the health workers to provide both routine and supplementary immunization activities targeting vaccine-preventable diseases. Support will include training on Immunization in Practice, developing and implementation of micro plans, requesting and management of vaccines, and monitoring of uptake of vaccine services.(d) Support management of SAM with medical complications. UNKEA will train clinical officers at the supported PHCCs and hospitals to manage cases of SAM with medical complications at the established stabilization centers. The project will work closely with the nutrition departments and ensure the establishment of a robust referral and follow up mechanism.(e) Strengthen WASH in health facilities. The project will support the construction of gender-disaggregated pit latrines, construction of incinerators and placenta pits, provision of handwashing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control, and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, handwashing will be promoted at all health education sessions.(f) Strengthen sexual and gender-based violence services through training in CMR: UNKEA will train health workers at PHCCs and hospitals in Clinical Management of Rape. The training will equip the health workers to identify, counsel, and manage victims of SGBV, setting up a robust system of referral of cases from PHCUs and the community to PHCCs, receiving and accounting for CMR kits from UNFPA, and handling of confidential information. (g) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS). UNKEA will train health workers in the identification, referral, and management of patients with mental health issues. Health workers at the lower facilities (PHCUs) will focus on identification and referral, while those at the PHCC and hospital will manage the referred cases.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project legally recognizes the rights of the beneficiaries. All beneficiaries have the right to access to medical services, to complete information, to be treated with dignity and the right to refuse or accept any medical intervention. The project will thus strive to educate the beneficiaries on their rights using the patient charter and during health education, and community dialogue meetings. All the engaged health workers and project staff are required to sign up to a code of conduct, and are expected to do no harm during provision of services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Displaced affected people are among the key beneficiaries of the project. During project design, the project staff conducted consultation with various stakeholders among whom were the displaced people. Selection of facilities, and outreach sites were guided by accessibility of the services to both the host community and displaced people. In addition, mobile clinics will ensure that displaced people living in hard to reach areas are also served. All services provided by the project are free of charge and thus accessible by anyone. In addition, the structured and frequent consultative dialogue meetings will ensure that the perspectives of displaced people continue to filter through and guide the subsequent project activities and interventions. UNKEA conducted both formal and informal consultations. The formal consultations included 20 focused group discussions (5 Nasir, 5 Ulang, 2 Maiwut, 3 Akobo west, 3 Gogrial East and 2 Tonj North), in-depth interviews with key informants that included commissioners, payam administrators, leaders of women groups, representatives of people living with disabilities, a youth representative and CHD directors, and 5 semi-structured dialogue meetings with community members in all the target implementation areas. In total, 600 people were consulted of whom 129 were displaced people.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to government/community systems. Among the interventions is the roll-out of the Boma Health Initiative that includes a strong components of engaging the community in service delivery. Under the BHI, the project working with the community will identify community health workers who will provide health promotion and curative services for uncomplicated cases of diseases among children less than five years.The government has proposed to roll out the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition and the BHI as the key health interventions. Almost all the project activities are aligned to the BPHN and BHI that are long-term interventions. The key reporting system will be the DHIS2, which is a more sustainable and a long-term durable solution to management of health information. In delivery of activities earmarked in this intervention, the project will empower and work alongside the county health departments of respective counties. This is intended to strengthen mandated government agencies in assuming their roles more competently both in medium term and long term.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the South Sudan health policy and South Sudan national strategic plan that prioritizes access to quality basic services to promote health. The main objective of the project is to contribute to a reduction of mortality and morbidity by focusing on the key causes such as malaria, measles and acute watery diarrhea. The mentioned diseases are among those prioritized by the government. There is a strong drive to build the capacity of health workers and align the incentives to the government structures. Interventions shall utilise local-based procurements in order to promote productivity in targeted counties. A combination of both human and non-human resources required shall be sourced from beneficiary communities as first prority. This is in line with South Sudan national protection policy that considers combined approach of providing assistance while strengthening capacity of individuals to increase their productivity and ability to withstand future shocks. At conclusion of the project, established structures and local built capacities will remain. Skills gained by the health workers will continue to serve the population long after the the project is closed.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The recent peace agreement has resulted in some safety and stability providing opportunities for people to return to their homes. For example, large numbers of returnees have been registered in Nasir county and Gogrial East. Engagements between oppostion and government actors have paved way to allow unhindered access and movement of humanitairan workers and items. Movement options have been widening to enable access to affected populations who are in most need. Local institutions of police and community policing are gaining traction. The buy-in of different protection measures by armed groups even in viotile conditions are conducive for delivery humanitarian assistance. The project has integrated protection within its activities to ensure that no harm is done to the individuals and communities served. At all times, interventions will taking heed of the best interest of the people and ensuring that the people are served with dignity. The project will also benefit from UNKEA's integrated protection approach that mainstreams protection across regular sectoral activities. In Ulang and Longechuk, UNKEA has already supported development of four (4) protection plans as means of creating an enabling environment for interventions in 2020. Similar initiatives will rolled out in other project locations proposed for this intervention.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project interventions are sustainable and will easily be handed over to the government structures. The project is working to strengthen government recognized structures such as the community health departments, and is also providing incentives based on the recommended harmonized incentive that was proposed by the government. Joint integrated supervision and monitoring will be conducted and this brings together both the project staff and other government actors. The project is also working closely with the relevant government structures through the MOH coordination mechanisms to streamline access to some key medical supplies and equipment, supported health facilities are included in the MOH data base, and that DHIS2 is the major HMIS used. In addition, the project is strengthening government structures such as the community health departments, structures that are envisaged to take on greater role and responsibility in the future. The project will not implement parallel systems but will strengthen the existing systems such as the health management information system.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNKEA conducted a Protection Risk Assessment with the aim of doing no harm. The assessment indicated a low risk of harm that would arise from inappropriate disposal of medical waste, propagation of infections at the health facility and community outreaches, not being sensitive to the cultural expectations of the communities during service delivery, GBV from sharing of WASH facilities, and long distances to available health services that will require women and girls to walk distant places. The risks will be mitigated through training of staff and health workers in Infection Prevention Control measures; ensuring that all supported facilities develop and adhere to a waste management plan, ensuring availability of an appropriate chaperone during consultations, health workers follow universal precautions at all times, and women/girls are consulted in the selection of outreach sites. Wherever possible, the patient charter will be displayed and patients sensitized on their rights. The project will also ensure that privacy and conﬁdentiality of information is prioritized at all times through provision of ﬁling cabinets, availing screens, minor rehabilitation of consultation rooms, and ensuring informed consent for all medical procedures. To further strengthen the protection component, UNKEA’s protection specialist will be tasked with conducting regular assessments to identify any gaps and propose strategies to mitigate the risks.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will adopt plural communication channels that will include low technology and non-technology options to cater for both sensitive and nonsensitive feedback, and will ensure that the chosen communication channels are relevant and inclusive to even the most vulnerable populations such as women, girls, and people living with disabilities, illiterate people, and people living with HIV. The Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems (suggestion boxes, notice boards), interpersonal communication through trusted liaison persons, and anonymous calls using the telephone and or email where possible. Feedback will be solicited both in person especially for those people that cannot read and write, but also using other means such as telephone, and writings. In addition, the project will conduct regular systemic assessments of appropriate channels of feedback as guided by community preferences. Regular feedback/ complaint mechanisms will be incorporated through the project life span; at the health facility, suggestion boxes and client exit interviews will be used; in the community, quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various community members. Project achievement will be posted on notice boards both at the CHD and at the health facilities. The project in collaboration with the beneﬁciaries will identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison people. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the ﬁeld and also at the organization headquarters. All complaints will be investigated in a systematic method ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times. The project M&E system is designed to capture client feedback through the scheduled client exit interviews and qualitative analysis.
