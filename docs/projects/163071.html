Project Summary

The planned interventions are aimed at improving the food and nutrition status of 24,000 vulnerable households (120, 000 individuals) disaggregated as follows by district; Mudzi 8,000 H/H (40,000 people), Mutoko 9,000 H/H (45,000 people) and Murehwa 7,000 H/H; (35,000 people) in Mashonaland East Province. The project is an extract from the ZimVac report. Mudzi and Mutoko districts are semi dry and associated with food deficits due to the consecutive droughts experience in the country. As a result of the unstable macro-economic environment in Zimbabwe, household food stocks have been depleted. Households have diminished purchasing power, which is exacerbated by the hyperinflation affecting prices of basic commodities. Following the 2018/19 drought, crop production was well below average and the country is faced with a 2019/20 national cereal deficit estimated at over 800,000 MT according to the Zimbabwe Food Security Outlook for October 2019. The food security situation in the 3 districts is dire and the ZimVAC 2019 Rural Livelihoods Assessment Report indicates that 77.2%, 62.4% and 58.6% of the HH are food insecure in Mudzi, Mutoko and Murehwa districts respectively. The high food insecurity levels have also been attributed to the long dry spells which negatively affected the yield levels for the smallholder farmers during the 2018/2019 farming season and to the poor post-harvest handling methods that are being used by most of the households. Marginalized households without access to either cereal or alternative coping strategies have been rendered chronically food insecure, vulnerable to shocks and stresses, and heavily rely on external support for their daily food and nutrition needs during the food deficit periods.
Mercy Corps proposes to implement in the selected districts in order to scale up on the work currently, undertaken in Murehwa district funded by the EC under ZAGP, where the project is supporting small livestock producers promoting the market value chains. This project will leverage on the current work, up to 2023 providing room for scaling up to the two proposed districts not currently covered by interventions.
The broad objective of the project is to prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population, by providing emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring that can be food secure and resilient to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors.
The project proposes to reduce food and nutrition insecurity through rehabilitation of productive assets, capacity building of extension staff and farmers, distribution of crop and livestock input packages through a voucher system, distribution of small livestock to vulnerable households and increasing access to affordable feed for livestock through the voucher system. The project proposes to use the voucher system instead of cash transfers to support the distribution of inputs to ensure that farmers get the value of the support as mobile money has been observed to disadvantage the beneficiaries through extra charges levied by service providers. The growth points in each of the target districts have adequate retailers to support the programme with inputs.
Mercy Corps recognizes that women and girls face challenges in the society given their limited capacity to contribute better to household food security and income. The proposed project recognizes the roles that women, men, boys and girls play in families and society and activities have been tailored to accommodate these needs. Mercy Corps will build capacity of program staff as well as government and private sector partners in gender analysis tools, gender perception, and their influence on food security.

1 - Activities

Strategic Objective O2: Prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population, by providing emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring they can achieve food security and resilience to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors Specific Objective 1: Minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs for 3 000 households (15 000 individuals) in Mudzi, Mutoko and Murehwa districts in Mashonaland East Province. Participatory beneficiary selection criteria will be followed where key stakeholders will facilitated community meetings that will select vulnerable households using agree poverty indicators. The process will be inclusive and ensure the targeting of marginalized and minority groups such women, girls, youth, elderly and persons with disabilities. The project is proposing to issue each seed beneficiary with a voucher worthy $90 (fertilizer, seed for both nutrition gardens and dryland farming). Key Activities1.1 Provide vegetable seed packs and tools for 1 500 households, 30 community and school nutrition gardens1.2 Provide drought-tolerant seeds and fertilizers for 6 000 households1.3 Procurement and distribution of 600 goats and 9000 indigenous chickens to 3000 households (restocking)1.4 Procure and distribute 90 tonnes of cattle survival feed to 6 000 household1.5 Procure and distribute basic cold chain equipment 1.6 Conduct livestock vaccination and disease surveillance in 3 districts. Specific Objective 2: Enhance access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for crops and livestock production through infrastructure rehabilitation and construction. Identification and selection of community nutrition gardens will be in consultation with the beneficiaries and agriculture extension staff and priority will be given to gardens with high numbers of beneficiary, high yielding water points and potential crop yield. Community gardens will be supported with a crop kit which will include beans, leafy vegetables, root crops, pesticides and knap sparyers. Key Activities2.1 Rehabilitation of 15 dip tanks and community animal handling facilities and provide dipping chemicals2.2 Rehabilitation of 15 piped water schemes and canals 2.3 Establish fodder plots at individual and community gardens2.4 Establish 3 solar powered micro-irrigation schemes to support 1500 households 2.5 Construction of 30 animal drinking troughs at high yielding water points Specific Objective 3: Optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to improve agricultural yields and manage crop pests and livestock diseases.The livestock kits will be made of veterinary drugs, vaccines, weighing belt and acaricides to ensure basic animal health. Community Based Animal Health workers will be supported and facilitated to service the target farmers. More farmers will trained because the project will targeting farmers from communally owned gardens as farmers with individual gardens, and those who might not have received inputs, but within the same villages. Key Activities3.1 Conduct 6 ToT sessions for lead farmers on animal husbandry including local feed formulation3.2 Recruit, train and equip 60 community based animal health workers (promote ethno-veterinary practices) 3.3 Provide training, extension and advisory services to 60 extension officers and 1500 lead farmers focusing on good agriculture practices, climate-smart agriculture. crop pest management, postharvest handling, storage and processing.
