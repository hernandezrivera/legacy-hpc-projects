Project Summary

The humanitarian needs in Syria continue to rise, year after year. Escalating violence in 2023 has led to further displacement and suffering. The socioeconomic situation has continued to deteriorate, further exacerbated by the February earthquakes, negatively impacting social cohesion, and amplifying vulnerabilities. This project is strategically aligned with the 2024 HNO summary and the 2024 PiN for education cluster in NWS, particularly in terms of targeted locations. Specifically, the project focuses on educations gaps in Rata, Azaz, Afrin and Idleb districts. These areas have been identified as the first-priority underserved regions, characterized by high severity indices and significant numbers of internally displaced persons. The project encompasses various components:
1. Formal Education: the project aim to support the quality of formal education in 12 publish schools through the support of teachers professional development, school-based psychosocial support (PSS), monthly operational costs, distribution of teaching materials and learning supplies for teachers and students, Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) establishment as well as providing light furniture, rehabilitation, safety equipment and solar systems. This comprehensive approach seeks to enhance access to quality formal education for 6,000 children (boy: 3000, girls: 3000) and 264 teachers, with a focus on gender equality mainstreaming. This will include the payment of monthly incentives for 264 teachers (M: 132, F: 132) (only in Idleb), and the provision of schools transportation for 100 CwDs (boy: 50, girls: 50) .
2. Non-Formal Education: the project aim to support the dignified learning spaces alternatives initiative in informal settlements and 12 non-formal learning facilities with the same array of interventions mentioned above for formal education. This initiative aims to enhance access to non-formal education response, particularly for 6,000 children (boy: 3000, girls: 3000) who are dropped out of schools or at risk of dropping out due to barriers related to access/supplies, 2,400 (boy: 1200, girls: 1200) for non-formal TVET and 800 children (boy: 400, girls: 400) for ECCE. This will include the payment of monthly incentives for 264 teachers (M: 132, F: 132), the cash transfer for 800 children (boy: 300, girls: 300) and the provision of schools transportation for 100 CwDs (boy: 50, girls: 50)
3. Winterization Support: To mitigate the adverse impact of winter on children's education during the academic year 2024-2025 and 2025-2026, the project will support the provision of winterization items in the form of heaters and fuel to the 24 education facilities benefitting 15,200 children (boy: 7600, girls: 7600) and 528 teachers (M: 264, F: 264) directly.
Note: The target is cumulative for the 2 years of 2024 and 2025.
Disability inclusion is a cornerstone of our project. BO will conduct FGDs to assess needs, install ramps, offer special latrines, train education personnel, provide transportation for CwDs, and register children with disabilities door-to-door during the back-to-learning campaign. We're committed to ensuring equal access and participation at every stage.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs assessments: According to the HNO 2024, there has been a 4 per cent increase in the PiN, compared to 2023 year. This emphasizes the urgent need for emergency education services to support up to 7.2 children in the whole of Syria. The governorates with the highest percentages of OoSC include Idleb (69 per cent), Aleppo (38 per cent) and Ar-Raqqa (48 per cent). The main drivers of needs relate both to the supply and demand of education. OoSC are exposed to a broad range of gender protection risks. The longer children remain out of school, the less likely they are ever to return. Across Syria, the main driver reducing access to education is economic, affecting both the households and teaching personnel. Although education is officially free, including textbooks, financial hardship causes the family to pull their children out of school to reduce spending on indirect cost of education (such as school supplies and transport) and resorting to negative coping strategies, exposing children to gendered protection risks such as child marriage and consequent early pregnancies. Furthermore, access to accreditation, including exams remains a need. Monitoring and evaluation mechanism: The M&E mechanism in this project will ensure effective implementation, accurate progress tracking, prompt challenge identification, and continuous improvement. It includes field visits, data collection tools, feedback mechanisms, activity logs, and partner meetings. Evaluation methods include surveys, outcome mapping, case studies, interviews, focus groups, and feedback surveys. Risk: Inadequate safeguarding measures may expose children to risks of abuse, exploitation, and violence within schools and informal learning spaces.Mitigation Measures: Implement child protection policies and procedures specifically tailored for educational settings.Provide training to teachers and staff on child protection principles, identification of risks, and appropriate responses.Establish child-friendly spaces within schools, equipped with trained personnel to provide psychosocial support and assistance to vulnerable children.Risk: Gender-based violence, including sexual harassment and assault, may prevent girls from accessing education or force them to drop out of school.Mitigation Measures:Conduct gender-sensitive assessments to identify barriers to education access and retention, particularly for girls.Provide targeted training on gender equality and GBV prevention to teachers, students, and community members.Establish safe transportation mechanisms and safe routes to school, particularly for girls living in areas with high GBV risks. PSEA strategy: BO will ensure confidentiality, access, and follow-up for SEA allegations. Complaints will be made anonymously via hotline, email, or in-person. Trained operators handle the hotline, providing information and support. Zero-tolerance policy communicated to all staff. Training on safeguarding and PSEA provided to staff and BNFs..
