Project Summary

This program will increase the resilience and livelihood opportunities of the most vulnerable women-headed HHs and PWDs in Aleppo (A'zaz subdistrict), Idleb (Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Mhambal sub-districts). To address the many gaps in livelihoods opportunities and income generation that have arisen in the target locations due to the ongoing crisis in Syria, Ihsan RD is proposing to support 400 HHs (2400 individuals) in 2023 with small business grants through cash vouchers worth to 1000 $ per BNF. The targeted BNFs will be 150 women head HHs, 150 youth, and 100 PWDs). All the supported businesses will be the non-agribusinesses such as haircut, sewing, embroidery, clothes marketing, minimarket …etc, and any other business unrelated to agribusiness). The grants will be distributed in two installments; each installment will be 500 USD with intervals of one month between the two installments as follows:
1- First stage: BNFs will receive a cash voucher worth 500 USD (this fund will cover the basic needs of a small business project, such as rehabilitation and purchasing some equipment needed to start the initial establishment of stage small business projects.
2- Second stage (after one month of the first one): BNFs will receive 500 USD (this amount will cover the additional needed materials to complete their own businesses). The second batch of the grant will be distributed to the target HHs after making sure that the beneficiary showed a clear obligation to start his/her project and spent the first batch on the established project
In addition to small business grants, each BNF will be supported with entrepreneurship and innovative training programs to improve their Knowle of planning, designing, managing, and marketing for their own businesses.
It is worth mentioning that IRD will conduct an in-depth labor/ need assessment for non-Agri small businesses during implementation to identify the gaps in these professions, then the BNFs involved in this project will be selected based on the following selection criteria
Minimum eligibility criteria: The applicant should not be a beneficiary of a similar grant from another organization, no access to regular income.
Prioritization criteria: Households with pregnant or lactating women, families with a higher number of children, and households has one or more disabled family members.
To ensure harmonization with no duplication of efforts in the proposed location, IRD will coordinate and cooperate with the ERL cluster and all actors in the target location to obtain full details about their planned interventions in the target area. IRD will also conduct cross-reference beneficiary lists through coordination with the acting organizations in the same locations to ensure that households who are directly benefiting from similar projects by other organizations are not included on beneficiaries’ lists for IRD intervention.
To ensure community engagement, prior to implementation IRD will facilitate the community to come up ‎with their own action plan and ‎prioritize the ‎necessary small business projects and provide ‎their ‎opinions, and suggest ‎possible solutions. A key ‎relationship in this program will be between IRD ‎and the local ‎councils. IRD ‎will ‎‎arrange introductory workshops with the Local Council, Community Leaders of both women and men. The purpose of the ‎workshop will be to ‎present the project ‎concept, its purpose, and expected outcomes. The workshop will ‎also describe ‎the criteria that will be adopted in the project. The workshop will also be a key ‎opportunity to ‎ensure that IRD receives feedback from the local ‎community ‎on the project planning and look at ways ‎in which IRD can incorporate ‎recommendations from the local ‎community to confirm the actualization, maintenance, and sustenance of the project.
Note: The activities for the year 2023 are planned activities (unsecured funds).

3 - Needs

The planned interventions had been designed based on key messages of the HNO report, 2022, which mentioned expectations of a further increase in the cost of living, including reduction of subsidies and general prices increases as well as volatility in the exchange rate, among others, are projecting onto an additional deterioration in terms of real income and purchasing power across all population groups. the sector foresees the income/expenditure gap to continue increasing in the coming year across all population groups. Furthermore, for 90 per cent of females and 87 per cent of males, the loss/insufficient income and the lack of opportunities to access income sources constitute their first concern vis-à-vis physical and mental wellbeing, followed by the unaffordability of food and other essential items. Borrowing, debt increase, and loss of income as the main causes for debt change present big and extremely comparable trends among IDPs, residents, and returnees, indifferently of the group. This evidence across all population groups is confirmed by the fact that livelihood support is considered the second-highest priority for IDPs, returnees, and residents, with comparably high percentages across the three groups (overall 56 per cent).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk (1) Low Gender equality: Ihsan RD will conduct a gender analysis to identify the barriers and obstacles faced by the active participation of women in design-making throughout all project phases and analysis of gender‐based violence to equal access to planned support. Protection risk (2): Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: 1- Availability of wheelchairs for PWD at the reception point (IRD contribution), a trained staff to deal with PWDs Protection Risk (3): Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. Mitigating measures: Choosing appropriate time for distributions of small business grants (cash vouchers), voucher exchanging in Hawala agent will take place during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting, conducting awareness sessions about GBV, ensuring that the field staff includes female members to coordinate with female BNFs.Protection risk (4) : Low access to an accountability mechanism, self-protection, and for beneficiaries to claim their rights, especially (SEA) Mitigation Measures: By starting of the project, Ihsan R D will manage complaints mechanism and ensure accountability to beneficiaries by assigning one MEAL staff to manage any received feedback/complaints throughout the implementation stage of the project, with close attention to PSEA cases after advanced coordination with Ihsan RD protection centers and PSEA focal points


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Base on Ihsan RD PSEA policy and before starting of project interventions, IhsanRD will conduct SEA risk assessment of to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while provide the support to BNFs, 2-BNFs do not report SEA cases for Ihsan RD staff, which results in the Ihsan RDbeing unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance tothem.Mitigation: After in-depth assessment for these risks, Ihsan RD throughcollaboration with PSEA focal point will designed the mitigation plan all these risksas follows Mitigation measures: 1- Ihsan RD will be ensure that all its staff hadsigned code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staffshould attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs. 2- Beneficiaryawareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware on: what SEA is, what their rightsare in regard to SEA, what they can do if SEA occur3- Establish safe andaccessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be alsoavailable (e.g. complaints box / telephone hotline to deal with these cases)
