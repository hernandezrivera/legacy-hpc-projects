Project Summary

Physicians for Human Rights Israel (PHRI) will strengthen and support the local health system in areas lacking in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt). This will be done through a multi-level modus operandi, combining referrals, workshops, supporting patients whose permits to exit their areas of residence have been denied through legal representation and litigation; advocacy in front of Israeli and international stakeholders.
PHRI will facilitate referrals for 180 patients from the West Bank who require medical care outside their areas of residence. In some appropriate and/or emergency cases, PHRI will also fund such treatments, tests, or follow-up examinations.
For those patients who need treatment unavailable in the West Bank and whose requests for exit permits from the Israeli authorities have been denied, as well as for health staff needing to exit the West Bank for training, PHRI will intervene to reverse these denials in 50 cases. PHRI will use legal action combined with a range of advocacy tools when necessary.
The intervention also aims to enhance preparedness within the health sector and reinforce the capacity of the Palestinian health system. Trainings will take place through online modules and workshops.
The intervention will also address the Health Cluster’s strategic objective 2, carrying out advocacy to protect the right to health of the most vulnerable in the oPt. PHRI will use a multilayered strategy to challenge violations of right to health, specifically focusing on impediments to Freedom of Movement. PHRI will reach out to international medical organizations to bear pressure on the Israeli medical community, while continuing to raise awareness among local diplomats in embassies and consulates, with the hope that they will flag violations of the right to health with Israeli stakeholders. Concomitantly, PHRI will reach out to UN instruments, which will hopefully issue statements that generate interest and pressure. Focus will also be given to women and children in PHRI’s advocacy work. Additionally, Palestinian prisoners detained in Israel will be supported to overcome barriers to health and access medical treatment.

1 - Needs

Israel’s half century occupation of the oPt has rendered the Palestinian medical system dependent upon Israel. This has led to both medical dependence on the treatments Israel can provide and unreasonable economic burdens on the Palestinian Authority. As indicated in the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan for the oPt, the occupied Palestinian territories are amidst an all-encompassing protracted protection crisis, brought about by the occupation. This is compounded by the COVID-19 crisis, which has forced the Palestinian Authority to declare a state of emergency on March 5th 2020. The COVID-19 crisis, which affects all aspects of the local population’s daily life, places further strain on the healthcare system, and impedes the right to health of the general population in some West Bank communities, both in terms of capacity and resources. After the coordination between the Palestinian Authority and Israel ceased, PHRI played a key role in processing and coordinating freedom of movement requests; according to the WHO oPt Health Access August 2020 Monthly Report, PHRI was one of two organizations who processed permit requests and had the highest percentage of approval (85%). Since 6 September, the WHO has been operating a temporary coordination mechanism for permit application coordination. Based on PHRI’s extensive experience in protecting the right to health of Palestinians detained in Israeli prisons, we learned that they are among the most vulnerable detainees, as they are subjected to a series of restrictions and may endure gunshot wounds or violence during arrest, followed by cruel, inhumane and degrading treatment and torture during interrogation. In addition, as they are detained for the long periods, their health deteriorates in prison where services are limited and sub-standard. PHRI’s activities on the ground allow us to track developing and existing trends on the community level. PHRI’s needs assessment mechanism involves both continued cooperation and contact with stakeholders such as village councils and Ministry of Health officials, as well as an analysis of the data gathered on the ground through our medical delegation of specialist doctors work. PHRI has observed growing needs for referrals in the fields of Internal and family medicine, orthopedics, physical medicine and rehabilitation, gynecology, nephrology, neurology, cardiology, among others. PHRI’s needs assessment is based on a variety of sources: WHO situation reports, the Humanitarian Response Plan for the oPt (HRP), the Health Cluster’s Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), reports from other organizations, and its own practical assessments. PHRI is in constant dialogue with local partners, the Ministry of Health and physicians themselves. PHRI has been handling permit denials for 25 years. PHRI’s local partners are in contact with the beneficiaries and report to us on the needs of our patients.

2 - Activities

Subsequent to the outbreak of COVID-19 in the oPt, PHRI will continue to adapt its activities to the new and changing climate, addressing the newly emerged emergency needs of beneficiaries in the West Bank. Therefore, PHRI will employ significant adaptability and flexibility in the implementation of the planned activities. Activity 1: During the women’s medical delegations, PHRI’s volunteer professionals will conduct health education awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, addressing women’s health issues, in an effort to empower them to claim their rights. Activity 2: 180 patients in need of treatment unavailable in the West Bank will be supported with referrals to hospitals or appointments in East Jerusalem or Israel. Activity 3: The project will enhance the skills and knowledge of Palestinian medical staff to improve health services through practical workshops. Activity 4: PHRI will support patients whose permits to exit the West Bank for treatment have been denied. While the coordination between the Palestinian Authority and Israel ceased, PHRI will continue to play a key role in processing and coordinating freedom of movement requests. Particular attention will be given to female patients, as well as parents who need to accompany their children. This will be done parallel to and in close coordination with the WHO’s temporary coordination mechanism, which relies on human rights organizations as articulated in the temporary coordination mechanism flowchart prepared by the WHO. When deemed necessary, PHRI will take legal action to appeal refusals, including principal action when it becomes aware of a new security regulation affecting a grouping of patients. Activity 5: The project will protect the right to health of Palestinians through advocacy activities targeting Israeli duty bearers, including security officials and Members of Knesset, combined with media work to increase pressure. International advocacy activities will challenge Israel's policies and their adverse effect on Palestinians' health. Activity 6: Through individual casework, legal action and advocacy, the project will support Palestinian detainees in Israel overcome barriers to health, with a special emphasis on the most vulnerable.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The direct inquiries, feedback, and complaints raised by our beneficiaries and partners constitute the primary focus of projects’ needs assessment, implementation, M&E, and future project design. The feedback mechanism is integrated into all aspects of the project.PHRI’s feedback mechanism operates through two channels. First, feedback from partners and the MoH aids PHRI in project assessment, modification of implementation, M&E and future project design. PHRI’s partners and local physicians are in constant dialogue with the Director of the Medical Delegation who is in charge of the referrals mechanism. The Ministry of Health is a key partner in all activities. Palestinian organisations, most of which are grassroots NGOs well embedded in the host communities, play an important role in pointing at patient needs and providing feedback. The second channel for PHRI’s feedback mechanism is receiving feedback directly from beneficiaries through questionnaires, which will be distributed to a certain percentage of beneficiaries- depending on the activity. A questionnaire will be re-designed for this project, with a strong emphasis on beneficiary feedback and assessment, and will be distributed, scored, and analyzed. Further, complaints will be received by PHRI’s volunteer doctors and partners, who will self-report on any complaints received. Regarding freedom of movement cases, PHRI plans to conduct two in-depth interviews with beneficiaries every month (one successful and one unsuccessful case), to get a better understanding of its working modalities and how they are perceived. Appropriate measures will be taken when relevant. PHRI is also in regular contact with prisoners through lawyers and families. Cases are followed-up on until a final resolution. PHRI receives feedback and cooperates with other organisations working on Palestinian prisoner issues.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

During all interactions with beneficiaries, PHRI ensures COVID-19 precautionary safety measures, such as physical distancing, surgical mask wearing and hygiene promotion are adhered to. Further, PHRI ensures that secondary negative effects of COVID-19 are identified and responded to in a timely manner. Do No Harm principles are considered through all PHRI’s projects. All staff and volunteers have experience working in the oPt and have a well-integrated conflict-sensitivity. The physicians are all volunteers working in their free time in solidarity of their Palestinian colleagues and patients and have great professional and personal integrity. PHRI’s work is also guided by our safeguarding policy document. It inter alia describes how PHRI is committed to preventing and responding appropriately to harm to children and vulnerable adults; how PHRI openly communicates a lack of tolerance towards abuse; commits to preventing a person from working with children and/or vulnerable people if he/she poses an unacceptable risk; places clear responsibilities and expectations on PHRI’s staff and associates and supports them in understanding and acting in line with the safeguarding policy. The policy is implemented by providing training for personnel, contractors, volunteers and board members. Furthermore, PHRI has a robust recruitment screening process and employment contracts containing provisions for dismissal, suspension or transfer to other duties for any employee who breaches the safeguarding code of conduct. All our staff and volunteers are guided by the Health as a Bridge for Peace concept. PHRI prioritizes the safety and dignity of its patients. Information shared by beneficiaries is always kept confidential, unless otherwise instructed. PHRI’s activities support the development of self-protection capacities and help empower beneficiaries, allowing them to claim their right to health. Patients who approach PHRI needing referrals and appointments outside the West Bank, who ask PHRI to try to obtain an exit permit after their application has been turned down, must claim their rights themselves – this is particularly true of female patients which are empowered to own and defend their cases. Indeed, each permit request requires quite a commitment on the part of the patient, who needs to go back to her doctor for more information in her file, understanding fully her case, etc.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PHRI strives to remove all access barriers to our activities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. In the context of the COVID-19 crisis, PHRI will continue to ensure that hygiene standards are upheld, in order to remove barriers for high-risk groups. PHRI provides assistance in accordance with basic humanitarian principles, based on identified needs (impartiality), without any adverse distinction, as indicated by the different categories of patients that are treated by our clinics, have underwent surgeries or received referrals or permits in the past decade: women, men and children, with a special focus on the most vulnerable that our partners help identify (humanity). Vulnerable groups are targeted through our network of well-recognized partners on the ground – women grassroots organizations, rehabilitation organizations, cancer organizations among others. Acceptance levels within the target population are high – PHRI is well known after over three decades of regular activities and is vetted by partner organisations on the ground. PHRI –an Israeli organization- is perceived as a professional, trustworthy and neutral. In its work advocating for the right to health of Palestinian prisoners, the latter’s families or lawyers contact PHRI on a hotline made available at fixed hours which are advertised with Palestinian partners, lawyers, and on the PHRI website. Volunteer physicians and lawyers are sent to visit the most vulnerable cases. Torture victims in particular benefit from a very close follow-up.
