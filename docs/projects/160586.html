Project Summary

The protracted armed conflict since December 2013 has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population in South Sudan, further stretching their coping capacity. The security situation in many states, as well as the deteriorating economic state across the South Sudan is affecting the provision of basic, lifesaving services. This situation of armed conflict and population, coupled with a longstanding environment of poverty, lack of infrastructure and food insecurity, has caused mass displacement. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11 % coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF/WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme. The situation is further compounded by the lack of sustainable operational and maintenance systems for existing WASH facilities and infrastructure because of governance challenges.
Through the 2020 humanitarian need overview and humanitarian response plan, World Vision International South Sudan intends to achieve to WASH cluster objectives which are: 1) Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees. 2)Mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence. 3)Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.
To materialize the WASH cluster objectives, World Vision will provide lifesaving WASH emergency interventions in the former Upper Nile, Warrap state, Northern Bhar El Ghazal and Western Equatoria states. The WASH emergency interventions will improve access to safe water, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion, which will be integrated in other sectors, among them health, nutrition, Food security and livelihood, and child protection/ GBV.
In the year 2020, World Vision will reach out 212,766 beneficiaries (104,256 females, 108,510 males); out of these beneficiaries, 21,406 females and 89,360 males will be from the host communities.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict since December 2013 has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population in South Sudan, further stretching their coping capacity. The security situation in many states, as well as the deteriorating economic state across the South Sudan is affecting the provision of basic, lifesaving services, such as WASH, to displaced and vulnerable populations. This situation of armed conflict, displacement and re-displacement, coupled with a longstanding environment of poverty, lack of infrastructure and food insecurity, has caused mass displacement. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11 % coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme. The situation is further compounded by the lack of sustainable operational and maintenance systems for existing WASH facilities and infrastructure as a result of governance challenges. The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan stands at 7 million, or well over half the population. On-going fighting and surges of violence in new areas have forced people to flee their homes, many of them multiple times. The number of people uprooted since the start of the conflict in 2013 has reached more than 4 million, including 1.9 million internally displaced people (IDPs), with up to 85% estimated to be children and women.Violence and rights violations continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians. Internally displaced people’s access to services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and older people, suffer the most intense consequences of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to services. In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist in South Sudan. The counties where World Vision has nutrition intervention are classified between IPC 1 and 4. Access to clean water and adequate sanitation is a huge challenge to most of the population; access to health facilities is limited with only 56% of the population having to health services. Morbidity due to water born disease and poor sanitation is high with a risk of malnutrition, particularly among Under 5 children and PLW. In response to the humanitarian needs, World Vision intends to provide WASH interventions in line with the WASH cluster’ s objectives as per the 2020 humanitarian response plan: 1. Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package.2. Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level.3. Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.4. Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.5. Mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence.

2 - Activities

1. Construct, rehabilitate, and maintain water supply facilities for 212,766 people in Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Unity states2. Provision of with household water treatment products to households in the target locations. 3. Water quality testing, analysis and chlorination for water source points. 4. Rehabilitation of water points from non-functioning water sources rehabilitated.5. Construction of household level sanitation facilities at selected beneficiaries to support open defecation free communities in target locations in Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Unity states6. Training water management committees of 8 people per each WMC committee. 7. Conduct hygiene promotion activities to improve the knowledge on prevention and mitigation of WASH-related diseases.8. Provision of WASH integrated interventions starting from the OTP/SC will target the couple “mother/caretaker – malnourished children” until the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrheal diseases/nematodes with malnutrition, addressing also smallstock open defecation affecting the young children.9. Carrying out comprehensive water points management for cattle and smallstock requirement during the dry season, with promotion of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent animal open defecation down to the household level.10. Provision of WASH in Health lifesaving engineering for outbreak control (cholera/AWD, typhoid, HEV, EVD etc.) by transmission contexts (water-borne, intra-domiciliary, school, market, funerals, health facilities upon specific demands) upon epidemiological investigations.11. Using WASH intervention to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses.12. Conduct WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation in and outside PoC/settlement will be based on Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children for WASH activities adequately planned with the community input13. Provide sustainable WASH services that address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the project will recognize legally the rights of beneficiaries. Being a child-focused organization, World Vision will mainstream child protection in all components of the project. World Vision supports the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; children have the right to be protected "from all forms of physical or mental violence, injury or abuse, neglect or negligent treatment, maltreatment or exploitation, including sexual abuse". World Vision works to uphold this right through working with local communities and national and international governments to address both the causes and manifestations of violence against children and will incorporate child protection into this program through ensuring that all beneficiaries are protected and their rights are upheld during the project implementation. Project staff members will receive training in protection, child protection, and humanitarian standards to uphold accountability. The special concerns of physically and mentally disabled people will be addressed through careful targeting and active project participation. To promote peace building, project stakeholders will be involved in a Local Capacities for Peace/Do No Harm analysis at the initial stages of this project. World Vision also recognizes that all organizations that come into contact with children have a fundamental duty of care towards them. As such, World Vision acknowledges the level of responsibility to keep children safe in both relief and development interventions, and is working with a number of other agencies to develop and promote good practice in child protection among international organizations that come into contact with children.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

World Vision will ensure that all the target beneficiaries, in the four states, have access to nutrition specific and sensitive interventions through both fixed and outreaches modalities. World Vision International South Sudan will use its Fragile Context Programming Approach ( FCPA) which allows flexibility in the modalities of the lifesaving nutrition interventions. With such approach, World Vision will move from provision of WASH in static points to Rapid response mechanisms in case where Rapid Response can be the only option which can permit accessibility to beneficiaries.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

World Vision will implement this project in collaboration with the Ministry of Water and Irrigation at national level; at the state ministry level, World Vision will work in collaboration and will support the state and county department in ensuring that the target populations have access to safe and clean water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion. World Vision will work in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture, the Ministry of Health, etc.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project will adhere to Ministry of water and irrigation policy and guidelines. Additionally, the project will be implemented by adhering to humanitarian principles; World Vision will continuously get support from the WASH cluster.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/ stable. Environmental issues are key in all World Vision programming and environmental protection measures will be taken to mitigate negative environmental impact as a result of activities of this project. Safety measures will be taken in the disposal of waste in the nutrition facilities by use of an incinerator and nutrition staff will be sensitized on proper and appropriate disposal measures. Environment and natural resource being a critical, target beneficiaries will be encouraged to conserve the environment and avoid practices that contaminate or affect the nature during the project activities. Hygiene promotion will help to promote messages about the importance of water conservation in addition to basic hygiene and sanitation practices. It is predicted that these sanitation messages will also have a positive impact on the surrounding environment as communities are encouraged to avoid water contamination through keeping their area clean and clear of possible diseases or infections. As all the interventions planned to protect and further promote environmental sensitivity, this project will positively impact on the environment in the project location.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

World Vision will strengthen community support structures to improve community resilience and create an enabling locally based prevention and response mechanism. World Vision will build the capacity of county workers in the department of water supply and will ensure that the Water management committees are well established and well trained to take over from World Vision when the time for exiting arrives.
