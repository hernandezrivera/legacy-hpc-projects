Project Summary

Nutrition Sector data for HNO HRP 2021 for the two localities (Guli and Ad Diwaim) in White Nile being proposed for this intervention is as follows (on a scale of 1-5 with 5 being worst): For U5 GAM rate, Ad Diwaim ranks 5 and Guli 4; Both localities rank 5 for stunting rate, rank 4/5 for overall nutrition sector severity, are at IPC level 3 and above for food security needs. Majority of the population are practicing open defecation due to inadequate WASH facilities, being exacerbated by recurrent floods affecting White Nile (being on banks of river Nile) and people's deteriorating purchasing power due to collapsing economy and rising inflation rate. The impact of COVID 19 in 2020 has wreaked havoc on basic needs of vulnerable populations in White Nile.
AAH is present in Sudan since 2018, with operational bases in Khartoum, White Nile, Central Darfur and South Kordofan states. AAH is currently implementing two emergency interventions in White Nile State as part of the COVID-19 response (funded by GFFO) and flood response (funded by SIDA).
Overall objective of proposed intervention is Reduce severe undernutrition on children & PLWs of emergency-affected & highly vulnerable households through multi sectoral life-protecting interventions. Project targets already vulnerable (in terms of nutrition, health and WASH indicators)14 communities in 2 localities, comprising of around 190,209 people, recovering from shocks in 2020 (i.e. floods emergency and COVID 19 and its cascading impact on economy and livelihoods) while these communities were not fully recovered from prior shocks (for instance, 2019 floods emergency). Assistance will be in form of nutrition (support to essential primary health care and nutrition public services, as well as integrated community case management), WASH at health facility and community levels (improving access to basic water services, adequate (basic or limited) protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services and hygiene promotion services), and mental health care practices gender protection (IYCF, sensitisation on gender discriminatory harmful traditional practices such as FGM and early marriage). Community participation will be fostered via capacity building, mobilization and follow-up of community volunteers that will engage in sensitization activities (related to nutrition, health, hygiene, sanitation, gender). Some volunteers will also be supported to identify and manage cases of undernutrition and common childhood illnesses in the community. Select volunteers will be provided training to be able to facilitate participatory vulnerability capacity assessments (PVCA) at their community levels. Community volunteers will conduct screening and referral of malnourished children and PLW; they will identify, treat and refer children with common childhood illness, do rapid IYCF assessment at HH level and refer PLWs with IYCF problem, conduct home visit, defaulter tracing and community sensitization. AAH and their local partner in White Nile, CAFA, will co implement this project together, as per AAH localization strategy in Sudan. The proposed intervention will encompass emergency preparedness activities for sustainability of this intervention and as exit strategy - AAH and CAFA will establish Water Sanitation User Committees (WSUC), Disaster Risk Management (DRM) Committees at community levels, in addition to Mother-to-Mother support groups, and provide extensive trainings to community volunteers on several essential topics. All volunteers will be trained on needs assessment and AAH complaint response mechanism.
Target population is based on HNO 2021 data and White Nile MoH data, with vulnerability selection criteria focusing on very poor and vulnerable households. Key beneficiaries are 14 OTPs and 56 staff (4 per center); 150 community volunteers; Mass Screening: 32,336 (16,491 girls & 15,845 boys) ; 4300 HH receiving hygiene & dignity kits; soft activities to target all 14 village population

1 - Needs

In Sudan, a decline in the economy is driving nutrition and food insecurity and raising poverty levels while the country continues to face the health and humanitarian consequences of COVID-19 and recurrent disasters, mostly floods. Approximately 81% of Sudan’s population experiences barriers to accessing healthcare (MSNA 2020). Over 9.8 million people (21% of the population analyzed) were severely food insecure at the peak of the lean season (June to September). 21.9% population is living under the poverty threshold of US$1.90 a day and 57% of Sudan population is living under the poverty threshold of US$3.20 a day (SDG report 2021). 28% of total households resort to the food based negative coping strategy and 51% of the households had to resort to livelihood based coping strategies, focusing on immediate food needs and depleting their assets (CFSVA 2020). Food insecurity rates in Central Darfur, South Kordofan, Blue Nile and White Nile are 18%, 20%, 18%, and 18% respectively (IPC Feb 2022). According to S3M 2018 survey, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate is 16.5%, 7.8%, 7.2%, and 9.6% in CD, SK, BN and WN respectively with wide difference by localities. The stunting rate in CD, SK, BN and WN is 42%, 40.7%, 37.3%, and 40% respectively which is considered critical/very high based on WHO emergency threshold (>30%). Recording low immunization coverage (Pentavalent 3: 58.9%), Sudan has high prevalence of common childhood illness including Diarrhoea (21.3), ARI (23.8%) and Fever (24,2%). Key feeding practices of children such as dietary diversity is low (24%) . Sudan maternal mortality rate is also among the highest in the world, at 311 per 100 000 livebirths Wharton 2020. Only 51.3% of pregnant mother attended 4 or more ANC visit with 70% mothers delivering at home (HNO 2021). Contraceptive prevalence rate (Any Method) and unmet need is 8% and 18% respectively (UNFPA). Essential medicines are accessible in only 43% of health facilities, while only 33% of health facilities can provide all main components of primary care, with services particularly limited in conflict zones such as Darfur, SK , and BN (HNO 2020). In Sudan, 50% of children’s acute malnutrition is associated with repeated diarrhoea or worm infections related to poor WASH conditions in Sudan (UNICEF). According to S3M Sudan has the highest F2018, nearly a third of households practice open defecation which poses grave public health risks, including the transmission of diseases. People with access to improved water source on average are 32.7% in WN, 71.3% in BN, 86% in SK and 50.6% in CD. Population with access to improved sanitation facilities are 39.7% in BN, 10.4% in SK, 15.8% in CD and 29.8% in WN (MICS 2014). As per AAH Gender Analysis findings in CD, SK and WN (currently ongoing in Blue Nile), women/girls’ lack access to nearby and well-located water points and gender-segregated sanitation (and MHM) facilities have contributed significantly to GBV risks directly impacting their health and nutrition status. Only 29% of households in Sudan have access to GBV services (MSNA). GBV referral pathways are either not fully established (some states and many localities), and if they are, majority of people are not informed. In2022, AAH will provide nutrition service in a total of 50 OTPs including scale up of SAM treatment in 10 OTP targeting 8,572 U5 children(Tadamon, Ghadeer). Estimated 1,431 SAM children with medical complication will be referred to 10 SCs and over 35,000 PLW will be reached through IYCF services and practices. Nutrition service will be delivered integrated to health service including outpatient service, Immunization and RH. Among other criteria's, FSL will target HH with SAM and MAM case to ensure that the community have access to foods, cash or agricultural while WASH focus on provision of safe water , construction of gender sensitive sanitation facilities and promotion of hygiene including kit distribution to SAM HHs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The programme will continue to focus on Four core sectors, by promoting synergies and complementarities. In Nutrition and Health, AAH will focus on delivering integrated high impact health and nutrition services to vulnerable children, adolescents and women, provision of quality treatment for acute malnutrition cases integrated with primary health care and increase knowledge and skills of households and caregivers on key household practices. Interventions include screening, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW , IYCF services, outpatient treatment (IMCI), reproductive health (ANC, SBA, PNC & FP), vaccination and health education. To ensure quality of service, the program will provide multiple capacity of training for health workers including SAM management, IMCI, EPI , BeMONC. Health facilities will receive adequate therapeutic products, Essential drugs and medical equipment's, IPC supplies, furniture's , stationaries, monitoring reporting tools. Effective referral system will be established for smooth transfer of cases from community to facility, and within nutrition and health services. Baby-Child Friendly spaces will be created and supported to provide cognitive and emotional stimulation for crisis affected women and children through psychosocial and protection support. In WASH, AAH will maintain regular WASH assistance for the most vulnerable populations to ensure communities have access to safe water, dignified sanitation services and increased good hygiene practices. The proposed activities include: drilling of new boreholes, upgrading of existing hand pumps into mini water yards, construction of water treatment plants, construction/rehabilitation of Gender-sensitive dignified latrines, incinerators and hand washing facilities in health facilities, strengthening capacity of water users’ committees and health facilities staff to ensure proper use, operation and maintenance of WASH services. To conduct water treatment at HH level, AAH will distribute chlorine tablets (0.33mg/l) targeting HH with PLW and children with SAM/MAM. Furthermore, the project will carry out rapid water quality tests to measure prevalence of E. coli bacteria. To improve menstrual and reproductive health, AAH will distribute dignity kits for targeted women/girls. The project will also distribute hygiene kits for households. AAH also will carry out regular hygiene promotion sessions aiming to prevent spreading of diarrhoeal disease and COVID- 19 including implementation of baby WASH. In FSL, in line with HRP 2022 and FSL sector objectives, AAH will focus on promotion of beneficiary resilience and reduction in beneficiary aid dependency through multi-purpose cash assistance, income-generating activities and distribution of agricultural inputs, home garden kits, tools and seeds. AAH will also mainstream disaster risk management into FSL activities with emphasis on climate change. Beneficiary selection will be based on vulnerability criteria including HH with acute malnourished children and PLW, Female headed HH, elderly and disability. HH registration, selection and beneficiary verification by community will be undertaken before delivering assistance.Gender and protection will be mainstreamed throughout the programme for effective and impactful programming, to contribute concretely to GBV risk mitigation. AAH will focus on bridging gap of access of girls and women to essential services and ensuring their participation through programme cycle, sensitizing people on GBV at community and facilities engaging local actors. AAH will prioritize reinforcement, activation and / or establishment of referral pathways for enhancing support for GBV survivors, as well as supporting free psychosocial counselling (by phone). Mother to Mother support groups, Youth forums and Male forums will be established in different villages to support service uptake, increase women/girls participation, leadership and decision making.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In order to minimize the environmental impact of the implementation of the project activities, several mitigation measures will be taken. To limit the emission of greenhouse gases, the project will sensitize drivers of machinery and vehicles to switch them off when not in use. Any lasting changes to landscape and/or infrastructure will need to be accounted for and done in the least invasive manner possible. Materials used for construction and rehabilitation will be environmentally friendly and preference will be given for locally made materials. The project will sensitise communities to avoid wood cutting as part of the implementation of CLTS. To this end, the project will support the construction and supply of slabs for family latrine pits. The project will ensure that latrines are located at least 30 m from water sources and water surfaces. In addition, latrines will be build in areas where there is at least 2 m of unsatured soil between the bottom of the pit and the water table. For kits provision and distribution, packaging will be reduced at minimum and locally made and environmentally friendly items will be promoted. AAH will distribute menstrual hygiene management kits (MHM) containing locally produced reusable cloth pads which will decrease waste product going into environment.In addition, proposed project activities will ensure positive and effective impact on health and environment which will include: The rehabilitation of non-compliant community water facilities in order to stop possible risks of contamination. Through the construction of an apron with a drainage system, the rehabilitation of handpumps will ensure adequate drainage around the water point, avoiding the creation of stagnant water and preventing contamination of the water source. Each water point will have a fence around it to prevent animals from contaminating water. The implementation of CLTS will promote the construction and use of latrines among consulted vulnerable communities in rural areas, in order to stop practice of open defecation, thereby preventing health risks and environnmental contamination. At health facilities level, the project will train and equip health facility staff to ensure proper use, operation and maintenance of WASH facilities, as well as to improve hygiene practices and safe management of medical waste. Mothers/caretakers of SAM/MAM children will be advised to bring in empty RUTF/RUSF sachets for proper disposal. The project will ensure the safe desludging of full latrines and safe disposal of sludge. Medical waste will be managed and disposed of in adherence to MoH guidelines, starting with the segregation of different categories of biomedical waste in specific colored containers at all locations where waste is generated. Subsequently, waste will be incinerated in accordance with the environmental rules governing the use of incinerators.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender dimension at all stages of the intervention will be considered through an active and inclusive participatory approach, working closely with CAFA and JMCO and all stakeholders on the ground, ensuring appropriate responses throughout the ground for each target group: girls, women, boys, men and disabled groups. Activity data will be collected and disaggregated by sex , age and Disability where possible, and the results of their analysis will be used to adjust the program. AAH will also ensure gender balance in each deployed team, including maximizing women's and girls' access to the project and facilitating communication, awareness and exchange of information. AAH will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action. According to assessment findings of Plan International in Guli and El Duiem localities, girls are being subjected to early marriage (indirectly related to multi layered crisis this year) and boys and their families are refusing marriage if girls have not had FGM (even though it has been banned in Sudan this year). Also, girls and women are exposed highly to protection concerns during floods outbreak due to collapse of infrastructure, especially water and sanitation. AAH strongly adheres to the principles of Do No Harm and given the specific context will ensure close monitoring of project implementation and post-implementation in order to ensure supported rights holders are not subjected to negative externalities as a direct result of AAH or LNGO activities. Furthermore, Protection will be at all cases considered and AAH, CAFA, JMCO will continue to actively work together with partners to ensure Protection remains a topic on the radar. AAH works in close collaboration with the UN Clusters (WASH, Health and NUT and the technical working group), which remind and include protection rules in the response so that all actors are in line with the same standards. Therefore, AAH will follow the clusters’ recommendations together with their own standards against GBV. As with any emergency, there will be a Protection working group which includes the specific actors who are tasked with protection. AAH will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action. Through proposed intervention, AAH will provide sensitize communities, including male community and religious leaders, on gender discriminating practices and provide dignity kits for girls and women of reproductive age. Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) is also mobilized by AAH MEAL through multiple channels (hotline numbers, complaint boxes) in order to maximize potential for having feedback from marginalized community members and enable them to have direct access to AAH, for reporting on any concerns, including protection issues.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AAH will involve the technical services as well as administrative and customary authorities in order to ensure the smooth running of the activities and to guarantee the quality of the work carried out. Furthermore, the project beneficiaries will be mobilized at all stages to ensure ownership and sustainability of the work. In its intervention, AAH will adopt approaches that encourage the needs of the most vulnerable people to be taken into account when targeting and carrying out activities, thus helping to meet their immediate needs. AAH has conducted multi sector need assessment in targeted states to understand the nutrition, health, WASH and FSL through consultation of key informants and focus group discussion with women, mew, boys and girls. Recommendation from the community are considered on the design of project including the need to recruit more female health workers and Community volunteers. AAH has consulted beneficiaries through PDM regarding their current needs and gaps, what type of food and non-food items they suggest for future distributions and whether they prefer cash distribution over in-kind assistance. The results showed that most of the beneficiaries suggested Cash grant while some suggested food items such as oil, maize, sugar, rice, wheat, lentils which is what AAH distributed in 2020. Contents of the different food and NFIs Kits and selection criteria are displayed using banners/communication materials at distribution sites with the information to beneficiaries on channeling their feedback/complaints to AAH. The project teams will organize awareness and information sessions with beneficiaries on project’s objectives including dates and periods for each activity. AAH teams will use 'talking points' as way of formal introduction to each community upon arrival in order to avoid any confusion or dispute regarding the project and beneficiaries’ selection process. During that time and subsequent visits by staff and volunteers, all information will be shared with beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism established by AAH. The reception of complaints and feedback from the populations of the intervention areas, partners, authorities, NGO staff and the different actors is done through complaints boxes at villages committees’ level and through CRM (including hotline numbers) which will be distributed to targeted beneficiaries. In addition, informal feedbacks from beneficiaries will be collected during the monitoring activities. Each information request or complaint received is recorded, documented, categorized and managed accordingly. The ethic committee at HQ level in Madrid would be involved for complaint and feedback related to corruption, fraud, PSEA.
