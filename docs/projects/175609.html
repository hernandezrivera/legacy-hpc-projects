Project Summary

The project aimed at implementing 12 months project. With little or no initiatives on supporting engagement of crisis-affected people and key stakeholders in humanitarian work, humanitarian operations have never been going on smoothly in South Sudan. Many obstacles/challenges had been experienced by humanitarian aid workers at different levels during delivering of humanitarian services. In some cases, young men from either host communities or IDPs who are in possession of firearms and/ or illegal guns do harass humanitarian aid workers. Vandalization and/or destruction of public amenities such as health facilities and school structures. Therefore, in light of this, there is need to empower community especially crisis-affected peoples’ participation in humanitarian service delivery. This can be done through establishment of key contacts/relationships building between community leaders and/or representatives of various interest groups. Engage in capacity building, reinforcement of community governance structures and strengthening of feedback systems. This will ensure that the humanitarian aid agencies and key stakeholders are accountable to the beneficiaries in all project location /areas. By bringing these groups together, the crisis-affected people and key stakeholders will have a leverage and opportunity to voice their opinions and views. To facilitate linkages to better, appreciate and understand the impact of humanitarian services. This relationship will in turn make the communities to promote mutual respect between the communities and humanitarian aid workers. This is to help increase partners’ confidence and a safe environment for provision of humanitarian services. AADO will install and reinforce community governance structures that will oversee activities at Payam and county levels respectively. The Payam appointed leadership will oversee monthly meetings of community groups and / or working groups on Communication with Communities (CwCs). The essence of community engagement and accountability points shall constitute sensitization on humanitarian work of different clusters. The feedback will be obtained and awareness sessions will be conducted based on the gaps identified. The multi-sectoral feedback committees’ will be responsible for coordination of activities in their respective clusters directly report to AADO on monthly basis. This will be non segregatively or discriminatorily constituted by sex or age and will include sub committees of Health, Education, WASH, Shelter/NFIs, Food security/ Livelihood, Protection, Nutrition, CCS, Logistics and CCCM respectively. These sub committees shall be part of community development, gender sensitivity and social protection committees, advocacy committee for marginalized persons and a peace building and conflict resolution committee. AADO will perform capacity building training to empower the community groups and multi-sectoral feedback committees to enable them drive community participation and protection during their involvement in humanitarian work. Once all their structures are carefully and logically linked to each other and capacitated to their respective roles and responsibilities, its eminent that humanitarian work will become meaningful and credible to all stakeholders. Subsequently, smoothen and facilitate peoples’ resilience and transformation from shock of different gaps created in peoples’ way of life. After exit of AADO, the capacity given and technical supported rendered to communities in all project locations the community shall have attained capacity depend on its itself.

1 - Needs

Aware of recently signed “2018” revitalized peace agreement that gives a ray of light at the end of the tunnel to South Sudanese for peace and stability, the general situation remains dire in many parts of the Country. The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence, and /or paralyzed / destroyed livelihoods. These left more than seven million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need for humanitarian assistance and protection (WHO September, 2020). Therefore, as humanitarian aid agencies are delivering humanitarian assistance in terms of food, education, Health, WASH, Shelter / NFIs, protection and nutrition, population in need continue to suffer. The services required to crisis-affected population in various locations in South Sudan remain insufficient. This is due to very weak community governance structures or even none at all. With many issues of insecurity, civilians who are in possession of illegal guns threaten humanitarian aid workers have never been able to deliver services freely and /or without fear of being harm. According to the report on humanitarian access in South Sudan, 2020, it was reported that South Sudan is one of the most challenging humanitarian operating environments in the world, due to security concerns inclined on logistic challenge. Access to people of concern in the population is particularly challenging in disputed areas. Of recent, it was reported that about 1.5 million people are still living in areas where armed hostilities, violence against humanitarian aid workers and assets plus other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely hampered and restricted. And in some cases impossible according to UN-OCHA report, 2020. Many at times there are issues to do with revenge killings targeting national humanitarian aid workers, harassment of humanitarian aid workers, Vandalization of boreholes, health facilities and school structures and looting of aid supplies by army youth civilians/fighting forces. This culture of Vandalization of boreholes, schools and health structures by armed youths or people who are or whose relatives are mainly beneficiaries of such facilities has been making humanitarian aid agencies to incur un necessary costs. Similarly, the culture of revenge killings and harassment of humanitarian aid workers created an adverse effect where the wave of these attacks have prompted the humanitarian aid workers to pull out of all their services if the revenge attacks and other forms of harassments continue. In light of this context, there is need to set up and reinforce community management/governing structures to relook at the issue of revenge killing and harassment of humanitarian aid workers. To mitigate Vandalization of boreholes, health and school structures from a critical perspective through empowerment of community participation and involvement in the work of humanitarian aid workers. Crisis-affected people and other stakeholders should be enlightened on recognition of humanitarian work. The commitment to uphold the safety and security of humanitarian aid workers, including by providing a feedback mechanism and/or platform that is relevant, feasible and sustainable for both humanitarian aid actors and host community.

2 - Project activities

Through this project AADO will expand its scope and build from its previous project, Support Humanitarian Services by setting up/strengthening community management and governing structures and feedback mechanisms to empower engagement and participation of host community and / or crisis-affected people. AADO has the following strategic objectives:1. Strengthen joint needs analysis informing strategic response planning for effective and coordination humanitarian action;2. Enhance program equality through strengthened accountability to affected population. For sustainability purposes AADO will include key stakeholders in humanitarian work in Pibor, Wau and Longecuk Counties. AADO will set up different sectoral groups and representatives/working groups on Communication with Communities (CwC). The community engagement and accountability point of view, from community and key stakeholders in its current project locations. These will strengthen already formed in project locations / areas. These groups and representatives will include; representatives from youth groups, representations of churches, women groups, other interest groups or organizations. All these groups and their representatives will be trained on relevant thematic capacity building topics. This will enable them to play crucial and critical coordination role. By their participation, we enhance the work of humanitarian partners and /or organizations. Coordination and gathering of information in hard to reach areas is imperative for humanitarian operations. Yet conducting social mobilization and sensitization of communities on importance of “not practicing revenge killings” and more especially on humanitarian aid workers is of paramount significance. To enlighten communities on importance of taking good care/management of public assets such as boreholes, schools and health facilities. There will be a well elaborated feedback and complaint mechanism in place. We shall install suggestion boxes in more accessible points in the communities. It will be set up in public places. The data and / or pieces of information obtained from these boxes shall be analyzed. The outcome of the analyzed pieces of information shall constitute a regular feedback and complaints. The role of AADO monitoring and evaluation team will be to analyze the data. The results of the analysis will be shared with AADO’s higher management and other humanitarian actors who are operating in these Counties. This was seen working very well in the ongoing CCS project AADO is implementing in Pibor, Wau, Longecuk. The project will reach a total of 15,000 direct beneficiaries and 90,000 indirect beneficiaries. The summary of the crisis-affected persons/beneficiaries as per county is as follows: Pibor has 5,000 direct beneficiaries (1,200 Male and 1,800 Female) and indirect beneficiaries (7,200 Male and 18,000 Females); Longecuk has 5,000 direct beneficiaries (7,200 Male and 18,000 Female); And Wau has 5,000 direct beneficiaries (7,200 Male and 18,000 Female). These brings the total population targeted for this intervention to 15,000 direct beneficiaries and 90,000 indirect beneficiaries. The fifteen thousand shall be enlighten on the importance of full participation and involvement in the work of humanitarian agencies.
