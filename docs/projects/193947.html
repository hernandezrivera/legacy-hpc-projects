Project Summary

The Child Protection component will target 11 states which prioritize vulnerable boys and girls on the move, with a particular focus on unaccompanied and separated children as well as those at risk of various forms of violence and exploitation, including sexual and gender-based violence, recruitment, child labor, abduction, and other forms of abuse through provision of life saving support through individual case management services based on a best interest assessment.
In addition to children, the intervention will also aim to support foster parents, women, and men in the affected communities who have been displaced or forced to migrate, including internally displaced persons, refugees, returnees, asylum seekers, and immigrants.
Furthermore, the response will be built around MHPSS through Children’s regular and consistent engagement of children in group activities that will positively impact their well-being, enhance their resilience, reduce their stress and provide a sense of normalcy in a difficult context through CFS/GFS. Save the Children and its partners will facilitate structured play and recreational activities through GFS/CFS which will provide children opportunities to come together in a predictable and stimulating environment to be safe, to learn, to express themselves, to make connections and to feel supported. This will not only promote protection but allow the project identify children who are vulnerable or who are experiencing abuse, neglect, exploitation or violence and supporting appropriate referrals.
The response is framed around the centrality of protection, involving conflict sensitivity analysis, CP mainstreaming and integration across sectors, with a focus on identification and response to grave and other serious child rights violations including recruitment, and private advocacy on establishing accountability mechanisms for child rights violations.
To strengthen protection of children, community-based child protection structures will be established/ strengthened to enhance and elevate the community-level protection and outreach activities and support the parents and caregivers to provide a safe atmosphere for their children and monitor the hazard and risks in the community such as UXOs.

1 - Needs

Sudan’s growing humanitarian crisis is set against the backdrop of a protracted economic crisis and pre-existing conflicts in some parts of the country, with an estimated third of the population already in need of humanitarian assistance. In addition to large-scale displacement, the conflict has shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care and other basic services for millions of people. High insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices has compromised humanitarian access to key locations. Sudan is now facing violent urban warfare and civil unrest, a surge in criminality and communication breakdowns (Revised HNO 2023). The ongoing conflict is a major protection crisis, among the killed and injured, many are children. Indiscriminate attacks have been reported against civilian populated areas and infrastructure, including schools and hospitals. There is a high risk of children on the street being associated with organized criminal gangs engaging in looting and of being exposed to recruitment and use by parties to the conflict. Large scale family separation while on the move have been reported, with many unaccompanied and separated children. Children, especially adolescent girls, are at risk of sexual and gender-based violence amongst other protection threats. Unexploded ordnances (UXOs) pose a grave danger to children; many have been reported either killed or maimed due to mines in the on-going conflict. (Based on CP 2023 HRP). From January 2021 to June 2022, the UN verified 349 grave violations against 326 children (228 boys, 93 girls, 5 sex unknown) in Sudan, of which 147 violations against 131 children took place in the first six months of 2022. The most prevalent violations, killing and maiming, and Gender Base Violence (GBV) against Children continue to remain concerning. (2022 HNO). In 2023 Save the Children's response will contribute to the overall HRP of Sudan through delivery of Child Protection and GBV component. The intervention will prioritize vulnerable boys and girls on the move, with a particular focus on unaccompanied and separated children, as well as those at risk of various forms of violence and exploitation, including sexual and gender-based violence, recruitment, child labor, abduction, and other forms of abuse through provision of life saving support through providing individual case management services based 0n a best interest assessment. In addition to children, the intervention will also aim to support foster parents, women, and men in the affected communities who have been displaced or forced to migrate, including internally displaced persons, refugees, returnees, asylum seekers, and immigrants. The proposed response will be built around MHPSS through Children’s regular and consistent engagement in group activities that will positively impact their well-being, enhance their resilience, reduce their stress and provide a sense of normalcy in a difficult context. We will facilitate structured play and recreational activities through GFS/CFS which will provide children opportunities to come together in a predictable and stimulating environment to be safe, to learn, to express themselves, to make connections and to feel supported. Furthermore, the response will be framed around the centrality of protection, involving conflict sensitivity analysis, CP mainstreaming and integration across sectors, with a focus on identification and response to grave and other serious child rights violations including recruitment, and private advocacy on establishing accountability mechanisms for child rights violations. All of the above, clearly dependent on strong risk management and what is possible in context.For the next six months, SCI and it's partners will engage in Community Awareness on Un Exploded ordnance and mine risk education, SGBV, due to the presence of UXOs in the streets and houses which can expose children to high risk of death and injuries, as well as positive parenting.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction works will be done to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. In addition, some activities the Multipurpose centers and Girls Friendly Spaces, would look into teaching children about environment and encourage them to have some planting through the green spaces activities as part of the center and child participation interventions. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. The programme design and implementation are informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work and coordinate with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs, and INGOs) which will be done through ensuring information sharing based on need, the aim is ensured relevant and related actors are informed and maximize the use of resources and sharing of best practices and to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. Save the Children will support and work with the CP and GBV AOR to provide regular updates and analysis on certain protection issues, shading lights on immediate issues that needs urgent response throughout the active monthly coordination meetings at both federal (Khartoum) and states levels, and investing in strengthening humanitarian practitioners' capacities in child safeguarding to do no harm to children. Save the Children Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

SC has a zero-tolerance approach against all forms of abuse and exploitation, our child safeguarding policy works to protect children and families in contact with SC. SC safeguards children from deliberate/negligent actions that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury, and other harm. The child safeguarding reporting procedures ensure cases are investigated and concluded within a specified period depending on the categorization of those cases. SC will take necessary disciplinary measures against any staff, including reporting to local law enforcement authorities, and will ensure all consortium partners adhere to the safeguarding policies. The Whistleblowing Policy allows a person to raise a concern directly with senior leaders if they feel unable to use usual reporting processes. All staff, volunteers, and partners that work with or on behalf of SC sign the policy before beneficiary engagement, and regular training ensures employees/volunteers are provided with necessary information regarding child safeguarding issues. We have focal points/champions at each field office and area office to coordinate all safeguarding matters. Information sharing will include feedback and complaint mechanisms available for recipients and accessible to all gender and age groups to provide feedback or raise concerns. For SC, feedback and complaints will be collected through meetings as far as the conditions allow, and SCs accountability phone number to link response beneficiaries with SCI, and other local partners. Feedback and complaints received will be transferred to the relevant SCI to be addressed, documented, and analyzed to contribute to the learning processes and decision-making regarding the action. SC has established a functional accountability and MEAL system in intervention for direct beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback or raise concerns about response implementation and progress, including call-in numbers, feedback, complaint boxes, and direct collection. Feedback and complaints received will be categorized and accordingly transferred to relevant SC staff to be addressed, documented, and analyzed to contribute to quality delivery, learning, and decision-making processes regarding the action. In addition, accountability information-sharing materials which will include program standards and the account number will be produced to be distributed and pinned up on all the supported facilities under this project. Furthermore, SC will involve persons and representatives from other vulnerable groups to participate in the implementation and monitoring process of the project. In particular, the committees/networks and the women’s centers will form a key part of beneficiary identification, referral, advocacy, PSS, awareness-raising and sensitization, networking, and monitoring for this project. All these actions and measures taken by SC will adhere to the (Do No Harm) approach.
