Project Summary

This project's objective is to contribute to reducing the mortality and morbidity of the most vulnerable, IDPs and host community, including children under 5 years old, women and girls, the elderly and people with disabilities, in Borno State's Maiduguri Metropolitan Council, and Borno State's hard-to-reach areas of Monguno (Monguno LGA) and Pulka (Gowza LGA), by providing integrated Wash in health in the health structures already supported by PUI, and WASH emergency response to the community living in the vicinity of these facilities. The proposed activities align with UNICEF's strategy and the WASH sector's priorities, including targeting hard-to-reach areas in Borno Sate, which remains the state most impacted by the large-scale humanitarian crisis which has been affecting Northeast Nigeria for the past 13 years.
PUI will implement a range of activities to ensure safe and adequate access to drinking water supply and sanitation services in the health facilities it supports across the three locations, as well as for the most vulnerable people in the community living in the facilities' vicinity. These activities will include: the rehabilitation/ construction of water points or storage tanks, the construction of gender-separated latrines/showers in the facilities, and the provision/construction of waste management equipment. Members of the communities will be trained / their skills strengthened to ensure sustainable management of the works. Repair craftsmen, identified within the communities, will benefit from training and recieve equipment for the maintenance of the works. Hygiene kits and water treatment tabs will be distributed within the facilities, and to the most vulnerable households in their vicinity. Health structures will also be equipped with sanitation kits.
The activities will also include communities' sensitization: WASH awareness-rasing sessions, including door-to-door sensitization, will be conducted to ensure wide sensitization, complementing nutrition education and information dissemintaed through our existing networks of Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs), and Mother Care Groups (MCGs).
The targeted beneficiaries will be IDPs and the people of the host community, with a particular focus on children, women, the elderly and persons with disabilities. Water access works will be conducted in the communities, targeting IDPs and vulnerable people among the host community. Works carried out in the health structures will benefit the population attending the facilities, as well as health workers.
Sanitation activities will benefit displaced people as well as vulnerable people in the host communities, and the health structures. Targeted beneficiaries, and the communities, will be consulted throughout the project regarding the different activities, their outcomes, and achievements.
All the beneficiaries targeted by the water and sanitation works will benefit from sensitization and hygiene promotion, while the most vulnerable (children under 5 years old, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, people with disabilities) will be provided with hygiene kits.

1 - Needs

According to the last Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 3 million people in the three BAY states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe did not have in 2022 an adequate access to clean water, and were suffering from poor sanitation and hygiene practices.According to a SMART Survey Premiere Urgence Internationale conducted in August-September 2022, in Maiduguri Bolori II, hand-pump/boreholes are the principal drinking water source for 32.9% of the households; in Monguno and Pulka, public-tap standpipe are the principal drinking water source for respectively 37.2% and 13.7% of the households. The survey also showed, however, that a majority of the households in Pulka -- 39.6% -- resorted to combination of other water sources such as water sellers / kiosks, rain water collection, unprotected hand-dug well. These numbers clearily show that less than 50% of the population have access to the principal source of drinking water in the various locations. In 2022, a WASH assessment conducted by PUI in two health facilities in Pulka showed a very low rate of access to water, hygiene and sanitation – respectively 24% and 15%. This situation is not new: a report from MSF dating back to March 2021 showed that poor water quality in Pulka was also a source of disease. PUI SMART survey also showed that about 45% of households in Maiduguri Bolori II and in Monguno, and 57% of the households in Pulka, did not doing anything to their drinking water to make it safer to drink. Regaring latrines, PUI survey showed that about 95.2% of households in Bolori II and 78.3% in Pulka use household latrine for defecation. In Monguno, however, 51% of the households use communal latrine.There is a need to scale-up / initiate water, sanitation and hygiene access in Maiduguri's Bolori II, Monguno and Pulka. All the more than the three LGAs of Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC), Monguno and Gowza, where Pulka is located, are respectively the second, third, and fourth LGAs in the Northeast with the highest numbers of IDPs, according to IOM IDP and Returnee Atlas of July 2022, with 240,580 IDPs in MMC, 160,239 in Monguno and 159,584 in Gwoza. For most IDPs, access to safe water, or clean latrines remain particularly challenging. Their presence also puts pressure on insufficient existing facilities for host communities. In Monguno, IDPs', and host communities' vulnerabilities, were even further exacerbated when devastating floods due to above-average rainfall damaged or destroyed many shelters, leading in secondary displacements and to the presence of dirty waters.Poor access to water, health and sanitation services are an underlying cause of malnutrition, and worsen it, and increase morbidity risks, increasing the occurrence of water-borne diseases. In September 2022, a cholera outbreak was declared in Borno State, affecting Maiduguri MC, and most sepcifically Gwoza LGA, with 252 cases recorded -- the LGA had already been affected by an outbreak in 2021. There is, across Borno State in general, and in the hard-to-reach areas, like Monguno and Pulka, a high prevalence of malnutrition. Monguno and Gwoza LGAs are currently in IPC AMN Phase 4 or "critical" phase (IPC, November 2022). The SMART Survey PUI recently conducted indicated Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 24.4% and 12.9% in Monguno and in Pulka, and 14.4% in Maiduguri Bolori II, and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rates respectively were 10%, 2.9%, and 3.8%. These rates are above WHO thresholds and very concerning.The overall prevalence of acute malnutrition is generally highest among young children (6-23 months)compared to older age groups (24-59 months); in Borno’s hard-to-reach areas for instance, GAM ratesin September 2022 among young children ranged between 22.4% and 25.7%, whereas the range was14.3% to 17% among older age groups, as highlighted in the Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM)Bulletin of September 2022.

2 - Activities

The activities planned in the proposed intervention include: -Rehabilitation/construction of 19 Boreholes with human motor pumps, in the health facilities PUI already supports and in the communities, with 7 being rehabilitated/constructed in Pulka, 7 in Monguno, 5 in Maiduguri;-rehabilitation/construction of 4 solar pump water supply systems in the communities, including non-developed areas comprising the PDI (In Pulka and Monguno)-strengthening the beneficiaries' capacity regarding the drinking water supply infrastructure management, with 10 repair craftsmen in Pulka, 10 in Monguno, and 5 in Maiduguri;-setting up 23 water point management committees, one for each infrastructure built/ rehabilitated , 9 in Pulka, 9 in Monguno, 5 in Maiduguri, and training their members, ensuring women are equally represented in Pulka, Monguno and Maiduguri. -construction of 100 family latrines and showers -- 20 in Pulka, 60 in Monguno, 20 in Maiduguri -- for the most vulnerable households in the community and in areas hosting displaced populations - construction of 1500 family latrines using Community-led total sanitation (CLTS) in the three locations. -the construction of 20 latrines at the supported health facilities -- 8 in Pulka, 9 in Monguno and 2 in Maiduguri) in the targeted areas, with separate latrines for women, and ensuring people with reduced mobility have the necessary access, with adequate equipment such as ramps for instance if necessary;-Conduct hygiene promotion and community mobilization activities with women, men, girls and boys to contribute to behavioral change. Two KAP surveys on critical hygiene practices in communities on Pulka, Monguno and Maiduguri (Bolori II)- Distribute hygiene kits to 2000 households (IDPs and host community) including 750 in Pulka, 1000 in Monguno, and 250 in Maiduguri, Bolori II-Training health workers (35) and community-based health workers (70) on Infection Control Prevention and provide them with PPE kit

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Premiere Urgence Internationale works with a holistic approach, guaranteeing the centrality of protection and ensuring cross-cutting protection and gender issues are integrated across all sectors at all stages of implementation and monitoring. PUI also strives for the safety and dignity of people and to minimize unintended adverse effects of its interventions, with all of its programme having the minimum requirement to apply the “do-no-harm” approach. PUI hence ensures accountability to beneficiaries, guarantees equal access, and consider gender needs. Communities and direct beneficiaries will be involved throughout the whole project, from the design phase, throughout implementation and at the end of the project, with community-based and beneficiaries-based dialogues, with Focus Group Discussion, Key Informant Interviews, conducted individually and at the household level, and with regular Community Committee Meetings, involving key community leaders and representatives, among whom women and youth leaders and representatives, as well as religious leaders. Premiere Urgence Internationale will this way ensure women and girls, but also people with disabilities are part of the decision-making process, and give an enlightened advice on the modalities of the project.PUI will moreover ensure regular analysis of the local protection environment to ensure that the designing / building and use of water and sanitation equipment or any facilities / device does not put beneficiaries, especially women and girls, and people with disabilities, at greater risks of violence, abduction, or abuse. Latrines, but also showers, will be gender-sensitive, separated for women and boys, and equipped as needed or required for people with disabilities or reduced mobility to be able to use them.Premiere Urgence Internationale MEAL department has accessible and well-understood mechanisms for suggestions and complaints (see below) in the project it implements, in coordination with the protection coordinator and Human resources department to ensure that all complaints mechanisms are in line with PUI policies and Protection cluster recommendation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Premiere Urgence Commitment is to make sure that communities, beneficiaries, and among them the most vulnerable members, can provide feedback, make suggestions or express, file complaints, if required. PUI has developed a community-based feedback system that consists in multiple entry points such as the helpline, the mobile helpdesk, community conlunteers' networks, and monthly Community Committee Meetings. These are bilateral meetings with beneficiary representatives. PUI also regularly conducts key informant interviews (KII) and FGDs to understand the evolving context of its intervention areas as part of the monitoring strategy. Besides, PUI works alongside community leaders and representatives to gather inputs that influence the program design and implementation, the targeting methodology, the gaps, and unaddressed needs analysis.These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiary feedback to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys and men. Accountability teams are made of both male and female members who speak the most popular language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to easily express their concerns. Furthermore, the helpline is free of charge for the beneficiaries and functional at any time during the working hours/days. To ensure a consistent feedback management process across the intervention areas, SOPs emanating from the organizational accountability field guide are in place to track and respond to complaints and feedback. The SOPs describe steps for managing both non-sensitive and sensitive cases (e.g, fraud, corruption, PSEA, child protection). A well-trained accountability team is responsible for collecting, documenting, and categorizing the complaints and feedback from the different channels in a dedicated secured database.
