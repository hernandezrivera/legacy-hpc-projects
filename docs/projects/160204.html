Project Summary

Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South and the greater Aweil counties are some of the counties in south Sudan with limited access to S/NFI materials both from the surrounding markets and humanitarian support. This is associated to the major shocks faced by the populations, leading to constant displacement, destruction of shelters and erosion of their livelihood opportunities. According to the south Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020, returnees, IDPs and a percentage of the host communities will require shelter and NFI support, to help them rebuild their lives and return to normalcy. MaCDA proposes to respond to the S/NFI gaps by increasingly employing creative, more community-appropriate solutions to build on coping mechanisms and increase resilience, such as self-supported solutions for shelter reconstructions.
The target beneficiaries will be the most vulnerable households, desegregated by gender and age.

1 - Needs

Based on the South Sudan HNO 2020, more than 2 million individuals in South Sudan require S/NFI assistance. Returnees face the challenge of accessing naturally grown shelters, thus necessitating regular provision of shelter materials and non-food items. On the other hand, IDPs' access to S/NFIs is hindered by absence and non-functionality of most markets. A percentage of the host communities have remained vulnerable as they have to share the already strained resources with the returnees and the IDPsThe population in Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East and the greater Aweil counties have had increased shelter needs arising from numerous shocks including the normalized cattle raids, persistent floods caused by torrential rains and spontaneous returns. Further, IDPs from other counties will need S/NFI support as they leave their household necessities behind while fleeing from their homes. Multiple displacements, political and economic instability have continued to aggravate the living conditions of the populations due to the continuous depletion of assets and erosion of livelihoods. Additionally, due to the current laws and policies, returnees and IDPs have no guarantee of land tenure, which may pose threats of tensions among the communities and exacerbate conflict. Constant ranging floods and communal disputes have resulted to significant displacement of people, destruction of houses, deaths and disruption of livelihoods and road networks, consequently undermining population’s stability in a multi‐causal manner, with detrimental impacts such as of disruption of livelihood sources. This has rendered many roads impassable, constraining people’s access to markets and humanitarians’ ability to move across affected areas, damaged essential service infrastructure like schools and health facilities, which would have acted as temporally shelters to safeguard the affected populations. Thousands of families are temporarily relocated from worst affected to safer areas but without shelter materials or basic household items. This leaves a significant proportion of the population prone to protection risks if humanitarian assistance is not offered. Some populations have never received any S/NFI assistance since the break out of war in 2016. This has contributed to a big carryover of unmet gaps in assistance especially with only few shelter/NFI partners to respond to the high rocketing needs

2 - Activities

MaCDA proposes to address these gaps through coordinated efforts with the communities, local leadership and other partners on the ground to ensure that the most vulnerable are reached with the most essential services. 1. In depth needs analysis- assessments will be carried out in the target locations to verify the specific needs of the target communities. This will be done in coordination with the specific clusters, other partners and the state focal points. MaCDA will participate in joint assessments and PDMs that enhance identification of gaps 2. Community engagement and consultations: Consultations with the communities will be done in the first phase of the project implementation. The deliberations of the consultations will guide on project design and have a huge impact on targeting criteria. In addition, this will be done through out the project to ensure that the services offered by MaCDA are in line with the humanitarian principles, help in understanding the most feasible response modality as well as determine the most preferred mode of communication with the beneficiaries3. Targeting of beneficiaries: MaCDA will use the standardized cluster tools to carry out registration and verification of beneficiaries. Community based protection committees will be formed in each location to help in the process and ensure that only the most vulnerable households are targeted. MaCDA will dedicate gender balanced staffs who well understand the cluster methodologies and strategies. The staffs will be required to stay in the field locations for periods long enough to allow them engage with the communities in a way that improves their resilience, livelihoods, understanding of pressing issues areas like Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Housing Land Property. Beneficiaries will be informed of the type and modality of response including the quantity of items they should expect.4. Setting up of complaints and feedback desks: MaCDA will ensure to set up feedback desks in all distribution and training centers, and ensure that the CFM is accessible and responsive. The aim of this is for MaCDA to track communities' perception of assistance. Feedback received will be kept confidential and will be adapted to enhance programming. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to record their feedback on the project implementation. 5. Distribution of shelter and non food items: This activity will be accompanied by intensive awareness creation and demonstrations on usage of the NFIs and disposal of wrapping materials. MaCDA will coordinate with the logistics cluster and pipeline to ensure timely acquisition and delivery of items to the different areas of operation.6. Training on shelter construction: Training of trainers will be done immediately after distribution. The shelters models will be community based. The ToTs will later train the rest of the community members with the supervision of MaCDA project team. MaCDA will also work within the HLP working group framework to mitigate risks of conflict associated with land tenure to enhance project implementation. 7. Post distribution monitoring: This activity will be carried out between two weeks and two months after distribution of S/NFI materials. The monitoring and evaluation strategy will measure if the beneficiaries felt well-informed and respected during project delivery, overall satisfaction of the beneficiaries with the project and test if the feedback from beneficiaries shaped project delivery. It will also test how well the project tracked and responded to beneficiaries' complaints

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

MaCDA recognizes and will respect displaced peoples’ right to freedom of movement and freedom to choose their own residence, and refrain from pressuring the IDPs to settle in specific areas for reasons associated with political and/or ethnic power dynamics. MaCDA will therefore undertake to gain the best possible understanding of the dynamics and contextual factors to assess risks and use this to inform actions for sustainable returns and conduct interventions. This is to ensure that movements are voluntary, based on informed decisions and are safe and dignified; that interventions do no harm and are conflict sensitive; maintain a rights-based approach and prioritize accountability to affected people MaCDA will take part in planning of responses, determining approach on basis of need and endeavor to integrate new arrivals into existing services and processes.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

At the incision of the project, MaCDA will carry out an in-depth assessment to identify the internally displaced populations in Kapoeta East and South and counties of the greater Aweil. MaCDA will initiate a dialogues with the community leaders and the local government on allowing the IDPs settle in the respective locations, until, they either feel comfortable going back to their areas of origin, or plans to relocate them to another area are put in place. Shelter materials will be provided based on need. MaCDA will ensure equitable access to commodities by all targeted individuals and ensure orderly distributions that do not place vulnerable people at risk of physical injury. In addition, timely information to the communities will be paramount and will be done in a way that can be understood by illiterate and literate people on distributions especially about what commodities will be distributed. Potential conflicts between recipients and non-recipients will be managed through encouraging community dialogues and need oriented targeting. Beneficiaries will be encouraged not to send children to receive items at time of distribution. MaCDA will provide staff/volunteers to assist those with special needs to carry distributed items and take measures to reduce risk of them being robbed. Include measures to be put in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking nutrition services.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

MaCDA will work closely with the community networks and the local leadership in all the locations.Shelter construction and making of reusable sanitary towels will involve training of trainers first, who will in turn train the rest of the beneficiaries, with supervision from MaCDA project team.Local government and leaders will be engaged throughout the implementation period to ensure ownership, up to date information in regard to the security situations and proper handing over of mandate after the project cycle. Materials and tools used for training the women and girls on making the reusable sanitary towels will be handed over to the government offices to ensure that the participants continue the exercise even after the project elapses. Participants who will be well equipped with the skills will be empowered by connecting them to markets and other partners who can work with them as ToTs. MaCDA will engage other partners in the entire process. Protection partners will be actively sought out to ensure that protection issues are identified and responded to before, during and after the project period.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project targets the most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and the affected non-displaced host communities. MaCDA will enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills. This will be achieved by ensuring community participation in the project planning, design and implementation. Each distribution and training site will be equipped with community feedback and complaints desks and participants and beneficiaries will be encouraged to record their feedback and complaints. Clear emphasis will be put on improving the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and affected non- displaced host communities who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs. These vulnerabilities will form the basis for targeting. Better settlement options and shelter designs will be explored during community consultations. Further, the type and quantity of non food items will be based on the identified needs. Certain proportion of the population will be supported on replacing their damaged S/NFI materials and upgrading their shelters.Training of women on making the sanitary towels and distribution of the same will be a way of preventing and mitigating protection risks among the community. These measures will enhance the preparedness and resilience of the target population in regards to menstrual hygiene management. Woman and girls will comfortably afford and manage their menstruation even without humanitarian assistance.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Kapoeta and the greater Aweil are relatively calm areas which allows implementation of more durable interventions. Formation of community based protection committees comprising of all community stakeholders (local leadership, legal departments, community group representatives, humanitarian partners from different sectors) will help the community to own up the project, hence long term community oriented solutions. To ensure sustainability, shelter materials will not just be provided. Rather, MaCDA will undertake training of beneficiaries on shelter construction techniques, with ethical considerations on the culturally acceptable models. The disadvantaged households will be supported by the more able individuals. Local materials such as poles, ropes and grass will be sought out where possible to enhance self reliance and sufficiency. This will ensure that, the affected populations can manage construction of their own shelters even in case of displacement, relocation or returns to their areas of origin. Returnees will be enlightened on their legal tenure rights.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The sustainability of this project will be enhanced through integration with other sectors and partners to increase more long term and robust responses. MaCDA's nutrition department in Kapoeta South has established several nutrition centers and successful formation of mother support groups . The project activities will be linked with the nutrition activities to help in building on nutrition sensitive cooking strategies, with the aim of contributing towards a decrease in levels of malnutrition among children under five and pregnant and lactating mothers. In addition, the centers and the mother support groups will provide a strong base of communications and trained community groups, all of which S/NFI partners can utilize for AAP activities, such as messaging about malaria, menstrual hygiene management and skills training. Engagement with the government will be prioritized especially in the initial stages of the project implementation to ensure security of tenure for returnees.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Members of marginalized communities are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance- MaCDA will ensure that consultations are widely done to cover a wider range of opinions. Gender balanced staff will be dedicated to reach into the lowest administration levels to ensure that all the program implementation incorporates inputs from majority of the community. Targeting for services will be done based on the need and not by race, color or political and ethnic affiliationsLack of access to referral services for survivors of protection incidents- MaCDA will work in coordination with other partners and stakeholders to ensure strengthening or establishment of referral pathways. Staffs involved will be encouraged to be on guard to check out any protection issues that may arise and report them immediately Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas- A big percentage of land in South Sudan is not registered. MaCDA will ensure proper consultation with the government, local leaders and host communities to ensure no land tenure issues will arise during and after the project period Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services- The distribution sites will be determined after consultations with the target communities. Unless the community advises otherwise, the sites will be within a close proximity to the community current settlements. This is to mitigate protection risk associated with long distances the target beneficiaries have to walk. Both recipients and non recipients of this assistance will be sensitized on peaceful coexistence regardless of inability to have a blanket coverage. This is to ensure that recipients do not become a target of violence from the non recipients.
