Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen and scale up lifesaving protection services in the targeted location to prevent and respond to protection risks/concerns experienced by women, men and children especially in Internally Displaced Peoples camps but also in the host populations. The key focus will be sustaining and scaling up prevention and response to separation of children from their families and gender based violence survivors. The project will conduct regular analysis of the situation of children regarding causes and patterns of separation and respond accordingly through prevention and strengthening of appropriate protection systems and services, build capacity of protection service providers and stakeholders. This project also strengthens service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals and coordination and linkage in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) children at risk of being recruited by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG) and gender based violence (GBV) survivors.

Needs

The recent droughts coupled with intermittent conflicts in the targeted regions has exacerbated the protection concerns of the most vulnerable sections of the community. Displacement and family breakdown means children are at high risk of separation which puts them at risk of trafficking, Child labor, sexual exploitation and recruitment in armed groups. There is a crucial need to scale up and sustain the provision of comprehensive case management, psychosocial support, interim care and tracing services for these children. Reports of grave child rights violations remain a major challenge tooAccording to the preliminary reports of the recent assessment report show that there are significant barriers for survivors seeking support that needs to be improved: 1) Limited awareness of the consequences of Gender-Based Violence and Child Protection and available services; 2) Fear of retaliation by perpetrators; 3) Blaming of survivors and lack of confidentiality in the community and service providers. Furthermore, there are low levels of knowledge among humanitarian workers to respond and mitigate. Gaps to address are: 1) strengthen community engagement and awareness of GBV and services available, and promote community-based mechanisms for; 2) improve the quality of response services, particularly medical care, Psychosocial support, case management and safe spaces; 3) Engage with formal and informal security and justice actors on human rights and protection of survivors and support in referring cases; 4) strengthen the knowledge and practice of humanitarian actors on CP and GBV. How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

INTERSOS views the proposed intervention as an entry door for longer term humanitarian implementation in such acritical areas that needs urgent and constant humanitarian assistance. Somalia is an unstable volatile political and humanitarian context, and the ability of humanitarian organizations to foresee the transition of funding and responsibility to local mechanisms is limited. Across the regions, the gap in the basic humanitarian’s services remains large due to prolonged conflict and a very weak central government overseeing what was intended to be a highly centralized human care system. Due to this, it is clear that the need for external humanitarian support to these areas will remain high. The Somali Federal Government established the Ministerial Emergency Response Committee, describing the unfolding situation, “as a precursor to the situation in 2011 in terms of intensity.” INTERSOS Somalia has been responding proactively to the unfolding crisis through its traditional emergency programming, concerted efforts to raise awareness with traditional government donors, SOs and multi-lateral donors, and targeted early actions in its livelihood activities communities to protect resilience gains and avert emergency. For this project, INTERSOS will transfer responsibilities to the established local authorities/community members. In the long term, INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger; its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility for the delivery of humanitarian services. To further enhance the sustainability and the ownership of the project it will include activities and support to ensure sustainable protection outcomes beyond the period of protection interventions (such as referrals, awareness sessions and enrollment in activities to support income generation based on the activities prioritized by the community). This will be achieved through coordination with other agencies and partners, engagement of affected population and local authorities as well as development an exit strategy together with the beneficiaries which will rely on structures already existing at community level.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Some of the major protection risks facing the targeted population includes incidents of Gender-based violence (GBV), especially in IDP sites. Abuses against civilians, including widespread sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), child recruitment, child separation and limitations on the freedom of movement, social exclusion of marginalized communities, forced evictions and secondary displacement remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in targeted locations.INTERSOS will ensure meaningful representation of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In order to cater for the specific needs of person of different age groups, INTERSOS will collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming.INTERSOS will identify groups who face difficulties accessing services (e.g. women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, persons from minority groups, persons belonging to marginalized groups) and work with community leaders, religious leaders and service providers to support equal and safe access to services without discrimination. INTERSOS will identify the power dynamics in the area by focusing on who has access to service, who manages the facilities and who is vulnerable and marginalised.INTERSOS will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritising the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Take this into consideration when determining locations for interventions. Furthermore, INTERSOS field staffs will undertake training's on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection coordinator.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to protection needs specifically by consulting women, men, boys, girls, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.Promote and support self-protection capacities and avoid persons resorting to negative coping strategies to mitigate risks and reinforce the capacity of the community and family to act as a protective layer for children against violations. INTERSOS will set up accessible, confidential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct eg. Feedback box in centres. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the project weakness thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services.
