Project Summary

According to the recent South Sudan IPC analysis 18, the acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.
Between September and November 2024, 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) were classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people faced critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people faced catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicated an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above.
The first projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) is likely to experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 4.33 million people (32 percent of the population analysed) are likely to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), 1.71 million people (13 percent of the population analysed) will likely face IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees (0.2 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Of which 17 counties and estimated 220,000 returnees (35 percent) returnees will likely be in Phase 4, 52 counties who will likely be in Phase 3, and 10 counties who will likely be in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed)
Basing on the same IPC analysis 18 of 2024, Community development Aid would wish to intervene in the warrying food insecurity in the country by reaching out the food insecure households with crop kits and training to raise their capacities to produce their own food sustainably. The planned project will provide to the tune of 120,000 beneficiaries with crop kits and vegetable kits and tools as well as trainings to the beneficiaries with knowledge in kitchen gardening and farming, and saving skills. This will uplift the capacity of beneficiaries to cultivate their own food.
The project will target food insecure households of the host communities in Akobo and Uror, men and women with land to cultivate. The purpose of this project is to empower the household to produce their own food and reduce on the dependence from humanitarian assistance by 2025 and beyond, hence the need for CODAID to request funds to reduce this food gaps in the two counties.

1. Needs

According to the South Sudan recent IPC 2024-2025, the acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.It is projected that from December 2024 to March 2025, 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed in the recent IPC) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 4.33 million people (32 percent of the population analysed) are are likely to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis), 1.71 million people (13 percent of the population analysed) will likely face IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees (0.2 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).According to the IPC AMN (October 2024 to March 2025), a total of 38 counties are classified in IPC Phase 4, 15 counties are in Phase 3, 19 counties are in Phase 2, and 8 counties are in Phase 1. Compared to the current period (July to September 2024), the acute malnutrition situation is likely to remain similar in 43 counties, improve in 24 counties and deteriorate in 13 counties during the first projection period. However, the IPC AMN Classification Phases remains similar in 76 out of the 80 counties. Among the counties classified in Phase 4, 24 of the 38 counties are located in Greater Upper Nile region i.e., Jonglei State that has 10 (Akobo, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor, Pochalla, Twic East, and Uror), in Upper Nile State (Akoka, Baliet, Fashoda, Malakal, Manyo, Melut, and Renk), and in Unity State (Guit, Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Panyijiar, and Rubkona. According to the lean season (April - June 2025), seasonal deterioration in acute malnutrition is expected. Compared to the current period, the situation is projected to deteriorate in 62 out of 80 counties and remain similar in 16 counties. The deterioration in the situation is expected to change in 13 counties.The recent South Sudan IPC analysis18 projects that from December 2024 – March 2025, an estimated 1.08 million people (51.3 percent of the state’s population) are likely to face Phase 3 or above, of which 349,000 people are likely to be in Phase 4 and 727,000 people are likely to be in Phase 3. A total of six counties (Akobo, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Pochalla, and Twic East) will likely be in Phase 4, four (Akobo, Ayod, Duk, and Pochalla) will likely be in Phase 3, and one (Bor South) will likely be in Phase 2. It is also projected that from April – July 2025 (Projection 2): In the lean season, an estimated 1.31 million people (62.4 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Phase 3 or above, of which 22,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), 511,000 people will likely be in Phase 4, and 775,000 people will likely be in Phase 3. The 22,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) are in Pibor County of Pibor Administrative Area and Uror County. The food security situation is expected to seasonally worsen due to depleted household food stocks and increased market dependency amid high food prices. The likelihood of rising civil insecurity will further restrict access to markets and livelihood activities, compounded by road inaccessibility as the rainy season begins. This growing market dependency will be exacerbated by a deteriorating economic situation, potentially leading to a depreciation of the local currency. Households are likely to rely on wild foods, fish, and milk, especially as livestock return closer to homesteads. Hence the need for intervention

2. Project activities

The project will have the following activities that will be undertaken during the project implementation period: 1. Conduct an inception meeting to inform key stakeholders of the project scope and process, including the FSL, cross cutting related activities, i.e GBV, protection, and the basis for targeting. The meeting will also be an opportunity to make the community own the project and receive feedback from key stakeholders and develop an action plan. Verification of the register beneficiaries to confirm that the vulnerable group were target based on the plan. Conduct community engagement session to ensure the targeted communities understand the roles of the project management committees (PMCs), and the importance of having women driving the community development initiatives. This will ensure acceptance from local leadership; the project management committees will be informed to liaise very closely with and update local leadership often on the project’s progress. 2. Distribution of Food Assistance. CODAID will distribute food assistance to the vulnerable people in Akobo and Urol counties to 30,000 beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be provided with nutritious food kits including sorghum, lentos or beans, cooking oil and salt.3.Distribution of improved seeds, vegetable kits and tools with the resolved of increasing local food production that will assist households to cut back on food assistance. Establishment CLOSE nutrition kitchen gardening at nutrition and health center's for growing of nutritious vegetables and training of caregivers. Training of vegetable (seeds) kits recipients on best gardening practices including climate smart techniques, and marketing. Households that qualify for receiving the vegetable kits will be trained on best gardening practices at the demonstration/kitchen garden sites. These sites will be negotiated with the facilities (OTP/Health). All demonstration/kitchen garden sites will be owned by a household or two for sustainability as CODAID will not continuously support any institutional learning sites. All produce harvested from the learning/demonstration sites will remain the property of the irrespective owners for consumption and income generation. For ease of travel, CODAID will conduct these trainings for cluster of beneficiaries closer to the site nearest to them. 4. Hence, CODAID will encourage/support many community members to establish gardens in their areas that could potentially be used as learning sites for surrounding beneficiaries 5. Train the selected beneficiaries on good farmer practices and food management As cross cutting issues Gender and protection related activities will be also implemented in the community through an integrated approaches that seek to address the priority needs of vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and promote actions which help reduce violence including GBV and its impact on communities.6. The project will update the accountability to affected people (AAP)framework/ work plan at planning stage. This will ensure all activities and interventions related to protection and genders are captured as part of core activities. 7. CODAID staffs will be responsible for establishing daily routines, and site monitoring to ensure that activities are on schedule.
