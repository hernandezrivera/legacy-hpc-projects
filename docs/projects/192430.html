Project Summary

Through this project, Caritas Lebanon’s goal is to improve access to quality protection and specialized GBV services to vulnerable women and girls, survivors and/or individuals at risk of GBV in Lebanon.
Women and girls' vulnerability to GBV, particularly sexual violence, is strongly ingrained in a culture where they have relatively limited access to authority and resources compared to men and boys. GBV, on the other hand, is not only a grave violation of human rights, but provides physical and psychological harm limit women’s and also men’s ability to participate fully in economic, social and political processes that results in decreased productivity and reduced family income. Some of the barriers to obtaining treatment include stigma, humiliation, fear of rejection, a lack of information, geographical distance from the center, and a lack of time and/or money. While numerous response elements have been recommended, the most important appear to be ensuring safety, confidentiality, and spreading knowledge of help availability. CL strives to provide safe access to competent and suitable services, as well as access to justice, in order to respond to GBV acts and threats.
The intervention will include different protection activities aligned with the strategic objectives of the sector and target vulnerable individuals such as victims of rape, SGBV, human trafficking, minors, abuse, exploitation, physical or moral abuse, violation of freedom of movement, disabilities, pregnancies, elderlies. The most vulnerable individuals will be referred to safe shelters, while the remainder beneficiaries who are not in immediate danger will be served through the social center.
Aligned with objective 1 of the sector, through a specialized team, CL will guarantee that survivors, at-risk women, and girls receive protection services. Medium and high-risk individuals will collaborate with the team to develop individual action plans, while high-risk cases will be referred to the safe shelter for additional assistance.
Caritas Lebanon adopts the Journey of Care Model which combines immediate protection and emergency care with an intentional focus on preparing residents to safely leave the shelter and successfully integrate into the community. Upon leaving the shelter, residents will receive transitional cash support to meet essential needs as they establish linkages to other services provided by CL. Survivors who choose to return to their home countries will be assisted by Caritas Lebanon and the International Organization for Migration (IOM). To make the move back to their native country easier, they will be given cash support and travel supplies.
The intervention will aim to target 130 women and girls’ survivors and/or individuals at risk of GBV in Lebanon with a GBV protection package; out of whom 100 will receive awareness raising sessions on GBV, 35 will receive case management, legal support, PSS and 3-month protection cash assistance, while 30 high risk cases will reside at the safe shelter. At the end of the intervention, Caritas aims to measure an increase of the % of survivors receiving GBV services reported feeling safe and the % of residents receiving shelter care reported improved access to good quality of service.
The proposed project has been designed based on input and monitoring of activities with GBV survivors currently residing at the shelters. CL conducts participatory assessments with residents to plan shelter activities. The case management process and action planning will ensure program participants are empowered to define their goals within the project and decide which activities meet their needs.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under Obj 1. Act. 1.1 – Safe shelters have expanded case management: CL will increase the capacity and quality of services based on the Interagency GBV Case Management Guidelines – the team will include 1 GBV case worker, 1 case management supervisor, 1 lawyer and 1 psychologist. Act 1.2 – Provision of case management – After a vulnerability assessment and the risk rating form, An individual case management action plan will be developed by survivors and their case worker for medium and high risk cases, services will include protection cash assistance for 3 months and legal assistance. Act 1.3 - Quality, essential services are available at the shelters: Extreme high-risk cases will be referred to CL Safe shelters for temporary stay of 4-6 months, where they will receive quality, essential services, including security in the safe shelter.Act 1.4 – Case workers accompany women and girls in their transition to independent living: once durable solutions are identified, case workers will begin forming an exit strategy for residents. This strategy will ensure that their concerns have been addressed. Upon closing the case, the survivor will receive a post shelter cash assistance to support her transition. If the survivor wishes to return to her home country, she will be referred internally to start the repatriation process, in coordination with the relevant UN Agency.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CL acknowledges the importance of communicating with affected communities, coordinating with other humanitarian actors, and having a robust referral mechanism in place to facilitate access to services that Caritas does not provide. Using the 4-Agency Protection Mainstreaming approach, Caritas identifies key protection mainstreaming needs and gaps and prioritizes actions on this basis. These include a regular gap analysis, and a robust complaints mechanism with standard operating procedures to deal with both sensitive and non-sensitive complaints. Caritas Lebanon adopts its Protection Strategy to guide all protection programming - including GBV and protection activities. Additional steps taken to prevent and respond to incidents of GBV include: ensuring confidential locations fortified and security guards to reduce the risk of access to the shelter and emergency protocol to respond to incidents of GBV in shelter SOPs and ensure all staff are trained on it

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability of affected populations is ensured throughout all phases of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project design is based on continuous assessments at field level that analyze the changing needs of affected populations and the relevance of ongoing and potential activities. During implementation, CL team ensures that beneficiaries have the tools to be able to express their concerns and suggestions for improvement. CL has established comment boxes in its Centres for beneficiaries to provide feedback - positive, negative, and suggestive - on activities and services provided. Additionally, the Centre Manager is responsible for developing and encouraging a culture of accountability and feedback in the shelters through participatory approaches
