Project Summary

Crisis-affected population including school age girls and boys, women and girls in the Far North Region of Cameroon have their most urgent protection needs met and benefit from community-based protection through the following outcomes and associated activities.
Outcome 1: Protection of the affected population is strengthened through the provision of legal
support by community paralegal women.
Outcome 2: Women and girls are protected from and treated for the consequences of GBV.
Outcome 3: School-age girls and boys receive support and access to protection services to reduce
their exposure to risk and improve their safety and access to education.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The context in the Far North Cameroon remains extremely volatile due to significant unmet needs, making it difficult for humanitarian actors to predict its future evolution. Several factors indicate a prolonged and complex situation that combines the continuation of the ongoing Lake Chad Basin crisis, which has direct effects on the Far North region of Cameroon, with insecurity and attacks of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and the increasing impacts of climate shocks on the most vulnerable. Displacement trends over the past few years remained high, while the severity of needs continues to be classified as significant. Therefore, the IRC does not anticipate being able to transition completely from humanitarian assistance at the end of this project. Nevertheless, the proposed project will allow the IRC not only to address immediate, lifesaving needs, but also to build a more sustainable intervention to strengthen social cohesion, community-based structures, and populations’ resilience. The number of displaced people in Cameroon continues to rise as violence in the Far North region has led to a steady increase in displacement since 2014. As of 2023, the violence has uprooted a total of 700766 IDPs, returnees and refugees, an increase of 38 766 since October 2022 . Conflict between security forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and NSAG attacks accounted for 70% of displacement between March and August 2022 (. Since then, inter-communal conflict has become an increasingly important reason for displacement. Approximately 46% of new displacements recorded between January and February 2022 were related to the inter-communal conflict that broke out in Logone Birni in December 2021 . Although there was no further escalation in 2023, the inter-communal conflict remained responsible for 26% of the displacement recorded between March and August 2022 (IOM DTM October 2022). From January to December 2022, a total of 927 security incidents were reported in Cameroon’s Far North region by INSO; that is, an average of 77.25 incidents per month. In 2023, insecurity is expected to remain high in the Far North region and will continue to cause population displacement, exacerbating limited access to food and basic services. Tension between communities and the host populations and displaced persons are likely to continue. Also, the crisis’ protracted nature has led to a steady decline in living standards; further exacerbating the impact of natural disasters, especially flooding and the conflict over natural resources.As a result, it is estimated that over one million people in the Far North region are in need of urgent assistance ). The already precarious situation of education and protection services in the Lake Chad basin has been exacerbated by years of conflict, displacement, and direct targeting of schools. land and property issues (access to land and housing, secondary occupation, etc.), and 30 cases on average seek for legal assistance on issues related to gender-based violence (GBV), such as forced marriages, rape and other sexual violence.115 persons were referred from Protection and Information Centers to legal services during the last quarter of 2022. According to the key findings from the evaluation conducted by the Protection Cluster from October 20th to November 11th, 2023, 52% of people had lost their civil documents due to the floods that affected the departments of Mayo Dani and Logone et Chari, ; 40% had never had a civil document; and 8% claimed to have lost them during inter-community conflicts. In addition, the 2023 statistics from BUNEC in terms of civil documentation in different project locations show important facts: In Kolofata division: 25,763 infants from 0 – 59 months, 8,687 adolescents from 10 –14 years female and 8,170 adolescent males are exposed to protection risks due to the lack of civil documentation. The lack of civil documents exposes clients to several protection risks.
