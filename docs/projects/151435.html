Project Summary

This project aim is to meet the short to medium term shelter needs of conflict affected boys, girls, women, men and elderly through appropriate interventions designed together with communities (winterization and shelter cash assistance & NFIs)

Needs

Lifesaving assistanceThe HNO 2018 highlighted seasonal lifesaving assistance (access to warm clothes, bedding, heaters/fuel, etc) to be a priority especially in contact line areas which continue to experience disruption to markets and facilities particularly for children, women and elderly. More women headed HHs in GCA Donetsk and Luhansk require winterization (22% of IDPs, and 23% of ND vs 17% of men headed HHs) particularly on winter clothing. According to REACH, significant issues with access to fuel for winter heating have been reported especially in rural areas.Availability and accessibility of hygiene items for women and infants also remains an issue for families in insecure areas and those renting accommodation who struggle to afford these items in addition to food, rent and utility payments. Addressing sub standard and over crowded accommodation is of particular concern for children, elderly, persons with disability or chronic illness, and for women at risk of sexual violence. Rent has been reported as a significant expenditure of HH in all assessments. The HNO 2018 reported more than 80% of IDPs living in private housing (3/4 are paying rents). As a last resort, people still live in collective centers mostly near the contact line or in large urban centers due to inability to pay high rents. SCI’s MSNA indicated protection concerns related to the lack of privacy for women and girls in these centers. Families with children and pensioners (in rented accommodation and collective centers) are at risk of eviction or having utilities cut off due limited finances. SCI’s IYCF assessment also showed infants living in collective centres were significantly less likely to achieve minimum dietary diversity. Gender analysis showed that more men have self owned shelter than women while more women live in collective centres than men. Comparing the number of rooms of the shelters it is visible that female headed HHs have more often less rooms and rent less expensive shelter, and taking into account the conclusion that such HHs have more children and elderly, they have worse shelter conditions, because they have lower income.

Activities1) Conflict affected boys, girls, women, men and elderly receiving winter NFIs and coal, in NGCA2) Conflict affected boys, girls, women, men and elderly benefitting from winterization cash grants, in NGCA and GCA Post distribution monitoring will be conducted to assess the outcome of the activity and monitor cash utilization Price monitoring will also be implemented to assess effects of the cash to the market.In NGCA implementation monitoring will be done jointly with local partners Outcomes% Of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age) reporting that humanitarian assistance is safe, accessible, child-friendly and age- and gender appropriate% of persons/target population in a given context reporting an improved feeling of safety and dignity by the end of the intervention compared to at the beginning% of surveyed households who report that the winter and hygiene NFIs and coal received has supported their winterisation needs
