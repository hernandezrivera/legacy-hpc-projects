Project Summary

In 2024, the World Health Organization South Sudan through this HRP project titled “Strengthening capacity for Water quality surveillance and Infection Prevention and Control measures in cholera hotspots and other priority locations facing acute emergencies in South Sudan” will reduce prevalence of malnutrition as well as contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in line with the WASH Cluster Strategy. To achieve the above objective, the project will prioritize water quality testing, monitoring and provision of real time data to WASH and Health partners for programming decision making. WHO considers safe water to be water that has zero Escherichia coli or thermotolerant coliform bacteria in any 100-millilitre sample of drinking water in accordance with national and WHO water quality guidelines. The project will further build capacity of WASH, Health and Nutrition cluster partners in waste management, and sanitary inspection. In addition, WHO will procure and provide WASH partners with water quality testing kits to expand capacity for testing.
The project will further address gaps in clean and safe water in Nutrition stabilization centers where children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complications are treated by providing candle filters to partners supporting stabilization centers to increase access to safe water.
Through these activities , WHO will reach 59,937 men ,women ,boys and girls from the following priority locations which have been identified as hotspots for cholera as well as other places facing acute emergencies; sample locations selected by the project are Manyo, Fangak, Aweil East, Rubkona, Ayod, Leer, Renk, Kapoeta East, Aweil South, Awerial, Yirol East, Aweil North, Twic, Pibor, Abyei Region, Pariang, Abiemnhom, Twic East, Wulu, Rumbek Center, Yirol East, Juba, Wau Counties which are listed by the cluster under priority 1, 2,3, and 5 respectively.

1. Needs

The need for stronger Infection Prevention and Control measures (IPC) along with effective WASH interventions that guarantees communities with portable water in South Sudan are undermined by the collapsed water, Sanitation and Hygiene infrastructures due to climate change/floods, conflict, widespread displacements and under investment in the sector. There is general deficiency of available safe drinking water, hygiene and sanitation services for households and institutions (health facilities, stabilization centers) across the country that is responsible for recurrent outbreaks of water borne diseases and other epidemic prone disease. The South Sudan WASH cluster estimates that access to WASH services is very low with only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs. Countrywide, 80% of the population don does not have access to latrines. This creates a favorable environment for diseases such as Acute Watery Diarrhea, Cholera and Hepatitis E specifically in historical hotspots.Available data from WHO’s Water quality surveillance conducted between 2018-2023 showed high levels of water contamination as over 59% samples collected from Health facilities water points, POCs setting, IDPs/ Refugee Camps, Urban water systems, factories, and private water points were reported to be contaminated with fecal coliforms. The samples were collected from boreholes, community open well, handpumps, urban pipe connection, household, water trucking, surface water treatment and storage tanks. In 2023, a total of 550 water samples were tested from 18 hotspots in Jonglei, Abyei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el ghazal, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bhar el Ghazal and Lakes states where 318 samples tested positive for fecal coliforms representing 57% contamination rate. People drink un- treated water directly from the unsafe sources, heavily polluted with human and animal excreta resulting in recurrent water borne diseases outbreaks for instance the ongoing Hepatitis E outbreaks in Bentiu IDP Camp where a total of 4, 369 cases including 27 deaths have been reported since 2018, the recent HEV E outbreak in Fangak, cholera outbreak in Malakal County where 1,471 cases and 2 deaths were reported.The gaps above require capacity among stakeholders to increase access to safe drinking water for the affected population as well a strong mechanism to ensure routine water quality testing and data dissemination to WASH responders to inform programming decisions. In addition, the country needs to invest in Infection Prevention (IPC) measures to prevent health facilities related infections. This capacity is however sub-optimal in the country evidenced by limited available water quality data with the line Ministries as well as the WASH partners in the country. WHO conducted IPC/WASH assessments in 209 Healthcare facilities from 15 counties between 2020 and 2023, available data showed an average score of 46% for WASH in health facilities minimum requirement based on WHO’s assessment. In 2024, the WASH Cluster estimates that nearly 5.5 million people will lack adequate WASH services in South Sudan. The WHO’s HRP project will prioritize interventions that will address priority Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures in facilities and stabilization centers, strengthen water quality testing in hotspots as well as increase access to safe water in nutrition stabilization centers by providing candle filters. The project will also build capacity of WASH front-line partners on Basic water quality, treatment, and environmental inspection. This will reduce prevalence of malnutrition as well as WASH-related morbidity and mortality in line with the WASH Cluster Strategy and sectoral objective one.
