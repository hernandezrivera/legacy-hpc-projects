Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to provide access to basic Nutrition services over 2023 in order to bring about more profound change for 59,145 of PLWs of IDPs, returnees, and overburdened host community at-risk groups in the highly neglected sub-district of the below-mentioned areas than is possible with short-term projects through 2023:
Ashara, Jalaa, Mayadeen, Deir Ezzor / Deir Ezzor Governorate 2500 PLWs.
Ma'arrat An Nu'man, Saraqab, Khan Shaykun, Kafr Nobol / Idleb Governorate 624 PLWs.
The consequences of the conflict and the ongoing cholera are particularly severe, as many parts of various governorates have been transformed into hard-to-reach areas where people suffer from loss of livelihood and income, high cost of basic food items, and severe currency devaluation and high rates of malnutrition and anemia. IDPs, returnees, and overwhelmed host communities face massive conflict as they try to provide food for their families. In addition, The ongoing cholera worsening the situation where 5 million faces heavy financial burden of purchasing food. Through Nutrition support, Health conditions will be improved for 3,124 people in areas with (45% high displacement rates with severity 4-5). Provide vouchers for 3,124 of vulnerable PLWs (the vouchers include diversified complementary foods (Identification of the targeted beneficiaries will be in coordination with local authorities and other partners on the ground. RSRP will adopt door-to-door visits delivering modality, because this method is used due to COVID19, to avoid overcrowding during delivery and ensure the safety of beneficiaries. Following an assessment of the Nutrition gap,needs,and timeline for intervention through 2023by the RSRP team, and the coordination with the stakeholder taking into account all vulnerable and gender groups, including men, women, boys and girls, female HHS, vulnerable elderly, persons with disabilities, and households with limited access to basic food items and considering sector results, the number of vouchers and components and target locations were determined. In addition, the available budget has not yet been confirmed by the donor. It should be noted that the project is consistent with the 2023 HNO and the Nutrition Sector Strategic Objectives (SO1) and priorities: Strategic Objective 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in syria, by promoting international law, IHL, IHRL and though quality, principled assistance. The intervention areas may be increased according to the increase in the areas that will be under control in the year 2023.

3 - Needs

According to 2023 HNO, about 361,184 of people in Deir Ezzor and 880,510 of people in Idleb are in need for nutrition assistance, and that 25% of the households are female headed HHs which are still considered the most vulnerable sector in the society and most probably are facing food insecurity. Reports and surveys (SMART 2019) clarified the highest rates of malnutrition and anemia among pregnant and lactating women(PLW) in Deir Ezzor and Idleb governorates and has severity scale of 4 and 5 with total of PLWs 9% in Idleb and Deir Ezzor. The rate of PLWs Anemia reaches 53% in Idleb and 33% in Deir Ezzor and average of 12% underweighted children in Idleb and 19% in Deir Ezzor. All of which led to pregnant and lactating women, are one of the most affected population groups and in need for urgent support. The deteriorating economic situation which is affecting the food security situation of the people making it worsen and not improving. RSRP estimates that inflation in the market is high due to the exchange rate and has reached a value of 5000SYP/US which is affecting the pregnant and lactating women as their nutritional needs increase during the period of pregnancy and lactation. During 2023 RSRP will be focusing in help prevent further unhealthy coping mechanisms and reduce cases of anemia, also provide 3,124 PLWs with basic nutrition services of distribution of 3,124 vouchers support

4 - Implementation of activities

RSRP responds to improve access to Nutrition services for affected PLWs in Idleb and Deir Ezzor. In line with the Nutrition sector, the 2023HNO framework up to the sub-district level, and in compliance with the HRP strategic objectives(SO1 and SO3). The main pillars to achieve the proposed activities in coordination with MOLA, MOH, STD, Nutrition sector and other relevant stakeholders:-approval in coordination with STD-Hiring process- training for RSRP staff who will conduct MUAC survey-Identification of PLW in need by KOBO according to the vulnerability criteria of female HHs, PLW with high malnutrition, persons with disabilities,HHs living in overcrowded conditions and without income sources(in coordination with local authorities and partners)-Provide vouchers for 3,124 of vulnerable PLWs(the vouchers include diversified complementary foods)each PLW receives monthly 40$ since begin of the pregnancy till 15th month. In addition, a coordination will be made with UNICEF and WFP to determine the vouchers materials included. All in consideration of safety measures related to Covid19.-Conduct awareness sessions for PLWs including distribution of brochures and SMS,focus group discussions.-Regular site visits to the health centers to ensure received information of PLW, photos of registration and distribution, PDM surveys to measure impact of vouchers-Accountability is ensured through accessible and safe help line and compliant box and regular evaluation feedback

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PR1: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV and PSEA during all project-stages. 2. Selection of competent and professional hygiene volunteers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. 3.Establishment of an effective feedback and complaint mechanism. 4.Assigning PSEA focal point to deal with all PSEA cases that may occurred. PR2: Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organization or its partners. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ laboure’s. 2.add zero tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts. PR3: Targeted communities including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in RSRP being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure:1. Train staff on PSEA policy and procedures. 2. RSRP will work on raise awareness of targeted communities about PSEA and how to report misconduct.
