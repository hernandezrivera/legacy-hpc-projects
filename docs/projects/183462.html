Project Summary

This project will be implemented jointly by Ard El Insan (AEI) and We World GVC (WW-GVC). It will target children 6-59 months old via house hold interventions. Out of them 1789 boys and girls will be referred to the nearest Ard El Insan centers in order to be treated from moderate (˂-2 Z. Score weight//length/height) or severe (˂-3 Z. Score weight//length/height) wasting. Besides, the moderately and severely anemic children (Hgb. ˂10gm/dl) and (Hgb. ˂7gm/dl) respectively will be referred for the same treatment purposes. Should it be detected, common childhood illness accompanying those nutritional health disorders will be also treated.
The main goal of this project is to contribute to the provision of life saving health and nutrition services for boys and girls under 5 years old in Gaza. The notion is to ensure maintained accessibility towards providing specialized nutritional health services for targeted children within combined community and center-based activities. Thematically, the project is designed to precisely address and mitigate life-threatening childhood under-nutrition, especially, when comorbidity with other clinical conditions exists. Drastically crosscutting and intermingling risk factors for malnutrition and childhood illness appears clearly in the marginalized communities. Ruined infrastructure, extremely low sanitation and significantly poor hygienic sanitation standards are inevitable qualifiers for further deterioration of health and nutrition in children and families residing in these miserable areas.
Considering the current pandemic of Covid-19 virus, the high risk is augmented several folds all over the world (WHO updated reports on website). But, in the unique situation of the Gaza Strip the ominous expectations are undoubtedly more severe.
This project is driven with absolute necessity for combating the overlapping complex of morbidity, mortality, life threatening malnutrition and the attributable disorders of Covid-19 pandemic at all life sectors especially poverty and the already poor resources.
AEI and partners will provide health and nutrition care services for boys and girls under age five. Vulnerably fragile and marginalized communities will be systematically accessed aiming at providing lifesaving nutritional health interventions including; clinical and nutritional assessment, advices on diversified age relevant nutrition, hygiene, sanitation and referring of needy cases for therapeutic purposes. Tangible positive changes of health and nutrition of referred 1400 boys’ and girls’ children are planned to be reached satisfactorily in accordance with project outcome. Vulnerability criteria are the denominators for selecting and referring the direct beneficiaries considering their location, nutritional and health status, protection risks and needs. Areas targeted include; Gaza North; , Gaza; , Khan Younis and Rafah . Right based approach, leave no one behind and “do no harm” are the leading project’s principles. Currently, it is strictly reflected by providing least contact in order to reduce the risk of contracting Covid-9 virus. Children and families in 852 Households (HH) will be screened in targeted areas. Referred children will be provided with proper medical and nutritional treatment, micro nutrient supplements, follow up visits, individual counseling sessions and provision of WASH facilities (i.e. 852 Hygiene Kits). Hygiene Kits will be also provided to 74 women heading 852 HH.
The project will follow updated approaches to achieve resilience of beneficiaries, participatory monitoring, gender/age differences, inclusion of women and children with disabilities, environmentally neutral context, protection mainstreaming, security and full access to project service.

1 - Needs

The already fragile health system in the Gaza Strip which is permanently overburdened with many disabling factors has been further victimized with the Covid-19 pandemic. The term double burden was not enough to describe the devastating condition and deterioration that had been drastically affecting all life aspects in the Gaza Strip causing a triple burden instead. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, poverty is one of the main determinants of food insecurity. According to the latest available data, around 1.5 million Palestinians in the oPt live below the poverty line, with 53 per cent of the population below the poverty line in Gaza (expected to raise to 64% post COVID-19 according to estimates by the World Bank). The unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip during the second quarter of 2020 reached 49.1 per cent, an historical high, up from 45.5 in the previous quarter.51The sharpest increase in unemployment was recorded in restaurants, hotels, commercial and construction activities. Additionally, some 7,000 people employed in Israel have lost their jobs following the outbreak of COVID-19, that’s all cause increasing poverty drastically in the coming period which lead many families to cannot afford the cost of food nor basic needs including clean water and hygienic material, which increases the health risk exposure particularly among children.“WASH’ Nutrition” intervention aiming at complementing the prevention (before the incidence of undernutrition) and the treatment of undernutrition (MAM and SAM) through the strengthening of access to water, sanitation and hygiene services. The three main causes of undernutrition, namely unsuitable or insufficient food intake, poor care practices and disease, are directly or indirectly related to inadequate access to water, sanitation and hygiene. improvements in drinking water, sanitation facilities and hygiene practices have positive effects on disease reduction. When carried out effectively, WASH interventions have the potential to interrupt transmission of pathogens, reduce disease burden and bring significant health and non-health benefits.AEI and WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC) conducted a complementary field survey targeting 1304 HHs during last June up to September 2021. for Malnourished children (moderate and severe wasting and Anemic children) at vulnerable areas to assess the WASH conditions in particular (in terms of water, sanitation, hygiene and general shelter conditions), considering the different needs and expectations of women, men, girls, boys, the elderly and people with disabilities. The assessment identified 852 vulnerable HHs requiring: 852 HHs are in needs to intervene in rehabilitation/upgrading of the WASH facilities (bathroom and kitchen). The proposed intervention aims to enhance the resilience of these vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys, considered some of the most vulnerable across the whole Gaza Stip, by improving their hygiene conditions. Further, it also aims to develop and adopt a suitable environment for people with disabilities that serve the community to incorporate all “inclusivity” principles. Women especially will benefit from this project since, according to local culture, they spend more time inside the house as they are in charge of all housework.Poor nutrition among children under the age of five years as a result of several reasons such as poverty, lack of access to nutritional foods, and poor feeding practices, have led to a rise in cases of wasting, and anaemia in children aged 6-24 months . Malnutrition is also strongly linked to recurrent infections such as gastroenteritis and respiratory tract infections. Water, sanitation and hygiene conditions (e.g. quantity and quality of drinking water availability, presence of waste disposal facilities) are vital to ensure that all health and nutrition interventions integrate key elements of water, sanitation and hygiene e.g. promotion of handwashing, provision of soap and latrines. Due to the increase in the level of poverty, many families cannot afford the cost of food nor basic needs including clean water and hygienic material, which increases the health risk exposure particularly among children the spread of COVID-19 exposes children and their caregivers to potentially massive disruption to their healthcare, access to basic needs and services like food, protection and social interaction. The capacity of the Palestinian health system to cope with an expected increase in COVID-19 cases is severely impaired by longstanding challenges and critical shortages. The situation is particularly severe in the Gaza Strip, where the health system has been undermined by the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, , a chronic power deficit and shortages in specialized staff, drugs and equipment and with massive casualties from the ongoing “Great March of Return”. Various preventive measures have been put in place by the government and other actors to mitigate or slow down the spread of the virus into the Gaza Strip. A major concern is for children being treated for malnutrition, including anaemia cases, is that they may not be able to access their treatment services or that may suffer severe consequences if they get infected with the Corona 19 virus.

2 - Activities

Activities to be conducted by AEI : AEI will provide access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring essential newborn care services are available at all times through: Activity 1: Provision of Basic nutritional supplies (micronutrient supplements, therapeutic treatment of moderate and severe wasting ,will be procured and provided to AEI nutrition centers that will join and support the project. These will be used to provide the full support for the 1,789 targeted children identified with specific nutritional needs and support (quantities are estimated based on AEI database and are subject to change upon actual admission of cases) • Nutritional powder (Baby calories), (2146 tin) • Multivitamin Syrup 200 ml, (7.156 Bottle). • Vitamin (A&D) Drops 10 ml, (8,945 Dropper). • Iron drops 15ml (50mg elemental iron per ml) supplements as prophylactic, (8,584 Bottle). • Anti-biotic, (643 Bottle). • Anti protozoal and Anti helminthic (1,073 Bottle). • Anti-fungal (1,287 Tube). • Anti-pyritic (858 Bottle). • Folic acid , (70,818 tab) . Activity 2: management of malnutrition children with moderate, severe wasting and anemia Children 6 to 59 months with signs, symptoms of moderate or severe wasting and or anemia discovered through screening and assessment from HH beneficiaries will be managed at AEI Health Nutrition care centre to start malnutrition management and treatment process. Activity 3:Provision of appropriate hygiene kit for 852 H.H. AEI will prepare the list of contents of a hygiene kit to help protection from COVID 19 infection for the whole family members of 1,789 targeted children, considering feedback from women/girls and men/boys separately, and adapting the kits to their specific needs. WW-GVC intends to carry out the to improve the essential services of WASH facilities at 852 HHs including at least 202 women-headed HHs; Activity 4; Pre/post needs assessment, (4.1) conduction of a deep assessment in order to verify and monitor the exact needs including a technical survey for planning and design; (4.2) individual field visits for identified 852 HHs; (4.3) carry out 21 gender-separated focus groups (at least three FGs for each locality) to collect feedback and incorporate them in the action. The assessment will be supported with the consultation with HH members, especially women, to collect feedback and prioritize the rehabilitation methodology, ensuring to increase their comfort and privacy. Activity 5; Rehabilitation works, including mainly the WASH interventions; (5.1) Bills of Quantities, technical specifications, and tendering process, to meet basic humanitarian principles and a signed commitment to comply with Special Measures for Protection from any kind of abuse and harassment; (5.2) repairs / upgrading of bathrooms (toilets, hand washing/ shower facilities, partitions, windows and doors, tiling, plastering, and painting works, solar water heater, access for disabled people); (5.3) repairs / upgrading of kitchens (dishwashing sinks, counters, tiling, plastering, and painting works); (5.4) rehabilitation/upgrading of the access ramps for PwDs; (5.5) basic intervention aiming to improve the water storage. The WASH intervetions will ensure women’s and men’s physical and climate protection, privacy, and dignity; improve access to essential HH WASH services, safety, and security, and reflect gender and protection.
