Project Summary

ICT is a powerful tool to improve prospects for people of all ages and backgrounds. Through its flagship ICT Academies, Roia provides vocational training in ICT to disadvantaged youth in hard-to-reach communities. The academies take beneficiaries through a comprehensive curriculum covering a range of employable skills before actively connecting them to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities.
The courses focus on mastering computer fundamentals as well as web design, graphic design and data entry. The goal is to disseminate these skills among the general population of the region to improve the region’s general productivity and future prospects. Combined with skills in communication, idea incubation and job search, we enable students to become change-makers in their community and earn a living from remote work. Besides language and computer classes, the ICT academy will also provide a safe and fully equipped incubator/co-working space to learn, experiment and collaborate. Given the patchy electricity supply and costly mobile data packages, such a place is needed for students to practice their newly acquired skills and start their career as freelance developers. Supported by Roia’s innovative online platforms, these opportunities take many forms and include disbursing micro-grants, connecting beneficiaries to both local and global businesses and organisations, and training beneficiaries to become online freelancers. The ICT Academies are designed to provide youth with lifelong ICT vocational skills and immediate, improved access to livelihood opportunities in both the local market and globally through online freelancing. Project activities include comprehensive and meaningful ICT training as well English training and direct livelihood activities, which include an internship program and incubation phase for online freelancing opportunities and platforms. In total, each ICT academy accommodates 200 beneficiaries and emphasises IDP-host integration. Roia is planning to continue running its ICT Academy in Atarib and to establish another ICT Academy in Azaz or in another location in the Aleppo Governorate. Roia is planning to organize ICT Awareness Events for women inside the facility. The goal is to make women in Syria aware about the opportunities and the potential of ICT, digital technologies, freelancing and the Internet market. It will also provide them with the digital basic skills that will help them getting better access to education, healthcare, employment and financial inclusion.

Needs

Across the whole of Syria, an estimated 69% of people now live in extreme poverty and support themselves on less than $ 2 a day. Of these, it is estimated that 35% live in abject poverty, unable to afford the basic goods necessary to survive. Among young people, who make up over half the population, unemployment rates are estimated at 78% with the figure significantly higher among young women. Such figures are reflected across Western Aleppo. The violence in Syria over the last 7 years has severely affected the regular economic cycle of communities. This project aims to re-insert stimulus into this cycle by providing internationally competitive ICT skills. Normally, there exists a circular economic flow between households and firms. Households provide firms with labour while firms provide wages in exchange. With these wages, households in turn buy goods and services from firms, thus providing firms with the income for wages and other factors of production (e.g.raw materials for goods and services). However, as consequence of the conflict, this circular flow is broken. Firms have been destroyed or forced to close due to fleeing owners, and fewer firms means fewer jobs. Meanwhile, those firms that have remained open have fewer goods and services to offer due to the inaccessibility and unaffordability of raw materials. With fewer jobs, there is less income in local households to buy goods and services—a problem exacerbated by the now higher cost due to scarcity. With fewer goods and services being bought, firms cannot afford to hire labour and, overall, the flow becomes a negative cycle. In Atarib subdistrict, several communities report being unable to afford basic goods and food. Accordingly, a high risk of negative coping mechanisms has been recorded, with child labour, meal skipping, debt, and high-risk/illegal work being resorted to by many community members in order to survive. As well as Atarib, Roia's market assessment in Azaz has revealed that unemployment remains high. Moreover, industry and mechanic businesses are unable to operate due to a shortage of spare parts - despite Azaz' proximity to the border. This renders many business sectors unprofitable for earning a living. Consumers identify "lack of opportunities", "low salaries" and "enertia in the market" [sic] as main hindrance to making money. Even when jobs are available, they are often inaccessible. Two thirds of 60 business owners surveyed, indicated that their new hires are found through a network of relatives or friends. Azaz and similar towns seem therefore as ideal location for an ICT academy. With a disrupted economic cycle, lack of job opportunities and lack of access to the few opportunities available, people of Northwest Syria need to look elsewhere to make a living. Online freelancing is one such option. Our market assessment has revealed that especially the unemployed have high hopes of office jobs. The wordcloud gathers responses to the question "What skills do you think will help you make money?" from Azaz. Respondents expressed a desire to get skills in accounting and financial management or marketing, communication and advertising. Though these skills are not in high demand by local businesses, online freelance platforms do require these skills greatly. Finally, ICT is a recognised powerful tool for women empowerment. It can be useful for personal security, better access to education and employment, financial inclusion and independency and access to basic healthcare information. By raising awareness, equipping women with digital technologies and providing them with opportunities will help them teach their communities how to make the most of these tools.Sources: OCHA, Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018 (2018), 19 REACH, Aleppo Governorate, April 2018; Humanitarian Situation Overview: Sub-district factsheets (2018), 28; Needs and Skills – Market Assessment in Azaz, Roia, 2018.

Implementation of activities

ICT skills, together with certification, English language classes, a place to experiment with software and strategies to become a freelancer are provided by Roia's ICT academy. Beneficiaries go through different stages of assistance, ultimately enabling them to earn a living for themselves. At the heart of the program is a competency building program with English lessons. The competency building program has three tracks targeting different groups and informed by lessons learnt of our ICT academy in Eastern Ghouta and Atarib. English lessons are also taught in different levels, from beginner to medium and advanced. Once the training cycle is completed, students enter a phase of guided workshops, where hands-on projects relevant to the respective track are carried out. This can be a small coding project or redesigning one's own CV. This Competency Building is anchored in the needs of beneficiaries and enables them to overcome obstacles to earning a living. After this, students will leave the classroom setting and be exposed to real freelance offers, through Roia's affiliate platforms, such as Subul.org and Turnsole Ltd. Beneficiaries will have access to the incubator, a space for project-based learning. This involves hands-on capstone projects, mentored by IT professionals or simply revamping their CVs and creating accounts with competitive skillsets on freelancing platforms or Roia's in-house platform Subul.org. This work package gives students the necessary skills to then proceed to an internship with a partnering company or offer their skills on freelance platforms. Thanks to our partnership with Microsoft, we can offer internationally accredited certifications upon completion of the tracks. Awareness-raising events for women will be held inside the Academy to introduce women and girls to the ICT domain and help them gaining knowledge and consciousness to learn and use ICT and digital technologies. The events aim at literate women about the importance of using ICT and its benefits. They will outline the barriers commonly experiences by women in the tech sector such as the infrastructural, social, cultural and linguistic and explain how the programme will enable beneficiaries to overcome these barriers. Success stories of women from the ICT sector, especially in Syria, will be illustrated to motivate beneficiaries and encourage them. The events will promote technology and Internet access and share content that is relevant for women and girls. The events will be conducted for different beneficiaries, and it will include presentations about freelancing, promoting home-business online and how to use internet to increase their income. The events will also outline to attendees the chance of enrolling to ROIA’s ICT Academies in Syria and joining ROIA’s livelihood platform, Subul.org, for freelancing opportunities.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Risk of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse within the academy Women are prioritized in the ROIA working. ROIA plans that 50% of its staff and beneficiaries in Azaz will be women. Having mixed genders in our programs and a mixed staff, there is a risk of sexual harassment and assault and gender-based violence. Because of the taboo around the topic within our target communities, this threat risks remaining undetected and/or unresolved. Mitigation measures: 1.As a preventive measure, we plan a yearly workshop on sexual harassment for staff members and a workshop given to the beneficiaries at the start of every program cycle. The workshop will start with a general explanation, followed by a gender-specific workshop in which male and female persons will be separated. 2.The workshop for women will be given by a member of the same gender, selected from the local community. In there, information will be given on how to react in cases of (or threats of) sexual violence. The options to act will be given, and it will be made clear that total anonymity is guaranteed. 3.The consequences of sexual violence will be made very clear. We are working on a procedure on how to handle the issue. When a serious complaint is made and assessed, ROIA will immediately suspend the alleged perpetrator until the investigation is completed. If the allegations are proven correct, the perpetrator will be fired immediately. Monitoring 1.ROIA will Include sexual violence as part of the M&E procedure 2.We will ensure that communities fully understand – and subscribe to – the proposed program response, and are empowered to participate in M&E 3.We will ensure that staff and beneficiaries fully understand the possible communication pathways they must signal any gender-based violence or sexual abuse issue within the center. Resources required 1.A staff member at the ROIA office will appointed as the official focal point to be addressed in case of complaints. Her or his contact details, such as a WhatsApp account will be made available to all staff members and beneficiaries 2.An awareness training for both staff and beneficiaries will be held at the beginning of every module. 3.It will be made clear in the contracts of both staff and beneficiaries what the consequences of sexual violence are, i.e. immediate suspension and the appropriate legal action to be taken. Risk 2: Failing to incorporate persons with a disability into our programs There is also the risk of older persons and persons with a disability falling of the boat, because it may be more difficult for them to find their way to the courses and adequately follow them. Mitigating measures 1.In our selection criteria for admission to the courses, ROIA will put in place a practice of preference to PWD and orphans. The selection is based on tests which objectively measure the technical qualifications of the applicants. 2.There will be facilities for beneficiaries with wheelchairs to comfortably follow the courses. 3.There are not yet special need-based measures taken for possible blind or deaf beneficiaries. However, ROIA is planning for a specialized program which is adapted to their needs. In fact, ROIA wants to elaborate its reach-out to PWD in the future, as we believe that ICT is a very accessible tool and skill to have and could easily be made accessible to PWD. Monitoring: ROIA will monitor the well-being of PWDs in its program by including their specific case in its M&E. The field officers and teachers will also be instructed to pay extra attention to verify if their disability negatively affects their performance and search for solutions together with the beneficiary. Risk 3: Lack of attendance of women participants Likelihood: medium Impact: high Measures: 1. Roia will establish childcare room for children during the events and trainings in order to facilitate women engagement 2. Roia will provide transportation allowance

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: ExacerbatingTensions between IDPs and the host community Measures 1.The risk of tensions between IDPs and the host communities is considered in ROIA’s project design. ROIA aims for 30% of its staff and beneficiaries to be from the IDP community. 2.Through a transparent selection process, possible tensions between the host community and IDPs will be mitigated. The beneficiaries will be selected through multiple phases on the basis of tests that measure their technical skills. ROIA will also undertake an effort to obtain a high transparency of this selection process. The selection procedure will be clearly communicated to the community. Official communication channels will be provided(official Facebook page through which complaints can be communicated). 3.ROIA plans to further improve its feedback-system in Azaz by creating an online application to specifically deal with complaints. Risk:Escalation of the security situation Measures 1.ROIA aims to closely correspond with the staff and the beneficiaries on how to proceed with the response in a deteriorating security environment. In a crisis situation, the ROIA board holds an emergency meeting to decide upon suitable responses that ensure both the safety of the field staff and the beneficiaries.2.ROIA is currently designing a plan to organize online courses, allowing the beneficiaries to complete the program from their homes in case of an escalation of the security situation. Monitoring 1.ROIA will be constantly in contact with the field team, which also makes assessments of the security situation in Azaz. 2.ROIA aims to prepare contingency plans in consultation with field staff to be able to swiftly respond to preliminary indicators of escalation. 3.ROIA aims to develop a ‘duty of care’ strategy for local partners. This duty of care will be mentioned during staff meetings among the field team. Resources:1. As Azaz is located in an area prone to sudden insecurity, ROIA will invest more-than-average resources in conflict sensitive analysis and will ensure effective and representative community participation, including M&E. 2. ROIA aims to develop an advocacy strategy, so that indicators of rising insecurity are heard and understood by the ROIA board and are acted upon swiftly. Conclusion: The security situation in Azaz is a risk with a medium likelihood, but high consequences. The safety of staff and beneficiaries should always be a priority. Therefore, the security situation in the city needs careful attention and constant monitoring. If the situation has reached a critical point, as happened in ROIA’s other ICT academy in Atarib, ROIA will temporarily suspend its program and look for other possibilities to continue delivering its services, such as organizing online courses or moving the whole operation to another, safer place. A thorough assessment must be made of the responses carried out in Atarib in order to incorporate the lessons learned in the project plans for the center in Azaz.
