Project Summary

Sirri Care Foundation will improve the protection of 1410 (60% females, 40%males) conflict-affected children through the provision of tailored case management, PSS services, provide Covid 19 and GBV prevention messaging. The project is designed to support community-based child protection mechanisms. This will be implemented in Gwoza, Mafa and Jere LGAs of Borno state targeting the most at-risk children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and other at-risk children in the project locations. Identified parents, caregivers, communities and stakeholders including Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) will also be targeted so as to attend and improve on the immediate needs of Children within the communities and strengthen child protection system by addressing root causes.

1 - Needs

The prolonged insurgency situation in the Northeast of Nigeria has exposed boys, girls and their caregivers to protection risks. This has been compounded by the outbreak of COVID 19. An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents in North East Nigeria are in need of child protection services. At least 6,000 children (over 50% girls) have been recruited, abducted or victims of violent attacks since the start of the conflict, and at least 15,250 children (over 50% girls) have been affected by risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence. According to the severity need for Child protection, Borno’s needs are heightened and Mafa, Jere and Gwoza are among the severe rated LGAs Particularly, risks are heightened for adolescents, such as underage recruitment into armed groups, sexual exploitation, child labour, early pregnancy, early marriage and other reproductive health issues with the likelihood that girls and boys may be exposed to different types of risks, it is imperative that urgent need for tailored child protection interventions to reach the different specific needs of at-risks children such as MHPSS and case management of at-risk children, including UASC and CAAG, who have been identified as a significant gap in the current child protection response in the northeast. Build capacity of community structures to address child protection need including addressing root causes. Also, adolescent boys and girls have been identified to face heightened risks thus are at increased risk of neglect, violence, abuse and exploitation. There is need to provide them with life skills empowerment, tailored psychosocial support to help them cope with stress. Even as cases of COVID 19 have reduced in the Northeast, there is still need for community sensitization and adherence to the protocols to reduce the rate of infections. It should be noted that, the NE being reported 25 new cases as at 3rd December

2 - Activities

The project will be achieved through the following activities: Act1. Provision of psychosocial support (PSS) to 3.000 boys and girls through recreational activities including age and gender specific child clubs. Act2. Provide Psychosocial support to 150 caregivers to ensure positive parenting. Act3. Provide gender and age appropriate context specific life skill activities to 90 adolescent boys and girls through group discussion to enable them build capacities ensure child participation. Act4. 120 children affected by protection risks (55%girls) including UASC, CAAG, and other at-risk children will be targeted for tailored case management (including referral for specialized services as needed). Act5. Mapping of referrals path ways and establish or strengthen community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs) to empower community members to be able to identify child protection concerns and respond. Act6. Conduct training for PSS facilitators and case workers who will be members of the community to ensure sustainability. Act7. Conduct referrals for UASC, CAAG to access vocational skill training in already existing training centers. Act8. Disseminate COVID 19 and GBV prevention messages with in the project communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and age appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for boys and girls including community members and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program and staff behavior. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project.
