Project Summary

Sesame Workshop has been implementing Play to Learn, a six-year, large-scale ECD program with generous funding from the LEGO Foundation, a cornerstone program in Bangladesh. The program combines educational media with direct services to meet the holistic needs of children ages 3-10, including health, nutrition, protection, playful learning, and responsive caregiving by 19–59-year-old project personnel and caregivers. Play to Learn reaches children and caregivers with play-based early learning opportunities in child-friendly spaces, community-based learning facilities, homes, and schools in the camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. Direct interventions include Establishing ECE grades in 80 centers integrating early learning videos (WPL) prioritizing children with a disability where projector will be used to show early learning videos in each center. Distribution of 1200 sets of ECD HomeKit for 1200 learners covering 900 for Rohingya children and 300 for HC children for 3-5 years aged children covering a 50% gender ratio. Facilitate caregivers' parenting sessions prioritizing MHPSS. Implementation of early learning videos (WPL) in KG for 30 KG centers where 1800 children will get support with 50% gender ratios. Organize training for 400 staff and facilitators on ECD HK, ECD curriculum and WPL-integrated KG curriculum and Conduct 2 advocacy roadshows where 150 participants from different Sectors, and partners will be attended. Moreover, the Sesame workshop arranges capacity-building initiatives for the Play to Learn project officials. Around 26000 children and caregivers will benefit through the different activities of SW.
Sector-wide interventions- Sesame Workshop has been working for the last 6 years in the humanitarian context involving the Education Sector. Continuation of the running activities, the sesame workshop will develop 15 storybooks in Burmese, English, and Bangla languages as well as print 30,000 units and distribute to the Education Sector partners. Organize ToT storytelling sessions to strengthen the capacity of the Education sector partner. Support the ECEWG and organize capacity-building initiatives for the ECEWG group partners. Develop and distribute learning and awareness-raising materials through Education partners emphasizing MHPSS and disability inclusion. Print and distribute SW storybooks to the 146 Govt. primary Schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf including 4 community-based schools in Teknaf. Early Childhood Education's new curriculum development is in the final stage under the Education Sector where SW will be a part of its implementation through a partnership approach. To promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees, SW conducts Teachers professional development for the implementing partners under the Education sector.
SW followed the gender-sensitive program development cycle in the Play to Learn project and was reviewed by the gender advisor through gender lenses. The MEL plan and tools of the project also ensure gender-segregated data collection. All the data collected from the field was analyzed through gender lenses and shared with the Gender advisor for her review. SW maintains organizational gender policy in all activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the ISNA 2024, children aged 3 to 18 from 78% of the Household are enrolled in different Learning facilities. While children from 22% of households are completely not accessing education 71% of children of 3 years old, 31% of 4 years old, and 17% of 5 years and 10% of 6 years old children were not enrolled in Education. Learners are performing better in English as compared to Burmese with only 3% needing support in English compared to 17% for Burmese. The average attendance rate is lower compared to the sector expectation. So, SW planned to implement activities from the early grades through print content, early learning videos engaging Education Sector partners and home-based activities. The rationale of the proposed activities will target 3-6 years of children for a strong foundation and provide play-based early childhood development opportunities, including well-designed ECE courses, play-based tools, and instructional content and 5-10 years children to provide teaching-learning materials (story books) to create a playful and joyful environment as well as language development. SW will leverage its global expertise in developing and using multimedia platforms and Edu-Tech in delivering play-based ECD services and content in Burmese, English language, and Bangla where required. Rohingya Muppets first ever developed by SW and other characters will be used in the project with its series of AVs in the Burmese language and will bring matches for the project. Due to the huge influx of refugees in Cox’s Bazar, the quality of education provision for learners in the host communities has been adversely affected. In consultation with the Upazila Education Officer of Ukhiya and Teknaf. Play to Learn project of SW and its partners working with lower-performing nationalized primary schools. This project will promote and support social cohesion between host and refugee communities and importantly build the capacity of local partners. Implementation together with local organizations will also sustain the viability of future operations and strengthen local capacities.

Ideally, Protection mainstreaming is an integral part of any humanitarian intervention. Play to Learn project is not an exception. Moreover, Greater protection issues and dynamics are integrated into the operation guidelines to implement the project for both children and parents in all aspects. Our activities will be implemented through the partnership process where the Child Safeguard policy and Referral mechanism will be followed. SW signed MoU with those partners who have strong CSP and refferal mechanisms. Teachers Professional Development on protection in inbuild activity of our program component.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The Play to Learn project has a target to prioritize children with disabilities in all implementing interventions. It is our project mandate for the 2025-2028 period. Our intervention focused on 3-18-year-old children and 19-59 years community people of both host community and FDMN.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SW as an INGO working in different conflict and crisis contexts around the world is committed to AAP principles, thus AAP is a central consideration in every sphere of our actions in humanitarian contexts wherever we work. When we select partners, we check their AAP policies and practices. When we design programs and contents, the affected population is part of our program design and decision-making, implementation, monitoring, and assessment. We also ensure whatever data we collect and findings and analysis we do, we consult and validate those with them first. We systematically give feedback to the population. We also ensure the representation of all groups including age, sex, social power, and ability and other diverse groups. Through the process, we aim to ensure the dignity of the affected population as well as that the intervention is relevant and effective for them.
