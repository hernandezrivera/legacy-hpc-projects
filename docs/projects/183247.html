Project Summary

This project aims to support vulnerable individuals overcome barriers to accessing healthcare in Lebanon, while also supporting Lebanon’s ailing health system against pressures linked to deteriorating economic conditions and against the continued challenges posed by COVID-19. The project will provide essential health services across the continuum of care either through an implementing partner where a gap exists or in close coordination with partners already providing the services.These services are at the level of primary Healthcare, the advanced diagnostics, ER and hospital coverage. All interventions will be closely coordinated with MOPH, WHO, the Health sector and the active partners. The selection of supported facilities is based on the geographical distribution of migrants and is done in close collaboration with MOPH–PHC department aiming at expanding the partners existing support. The PHCs providing specialized services will be prioritized. The modalities follow the existing support schemes by providing subsidized consultation fees for the available pre-defined packages in addition to the running costs incurring from the additional working hours. Focus will also be given to providing mental health support as well as reproductive health support to the most vulnerable migrants. Above activities will be reinforced by supporting 2 hospitals to continue with their essential services focusing on a migrant-dense area. The selection of the 2 hospitals is based on the discussions with MOPH and partners planning to provide such support as well as with the Health sector. The support will entail providing its operational needs on an emergency basis to continue with its operations. Facilities with psychiatric wards, OPD and vaccination center will be prioritized. Finally, the project will continue supporting the COVID-19 response in complementarity with the existing support.Homecare support for vulnerable migrants will be strengthened through 2 homecare teams, one covering BML and the South and one covering Beqaa and the North. The team is composed from a practitioner, a community healthcare worker and a social worker. This team will provide medical care and follow up as well as health education and necessary support to the patient, his caregivers and family. Each team will be able to cover 25 patients at a time. Those 2 teams will be assisted with one mobile team of a nutritionist and a physical therapist to cover the post-COVID rehabilitation needs of a smaller number of patients. Under the COVID section, IOM will as well support the cost of PCR testing requested for vulnerable migrants to undertake surgeries or be admitted to mental health facilities or shelters. IPC trainings for all frontliners are planned and supported under the C19RM successful application that will be channeled through the Middle East Response funded by Global Fund which IOM is the primary recipient. A network of community mobilizers will be engaged in accordance with the sector guidance and equipped with the necessary trainings and materials to deliver quality, integrated health messaging including COVID 19 prevention and vaccination. Communication will be done following different channels and in the different migrant languages. Upon the request of multiple partners and noting that many partners are new to migrant programming; IOM aims to provide a cultural adaptation training. The main objective is to render the services migrant friendly and accessible to this vulnerable group. Building on IOM’s active work with the national IHR focal point to assess the POE’s in regards to COVID 19 preventive measures and the gaps identified and in close coordination with UNICEF, IOM aims to strengthen the COVID 19 preventive measures 5 key POE: Masnaa, Qaa, Aaboudieh, Aarida, Bokayaa by strengthening MOPH capacity at the said POE through deployment of Port Health Officers in addition to the development and implementation of relevant SOP and a TOT for non-health actors

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Provision of inpatient care will be in facilities already selected by WHO, and the Syrian American Medical Society Foundation (SAMS). IOM will provide support to deliveries via a third-party administrator. Access to delivery assistance based on vulnerability criteria, also pertaining to ER, and advanced diagnostics. Mental health support, both at PHC level, and via psychiatric facilities in hospitals, will be specialized to culturally sensitive considerations, and provided through a skilled and well-established implementing partner. IOM will select PHCs through discussion with MOPH, to identify locations with capacity to expand working hours. IOM will support with the extra maintenance costs, subsidize consultations, cover transportation. Implementing partners to provide HR support to PHC in delivery of services. Hospital selection based on needs assessments. IOM will support with energy provision, fuel for water supply, and other critical infrastructural services, needed for the continuance of operations. Homecare teams will provide IPC awareness and guidance to patient, carer, and wider household, as well as clinical evaluation of patient, symptomatic treatment where necessary, as well as check-up and potential referral pending patients’ progress. POE activities will see deployment of health officers, provision of TOT sessions, and equipment such as medical waste bins, PPEs. IOM will adapt and translate RCCE material, train community members to carry out outreach.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All staff working under the project including front liners, and as per IOM standards, are trained on PSEA. All staff and IPs are as well introduced to IOM’s code of conduct and are bound to sign it. Information on the complaint mechanism is shared with all beneficiaries through visuals posted in service delivery points with a hotline to call when needed. Project teams work in close coordination with protection teams to ensure emerging risks are identified and addressed promptly. Project staff and beneficiaries alike are provided with PPEs and COVID-19 prevention measures are respected in all activities and service delivery. In addition, staff are provided with the needed contacts for mental health support through the National System and two IOM staff wellbeing officers provide confidential counselling to staff wishing to access this service. Regular workshops and a wellbeing network have been put in place in IOM Lebanon mission to provide the needed support to all staff.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To meet AAP and related principles, modalities will integrate consultative procedures to maximize target groups input into project design. Community engagement will shape RCCE delivery, and adaptation of material to enhance, communication, and translation of material. PHCs will have feedback slips allowing patients to rate the service and provide feedback on improvements. IOM as the mandated agency to address migrants needs has solid channels of communication with migrant community leaders, activists, consulates and embassies, all of which form a valuable base from which to extract migrant-oriented perspectives and concerns, to inform programming. These strategic relations will be leveraged in the service of the target population. In addition, project reports will be shared for transparency purposes. The project implementation is reviewed quarterly with the different stakeholders through a series of bilateral discussions and lessons learned, are developed, and disseminated.
