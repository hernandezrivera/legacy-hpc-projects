Project Summary

The project will provide lifesaving WASH assistance to IDPs, Returnees and Host community affected by conflict, disasters, malnutrition and disease outbreaks to mitigate WASH related GBV, Nutrition and Health risks. This will apply gender responsive and safe programming approaches including gender risk analysis for activities. The project will adopt community engagement approaches through FGDs, peer to peer talks engaging girls, boys, women, men and teachers on MHM in school, community led hygiene awareness creation campaigns through the CHPs and community leaders. WASH in schools will be promoted through reinforcement of school hygiene clubs. This project will help improve access to safe water points among women and girls through drilling, rehabilitation and upgrading boreholes using Oxfam borehole equipment.
WASH in schools will aim at improving enrollment and performance of girls and boys in schools. Interventions will involve providing safe and equitable access to WASH services including MHM dignity kits. The WASH needs for disabled persons and other at risk groups shall be incorporated into the project. Light infrastructure interventions have been prioritized that include repair and rehabilitation of water supply and sanitation facilities and provision of WASH NFI kits to create greater impact among the target population. In addition, training of water management committees (with at least 50% female membership) will be promoted to build their capacity to manage and maintain their WASH facilities. Emphasis will be placed on female headed households, PLWDs, PLWHIV/AIDS and the elderly. The needs of the population in terms of WASH is directly related to the number of IDPs in the community, which always puts a strain on existing services and water sources.
In conflict-affected areas such as Western Bahr el Ghazal, WASH infrastructure in IDP camps are often overstretched by the new arrivals thus need to be upgraded/maintained due to the protracted situation. These influxes in areas that are already underserved in terms of access to water and sanitation facilities are creating additional strain and health risks whilst emergency response struggles to meet sectoral standards in time.
The project shall foster self-reliance among targeted populations to ensure cost effective and sustainable response across all projects. This is due to the increasing demand for natural resources in densely populated areas, coupled with climate change which continues to challenge sustainability of water resources and their exploitation. The project shall target 70,000 most vulnerable people that include women, men, girls and boys and persons living with disabilities in Wau, Raja, Akobo, Lankien, Pibor, Jubek and Kajo keji. These are communities affected by crisis, food insecurity (high GAM rates) and disease outbreaks, with a strong focus on the most vulnerable members of the community.

1 - Needs

Oxfam has been working in South Sudan since 1983 delivering humanitarian, development assistance and resilience programming. Oxfam has a long and strong presence in Wau, Lankien, Pibor, Akobo and lately in Kajokeji and Nimule locations. Oxfam is currently the WASH Cluster sub-national coordinators in Wau and has been delivering humanitarian assistance since 2014. The 5 years political conflict from December 2013 has significantly affected the security and the livelihood of people in several villages in Western Bahr el Ghazal. Following the April 2016 crisis, more than 70,000 people in Raga were forced to flee as refugees to Sudan and 2,500 were displaced to Wau and settled in Wau IDP camps. The South Sudan WASH Cluster Strategic Operational Framework (SOF) of 2018 indicates that only 41% of South Sudanese population have access to clean water while 7% of the population has access to adequate sanitation — the lowest rate of sanitation access in the world. The 2019 WASH Cluster severity mapping indicates that Raga and Wau are severely constrained on WASH/GBV issues. Counties in Jonglei such as Pibor, Nyirol and Akobo have below average access to WASH services and are severely affected by the 2019 flooding situation that has led to the destruction of the available WASH infrastructure. The current wind of stability in South Sudan is building trust, causing refugees from Sudan and IDPs in Wau to return home to Raga and surrounding villages of Boro Medina, Deleba, Timsa, Mangayat, Sopo, Kuru, Deim Zubeir, Melemba and Kata. The returnees have exerted pressure on the existing WASH facilities where they have settled. Some of the villages have no protected water sources, while the existing water sources are dysfunctional due to lack of maintenance. Similarly, lack of sanitation and hygiene awareness has created rampant open defecation thus the need for safe sanitation practices, hygiene behaviour change and construction of latrines. Female learners face the risks of harassment at schools lacking safe sanitation facilities and while women including girls are exposed to rape and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV) risks while walking long distances to fetch drinking water from protected sources. In greater Bagari, people were displaced from their protected water sources thus need improvement in access to water points for women and girls. There is also the need to improve access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene behavior change through hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH NFIs. Findings from the IOM displacement tracking matrix of 2018 – 2019 shows that more than 90,894 Internally Displaced Persons are living in Wau. 65% of these population still face difficulties in accessing safe drinking water and basic sanitation facilities. Adolescent girls face risks of being attacked when at schools which have no safe sanitation facilities while women are exposed to high GBV risks while walking long distances in search of water from unprotected sources such as seasonal rivers and surface ponds. Oxfam will work to support in coordination with civil authorities in the target areas, rehabilitate existing water sources to improve access to safe drinking water. This is through working with community leaders, women and youth groups to create awareness towards attitude and behaviour change aiming at adaptation of good hygiene and sanitation practices. Formation and training of elected water management committees will ensure proper usage of the water sources, capacity building community resources persons as village pump mechanics will support repairs and sustainability of the protected water sources, while trained Community hygiene promoters and nutrition volunteers will strengthen community awareness towards prevention of common water and sanitation related diseases.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the beneficiaries' rights will be legally recognised. Beneficiaries entitlements to services is legally recognized by Boma, Payam, County and state authorities through involvement of the community leaders. This include the sub-chiefs, executive chiefs and paramount chiefs representing the local government. While the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) serves as the link between the humanitarian agencies and government line departments and ministries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the project includes perspectives of displacement-affected people. Displaced communities will have access to and benefit from the project through the proposed activities to be implemented in the displacement camps in Wau. These challenges have been incorporated during project design after lessons learnt from circle of displacements in Western Bahr el Ghazal in general.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to government and community systems. The project is linked to the National and State government systems and will work through close coordination with different line Ministry of water and related departments/sectors. The project will closely interact with the civil authorities at the Boma level to Payam, at the County and State authorities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the Project is aligned with national objectives. The implementation shall be done through close coordination with the line government departments at different levels at the Boma, Payam, County and State levels. This is aligned with the policies and shall seek collaborative support from technical sections related to social protections/IDPs and national refugees Return framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable. Successful implementation of the signed revitalized peace agreement will guarantee political peace and stability in South Sudan and the general protection environment. Government, international agencies and the civil society are vital in ensuring that this is achieved. This will enable continuous community engagements, dialogues and discussions in peace initiatives with durable solutions.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes sustainability among the proposed activities and proper handover to the target communities. The project implementation has clear exit strategy that includes development of MoU to be signed by the relevant parties during hand over. This shall include Oxfam, government authorities and community leaders and representatives. The project has community capacity building strategy focusing of the roles each trained group will play to support the sustainability of project activities. Capacity building through refresher and new trainings targeting various community groups shall be done to bolster the WASH capacity in the community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will ensure that Women and girls are considered during gender risk analysis sessions for the various activities to be implemented. This shall be done through FGDs, continuous dialogue and having consultative forums with the target group. The project will ensure that displaced women and girls are not exposed to sexual violence as a result of staying in highly insecure environment. WASH facilities shall be sited in secure and safe locations identified by the target women and girls through gender risk analysis sessions to be carried out. The project will ensure that allocation and distribution of WASH support services will be done through openness and transparency while involving all the key stakeholders during beneficiary identification and selection. This will help promote peaceful coexistence among the target community through equitable allocation of WASH resources.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure that feedback from the most vulnerable community members is provided through the established CFM mechanism. This shall be carried out through holding continuous meetings with the beneficiaries through door-to-door visits, having coordination meetings with key stakeholders, community leaders and governement authority to ensure that all issues raised are amicably addressed. The project will ensure timely and continuous monitoring of project implementation and collect feedback from the beneficiaries through conducting post distribution monitoring and WASH KAP survey. Person to person talk sessions will be conducted through use of the local language so as to collect the views and issues relating to the project from the most vulnerable persons. FGD sessions and dialogue sessions will be conducted with persons living with disabilities, not so literate persons including women and girls to ensure that their views are captured and are addressed. Oxfam has existing Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) that provides for follow-up action on issues reported and are affecting the beneficiaries and thereafter provide the necessary feedback. The CRM approaches will be adopted and used under this project to ensure that all interventions are community centered and encourage community dialogue, consultation and their participation in delivery of WASH activities. The project will ensure that girls, boys, women, and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have access to timely and appropriate feedback on any complaints. This will promote corrective actions towards addressing their specific protection and assistance needs and documentation of the same. Regular meetings with community representatives will be held to provide a platform where Oxfam will regularly inform and update community representatives on the planned, progress and achieved project activities including timelines.
