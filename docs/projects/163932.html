4 - Needs

Since the beginning of the crisis in 2011, Syria has witnessed signiﬁcant challenges in the humanitarian issues and civilian infrastructure, resulting in huge demand for urgent help in almost all sectors. And as more areas are witnessing increased stability and security, a larger number of spontaneous returns of IDPs to their original areas has created the necessity for plans to respond to the needs relating up to 31,030 long term shelters to be provided to returnees, (according to the published 2020 planning figures-UNHCR), with twenty per cent of returns expected in the governorate of Rural Damascus.(HRP 2019)Planned project activities of providing durable shelter solutions to owners, tenants and host communities, with necessary repairs to homes with materials, professional assistance and skilled laborers, are based on a need assessment conducted by the engineering staff of the Syrian Society for Social Development (SSSD) on July-August 2019 in the suggested undeserved area, in coordination with municipalities in the chosen location, aiming to identify beneficiaries among vulnerable groups such as women headed households, children, elderly and persons with disabilities subject to significant protection risks (as per the agreed criteria), all being in line with the Shelter Sector strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs, and to complement ongoing projects being implemented by SSSD in the area.

5 - Implementation of activities

Following the findings of the needs assessment, official approvals have been obtained for the implementation of work of rehabilitation in the area for 75% of the selected beneﬁciaries suggested, expecting the rest of approvals to be obtained along with the process. Procurement of material shall be done through a tender to be announced in the local market, once the ﬁnancial fund is secured, implementation of the work shall be realized using local contractors, with supervision by the staff of the Syrian Society for Social Development from the main office in Damascus.Monitoring and Evaluation shall be conducted by SSSD's engineering team following the technical speciﬁcations presented in the tender and the sector's guidelines.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Medium / Impact: High / Mitigating measures: - Ensure that service is provided according to beneficiaries selection criteria & vulnerability scoring Protection risk: Tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out if selection criteria is influence by local authorities Likelihood: medium/ Impact: medium / Mitigation measures: - Selection process for private shelter to follow sector guidelines ensuring all steps are followed in a transparent and compliant way. (Criteria, code of conduct..). Protection risk: Physical safety of beneficiaries/staff is at risk due to structural damages of the building. Likelihood: medium/ Impact: high / Mitigation measures: - Selection of structurally safe buildings. - Structural assessment of buildings to be done by qualified personnel. Protection risk: Exclusion of benefiting from shelter repair/rehabilitation works due to lack of tenure document Likelihood: high / Impact: medium / Mitigation measures: - Communication of alternative documents to prove ownership. - Engages local authorities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Lack of building materials in the local market; Mitigation measure is to ensure the procurement of the total quantity to be stored in the project's warehouses ahead of implementation. 2. Difficulties to access the location by the staff of the Syrian Society for Social Development due to security problems; Mitigation measure would be to recruit a part of the staff of local inhabitants of the selected location. 3. Changes in prices of materials; Mitigation measure would be to ensure flexibility in the specifications of adequate materials. 4. Delay in obtaining official approvals; Mitigation measure would be to apply for approvals ahead of the assessment stage of the project/ Change of the location.
