Project Summary

4 - Needs

Keeping a dual approach of early recovery and rapid response in mind, the ultimate goal of this project is to ensure the most vulnerable households are able to meet their basis needs through livelihoods-oriented programming. This remains in line with the Syria HRP, which has three strategic objectives, the first being to save lives and the third to increase resiliency and access to services. Syria still remains a multi-faceted, protracted emergency where some populations are able to maintain a sense of normalcy and seek opportunities to reduce dependency on the humanitarian structure, while others continue to be displaced en masse. This project will support both communities to reduce levels of dependency without neglecting those who need the most basic assistance, such as food and hygiene items. The project objectives also fit in line with the Syrian's populations' perspective of need, according to the HRP, whereas the surveyed population chose (1) economic opportunity and (2) basic services as their top priority concerns with food as the #1 type of preferred assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will include; support to small businesses entrepreneurships, support to livestock, provision of equipment and materials in Homs and Aleppo

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project has a protection focus. Once enrolled in the program, selected beneficiaries will be recorded in a data base, counslled and a plan of action will be made for each of them. Many of the beneficiaries will be chosen as they are referred as SGBV cases or are in need of specific assistance such as referral for PSS, legal counseling.. The project will additionally continue to support those activities of the center which were initiated during the recent covid-19 lockdown, during which many people reported feeling isolated and depressed, made worse by the country's socio-economic situation and the ongoing conflict.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security: the security risk is different in key project locations and these are managed differently. Dorcas and the partner have regularly updated security management process and SOPs in place; monitor security information from INSO and other sources, well trained staff to mitigate the risk of security incidents. This includes a security officer in Syria and a security technical advisor at International office. If security risks become too high for a protracted period in one of the 3 key locations, the project can continue and in other locations. Incorporating local NGO with good access also mitigates the risk of projectsuspension for security reasons. Sanctions: Dorcas will abide by all sanction legislationwith procurement done by the organization itself for COTER/sanctions compliance andchecks.
