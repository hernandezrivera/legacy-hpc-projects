Project Summary

Acute malnutrition remains a major public health problem in Somalia, conflicts, climate shocks, food insecurity, population displacements, high disease burden and recurrent outbreaks are all contributing factors to sustained malnutrition. 2021 Post Gu FSNAU nutrition analysis shows that the national median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) remains serious with a GAM rate of 11.1%. Estimates indicate that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of five years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently, 295,515 will be severely acute malnourished (SAM) children and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM). In addition to this, around 245,909 Pregnant & Lactating Women (PLWs) will be at risk of acute malnutrition.
Medair’s target locations are among areas of concern and urgent scale-up of integrated health and nutrition intervention is needed, Mogadishu and Shebelle states are among those with the highest numbers of individual conflict-induced displacement. The Shabelle riverine areas of Balcad and Afgooye recorded a critical nutrition condition with a GAM rate of 15.9% while the Mogadishu IDP settlements (Daynile-Afgooye corridor) and Mogadishu urban area (Karan) reported a GAM rate of 16.5% and 14.8% respectively. Kismayo district reported a GAM rate of 11.4% which illustrates serious nutrition conditions, the situation is likely to worsen owing to current drought condition in Jubaland. COVID-19 pandemic is negatively impacting on already fragile livelihoods and health system in Somalia, Health workers, who are key in management of acute malnutrition, are overstretched and threatened by spread of COVID-19, this has significantly reduced the capacity of the health system to address urgent medical complications, leaving women and children more exposed to acute malnutrition risks.
Medair proposes an all-inclusive nutrition intervention comprising of early identification of malnourished cases, their treatment and referral as necessary. The proposed project interventions will provide integrated services targeting children between 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with preventive, promotive and curative nutrition services both at the health facility and community levels. nutrition services will be provided using fixed, outreach and mobile clinics targeting inaccessible areas. At the community level, a large network of 1,447 community health workers spread across Medair project sites, will be supported to engage with community members including mothers, caregivers and opinion makers with the aim of encouraging optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and childcare practices. They will also link children with catchment health facilities to access micro-nutrient supplementation and immunization services particularly measles vaccination. The community network, using the care group model approach, will identify protection and GBV-related issues at community level and respond aptly through linkage to appropriate services. All nutrition activities will be integrated with health and WASH to ensure a multi-sectoral response. At the health facility level, OTP and SFP nutrition services will be provided at the same clinics and locations to ensure a full transition from SAM to MAM and then to recovery. Medair will target 3802 SAM children with OTP services and 12,069 MAM with TSFP. Essential micronutrients will be provided to pregnant and lactating mothers and children under 2. Medair will ensure effortless access to these nutrition facilities for persons with disabilities, community health workers will be trained to identify acutely malnourished children with disabilities and refer them to nutrition programmes. Around 6,823 PLW with acute malnutrition will also be targeted in this response. Children with medical complications will be referred and treated in hospitals with Stabilization Centres.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Minority clans/Ethnic groups are not included in consultations and or are excluded from the humanitarian services. Medair will embark on identifying and addressing differential risks of exclusion and discrimination from humanitarian services, including those based on societal, power structures, vulnerability, age and gender - with the ultimate aim of all eligible beneficiaries being included. Medair will ensure that minority ethnic groups including internally displaced persons are fully included in the entire project cycle. A consultative committee will be formed comprising host and displaced population representatives as well all minority ethnic groups. Static nutrition sites will operate seven days a week for 8 hours ensuring equal, unrestricted, and direct access to nutrition services for both the IDPs and the host communities. Existing engagements with the local leaders from the IDP marginalized communities will be strengthened. Safe, accessible, and confidential community-based complaint reception mechanisms have been established in field locations. The existing feedback mechanism at facility level, through a toll free hotline number will also be widely publicized. Nutrition staff and committees will include representatives from the minorities and marginalised ethnic groups. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence including sexual exploitation and abuse.All Medair and partner staff will receive training on sexual exploitation and abuse; they will also be expected to sign a code of conduct which forbids them from sexually exploiting beneficiaries. The humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence will be observed. Individuals who are known to have committed acts of sexual exploitation and abuse will not be hired anywhere in the Medair Somalia system. Screening of candidates will be done for every Medair post prior to hiring. Once employed, Medair will continue with organization-wide awareness-raising about what constitutes acceptable behavior by staff and personnel and how to report wrongdoing through the existing mechanisms. At supported health facilities, trauma and counselling first aid will be provided to survivors of SEA. There will also be improved and sustained provision of clinical management services including specialized psycho-social services where available. A clear referral pathway exists at all facilities for those requiring advanced support not available in supported facilities. Risk assessment- Medair will endeavour to identify and prevent misconduct risks before wrongdoing occurs. Regular risk assessment visits and risk-mitigating actions will be carried out in all field locations, with a particular focus on risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse. Nutrition services will be provided as close as possible to the beneficiaries with a view to minimizing risks to beneficiaries who may have to travel long distance to access services. The extended community network will further optimize protection by bringing sensitization messages on stigma and available services. Medair will continue to support a taxi referral network to ensure that beneficiaries with complicated SEA cases safely reach the appropriate level of care. Activities incorporate a basic mental health component to help the targeted populations deal with trauma following a crisis with referral for those needing more in-depth clinical mental health support.
