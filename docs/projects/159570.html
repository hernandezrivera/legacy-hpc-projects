Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s food, hygiene and winterization components are implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) is provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU). The PSS component of the project aims at reducing social isolation and loneliness of conflict-affected older people as well as increasing stress resilience of children and adolescents.
The project is funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.

1 - Needs

Since the commencement of the conflict in Eastern Ukraine in 2014, hostilities have negatively affected psychological wellbeing of children, adolescents and older persons, particularly those living within the 5-kilometer zone of the ‘contact line.’ Older PeopleThe conflict in Eastern Ukraine also led to massive displacement of local populations. Since older persons were not able or willing to flee the conflict-affected areas, many of them are now separated from their families and left in isolation. As a result, social seclusion and loneliness have negatively affected physical and mental health of older persons in Eastern Ukraine. According to the baseline report by Help Age International (July 2018), 96 % of older people are experiencing various conflict-related psychological issues, whereas 41,2 % of older people (86 % of them women) are living alone. Consequently, socialization for conflict-affected older people who live alone is essential for improvement of their physical and cognitive health condition. Social interactions with peers on a regular basis (e.g. at the social centres) will have a positive impact on the older people’s well-being. A significant number of older beneficiaries have problems with mobility or are bed-bound. Therefore, the bed-bound beneficiaries who are not able to attend the social centres require home-based psychosocial support (e.g. through the mobile groups).So, the project activities are tailored in order to address psychosocial needs of vulnerable older persons, both mobile and home-bound ones. Children and Adolescents According to the UNICEF report, 54,000 children live within the 15 km of the contact line, whereas over 12,000 children out of more than 19,000 children living in the 5-km zone are exposed to shelling at least once a month. As a result, children living in the conflict zone suffer from symptoms consistent with post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety, night terrors, bed wetting, aggression and depression, among other things (UNICEF’s report “The Children of the Contact Line in East Ukraine”, June 2017).Accessibility of psychological support is very irregular, especially in the 5-km zone. Even where it is provided by humanitarian actors, the lack of long-term consistent psychosocial support remains a major challenge. Since parents in the conflict-affected areas are also under significant stress because of the ongoing conflict and difficult socioeconomic situation, children’s psychological state is often complicated with neglect at home or even domestic violence (UNICEF’s report “The Children of the Contact Line in East Ukraine”, June 2017). The problem is aggravated by the fact that school teachers often lack techniques on how to communicate with children who suffer from different stress disorders. Due to the lack of organized extracurricular activities, children and adolescents face the risk of overcoming stress through antisocial behavior (alcoholism, drug addiction, etc.). Therefore, such project activities as organizing leisure for conflict-affected children and adolescents at the social centres and providing PSS to home-bound children with disabilities through mobile groups are of utmost importance. By attending social centres, boys and girls will have an opportunity to receive PSS from a trained psychologist and communicate with peers. In case boys or girls experience gender-based violence, these problems may be identified during the individual sessions with psychologists. Older people, especially women, who experience some form of psychological abuse have an opportunity to build up their emotional resilience through the trainings provided at the social centres. Moreover, communication with peers is of utmost importance for older men and women who live alone.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Prior to the project start, the local communities of Zolote and Toshkivka (Popasnianskyi raion, GCA of Luhanska oblast) requested support in organizing leisure for conflict-affected children and adolescents in these settlements, which are located with the 5-km zone from the ‘contact line’. Therefore, the choice of these locations was prompted by the respective communities. Beneficiaries who attend social centres or receive PSS at home within the framework of the current project demonstrate significant vulnerabilities: • 22,5 % are older persons;• 64 % are women; • 77,5 % are children; • 18 % are persons with disabilities.The project was presented in selected localities. The contact details of each social centre and the organization's hotline number are available, so that any interested in PSS can apply for assistance. People in the target localities within the 5-km zone from the ‘contact line’ who meet the criteria (age, need for PSS) have an opportunity to attend the social centre or (in case of home-bound people and persons with severe disability) be attended by a mobile psychosocial group. A mini-van brings children, adolescents and older people from the localities in the 5-km zone to the nearest social centre (Druzhkivka, Zolote or Toshkivka). Three mobile psychosocial groups travel by car to home-bound beneficiaries (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities). Each group consists of a trained psychologist and engaged volunteers from among youth and mobile older people. Activities (Senior beneficiaries):• Provision of psychosocial support for mobile single older people visiting social centres;• (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities). Outcomes for senior beneficiaries:• Improved social inclusion of older men and women;• Older beneficiaries’ well-being is improved.Activities (Children and adolescents):• Provision of group and individual psychosocial support for children and adolescents who attend the centres;• Organization of outbound recreation (e.g. summer camps);• Conducting trainings on active listening, inspiring dialogue and anti-stress techniques for school teachers.Results for children and adolescents:• Children have developed resilience to stress;• The risk of negative coping strategies (e.g. alcoholism and drug addiction) among the conflict-affected children and adolescents has reduced;• Teachers are trained on the techniques of dealing with the consequences of post-traumatic stress disorder in schoolchildren. The project will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives (PCO) 1 “Strengthen protection, including prevention and mitigation of rights’ violations, for conflict-affected people, including IDPs”, PCO2 “Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs” and PCO3 “Improve social cohesion and resilience of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, through community-based protection and identification of durable solutions” through the following activities: • Psychosocial Support;• Provision of Community spaces for older people and people with disabilities;• Capacity building.

3 - Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms;• 450 children reached by psychosocial support activities through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), Group Sessions, or outreach;• 85 older people accessing community spaces; • 50 home-bound older people reached by individual psychosocial support;• 15 home-bound children with disabilities reached by individual psychosocial support• 20 teachers trainedProject beneficiaries are informed about the complaints procedure and provided with relevant contact details (e-mail, phone, etc.). All complainants are treated respectfully and provided with information on how their feedback was addressed. Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the effectiveness of the programme and make the changes in line with the feedback provided. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age and gender disaggregation.
