Project Summary

Since 2018, continued conflicts in Oromia region have resulted in massive displacements of inhabitants to different parts of the region. However, due to the volatile nature of security and surge of IDP influx there is a wide response gap in the area. This proposed project will provide Emergency Shelter and NFI items to 900 vulnerable IDP HHs and host communities residing in Sasiga Woreda, East Wollega Zone, Oromia Region. According to the data obtained from zones, there are more than 200,000 in Wellegas and 100,000 in Kamashi zone displaced people, as of November 2021. Currently, it has been reported that a total of 24 woredas are hosting IDPs in East and West Wollega Zones. Security condition remains highly volatile in these areas due to expanding movements of UAGs within Oromia and BGR, 49,530HHs/ (276, 768 individuals) are reported to be living in desperate conditions in the Four Wollega zones (East, West, Horo Guduru and Kellem Wollegas) and recent IDP data recorded 24,174HHs / 147,128 individuals in East Wollega Zone are in need of assistance. Out of this, only 7,173HHs/ (35,865 individuals) have been provided with shelter intervention thus showing the wide response gap and need in the area. Particularly in Sasiga Woreda, there are about 3,445HHs/21,641 individuals living in a dire condition out of which 2,731HHs/ (13,144 individuals) are male and 714HHs/ (8,497individuals) are female. ESNFI needs are stated to be high in these woredas due to continuous displacements and the absence of humanitarian partners. IDP population is living in a congested situation with 4 to 6 HH in one rented shelter with a price ranging from 800ETB to 1000ETB. Since most are not engaged in income-generating activities, rental houses are unaffordable for many of them. From the FGDs conducted among IDPs in Sasiga woredas, it was observed that most are living with relatives and in substandard shelters. Others reported that they are sheltering outside with no privacy or protection from the weather, particularly vulnerable groups such as women, children, and the elderly. Overcrowded shelters have an increased risk for the spread of COVID-19. IDPs also shared that they lack basic housing appliances that oblige them to share with other community members. Continued IDPs influx in these areas necessitates prompt intervention taking into account cross-cutting issues. This target area is selected in accordance with the Ethiopian Emergency Shelter/NFI cluster prioritization depending up on the need and response gap in the intervention area. The overall project objective is to ensure access to Emergency Shelter and NFI items for IDPs and host communities in Sasiga Woreda in order to ensure the safety, well-being and dignity of the affected population. This project is designed in check with the cluster’s strategic objective and EHF second round standard allocation strategy. It targets 15% of the host community and 5% to HHs with family members with disabilities to ensure inclusion of all groups of the community in the assistance. The intervention will be in line with the standard guideline and recommendation of ESNFI cluster. FIDO will also give due attention to COVID-19 prevention strategies, accountability and cross-cutting issue. The project period of implementation is 6 months with a total budget of 180,000. USD.

1. - Projects activities

Signing Project agreement with the responsible government authorities.Training on common safety and security protocols for FIDO's field-level operational staff regarding communications, travel protocols, security management structures, contingency planning and mitigation measures for possible threats, COVID-19 mitigation measures, protection mainstreaming approaches, humanitarian accountability, PSEA code of conduct and conflict resolution methods and beneficiary selection, verification and registration. Conduct introductory meetings to share clear communication about the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process with representatives of the affected population , local authorities and government stakeholders. Community volunteers will be recruited from the IDPs and be given training GBV & PSEA issues, COVID-19 mitigation measures at distribution points, conflict resolution methods, accountability issues, and data collection for post-distribution monitoring. Setting up beneficiary selection committee, complaint handling committees and Help Desk. Beneficiary identification , registration and verification for 900 HHs based upon vulnerability criteria.Groups with a specific need that will be prioritized for assistance include girls and boys at risk, including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with serious health conditions, persons with special legal or physical protection needs, single women, women-headed households, older persons, persons with disabilities will be selected by the established beneficiary selection committee. FIDO will carry out self-assessment, and discussion platforms for people with disabilities. FIDO will perform the final verification of the beneficiaries independently and check if the selected beneficiaries meet the vulnerability or selection criteria. Processing Procurement and delivery of Emergency shelter and NFI V2 item kit for 900 HHs out of which 50HHs kits are for people with disabilities. Distribute ESNFI items Kit (V2) for the targeted 900 IDP and Host community households. FIDO will take measures to ensure that vulnerable households with mobility challenges or other special circumstances (e.g., disabled/elderly/child-headed households, income limits) are able to access the distribution sites and safely transport and utilize the ESNFI kits. If households cannot access the distribution site, FIDO staff will deliver the kit directly to the most vulnerable households. Alternatively, FIDO will mobilize volunteers to assist in delivering the ESNFI kits to this group of beneficiaries. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to affected population will be held during distribution by using different printed IEC/BCC materials translated in the local language. Production and distribution of age and gender-sensitive IEC materials on how to report sexual exploitation and abuse and how to access survivor-centered assistance will be distributed for beneficiaries at distribution points. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted for targeted HHs. Enumerators will be trained on how to collect data and conduct assessments using tools adapted to this project, and they will survey a sample of households in the area that received assistance. Participatory tools such as focus group discussions and key informant interviews will be used in the monitoring process. Data entry, data analysis processes will be completed at the field level and final analysis and report writing will be done by the MEAL coordinator based in the Head Office. Reports will be shared with the cluster. . The monitoring and evaluation team from the head office, in collaboration with government signatories and beneficiary representatives will conduct a final evaluation. Lessons learned from evaluation findings and the project's experience on areas to improve on and on which areas to further strengthen to provide the utmost quality of service to the community.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

FIDO has well-structured program execution and monitoring mechanisms at different structural levels which is designed in a way that enhances its efficiency and effectiveness in responding to the emergency needs of the community. FIDO’s presence as operational in the proposed areas comprises professionals which are supported by the zonal cluster leads and regional leadership. The regional and zonal leads also work in harmony with the technical leads and units at the head office. This project will apply participatory monitoring approaches that engage all concerned bodies and beneficiary representatives. The process engages the beneficiary representatives and local authorities from the onset of the project with special emphasis on screening. Joint monitoring with the involvement of ESNFI shelter cluster, progress and performance will be tracked and a range of data collection methods of both qualitative and quantitative will be applied. A comprehensive monitoring framework will be developed at the start of the project to ensure the existence of a well-planned monitoring schedule, partner’s involvement, required resources allocation, and supportive tools for data collection and analysis prepared.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Initially the project is designed based on the identified gaps, and as any other projects, FIDO will create an effective communication channels between the government stakeholders and the affected population prior to the implementation of any of the project activity. FIDO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. The mechanism will ensure comprehensive and confidential system set up. Site and beneficiaries selection will be take place after consultation meetings held with government stakeholders, cluster representatives and representatives of the affected population. Different meetings will be held with community members to provide information and discuss on planned interventions from beneficiary targeting, verification, prioritization, distribution process, to informing the rights and entitlements of recipients. FIDO works to integrate inclusion of people with disability in all cycles of the project. It will ensure the participation of this groups in beneficiary selection committees, complaint handling committees and monitoring framework. It will also organize consultation on planned projects with people with disabilities and understand the barrier to access to assistance. Feedback mechanisms will be designed to adequately record issues faced by persons with disabilities and staff is empowered to act on these suggestions. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to affected population will be held during distribution by using different printed IEC/BCC materials. Complaint handling and Beneficiary selection committee along with responsible government stakeholders and FIDO project staffs will involve handling any concerns or complaints that arises from the beneficiary and act accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, FIDO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to concerns of beneficiary and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of FIDO staffs and selected focal representative of the community. FIDO will use the standard checklist on incorporating accountability to affected population in the humanitarian programme cycle developed by the Global protection cluster to ensure that AAP is integrated into all cycles of the project. FIDO project staffs and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on ground in every level of the project. FIDO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age dis-aggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings
