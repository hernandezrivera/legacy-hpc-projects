Project Summary

The SAFE project is multiple year project (2019 to 2021) and will be implemented by GREENCODE for 36 months (12 months per year) planned for 12 months.
GREENCODE plans to adopt 4 approach to improving SAFE access; this will include;
Firstly, community and target group mobilization, train enumerators, conduct baseline survey on understand and validate early secondary data on SAFE in Dikwa and Bama LGA.
Secondly, GREENCODE will identify, select and train 300 (150 in each LGA) target group on construction of fuel wood efficient cook stoves (the stove is designed to use either firewood or charcoal and uses Rocket design technology from Ghana).
Also, contracting ROSAN limited to construct 2,000 for distribution in both LGAs (1,000 per LGA).
The stoves will be of two types (firewood and briskets) based on the choice of each beneficiary.
Thirdly, 700 already assembled solar lighting kits (lamp and mini panels) will be procured from Solar Sister or Total Companies for distributed to new arrivals, some selected households in camps and host communities.
Also, conscious effort will be taken to mainstream gender and protection activities into the project, some of the gender/protection activities already planned include mass awareness on GBV (sexual abuse/exploitation), Mine education and early warning signs/response mechanism.
The project is estimated to reach 2,000 households (12,000 persons as direct beneficiaries without double counting) and over 24,000 indirect beneficiaries with all the project activities.
Currently, the beneficiaries mainly women and children are going out of the camp into far way bush to collect both dead and fresh woods for cooking, while some trade off their in-kind food or cash for food from humanitarian agencies to buy charcoal/firewood from traders.
Similarly, in these locations target beneficiaries lack access to safe and sustainable source of energy for lighting. Some households are forced to sleep in the dark as they don’t have lamps or afford fuel to power the local lantern
The risk some of them faces in search of energy for cooking ranging from adoption from the bush by Boko Haram insurgency, while some are killed, upper respiratory health effect arising from inhaling of carbons/fumes from the open firing method of domestic cooking with 3 stones, poverty and some gender based violence from men who owns and trade on fuel (wood and charcoals),
The rationale for this project is to reduce by 40% the amount and volume of wood /charcoals used for cooking, improve health condition through improved cooking method, reduce the high risk of sexual exploitation faced by women and girls for procuring and distributing solar lamps that will be sustainable and less cost effective in its maintenance.
GREENCODE in the implementation of this project will be working closing with following organisations; -NEMA/SEMA, IOM, local communities, ROSHAN Global Company (Happy Stove), UNOCHA and Global Alliance for Clean Cook Stove- Nigeria chapter and SAFE Working group chaired by FAO with the FSS (co-chaired by FAO and WFP)”
As gender sensitive organisation, GREENCODE has in place a staff Code of Conduct and integrate commitment to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), this will be closely monitored and all staff wil sign it to ensure that beneficiaries are protected.

Needs

UN agencies and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) from the Food Security, Camp Coordination and Camp Management, Shelter and Non-Food Items, Protection humanitarian response sectors have highlighted the lack and inappropriate use of energy for vulnerable populations – including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities and the key protection concerns. Assessment report has also indicated that in densely crowded camps, cooking on a three stone fire or rudimentary mud stove may pose a significant fire risk. Women and children are the most exposed to serious harm due to the risk of burns when cooking on open fires and are often exposed to harmful smoke and related respiratory illnesses due to indoor air pollution caused by cooking on these rudimentary cooking technologies. IOM-DTM report shows that women have traditionally taken on the role of collection and sale firewood as a critical source of energy for households cooking needs. In addition, several articles and reports highlight women, girls, men and boys being attacked, raped, killed and abducted while collecting firewood particularly as they travel out of the secured boundaries of towns/villages, beyond the security provided by the Nigerian Armed Forces.FAO and UNCHR Rapid Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) survey with IDPs in Borno state further found that a total of 96% of the surveyed households depend on fuelwood or charcoal for cooking and nearly 20% of the households reported that at least one person in their community had experienced sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) while collecting fuelwood. Due to the rapid depletion of fuelwood resources around camps & villages, which has in some cases caused deforestation, women must walk long distances in order to collect fuelwood. The production of charcoal and selling of wood-fuel, for instance, fuelwood and charcoal, are also contributing to increased deforestation rates.Inappropriate cooking practices and lack of energy may also have severe implications for the safety, health and nutritional status of women and children, who are primarily tasked with food preparation.Similarly, on the spot observation by GREENCODE team during FAO project in Dikwa and NERI in Bama LGA shows that due to the insurgency, 100 percent of the population in Dikwa and Bama and other near-by LGAs mostly IDPs and host communities lack access to electricity and same percentage lack clean cooking facilities. The IDPs are continuously affected by the total absence of sustainable and reliable energy, with increasing difficulty in remote areas. In both LGAs as well as other northeast LGAs in general, access to fuel and energy is critical to essential activities such as cooking meals (food aid), heating shelters, cooling vaccines, charging mobile phones, and powering humanitarian operations.Current energy practices in situations of displacement are often inefficient, polluting, unsafe, expensive and inadequate for displaced people, harmful to the surrounding environment, and costly for implementers. Given the complex nature of humanitarian situations and the challenges of integrating sustainable energy solutions into the humanitarian program cycle, there is a need for systemic actions to mobilise resources, build capacity, raise awareness, and use the opportunity for energy solutions to enhance positive impact in sectors such as health, protection, food security, and WASH. At the SAFE – WG level in Nigeria, we the sector members have adopted ‘The Global Plan of Action for Sustainable Energy Solutions in Situations of Displacement (GPA)’ agrees to most of the identified challenges of access to SAFE in emergency situations as follows; (a) Energy is not a formal priority in humanitarian assistance. (b) Displaced people are often not included in national or international energy access agendas (c) Energy in displacement settings is under-funded (d). Activities

Some fo the key activities to be implemented by GREENCODE are;1.1. Community mobilisation for target communities and camps in Dikwa and Bama LGAs1.2. Baseline and Endline Surveys on KAP on SAFE1.3. Training of 200 persons in Dikwa and Bama LGAs to be empowered with skills in construction of fuel-efficient Cook stoves for domestic cooking and gradually generating income from future construction of cook stoves for other members of the communities.1.4. Construction and distribution of 2,000 fuel-efficient” Cook stoves to 2,000 HHs beneficiaries in IDPs in camps and host community in Dikwa and Bama LGAs of Borno.1.5. Procurement and distribution of 700 simple plug and play Solar kit lamps to 700HHs and demonstration on the use of solar dryers as means of promoting alternative source of energy in Dikwa and Bama LGAs.1.6. Routine/Post Distribution Monitoring and Evaluation of project activities1.7. Conducting community outreach on GBV & Mine Awareness education in project communities1.8 Project documentation and reporting.
