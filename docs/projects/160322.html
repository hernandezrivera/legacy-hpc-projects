Project Summary

CMD plans to implement minimum package of Integrating Out Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM without medical complications. This project plans to integrate Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting the 9 OTP sites and 2 SC’s within Northern and Southern Ayod (Including Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Kandak, Mogok, Jiech, Gorwai, Padek, Haat, and Kharmun) where CMD has existing health facilities while supporting Pagil and Gorwai SCs to treat SAM children with medical complications. In Yirol West, CMD will support 6 OTPs in Malek, Tinagau, Adior, Lekakedu, Yali and Pagarau payams and 1 SC in Adior while in Kapoeta East, activities will be integrated within the ongoing Health response to support 6 OTP sites (Narus,Kauto, Katadori, Lotimo, Mogos, Nating) and 1 SC in Mogos . 3,054 SAM, 9,419 MAM and 6,540 PLW’s (Ayod-SAM 1,002, MAM-4,629 PLW-1,966), (Yirol West-SAM 760, MAM 2,573, PLW 1,407) and (Kapoeta East- SAM 1,292, MAM 2,217, PLW 3,167) are planned.
The project plans to work in collaboration with MoH/CHD, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralisation of OTP and SC sites, increasing awareness through community mobilisation on: IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition, socio-mobilisation for vitamin A supplementation, deworming and advocacy for the proper use of LLITN.
With UNICEF and WFP collaboration, complemented support will enable CMD provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a SAM caseload of up to 3,054 (Including children with complicated severe acute cases) in need (< 5 years both boys and girls) and MAM caseload of up to 9,419 (<5 years) and PLW’s caseload of 6,540. CMD will train Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and Mother-to-Mother support groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM and Maternal Infant and young child feeding practices so as to provide quality nutrition services. Health staff and Nutrition staffs will be capacitated for integration of SAM and MAM management into health programmes especially on malaria screening and referral of severe cases to OTP and Stabilisation centres.
If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end of 2019, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Ayod, Kapoeta East and Yirol West remains critical due to a combination of dry spells, poor harvest, and heavy floods and past conflicts, one of the worst in South Sudan. The Counties are likely to fall under extreme Critical level (IPC Phase 5). Nutrition cluster target caseload indicates Yirol West has GAM rate of 18.4%, SAM rate of 1.8%, MAM rate 16.6% and prevalence of malnutrition in PLW is 35.2%. Ayod has GAM rate of 16.2%; SAM rate of 3.6%, MAM rate 12.6% and Prevalence of malnutrition PLW is 34.2%. In projection period of August to December 2019, and in the total absence of humanitarian food assistance, an estimated 353,498 people (67% of the population) will face Crisis acute food insecurity or worse, an increase of 8% from the 332,394 people (63% of the population) from the same period in 2018. With most of the population having lost their livelihood and with missed planting seasons consecutively for three seasons, coupled with insecurity, the host community and IDPs have been left food insecure and the malnutrition levels alarming. Lack of clean and safe water adequate for domestic and livelihoods use is directly linked to high malnutrition. Settlements within the locations are using extreme coping strategies such as only children eating, and spending days without food more than doubling between August (36%) and October (45%). CMD will help in providing Therapeutic foods for the Acute Malnourish children (SAM/MAM) Malnourish children under 5 years (<5-year children) and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLW’s) and treatment of Severe Acute Malnourish children with medical complications in Yirol West (Malek, Tinagau, Adior, Lekakedu, Yali and Pagarau payams). In Ayod county (Mogok,Pagil, Wau,Padek and Kuachdeng Payams) are planned. A lack of intervention will erode previous gains and worsen the nutrition situation and increase the effects of Acute malnutrition within the states.

2 - Activities

The project focus in reduction of morbidity and mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidents of most vulnerable 3,054 SAM, 9,419 MAM and 6,540 PLW’s (Ayod-SAM 1,002, MAM 4,629 PLW 1,966), (Yirol West-SAM 760, MAM 2,573, PLW 1,407) and (Kapoeta East- SAM 1,292, MAM 2,217, PLW 3,167). The Project will target 15 bomas in Ayod (Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Haat, Kandak, Mogok, Padek, Kharmun, Mhaar, Ayod town, Gorwai, Jiech, Wan-Machar, Kuachdeng and Nyawit.), 6 payams in Yirol West (Malek, Tinagau, Adior, Lekakedu, Yali and Pagarau payams).The project will support 2 SC’s ,15 OTPs/TSFP in Ayod, 6 OTPs/TSFP and 1 SC in Yirol West and 6 OTPs/TSFP and 1 SC in Kapoeta East in order to provide services to the affected population within and outside the villages of Ayod, Kapoeta East and Yirol West.Integration of nutrition into FSL, GBV, WASH and HEALTH will be encouraged through capacity building of the MTMSGS, CNV’s, Health and Nutrition staffs. Health and WASH education will be encouraged and shared to the caregivers/mothers during admissions at the OTP sites. The program will ensure all caregivers in the Stabilisation centres and OTP receive seeds and tools admission of SAM cases and will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening. The trained CNV’s will provide services through moving to the villages, screening, identify, passing messages on MIYCN to mothers, follow up of the recoveries and refer SAM cases to the OTP sites. Mother to Mother Support Groups trained will be able to provide messages on the important of IYCF to the caregivers/mothers at the facility level and community level. CMD Nutrition staffs and health staffs trained will provide quality services to the SAM cases within the OTP’s and SCs. During admission nutrition is integrated into health and so screening of malaria will be at the triage, MUAC, WHZ-score, referral of SAM cases who are severely malnourished is done within Health and Nutrition compound where CMD provides all these services initiate the same in the new established area. Conduct KPC surveys to evaluate the nutrition situation as well as behaviour change (including IYCF practices) at the community level, among IDPs, and host vulnerable populations. CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH, Health interventions in Ayod, mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and SCs run by CMD.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Selection of beneficiaries has been based on community composition; Host (53%) and IDPs (47%). The targeted counties host some of the largest number of IDPs in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equitoria states according to IOM DTM with majority of these female and children moving from Upper Nile, Unity and other parts of Jonglei. All activities will be designed according to cultural and society norms, considering the specific needs of IDPs. Access to services will be assessed and obstacles to equal access addressed. CMD staff has been trained on Gender and Accountability for Affected Persons in emergency. Vulnerability dimensions shall be considered during service delivery and beneficiaries accorded respect and treated with dignity.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project complements and supports activities of the state and National Ministry of Health, working in coordination with the County Health Department (CHDs) both for service delivery to the communities and reporting. The MOH provides oversight and through the MOH-driven Nutrition Information System (NIS) reports and information regarding activities within this plan will be relayed for state/national analysis and decision-making. Of particular interest, the project will work with the national and state ministry of Health to strengthen their participation in management and prevention of malnutrition.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project complements the National Ministry of Health objectives which also plays the oversight responsibility and works in collaboration with partners. This plan works in close consideration of IDP trends in the targeted counties, flagged as hosting some of the highest displaced persons in the country.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection objectives will be met through ensuring the project contributes in GBV safety and psychosocial well-being of all groups. A special focus of the project will be on the rights of the women, children and the elderly to be protected from the worst forms of physical and sexual violence, child abuse and child labour. The project will keep in perspective a rights-based approach to social protection and fight exclusion and discrimination against marginalized groups. This means the project will focus community dialogues on protection risk factors and GBV prevention measures through community gatherings and meetings that are socially inclusive and engaging local leadership. CMD takes a serious and proactive approach to ensure all of its activities are safe for community targets. This approach is outlined in its Beneficiary Protection Policy, which holds staff and volunteers to a high standard of responsibility for ensuring the safety and protection of the beneficiaries with whom they interact. The organization has a PSEA policy alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures apply at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. In addition, the project will promote implementation of activities that respect the environment and encourage local/inclusive procurement of equipment and materials. It will exercise constant awareness and sensitivity at the local level based on “Do no harm” principles. By focusing on inclusion and practical rights, it is expected that the project will contribute to social justice and social peace. Activities will be implemented where access can be ensured; and psychosocial support will be employed to provide appropriate care, protection and social integration.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

During and at the end of the project, CMD expects at least 85% of beneficiaries targeted to have recovered from MAM. Majority of the CNVs shall undergo different nutrition trainings to ensure knowledge sharing within the community. During project implementation, beneficiaries will be encouraged to consume locally available food and to cultivate short term maturing crops which are common food e.g sorghum, maize and groundnuts .The Organization also anticipates involvement of other partners i.e. UN-FAO and other agriculture partners to provide seeds and tools for the beneficiaries affected by conflict to improve the general nutrition status of communities in the targeted Payams. The beneficiaries will be informed of the entry and exit criteria as set to avoid protection related issues at the end of project. Nutrition education shall be taught at the distribution points to ensure beneficiaries understand the objective of the program. Referrals shall be made for Severe Acute Malnutrition cases to nearest health facility. Constant project monitoring with monthly and quarterly reports shall be provided to assess the success or gaps of project objectives. The project exit strategy is based on the project statement that is to build the communities’ capacity (including local volunteers such as CNVs and nutrition staffs) to #1) cope with recurrent shocks and stresses, #2) produce food through home-grown solutions to enhance household livelihood and #3) prevent and respond to harmful practices such as poor sanitation which trigger malnutrition. In this way the communities will be able to continue such programming in collaboration with other stakeholders such as the Ministry of Agriculture could take over various producer groups organized and established by the project) after the end of this project. Continuous assessment of the local capacity needs will ensure that the project activities are handed over to capable and knowledgeable persons.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The first working assumption of the project is that the political and security situation remains conducive to the project. The associated risk is that the security situation may deteriorate in a way that the continuation of the project is no longer possible. To mitigate against this likelihood, CMD will sensitize key stakeholders and capacitate them in a way that enhances the likelihood that the protection of vulnerable children will be improved, even in the absence of the project. There may also be a situation of difficulty for women to participate in the intervention. Their active participation in the social protection of vulnerable children and youth may be undermined and effectively undercut. CMD will engage in community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and importance of engaging women and girls. The project will not address issues which require changes in policies and legal frameworks.In this early recovery and transitional context, CMD’s life-saving activities will aim to encourage the return of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees, and to promote social cohesion among children. The project will focus primarily on IDPs, returnees and their hosts to encourage adaptation and integration. Ayod and Yirol West Counties are accessible by air from Juba and by road from Bor, Rumbek (South Sudan). All project locations remain safe for carrying out project activities. It is hoped this intervention will work as an incentive to draw children to safe spaces that offer additional support and support local authorities in service delivery.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CMD actively seeks the views of the affected populations to improve policy and programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in plans and shareholder dissatisfaction. 80% of CMD staff is recruited on project location among the affected population. In order to strengthen feedback mechanisms from communities CMD provides suggestion boxes in all CMD field offices to maximize on inputs from communities. Positive and negative feedback are incorporated into lessons learnt for project improvement. All CMD led assessments will have potential beneficiaries including the most affected/vulnerable taking part. We emphasize transparency and inclusion in project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. CMD has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions. Information will be made available to local communities in local languages through community mobile megaphone radios, forums such as church meetings amongst others. Staff, volunteers and consultants, both national and international, are provided with adequate and timely inductions, briefings, and clear reporting lines that promote positive organizational behaviors and enable staff to understand their responsibilities, work objectives, organizational values, accountability commitments, key policies and local context. CMD works with partners and other stakeholders to ensure the needs of the most vulnerable are addressed while upholding all standards of Do No Harm.
