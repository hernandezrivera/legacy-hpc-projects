Project Summary

The project contributes to the prevention of Severe Acute Malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) by for 178.600 beneficiaries across Borno and Yobe States. The action will ensure adequate food intake, diet diversification, early detection of SAM and orientation towards improved infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF).
In addition, the project intends to contribute to the nutrition sector of HRP for Nigeria by:
- Strengthen the quality and scale of preventative nutrition services for the most vulnerable
groups through supplementary feeding activities, appropriate infant and young child
feeding practices, micronutrient supplementation, and optimal maternal nutrition.
- Improve access to quality curative nutrition services through the most appropriate
modalities, systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished
cases in collaboration with the health sector to enhance sustainability.
- Reinforce appropriate coordination with other sectors and strengthen the situation
monitoring by undertaking joint assessments and analysis, while strengthening integrated
the response that mainstreams protection.
- Capacity building and training for MoH health workers in Yobe and Borno and
community mobilizers in all the target areas of Geidam, Yunusari, Gujba, Jakusko, Fika and Nguru LGAs in Yobe state and Kaga LGA in Borno State.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition remained a public health concern in North-East Nigeria and a key priority for emergency response. The nutrition situation shows that the prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under 5 years in most of the accessible areas of Borno and Yobe States is in the crisis phase, while those of Adamawa are in stress. In some inaccessible areas of Borno State, the prevalence of global acute malnutrition has reached very critical levels (Phase 4 and Phase 5). According to the FMS findings, the level of acute malnutrition among new arrivals from inaccessible areas for the month of October is Critical (Phase 4 IPC Acute Malnutrition Classification). The overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate was 22.0% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 9.0%. The high levels of acute malnutrition indicate an extremely stressed population including food insecurity, poor access to water and sanitation, and poor health conditions as the key underlying causes of acute malnutrition (Famine Monitoring System – October 2021 Monthly Bulletin Cadre Harmonisé Task Force on Inaccessible Areas). Infants between 6 and 17 months are 1.5 times more likely to be acutely malnourished than older peers (30 – 59 months). Children under 59 months are more vulnerable to shocks but also an indication of poor infant and young child feeding practices especially continued breastfeeding up to two years and poor complementary feeding. According to the FMS over half of the children are stunted (58.3%) and underweight (53.4%). The high stunting and underweight rates are a clear indication of a population that is chronically stressed with poor nutrition and repeated infection. Stunted children fall sick more often, miss opportunities to learn, perform less well in school, and grow up to be economically disadvantaged and are more likely to suffer from chronic diseases. Other nutrition sector data sources (such as the ETT screening) show that new arrivals from inaccessible areas are 5 times more likely to be acutely malnourished compared to those from accessible locations. This indicates the FMS data is highly probable and confirms the extremely poor nutritional status of the inaccessible population. Limited resources and funding that remain have been prioritized to support communities with the most severe needs, this means that many of those affected will not receive any food or nutrition support. The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including weak health infrastructures, poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; the prevalence of water-borne diseases, poor hygiene conditions and seasonal food insecurity. This worrisome trend is likely to place a heavy strain on already overstretched capacities and resources on the ground, including WASH services, due to the vicious cycle of malnutrition and diarrheal diseases, exacerbated by access constraints and insecurity. Women and children are the most affected and the displaced extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival. These groups continue to pay the price of the nutrition crises due to increased micronutrient deficiencies. If untreated, acute malnutrition could have debilitating consequences on the growth and development of infants and children, further exacerbating the already existing burden of malnutrition in the northeast.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The complexity of the current protracted humanitarian crisis has led theinternational community to realize that a silos approach is inefficient to respondto the needs of conflict-affected populations. As emphasized at the 2016 WorldHumanitarian Summit (WHS), it is of utmost urgency to mainstreamhumanitarian, development and peace realms into an integrated actioncapitalizing on their respective comparative advantages to successfully achievethe 2030 Agenda - Sustainable Development Goals. Indeed, there is worldwidegrowing recognition that there can be no peace without sustainabledevelopment and no sustainable development without peace. COOPI fullyembraces this "New Way of Working" and, in alignment with the HDP Nexus, it isproposing a project with a cross-cutting and inter-sectoral perspective whichsynergically combines life-saving humanitarian assistance with long-termdevelopment activities and peace-building dialogue sessions at communitylevel. This is based on the premise that to achieve long-lasting peacefulcoexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities, it is paramount toaddress immediate basic needs through a development lens which does notonly ensure early recovery but aims to strengthen beneficiaries' resilience toconflict-driven and climate stressors in the long-run. As a proof, besidespromoting the recovery of the main livelihoods and the rehabilitation of basicpublic infrastructures, COOPI simultaneously aims to provide skills-training foryouth and capacity-building sessions for LGAs departments as well as to carryout peace-mitigation and conflict-management dialogue sessions atcommunity level. By doing so, COOPI's goal complimentarily encompasses astrong contribution to social cohesion, economic development and self-reliantlivelihoods through a localization approach and state system's strengthening:both of them ensuring the sustainability of the action and thus an enduringpeace consolidation. To this purpose, COOPI relies on a broad variety ofpartners, widely ranging from LGAs departments and InternationalOrganizations to local associations and community leaders, all across the threeinterlinked sectors of humanitarian assistance, development and peacebuilding.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Mainstream protection into humanitarian assistance programs Protectionmainstreaming ensures that human rights and their promotion are central to eachintervention, laying the foundations for reconstruction and more equitable andsustainable development. In line with the international developments within thetheoretical framework, COOPI also ensures the full participation and involvement ofwomen, men, boys, and girls. Protection is mainstreaming in the key component of thedesign and the implementation of each step of the project. COOPI will closely monitorthe respect of its application. A strong sensitization will be carried out among thebeneficiaries. They will be informed of their right, and how to report any breach.Redemptions will be conducted in locations that are within easy reach for the targetbeneficiaries and during the daytime to ensure that beneficiaries get to their homes beforedark. The planning of project days and times will be done in a manner that will notinterfere much with other community daily activities and programs targeted atchildren, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and girls. Special considerations forpregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, children, and people with specific needswill be made to ensure that priority is given to them during distributions. The staff on-site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI staffinvolved in the project will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics andCOOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to signCOOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after thecompletion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will besensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition toreporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistleblower policy via email orhelpline phone number. In each LGA Protection Officer and one CRM assistant willbe available to gather and answer the questions of the members of thecommunities. They will fill a complaint book that will record all the complaints. Atevery step of the program, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries will be reminded that theyare free to use this platform to share information or complaints. A help and feedbackdesk and complaint boxes will also be set up at each distribution site for the peoplethat would like to remain anonymous. In order to reduce inter-communal tensions andfoster impartiality, COOPI will select beneficiaries according tovulnerability criteria only, not taking into consideration their status (Host communitiesand IDPs). Gender will be taken into account in the selection of the beneficiaries.However, the increased knowledge of the women regarding food security and nutritionwill empower them and might foster a more important role in the decision-makingamong the households. The Project Management Committees formed by COOPI in theprevious phase of the project has a strong woman representation and they willcontinue to be involved in the monitoring of the project. Gender and protection will betaken into account in the entire project implementation. The strong presence of womenin the project and their continuous protection will indulge COOPI to make sure theproject respects the safety, dignity, and integrity of the women, men, girls, andboys receiving the assistance. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of allbeneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender oppressive stereotypes.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI follows the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC)’s Accountability to Affected Populations Framework and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership’s (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management to set standards/benchmarks in its accountability approach to emergencies. COOPI already has in place a Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) with Community Feedback Assistants and Project Management Committees in alignment with guidance provided by Nutrition Sector, and donors. COOPI will promotes a comprehensive community participatory approach whereby community members are constantly involved in all the project phases from the need assessment, project design and following activities’ implementation. These include children, persons with disability, teachers, SBMC, key interest groups, nutrition committees, school management committees, protection committees, LGAs and other relevant stakeholders for all three sectors of intervention. COOPI will put in place a Compliant Response Mechanism (CRM) whereby beneficiaries can channel/express their grievances and seek redress. To this purpose, COOPI will employ dedicated Complaints Reporting Mechanism (CRM) Officers whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedbacks. Where the nature or the subject of the complaint exceeds his/her jurisdiction, the complaint will be referred to a designated member of COOPI’s Senior Management Team (SMT) to manage the complaint. Furthermore, all COOPI will set up complaint desks and suggestion boxes in areas that maintain anonymous usage and that are physically accessible to all during crucial activities to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and concerned community members. They will also provide Hotline Telephone contacts of the focal person (CRM Officer) to community members and posted in each community. All reported cases, as well as solutions, shall be recorded for future learning.
