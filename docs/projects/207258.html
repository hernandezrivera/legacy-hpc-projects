Project Summary

"Acted will implement this project under SCCCM sector titled "Providing Disaster Preparedness Response to Rohingya refugees in Kutupalong-Balukhali settlement, and Teknaf sub-district, Cox’s Bazar", aiming to strengthen response mechanism in the camps to effectively tackle fire hazards which is a regular occurrence.
The project will provide integrated training, awareness raising, and maintenance support to build capacities of Safety Unit Volunteers, camp residents, and teachers on fire safety and response. This will directly enable over 20,130 refugees and host communities to better prevent and respond to fire risks in the camps. Acted will implement a coordinated 'one camp approach'; under this framework: fire-fighting units scattered in the vast camp area work as a single unit to tackle disaster. Thanks to effective coordination mechanism, ‘One camp approach’ enables swift prevention and escalation of the risks. As the camps are vast, and Fire does not recognize boundaries or lines on a map and so providing an identical response across all camps ensures that when required volunteers can support each other and pool resources to extinguish any type of fire occurring in the camps.
The project will contribute to the priorities outlined in the 2023 HIP and 2023 JRP on enhancing the resilience and self-reliance of refugees and host communities in Cox's Bazar. In addition to capacity-building training, the program will also focus on the maintenance of firefighting equipment in all 33 camps, including Mobile Fire Fighting Unit (MFFFU) 3-Wheelers and Carry-wheely frame MFFUs, water pumps etc.

The breakdown of activities is as follows:
Activity 1:
a) Firefighting refresher training for all SMS volunteers (SUV’s/DMU’s), with 100 volunteers in each camp, totaling 3,300 volunteers (SUV’s/DMU’s). The training will include ‘Live Fire Training’ and the participants list will be provided by SMS agencies.
b) Fire prevention management training for household individuals, primarily those responsible for cooking, with 500 individuals in each camp, totaling 16,500 individuals across 33 camps including the host of adjacent camp area/union. The training will involve safe cooking practices, importance of female friendly cooking spaces, usage of wood or LPG as fuel, etc.
c) Training for learning center teachers on fire safety, water safety, and disaster awareness, with 10 teachers from 10 learning centers in each camp, totaling 330 teachers from 33 camps for 330 Learning centers. The teachers will use educational materials delivered by Acted to do the awareness to their enrolled students.
Activity 2:
a) Acted will maintain and service 50 UN-funded Mobile Fire Fighting Unit (MFFU) Vehicles (Three-wheelers), along with the water pumps (Carry and wheely frame MFFU).A dedicated service hub will be established in camp 9 with spare parts and a booking system. A well-equipped maintenance vehicle and experienced engineering services will be ensured, along with enhancing the capacity of firefighting volunteers regarding the equipment maintenance and usage.
Throughout the implementation of the project Acted will actively seek and engage all SMS partners present in the targeted 33 camps.Currently ACTED is a member of SAG. In 2024 acted will implement activities in close coordination with the RRRC office (CIC, ACIC), AOR focal points (UNHCR, IOM), SMS agencies, and relevant actors responsible for selecting targeted beneficiaries (Education focal for LC teacher training). Additionally, ACTED will consistently participate in SCCCM sector meetings at the Cox’s Bazar level, and at the ground level the project team will ensure participation in camp-level coordination meetings. The project will be executed through this coordinated mechanism. Also, for repairing fire-fighting equipment there is a dedicated Hotline number to raise the complaint related to MFFU’s and the technician team will respond accordingly to functioning the MFFU’s on a timely manner."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Needs Assessments conducted by Acted and other agencies in the camp show frequent fires have emerged as a major hazard in the congested camps in Cox's Bazar, causing loss of lives and properties due to gaps in coordination, awareness, training, fire safety knowledge and functional equipment to tackle fire accidents. Needs Assessments that are relevant to the interventions are:• Union Civil Protection Mechanism, Final report on Fire Safety Strategy, Rohingya Refugee Camps, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, 2022.• Technical Advisor of MOAS Dan Graham, Fire Investigation: Camp 4, Block F4, November 2022. • Joint Humanitarian Response to Fire incident in Rohingya refugee camps, ISCG, March 2021• Bangladesh humanitarian situation report- UNICEF, March 2022• 2023 Joint Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment• ECHO Humanitarian Intervention Plan (HIP) 2023• Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2023• Acted, ECHO Needs Assessment Disaster Preparedness (Fire response), 2023Needs Assessment conducted by ACTED in February 2023 demonstrates that the majority of the respondents (59.3%) reported that fires occur ""sometimes"" with 67.2% reporting having personally experienced a fire. These findings show a high level of sporadic fire accidents occur in the camps. More than one-third (72.78%) reportedly participated in fire awareness sessions conducted by various partners in the camps; however, One-third (66.8%) interviewees reported women of their family didn’t receive any awareness sessions under any partner. Similarly, about two-thirds of camp children (64.7%) never received any awareness training. Acted assessments also show while a significant number of fire accidents occur due to unsafe cooking practices as only 28.3% of the camp residents reportedly received fire safety training so far. About 40.7% lacked any training regarding cooking.According to the Joint Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment 2023, Shelter remains the second priority among the refugee population, with 33% indicating shelter gaps. In the camps shelter materials are easily flammable: plastic sheets and bamboo. Thus, the shelters are more prone to accidental fire incidents.Camp residents expressed the need of more knowledge regarding the matter. Most of the interviewees also expressed the need for more fire safety equipment’s, fire stands, awareness sessions, to effectively tackle fire hazards. However, the community lacks the understanding of SUV responsibility and mechanisms. The Joint Response Plan 2023 also identifies fire as one major issue to tackle regarding the frequency of fire accidents in the camps. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project promotes an integrated approach by bringing together specialized capacities of ACTED to implement standardized trainings, awareness raising and equipment maintenance across all camps. This unified approach aligns with the ‘one camp’ principle to provide uniform services. Through trainings and capacity building, the project also ensures continuity and sustainability by equipping communities to self-manage fire preparedness.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"ACTED places great importance on mainstreaming protection in all of its programming. For the proposed disaster preparedness and response project, protection principles and approaches will be integrated throughout the project cycle and across key activities.Community participation and feedback mechanisms will be prioritized to ensure the unique needs and rights of vulnerable groups are addressed. During the initial needs assessment, interviews with refugees members were conducted to understand fire-related protection risks. This analysis helped identify those requiring targeted support, such as female and persons with disabilities who may face barriers to accessing information or assistance. To promote safety and reduce vulnerabilities, fire safety trainings will be conducted separately for SMS volunteers, different age groups and gender to account for cultural sensitivities. Individuals like women will be trained on operating firefighting equipment to build self-reliance. ACTED’s CFM along with the SMS CFM desks and UNHCR and UNWFP hotline numbers will allow anonymous and non-anonymous feedback to continually improve the response.Community volunteering opportunities under the project empower refugees by leveraging their existing social structures for outreach and awareness activities. Campaign materials will be developed with refugee input and in local languages to ensure equitable access to information about rights and available services.Through this integrated approach, ACTED aims to mainstream protection considerations into all aspects of project cycle from design to implementation to monitoring through active community participation and collaborative partnerships. Ensuring a coherent approach to addressing safety, dignity and rights of fire-affected populations is a key priority."

3.3 - Age & Disability

"The project will mainstream age and disability inclusion through a thorough needs assessment process and inclusive project design. The needs assessment will identify the specific protection risks, vulnerabilities and needs of different groups within the target population through consultations with community members representing a diversity of ages, genders and abilities. This will provide important insights into how to best design activities and target services.Relevant beneficiary data captured through monitoring tools will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability, with the information fed into an interactive dashboard. This will help project staff evaluate whether any gaps exist in achieving an equitable response and adjust their approach accordingly.Activities like fire safety awareness sessions and training will be carefully planned and implemented using participatory methods respectful of cultural dynamics, to promote inclusion of all ages, genders and abilities. Accessibility measures will further support participation by persons with disabilities.Selection prioritization for any assistance provided will factor in vulnerability levels determined through a scoring system that considers age, gender and disability.Feedback from the refugee community itself will be continually sought at each phase of the project cycle to ensure their diverse needs, views and strategies for coping with risks are fully reflected.Moreover, learning center teachers trained in fire safety and water safety by ACTED will impart this knowledge to their students as part of regular class time. This expands outreach, especially among children as a vulnerable group.Through applying these inclusion mainstreaming efforts systematically and comprehensively, the project aims to equitably deliver fire risk reduction activities and services benefiting all segments of the refugee population."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project will demonstrate strong compliance with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments through meaningful access, participation and involvement of beneficiaries at different stages of the project cycle.The project design is informed through a needs assessment conducted directly with camp residents and Safety Unit Volunteers, ensuring their views and priorities are captured. Both men and women were consulted through focus group discussions and interviews using Kobo tool for data collection in a confidential manner.The project includes participation of beneficiaries in implementation through community awareness sessions, trainings on fire safety and drills. Engaging volunteers from local communities demonstrates cooperation with camp leadership structures. Safety Unit Volunteers, who are Rohingya refugees themselves, will be key actors in conducting fire response activities.The project will establish functional feedback and complaint mechanisms both at central and camp level. The internal MEAL system adheres to AAP principles. Baseline, Midline and endline surveys will measure effectiveness directly from the perspectives of beneficiaries. Outcome indicators will be tracked through disaggregated data. Lessons from assessments and feedback will be documented for future project improvements.Regular meetings with broader humanitarian community including UN agencies, local authorities and partners will promote upward and downward accountability. Joint response drills and training with different actors indicates responsiveness to accountability of all stakeholders involved in emergency services. "
