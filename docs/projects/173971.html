Project Summary

Abrupt school closures on March 13 and then again November 26 caused by the global COVID-19 pandemic have forced 42 million learners in Pakistan out of school and heavily disrupted their learning. In a country with an already existing large number of out of school children, prolonged school closures and loss of learning will have a detrimental effect on enrolment, learning, retention, and dropout rates in Pakistan. A youth perception study report recently conducted in Pakistan with support from UNICEF indicated that young people are concerned that economic distress because of COVID 19 will likely force more of them into (child) labour and early marriage. In addition, constrained access to regular activities such as going to school and playing has a negative impact on children’s opportunities to socialize and play with their peers and physical contact, all which is critical for their psychosocial wellbeing.

There is growing consensus that school closures combined with increased economic stress on families will widen learning inequalities and disproportionately harm vulnerable children and youth, especially girls. The experience of past shutdowns reveals a high risk of students, especially girls, dropping out of education – once they are out of school for extended periods of time, there is a risk that they will not return. As school closures forced children to stay at home and boys have had more access to education opportunities compared to girls who have been involved in household and child-care activities. “Girls’ education and COVID-19 in Pakistan”, a report commissioned by Malala Funds outlined gender disparities amongst boys and girls while they spent time at home during school closures. According to the report, 40 per cents girls reported being involved in household chores compared to 11 per cent of boys. The report also highlighted that girls found it more difficult to access distance learning opportunities and electronic devices during school closures compared to boys.

In the first wave of COVID-19, UNICEF supported coordination mechanisms both at federal and provincial level to ensure oversight and monitoring of the response. UNICEF directly reached out to School Management Committees (SMC) members, teachers and education personnel with COVID-19 prevention messages via SMS, robocall and social media. SMCs were trained on safe school reopening and teachers on safe school reopening and mental health and psychosocial support. As part of continuity of learning, UNICEF provided access to learning opportunities to the most vulnerable children and adolescents through supporting distance learning modalities such as sharing of learning curriculum-based video lessons, take home material and ‘catch up’ learning interventions.

To mitigate the impacts of the pandemic for the most vulnerable, UNICEF will continue to support the Education departments in all the four provinces with continuity of learning, safe return to schools and ensuring adherence to the Standard Operating Procedures for children in formal schools as well as in Alternative Learning Programmes (ALPs). As a sector lead organization UNICEF will also be supporting training of sector members on humanitarian response, coordination and planning for the education sector. UNICEF Pakistan prioritizes supporting 535,500 beneficiaries including 512,00 children (307,200 girls), 8,500 teachers (5,100 female teachers) and 15,000 SMC members (9,000 female members) in humanitarian response to COVID-19. If schools remain closed, children and adolescents need access to remote learning opportunities - including low-tech opportunities as radio or no-tech opportunities as take- home learning packages for those who do not have access to digital learning solutions. As schools reopen, safe opening protocols will need to be implemented and monitored, including collaboration with communities, SMCs, teachers and students to ensure a safe and accessible learning environment in schools.

1- Project Activities

UNICEF is a key stakeholder in supporting the COVID-19 response led by the Federal Ministry of Education (MoE) and the provincial education departments. UNICEF plans to support all the four provinces with coordination support, continuity of learning, safe schools reopening and adherence of the government’s Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) in targeted formal schools and Alternative Learning Programmes (ALPs) in the COVID-19 hotspot districts. UNICEF will directly target the most vulnerable within COVID-19 high risk districts. When schools are closed, focus will be to support home-based learning opportunities, especially for the most vulnerable children. UNICEF will support distance learning modalities such as radio broadcasts and Ministry of Education distance learning programme tele school. Given the digital divide and equity issues UNICEF will also support development and distribution for take home learning packs, including supplementary material and guidelines for teachers. Education officials and teachers will be trained on alternative teaching modalities to support home-based learning. Communities and teachers will need to be engaged to ensure that home-based learning opportunities are encouraged and adopted and to ensure continued commitment for girls’ education. Apart from formal schools UNICEF will continue to support non-formal education sector by focusing on Accelerated Learning Programme (ALPs) for older out of school adolescents, especially girls to continue their learning during closures. The ALP programme in UNICEF has been specifically designed to address issues of out of school adolescents especially the girl child and to provide them an alternative learning pathway to connect back and complete their education. As schools and alternative learning centers reopen, UNICEF will continue to support safe school operations, including implementation and monitoring of SOPs to ensure children have access to a safe learning environment. With a focus on girls’ schools, all targeted schools will be provided with a package of activities including teacher trainings on safe school reopening, remedial education, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) in coordination with Child Protection sector and school safety measures and provision of hygiene packages, waste management and WASH facilities in collaboration with WASH sector. Active participation of students, teachers and School Management Committees (SMCs) is critical in ensuring safety of children and learning facilities and implementation of SoPs in schools. Critical information on disease prevention will continue to be circulated in communities using the existing community platforms such as SMCs, Mothers groups and Parent teacher associations (PTAs). Communities and PTAs/SMCs will be engaged in back-to-school campaigns and effective school management, including safe school operations. UNICEF will ensure the implementation of the school level SoPs to also be enforced in the community-based ALPs as well as provision of Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and WASH facilities and teacher training activities for all its supported formal schools and ALP centers across the country. UNICEF as sector lead will also be training humanitarian sector partners in training for its partners on coordination, planning and response. Below are some key activities that will be undertaken •Training of education sector members on planning, implementation and monitoring of response • Ensure continuity of learning of children by supporting take home worksheets, accelerated learning with focus on adolescent girls, distance learning (tele school, Whatsapp videos, radio) • Provide teacher training on pedagogy, MHPSS, alternate teaching, life skills, handwashing and safe reopening to education managers, head/ teachers. • Implementation of safe school operation protocols and adherence to SoPs by development and distribution of risk communication materials and safe school guidelines.
