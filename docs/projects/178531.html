Project Summary

BINAA established two camps in Idlib (Haranbush, Kafr Nabi), and one camp in Aleppo (Bhorta) in 2020. as well as established protection help desks to support vulnerable children, women & girls at risk groups and affected population to easily access available services within the targeted areas. and BINAA seeks to continue the protection activities (GP,GBV, CP) in the 3 mentioned camps ( Haranbush, Kafr Nabi, Bhorta), nearby the targeted camps, and communities.
the project is designed to integrated protection services (GP,GBV,CP) in 3 targeted sites. through providing quality protection services, with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, community initiatives, through help desks and outreach mechanisms for 1400 BNFs( 600 W,800 M).
The project is designed to improve access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and W & G at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV through providing referral services to GBV related, medical , and specialized PSS services according to the referral pathway mechanism which provide by GBV SC, plus BINAA's Protection officers will develope the GBV service mapping referral pathways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on ground.
Training of 24 GBV workers in line with GBV SC (GBV SOPs, PSS, Women empowerment and CM) and participation in the training organized by the GBV SC when available to ensure alignment with SC GBV standards.
And enhance strategies to empower women and girls, with a particular focus on most at risk groups (adolescent girls, divorced and widowed W&Gs, W&Gs with disabilities and older women) to prevent GBV and to change negative attitudes and believes that foster harmful social norms,and COVID-19 awareness raising interventions (including on associated GBV risks and GBV key messages)for 4,000 BNFs(1000 M,2050 W, 300 B,650 G) at 3 sites.
As well as promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response through training 40 non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV risk mitigation according to (IASC GBV guidelines, GBV SOPs).Development GBV safety audits in the camps and establishment focal points (IASC GBV guidelines).
the project is designed to increased and more equitable access for 7417 BNFs,4600 Adults(2700W,1900 M), 2596 children( 1090 Boys,1506 girls) to quality child protection interventions in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action, through Provide community-based child protection by engaging 2500 children in structured, sustained child protection programs, and 200 (120 W,80 M) in parenting programs, awareness-raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues for 4600 Adults, providing specialized child protection services, case management for 96 (G,B) and Referral of child protection cases to specialized services (child protection needs) according to the referral pathway mechanism which provide by CP SC, plus BINAA's Protection officers will develop the CP service mapping referral path ways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on ground. Integrating of PwDs with the community by specialist for PwDs and integrate them into activities where the centers are on flat ground, surrounded by a fence, the entrance is paved, and all the camps are paved, and with special latrines' with hand railing and ramp. Training of 20 CP workers in line with child protection minimum standards.
The implementing activities will be according to Operational guidance note for GP, CP, and GBV Activities in COVID 19.
As BINAA will take into consideration all precautions and procedures related to protection from COVID-19 epidemic following the clusters guidelines and instructions including wearing face masks, using hand sanitizers and social distancing.

4 - Needs

In NW Syria, increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs remain high in camps, collective shelters and protracted displacement. Where HNO 2021 estimated 13.13m protection and GBV AoR PiN 19% of them 2,421,929 (1,214,938 M,1,206,991 W)PiN still in acute need in Idleb and 21% ,2,667,766 (1,311,977 M,1,355,789 W) in Aleppo.Older persons and PWD are often at heightened risk of forms of violence, abuse, neglect or exploitation.Additionaly,lack or loss of civil documentation was reported as an issue by 61%, 50 %of assessed communities reported HLP concerns. Children comprise almost half of the affected population 5.913m children (51.6% B,48.4%G) grave child rights violations remain a critical concern .CPM findings in MaaretTamsrin, 90.41% of KII reported that children in need of focused non specialized services,42.25%of KII reported that children in need of specialized services.Protection risks, especially GBV continue their disproportionate impact on women and girls. through different forms of (GBV), especially early marriage, intimate partner and family violence, sexual harassment and sexual violence (including rape). Multiple protection risks continue to threaten the ability of people with disabilities to engage in basic activities of daily living.whereas “The participants in Idleb IDP camps stressed the need and importance of the child protection and women's protection centers, which acted as a breathing space for women and children to unload and try to integrate and adapt to the surrounding environment”.Over 2020, GBV actors are only able to reach 8% (589 out of 7592) of communities, while 92% of communities still do not have access to specialised GBV services to support girls and women.(Voices of Syria 2021)

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

BINAA is a member of the PSEA network and align the PSEA intervention with the PSEA network guidance.As (PSEA) is in the core of BINAA responses and all BINAA staff must comply with. BINAA has a policy of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation. BINAA field team is trained on communication approaches, i.e. how to present the program transparently in a way that mitigates conflict, anticipating perceptions of unfairness or other issues that could create conflict, and in managing expectations. Risk1-the population, including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in the BINAA being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims”Likelihood:Medium. Impact: High.mitigate SEA Risks:Sensitise community about PSEA and how to report misconduct according to PSEA awareness materials according to PSEA network dataset.Risk2-Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services.Likelihood:Medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: -integrating PSEA in BINAA policies andmaking sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA. -Regularly train staff on PSEA policy-. - BINAA will ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse are accessible (Complaints boxes, WhatsApp, email) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: CP activities in the targeted camps will be are accepted by the community structures and will not conflict with CP principles.Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles.Risk mitigation:1. regular review of context and conflict patterns as part of considerations in programming.2.Community inclusion and consultation in all project phases to achieve community acceptance and required knowledge and awareness of the project.Assumptions: - Locations where the project activities will be conducted are secure and accessible. - Materials/labor are/is available. Risks: 1. Losing Access to targeted location Interference from armed groups or the local councils outside the context. BINAA will mitigate the risk of access limitation through: • Regular monitoring for INSO security and incidents reports. • If it’s during the implementation, it may be necessary to develop several different ‘exit scenarios’ according to GBV SC ethical closure of GBV activities as mitigating measure for GBV programming. will ensure continuous updating of the GBV exited Plan as needed. Assumptions: • Targeted communities are accessible. • Projectactivities are accepted in the targeted communities. Risks: • Targeted communities are not accessible • Project activities are not accepted in the community due to context and culture. Risk mitigation: • Targeted communities were selected amongcommunities that are far from frontlines.• Continuous coordination with active actors on the ground security update groups and protection cluster and sub clusters team to avoid overlapping.• Implementing security and safety measures and risk assessments on timely manner during project period.
