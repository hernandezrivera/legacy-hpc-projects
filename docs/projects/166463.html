Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims at protecting the most vulnerable children and their families during the COVID-19 crisis, helping the families maintain essential investments on child wellbeing as they navigate through the post-emergency financial crisis, and supporting inclusive and sustainable recovery of the families to ensure children can survive, learn and be protected to thrive. With aim of reducing challenges relating to lack of access to food, non-food items (NFIs) and negative copping strategies, SC plans an unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) of monthly 5,500 BDT per household up to 4 months that will enable the vulnerable population to purchase essential commodities like fresh food, medicine and other necessaries from local stores. SC also includes activities, such as, micro-nutrition gardens, NFIs and livelihoods opportunities to create self-sufficiency. SC ensures active engagement and participation of women, men, youth and children (boys and girls). SC acknowledges within the targeted beneficiaries the different needs of women/girls and men/boys, analyzes these needs within the needs assessment and integrates these needs into the planned activities and outcomes.

Needs

In Cox’s Bazar, the Rohingya refugee crisis profoundly affected the livelihoods of host and other communities. Most of the UN agencies, NGOs and INGOs are implementing largescale programmes in Ukhia and Teknaf, while the other parts of Cox’s Bazar are deprived of receiving humanitarian services. Agriculture is the primary source of livelihoods in Cox’s Bazar, and fishing is another critical source of livelihoods. Compared to the overall situation in Bangladesh, Cox’s Bazar has much lower employment opportunities. Wages in Cox’s Bazar are just below the national average. This probably reflects the lack of jobs and possibly of rural farm and non-farm employment opportunities. Cox’s Bazar is also characterized by high prevalence of stunting and moderate and severe underweight malnutrition among children (BBS, 2015c). This is caused by food shortages and food insecurity as well as unplanned pregnancies. The situation in Cox’s Bazar is comparably challenging. While socio-economic impacts on Cox’s Bazar due to Rohingya refugees are multi-dimensional, COVID-19 also threatens to exacerbate the already dire conditions for low-income communities. Limited livelihoods options, due to lockdown compounded by limited access to food, may lead the vulnerable groups to increased indebtedness and negative coping mechanisms. Supporting most vulnerable groups with appropriate cash assistance is urgently needed to ensure that they can meet minimum essential needs, measured in terms of the per capita Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). Considerable number of people have no or limited livelihoods options, and the increasing vulnerability to food insecurity and debt exposure may lead them to negative coping mechanisms. Local families need access to more diverse and nutritious food. There needs to be a shifting from relief assistance to self-reliance, and from immediate needs to enhancing resilience. A labor market survey was conducted in Cox’s Bazar from 7-18 July 2019, where among the youth respondents, 35% are girls and 65% boys; and age range is 15-26 years. The labor market survey revealed that youth are the most deprived, and they face high levels of unemployment. As such, anti-social activities and behavior have increased because of the pressure. Besides, these vulnerable groups living in Cox’s Bazar are susceptible to seasonal to annual vulnerabilities considering their weak adaptive capacities in terms of economic and social perspectives to tackle. They require immediate supports to withstand to the impacts of COVID-19, and mid-term supports to help transition to income generation that will ultimately ensure resilience to the communities. There is a need for skills development training and income generating activities to create employment opportunities and small enterprises for the youth.

Activities or outputs

• Distributing cash through feasible modalities – mobile financial services, bank transfers or direct • Providing brief orientations to the beneficiaries on proper use of cash • Providing orientations on homestead gardening • Distributing seeds, fertilizers and other inputs • Developing IEC/BCC materials and promoting messaging and campaigns on social distancing and hygiene practices considering COVID-19 prevention and control• Revising and contextualizing specific curriculum and lesson plan• Providing skills training through online or distance modality in partnership I/NGOs• Distributing necessary equipment and materials for each trade• Providing education allowance for each beneficiary (BDT 100 per day)• Providing soft skills training and Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) training• Facilitating job linkage and micro-entrepreneurship development• Using Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRMs)
