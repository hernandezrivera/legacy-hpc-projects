Project Summary

Palestinians in the West Bank are subject to a coercive environment by the ongoing occupation and settlement activity, resulting in a wide range of humanitarian needs, further exacerbated by the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2020, demolition of Palestinian structures recorded the highest average since 2009, along with recurrent settler violence, child detention and risk of forcible transfer, against IHL and Human Rights, with limited accountability. The recent May 2021 escalations in the West Bank and Gaza Strip further exacerbated the situation of protracted violence and increased the protection needs of Palestinians living in vulnerable communities. The different measures taken to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic had a clear impact on population, worsening the MH and developing symptoms of different problems (anxiety, stress, …). MdM-CH & YMCA work together to increase the quality of the MHPSS services provided to Palestinian population, building on previous individual experience (MdM: MHPSS support to ex-detained children and their families, peer-to-peer education and developing support groups in areas considered as most vulnerable. YMCA: academic rehabilitation for students who released from Israeli detention, vocational training or alternative education for children not able to return to school); both in developmental components and in Humanitarian Aid reinforcing MHPSS protection response to critical political violence related incidents, increasing resilience to IHL violations and engaging in evidence-based humanitarian advocacy.
This action will improve protection response while enhancing effective coordination with other humanitarian actors- especially within the WBPC, CBOs and local authorities, ensuring participation of beneficiaries and mainstreaming of gender-age approach, as well as considerations to PWD protection needs. The action comes as a continuation to MdM's work on MHPSS-ER in the West Bank under the PUI technical supervision for the WBPC. It further elaborates on the WBPC efforts by responding to ALL occupation-related incidents including excessive use of force by ISF, detention, and house arrest...etc.(not only demolition and settler violence), and by coordinating with WBPC members. Based on the needs analysis and expertise, this action is aimed at preventing, reducing, mitigating and responding to psychosocial risks and consequences of IHL violations in the West Bank, taking into account harmful coping mechanisms, gender inequality, specific vulnerabilities of children and adolescents, and accountability of duty bearers, through the following results in coordination with WBPC:
1. Reinforced access of victims of occupation-related critical incident to MHPSS Emergency Response and protection case management
2. Individuals, community and CBOs psychosocial coping mechanisms and resilience capacities are increased to face the coercive environment
3. Mobilize the public on the impact of critical incidents on Palestinian community through public awareness, evidence based operational advocacy initiatives .

1 - Needs

Palestinians live in a permanent state of extreme tension due largely to the encroachment by Israeli settlements. Palestinians are subjected to military occupation, as well as to generalized poverty and lack of services. In addition, the current C19 pandemic and its multiple collateral impacts are highly affecting an already fragile and fragmented system. According to the UNICEF - NIAF report 2020: Children during C19 have been totally disconnected and isolated with no access to services and recreational activities. Results also acknowledge how manifestations of mental health and psychosocial well-being are strongly influenced by multiple context-specific factors. This study also showed that during the C19 pandemic, children and caregivers have overwhelmingly been affected by negative emotions. Adults resorted to negative coping mechanisms including aggressive behaviors towards children. Economically, the PA’s fragility remains unchanged and has even worsened. Surrounded and controlled by a strong military presence, Palestinians are submitted to regular requisitions at checkpoints, violence, and random limitations in movement and access permissions, destruction of crops and other livelihood means, repeated home demolitions, and the revocation of their residency rights in EJ. These communities face also daily violence and harassment from settlers, intensified settlement activity in EJ, H2 of Hebron, and the WB’s Area C. Uprooting of trees and limited control over natural resources contribute to environmental degradation and hinders socioeconomic development. During this past year, MdM’s activities allowed the organization to also bear witness to a clear increase in the number of critical incidents. In total, these challenges generate a web of complex protection threats to women and girls, including heightened risks of GBV, intimate partner violence, sexual abuse, and forced and early marriage (UN WOMEN-Gender alert). Further, child detention remains an issue of high concern given the fragility of children who undergo such traumatizing experience; Israel maintains its child detention measure all over the WB, as well as its house arrest policy in EJ. According to the Human Rights Watch, as of 31 August 2021, there were 4,207 Palestinians held as “security prisoners” in detention facilities including 153 children (12-17 years). According to the IPS, 77% of child detainees were forcibly transferred and/or unlawfully detained in Israel in violation of Article 76 of the Fourth Geneva Convention. MdM-CH and YMCA, since November 2018, have worked in the southern governorates of the WB (Bethlehem, EJ, and Hebron) to alleviate the impact of occupation-related incidents under OCHA HF 2018, and the ECHO 2020 WBPC. As a result of this presence- and as part of MdM-CH’s standard project cycle management procedures, monitoring, evaluation- SWOT analyses were regularly conducted. An emergency needs assessment and risk analysis update were conducted, alongside a lessons-learned exercise, following escalations in Essawiyeh village during May-June 2020. Moreover, the results of MdM-CH’s community surveys and the results of its regular Beneficiary Satisfaction Surveys (BSS) for MHPSS Emergency Response (ER), have informed the design and strategy of this proposed response plan. The project targets the cluster’s geographic priority areas after a thorough review of needs assessments conducted by different clusters and/or other partners, consulting academic papers, research and studies at national and international level in order to take informed decisions and propose responses based on MdM-CH’s and partners’ added value. As a result of all the above we would like to emphasize the following key findings: According to UN reporting, the first quarter of 2021 Israeli authorities have seized and demolished Palestinian-owned structures with a 121% increase compared to the same period last year. In EJ, communities have been witnessing an alarming escalation in violence, fueled by Israeli practices over this year 2021. Findings of needs assessment, which documented grave violations against children (85% of the surveyed sample indicating harassment by ISF as one of the major concern affecting children’s access to education). YMCA and MdM-CH’s past experience in Hebron Governorate has enabled both organizations to identify efficiently the particularities of the area. The current activities and involvement of the partner organization YMCA with UNICEF and Save the children in H2 of Hebron have allowed us to design a needs-based response where complementarity with existing projects is guaranteed at all stages of implementation. WHO & Juzoor 2021 study on adolescents revealed significantly elevated rates of psychological distress, compared to before the pandemic (60% increase). Moreover, MdM-CH as medical organization involved in the national C19 country response plan through other projects will ensure that C19 SOP’s and M&E of hygiene and safety measures is respected and promoted. Finally, this project seeks addressing MHPSS impacts of political violence and PWDs to promote psychosocial wellbeing and coping mechanisms, offer them with services facilitating their access to opportunities and rights as well as promoting an inductive and supportive environment through targeting their caregivers and concerned actors as well as the community as a whole to further facilitate their reintegration, protect their rights and timely address their needs. Gender and disability are considered as interrelatedness of factors for vulnerability of children in Palestine as mentioned in the NIAF C19 report. In cases of violence, ignorance, and other limitations children with disability and girls are the most affected by injustice.

2 - Activities

In coordination with WBPC members, the project aims to conduct the following activities:►MHPSS Emergency Response (ER) to Critical Occupation-related Incidents ► MHPSS ER mobile units will be activated upon the reception of OCHA or community/ media triggers for different types of Settler violence, demolition and ISF excessive use of force incidents. ► Subsequently, an initial visit to the survivors within the first 72 hours after crosschecking the critical event with the local community will take place. During this initial visit, Psychological First Aid (PFA) will be provided, if needed, with beneficiary consent. This screening will aim at detecting mental health and psychosocial wellbeing issues and other related concerns resulting from the critical event or pre-existing (but exacerbated). PFA & screening will follow MdM Clinical SOPs, based on WHO PFA guidelines (IASC Pyramid Level 3 Focused, person-to-person, non-specialized support).. ► Persons in-need are referred to other actors for different types of protection services (shelter, livelihood, financial assistance, medical, WASH, advanced MHPSS). IASC Pyramid Level 1 Social Considerations in Basic Services & security. ► 10-15 days after the initial visit, MHPSS ER mobile units will conduct a second visit with prior beneficiary consent to further follow up on emerging MHPSS needs resulting or intensified by the critical incident/s. The second visit will include a complementary individual assessment catered to survivors, further detecting MHPSS-specific individual and family needs. At this stage, the assessment will be more comprehensive, as the persons' and families' initial distress might have subsided (IASC Pyramid Level 3 focused, person-to-person, non-specialized support). ► Based on the results of the in-depth clinical assessment of the second visit, beneficiaries provide consent to receive: Problem Management (PM+) sessions, individual or group counseling, referral to advanced mental health services. These are conducted following MdM clinical SOP for MHPSS ER. Psychosocial coping mechanisms and resilience capacities to face the coercive environment (IASC Pyramid Level 3 focused, person-to-person, non-specialized support) ► Group & community psycho-educational sessions on the impacts of traumatic events on mental health and connection to available resources. These sessions address occupation-related critical incident impacts on women, men, girls and boys and the potential ways of coping with them (IASC Pyramid Level 2 Strengthening Community & Family Supports) ► MHPSS peer support groups in recurrent exposure locations to critical incidents to mitigate the impact of violence and coercive environment. A special emphasis will be given to the most vulnerable beneficiaries namely women, children and/or youth for high risk of detention in the most affected and uncovered areas (IASC Pyramid Level 2 Strengthening Community & Family Supports) ► Individual psychosocial counseling for ex-detainee children: each child shall receive 12 individual counseling sessions (IASC Pyramid Level 4 Specialized Services) ► Group psychosocial counseling for ex-detainee children: each group engages 8 children and receives over 10 sessions (IASC Pyramid Level 4 Specialized Services) ► Family counseling and PSS support for caregivers and family members of ex-detainee children: this is individual psychosocial support where each beneficiary receives 4 sessions (IASC Pyramid Level 4 Specialized Services) ► Group psychosocial counseling for caregivers of ex-detainee children: each group engages 8 participants- usually the mothers- and receives over 8 sessions(IASC Pyramid Level 4 Specialized Services) ► Academic Rehabilitation: beneficiaries in need for this intervention shall be referred to academic centre or tutor to receive remedial education/ catch up classes to enable their reintegration in schools (IASC Pyramid Level 2 Strengthening Community & Family Supports) ► Vocational rehabilitation: This only includes beneficiary children 15+ who fail to get back into the academic path. Campaigning and awareness raising at community level on the importance of MHPSS with a specific gender focus (IASC Pyramid Level 2 Strengthening Community & Family Supports) ► Use data collected in the field for designing and implementing a campaigning and developing awareness raising materials for MHPSS promotion at a community level in the targeted areas, with a specific focus on the protection of mental health with a gender component (IASC Pyramid Level 1Social Considerations in Basic Services & security) ► Create social awareness on the impact of political violence incidents on mental health, and reduce stigma in mental health. Reduction of taboo in relation to gender violence, sexual harassment and/or child abuse. This will be achieved through: - Production of a billboard campaign in the targeted areas -Production of a social media campaign on partner SM pages -Production of a leaflet to be distributed in target areas -Beneficiary Satisfaction Survey & testimonies (IASC Pyramid Level 1 Social Considerations in Basic Services & security).In addition to the guidelines mentioned above, other documents are considered: MHPSS/CP AOR service directory, MdM internal services directory (Hebron and Nablus governorate), YMCA mapping database. Data are kept in a confidential manner (anonymized data storage) and consent forms are used before the analysis of the information.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM-CH’s Feedback and complaints mechanism is in place as part of the M&E process, in the form of a “letter box,” telephone number, and email address (rules of conduct for abusive behavior as part of MdM prevention alert and management standardized system since 2018). Feedback & complaint channels are handled exclusively by a designated office staff. Beneficiaries receive a card with information on how to submit feedback & complaints via phone or email. A form also exits in case beneficiaries would like to use it to submit written feedback or complaints. Forms & information cards are distributed with every service provision and intervention, even emergency ones. Response is provided regardless of whether corrective measures are feasible to be implemented or not. Complaint & feedback is handled on the basis of sensitivity. Less sensitive complaints & feedback are handled on country level, while sensitive (PSEA or corruption) are handled on an HQ level. All MdM staff abide by and sign a code of conduct document, as well as a feedback & complaint mechanism SOP. Staff receive refreshment training once a year to ensure principled assistance is always maintained.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management: Diagnosis, Implementation and M&E, protection mainstreaming is a cross-cutting theme for MdM-CH programs and projects. MDM-CH understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programs followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS & PSEA. We follow strong PSEA policies to protect beneficiaries and staff. Both partners remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard a number of steps and measures are adopted to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening PIN capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) - Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Sphere standards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR (Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO. - PSEA IASC guide our feedback & complaint mechanisms. MHPSS coordinator will be following all needed standards regarding mainstreaming during the implementation of the project, he/she will be responsible of following the guidelines and policies. Nevertheless, all trainings that designed for professionals and service providers will be ensure those standards and policies.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MdM-CH is basing their interventions on ethical and humanitarian principles. This project aligns with the principal approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter (and SPHERE MHPSS). Ethical principles in healthcare include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneﬁcence, the promotion of others’ well-being, commonly known as “do no harm,” as described above and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the second source of values and norms that animate both applicants’ actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conﬂict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, physical ability, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors. Therefore, in this project there are multiple measures were considered to guarantee equal access to services and better practices on the level of providing protection services. MdM with YMCA are aware of the importance of applying the Standard Operating Procedures for Child Protection, Case Management and Referral System. Moreover, in trainings and capacity building trainings we focused on raising the capacity of service providers and care givers in rights ofGBV, PwDs, NNCRPS and UNCRC. Children and their caregivers living in conflict-affected and fragile settings are paying a high price in the C19 pandemic. Limited access to health services and education, combined with social, political, and economic insecurity perpetuates cycles of violence, and increases the risk that children will experience violence and abuse (NIAF C19 Report 2020).
