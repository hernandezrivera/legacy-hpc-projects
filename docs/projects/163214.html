Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring access to essential WASH services, preventing and reducing the incidence of water-borne disease outbreaks amongst displaced populations and affected communities in the NW, SW and West regions of Cameroon. The project objectives include:
• Ensuring minimum safe water access for vulnerable and displaced populations and affected communities; and safe water access in high-risk areas and institutions during times of water-borne disease outbreak like the case of Ndian. The activities here include: Distribution of WASH kits to IDPs in the bush (water storage / treatment materials) and Protection of the main perennial water points in use; Repairs of damage and non-functional protected water points / systems; In times of WASH-related disease outbreak like the case of Ndian project activities will include: Water quality monitoring of protected point sources (boreholes, protected wells, protected springs); Bucket chlorination / chlorine diffusers at unprotected point sources and Chlorination of piped water supplies
• Ensuring basic safe sanitation access for vulnerable and displaced populations and affected communities and essential sanitation in high-risk areas and institutions / public places in WASH-related disease outbreak areas like Ndian. Activities include: Promotion of gender-segregated defecation areas and/or burial of wastes, amongst IDPs in the bush; In areas of concentration of IDPs in villages / (semi-)urban areas, installation of gender-segregated communal emergency latrines where feasible / appropriate, and with a regular cleaning / maintenance; Promotion of ‘traditional’ household latrines, coupled with distribution of latrine building tools/ equipment/ NFIs where appropriate; Repair / maintain / install any damaged sanitation infrastructure at key public institutions (e.g. Health Facilities, Schools, CFSs, etc) in locations hosting IDPs or affected by conflict; Support solid waste collection services and / or designated areas for solid waste disposal (burial / burning) to minimize vectors / hazards; Organization of periodic clean-up campaigns to remove human/animal faeces, wastes, stagnant water
• Ensuring that displaced and vulnerable households have adequate knowledge and means to ensure basic personal hygiene practices, and to maintain existing WASH infrastructure; and that at risk and affected communities are aware of the transmission routes and means of protection for common WASH-related diseases (including vector-borne). Activities include: Ensuring any WASH kit distributions (with household-water treatment) are accompanied by education on proper usage; Promoting safe hygiene practices amongst displaced peoples, affected communities, and public institutions, particularly with regards hand washing, safe water treatment, handling and storage, food handling and storage, menstrual hygiene, waste management and the elimination of open defecation; Establishment / refreshment of WASH Committees to maintain WASH infrastructures. In times of WASH-related disease outbreak activities will include; Promoting critical safe hygiene practices (e.g. ORS, safe burials, etc.) in addition to ongoing hygiene promotion messaging and Distribution of WASH / hygiene kits to vulnerable families in high-risk areas
The project targets Menchum and Bui division in the NW, Ndian and Manyu division in the SW, and Noun division in the West region. The criterion for selection of target population is based on need and vulnerability. The project is expected to last for 12 month and to meet the immediate, short term and long term WASH needs of more than 50000 vulnerable population , IDPs and affected communities’

Besoins / Needs

The Anglophone crisis that started since 2016 in the two English speaking regions of Cameroon escalated into an armed conflict and insecurity in 2017 and has rendered many people displaced from their localities. The increasing tension and hostilities has promoted insecurity and significant internal displacements. While humanitarian needs, joblessness, widowhood, crime and scarcity of food are on the increase, most farmers have fled from the villages into bushes, forest and a large number made it to nearby citiesA need assessment conducted at the end of March 2018 identified that priority needs were: food security, shelter and non-food items (NFI), health, and access to safe drinking water and sanitation (WASH). WASH related needs are at the top of the list; this is evident by the recent outbreak of cholera in the Ndian division in the SW region, which has claimed the lives of many. According to a need assessment conducted by SUDAHSER Foundation other areas like Manyu division and Mbonge sub-division in the SW, and Menchum and Ngo-ketunjia divisions in the NW region are also of high risk given the present WASH condition in the area as follows:• Non-functional and unprotected water points / systems in villages / (seri-)urban areas hosting IDPs, or affected by conflict. Particular focus on key institutions and public spaces (e.g. health center, school, market) within targeted areas• Open defecation and/or unmanage wastes amongst IDPs in the bushes and villages.• No designated areas for solid waste disposal (burial / burning) to minimise vectors / hazards• In areas of concentration of IDPs in villages / (semi-)urban areas, they is lack of gender-segregated communal emergency latrines, and the existing once are not regular clean and maintained• Damaged sanitation infrastructure at key public institutions (e.g. Health Facilities, Schools, CFSs, etc) in locations hosting IDPs or affected by conflict. • No solid waste collection services and / or designated areas for solid waste disposal (burial / burning) to minimize vectors / hazards• Lack of adequate waste disposal within Health facilities and other public institutions to be promoted.• capacities of existing water point or system governance and maintenance need to be reinforce• Need for hygiene practices promotion amongst displaced peoples, affected communities, and public institutions, particularly with regards hand washing, safe water treatment, handling and storage, food handling and storage, menstrual hygiene, waste management and the elimination of open defecation.
