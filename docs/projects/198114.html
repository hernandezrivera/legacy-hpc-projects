1 - Needs

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs in Nigeria has disclosed that the humanitarian crisis in the Northeast remains undefeated, as more women and children were displaced in 2022.According to a new UN Global Humanitarian Overview (GHO) in 2023, millions of vulnerable people in Nigeria and beyond will continue to face a daily struggle to survive.“North-east Nigeria remains one of the world’s largest humanitarian crises, with at least 8.3 million people in need of assistance in 2023. A specific needs assessment has been conducted to discover the need gaps present in Borno and Adamawa State. Humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis and the Covid-19 Pandemic has further slowed down the pace of humanitarian intervention, specifically in the Shelter and NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors.A specific needs assessment has been conducted to discover the need gaps present in Fufore, Yola North, Yola South LGA of Adamawa State and Jere, MMC LGA’s in Borno State. as well as other intended locations., humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis, specifically in the CCCM, Shelter/ NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors. The fresh population movements and the high influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in some of the hosting sites, where capacity has been stretched from 5,000 to 28,000 people in one month. As a result, significant numbers of new arrivals are sleeping outside in the open, without shelter. The humanitarian situation remains critical in northeast Nigeria, with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 1.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Adamawa State alone hosts more than 1 million IDPs – approximately 67% of the total number of IDPs in the northeast. This displacement, coupled with, until recently, lack of access by humanitarian responders to areas controlled by Boko Haram, has resulted in critically low levels of essential, lifesaving services. Humanitarian assistance is increasingly and urgently needed as more areas are coming under Government of Nigeria (GON) control, and thus becoming accessible to relief actors. In Adamawa alone, there are an estimated 1. Million individuals in need of food assistance, and 85,000 children are severely acutely malnourished. Life expectancy and key health The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. As the conflict continues into its 12th year, the attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Some 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022.Final results from March 2022 of the Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis projects further deterioration of the food security situation in the BAY states in 2022, with 4.1 million people projected to be food insecure in the upcoming lean season. Approximately 1.74 million children under five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition across the north-east in 2022. Of these, over 300,000 are expected to suffer from severe acute malnutrition and at high risk of death if they do not receive urgent treatment. Acute malnutrition is subject to seasonal variation with deteriorating situation in the lean season due to high food insecurity and increased infection rates.Violence against women, girls and children, including sexual violence, exposure to trafficking, and other forms of gender-based violence, is all too common yet underreported. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Concern was given to ensure that protection concerns were considered during planning as shown by the indicator that will guide the monitoring of the project. We intend to mainstream protection by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. The following elements must be taken into account in all humanitarian activities: 1 – Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. 2 – Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3 – Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4 – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

HARAF will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp Management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the HARAF Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. For any distributed items, HARAF will ensure the community receives proper briefing on the selection methods and the date, time and place of distributions to take place ahead of time. During distributions, demonstrations and sensitization is done on the usage of the items and sampled post distribution visits. After the project has been completed, HARAF will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
