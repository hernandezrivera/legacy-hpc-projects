Project Summary

The project aims at providing life-saving WASH services to most vulnerable 10,000 persons (4800 women, 3200 men, 800 boys and 1200girls) of all ages in Banadir District, Somalia by constructing latrines in IDP camps and host communities including disabled friendly latrines, rehabilitation of community water points such as berkads and shallow wells, capacity building for water management committees and hygiene promotion campaigns in IDP camps and host communities.
More than 20% of Somalis (2.7 million people) require support to access basic WASH services in 2020. Inadequate access to WASH services is a leading cause of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) and Malnutrition, especially where health services are too few and/or too distant (23% of nondisplaced and 35% of IDPs do not have access to a health care facility). Access to basic WASH services remains a priority for around one third of the IDPs (0.8M) in the country. In those settings, users of WASH facilities, especially women and children are usually at higher risk of facing violence or other protection related issues. As country’s vulnerability to climate change is projected to increase, lack of durability of water and sanitation outputs continue to limit the sustainability of other sector outcomes.
The internally displaced are among the most vulnerable people in Somalia. A combination of poor access to safe drinking water, lack of adequate sanitation facilities especially in IDP settlements and poor hygienic practices combine to threaten the survival and development of Somali population.
Limited access to WASH facilities and services poses a huge burden on women and children. Women who lack safe water are more prone to WASH related illnesses, such as hookworm infestation and hepatitis, which, when occurring during pregnancy, is linked to low birth weight and slow child growth. Women and girls perform most of the unpaid labor associated with WASH in households and communities which reduces the time they have available for education, economic activities and leisure. Women and girls are often vulnerable to harassment or violence when they have to travel long distances to fetch water, use shared toilets, or practice open defecation. The shame and indignity of open defecation also affects women’s self esteem, as does a lack of facilities for menstrual management, lack of water for washing clothes and personal hygiene.
As a result, the project will aim at improving sanitation conditions by construction of 50 latrines , Establishment of 20 community water points in the IDP camps and provide water the vulnerable population, hygiene awareness campaign in for 5000 IDPs and host communities and capacity building for water management committees to the most vulnerable conflict and drought displaced as well as vulnerable host communities/men, women, boys and girls including the elderly and people living with disabilities of all ages.
To ensure accountability to affected populations SSWC will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct and thereafter set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff.
The WASH staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community ie gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability, and all staff and members will be given protection mainstreaming training.

Project Activities

The project will implement the following activities :- -Extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply systems-Activities related to WASH/Safety and Protection: latrines with solid walls, locks on doors, light if justified, gender separated if justified, reasonable walking distance to water points and toilets, involvement of population in design/location of WASH facilities-Establishment of water committee to ensure proper maintenance of water services-Hygiene promotion campaign-Distribution of hygiene kits Constructing 20 latrines in IDPs and host communities. To preserve the safety and dignity of users,the latrines will be build separate for males and females and marked clearly in pictorial form. For privacy, secondary enclosures will be provided around facilities and the doors will also be lockable. Establishment of 10 community water points in the IDP camps. Considerations will be in place for children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities. A Raised platform and ramps will be constructed to ensure easy access of the services. Hygiene promotion campaigns will be conducted by hygiene promoters. SSWC will ensure the promotion services are respectfull and inclusive of religious and cultural practice and there is appropriate number of female hygiene promoters. The hygiene promoters will work in the selected IDP camps and host community, they will conduct door to door visits in the camp to disseminate information to the residents of the camp. SSWC will use 20 hygiene promoters in sensitization campaigns who will target 5000 individuals drawn from the targeted camps. SSWC will train hygiene promoters on how to pass key hygiene messages and how to use the tools for gathering information (Hygiene Promotion Tracking tool/Check list). The 20 hygiene promoters will be trained for two days. In the campaign SSWC will also use IEC materials to disseminate hygiene messages and provide timely information to the public. The project will provide hygiene kits for use by the vulnerable persons in IDP camps. the items in the kits will improve the hygiene situation of both at household levels and the camps at large. The hygiene kits contain sanitary pads, water containers, cleaning detergents, mats, chlorine and others.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement • Provide the local/host community with access to WASH camp assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic WASH services and facilities.• Consult both displaced and host communities about WASH needs and assess whether WASH interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.• Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups.• Include the host community in the sensitization and planning meetings.• consult both the IDPs and the host communities about WASH needs and at the same time the staff will be assessing whether the intervention will cause tension and conflict among the two target beneficiaries. The risk of exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to location of humanitarian assistance and services.• Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement• Install lights near communal facilities. If lighting is not possible, consider coordinating with NFI sector for the provision of solar lamps / torches for each household. • Introduce accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups • Identify central locations for distribution points - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points• Build separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females. Make sure they are clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. The risk of women, girls and boys not being included in consultations and decision - making processes• Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities. Topics to discuss include privacy - use of lockable doors and ‘walls’ to provide sufficient/safe cover; access for persons with disabilities.• Considering that most often women and children collect water, talk to them directly about safety of the location of and routes to the water collection point. • Engage the community and committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.
