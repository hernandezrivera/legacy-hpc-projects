Project Summary

UNICEF GBV programme comprises of three key pillars – primary prevention; risk mitigation and response as summarized below;
Prevention – Actively engaging in social norms and gender attitudes to reduce GBV prevalence and end harmful practices such as FGM and child marriage.
Mitigation – Strengthening of community, government and humanitarian services to improve the safety of children and increase early reporting of vulnerability;
Response – Providing minimum standards of care (clinical, psychosocial, legal, security, safety) for survivors of rape and sexual violence.
The objective of this project is to enhance the well-being of girls, boys and women subjected to gender based violence through quality life-saving interventions and services tailored to their individual needs. This project will therefore seek to contribute to the above 3 pillars by scaling up the ongoing interventions being implemented by UNICEF throughout Somalia with a network of nongovernment partners and working across line ministries in Puntland, Somaliland and with the Federal Government of Somalia in Mogadishu.

Needs

According to 2018 HNO, 2.6 million people are said to be in need, 53% of them being female. Women and children constitute the vast majority of populations affected by conflict and displacement, with a considerable proportion of households headed by women. In a joint research conducted by Oxfam, one of the coping strategy employed by some households in trying to deal with the impact of drought and displacement on livelihoods and family dynamics was for women to find work: 48% of households in Somaliland and 43% in South Central and Puntland regions reported that women had become the main breadwinner. Respondents in one site reported the prevalence of forced prostitution, and cases of trafficking were mentioned in five regions. Work outside the home puts women and girls at high risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Men and women alike reported that sexual violence is common on the way to and from work and, in some instances, is perpetrated by their host community employer. Gender-based violence affecting women and girls remains underreported but widespread, with IDPs being particularly vulnerable. According to the GBVIMS, 10,561 new GBV incidents were recorded in 2017 with 96% of them being reported by female survivors. over 80 per cent of recorded incidents were affecting IDPs. Data indicates that GBV occurs when people are in transit to IDP sites. Exposure is especially high in IDP sites, in particular at water points, after dark or in shelters that offer little physical protection. 62 IDPs from minority clans are reported to be more frequently affected, allegedly due to outsiders intruding into IDP sites. GBV cases related to intimate partner violence, especially domestic violence and deprivation of resources, continue to be the most reported type of GBV. Gaps exist in basic service provision for survivors, including post-rape care, psychosocial support, and comprehensive survivor-centered care, including case management.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

UNICEF GBV Prevention & response interventions aims at ensuring that survivors of GBV receive survivor centered & multi-sectorial services that would allow them to heal from their trauma while helping them to reduce their risk of GBV for example helping them to be aware of personal risk, how to mitigate them & on the protective factors around them. The interventions also aim to link the survivors with other services including livelihoods to help them gain & remain resilient. UNICEF communities’ cares program is a long term primary prevention intervention that aims to create safer communities for women and girls through transforming harmful social norms that contribute to sexual violence into social norms that uphold women and girls’ equality, safety and dignity. The programme uses a pathway that first promotes action to strengthen community-based care for survivors. The change process then concentrates on engaging community members in collective reflection on values, aspirations, and harmful practices and social norms that perpetuate violence and discrimination. The program is designed to Promotes resilience by strengthening community-based systems that prevent and mitigate GBV, and by enabling survivors and those at risk of GBV to access specialized care and support

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risks of exclusion and discrimination, including those based on societal discrimination, power structures, vulnerability, age, and gender was identified as a priority area by the Protection Cluster’s analysis in consultation with the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) and Inter-Cluster Coordination Group. According to the HNO, nearly two million people are estimated to be living in hard to-reach, conflict-affected areas which constrains safe humanitarian access. With limited access to life-saving assistance, crisis affected communities in these areas are disproportionally facing specific protection concerns including SGBV. As noted by HNO and safety audit, many IDPs reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Basic needs of some IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting. One of the Key concerns observed during the safety audit was that Most IDPs who arrived during the second half of 2017 were not included in the beneficiary lists for humanitarian assistance, and are only able to cope by sharing with relatives. The appropriate prevention/mitigation measures to be undertaken shall include GBV risk assessments through safety audits and other joined assessments to inform risks, such as barriers to services for groups, concerns that have been raised by the community, scenarios of concern that could lead to exclusion & exploitation. UNICEF shall also work closely with other clusters to implement recommendations of action points from 2017 & 2018 safety audits some of which includes strengthening feedback mechanisms, strategies for disseminating information about available GBV referral pathways, particularly focused on frontline service providers that directly interface with the community

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

HNO 2018 highlights that exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized IDP groups have contributed to a high level of acute humanitarian need and a lack of protection among some of the most vulnerable. Outcome of the safety audit done by UNICEF & the GBV Sub cluster noted that Most IDPs who arrived during the second half of 2017 were not included in beneficiary lists for humanitarian assistance, and are only able to cope by sharing with relatives. The lack of core services contributes to serious public health risks, preventable deaths, and increased risks of GBV for women and girls, often with inadequate access to reporting and referral mechanism or accountability of perpetrators. Of specific concern is the large number of drought-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks related to hazardous livelihood activities and reduced protection to GBV. UNICEF will continue to undertake GBV risk assessments through safety audits and other joined assessments to inform risks, such as barriers to services for groups, concerns that have been raised by the community, scenarios of concern that could lead to exclusion & exploitation. UNICEF shall also work closely with other clusters to implement recommendations of action points from 2017 & 2018 safety audits some of which includes strengthening feedback mechanisms, strategies for disseminating information about available GBV referral pathways, particularly focused on frontline service providers that directly interface with the community

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Protection needs in Somalia stem from acts of violence, exploitation, abuse, coercion, and deprivation, especially in situations of conflict and displacement, violating international human rights law and international humanitarian law, including grave violations against children and SGBV. Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) remains widespread throughout Somalia as a result of the continuing insecurity, weak rule of law, gender inequality and large-scale displacement. SGBV is particularly prevalent in camps for internally displaced persons (IDP) and in conflict zones. Women and girls who are displaced or who live in areas under Al-Shabaab control are particularly vulnerable to SGBV owing to a lack of or limited access to protection mechanisms. Prevention & mitigation measures to be undertaken to address impact of armed conflict and violence shall include Mapping and scaling up of sources of care and support, Providing minimum standards of care (clinical, psychosocial, legal, security, safety) for survivors of rape and sexual violence. Scaling up of GBV interagency referral systems, assessing gaps in services and barriers in accessing services by undertaking safety audits and following up with the recommendations.
