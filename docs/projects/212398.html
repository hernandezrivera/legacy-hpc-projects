Project Summary

The project focuses on Syria-wide protection programming with elements of nutrition and WASH. Key protection activities include child-friendly spaces, life skills programming, psychosocial support activities, support for parents, peer-to-peer caregiver sessions, child and youth clubs, and case management to survivors of SGBV (specifically in widows’ camps in NWS). The case management services are provided at the already-established women and girls safe spaces (WGSS) by trained GBV case management staff, informed by the GBV sub-cluster SoPs, guidelines and tools specifically the Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS+). These protection interventions are complemented by screening for and treatment of malnutrition, provision of WASH supplies, and hygiene promotion activities. (For the purposes of HRP submission, however, only Protection will be reported on.)
Needs Assessment:
In the Syrian Arab Republic, children continue to suffer from the long-term consequences of twelve years of conflict, worsened by the earthquake in February 2023 and an escalation of the intensity of the conflict in the three targeted areas. In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. A total of 5,219 grave violations against 5,073 children were verified between 1 July 2020 to 30 September 2022, with most violations committed in the north-east and north-west of Syria. From 1 January to 31 October 2023, 454 civilians, including 88 women and 115 children, were killed due to the conflict. In October 2023, northern Syria and Deir-ez-Zor Governorate witnessed the most significant escalation of hostilities since 2019, resulting in the displacement of over 120,000 people in north-west Syria. The risks faced by children and adolescents in Syria include physical and emotional violence and abuse; separation from parents; emotional and psychological distress; gender-based violence; neglect; exploitation; child labor; recruitment and use; early / forced marriage; risk of kidnapping; and lack of access to basic services (food, health services and education). Overall, 34% of girls and 31% of boys reported psychosocial distress according to the household survey conducted in the northern Syria. The rapid assessments conducted in the earthquake affected areas (GoS-controlled areas and NWS) showed that 83% of children exhibited severe behavioral psychological distress. It is estimated that 1 in 10 people in Syria lives with a mild to moderate mental health condition, 1 in 10 needs focused mental health care and 1 in 30 is likely suffering from a more severe condition.
According to HNO 2023 report, approximately 8.5 million people in Syria need GBV assistance (a 1.2 million increase from 2022), of whom 55.7% are women and 37.5% are girls. Safety and security concerns continue to be a gendered issue, as women and girls are disproportionately affected. Various forms of GBV are widespread, including partner and family violence, technology facilitated violence, sexual violence and harassment, child and forced marriages and unions, and incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). These forms of violence are systematically normalized and justified through patriarchal gender norms and institutions that maintain gender inequality. Overcrowded, unsafe and undignified living conditions in camps in NES, a lack of adequate infrastructure such as women and girl friendly spaces and gender-segregated WASH facilities, and a lack of income generating work opportunities are some of the factors exacerbating vulnerabilities to GBV, as well as the ongoing conflict and economic and food crises.
