Project Summary

DRC proposes a 12-month WASH project with the aim of sustaining the gains made by existing WASH interventions as well as reducing the spread of the COVID-19 virus in the targeted camps and host communities. The proposed activities are aligned with WASH sector response strategy and JRP’s 2021 objectives and will complement existing DRC programming in Site Management and Development, Livelihoods and Protection in the targeted locations. DRC will contribute to the strengthening of solid waste management systems through engaging targeted households in community compositing and linking them to recycling activities currently being implemented by DRC’s livelihood team. 1,000 waste collecting bins will be distributed to households and the targeted households will be linked to livelihood team where they will receive behaviour change communication including awareness and training on zero waste village concept, where households regenerate kitchen waste by producing compost from organic sources. 10,000 sq. meters drainages which had been blocked after the recent rains will be desilted/cleared to prevent accumulation of waste. With the risk of COVID-19 spreading in both the camps and host communities, DRC will procure and distribute COVID-19 related IEC materials and carry out hygiene promotion campaigns to continue to reinforce positive behaviour change and urge the targeted population to continue adhering to the COVID-19 prevention measures in place. Building on lessons learned in 2020, 60 volunteers will be trained on hygiene promotion to be able to carry out activities in case DRC loses access hence ensuring continuity of interventions. Through the implementation of the proposed activities, DRC facilitate adequate access to WASH services to the targeted population as well as contribute to the reduction in the spread of COVID-19 virus.

1 - Identified Needs

Improved Water Services is one of challenges in both host and Rohingya camps. According to MSNA September 2020, 54% of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks as implementing organization have achieved 102 water networks while 68 are still under construction over the 189 planned as part of the Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with handpumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without faecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. 88% of households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs, but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf area (55% of refugees reporting enough water in camp 26). Most households in the host communities (88 percent) access water from tube-wells/handpumps, but only 77% of households reporting having enough water for their domestic need due to the drilling 893 boreholes in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Despite high pumping in the aquifers below mega-camp, groundwater availability has been proven sustainable however, impact on the surrounding communities is significative due to depression from pumping cone requiring deeper tube-wells and better handpumps in host’s communities (especially in the north of Kutupalong camp).Based on the lesson learnt in 2020 this has been remarked that solid waste management remains a challenge in the camps with only 33% of the households using bins and 39% reported presence of garbage near their homes. According to the Sector operational guidelines on Solid Waste Management – WASH sector July 2019, with 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area and SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51 percent of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area3 and 27 percent reporting the presence of garbage near their houses (MSNA September 2020). For the host community, as per the MSNA report 63% of household have at least basic access to sanitation leading to 14% of the HH practicing sometimes open defecation. Regarding waste management, 11% are reporting having often visible waste in the vicinity of their house. Appropriated hygiene practices remain low even though progresses have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% (MSNA September 2020) of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% reporting having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), 1.3 MHM kits were provided to women and girls on average between January to September (4W – WASH sector – October 2020) instead of one every trimester. On the other hand, in 2020, Host’s communities have been less supported by hygiene items and campaign, but findings show encouraging data as 96% of the HH reported having access to soap. Hygiene knowledge seems also better than in camps as 81% of HC respondents being able to mention 3 critical times for handwashing. DRC is already carrying out innovative waste management activities and cleaning drainages in the camps; experience that will contribute to the implementation of this project. This year DRC will focus on waste management and hygiene promotion and the proposed intervention will benefit from DRC’s existing infrastructure and staff in addition to the existing programming – livelihoods, protection, shelter, site management support and improvement, and will have access to a range of services.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Procurement and distribution of waste collection bins. Activity 2: Cleaning of existing drainage. Activity 3: Procurement of PPEs for solid waste collection and drainage cleaning staff/volunteers. Activity 4: Awareness raising on household solid waste management. Activity 5: Procurement and distribution of COVID-19 related IEC materials. Activity 6: Distribution of hygiene kits to vulnerable families in camps and host communities. Activity 7: Hygiene promotion in camps and host communities.
