Project Summary

The consortium led by IRC with members Concern Worldwide and BBC Media Action will deliver an integrated program model to respond to the health, nutrition and protection. IRC will implement health and protection part of the project and SARPV will support nutrition services implementation as a partner of Concern Worldwide.
The program will leverage the expertise of the existing Common Service, led by BBC Media Action, on communication and community engagement support and tools provided to each of the key focus areas of the program and in-depth language expertise provided by TwB.
BBC Media Action and TWB will undertake research and develop knowledge products that help inform project partners about perceptions, concerns and priorities of residents in the camps and host communities. Based on this research, BBC Media Action will produce a wide range of communication tools, specifically developed for Rohingya and host community audiences, in the right language and about literacy limitations BBC Media Action will also provide in-depth, targeted communication and community engagement activity in specific areas where attitudinal and social norm shifts are crucial to success. Especially in SGBV and MHPSS, there are strong attitudinal barriers to overcome. Addressing these areas will take more than simple, informational ‘messaging’ –long-term, in-depth engagement will be required, using audio or video drama combined with facilitated community discussion and dialogue.
A responsive, demand-driven training and mentoring service will be delivered by BBC Media Action and TWB to consortium members and local partners, covering IPC, community engagement techniques and accountability. Specialist language support will be provided by TWB, including in-depth training, glossaries and guidance tools for front-line staff, to ensure that communication with beneficiaries is clear and effective.
The common service will support the establishment and running of listening groups, information desks or other community-facing communication facilities and services by consortium partners. The services will also be available to the government organizations and the wider humanitarian community. The training and mentoring support will also cover range of diverse aspects with the humanitarian agencies, sector and working groups work in multi hazards, cyclone preparedness, disaster preparedness, gender, age and disability components.

1 - Identified Needs

While there has been a strong increase in information access among refugees, compared with the beginning of the response in 2017, gaps remain. (GTS Nov 2019, J_MNSA 2020) Women are underserved by current information and community engagement provision, and there is a need to ensure that organisations within the response are supported to better communicate with women and understand their needs. Women’s language and communication channels differ from men's and are not yet well understood or appreciated by most agencies working in the response. (ACAPs CwC V1, J-MSNA 2020) Information needs are also not currently being well met in host communities, (GTS Nov 2019) a group with increasing concern for their livelihoods and finance, leading to risks of increased tension between the two communities. There is therefore a need to increase support to practitioners to communicate with host communities on these key areas. Access to information also varies from topic to topic. While information about basic services appears to be getting through, refugees cite the need for information on topics such as finance, aid registration, events in Myanmar and long-term options for their and their children’s futures. (BBC Media Action What Matters) Language gaps are particularly challenging in these complex, socially sensitive areas for refugees. Further progress in more complex topics must involve supporting aid providers to communicate clearly in spoken Rohingya, which is preferred by refugee (TwB 03/2020) Critical gaps exist in the provision of comprehensive reproductive and maternal health with low rates deliveries in the camp occurred in health facilities. GBV is often attributed to patriarchal norms, limited livelihood opportunities put pressure on adolescent girls to marry early or risk being trafficked. (IRC Protection Monitoring Report)
