Project Summary

This project aims to address the health needs of the population in NWS, with a special focus on displaced people. The project consists of support for mainly primary health services, including reproductive health and community health services, in addition to the provision and integration of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. Moreover, adequate provision of pharmaceuticals and medical supplies is directed toward these clinics to ensure no gaps are experienced in the provision of services. The IRC has been working in Syria for over 6 years and has a good understanding of the context and the challenges faced in program implementation. Accordingly, efforts are directed toward the pre-positioning of medicines and medical supplies, as well as on technical capacity strengthening, in many areas including Clinical Care for Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS), Problem Management Plus (PM+) and Pharmaceutical Management.
In addition to primary health services, the IRC aims to cover gaps that are experienced in NWS, and supports secondary, maternity and mental health hospitals, as well as ambulatory networks and mobile clinics, in order to be prepared to respond to emergencies.

4 - Needs

13.2M people are in need of health assistance in Syria (HNO 2019). Key health needs include access to primary health care through an Essential Health Service Package (EHSP), and access to secondary health including trauma care. 37% of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health services; an estimated 41% of adults require treatment for one or more non-communicable disease (NCD); and at least 15% requires MHPSS services (HNO). The infrastructure and referral system is inadequate to cope with existing needs. There is one functional hospital bed per 1,000 population (global average is 3 per 1,000) and psychotropic medications were reportedly unavailable in 33% of surveyed communities, with unavailability exceeding 70% in NES. There are 0.41 psychiatrists per 100,000 population currently active in Syria, (world median is 1.3 per 100,000), and only 50 gynaecologists serve a population of 4M. The ratio of essential human resources to 10,000 population remains low, with none of the governorates meeting the Sustainable Development Goal Index Threshold.Data in NWS showed 53% of health facilities were fully functional compared to 64% the previous quarter, 43% of functioning hospitals are without intensive care service, and 34% without trauma and surgical care (HeRAMS Q3 2019). On average 62% of hospitals provide maternal and new born health services and 26% offer mental health services. Aleppo and Idleb have an average gap of 61% in availability of health workers. In Sep 2019, 97 out of 1,051 communities reported that the majority of women do not give birth in a formal health facility (REACH). The top 3 needed services were: antenatal care 50%, chronic disease support 48%, and provision of medicine 48%. This is further exacerbated by targeted attacks on humanitarian and civilian facilities including hospitals, schools, protection centres, and water, power, and utility stations.

5 - Implementation of activities

Support operational and staffing costs a total for 17 health centers and an ambulatory system through two modalities, 3 directly implemented and 14 through partners. The type of health facilities varies, but include 10 PHCs, 2 hospitals, 5 mobile clinics, and an ambulatory system. Provision of pharmaceuticals and medical supplies and equipment. Provision of technical training in a variety of topics.Support and strengthen the referral system.Enhance the integration of GBV and MHPSS services in primary and secondary care services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Security situation of Syria remains challenging, especially for health services. The IRC has a team dedicated to the assessment of new project locations and this team extends its support to partner organizations to ensure that whilst health needs are being addressed, that the security of staff and service providers is not compromised. The IRC's integrated approach for health services reduces the stigma that health seekers may face, and reduces the number of facilities and the length of transportation (which pose security risks), by ensuring clinics are able to provide a wide range of services that meet the patient's needs. Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FARM) reinforced by IRC assists beneficiary accountability and credibility.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption is that border will remain open for the transportation of life saving supplies into NWS. The risk is that there will not be access, and the IRC mitigates this through the provision of at least a 3 month supply per facility, in addition to the provision of additional supplies for pre-positioning. Another risk is the security of health clinics and hospitals- the IRC does a thorough analysis to assess locations and develops security management plans that include evacuation protocols and the transport of equipment to safer locations.
