Project Summary

MdM has 30 years of experience in implementing Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) projects across 45 countries and has been present in oPt for over 20 years. As our experience has identified the multitude of cultural, political and economic factors that affect a woman's right to make decisions regarding her reproductive health, there is a strong need for a rights-based approach to develop programs that decrease violence against Palestinian women and improve access to quality family planning, including emergency contraception. Based on MdM's experience in implementing SRH activities in the oPt alongside local partners and based on its continued involvement with the local community and operational networks, the proposed project intends to reduce gender inequalities by facilitating access for people and communities, especially vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, Gender-based Violence (GBV) survivors, women and girls without, or excluded from, basic healthcare provisions and provide key components of women's rights and SRH.

This will be achieved through strengthening the public health and local partner's provision of quality, integrated, and gender-responsive, adolescent-friendly SRH and GBV services in the West Bank (WB) and Gaza. The intervention will provide support for GBV survivors of all ages and gender, with transversal mental health support as well as a community-focused approach that reinforces the activities through awareness raising and empowerment activities, ensuring boys and men's involvement. Advocacy is also an important component of the intervention to continually aspire for sustained improved access to SRH services and commodities and garner support for policies and practices promoting SRH and rights among women, girls, boy, and men. This will ensure a strong impact on a long-term perspective. In Gaza strip, apart from supporting the public health delivery of SRH and GBV services, MdM partners with a local NGO, the Culture of Free and Thought Association (CFTA), a reputable grass root organisation which is a long-term partner of MdM, enjoying a long experience and excellent reputation for implementing and advocating for better SRH access. CFTA is based in the southern area of Gaza strip and runs a Women Health Center in Al Bureij Camp and 4 Cultural Centers in Khan Younis which this proposed project will be supporting. SRH and GBV activities will be implemented in the Women Health Center in Al Bureij Camp, targeting the Gaza area of intervention through outreach teams while outreach community activities will be done in both Middle Area and Khan Younis governorates. In the WB, MoH will be the operational partner of MdM while community activities will be implemented by Community-based Organisations (CBOs) to promote local actors and community empowerment, especially in tackling SRH and GBV issues. PSEA will be mainstreamed in the activities, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups, such as PWDs.

1 - Needs

This project aims to address the priority needs highlighted in HNO 2022 concerning access to essential health services, specifically SRH, and its related restrictions and barriers. GBV and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) needs that have both health and protection cross-cutting issues, will also be addressed in this proposed project. Specifically, the interventions target the needs of vulnerable groups, including of 1) Women of Reproductive Age (WRA), especially those with high-risk pregnancies, women with unmet need for Family Planning (FP), female adolescents, pregnant and lactating women; 2) boys and men; 3) GBV survivors; 4) persons with disabilities; 5) and persons with mental health and psychosocial issues. MoH (Annual Report, 2020) reported that there are 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women, 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates identified to be in need of the provision of SRH healthcare, GBV, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services. Moreover, the need to increase men and boy’s participation in SRH activities is highlighted in the recent study by UNFPA (2021) in Gaza which shows that knowledge about men-specific SRH and Rights (SRHR) is limited and mainly concentrated around infertility (45% reported by men, 47% by women, 41% of boys and 60% of girls), sexual dysfunction (39% by men, 56% by women, 50% of boys and 46% of girls), and the need for contraception (38% of men, 30% of women, 30% of boys and 34% of girls).Latest available data from the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (2019) showed that 38% of women between ages 18-64 years old in Gaza and 24% in the WB reported psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic violence by their husbands during the preceding year while 11% of currently married or ever married women in Gaza, and 8% in WB experienced sexual violence by their husbands. There is no information collected for children regarding sexual violence in the previously cited report, however 3% of the youth (18-29 years) reported that they experienced sexual violence or sexual harassment when they were below the age of 18 (PCBS, 2019). Persons with disabilities experience additional threats that increase their vulnerability. There was a 52% increase of domestic violence against persons with disabilities in 2020 by family members and a lack of information about GBV prevention and response, including a 58% lack in how and where to access relevant assistance and services, such as MHPSS, during emergency (Educaid- Rapid Assessment: Ensuring Disability Inclusion in COVID-19 Response-Palestine). Studies in the oPt have revealed disproportionate access to health services based on gender. In terms of taking FP decisions, women tend to be creative in “playing around decision-making power” as husbands are the final arbiter in such situations (UNFPA, 2021). This finding echoes the result of a study in Palestine showing that 80% of men and 48% of women believe that men should be the final decision-makers at home (UN Women, 2017) and that husbands have greater control over family resources (CARE, 2020). In the same study by UNFPA conducted in Gaza, 36% of women and 34% of men agree that men impose pressure on women and restrict their access to SRH services while a considerable portion agrees that never-married girls who need invasive gynecological services are denied access (44% of males, 53% females, 56% boys, and 53% girls). The same study shows that men and boys also encounter discrimination in accessing SRH services as 10% of male and 10% female respondents know a man who was maltreated or mocked when attempting to access the services while 13% of male and 14% of female know a man who was sent back home when attempting to seek SRH services. Women and girls also spent more time doing household chores (4 hours for women, 2 hours for girls and 1 hour for men and boys) and spending meaningful time with kids (1 hour for men and 3 hours for women).The need to focus on adolescent SRH is also revealed by the same study showing that young people are less knowledgeable than adults on SRHR and that knowledge on Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) and adolescent health are rarely known. Boys also tend to receive SRH information from informal sources, such as married friends or from the “streets” compared to adults who receive mostly from classic sources (parents, mothers and personal experiences). To identify these needs, several methodologies were adopted, which include consultation with the MoH, local partners, and community members as well as referencing secondary data to support primary information collected by MdM. In 2017, MdM ran an SRH programme which provided FP to the community of Al Bureij in Gaza. The intervention was successful but highlighted the widespread problem of unwanted pregnancies in Gaza. In 2017, an exploratory mission, conducted exclusively in the WB, found the same widespread problem of unwanted pregnancies while also highlighting its deep connection to endemic GBV. Further deep assessment conducted in 2018 concluded that GBV-related unwanted pregnancy continues to be a significant health need in the WB and also identified similar links between GBV and unwanted pregnancies in Gaza. In 2020, MdM supported the MoH in upgrading the quality of SRH in Khan Younis Governorate, which reflected the needs in the SRH continuum of care and improving the quality of service’ delivery. MdM current engagement with MoH, partners and community members reveal that the same needs continue to exist in the current context. This proposed intervention will be implemented by MdM leveraging our 30 years of experience in implementing SRH projects across 45 different countries, including Palestine, through contributing to address the socio-cultural, financial and attitudinal barriers hindering access to SRH and GBV services while considering aggravating limiting conditions brought by the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities

MdM activities are aimed at improving SRH and protection services for women, men, boys and girls living in oPt through a rights-based and global approach that address the gender needs and priorities. Specifically, the actions will contribute to addressing access barriers; limited knowledge on SRHR among men and women, especially among adolescents; inclusion of men and boys and increased participation; protection priorities (specific risks facing women and girls—GBV, psychological distress); combating stigma; prevention of unwanted pregnancies, ensuring that vulnerable groups, especially PWDs, are taken into consideration and PSEA is mainstreamed in the activities. I – Strengthened health system and local partner’s provision of essential SRH and GBV services In this component, our partners include MoH (operational partner in WB and Gaza) and CFTA (implementing partner in Gaza). In the WB, three PHCCs (Qalqilya City Central PHCC, Azzun PHCC and Habla PHCC) in Qalqilya governorate will be supported in the provision of integrated SRH and GBV services. In Gaza, MdM will focus on strengthening the capacities and providing technical support to two PHCCs each in the Middle Area and Khan Younis and to our partner's (CFTA) Women Health Center in Al Bureij Camp, Middle Area and 4 Cultural Centers in Khan Younis. The targeted intervention areas were based on MdM and local partner’s needs assessment. The following activities will be implemented under this component: 1. Provision of quality SRH consultations (FP, ANC/PNC, access to emergency contraception) with integrated GBV and MHPSS services in PHCCs in WB and Gaza and CFTA clinics, ensuring services are adolescent-friendly. Adolescent SRH services will be tailored to married and non-married individuals, respecting the local culture. All services will be provided by the PHC staff in WB and by both PHC and CFTA staff in Gaza. 2. Conduct supportive supervision with MoH team in public PHCCs in both Gaza and WB and with partner’s senior technical team in CFTA clinics in Gaza. 3. Training on IUD insertion among PHCC staff; on provision of adolescent-friendly SRH services; on specific needs of PWDs; and training local partners on PSEA policy and on Community-Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) which will be implemented in both WB and Gaza. 4. Provision of essential life-saving medications, equipment and supplies, including PPEs to adapt to COVID-19 context to MoH PHC in Gaza and WB and to CFTA in Gaza.5. Follow-up of guidelines and develop specific protocols on SRH, FP (emergency contraception), adolescent-friendly services, MHPSS, and GBV, in coordination with relevant stakeholders and GBV AoR. 6. Adaptation of health facilities in WB and Gaza to ensure accessibility, respect for privacy and confidentiality and non-stigmatizing experience for PWDs and GBV survivors 7. Designing MEAL tools and provide data reporting of GBV and SRH to health clusters, disaggregated to gender, age, PWDs and other relevant aspects. II - Reinforced community participation and engagement on SRH and gender equality This community component will be implemented by our IPs, CFTA in Gaza, and CBOs in the WB. Activities include 1) community awareness and social behavioral change on danger signs of pregnancy, Antenatal Care/Post-Natal Care; GBV, including the tackling of cyberviolence; adolescent SRH needs, especially targeting men and boys through awareness and counselling; 2) Psycho- education to PWDs and their caregivers, addressing healthy relationships in order to avoid, recognize, and report instances of exploitation, violence, and abuse; 3) Organize youth-friendly space to talk about new masculinities and GBV. III Palestinian stakeholders are sensitized and effectively act for improved quality standards and services related to SRH and GBV. Advocacy activities include advocacy campaign on mainstreaming SRH, gender and counter GBV, and PSEA both at policy and local levels, addressing religious authorities and community leaders. IEC materials, posters, and social media campaigns on SRH and prevention of GBV will be developed for communications and awareness raising. MdM has developed a guideline on SRH and GBV in crisis settings that provides the framework for the intervention in Palestine, apart from following WHO, UNFPA national protocols and the development of specific protocols planned under this project. MdM was an associate organization member of Inter-Agency Working Group on RH in Crises in 2019 and continues its active participation in the forum, ensuring access to updated materials on SRH and GBV. MdM has also developed SOPs on Child Protection, Case Management and Referral that guide coordination with other actors, apart from being active in both Health and Protection clusters, and continued meaningful collaboration with other actors. This proposed intervention strives to minimize multiple referral points and create an efficient patient ﬂow to ensure safety and dignity of beneficiaries and upholding the Do No Harm approach. GBV survivors will be directed to a designated consultation room where auditory and visual privacy and confidentiality are guaranteed. PHCCs will be adapted to allow accessibility of PWDs and ensuring non-stigmatizing experience for GBV survivors. Working with local partners and CBOs contributes to addressing access barrier experienced mostly by vulnerable groups in the communities. Trainings, such as on specific needs of PWDs and adolescents, ensure service delivery without discrimination and guarantee SRH right as a human right, regardless of age and gender. PSEA is built-in the project activities through integration of PSEA into MdM’s and partner’s Feedback and Complaint Mechanism; training partners and MdM staff on PSEA policy and receipt of complaints; identification of PSEA focal point in the PHCCs and partner clinics, ensuring that PSEA awareness messaging is integrated in community outreach programs.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, such as complaint box located in the clinics or through phone calls, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed ﬁrst by the MEAL oﬃcer who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback/complaints. Regular complaints are non-sensitive which relate to program design and implementation/service delivery.. Sensitive complaints include issues related to sexual exploitation and abuse; abuse of authority by MdM staff; derogatory behavior with beneﬁciaries involving verbal abuse/physical assault; discrimination of beneﬁciaries based on race, gender, sexuality, child abuse and neglect, counterfeiting, threats, or blackmails Adapting the response to the situation of COVID 19, patients will be informed that they can re-launch complaint mechanisms through social networks, such as WhatsApp or Facebook. Users who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and ﬁnal decisions and actions will be oﬃcially communicated through oﬃcial mechanisms (e.g. letter). Feedback from CBCM on PSEA will be dealt with in full confidentiality and managed using the proper channels. All complaints will be registered anonymously and treated following MdM internal procedures. Feedback will be used to improve service delivery and to adapt activities for better programming.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) of MdM, where details on PSEA complaint mechanism are also integrated, is accessible to all project beneficiaries, community leaders, stakeholders and suppliers. All of them are updated and reached by MdM through the distributed FCM card. The FCM cards highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by MdM independently from project teams and are dealt accordingly, respecting the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. The cards contain the following information: a unique mobile and WhatsApp number to receive the feedback via calls or text messages, working days and hours to receive the feedback, a unique email address to receive electronic feedback. All of this information is clearly written in Arabic language. Beneficiaries can also share their feedback/complaint via in-person. All of these modalities are in place to allow safe access to the FCM to different groups regardless of sex, age, disability status, literacy, etc. MdM and partner teams distribute and explain the FCM cards to the beneficiaries during their presence in the field/clinics, targeting all the individuals who receive services through the project, in coordination with partners, village councils and community leaders with whom these activities were coordinated, the FCM card will also orient clinics services users to submit feedback or complaints through the complaint boxes installed in the clinics. In addition, MdM logistics department distributes and explains the FCM card to all suppliers involved in the project. A beneficiary satisfaction survey is developed for each activity. MdM MEAL team analyzes the data and feedback and share it in quarterly basis with the technical and operational managers, to adapt and improve the activities accordingly.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The principle of "Do no Harm" is observed at all stages of MDM Project Cycle management. Protection mainstreaming is a cross cutting theme for MdM programs and projects. MdM-F and CFTA understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programs followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards regarding SRH, GBV and MHPSS. All organizations remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard, MdM-F and CFTA are adopting several steps to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening their capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming, the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following UNICEF, MdM and Sphere guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO. All activities conducted by MdM-F and its partners will ensure and monitor that health personnel involved in providing care follow risk reduction measures to prevent the spread of COVID- 19 in the communities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender and inclusion of PWDs are principles adopted by MdM, reflected in the execution of activities and extended towards local partners. The team providing quality consultations on SRH, GBV, and MHPSS will be gender-balanced, ensuring that male and female clients have equal access to services in the clinics. In conducting community activities, respect for culture and traditions of the local community will be observed, such as visiting them in proper timing (visiting the mothers in the morning hours and fathers after working hours). Using the outreach approach, MdM is ensuring the access of all community members including females, elderly and PWDs, and adolescents to the services and reduce the risk and community security concerns while moving to obtain the services in other locations. In 2021, MdM reinforced the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV and inclusion of Persons with Disabilities. A specific SOP was developed to address the needs of GBV and PWD cases identified and ensure proper referral, follow up and documentation of these cases. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based on a gender-based approach (gender specific health), for instance, developing different IEC for women, men and children, developing different protocols based upon gender and also addressing all the barriers preventing persons with disabilities to access services. By its community activities through local partners, MdM is able to bring services more accessible to community members. By having an outreach program, MdM MEAL process is taking into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities and targeted locations. All beneficiaries referred to any external service are documented and tracked accordingly.
