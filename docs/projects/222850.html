Project Summary

The main goal of this program is to collaborate with the youths both male and female in the host community to create sustainable livelihood opportunities that are oriented towards the market. Additionally, it aims to foster social cohesion by involving them in volunteer work for development. The project will assist 750 both male and female individuals (with majority are women) to acquire the necessary skills for either finding employment or starting their own businesses. The project places significant emphasis on empowering women and girls, recognizing that there are power dynamics within families and communities that need to be addressed in order to promote gender equality. The training and mentoring support provided also incorporate elements focused on maintaining peace and harmony within and between communities, preventing sexual and gender-based violence, promoting female leadership opportunities, and preventing further victimization of women and girls. ACF will enhance livelihood opportunities for youth and adult from vulnerable households in the host community through IGA and vocational skills training. ACF have already conducted an assessment to identify the preferred skills for training among the community's youth. ACF will offer short course by engaging technical institutes or resource persons in areas such as handicraft, tailoring, prickle making etc based on the needs and preferences of the youths and adults. After completing the training, participants will receive the necessary technical tools and equipment and/or start-up capital. ACF will also provide support for off-farm Income Generating Activities (IGA), such as fish drying, small business where participants will receive two days of training, along with start-up capital and inputs. Financial literacy and disaster risk reduction actions will be integrated into all types of training sessions. Under this project, ACF aims to target Hnila, Whykong and Sabrang union of Teknaf Upazila. The project participants will be selected from the most vulnerable households, including those headed by females, households with persons with disabilities, orphan-hosted households, and households with elderly individuals. Job fairs will be organized to connect employers with vocational training participants, creating opportunities for employment. This will establish a connection between the participants, financial service providers, and employers. ACF's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) team will develop tools based on the project's output and outcome. Female and adolescent/young girls from the beneficiaries HH will be selected for livelihoods and skill development activities. Women empowerment and gender responsiveness in business activities will be promoted . The MEAL unit will conduct periodic surveys, focus group discussions, and participatory development meetings with the targeted population. In the project implementation area, an effective complaints and feedback mechanism will be established to ensure community participation, transparency, and accountability. Additionally, a contingency plan will be included in the project to address any disasters that may occur during the project period, and all staff will be equipped with the necessary tools and protective gear to respond to emergencies within 72 hours. The proposed project will adhere to the Livelihoods and Skill Development Sector policy and guidance.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Bangladesh hosts around one million FDMNs, most of whom fled for their lives from Myanmar in 2017. The country set an admirable example of solidarity with the refugees despite its resource constraints. Over the years, solidarity with the plight of FDMNs evolved because of economic instability because of the wage fall and price hike, unequal access to humanitarian aid and uneven distribution of resource opportunities created through substantial donor support to the FDMNs. Because of the disrupted economy that comes with hosting so many refugees, the tension between host communities and refugees increase. To address these tensions, creating increased and sustainable income opportunities of host communities, which will ultimately contribute to peaceful conflict resolution. According to RIMA round 2 (October 2023), half of the households from the refugee community were found having inadequate food consumption in 2022. 68% of the households from the Rohingya community adopted at least one consumption-based coping strategy to cope with the food crisis. Dependency on less preferred or less expensive food is the most used coping strategy in Rohingya (66%) communities; one-third of the households from the Rohingya community followed at least one crisis or emergency strategy and 42% followed a stress strategy of coping. Additionally, 34% of the households from host community are suffering from inadequate food consumption (borderline and poor). According to the Refugee-Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) round 7 (July 2024), this is the first time since 2017, a significant number of Rohingya households could not afford an acceptable diet. The survey revealed that 70% of households were experiencing poor or borderline consumption, highlighting alarming nutritional consequences. According to the IPC April 2024 projection, Cox’s Bazar FDMN is estimated to have 20% of its population is classified as IPC Phase 4 and 40% as IPC phase 3. Similarly, the host communities are expected to have 5% of their population in IPC Phase 4 with 35% in IPC Phase 3. There were discrepancies in food consumption between female and male-headed households among Rohingya Refugees, despite of standardized assistance from WFP. A higher proportion of women-headed households reported inadequate and poor food consumption compared to male-headed households (49% versus 41%, respectively). Similarly, in the host community, food consumption discrepancies were evident (41% versus 33%, respectively). REVA -7 revealed that the Minimum Dietary Diversity for the women of reproductive age population in both community is very alarming. More than 80% in both the camps and more than 70% in the Bangladeshi community could not access nutritious food. The above information and context analysis conclude that Rohingya refugee populations and the host communities are facing a severe food security crisis characterized by inadequate food consumption, nutritional deficiencies, and limited access to basic necessities. This crisis is exacerbated by factors such as, gender inequality, tension between Rohingya and host communities and natural disaster and economic shocks. To address this crisis, there is an urgent need for diversified livelihood opportunities to reduce dependency on aid, increased food production and income, improved market environment and supply systems to ensure adequate supply of nutritious food.The percentage of Bangladeshi community households experiencing income deprivation has decreased to 18 percent. As per the MSNA 2023 report, approximately 36% of households in the host community faced diverse challenges in accessing income-generating prospects. Moreover, 29% of households indicated zero income (in Sabrang Union, this percentage was 37%). In the 18-25 age bracket, 58% of individuals were employed out of the total population that reported working, with only 8% being female.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ACF is a member of PSEA network and have an assigned focal person. It will strengthen the protection plan by integrating a non-discriminatory approach, safeguarding policies, and measures to ensure gender, age, and disability inclusivity. ACF will conduct PSEA training for all staffs and volunteers involved in project implementation. The project is committed to fostering inclusive participation, specifically targeting diverse groups, including individuals with disabilities, while steadfastly adhering to the "Do No Harm" principle. ACF will establish Women-friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) as an integral component of this project. The construction of WFIS will prioritize the safety, security, and unique needs of women, person with disability and persons with special needs. These spaces will serve as venues for meetings, awareness-raising sessions, and other activities aligned with the project's objectives. ACF will install complaint boxes at the community level within WFIS. Additionally, ACF will ensure the dissemination of a hotline number through leaflets, stickers, beneficiary passbooks, and other mediums at various project sites. A conflict-sensitive approach will be consistently applied throughout, incorporating risk and security assessments to mitigate potential harm. This proactive strategy aims to prevent interventions from escalating tensions or compromising the safety of both staff and beneficiaries.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF is committed to the mainstreaming of the PSEA principles and has own policy. This entails embedding a robust framework that prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of all participants, particularly those who may be vulnerable or at-risk. ACF will take initiative to aware community people, staffs, beneficiary and all stakeholders involved, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to recognize signs of vulnerability and report any incidents promptly. ACF have clear reporting mechanisms, including a confidential hotline, complaint box and email to encourage open communication and facilitate immediate response to any concerns raised. Additionally, ongoing monitoring and evaluation will be conducted through a PSEA lens, ensuring that interventions remain aligned with best practices and uphold the highest standards of protection for all individuals involved in the program.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will incorporate climate actions including DRM, environmental issue through different actions.DRR/DRM: The project link with disaster management committees at the union and upazila level. Community based DRR activities will be facilitated through the Youth Clubs, Self Help Group (SHG) through awareness campaign. These campaigns will cover various aspects, including raising awareness about early warning systems, conducting search and rescue operations, practicing evacuation procedures, promoting hygiene practices, tree plantation drives etcEnvironmental activities: The project will carry out environmental assessment using NEAT + software to identify environmental impacts and implement mitigation measures. For maintaining cleanliness in the area ACF will distribute households level waste management system through distribution of waste bin at the HH level. Youth clubs, SHGs will be provided with orientation and will be connected with CPP volunteers to support DRR activities. Youth CPP volunteers will receive priority consideration for membership in the youth clubs. ACF will also be responsible for developing and periodically reviewing contingency plans to ensure the safety and well-being of project participants in the event of a crisis.
