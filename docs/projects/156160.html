Project Summary

This project will provide protection assistance in Kafr Taal ( community in Atareb sub-district/ Jabel Saman district), Akhtrin and Arshaf ( are a communities in Aghtrin sub-district/ A’zaz district) and Kafrantin (is a community in Daret Azza sub-distirct/ Jabel Saman district) all in Aleppo governorate, through CP services in (CFSs) and community based protection center.
The several displacements from different areas to the north of Syria caused a huge influx of IDPs to western and northern country side of Aleppo as well as Idlib governorate.
There is a huge need for protection services and especially CP and YEP programs in many areas of northern Syria and till now there is a huge gap in the coverage of the community based services due to many reasons related to funds and lack of capacities and most of the established centers are suffering from the cutting in the fund.
Therefore SOH chose to help in providing spaces for children and adolescents and to help those affected groups where children and adolescents will be able to feel physically and emotionally safe.
Safe in this context refers to the absence of trauma, excessive stress, and violence and express themselves without fear of judgement or harm, where they can build their social networks, receive support from their peers, and learn something new.
The proposed activities are:
- Outreach activities including (awareness session, life skills and PSS): Outreach Team will target households, community establishments, schools, and any reachable places that are considered suitable for such activities.
- life skills for adolescents: The proposed activities will use the protection cluster developed YEP life skills guidelines.
-The centers will offer recreational activities for children and adolescent boys and girls.
-The centers will provide parenting sessions to discuss many issues related to protection of children and their rights.
-Capacity development for the centers staff on CPMS, PSS, PFA, self care and case management.
-Vocational courses for adolescents, administrative skills, languages, computer skills and other vocations.
-There will be 2 case managers at the centers where they will receive cases from other activities. They will do case study, basic counseling and referrals to other services e.g. Psychologist when needed.

Needs

In 2018, protection issues in Syria remained widespread, with 97% of covered communities reporting the occurrence of at least one protection concern for one or more population groups. Despite a steady increase of protection interventions, responses and services throughout the country, the magnitude of protection needs still outstrip response capacities. (Whole of Syria 2018 Protection Needs Overview, Whole of Syria Protection Sector Report).In order to better understand the main child, adolescent and women protection concerns and issues affecting them in north Syria (Aleppo Governorate), Space of Hope (SoH) team has conducted an assessment funded by UNICEF in November 2018 to inform responses, planning, advocacy and resource mobilization in Areas of: Azaz, Al-Bab and Jabal Samaan districts.In the assessed areas, half of participants from males and females (41%) in the FGDs noted that there were some cases of local conflicts among families in the last period. This happens as a result of deteriorated security situations. This caused to spread of weapons and guns in hand of people.KIs noted that there are high numbers of people who were victims (killed, injured, lost or wounded by landmines or UXO in this crisis). Meanwhile, 58% of respondents noted that there are availability of team who is responsible of demining the mines and UXOs remnants of war in the area of the assessment, while 42% of respondents stated there is no presence of demining team although the need for that.Respondents noted that there are children include: separated children, disabled children, child-headed households, and some children from IDPs’ HHs, children live in camps and children who live in informal settlements have difficulties to access essential services.Assessment results have identified a total number of 8,932 of vulnerable children and adolescents in addition to 3,229 women who need special care and assistant in the assessed area from the groups mentioned above.71% of officers who work in NGOs have confirmed about presence of children are involved in high-risk behaviours, indicated as children and adolescents addicting drugs, addicting narcotic medicinal pills, thieving and fallen under sex exploitation.33% of KIs confirmed that there were groups of unaccompanied children (some of them under 5 years) who came with displaced families.Results of the assessment showed that 4% of total children are working in the assessed area (both males and females).According to KIs, there are some cases of children who took part in the armed conflict (61% of KIs’ answers) in the area of assessment.All Participants from males and females in FGDs noted that they have noticed changes in the behaviour of children or youth in last years.All respondents confirmed that there are functioning and available primary and secondary schools in the area of assessment supported by the Turkish government (Education Directorate) in districts of Azaz and Al-bab, and some local and international NGOs in addition to some private charities in district of Jabal Samaan. Assessment results indicated a high percentage of children and adolescents who are not attending schools, noted as below: Girls (Primary: 45%), Girls (Secondary: 66%), Boys (Primary: 40%) and Boys (Secondary: 62%).According to respondents, the main concerns for the households in the current situation are: a new displacement, security situation deterioration, losing a job, weakness of the financial capacity and concerns of missing one of their children either by involving to armed groups or by exposing to abduction.All female respondents confirmed that marriage age for females and males has changed, and the age of marriage has become younger for reasons noted as: Girls: poverty, concerns about girls’ future in addition to cultural and social traditions in the community. Boys: idling, dropping out of schools and lack of sufficient knowledge.

Implementation of activities

SOH will prepare a training plan, an activity plan and a monitoring plan and will share these with the CP staff at the start of the project. SOH will have overall responsibility for the project, including technical advice, monitoring of outputs and reporting. The main activities of the project are: • Training of CP staff on CPMS, PFA, Self care and case management• Design and print of context and audience speciﬁc CP materials; • Provision of tailor-made audience-speciﬁc targeting CP ; • Provision of safety brieﬁngs to humanitarian staff; • Coordinated awareness raising campaigns on awareness raising sessions on protection risk mitigation with leaﬂets. The project teams will provide direct face-to-face PSS sessions and life skills activities in a number of different ways: • House to house to awareness raising sessions on protection risk mitigation. targeting children and adolescents boys and girls and adults. • Vocational courses for adolescent boys and girls in Youth centers and outreach activities. • Safety brieﬁngs to humanitarian workers. SoH has developed several tools and approaches to ensure close monitoring of its programs including: • Weekly Activity Reports, Monthly Plan • Feedback and complaints mechanisms (complaint boxes, hotline number, Whats App and Email address)• Routine site monitoring visits and observation monitoring • Training Assessments • Mobile data collection through KoBo toolbox.Indicators tracking will be monitored by the M&E Manager in monthly basis through excel spreadsheet. This tool is shared on a monthly basis with the executive manager for review and feedback.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served.Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision. Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceive as a threat).Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups / individuals at the exclusion of other groups/individuals.The timing, content, or location of a service results in empowering/strengthening parties to the conflict or contributing to /increasing political tensions.Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality.Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialized services.Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately.Risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns.Risk to the well-being of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma).Impact of activity is minimal on children's protection.Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development; compromised quality of trainings due to contextual constraints; high turnover of staff.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

"1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed.2. Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments.3. Liaise with ""neutral"" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making.4. Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required.5. Ensure safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location."6. Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision.7. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues).8. Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.9. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered).10. Ensure consultation and mobilization of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children.11. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 12. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.13. consider geographic locations for both fixed and mobile services and implications vis a vis the dynamics of the conflict and political events, especially with consideration for types of information shared."14. ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context15. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.16. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.17. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services.18. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.19. Child Protection messaging to non Protection actors 20. Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organisational HR policies.
