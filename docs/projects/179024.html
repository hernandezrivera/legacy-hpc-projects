Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria to be 14.2 million (48.7 % male and 51.3 % female), of which almost six million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.7 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. More than 70% of the population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 12.4 million of people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.27 million severely food insecure and 9.5 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 98,300 crisis-affected households through support to vegetable and poultry production (equivalent to 589,800 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve the food security and nutrition of vulnerable crisis-affected households. Project outputs include:
Output 1: Increased capacity for vegetable production for 54,150 households.
The output aims at increasing the vegetable production of 54,150 vulnerable households (of which 16,245 are women-headed) through the provision of full vegetable production packages, which include vegetable seeds/seedlings, drip irrigation networks, and organic fertilizers.
Output 2: Increased capacity for poultry production for 48,735 households.
This output aims to support 48,735 vulnerable households (of which 15,162 are women-headed) to engage in poultry production for both consumption of poultry products and as part of income generating activities.
It is expected that the project support, through provision of vegetable and poultry inputs and associated training, will diversify the diets of the targeted households and at the same time, the sale of vegetable and poultry products will increase household income and create employment for the most vulnerable members of the households, especially for women and youth. It is expected this will lead to improvement of livelihoods, increased local availability of food, increased self-reliance and improved food security and nutrition of crisis-affected households, especially the most vulnerable.
This project will enhance household production of vegetables and poultry to increase dietary diversity and micronutrient consumption. The project will provide inputs to improve household food availability and income generation through backyard poultry production and vegetable production). Household-level farming is an activity often undertaken by women that will benefit from this intervention, and they will be prioritized in the selection process. This support will be complemented by training and capacity building to strengthen the technical skills of beneficiaries and improve their food production and income generation capacities for food and nutrition security. Household food production activities (vegetables, poultry.) will also benefit vulnerable groups with limited access to land such as IDPs, the landless rural population, and urban and Semi-urban populations.
Support provided to agricultural production will help alleviate the scarcity of food commodities and reduce dependency on imports and food aid. A close partnership with other UN agencies, such as UNICEF, UNDP, WFP and IOM, will further contribute to creating synergies between using agencies comparative advantages and improve the food security and nutrition of targeted households

4 - Needs

The Syria crisis now in its tenth year and has severely affected lives, livelihoods and coping strategies of the population. Currently, the number of acutely food insecure people and those at risk of food insecurity is estimated at 12.4 million people. The total number of Syrians requiring humanitarian assistance in the coming year is 14.2 million (48.7% male and 51.3% female).The crisis has severely limited access to food due to the impact of the crisis on the agriculture sector and the production value chain (production, processing and marketing). The quantity and quality of food in the markets, especially vegetables and poultry products, have reduced significantly. The purchasing power of families has been progressively eroded due limited employment opportunities, inflation and steep depreciation of the Syrian pound.Unemployment has reached 55 percent and purchasing power has declined to one tenth of the pre-crisis level (if compared to the income in 2010) and the poverty level is reported above 90% according to various studies. As a result, families cannot meet their basic daily food requirement, have a poor and unbalanced diet and are forced to adopt unsustainable coping strategies, leading to complete assets depletion. The diets of the most vulnerable households largely consist of potatoes and commodities provided under food assistance.The project will give priority to rural women as they were particularly marginalized with over 70 % of women participating for more than 15 hours-a-day in unpaid and informal labour with no access to benefits available to formal employees. This greater involvement of rural women in intensive and informal labour was accompanied by greater lack of schooling and lesser awareness

5 - Implementation of activities

The FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the activities, The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity which can be scaled up whenever required and aligned to sector response packages.Output 1: Increased capacity for vegetable production for 54,150 households.1.Refine composition of vegetable production inputs based on local conditions. 2.Identify beneficiaries ads per FAO vulnerability criteria3.Contract implementing partner for the distribution of vegetable production inputs.4.Procurement and distribution of vegetable production inputs (assorted vegetable seeds/seedlings, micro irrigation inputs, fertilizers to the beneficiaries).5.Conduct training of trainers programme for technicians to facilitate community-level training.6.Provide training for the targeted households using the farmer field school approach.7.Create awareness on basic nutrition and health, especially for women.8.Conduct post-distribution assessment and impact evaluation. Output 2: Increased capacity for poultry production for 48,735 households.Activities 1) Contract implementing partner for the distribution of poultry production inputs (chicks, feed, and vaccines).2) Identify beneficiaries as per FAO vulnerability criteria3) Procurement and distribution of poultry production inputs (chicks, feed, and vaccines) for the establishment of small-scale poultry production and according to SO2/SO3 guidance. 4) Provide the training for the targeted households using the farmer field school approach.5) Undertake routine vaccination of chicks.6) Conduct post-distribution assessment and impact evaluation.NB: All the activities will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of the being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible frictions between the two groups, and special attention will be provided to returnees to help them in their re-settlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating their assets.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities, conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework, diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities. 5. Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the period/area concerned. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Assistance
