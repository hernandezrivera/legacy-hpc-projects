Project Summary

The objective of this project is to enhance protection, recovery and resumption of agricultural and livelihoods activities of the most vulnerable and food insecure (classified as IPC 3 and 4) households in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State of South Sudan.
The IRC will provide immediate and short term livelihood protection support in the beginning of 2019 lean season to the most severely food insecure and vulnerable households in NBeG. To enhance recovery and resilience, interventions will include mainstream support to at-risk households focused on improving crop, livestock and fisheries production and bolstering resilience towards recurrent shocks. Cash based activities will be used to build household and community productive assets while promoting job and income opportunities for vulnerable households.
This objective will be achieved through the following main activities:
(a) Community and local leaders engagement and consultation to prioritize needs and identify most vulnerable households
(b) Distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) in the form of kits and through seed fairs to farmers. In kind inputs to be accessed through the Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO). Input trade fairs will also be utilized to enhance restocking of livestock assets – mainly small ruminants (sheep and goats) and backyard poultry targeting most vulnerable households including female headed households, widows and widowers, the elderly and households with malnourished mothers and children ;
(c) Conditional cash transfers for asset development (public works) will be distributed to develop community viable assets as well as provide job and income opportunities for households in IPC phase 3 and 4 food insecurity situations. IRC will engage the community to prioritize economically viable assets for rehabilitation and or establishment through conditional cash transfer activities. Cash vouchers will be provided to households who have completed tasks during assets establishment and they will use vouchers to access prioritized services including food and seeds during seed fairs.
(d) Training of extension agents (CAHWs, AEA), crop growers, pastoralists and fisher folks on sustainable production skills and provision of extension services around the package of production inputs and post-harvest handling technologies supported by the project. Furthermore, the project will promote value addition and marketing through improved post-harvest handling, food storage techniques and facilities at household and community level – including household granaries, fish processing, food storage bins and local market structures to be established through cash based interventions.
(e) The Project will promote resilience building through inclusive livelihoods diversification by providing support to women-led VSLAs, income-generating activities, business and life skills trainings and start-up capital provision to youth out of school.
During planning and implementation of project activities, IRC will mainstream gender equity and promote gender inclusiveness, allowing for the most vulnerable and disadvantaged women and girls to access and participate in project activities for household benefits. Participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project will be enhanced through community and local leaders’ consultation at project launch, participatory beneficiary identification and selection (PBIS) including agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 60% female beneficiaries under the project. IRC and the community will set up a project feedback and complaint mechanism consisting of an inclusive feedback and complaint committee (equal men and women representatives) to promote equal opportunities for beneficiaries to voice their interests and feedback. In promotion of complementary programming with other projects and partners, IRC will exercise robust coordination with nutrition, protection and health projects including providing support to referred vulnerable persons affected by chronic food insecurity and malnutrition.
Through this intervention, IRC will support a total of 12,000 (6,000 Women, 3,820 Men, 1,200 Girls, 980 Boys) beneficiaries. By program type: 12,000 through input trade fairs, 2,200 through communal works and cash vouchers, 600 through farmer field schools, 1,000 restocking and pastoral field school, 270 through seed groups , 60 through fisheries support and 2,180 through livelihoods diversification and restoration.

Needs

Over 6.1 million people are reported to be facing severe food insecurity in South Sudan with an estimated 47,000 people in catastrophe by September 2018. Although the situation is expected to slightly improve, deteriorating food insecurity is expected to prevail into 2019 with over 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, and 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). These estimates are all in the presence of planned humanitarian food assistance. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Fall Army Worm (FAW) destroyed crops with a resultant negative impact on the 2018 agricultural season harvest which further worsened the anticipated drop in food harvest benefits of the season. Seasonal flooding affected crop harvests in the main productive counties of Aweil South, East and Centre lowering the anticipated seasonal crop harvests of farmers. Coupled with increasing livestock loss due to livestock diseases (Including Foot and Mouth Disease), the food insecurity situation has exacerbated. Although fishing has progressively increased as a viable livelihood and food source, the lack of appropriate fishing gear and knowledge on fish preservation techniques have limited benefits. These trends coupled with low household coping capacities have negatively impacted on the livelihoods of households in NBeG and resulted in a deteriorating resilience index for the community. Years of unabated conflict in South Sudan has affected all ethnic groups across the country limiting access to crop farms and harvests, and propelled depletion of livestock assets and livelihoods. Although not directly affected by conflict, NBeG remains extremely vulnerable to the worsening economic crisis and disruption of normal trade including livestock. Hyper-inflation (estimated at 75%) continues to drive high food prices and cost of living - a situation which is forcing many people (majority youth) to flee to Northern Sudan in search of income and food . Extremely poor and vulnerable households include the displaced and those without kinship support such as the elderly, disabled, children under five years of age, widows, and female headed households. Youth remain a concern as they continue to be coerced into fighting, lack incentives to cultivate, face limited employment opportunities and are excluded from a stake in the future of the country. Furthermore, constrained humanitarian access due to poor road infrastructure and flooding continues to pose challenges to humanitarian assistance in deep field locations in NBeG. This is affecting some of the most vulnerable households and communities who are in dire need of emergency food security and livelihoods support. Although the new national peace accord does offer hope, conflict and displacement in NBeG, neighbouring states of Warrap and Western Bahr el Ghazal (WBeG), and around the country is still anticipated due to the fragmentation of military factions vying for political power and persistent localized intra and inter communal level conflict. Continued conflict is anticipated to further disrupt livelihoods, and propel poor economic conditions and food insecurity. The currently volatile economy will further contract when the population now 82% ‘officially poor ’ starts to receive significant returnees leading to increased numbers of vulnerable persons facing multiple shocks (climate, pests, public health and probable risk of Ebola).

Activities

IRC will implement the following series of activities during the annual livestock and cropping season calendar for NBeG State. Project integration with Nutrition and WASH projects will be prioritized through beneficiary referral and support, data sharing, reporting and follow up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support- e.g households with malnourished children and mothers referred from the Nutrition projects or cluster in NBEG state. 1. Community engagement and consultation through sensitisation of county community leaders (Commissioners, Senior Agricultural Inspectors, Paramount chiefs, Executive Directors and Payam Administrators), Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and other stakeholders at the ministry and state level in order to create awareness and rally support on the project. 2. Identification of vulnerable households and formalization of potential FFS and APF groups, seed groups and fish farmers groups. These will follow the standard stages of registration and verification, group formalisation (i.e. groups names, management structures, norms, by-laws and guiding constitutions, and registration at Payam and County level offices). 3. Targeted most food insecure and vulnerable households will receive categorized emergency livelihood support in NBeG State. The IRC will organise and conduct agricultural input trade fairs (ITFs) to ensure beneficiaries access seeds, tools, and restocking support. The Input Trade Fairs will include identification and registration of beneficiaries and local seed dealers, holding the Input Trade Fairs, payment to seed dealers and conduct of post distribution monitoring exercises. Indicators:4. Targeting households in IPC phase 3 and 4, IRC will engage the community to prioritize economically viable assets for rehabilitation and or establishment through conditional cash transfer activities. Agricultural and livelihoods assets such as grain storage facilities for seed producer groups, fish preservation and processing units, livestock rearing pens (goat and sheep pens/shades) will be established at the group and household level. Selected beneficiaries capable of providing daily labor during public works will be engaged in communal assets development and upon completion of tasks will be provided with cash vouchers to access seeds, food and other prioritized needs of the beneficiaries at project launch. Indicators:5. In order to build local adaptive and absorptive capacity of target households and the community as well as enhance production and marketing of agricultural products, IRC will identify and train community based extension agents (Community Animal Health Workers and Agricultural Extension Agents) who will in turn train target beneficiaries and continue to provide extension support to them. Indicators:6. Targeting women’s groups, IRC will focus on enhancing access to livelihoods and income opportunities for women through formation of Village Saving and Loan Association groups (VSLA) and support to small Income generating activities (IGAs). Out of school youth will be targeted for entrepreneurship and life skills trainings and later provided with business startup capital (cash grants). Indicators:7. IRC will deploy a team of technical staff to directly implement and monitor the project activities in the field. Through engagement of beneficiaries and local authorities, participatory monitoring and evaluation of the project will be exercised using Gender Inclusive Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (GICFM). Payam level project monitoring committees and routine joint field monitoring exercises will be organised by the project manager. Project M&E systems will include periodic data collection and tracking through standard tools for sector (agronomy, cash vouchers, livestock and VSLA groups). The IRC will ensure project baselines evaluations and end line evaluations are conducted to inform project design.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

At the launch of our livelihood projects, IRC conducts sensitisation and mobilisation meetings with the beneficiary community and leaders to share project scope and expected results of the interventions. During these meetings the target community and stakeholders are made aware of expected deliverables from IRC and community contributions. The meetings are also used to clarify roles and responsibilities of IRC, beneficiaries and local leaders.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

From project needs assessment to design and implementation, IRC exercises inclusiveness by consulting the different beneficiary profiles of Men, Women, Boys and Girls. Through focus group discussions held with the different groups, unique needs of the different vulnerable groups are identified and factored into the project design. Age appropriate project activities are designed for adults and youth delivered through proven field tested modules such as farmer field schools, VSLA, and youth business and life skills through the learn to earn curriculum. Project distributions are usually consulted with beneficiaries to choose an appropriate time and venue that enables safety and dignity of our clients.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Globally IRC has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse by our staff, volunteers and stakeholders. All projects are designed with a strong protection bias to safeguard the rights of beneficiaries and ensure they are protected from abuse. At project start, IRC conducts thorough sensitisation and awareness for project staff, beneficiaries, community leaders, community representatives and elders on zero tolerance for sexual abuse and exploitation. Staff and volunteers are further trained on the core humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, humanity and independence while providing and accessing support. At community level, project feedback and complaint mechanisms are set up to ensure flow of reports and information on project implementation satisfaction and beneficiary rights violation and abuse are addressed through local and state legal structures if identified. Stakeholders found to be in violation of beneficiary rights including sexual exploitation are immediately penalised in accordance with the IRC policy on sexual abuse. The integration of IRC projects ensures that the women’s empowerment and protection projects provides victims of sexual abuse immediate support when they are reported to have been victimised.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

IRC conducts community needs assessments through focus group discussions with women, men and youth groups to determine the different needs of vulnerable groups. Project baselines target beneficiaries to gather relevant data and information that helps in tailoring project implementation in a way that addresses gaps and needs of beneficiaries. Local leaders are equally consulted to validate information and affirm project needs in line with local community development plans. The consultation and involvement of both community and local leaders is central in ensuring acceptability, local participation and support to projects and including participatory monitoring and evaluation of project success.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

By working with community leaders, community representatives and elders, IRC undertakes through sensitisation and awareness raising of stakeholders on the rights and responsibilities of all project parties. Beneficiaries are made aware of their entitlements, freedom of choice and how support is provided. Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Beneficiary access is measured through ability to physically access locations and the number of vulnerable persons reached/supported, disaggregated by male and female.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

During project baselines, IRC conducts focus group discussions with men, women and youth to ascertain their unique safety needs. IRC institutes measures to mitigate and address safety gaps that exist such as provision of shelters for both male and females at input distribution points, setting up temporary shades for mothers and children to rest and breastfeed during public works, and provision of sanitary materials for women and girls through integration with the protection program. Beneficiaries (men and women) are also consulted to decide on the suitable time and locations for project distribution exercises and activities. This allows for women to be involved in activities and distributions while having ample time to attend to their household chores. A relative measure of beneficiary safety is conducted through tracking numbers of all beneficiary gender, complaints addressed through the feedback mechanism and number of safety cases recorded and addressed under the project.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

IRC exercises beneficiary consultation during project design to identify and prioritise needs. Beneficiaries are then involved in the implementation and monitoring of project activities while providing feedback through an inclusive (equal representation by both gender) feedback and complaint mechanism. Post distribution monitoring exercises are conducted to determine level of beneficiary satisfaction and utilisation of inputs provided as a measure of dignity and freedom of choice exercised at project start.
