Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 11.06 million (49.8 % male and 50.2% female), of which 4.6 million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.1 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated; 341,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 480,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. Of the population in need of humanitarian assistance, 70% are accessible from within Syria according to the Syria Response Access Dynamic report by OCHA (December 2018).
Overall, 7.9 million people are estimated to be food insecure (including 0.57 million severely food insecure and 7.3 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to support the coordination of the Food Security and Agriculture Sector to ensure the food security needs of 7.9 million people in need are adequately addressed by all partners responding to the Syria crisis from different hubs.
The objective of the proposed action is to strengthen the effectiveness and quality of the food security responses (including all hubs) based on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination within the Food and Agriculture Sector and across sectors. Project outputs include:
• Output 1: Accessibility of food security and agriculture information for stakeholders response planning improved.
• Output 2: Capacity for national and subnational Food Security and Agriculture sector strengthened.
• Output 3: Capacity for subnational coordination of Food Security and Agriculture sector built.
This project is expected to contribute to improve planning (evidence-based decision–making) and supporting timely, efficient and effective responses to the needs of affected population and avoiding duplication to ensure efficient use of limited available resources.

4 - Needs

Nine years into Syrian crisis, the cumulative number of the food insecure population continues to be high. The complex nature of the crisis is made worse by deteriorating socio-economic situations, continued insecurity in pockets of the country and compounded by multiple population movements/displacement and the destruction of infrastructure and services. Reduced production capacity has caused loss of livelihoods, which in turn has reduced access to food. Of the 11.06 million people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance countrywide in 2020, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are currently food insecure and in need of a range of food security, livelihoods and resilience building assistance. The Food Security and Agriculture Cluster/Sector, co led by FAO and the World Food Programme (WFP), was established to coordinate responses to the sector. Operating from Syria and southern Turkey, the Sector coordinates activities implemented by more than 100 partners across all hubs, and links with inter-sector coordination. FAO and WFP, as cluster lead agencies, jointly contribute to the sector secretariat, which includes coordinators, information management officers and technical experts. Annually, the sector/cluster develops key components of strategies including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) based a number of assessments and technical studies undertaken by the sector and the lead agencies to guide responses. The current strategy of the Sector aims to ensure food security for all crisis-affected Syrians through food assistance, support to agriculture-based livelihoods to increase productive capacity of households complemented with light rehabilitation of critical agriculture-related infrastructures that support production to ensure adequate food consumption and dietary diversity for the most affected people. 5 - Implementation of activities

Currently FAO is operating in thirteen governorates – Aleppo, Al Hassakeh, Ar Raqqa, Dar’a, Deir e Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, As Sweida, Quneitra, Rural Damascus, Tartous and Lattakia. In governorates where UN staff presence is not permitted by UN security regulations, FAO operates through government seconded officers and NGO service providers who are directly supporting implementation of activities and report to FAO.The FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the activities, backstopped by FAO headquarters with the technical support of FAO’s multidisciplinary team in the Regional Office for the Near East and North Africa. FAO is working closely with MAAR to ensure that project goals are achieved through proper technical expertise and monitoring capacity. The implementation will be further supported by:• A central coordination committee that includes representatives from FAO, relevant MAAR directorates, community-based organizations and implementing partners. The committee’s role is to ensure facilitation of the project implementation and application of the criteria for beneficiary selection.• FAO Resilience Officers in the targeted governorates will operationally and technically supervise support coordination functions, training and monitoring of the project.The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity, which can be scaled up at short notice whenever required and aligned to sector response packages. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Aid workers being targeted as a result of being aid worker or coming from particular ethnicity/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish a community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do-no-harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the differential requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement will be aided. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible friction between the two groups. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways to express their concerns and grievance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and assumptions beyond project control and their mitigation measures:1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities and conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework; diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas; establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities.
