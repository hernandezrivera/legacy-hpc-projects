Project Summary

IOM will carry out a DTM-based comprehensive child protection risk and needs assessment in Nampula. The project will further strengthen Reference Groups on Child Protection and Combating Trafficking at province and district levels in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa; build capacities and improve children’s access to specialised MHPSS services.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Throughout the action, which will be closely coordinated with the CP AoR, UNICEF, the national and province level Reference Groups on Child Protection and Combating Human Trafficking (RGs) and other CP partners, IOM will target key child protection services, including those providing MHPSS to children, IDP children, adolescents and caregivers. IOM will aim to contribute to addressing key gaps in child protection knowledge, services and quality, which will have been identified in the upcoming UNICEF-IOM Child Protection Needs and Risk Assessment. Based on IOM’s field-tested methodology combining DTM and qualitative methods a comprehensive child protection risk and needs assessment will be carried out in Nampula province providing an evidence base to government, UN and NGO child protection actors to plan their interventions. Furthermore, the project will focus on further strengthening the RGs on Child Protection and Combating Human Trafficking at Province and District levels in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, increasing their capacities to protect children in the current emergency setting. Particular attention will be paid to strengthening case management and coordination capacities and forging linkages with Community Child Protection Committees. In order to improve children’s access to MHPSS services, IOM will work with the Child Protection AoR to strengthen capacities of child and social services at province and district levels in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, as well as health services and NGO/INGO partners, as well as to increase the referrals of IDP and host community children to such services.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Child Protection Risk and Needs Assessment in Nampula province: Extending the applied methodology combining DTM-based data collection and qualitative methods tested in Cabo Delgado in 2020, the assessment will be expanded to Nampula’s districts hosting most IDPs. The results of the assessment can inform child protection programming for a variety of actors2. Building the Capacity of Reference Groups: IOM will expand its support to case management and coordination capacities of Reference Groups and sub-commitees at province and district levels through trainings, coaching and support to provide direct assistance to child beneficiaries. In particular in Niassa, which has so far not been supported in a targeted manner, non-served district will be suppoted in the set-up and basic training as well as targeted support of the Reference Groups. In Cabo Delgado and Nampula, IOM will work to further improve their work as well as coordination with Community Child Protection Committees. 3. Strengthening MHPSS services for Children: Based on its global expertise and local capacities in providing MHPSS to the IDP and host community population, IOM will strengthen its community based MHPSS interventions focusing on children, including building children’s capacities through informal education, and build the capacities of child and health services to improve the access of children to such services thorugh targeted trainings, on-the-job coaching and coordination efforts.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM employs an integrated protection/MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks through outreach to IDPs thorugh its activists and by conducting regular protection/safety audits in the districts. IOM’s community-based activists are trained to identify vulnerable groups, incl. among children and adolescents, refer them to available services, advocate and assist them to gain equal access to available humanitarian aid and child protection services.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

In the provision referrals and assistance IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower inidviduals and communities assisted.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams and community-based protection activists identify and refer people with special needs including children with physical and mental disabilities to adequate services. IOM’s trained MHPSS professional are able to provide counselling and mitige some mental health impact of displacement, incl. for children. IOM pays special attention to ensuring that children and other persons with disability, incl. albinism, are protected from human trafficking, abuse and exploitation. under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Proposed project does not include any construction work or other activities with significant environmental impact. In IOM’s community-based MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is inluced in some awareness raising sessions with adolescents.
