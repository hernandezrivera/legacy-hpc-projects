Project Summary

The project aims to deliver child protection services and longer-term protection solutions (durable solutions) to 196,281(97,959G, 98,323B) refugee & 4,470 (2256G, 2,214B) host community children, makes total target 200,751 (50%G) and will be implemented in 16 refugee camps as per rationalization mapping and 6 host community sites in Bangladesh. The protection activities will focus on child protection prevention and response interventions, applying child-friendly participation principles, the socio-ecological model and considering AGD and disability inclusion. This will include establishing and running children’s hubs and facilities, recreational activities and providing case management for children at risk. In addition to center-based activities, block-level initiatives for children and adolescents will be provided.
Furthermore, UNHCR will uphold core guiding principles and standards including principles of protection, accountability for the affected population (AAP), PSEA to guide its interventions, ensure quality of services and national standards and enhance its coordination and partnership with different humanitarian actors. The child protection intervention will consider disaster risk reduction (DRR) measures to prevent the recurrent disaster risks occur as a result of cyclone, landslide, fire and floods.
Child protection services will include child protection prevention activities coupled with child protection case management services to respond to incidents of abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence towards children. Structured and unstructured psycho-social support including learn and play will be provided to respond to children who are at risk of protection and in need PSS. UNHCR and partners will apply evidence-based approaches to strength Children’s own knowledge, skills and capacities for self-protection, and those of their parents and families.
Community-Based Child Protection Mechanism will be established and reinforced and Rohingya community-led initiatives in child protection will also be supported in identifying solutions to the protection needs of the children. Through this project, UNHCR will raise the awareness level of the target communities including community members, volunteers and other humanitarian social workers on child protection prevention, mitigation and response through the provision of training for a part of child protection mainstreaming, child safeguarding and PSEA.
UNHCR will improve the quality services provided to the target children by enhancing the capacity of partners including target communities and government through capacity trainings/workshops and strengthening the child protection intervention systems.
UNHCR will support positive parenting sessions to reinforce the capacity and will of the parents to care for their children and adolescent and youth activities/groups to reinforce their resilience of and foster a spirit of development and initiatives in the youth and adolescents. In addition, structured curricula life skills initiatives will be delivered to support adolescents and youth. Life skills for adolescents and GBV awareness, fostering adolescent engagement and social cohesion and peaceful co-existence, as well as skills development opportunities, will be provided for adolescents.
A special initiative/project will be implemented in coordination with the local authorities, the community especially the adolescent girls to support efforts in tackling child marriage.
Early Childhood Development activities will be initiated and supported; linkage will be made with child protection activities to tackle Adverse Childhood Experiences.
UNHCR will work closely with the respective government including but limited to the justice systems, department of social services, refugee, relief and repatriation commission, ministry of women and children affairs to coordinate intervention such as safety and security, legal protection, case management and protection coordination.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

During and after their flight from Myanmar, the Rohingya population faced severe protection threats. Rohingya refugee children continue to face child protection issues, among which child marriage, child labor and exploitation, child neglect, and the impact of COVID-19 continue to weigh heavily on the resilience and capabilities of families to protect their children. According to UNHCR fact sheet report, children represent 52% of the Rohingya refugee population in Cox’s Bazar (CXB) District. Refugee children face considerable risks and threats to their safe and healthy development in their day to day lives, including violence, exploitation, neglect and abuse. Girls are particularly vulnerable to gender-based violence, including sexual abuse, sexual harassment and child marriage. Supporting community-based structures and an enabling environment where children and adolescents can reach their full potential and make informed decisions on issues that affect their lives is a UNHCR priority. Children with disabilities, who are estimated at 10% of the population, face stigmatization and considerable barriers in access to critical services, including child protection services.According to the CPIMS June 2022 report, the main challenges of children facing in Cox’s Bazar operation areas are neglect, child labour, separation, physical abuse serious medical needs, child marriage and emotional and mental challenges which constituted 2,550, 590, 550, 540, 530, 520 and 300 respectively. Young teenage girls also face considerable challenges in moving around the camps safely, and families are frequently resorting to child marriage as mechanism for their protection as well as a result of harmful gender norms and practices. Married and unmarried girls face difficulties in accessing school and protection services and are exposed to life threatening risks like early pregnancies and intimate partner violence. Young teenage boys also face specific risks, including child labour and recruitment into drug- and gang-related activities, and may also be married early, forcing them to abandon development activities like education. Very young children are also affected by child labour as families struggle to make ends meet. All the above come to add on top of incidents of physical violence, emotional abuse, low to no child-friendly activities and lack of adolescents’ engagement activities.Family separation is also a challenge facing many children who are often left alone in the camp by their parents and family, who relocate to third countries or return to Myanmar in search of livelihood opportunities or survival means. Those children face many problems including inaccessibility to food, lack of an appropriate alternative family care and most of the time end up caught in child labor and exploitation.The low level of awareness on various dangers and hazards produces incidents like drowning, road traffic accidents, and children are also vulnerable to trafficking, abduction, and violence in the community. The Government of Bangladesh and humanitarian actors have combined forces to tackle child protection issues, but more efforts are needed to address the challenges facing children in their development and the full enjoyment of their rights. Thus, in collaboration with other partners, UNHCR will implement a holistic child protection prevention, risk mitigation and response interventions to address the priority needs of refugee and host community children as per the identified needs, protection principles and standards.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is aligned with the rationalization principles and process. UNHCR has worked with Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh for over 30 years, providing refugee and child protection services for all refugee men, women, girls and boys. UNHCR’s international protection mandate underpins our continuous commitment as both a first responder and provider of last resort, alongside the Government of Bangladesh, to ensure refugees’ access to protection, including child protection. UNHCR works hand in hand with all humanitarian agencies to ensure that all Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. UNHCR has participated in and contributed to the Child Protection Sub-Sector coordination fora to advance the rationalization process and applied its expertise in refugee protection to ensure attaining the principles of the rationalization process. UNHCR has provided to the sub-sector a matrix of its interventions in different camps and advocated for its interventions, without compromising the interventions of other actors, and on some occasions, UNHCR advocated near the sub-sector for some actors to be considered to intervene in the camps to reinforce the child protection response considering their capacity. Note that UNHCR's submission includes outstanding support for refugee protection case management and registration activities in all camps, which is conducted in cooperation with other CP agencies and does not duplicate comprehensive CP services. UNHCR's submission also includes all areas where UNHCR is currently implementing since this project will begin on 01 January, and time will be needed in 2023 to implement a phased withdrawal and handover to new actors as necessary.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

52% of Rohingya refugees are children, and UNHCR’s protection work, as part of general protection, and concerned with the wellbeing of children, has developed specific procedures, tools and engaged relevant partners to foster the rights of the children and respond to the needs linked to their particularity and vulnerabilities. The Framework for the Protection of Children (https://www.unhcr.org/protection/children/50f6cf0b9/framework-protection-children.html), and the recent 2021 UNHCR Best Interests Procedure Guidelines (https://www.refworld.org/docid/5c18d7254.html) are example of recent tools and procedures development to refine and enhance child protection activities and services globally, in the end of identifying long-term solutions to their refugee situation.

3.2 - Gender

According to the CPIMS August 2022 report, among the number of children who are engaged in child labour, 93% of them are boys while 53% of girls are victims of child marriage and hence UNHCR will address the priority protection needs of children according to their needs, gender and age. Gender disparities based on cultural, social norms and values as well as socio-political contexts is the main concern to the operation in terms of accessing and benefiting services and opportunities. UNHCR strive to ensure that the specific needs of children and adolescents are reflected in GBV programs via a consultative programs design approach, because girls and boys require specific care and age-appropriate interventions according to their age and maturity. UNHCR and partners also engage with Rohingya communities in various camps, including men and boys, to mitigate and prevent GBV against children, especially girls.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Across all programmes and projects, UNHCR and partners have collaborated with the CPSS to provide child safeguarding training to all staff in direct or indirect contact with children, and to refugees in general. Child safeguarding trainings are designed to raise the awareness of all partners on their responsibility to establish and implement procedural safeguards to guarantee the right of a child to have their best interests taken as a primary consideration in all decisions that affect them, including those not directly related to individual case management. UNHCR and partners will conduct risk assessments with children in order to identify risks and mitigation measures, aiming to make all spaces safer and more child-friendly and child-centered.

3.4 - Diversity

The first principle of the Convention on the Rights of the Child is non-discrimination. UNHCR has the responsibility to ensure necessary measures to include all children regardless of their status in programmes and projects which affect them and have their voice heard. UNHCR and partners have collaborated with various sectors, sub-sectors and working groups to put in place children’s and adolescents’ clubs and groups, including all children of all background, to foster their rights to voice their needs and concerns and have their contributions considered when deciding on services they benefit. Child protection services also, like child-friendly spaces, learning centers, case management reception rooms, to mention a few, are designed with diverse backgrounds of children in mind to foster a sense of belonging and consideration from UNHCR and partners in the Rohingya community children.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In the same spirit of applying the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, UNHCR and partners, in coordination with the CPSS and other sectors and clusters, are committed to foster a non-discriminatory approach in the design and implementation of their programmes and activities as reflected for example in providing services to Rohingya children and local communities children living in the vicinities of the camps, consider the best interests of the individual children when providing assistance and delivering case management services, give primacy to the survival and development of refugee children who fled the crisis since 2017 and children born in the camp by, for example, putting in place health, nutrition and ECD services in the package of services offered to children, and in every interventions where children are concerned, have their views taken into consideration.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR is facilitating the protection-led interagency protection emergency response (PERU) inthe targeted camps and host communities, and guided by an emergency prepardness and response plan (EPRP) to address risks of children during emergency situation in the target areas. UNHCR includes monsoon, cyclone, landslide, fire and other disaster preparedness aspects its communication with communities activities, and also in community initiatives. When disasters occur, UNHCR targets protection services, including child protection, to respond to the needs of those affected, with a specific focus on women, children and persons with specific needs. UNCHR and partners provide specific responses to disaster events through mobile psychosocial support as well as assessment and response to the specific needs of affected children. UNHCR built facilities include consideration of environmental and climate concerns, and DRR.
