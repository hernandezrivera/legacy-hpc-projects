Project Summary

UNFPA has a unique mandate to provide lifesaving support through coordination and implementation of integrated SRH services as well as GBV prevention and response. UNFPA Sudan has committed to scaling up humanitarian response and national capacities to prevent and response to gender based violence, through effective, integrated and appropriate programs. The current changes in Sudan is opening new window for UNFPA as GBV sub sector lead and other humanitarian actors to introduce supportive environments in which the survivors rights and wishes are respected, their safety is ensured and they are treated and managed properly. In Sudan the main humanitarian needs resulted from different factors on top are the insecurity and multiple conflicts namely in Darfur, BN and South Kordofan, add to that the natural disasters inform of droughts and flooding as well as disease outbreaks. After a year of civil unrest and political change, humanitarian needs continue to rise. Some 9.3 million people – 23 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2020. The transitional government is prioritizing peace and ending the economic crisis, issues closely intertwined with the drivers of humanitarian need in the country. Women, children and elderly people represent almost 80% and remain most vulnerable.
Through the proposed action, UNFPA will ensure that the affected population IDPs, returness, and vulnerable host community are actively engaged as partners in developing plans related to their protection and right based in nature. This approach involve direct consultation with women, girls, boys and men and those at risk to build community based protection networks. Integration and strengthening of GBV services within the health facilities will be advocated for with the Health sector.

1 - Needs

There are approximately 1.8 million people in need for Gender based violence (GBV) programming (prevention and Response). Women and girls especially those living in localities prone to armed conflict and tribal clashes and the IDP communities constitute more than 55% of total population and 27% less than 18 years old. This group is at greater risk of protection as they engage in daily chores such as farming, fetching water & firewood, and they are exposed to threats, harassment, and sexual violence from armed men. Women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV, poverty and violations of basic rights due to insecurity, low economic status and lack of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women's rights and GBV due to cultural and societal norms. Female heads of households (38 percent of women and girls), women in prison and pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls and children both girls and boys are the most at risk of GBV. According to available information from service providers, women and girls account for over 90 percent of survivors, and although grossly under reported, cases of sexual violence against men and boys have been reported. Rape, physical violence, FGM, early/ forced marriages, denial of resources opportunities and verbal abuse are some of the common violations against women and girls reported. UN Women study on GBV and women access to justice in Darfur reflected that 80 percent of women highlighted domestic violence as problem, 70 percent identified economic violence as an issue while 5 percent cited Rape as a concern. Displacement and poverty heighten cases of domestic violence and this reported to increase in the IDP camps as husbands' demand income from their wives involved in income-generating activities (IGAs).Survivors of GBV lack adequate access to Quality specialized lifesaving services, reporting mechanisms and justice. Over 90 per cent of Sudan’s localities lack specialized GBV services (such as clinical management of rape, specialized psychosocial support and counseling, legal aid and case management). Health centers are often the first point of call where people seek help, and they are inadequately equipped. Across all Darfur states, only 14 to 21 percent of health facilities provide CMR services according to the GBV sub sector mapping. An Inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessment to Sortoni, North Darfur in November 2019, findings indicated that an estimated 60 per cent of households are female-headed. Generally, women have become the main providers for their families and households. When men venture outside the camps, various armed actors target, and killed them. Furthermore, health and psychosocial services for GBV survivors are not available, and where available access is a challenge for IDPs. The available government facilities are understaffed, and in some the staff lack capacity; furthermore, the facilities are overwhelmed, lack basic equipment and drug stock-outs are common. Where available, women centers act as entry point for women to access available services mainly psychological first aid offered at the centers and referral to health facilities or the police and thus when non-functional, access to services for GBV survivors is compromised. The lack of succinct information and data affects resource mobilization as well as advocacy. Most sectors are yet to embrace GBV mainstreaming and integration thus they have not prioritized GBV mitigation activities in their response. GBV coordination across all affected states needs strengthening to ensure harmonized approach to service delivery. As UNFPA supported the development of the first national GBV Standard Operational Procedure, other states will have the opportunity for developing states context SOPs and Referral pathway. Building on the results of GBV interventions, UNFPA will continue investing on GBV prevention, mitigation and response using the survivor - centered based approach .

2 - Activities / Outputs

Strategic interventions: 1. Support to community structures (women centers & community based protection network) and community based referral mechanisms: • Referral systems will be established for the survivors of GBV through support to establishing community protection networks and women centers. The centers function as an entry point for referrals to other services as well as safe spaces for offering psychosocial support/ counseling with a minimum package of activities for social interaction, awareness creation on GBV risks and prevention measures as well as basic capacity building of vulnerable women on life skills and basic vocational skills. The centers will be standard in all targeted localities with space for family and children interaction.• Support the community based referral structures and mechanism for women in need of GBV services. • Identify and training of Community protection networks on GBV prevention and response and on women and girls Safe Space management.• Community awareness campaigns: The awareness sessions will be through radio programmes, mass campaigns combined with participatory educative theater (PET), community discussion forums, tea talks etc. It is envisaged that this will result in more survivors seeking services, more engagement of the community in FGM abandonment , Child marriage, GBV prevention and mitigation as well as reduced stigma for GBV survivors. 2. Capacity Building, Case management/ psychosocial support and referral:• Training and mentor-ship of social and health workers on case management including referrals and basic counseling skills.• Sensitization sessions for police, army and at the family and child protection units (FCPU), community leaders, women groups, teachers and other service providers on GBV and referrals.• Capacity building of partners on GBV topics, including managing GBV in Emergency. 3. Strengthening the National system and Institutional Support:• Training on prevention and response to GBV in Emergency, GBV in contingency planning and emergency preparedness, and minimum standards for response.• Provide technical support through deployment of GBV national experts and assistants, and international experts – prefer Arabic speaker.• Development and operationalizing of GBV Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) at both National and states level.• The Regional Emergency GBV Advisor (REGA) continue the technical support to GBV programming in Sudan especially for new government authorities. 4. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits: • Dignity kits (DKs) containing the most basic sanitary items (sanitary pads / cotton, underwear, toothpaste/ toothbrush) and relevant dress/ clothes will be procured & distributed to women and girls of reproductive age targeting the most vulnerable among them especially in areas with new displacement.• Provision of PHKs serves both a health purpose (prevention of potential infections when using unhygienic material for management of menstruation; psychosocial and mental well-being by allowing women and girls to maintain their dignity). Also for a protection purpose (by allowing women and girls to pursue their daily chores including accessing services which they might not be able to do during their menstruation).• DKs distribution program also serve as an entry point to discussion on GBV concerns with women & girls. 5. Strengthening coordination and advocacy:• Through the Sub Sector, UNFPA will work on strengthening coordination including with justice and legal collaboration, capacity building, expansion of quality GBV response and advocacy for action and resources.• Mapping of GBV services and strengthening/ establish of referral mechanisms.• Conduct period rapid assessments to determine survivor’s needs and access to services.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The activities and implementation design considered the neutral impact on environment with mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers will be rehabilitated using the no environmental harmful materials, The promotion of fuel-efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during disposable dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized on environmental friendly ways of disposing the used sanitary wear. in addition empowering women and girls through GBV education and awareness sessions, and income generation skills limited there exposures to risk of firewood and water.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is focusing on effective participation of targeted communities across gender, age and vulnerability throughout the project cycle to ensure GBV survivors and those at risk are not exposed to additional harm. In addition the will take into the account mainstreaming of meaningful access, safety, and dignity when implementing activities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected population is core in the project response andwill be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. The regular monitoring visit to the field and focus groups discussions are one of the methods that will be used to measures the satisfaction of the community. UNFPA will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services. In addition UNFPA is an active member of the UN country PSEA technical and coordination team.
