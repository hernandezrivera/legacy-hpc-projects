1 - Needs

The humanitarian context of the WB, including EJ, remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli occupation. Protracted socioeconomic and protection diﬃculties continue. Representing one-third of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem, many of the 843,931 Palestine refugees registered with UNRWA, experience challenges marked by continued violence, forced displacement, administrative and punitive demolitions as well as restrictions on access to services and markets, particularly for those living or owing in Area C and in the Seam Zone. In addition, the stagnant economy and lack of employment opportunities erodes the purchasing power of Palestine refugees thus classifying them as one of the poorest segments of Palestinian society. In 2019 Palestine refugees’ unemployment rate reached 17 per cent, and 21 per cent for those living in camps. The World Bank estimates that the economic contraction in West Bank induced by the COVID-19 pandemic will have the most significant impact on poor and near-poor households. According to the World Bank, the COVID-19 crisis has affected over 24,000 businesses and over 71,000 workers in the West Bank. This gives rise to the threat of more than a doubling in poverty rates from fourteen to 34 percent. Furthermore, besides the shocks to incomes, poor and near-poor households will also suffer disproportionally from higher food prices. Food insecurity have, according to the 2018 SEFSec decreased in recent years. The number of food insecure refugees remains relatively high however (11 per cent, 113,000 individuals, of which 46,500 are severely food insecure) compared to non-refugees (9 per cent). Those in deep poverty among refugees remain vulnerable, and poverty contributes to their food insecurity levels. Females are furthermore considered vulnerable as their unemployment rate reached 32 per cent in in the ﬁrst half of 2019 (15 per cent for male), as a result to several factors including availability of job opportunity and cultural norms inhibiting women’s participation on the labour market, further contributing to their vulnerability. Female headed households are thus especially vulnerable to poverty, furthermore affecting the food insecurity. In 2018, UNRWA faced a critical funding shortfall under its Emergency interventions which led to the discontinuation of most of its food insecurity interventions. This resulted in 90,000 refugees (48,000 which reside in camps) at risk of deepening poverty. Since UNRWA started discontinuing its emergency program, it has witnessed a signiﬁcant increase in the number of emergency beneﬁciaries applying to enroll in its main relief programme, the ECard program. The reassessment of beneﬁciaries’ poverty status revealed a signiﬁcant number of refugees that are living in abject poverty, less than 2 dollars a day. This forced UNRWA to reconsider its approach in halting emergency services & decide to integrate this load into its E-Card programme. This decision was instigated by the dire ﬁnancial situation of those families which will force them to resort to negative coping mechanisms that would have detrimental effect on their lives if they were left without assistance. The e-card modality, which provides cash to vulnerable refugees through bank debit cards contributes to empower beneﬁciaries, providing more freedom of choice and enabling greater ﬂexibility in household spending. UNRWA plans to continue this important programme in 2021.

2 - Activities

In 2021, UNRWA will seek to provide unconditional and non-restricted cash assistance to a minimum of 24,146 abject poor refugee individuals (although the number of needy households is bigger than this) in the West Bank and its 19 refugee camps through its E-card modality. This modality provides cash to abject poor refugees through bank debit cards dedicated to this purpose. Beneficiaries were identified in an assessment that was undertaken by the Agency over the period of 2017-18. UNRWA reassessed all the households served through emergency food security programmes operational in the West Bank. UNRWA’s social workers visited over 35,000 households located all over the West Bank to update their poverty profile and consumption information. The poverty level of the households was then determined using a proxy means testing formula (PMTF), which was harmonized with MoSD’s. In addition to consumption levels, this formula takes into consideration other aspects of vulnerability, such as the presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics and accessibility to utilities, as well as household composition (e.g. the number of elderly and persons with disabilities, and female-headed households). The PMTF uses the abject poverty line which is set at US$1.79 per person per day. This represents the minimum cost of food that satisfies the average nutritional needs. Families below the abject poverty line cannot meet their basic food needs. Heads of households will receive basic assistance of NIS 125 per person each quarter through e-card (bank debit cards). Social workers will continuously maintain contact with beneﬁciaries; households consider the social worker as one of their major sources of information. Beneficiaries will also be able to provide their feedback and get help addressing any challenges they face through the troubleshooting system and hotline established for e-card beneficiaries. Furthermore, the Agency created a number of tools to monitor the implementation and targets set through for assistance provided through the e-card modality including a bi-annual post-distribution monitoring tool in addition to satisfaction surveys. The tools make sure to be used on samples representing vulnerable groups, including women, PwDs, and the elderly. The tools monitor several aspects that are important to vulnerable groups, including – but not limited to – accessibility to services by PwDs, and gender dynamics in the family. The feedback collected from beneficiaries through the various tools is used to develop the programme. The results of the monitoring tools reflected the positive contribution of the e-card system on people’s food security and protection from falling into deeper poverty. Beneﬁciaries have reported that the new modality has improved their dignity and freedom of choice. The majority of respondents stated that obtaining cash through this modality has been easy while more than 90 per cent agreed that the e-card gives them ﬂexibility in regards to how and when to use the assistance. The cash provided is mainly meant for food rather than non-food items. The monitoring tools also showed that, generally, female members of the household are the main decision-makers in terms of deciding how to use the assistance.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWAs Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneﬁciaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneﬁciaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWAs Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritizes the "do-no-harm"-principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery in education, health, relief and social services, microﬁnance, and infrastructure and camp improvement. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of speciﬁc rights such as the socioeconomic rights to food, water, health and education. By addressing such concerns through ‘do-no-harm’ and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneﬁciaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneﬁciaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well as in staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action. UNRWA has developed mechanisms which contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneﬁciaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.
