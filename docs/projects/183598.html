Project Summary

This proposal is fully in line with the strategic objective 1 and 2 of Education Cluster, SDG4 and CRC art.28 and 29, aiming to guarantee accessibility to quality education for children living in Area C and H2 communities, and ensuring that school staff and students affected by the education-related violations and COVID-19 pandemic benefit from protective environment.
TCC and WW-GVC are active members of the Education cluster and are committed to promote the quality education in Palestine, supporting the MoE in providing adequate education services as rehabilitation of education infrastructure, responding to the lack of foreseen implementation by the duty bearers, due to the fiscal crisis and financial cuts from major donors.
The outbreak of COVID-19 has been a significant shock on the education system and Palestinian society more broadly. The severity and prevalence of COVID-19 in the West Bank and Gaza has varied since the coronavirus was first detected in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) in early March 2020.
Referring to the Education cluster status reports and a recent study published by NRC, there has been a steep decline in the well-being and academic engagement of students across Palestinian territories. As the findings from this study states the fact that the vulnerability status of schools in the West Bank became less of a determinate of well-being in 2020 highlights how eliminating the need to travel to and from school, along with learning in unsafe environments, may serve to improve the well-being of these young people. Israeli armed forces are frequently present outside schools. Israeli soldiers and settlers regularly attack schools and school students, and destroy Palestinian homes and infrastructure, especially in Area C of the West Bank. Eliminating the need to travel to school also has gender and age specific implications. Adolescent boys are more vulnerable to violence and are more likely to be targeted by Israeli forces at checkpoints for stops and searches when travelling to school. This is also supported by stakeholder interviews, conducted in January 2021.
TCC in partnership with WeWorld-GVC aims to reach children in the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank, through carrying on a combination of soft and hard component activities aiming to increase equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education in 26 communities in Area C/H2, in line with the mandates, strategies, and expertise of TCC and WW-GVC in order to contribute to the overall safety and dignity to children and their families. The proposed activities will target 26 communities in the West Bank through construction of two elementary schools in 2 vulnerable herding communities, direct rehabilitation of 19 existing school structures in need for rehabilitation, in addition to construction of 3 KGs and rehabilitation of 5 KGs either internally (Interior repairs, furnishing, WASH facilities) or externally (addition of classrooms, play area). Moreover, as part of soft activities, students attending schools will be targeted in extracurricular activities, ongoing remedial classes, counselling sessions, in addition to capacity building trainings for teachers.
The actions will benefit 5304 of which 4782 students (1798- boys, 2984 girls), 522 Teachers and counsellors (160 men, 362 women), including 32 PWDs, and reach official decision-makers, media and donors through advocacy actions, and at least 240 individuals living in the 26 targeted communities in Hebron, Tubas, Jenin, Ramallah, and Bethlehem governorate.
TCC and WW-GVC will implement the project proposal in coordination with MoE in line with signed agreements that facilitate the entrance and interventions in the government schools selected.

1 - Needs

In Area C/H2 context, WW-GVC through their Community Protection Approach (CPA) were able to collect quantitative and qualitative data related to Education sector in 160 communities, aiming to systematize protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities and coping strategies, with a community engagement method, which was last updated In February 2021. Additionally, TCC and WW-GVC implemented a need assessment in collaboration with MoE for a number of targeted communities in Education Sector supported by several field visits. According to WW-GVC data, the vulnerability in Education sector for the targeted communities is reflected through the high protection index, that ranges between 50% -90%, which signals an urging need for targeting Education sector as an immediate action. The combination of factors contributing to this are derived from the lack of schools and KGs inside the community or within a reasonable distance for children to be able to access them on feet, along with the risks faced by the students to reach their schools during their round-trip, in addition to the status of enrollment and the high rates of dropouts related to the long distance to reach schools. Since 2020 to date, these challenges have been compounded by the outbreak of COVID-19, which led to the prolonged closures of schools and kindergartens, as part of the measures adopted to contain the pandemic. While the Ministry of Education (MoE) and UNRWA have put in place various distance learning programs to be used in the new academic year, students in the targeted communities were not able to access these programs due to living in homes without internet connectivity, or not being able to collect the printed self-learning materials , or even due the inadaptability of their schools to cope with the remote learning. Therefore, many children with disabilities and resource room students, and children in preschools were excluded from online education.According to the research study led by TCC in 2021; The first need at this stage is updating the interventions in emergency plans to bridge the gap in early childhood stage, and thus in order to reintegrate them into schools, so that they are prepared for the next stages. Focusing on the children of the years 2019-2020 and 2020-2021. The second need stated is to assess the capabilities and competencies of female workers and educators in kindergartens and re-examine the needs of their competencies, so to ensure that they have scientific degrees that qualify them to work with children in the early stages of childhood. In addition to defining more specialized capacity-building programs related to the problems that children suffer, such as intervening with children in times of emergency, and programs adapting to the educational loss The third need addresses is to Re-examination of the kindergarten sites and infrastructure in order to provide a healthy and appropriate environment for children in kindergartens. Many government kindergartens depend on the support of local communities, and most of them lack the appropriate conditions for education, especially protection methods, educational games and advanced tools. On a specific level, two targeted communities who are still not served by any educational facilities (school, KGs), are in need for school and KGs construction, as students at all educational levels are forced to walk on feet for very long distances in order to reach the nearest schools that in some cases can reach 12kms, which takes hours per round trip. Additionally, the bad condition of the internal road in some areas increases the protection risks faced by children who walk carrying their heavy bags under extreme weather condition, along with the natural hazards (snakes, scorpions, wild animals etc), along with the harassments from settlers and IF during their trip; especially when there is a security escalation or high presence of settlers/IF or Israeli officials. Some students cope with the long distance through living with their other families and relatives in the village where the schools are located, where they go back to their shelter in the community by the end of every week, which also causes family separation due to sending part of the family away. Other students (especially girls), negatively cope with the lack of educational services through school drop-out, which are found to be increasing in Bedouin communities. In regards to the internal environment of the existing schools in the targeted communities, the joint assessment carried out by TCC and WeWorld-GVC with close coordination with MoE in September/ October 2021 during the proposal elaboration phase showed that 19 schools and 5 KGs in Area C/H2, in Hebron (Umm Al Kheir, Qilqes, As Simiya, Ar Rifa'iyya , Um Ashoqhan Tel Rumedeh , Dkaika, Isfey in Masafer Yatta), Tubas (Kherbit Atuf, Bardala), Jenin (Kherbit MasudTi’innik), Ramallah (Ras al Tin), Jericho (Marj Ghazal, Al Muarajat East, Marj Ghazal, Marj Naja, An Nuwei'ma Al-Fauqa), Salfit (,Marda, Kefil Haris), Qalqilia (Arab ar Ramadin Al Shamali, Arab ar Ramadin Al Janubi), Nablus (Kherbit Tana) and Bethlehem (ar Rashayida, Al Jab'a, Barriyat Za'tara, Khirbet an Nahla) are in a critical condition and need urgent rehabilitation: the schools’ interior environment is very poor, lacks proper lightening and ventilation, the classrooms suffer from water leakage, the humid atmosphere in the school imposes health concerns on both children and teachers; additionally, there is lack of laboratories or multipurpose rooms for any educational activity (including online courses), in addition to their lack of adaptability to meet the needs of children with disabilities. As for the external environment, 10 schools and 5 KGs are lacking healthy sanitation units for students, and no play area for the children. Moreover, the lack of space leads the children to express their energy through violence, as stated by the schools’ principles.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Complaint response mechanism is part of WW-GVC and TCC accountability and commitment strategies to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneﬁciaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints or suggestions in a secured way and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints are reported to the CRM focal point through a hotline number. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the focal point analyses complaints and classiﬁes each of them according to the internal classiﬁcation system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC or TCC team. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call by WW-GVC’s or TCC’s MEAL oﬃcer.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

TCC and WeWorld-GVC provide assistance in a way which endeavors to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Needs and risks are balanced through the Community Protection Approach where community is involved in a thorough assessment to understand the vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the programming reflects preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs in a participative way. Considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWD. The eﬃciency is encouraging all local actors and community involvement on the overall steps of the suggested intervention and prevent unintended negative impacts. Through this inclusive and comparative risk analysis organizations increase the accountability allowing themselves to quickly respond to any unintentional negative impacts created by the intervention. The proposal elaborates a remedial action to hazards that caused a change in educational needs and affects the ability of girls and boys to attend school. Which has an impact on the levels of literacy, numeracy and wellbeing of the affected population. TCC and WW-GVC aim to reduce the vulnerability of the beneficiaries caused by the systematic settler and IF abuse violence creating an increase of protection risks. The core action aims to create a safe, secure and inclusiveness environment in schools and realize supporting actions that focus on the need for physical protection, resilience building and psychosocial-emotional protection. The Do No Harm principle is assured during all the project cycle, guaranteeing that every project- phases and steps are endorsed by the beneficiaries themselves or their families, to prevent -in so doing- any potential negative consequence. The assessment lead by WW-GVC in Area C and the needs assessment carried out for the project writing focus on the understanding of the needs of the communities, specifically girls and boys. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by vulnerable groups such as women, whose participation is strongly encouraged to any decision-making process, and girls and boys in line with article 12 of the Convention on the Right of the Child. Specific attention will be also given to the lessons learned coming from previous similar projects of both partners. The elaboration of each single activity will take into account the different needs and investigate how girls and boys are differently affected. In the implementation phase, monitoring mechanism will guarantee direct interaction with the beneficiaries to make sure that their concerns and needs are heard and to guarantee their right to participate and be agents of change. Moreover, WeWorld-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system, allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions and concrete implementation. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration in the design of tailored activities to address the different needs and risks that girls, women, boys and men are currently facing.COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and TCC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project follows a sustainable humanitarian approach aiming to provide remedial and environmental-building action, ensuring equity, sustainability and impartial access to education. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinion. Impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every school targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers as a way of accountability and transparency. On another note, schools targeted for needs assessments have been collected through trustable sources, as public data from OCHA or the Ministry of Education, avoiding random targeting. During the design phase, several schools have been assessed by TCC and WeWorld-GVC staff in coordination with the Ministry of Education, to identify the different needs and potential activities and beneficiaries. The identification of the beneficiaries and activities followed the criteria of higher need and higher vulnerability. The proposal targets girls and boys students and KG children, besides the educational staff, as they are considered as vulnerable groups due to the high exposure to Israelis attacks while accessing to education services and schools, they are generally not safe as are exposed to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and the risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. Children with disabilities will be also targeted by the project and their specific needs will be analysed and addressed. During the previous needs assessment, no cases of severe disability have been identified but to guarantee the right to education for all, the activities will be implemented with an inclusive approach
