Project Summary

To increase access to life-saving sustained safe drinking Water, Gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, and promotion of good hygiene practices to support 8,500 people including PWDs in Galkacyo, Burtinle, Garowe, Galdogob, IDPs, and Host Communities in crisis and/or emergency affected Women, Girls, Boys, and Men. These groups shall be involved at the various project stages from planning to actual implementation to ensure their views and needs are fully captured. The project's cluster objective 1 will require 65% financial requirements while Cluster objective 2 will require 35% project finances for efficient and successful implementation.
This will be achieved through increased access to adequate, safe, and sustainable water following the rehabilitation and construction of flood-proof water infrastructure proposed under this project, improvement of access to gender-sensitive sanitation food-prone prone settlements, and promoting a culture of good hygiene practices in order to reduce the related protection and disease risks especially among women, girls and children in the targeted locations. As an initial lifesaving intervention, PMWDO will prioritize the provision of vouchers for hygiene kits to 2000 extremely vulnerable households to increase their household water storage capacity thereby reducing the frequent trips to water points, which will as a result reduce congestion at the existing facilities. Within the first 5 months of the project, PMWDO will establish and capacity build water committees and rehabilitate 10 strategic water infrastructures whose combined water supply will benefit 8,000 individuals. To ensure improved quality of the water both at households and supported water points, PMWDO will undertake initial tests and regular water treatment at the source (bucket and batch chlorination). Further, PMWDO will provide water treatment tablets to 2000 vulnerable households who will be sensitized about household water treatment and safe storage. PMWDO will utilize the capacity of the local market by promoting market-based programming in WASH through the use of vouchers (for NFIs) and contracting suitable construction services from the local market following extensive market assessments and monitoring. In order to improve the hygiene knowledge and practices among the target populations, PMWDO will determine the hygiene knowledge and practice gaps through KAP surveys, one (baseline survey) at project inception to inform approaches to awareness-raising and composition of emergency hygiene kits to be distributed to the most vulnerable households. An online KAP survey will be conducted towards the end of the intervention to gauge the impact made in water, latrine, environmental and personal hygiene, and in general, behavior change. To ensure community ownership and sustainability, PMWDO will help establish 10 water resource management committees 50 members (30 women and 20 men) water source management committees. Where possible, a technician from each village and a representative from the local administration will be incorporated into the committee. All the members will be trained in basic operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and orderly access to the source. The training will be conducted jointly by PMWDO staff, Ministry of Energy, Water and Natural Resources staff, and the private sector (suppliers of spare parts). To instill a sustained culture of good hygiene practice, the project will train and effectively engage the existing 24 community health volunteers recruited from the targeted villages to enable mainstream hygiene promotion activities into the communities. The hygiene promotion intervention is expected to benefit 8500 individuals.
PMWDO seeks to improve access to safe and affordable water and clean sanitation facilities in rural communities and sites where the internally displaced have settled.
