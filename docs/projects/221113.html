Project Summary

IRC will partner with Local Actors for a 24-month intervention in response to the conflict in Cameroon's NWSW regions to enhance community-based service delivery, targeting the most vulnerable, including HR violation victims, GBV survivors, and at-risk children. The Action will introduce livelihood components for resilience alongside continued efforts to address protection needs, mental health support, and capacity reinforcement in underserved areas, with an expanded footprint including new localities in hard-to-reach areas. Additionally, the Action will encompass a disaster preparedness component to ensure communities are better equipped to prevent and respond to disasters through proactive risk anticipation and response strategies. IRC will extend the education activities into Hile Alifa in Logone et Chari Division, FN region. Over the past year, Hile Alifa has received several waves of IDPs seeking refuge from cross-border attacks on non-state armed groups. Currently, no other actor is providing an educational response. The IRC will work with a local partner to provide an education response targeting IDP and host-community out-of-school children and build on lessons learned from the ongoing education work in the FN.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The conflict in the NWSW regions remains a protracted crisis characterized by its long-lasting nature and enduring humanitarian challenges. Years of violence have resulted in recurring displacements, perpetuating cycles of instability and persistent humanitarian needs. Communities are contending with the immediate aftermath of violence and the long-term consequences of sustained conflict, which include the erosion of social structures, prolonged disruption of essential services such as healthcare, education, and justice, and a general decline in living standards. Furthermore, the ongoing cholera epidemic and attacks on healthcare workers have added another layer of complexity and urgency to the crisis. Furthermore, as long as the conflicts persist in the two regions, population movements will continue. In 2023, protection activities and humanitarian responses encountered ongoing challenges, including frequent lockdowns imposed by NSAGs, ongoing military operations, and access constraints resulting from natural disasters or poor road infrastructures. These factors create an unfavorable operating and protection environment for civilians, notably involving violations of humanitarian law, infringements on the right to freedom of movement, and restricted access to essential services. These challenges also have devastating consequences on livelihoods and resilience-building efforts. Considering the operational context and protective environment described above, there are no preparedness actions implemented by government institutions or humanitarian actors critical to anticipate, mitigate, and respond to identified protection risks and promote the safety and dignity of girls, boys, women, and men of all ages. These actions help create risk-proof response interventions by designing them to reduce immediate and imminent risks. The IRC intends to include a disaster preparedness component to strengthen the capacity of affected communities to mitigate the effects of any hazard/threats through preparedness actions based on risk assessment and analysis, risk knowledge, and EWS. The IRC will work closely with local authorities, EWS members, CBOs members, and other humanitarian actors to monitor the operational context and activate crisis modifier modality to address promptly, immediate, and life-saving needs resulting from a rapid-onset crisis such as mass displacement due to an attack or any disaster. In the FN, the education situation remains alarming. Since June 2022, the Hile Alifa district, like other districts bordering the Lake Chad basin and the border with Nigeria (Fotokol, Makari, Hille Alifa, Darack, etc.), has been facing successive crises linked to flooding, the cholera health crisis, and repeated security crises, with population displacements within these districts. The needs assessment carried out in November 2022 by the RRM following an incursion by non-state armed groups into the arrondissements of Hile Alifa and Darack reported 2019 displaced households assessed (17,609 individuals): 889 households in Hilé Alifa 1, 673 households in Hile Alifa 2, 104 households in Boussaya, 73 households in Famaré, 131 in Ngouma, 24 households in Mafoulso 3, and 125 households in Mafoulso 4 (Midina).A second needs assessment by the RRM in July 2023 following the attacks in Goretal Goutoun, Bargaram, Terbou, and Mourdas concluded that 1,294 displaced households had arrived, i.e., 10,648 people. Group discussions revealed that 321 IDP children of school age were deprived of education, pointing to the saturation of classrooms and the inadequacy of teachers in Hile Alifa, with an average ratio of 116 pupils to one teacher.
