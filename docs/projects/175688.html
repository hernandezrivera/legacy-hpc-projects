Project Summary

The project aims at addressing ongoing FSL humanitarian crisis in Kapoeta North and KajoKeji counties targeting 4,000 HHs and stabilizing drought and conflict-affected people whose economic and social life have been severely disrupted through the provision of emergency food production to vulnerable women, children, men, boys, girls and PWDs in Kapoeta North and KajoKeji Counties.
Kapoeta North County is located within semi-arid agro-ecological zones that is characterized by low rainfall of limited and variable duration and intensity. Subsistence agriculture (with home-made tools and largely for self-consumption) is the main form of livelihood; cattle are herded mostly for mostly status/marriage/celebration purposes, although they are assets in time of need and are the two key sources of livelihoods for Kapoeta communities is livestock. Across Kapoeta region there are insufficient food according to 2020/2021 IPC and REACH data and their coping mechanisms is harvesting wild fruits and vegetables. Many of the food supply structures have suffered due to lack of rain for food production. To have sufficient food & animal production, there is need for sufficient source of water, but the region lacks sufficient water supply and there is need for water harvesting schemes. Kapoeta's restoration-indeed is dependent on an increase in the construction of surface water for emergency food production. This will greatly contribute to the sustenance of peaceful co-existence among the people and is a prerequisite to supporting long-term sustainable livelihoods.
Women are key actors in influencing the communities in food production in Eastern Equatoria State (e.g. in farming; vegetable/crops for humans and vaccination and treatment of livestock) and they need to be supported. However, simply providing food and water does not itself guarantee their optimal use or impact on the communities. Understanding gender, culture and social relations is essential in assessing, designing and implementing an appropriate integrated emergency food production that will be effective, safe and restores the dignity of the affected population.
Whereas, KajoKeji County is one of the counties in Central Equatoria in the green agro-ecological zone with high voluntary returnees/IDP numbers from the neighboring countries of Uganda, Kenya and Congo. The low food production can not adequately feed the growing population of KajoKeji . Generally livestock and fisheries wealth potential is very high but not fully exploited in Kapoeta North and KajoKeji counties. Outbreak of livestock diseases is common due flooding [usually between June to Oct annually] and there is low literacy in agro economic zone of KajoKeji. This necessitate the need to restore the dignity of the affected population through emergency food production and livelihoods.
Whereas Kapoeta North area is highly vulnerable to the climate vagaries and KajoKeji is highly affected by armed conflict. Due to climate change thas disrupted rainfall patterns (erratic and insufficient), partly justifying the extreme vulnerability [IPC 2020/2021] . Known traditional livelihood options are not able to meet food security needs of poor people. No integrated initiatives are undertaken by the State or County government as of now and RAAH's IRNA indicates few active static local partners to respond to the needs. As a result of these factors, food insecurity has increased hunger gap, and people are forced to migrate in search of a better livelihood. This project will directly work with 4,000 households in the two counties; but the benefits will gradually encompass a larger portion of the total local community. The project will target vulnerable households i.e [women headed households, PWDs, adolescents youth, young men, older persons]. They are being targeted due to their enormous support at HH level. The geographic areas targeted have multiple vulnerabilities and there is urgent need for integrated FSL interventions.

1 - Needs

The impact of 2020 flooding, voluntary returnees, elevated levels of food insecurity as classified in IPC phase four, inadequate quality, diversity of food its availability and utilization have left women headed HH, youths, persons with disabilities and elderly in Kapoeta North and Kajokeji in dire need of humanitarian assistance. Malnutrition rates have sored up especially amongst children and lactating mothers [REACH data 2020]. Inadequate health care services have further acerbated the situation. Notably, Covid 19 and its related vices have put a strain in HH pursuing all livelihood opportunities within reach along the borders in EES,CES leading to deterioration in living conditions for over 70% of the population in dire need for humanitarian assistance. [Data from Food Security and Nutrition situation update; WFP, FAO, Unicef and IPC 2020]. Kapoeta region an area predominantly pastoralists has over the past 3 years experienced food insecurity, dwindled house hold livelihood assets base. Pockets of armed conflict have left the communities in fear and broken down the social safety nets. Animal disease socks have increased. The erratic rainfall pattern mainly received once in a year has disrupted opportunities of the household to produce their own food and are forced to rely on highly priced food items in the market unaffordable to many. The only food the community can afford is to kill an animal and eat it many times without bread. Their animals can hardly produce milk due to poor feeding and even the little milk the animals produce cannot feed their calves. Essentially, the vulnerable households especially children who would have benefitted from milk is not possible. Vegetables are hard to come by in diets. This poor feeding practice has been the main cause of malnutrition. GAM rates in Kapoeta North have been high, above WHO threshold 15 % GAM rate [Ref: Household Food insecurity survey conducted by RAAH] Whereas Kajokeji area is a predominantly green agro-ecological belt with main economic activity being crop farming, fishing and animals rearing. The effects of 2016 war led to the fleeing of many of the community members to neighboring countries and a number of them have started returning home. Also from September 2020 to date there is increase in the numbers of pastoralist from Bor to Kajokeji. Internal clashes between agro farmers and the pastoralist have been on rise in the payams of Liwolo and Nyepo. There has been intermittent military clashes between SSPDF and NAS forces in parts of Kajokeji. However, peace mediation efforts have been steady and access has improved. The 90 HHs RAAH surveyed in the Kajokeji payams of Kangapo 2, Liwolo and Nyepo have indicated growing FSL needs i.e. lack of essential crop seeds and tools; many farmers lost livestock; the rising population due to returnees and IDPs has already put strain on little harvest food because of 2020 floods. High population densities (animal and humans) have created food and water shortages in KajoKeji and Kapoeta. Farmers and county local authorities have explained to RAAH, ‘’their animals have deteriorated in the body condition; are unable to produce enough milk to feed their calves and their death rates have increased due to diseases and starvation and are no longer getting good market value when they sell the animals. The few markets RAAH visited had high prices for cereal that is in high demand in the local markets of Kapoeta and KajoKeji Counties. Not many households have the means to buy food and access to basic services. Based on the needs highlighted in line FSL cluster strategies and HNO 2021and needs RAAH has assessed on ground prioritizing CO2 and CO3 integrated FSL responses to address the immediate household food production and livelihood is core and we plan to reach 4,000 HH in the counties of Kajokeji and Kapoeta North.

2 - Project activities

Under the FSL strategic objectives of CO2 and CO3; RAAH proposes to undertake a. Emergency food production through: i) Main season agriculture seeds & tools, fishing kits, vegetable & crop kits distribution targeting 100% of the 2, 000 HH each in Kapoeta North and KajoKeji counties. ii) Training farmers on quick impact agronomic practices and distribute farm inputs such as maize, groundnuts, sesame, okra, tomatoes, eggplant, amaranths, hoes, rakes, and watering cans iii) Establishing vegetable gardens and practically train women on vegetable gardening and preparation of nutritious meals b. Emergency livestock support through: Undertaking livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, community animal health workers (CAHWs) training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonosis’ e.g. RVF. i) Train 40 CAHWs in basic animal health, extension and business skills; equip and deploy them to deliver animal health services. The training will be conducted in line with the guidelines provided by the Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries while observing Covid-19 prevention protocols of Ministry of Health and Centre for Diseases Control ii) Conduct disease alert investigations for all animal disease alerts that occur during the project implementation period- samples will be collected from animals during investigation of all disease alerts. iii) Vaccinate 120,000 animals against priority animal diseases. The vaccination coverage will include Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS), Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Black Quarter (BQ) and Anthrax diseases in cattle, Pest des Petits Ruminants (PPR), Contagious Caprine Pleuropneumonia (CCPP) and Sheep and Goat Pox (SGP) in sheep and goats, Rabies in dogs and donkeys and Newcastle Disease (NCD) in poultry. Each animal (cattle) will be vaccinated against at least three diseases during the project period, depending on the ranking of the diseases in each region, while goats will be vaccinated at least against the three diseases – PPR, CCPP and Sheep and Goat Pox, and at least two vaccines in sheep (PPR and Sheep and Goat Pox). iv) Treat 50,000 animals against priority animal diseases and parasites. This will be clearly explained during the mobilization and sensitization phase of the intervention for livestock keepers and will be encouraged to report sick animals to the CAHWs as soon as clinical signs are detected so that treatment is commenced early. RAAH will endeavor to ensure that CAHWs carry out treatment of the number of animals against internal & external parasites, infectious diseases and blood parasites and record the data. c. Livelihood support to the vulnerable targeted householdsi. Undertake community livelihood enterprise mapping with priority actors such as most vulnerable women, girls, youths and PWDs; and prioritize three most productive livelihood activities with a marketing and income generating potential based on Kapoeta North and KajaKeji contextsii. Train the selected farmers (theory and practical) on basic entrepreneurial skills, business management skills, assessments and prioritization of most valuable livelihoods in Kapoeta and KajoKejiiii. Support in seed financing (in kind) of prioritized livelihood activities after training and successful induction. d. Integration of Nutrition and WASH into FSL intervention i) In close coordination with Nutrition and WaSH partners in the Kapoeta and Kajokeji support the multi-sectoral linkage of FSL, nutrition and WaSH assistance in line with IPC 4 & 5. e. Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programmingi) Increase peace building campaigns while undertaking integrated FSL activities in the communities for peaceful co-existence and Disaster risk reduction activities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RAAH management has signed a protocol of PSEA and all its staff, affiliates and service providers are expected to adhere to it in the day to day provision of humanitarian assistance. We closely work with protection partners in our project locations of Kapoeta and Kajokeji. During project design we talk to beneficiaries of different gender groups and pick risks related to PSEA or GBV and refocus targeted awareness efforts to all the beneficiaries. For PSEA related survivors we offer them with information on referral path i.e in Kapoeta North- save the children and KajoKeji-ARC are main protection partners that support in related services.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

over 98% of our project team come from the same community and this strategy enables the vulnerable persons to connect easily and share information using all locally affordable means. The project teams will pay visits to the most vulnerable members; organise focused meetings; use printed pictorial information and especially for women/girls they are assigned a mentor who they can confidentially share information. This approach enables every vulnerable and able member of the communities we work gain confidence and lead to great good of active participation, and involvement to address problems collectively

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project team with the help of project document will use project initiation meetings and other broad project review meetings to engage beneficiaries and other priority stakeholders to share information, and receive feedback. Specific roles and responsibilities will be assigned to beneficiary representatives selected amongst the community members. RAAH has in place help desk at critical service points especially during tools and seeds distribution to assist in receiving compliants or suggestions, beneficiaries are shared phone contacts of our front line staff to ease information sharing and more importantly we have community leaders whom we empower to be part of the project monitoring efforts and the feedback received in these engagements are always used to improve the project design; implementation approach with a common good for all.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Using our structured community engagement strategies such as: needs assessment, joint project monitoring reports, help desks for information sharing etc. These data are used to influence he and adapt the project to the stakeholder needs subject to the triple project constraints [cost, time, scope].

8.11. Mitigation Measures

RAAH in every project normally under risk reviews by anticipating risks, measuring the likelihood of occurrence visavis impact, and put in place controls to mitigate the risks and closely monitor/update project risk register for timely decision making by all stakeholders. RAAH staff and all its affiliated service providers are expected to abide by the code of conduct relating to PSEA and GBV prevention they sign before engaging for and on behalf of the organization. Therefore, here below are some of the ways of minimizing risks identified in the proposed project: During key activities such as distribution of FSL tools, seeds and communal demonstration; RAAH will ensure service points are gender disaggregated and this will ease any forms of harassment that could be meted on vulnerable gender groups. We also ensure the time of activity be convenient for the vulnerable groups and ensure the service is delivered close to the beneficiaries for safety concerns. Special and priority attention will be given be given to serve pregnant women; sick persons, PWDs first without necessarily being in long ques RAAH will ensure all gender groups [ women, girls, men, boys, PWDS and elders] have their leadership representation and are active members of the project cycle. In doing so the voices of every person will be heard and this involvement will significantly improve project participation, involvement and equitable service provision RAAH will endeavor to engage multi-dimensional stakeholders in this project through various fora such as: project initiation meetings, joint monitoring project visits, stakeholder validation workshops, coordination meetings and submission of project reports. All these engagements help to share vital information on the project and reduce resistance and any possible interference by some stakeholder if left out. RAAH with the support of safety audit and protection tip sheet regularly work in collaboration with the protection cluster partners in Kapoeta (Save the Children) and in Kajokeji (American Refugee Council) to closely monitor protection risks, create awareness on prevention of GBV/PSEA related issues during key FSL activities; share with beneficiaries the already available platforms for feedback such as help desk, hot lines, suggestion points, beneficiary led review meetings etc to share concerns with RAAH and vice versa and this will improve equitable service integrated FSL service provision to all gender groups in the project locations. Where risks of land disputes arise, RAAH will engage local leadership to arbitrate and ensure peaceful resolution of issues at hand more especially land for IDPS and returnees

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

With support from protection cluster, RAAH will incorporate safety audits and work with a protection specialist to assess protection risks using the protection tip sheet and any risk directly on survivors identified; use the information to support survivors to receive assistance and in services requiring specialized care ; RAAH will use the established referral pathway to help survivors receive help confidentially. 12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The Kapoeta and KajoKeji regions selected are faced with multiple vulnerabilities ranging from climatic shocks, food insecurity, high food prices; armed conflicts etc; and therefore the counties fall within the priority population of IPC4 and the proposed interventions here fall within FSL cluster strategy to directly contribute to improved food security and livelihoods of the vulnerable persons i.e. women, girls, boys, PWDs, men and elderly to reduce their vulnerabilities and improve resilience

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

All the age groups [children, women, children, the elderly, persons with disability ] are planned to benefit from this project with direct focus on the most vulnerable such as lactating mothers, women headed households, elderly and PWDS. The integrated FSL links well with nutrition, WaSH, protection/GBV and preventive health needs. It has been designed to also have indirect beneficiaries of about average 4(four) persons per household. The specific interventions for the cross sectoral linkages will be coordinated through the active sub-national cluster systems; RAAH will coordinate with FSL clusters led by FAO, WFP and other partners in Kapoeta and KajoKeji counties and other joint inter-cluster working groups to avoid duplication of efforts and same time improve linkages; share gaps and advocate for more inclusive programming approach.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

In the project, vegetables growing more especially led women for vulnerable households will significantly contribute to provision of dietary rich vitamins, minerals and other trace element useful for reduction of malnutrition for under 5 year children. Targeted trainings on how to prepare vegetable meals, hygiene [hand, food and environment] and sanitation awareness at household and balanced diet training [meals rich in proteins, carbohydrates, vitamins, irons e.t.c.] for the most vulnerable will be provided to reduce the risk of malnutrition especially targeting lactating mothers and under 5year children. Where there are serious signs and symptoms related to malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable persons; RAAH will liaise with Nutrition cluster partners and county health teams to refer the causes for to nearest heath facility or OTP site for management. RAAH will work closely with WaSH and cluster partners to ensure the vulnerable farming groups have access to clean water and medical care to improve on the outcomes of the family health.
