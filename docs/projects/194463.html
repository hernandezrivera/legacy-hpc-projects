Project Summary

This project aims to address the catastrophic situation of internally displaced, returnees, host community, and refugee children and people with disabilities in Juba IDPs camps, Fangak, Uror, Canal/Pigi, Nasir/Luakping, Maiwut, and Ulang Counties, which has been suffered multiple and frequent displacements caused by political crisis, inter-communal violence, cattle raiding, women and children abduction and severe floods that resulted to widening gender and protection gaps in those counties.
The project intends to address those gaps through the provision of life-saving gender and protection interventions and integration of gender and protection support services through, Provision of community-based Psychosocial Support Organizing age and gender-appropriate psychosocial support activities for children and their caregivers through (CFS, Mobile teams, Children/youth clubs, Parents groups, and individual support sessions; Provision of Case Management including identification, documentation, family tracing, and reunification to Unaccompanied and separated children. Increase capacity building of front-line actors and community-based structures to improve the knowledge and skills to protect children and advocacy and lobbying to influence duty bearers and to unlock barriers to the protection of children
Prevention messages on protection risks associated with flooding include the risk of family separation, child recruitment, Child labor, GBV, and other harmful practices through community sessions, CP Desks, school club activities, and CPCs. Continuous monitoring and reporting of CP concern in order to identify and provide appropriate support to affected children at risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation as well as supporting joint assessments to gather information on the situation of children in the affected targeted location. The project intends to reach out to 16,000 beneficiaries, ( 6,000 Boys, 1,500 Girls, 2,500 Women, and 400 with the majority as children who are the most affected population in the community with a range of activities described in the table below.

1 - Needs

Child Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to Child protection services. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. CAO, currently has an ongoing project in the IPD 1&3, CAO in its project implementation conducts protection assessments, and feedback reports from the IDP population and camp managers, and various concerns are constantly being raised and demand urgent intervention. During the protection monitoring assessments, a number of findings were introduced such as open defecation which happens at night when children move 1/2 kilometer, Water Points are very far, Food, firewood, wild fruits Garbage collection that has exposed these children to protection risks such as rape, sexual exploitations, child labor and all sorts of child abuses. Displaced people are more vulnerable to threats to their safety, health, and livelihoods. Adolescents are more exposed to risks as they have few other things to do especially in IDPs/POCs and other collective sites. CAO is seeking to scale up the response to the needs of vulnerable children and adolescent youths exposed to armed activities. CAO intends to provide Child Protection services, to the conflict and Flood affected Children in Juba IDPs camps, Fangak, Uror, Canal/Pigi, Nasir/Luakping, Maiwut, and Ulang Counties Through continuing responding to the needs of children and Adolescent youth, engaging them through a response strategy which includes: Focus on provision of quality Case management services, Capacity building support to communities base child protection network, strengthening adolescent and youths Centers, Community-based PSS mechanism and approach to respond to their needs when necessary, provide dignity kits and life-saving material assistance to the vulnerable adolescent, strengthening referral system for CP and GBV cases; increase in mine Risk education awareness at schools, CFS with recreational activities, coordinate Family Tracing and Reunification; establishment and support of schools club and child help desk and mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, and FSL

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The following methods and strategies shall be applied; Hold community meetings (including separate ones with children), which include representatives from minorities such as linguistic, ethnic, or person with disabilities; Work with children to help them design their own awareness-raising campaign for their peers; Train existing groups and community structures. (such as Child Protection Committees, Parent Teacher Groups, persons with disabilities, etc) in the Feedback mechanisms and ask them to share the key messages with their peers; innovative and Creative methods, for example using drama, art, or songs to share information about the and use of large print key messages to share in public spaces and service locations; Help desks are a good opportunity for two-way dialogue. They allow people to report concerns without risk of stigma from the community (as people might use the help desk to do anything from asking about distribution times to reporting sexual abuse). Help desks can also build trust if they are frequently open and the staff is helpful. Not suitable for persons with visual or certain mobility impairments if they cannot get there. Community or children’s feedback committee members would need to be recruited through a participatory process that builds trust. Members will be trained on their responsibilities and on how to handle sensitive concerns, such as those related to child abuse or sexual exploitation. The training will include how to support children with visual or mobility impairments in seeking assistance.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CAO CP team will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that response and complaint are streamlined, and deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from affected individuals regarding violations and/or physical abuse that may have human rights, psychological or other implications, and employ measures for handling these should be adapted accordingly. The affected populations will be allowed to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have reached them on a monthly basis. CAO will revamp and strengthen community-based complaints mechanisms to have gender-sensitive ombudspersons receive/hear complaints from the beneficiaries and escalate the same to project leadership or relevant authorities while ensuring the needs of survivors are at the center.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAO will use a community engagement and accountability approach, to put communities at the center of the project. In this regard, CAO shall ensure appropriate training is provided to staff and partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Prevention, training, and information campaigns shall be put in place for all CAO staff, training and information campaigns will include the distribution of information sheets, posters, videos, community meetings, focus group discussions, etc. CAO will provide community members with information about existing codes of conduct for personnel, as well as where to report sexual exploitation and abuse committed by staff providing assistance. Beneficiaries shall be also oriented on how they should report SEA incidents safely and confidentially when they occur. CAO will integrate the issue of sexual exploitation and abuse systematically into information campaigns, training, and meetings with affected people, with particular attention given to vulnerable groups. CAO team will Strengthen the community through awareness-raising, in Critical activities targeting women, girls, boys, and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders (including implementing and operational partners, government officials, civil society organizations, and companies in the private sector). CAO will prevent SEA effectively, to all staff and workers must make sure that PoC is aware of their rights and entitlements. Beneficiaries should know that they are entitled to assistance, that they should never be expected to provide any form of sexual favor, and that demands for such favors are strictly prohibited. CAO will sensitize the community/Beneficiaries on SEA on the project and Establish (safe and accessible) community reporting channels to report SEA. In many communities there exist already “community feedback/complaint mechanisms”, “Child Protection committees”, Women Centers, PSEA focal points”, protection Desk or other protection reporting channels. These existing focal points and channels need to be sensitized to be able to identify and report SEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project monitoring systems and reviews that gather information about beneficiaries and their perceptions, views, and responses to the interventions. Such information can help improve the assistance of both individuals and communities/beneficiaries through a wide variety of data collection methods. Even when the information is not comprehensive, it will undoubtedly help managers in improving the performance and impacts of their projects and programs. It will also help the organization build trust and facilitate effective communication between beneficiaries and the organizations that assist them. C.A.O will organize project performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries, and partners to assess progress towards the set objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality of activities based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback data, identify risks, and set risk management strategies during implementation. A project evaluation will be carried out at the end of the project and the process used to measure the achievement of project outputs and outcomes then be documented for the record. CAO will take immediate and appropriate action upon receipt of any report or complaint about SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA.
