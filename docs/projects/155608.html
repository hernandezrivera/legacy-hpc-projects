Project Summary

In light of the context and needs of vulnerable populations in Iraq, the IRC aims to provide vulnerable, conflict-affected women, men, and youth in Ninewa governorate (IDPs, returnees, and host communities) with the ability to generate income and assets through safe and dignified employment or self-employment. This will be done through a client centric, bundled services approach within existing livelihoods center in Mosul. The center will offer the following bundle of services targeted to meet the specific needs and situations of each beneficiary: micro-enterprise development training; business grants; business advising and mentor ship; job counselling and referrals; life skills training and apprenticeships services. Eligibility will be determined based on a vulnerability and livelihoods assessment tool, which calculates a vulnerability score based on criteria such as: female-headed households; household income; housing status; and a context-based coping strategies index score. The tool will also support the matching of services based on the individual’s experience and preferences.

Needs

People faced with protracted displacement and those returned to their area of origin are increasingly destitute, with few able to find employment or generate income, resulting in many displaced families depleting emergency assistance, savings and selling their assets or going into debt to survive. In locations hosting IDPs, vulnerable host community households and returnees have had to cope with the overall financial crisis in Iraq and resulting economic downturn, and competition over unskilled jobs as the heightened number of displaced casual workers have driven down low-skilled wages. For both returnees and protracted displaced populations, access to regular income generating opportunities is consistently reported to be one of the greatest needs among vulnerable households who lack or have inconsistent access to social safety net services to meet even the most basic needs. Displaced populations, including IDPs and refugees, as well as returnees often struggle due to unfamiliarity with different markets and a lack of specific tailored skills, resources or networks that would allow them to find meaningful and safe employment, as well as a lack of knowledge and capital needed to establish self-employment.While vulnerable households returning to their homes might have once had knowledge of the local markets prior to their displacement, the post-crisis economic situation is likely to be quite different, with deficient social safety nets and significant community rehabilitation and asset replacement needs. Vulnerable returnee households often struggle with depleted savings and high debt burdens after coping with displacement. Such populations require targeted market-based skills development to adapt to the changes in the local economy, resources to help them seek safe employment, as well as business rehabilitation, start-up, or development support, coupled with opportunities for capital to secure self-employment

Activities

Micro enterprise Training: In 2015, the IRC commissioned Making Cents International to develop a business and financial management training curriculum tailored for the Iraq context called Managing Your Finances (MYF). The training curriculum is administered over the course of 9 sessions, covering the following topics: selecting a business idea, conducting market research, financial record keeping for businesses, marketing your product or service, managing your business’ cash-flow, preparing a business plan, and managing the risks and challenges associated with running a business. The interactive curriculum provides training participants with both soft skills such as presentation and negotiation techniques as well as more practically-oriented small business management knowledge. In areas where the IRC has already been implementing livelihoods activities, the IRC will engage previous business training participants as well as successful business owners in the community to supplement classroom lessons and introduce business training participants to useful contacts in the community. Business Start-up Grants: At the conclusion of the MYF training, all participants have the opportunity to submit a business plan that is developed over the course of the training to a business grant evaluation committee, consisting of two IRC Livelihoods officers, one external Economic Recovery and Development staff and, if feasible, one business person from the local community. The committee reviews and scores the business plans using a grant evaluation tool developed by the IRC, and provides the strongest eligible business plans with a start-up grant with a value up to $1,200. Criteria for eligibility are: business plan quality (feasibility of plan, addressing market needs or gaps, marketing strategy, product or service pricing, and proposed business location); business financial model (do capital needs cover costs, what are estimated profits); and beneficiary competency (training pre- and post-test scores, attendance, engagement during training). Once grant recipients are selected based on the above criteria, the grants are then disbursed in two installment over a period of approximately three weeks. The second installmen of the grant is disbursed after IRC staff conduct monitoring visits to verify that grants are being used for business purposes according to the approved business plan. Small Business Development and Rehabilitation Advising: The IRC will accept applications from existing or formerly existing (for returnees) local small to medium enterprises (SMEs) to receive targeted business incubation and advisory services with a trained IRC business advisor. In order to tailor SME training tools to the needs of the local context, the IRC will build on the work done by a consulting organization Building Markets for the IRC’s livelihoods centers in Lebanon to adapt this model to the Iraqi context. Unlike the micro enterpris training for new entrepreneurs, these business advisory services will be tailored to the specific needs of each client, depending on the needs of the business and the business sector. Business advisers will meet with clients over a period of one month to conduct small group training sessions as well as one-on-one business plan development and advising sessions. Small Business Grants: SMEs participating in business incubation and advisory services will have the opportunity to submit business plans and a grant application to the grant evaluation committee, which will review applications using the grant evaluation scoring tool used in the start-up grants procedure, as mentioned above. In addition to the business start-up grant criteria, businesses demonstrating synergies with other IRC livelihoods activities will receive priority. This may include businesses that work in growth sectors identified through market assessments, businesses that are part of the same value chain as IRC supported micro enterprise, or businesses that are able to train apprenticeships and hire IRC supported job seekers. Grant applicants will be eligible to apply for funding up to $2,000, and the value will be determined based on specified need indicated in the business plan as well as a due diligence process undertaken by IRC staff. Business Mentor shi: All start-up and small business grant recipients will be eligible to receive business mentor ship over a period of approximately 40 days (exact timefram will be determined at the outset of the mentor ship service by the mentor and mentee). In the initial stages, business owners will be matched with qualified IRC business trainers, who will have both technical business knowledge and training in the IRC’s empowerment-focused mentor ship approach. In areas where the IRC has had ongoing business start-up activities, staff will work to involve these more experienced business owners in mentoring start-up grant recipients for a more sustainable model.

Mitigation Measures

The IRC will use its experience in community mobilization to ensure accountability to affected people through a community participatory approach. The project will ensure a balanced representation of all three ethno-religious groups and IDPs, returnees and host community to prevent negative community reactions and help contribute to social cohesion. All the business grants will be provided with particular attention on Do No Harm principle. The IRC will use a community-based accountability mechanism to empower locals’ access to a system for providing feedback and concerns about the project. To fulfill the IRC’s Do No Harm mandate, the project will be implemented through a gender-sensitive lens, allowing women’s active participation in the project and respecting cultural norms of the local community and other ethnic groups. The program team will closely coordinate IRC security team for movement and implementation of activities in field and follow the recommendations of security team. In addition, a feedback and response mechanism is already established and in place led by IRC M&E team. All the project beneficiaries and community will have access to this feedback and response mechanism where they can record complaints followed by a response mechanism according to nature of complains. In addition, the IRC will maintain a gender balance in their staff and female staff will be recruited in order to have better access to female segment of target community. Separate sessions of business training will be organized for male and female beneficiaries in order to enhance female inclusion while respecting the local norms.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The IRC Iraq country program has an ERD-dedicated Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEAL) team comprised of a MEAL Manager, Senior Quality and Compliance Officer, Data Management Officer, and MEAL Officer who will support this project. The IRC will carry out both process and impact monitoring. The impact monitoring will gather data on changes and improvements in beneficiaries’ welfare as a result of this project. Process monitoring will include analysis of beneficiary and non-beneficiary populations’ perception of, and satisfaction with, the project. This monitoring will also measure changes in the lives of conflict-affected people served, such as increased income and decrease in negative coping strategies. MEAL data quality will be enhanced through a data management platform called CommCare, which has developed MEAL tools to support quality implementation and project evaluation. Through an effective control system, the IRC will put in place measures to mitigate the risk of fraud, diversion, and mismanagement of funds. The project intends to use industry standard tools and indicators, such as those used by the World Food Program, to measure project outputs and outcomes and utilize reliable research methodologies and techniques
