Project Summary

Over the past twelve years of the Syrian crisis, as indicated by the HNO-24, there are approximately 15.7 million people in need who are unable to meet their basic needs. Lack of employment opportunities, essential services, and assistance contribute to the needs of 49% of households in north-west Syria. Loss of jobs or unemployment acts as a barrier to meeting basic needs and contributes to sexual exploitation, particularly among women and girls. Even full-time employment does not provide sufficient income for households to cover their basic needs. Unsustainable coping mechanisms are widespread, with 86% of households in north-east and north-west Syria relying on informal credit for essentials, leading to difficulties in debt repayment, with 11% of households in north-west Syria resorting to selling productive assets, compromising long-term resilience and recovery prospects. Child labor is also prevalent, with 9% of households in both regions depending on income from male children, while 10% of households in north-west Syria engage in socially degrading or high-risk jobs for survival. Further exacerbating the situation are reduced economic activities, deteriorating productive inputs, low employment rates, and limited access to electricity due to fuel shortages and price increases, particularly affecting female-headed households. Escalating food prices have compelled families to consume less and opt for cheaper, sometimes less nutritious, food. Some households are withdrawing children from school to save costs or to family income, leading to increase protection concerns WV program design meet the HNO findings as it is adopting ultra- poor graduation holistic approach model in NWS, through offering 4 pillars interventions,1. Pillar (1): Social protection and MPCA, Pillar (2): Livelihoods promotion, Pilar (3): Financial inclusion and promoting VSLA accompanied by financial literacy training.4. Pillar (4): Social empowerment and coaching that cut across all pillars, decision making, management of finances. Inclusion of men and women together in these discussions to address norms, attitudes and relationship dynamics in a safe, comfortable space. Where collective meetings aren’t possible, men’s groups and women’s groups include complementary discussion topics to foster continued dialogue between men and women outside of groups. during the design of the project, consultations with the local councils took place to share the UPG concept and foster women and PWD inclusion. the project aims to link participants with income generating opportunities of demand and meet their interest and capabilities. As UPG targets the most vulnerable hhs, high risk of enrolment PWD of severe disabilities, elderlies, individuals with chronic diseases that would limit their ability to perform their work and properly run their businesses, for that UPG will still target these HHs however, will be enrolling other family members who are in work age and capable of doing work related tasks. For PWD who are capable to do their work-related duties, WVSR will make sure that the identified TVT service providers are accessible to PWD and have child friendly spaces for children accompanying their mom to the vocational trainings. In addition to that risk, the long duration of the project and IDPs movement and instability might be faced with high dropout rate, the UPG staff and through the coaching and mentoring services will work closely with each HHs to meet their needs and provide the proper guidance and ensure full commitment to the project, also UPG will target IDPs who have been living for more than 6 months in the area that would ensure more sustainability. WV will target 460 most vulnerable and extremely poor HHs, out of them 138 females headed HHs in 2300 individual 690 females. The actual duration of the project is October 1st, 2023 - September 31st, 2025.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

WV is directly implementing projects adopting ultra- poor graduation holistic approach model in NWS, the program offers a package of 4 pillars interventions, including 1. Pillar (1): Social protection includes preventive, protective and promotes mechanisms to support basic income security such as consumption support, crisis relief, and access to health, legal, protection and education. 2. Pillar (2): Livelihoods promotion ensures regular and diverse income streams for households to support consumption, asset accumulation, and economic empowerment, especially for women. Interventions include asset or cash transfers for a viable livelihood or linkages to formal employment, technical and business skills training, and access to markets.3. Pilar (3): Financial inclusion provides improved income and risk management and financial empowerment. Interventions typically include formation and access to saving groups, accompanied by financial literacy training.4. Pillar (4): Social empowerment equips families with a confident mindset and promotes community inclusion and positive behavior change. Interventions include life skills training, social integration, and coaching that cut across all four pillars, underpinned by a commitment to gender equality.Together, the four pillars strengthen a family’s resilience, enabling parents to provide for and protect their children by ensuring that they are adequately nourished, educated, and have a sense of hope and positivity for the future. The Graduation approach incorporates principles of World Vision’s Empowered World View, helping women and men to understand their ability to influence their own circumstances, find value in their identity, and improve their standing in the community.Target is 460 HHs, 30% of them are women headed households (2300 individuals 690 are females)
