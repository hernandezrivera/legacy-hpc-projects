Project Summary

remière Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
To reduce the morbidity and mortality rates, PUI will continue providing free-of-charge life-saving health care services and support to primary health care facilities and health outreach sites, promoting sustainability through its support to the Ministry of Health (MoH).
Within the PHCCs and temporary health facility, PUI will provide the Primary Health Care package according to national and international guidelines: Curative Services for common injuries and diseases; Provision of Preventative Services for nutrition and immunization; Provision of essential medicines and consumables; Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) in MMC only; Comprehensive Care for Survivors of GBV, including medical care and referral to MHPSS and protection; Referrals of emergency and non-emergency cases to secondary care facilities; Health and Hygiene Education and Promotion; Data Management and Analysis, including contribution to Early Warning Alert Response System; Management of Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response; Psycho-social support activities like: PFA, psychoeducation, awareness raising sessions, individual counselling, family sessions, support group, and referral to specialized services (psychiatric hospital).

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in North east Nigeria has continued through the year 2020 with Borno state being the worst hit. The people who are in need of humanitarian assistance has risen from 7.9 million in the beginning of the year 2020 to 10.6 million people towards the end; over 40% of the health facilities have been destroyed in the region. Overall, the security situation across the north-east regions remains volatile. In MMC, the residents of Bolori-II ward where (border community to Jere and konduga LGAs) have continued to report arrival of displaced people from neighbouring communities in Konduga and Magumeri LGAs due to physical and socioeconomic insecurity. About 240 households arrived the community between August to October 2020 alone. These new arrivals depend on the existing health and nutrition facilities in Bolori-II, adding to the needs to be addressed.Monguno LGA has continued to witness increased humanitarian needs due to the conflict's prolonged nature and OAGs territorial extension, especially ISWAP around this area. The overall humanitarian crisis further deteriorated due to the adverse effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and related restrictive measures . Although strict lockdown measures were not implemented in Monguno, the impact was significantly felt since movements from and to Maiduguri were not possible from the 22th April to 15th May 2020, then followed by inter-state travel restrictions that lasted till June 2020. Mass displacements of people from communities around Kukawa who are mostly farmers and herders into Monguno have been recorded during the April till September due to GF operations' scale-up and counter-attacks by OAGs. During this period, around 250 households' arrivals were reported, including people coming by their means or rescued by GF who brought them to Monguno.In terms of health needs there is a lack of local capacity and resources to address health needs within poor host communities with high IDP concentrations especially in remote LGAs. Around 42% of medical facilities in North East Nigeria have been damaged either partially or completely with humanitarian aid partners offering health services either under trees, temporal mark shits or in the tents. Treatable communicable diseases are highly endemic especially in areas with high concentration of IDPs due to high congestion, poor environmental and hygiene conditions from originating areas, a weakened health system, and poor sanitation among other important infrastructure. Malaria has been one of the highest cause of morbidity and mortality in North-Eastern states with children under 5 years and pregnant women being the most affected. In addition, the COVID-19 outbreak further exacerbates the pressure on Borno states fragile healthcare system already weakened by violence against civilians, including attacks on hospitals and denial of humanitarian access.

2 - Activities

PUI will maintain full functional and technical support to PHCCs, temporary health facility and health outreach sites by providing free health care services - including both preventive and curative treatment through collaboration with MOH within 5 facilities in Bolori II (N’garanam PHCC, Herwa PHCC, 3 health outpost), and 4 in Monguno (ALGON, Water-board, Water-board extension, Gana Ali) PUI will continue to provide diagnosis, treatment, and referrals- when required - of communicable diseases through PHCs and Health Outposts through the delivery of curative services and case management. For the treatment of common illnesses and diseases, PUI will primarily use national validated guidelines to ensure best practices, international guidelines will also be followed if need be. Special attention will be given to patients living with a disability and who are marginalized so that they can benefit from adequate screening, orientation and referral services. Patient records will be kept through a new registration system software (administrative registration and medical details taken by the health staff) and are confidential. To prevent and mitigate the communicable disease outbreak, outreach teams will continue to rise community awareness through community health education around hygiene promotion, communicable disease, and immunization. PUI is further developing a longer term approach to support SRH services at existing facilities with: 24/7 maternal health care including comprehensive antenatal , neonatal, and postnatal services to Pregnant and Lactating Women. This includes performing deliveries through trained health-care providers and midwives within PHCs; Providing free-of-charge SRH consultation within health facilities, including BEmONC (according to MISP) and family planning; PUI has MoUs with 2 main hospitals in Maiduguri (UMTH and State Hospital) to receive and treat patients referred from PUI, along with 5 ambulances operational around the clock ensure the safe transport of patients; Distribution of clean delivery kits to women during the last quarter of pregnancy during ANC sessions; Training of PUI teams and partners on MISP, CMR, ANC and PNC standards, BEmONC and family planning by PUI Medical Coordinator, MHPSS Advisor and SRH advisor; The prevention and management of the consequences of sexual violence. PUI promotes case management for common chronic diseases in collaboration with the state hospitals through: early identification of non-communicable diseases (NCD via medical screenings and consultations at health facilities; awareness campaigns on health and lifestyle practices related to chronic diseases through PUI’s Outreach team and community health education session; provision of NCD medication that are available at PUI supported health facilities. Patients with chronic disease will be advised either to come back to PUI health facility for medication follow up or advised to visit nearest MoH hospital where they will receive medication not provided at PHCC for chronic illnesses; referral of chronic disease patients to relevant services for further investigations or treatment as required.PUI will conduct awareness session and provide IEC materials in Monguno through door-to-door sessions, meeting the community directly in their houses to provide health, hygiene, sanitation and nutrition advices; in addition, focus groups will be organized with different groups of the community in order to get quality information about habits and behaviors. These sessions will enable the Community Health Workers to adapt their messages. The messages provided will be based on a curriculum about health, hygiene, sanitation, nutrition, and general services.Finally, PUI will continue to ensure the provision of quality health services within the facilities through the procurement of medical consumables and drugs along with in-kind donations from UNICEF and UNFPA.
