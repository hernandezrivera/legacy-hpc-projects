Project Summary

The return situation in Quissanga, Metuge and Meluco shows the return of hundreds of thousands of people to an area devastated by the impact of the armed conflict. Due to the poor state of infrastructure, services and facilities combined with the limited presence of humanitarian actors and the reduced capacity of local authorities, the returning population faces significant challenges in re-establishing their way of life. Some of the main problems and needs identified and communicated by the community, as mentioned in the previous analysis, include reduced access to water, sanitation and hygiene.
Improved health conditions through assuring safe and dignified access to sustainable water, sanitation and hygiene services for displaced and returned communities:
Target 1: Provide emergency access to drinking water for at least 9300 people affected by the armed conflict, including IDPs, returnees.
Target 2: Provide emergency sanitation for a minimum of 12477 people to people affected by the armed conflict among IDPs and returnees.
Target 3: Reach 16241 people affected by the armed conflict among IDPs and Returnees, with critical hygiene messages and the provision of lifesaving WASH supplies for the most vulnerable.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The displacement of the population mass between outflows and returns has led to depravation of basic services and fundamental human rights, such as healthcare, education, protection and drinking water, with the most vulnerable segments of the population left without any support to respond to their needs. The aim of this project is to address critical water and sanitary conditions in the areas of return, preventing the outbreak of preventable diseases (TND - Tropical Neglected Diseases) among the returnees and, on the other hand, to ensure hygienic conditions of the communities supported by the humanitarian interventions during the emergency response.Quissanga has emerged as an important district for returnees, where the majority of its population (45,654 people and 19,432 families) has already been resettled. However, this activity generates a multitude of challenges, aggravated by the limited resources in the district and the few humanitarian organisations active in Quissanga. Existing water sources are scarce, often in poor condition or severely deteriorated. Of the 76 water points in the Quissanga district, only 33 remain operational, with a distribution of 13 in the Bilibiza administrative post, 10 in the Mahate administrative post and 10 in the Quissanga Sede administrative post. Quissanga Sede administrative post. The quality of the water available is poor, contributing to an alarming prevalence of diarrheal diseases. The priority districts according to the OCHA include Quissanga, Macomia, Mocímboa da Praia and Nangade, where many conflict-affected individuals do not have access to the minimum humanitarian standard of drinking water (15 litres per person per day), decent and inclusive sanitation and adequate hygiene services. In 2024, these districts received the least humanitarian WASH assistance and are the most vulnerable based on an intersectoral analysis. On the other hand, the district of Meluco has a high level of 20,255 families in extreme need in the components of sanitary water and hygiene, with an indication of severity in level 3, with WASH needs being among the top 4 and a priority for returnees. Recent OCHA studies have shown that more than 70% of women use inappropriate material to manage their hygiene needs, often resorting to rags and capulanas.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Ayuda en Accion as a humanitarian implementer presents proactive actions to prevent risk and safeguard cases at the concentration points of our emergency WASH activities. The following is a range of actions and is not limited to:- It would be desirable to promote a plan for dissemination and talks at water boreholes at times of high demand, where most of the users of the boreholes can be found, and to extend this topic to other training sessions.- It is important to identify other local committees such as the women's committee, the health committee, the protection committee, among others. The focal points need to be known by everyone and should not have any consanguineous links with local leaders, to avoid coercion in their activities. To this end, it is best that they are not appointed by them. - Post the green line contact on the borehole so that it can be used by anyone. Also provide complaint boxes.- Increase the sidewalk to 4m in diameter so that wheelchair users can maneuver; provide access ramps for wheelchair users.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AeA ensures that, during the design phase of its projects, information is rapidly gathered on the needs of each gender and of the different vulnerable groups. This is done through RNAs in the field. AeA analyses this data to target the groups we believe we can have the greatest impact on; also ensuring that duplication of activities is avoided through coordination with relevant stakeholders. Based on the information gathered, activities are then adapted to the specific needs on the ground. Participation woulf be key.. For reporting matters, the involvement of vulnerable groups during the project implementation will be highlighted. Monitoring of activities is also done through PDMs and on-site monitoring, with AeA ensuring a strong emphasis on using a gender-sensitive lens.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Ayuda en Accion as a humanitarian implementer presents proactive actions to prevent risk and safeguard cases at the concentration points of our emergency WASH activities. The following is a range of actions and is not limited to will ensure that the ideas and opinions of people with disabilities are solicited throughout the emergency response project cycle. Even in rapid interventions, people with disabilities will be involved in the design of WASH infrastructure, such as latrines and water points, to ensure that their suggestions can lead to improvements in access. To the extent possible, some physical facilities may include, but are not limited to, handrails, seats or ramps. AeA will also ensure that your issues are disseminated to the community in a meaningful way to help improve understanding of your needs. We will also share the progress of activities with the WASH cluster to share or receive more up-to-date contributions on WASH inclusion, as has always been the case.In order to ensure that barriers are removed, a survey of people with disabilities in the community will be carried out.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The implementation of this project will be preceded by a mapping of risks and determine their degree; An analysis will be made of the risks to the environment of each emergency installation and mitigation measures will be taken to minimize any negative impact or eradicate it completely. For example, the design of boreholes will be carried out to the highest standards, ensuring that aquifers are protected from contamination from above. This will include casing these boreholes as deep as possible and building good quality sanitary seals. Information about these boreholes will be provided to the SDPI local government structures, which will be responsible for the sources in the long term.
