Project Summary

Integrated emergency nutrition interventions for children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women in the Red Sea state. The project will support CMAM sites through strengthening community outreach and referrals, and access affected communities to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs. The intervention includes a cash component where mothers with malnourished children will be provided with cash to facilitate movement, especially those that stay far away from treatment facilities. The project will also work with communities to prevent under-nutrition. ACH will strengthen nutrition situation assessments, reporting and accountability mechanisms within the areas of operation including monitoring and evaluation.

Needs

The Red Sea state is one of the poorest regions of Sudan with a poverty incidence of 51,4% of its population with a consumption level below the poverty line. Most of the population of Red Sea State is today experiencing severe livelihoods erosion and increasing vulnerability.At state level, Red Sea recorded the highest inflation among all states at 101%. The inflation in staple sorghum retail price has been recorded at 182% from 14 SDG in November 2017 to 39,5 SDG in November 2018 in Port Soudan. ACH has conducted an assessment in Red Sea state on Dec,2018 that stress high prevalence of chronic and acute food insecurity due to high level of poverty, and environmental degradation such as climatic and economic shocks, degradation in the purchasing power and in the food access. The 2013 S3M alarmingly showed that some localities in Red Sea had some of the worse nutrition indicators in the country. Global wasting average rates were around 20%-22%, but localities like Tokar, Suakin, Sinkat and Halayeb had rates near or over 30%, followed close by Haya. Severe acute malnutrition rates were also worryingly high (near or over 20%) in Halayeb, Suakin and Tokar. Global stunting rates were over 45%, while in localities such as Sinkat, Jabiet Al Maadin, Agig and Suakin rates were near to or over 60%. According to the Red Sea MoH Nutrition Department, the priority localities are: a) in terms of rates of acute malnutrition Tokar, Agig and Suakin, with other localities following close; b) in terms of scale Haya, Tokar, Agig, Suakin, Sinkat and Dordeib, in this order; c) in terms of hard-to-reach population in need Halayeb, Jabeit al Maadin and El Qaneb.Main causes of undernutrition, according to the nutrition authorities, include low literacy rates, water shortages during summer season and very low food diversity. The main peak of malnutrition is in June-September, when there is water shortage, with another peak in October-Mars, due to the rainy season (and high occurrence of diarrhea and malaria).

Activities

1. Deliver quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to 1623 boys and girls 0-59 months and 6880 PLW in Red sea State Increased coverage of existing CMAM sites through strengthening community outreach and referrals of SAM and MAM and PLW for CMAM services in three localities, 4 Health facilities supported to integrate CMAM services into basic care packages 2. Increased access to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs preventing under-nutrition pregnant and lactating women at community and facility level across 3 localities. Access maternal infant and young child feeding messages or interventions in three localities of Red Sea State 3. Strengthen nutrition situation assessment reporting and accountability mechanisms in areas of operation as well as monitoring and reporting 4: Increased access to safe and integrated nutrition, health education and WASH FSL responses in three localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition - In collaboration with local an International partners, raise awareness in schools on the importance for early detection, screening and treatment of acute malnutrition - In collaboration with education partners, raise awareness among school children and teachers on targeted beneficiaries and use of therapeutic/supplementary food issued-that is like medicine and intended to severe and moderately malnourished children (OTP and TSFP) and not for sharing with other family members - Children with malnutrition tested for malaria and treated where positive - Nutrition sites provided with safe water for washing and appetite test to promote hygiene.
