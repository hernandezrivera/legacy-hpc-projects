Project Summary

According to the Water Department in Ikwotos, from 205 boreholes in Greater Ikwotos (including the counties form Ikwotos, Geria and Kideopo) 62 need repair. Caritas Switzerland with its over 40 years of WASH experience in Eastern Equatoria proposes to repair 16 of the broken boreholes, to revitalize the community water committees, and to practice intensive WASH trainings to improve the health situation of the community and make the boreholes sustainable.
The 16 villages have approx. 250 – 300 Households (HH) each which makes a total of around 4500 HH.

Needs

The population in the 16 villages has to fetch water from far and partly unsafe water sources due to broken water pumps. Some of the villagers are forced to migrate during the dry season to other places for access to water. With the repair of the boreholes the stress for drinking water, water for gardens and livestock will be reduced.Many of the local water committees that have the responsibility to manage and maintain the water sources are not anymore functional and the members of the committees do not have the necessary minimal repair knowledge. There is also a great need for hygiene education. Only few households have latrines and basic hygiene rules are not practiced. Health problems from water borne diseases are frequent.

Activities

Repair 16 boreholes in Ikwotos, Geria and Kidepo Valley Counties. The average Households per village is around 250-300 HH. Between 4'000 to 4'800 HH shall have access to safe water.The Water Management Committees of 16 villages will be trained and the hygiene requirements for a safe water point explained.In each of the 16 villages a pump technician will be trained and will receive a tool box.In each of the 16 villages the water quality will be tested.In each of the 16 villages a WASH awareness training for the community will be conducted.A GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls will also be conducted by female staff to make sure that the access to water sources will not pose additional risks for women and girls.Boreholes of the following villages are proposed for repair by Caritas Switzerland and the Water Department Ikwotos in Greater Ikwoto County: 1 Loudo Ateda Losite 2 Lotome gum Lotome Losite 3 Lotome konyokonyo Lotome Losite 4 Lokoro Losihet Ikwoto5 Huluaha Losihet Ikwoto6 Lorima tanama Tanama Ikwoto7 Lofus Lofus Losite 8 Hafolere Isohe Isohe 9 Lohirafa Isohe Isohe 10 Lodeker Lofus Losite 11 Iyaak Tanama Ikwoto 12 Longairo Ingoi Imotong 13 Lofulung P/S Ifose imotong14 Hebielo Geria Imotong15 Lolemu Okorohore Chorocol16 Lohihe Okorohore Chorocol

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

With every village an MoU will be signed. A first formal information to all villagers will be held at the beginning of the project. A final information workshop will be conducted at the end of the repair works and the hygiene trainings, prior to the official handover. A technical team will inform the villagers of the planned repairs and their expected contribution to the work. Further, the villagers will be informed and assisted to form a gender balanced Water Management Committee. The Water Management Committee and technically experienced person will be trained in the maintenance of boreholes and will receive a tool box.All households will receive hygiene training.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All information to the beneficiaries are tailor made. Water Management Committees are to be gender balanced. The hygiene trainings will be adapted - seperately for female and male villagers - according to their pre-knowledge of hygiene.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All partners and persons working in the project have signed the code of conduct and are adequately instructed about the sensitivity of the topic as well as about the consequences in case of misconduct.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Half of the proposed 16 villages for borehole repair have been visited by Caritas Switzerland in relation with other projects (Nutrition, School Gardens, etc.). The needs in these villages were discussed between the Caritas team and the population.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

With each village an MoU will be negotiated and signed. The Water Management Committee representing the village will be the link between the villagers and the project management. The regular monitoring of the work in the villages and information sessions held for the village inhabitants will allow the villagers to ask questions and give direct feedback to the project management.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the mutually negociated MoU between the villagers, Caritas Switzerland and the Ikwotos Water Directorate, the tasks and responsibilities of all involved actors will be clearly stipulated. The key representatives of the Water Management Committees will participate in all monitoring visits carried out by the project team. This way, they will be able to understand the issues of concern and the challenges that arise during the implementation of the activities.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the MoU between the villagers and Caritas Switzerland, protection risks are included. The MoU mentions the code of conduct for involved actors and equal access to the borehole for all, including the elderly, women and the disabled.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Prior to the start of the activities, meetings will be held with the communities in which the representatives of all population groups will be asked to participate (elders, disabled, women, youth, religious/ethnic minorities if relevant, etc.). In these meetings, the project team will identify the groups that might face additional difficulties in relation to their access to the project oucomes. Measures will be put in place to ensure that these diadvantaged groups can have access to the benefits of the project without facing additional barriers.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The borehole repair includes a training module for the local Water Management Committee. This training will include aspects of equal access to the water sources for all community members and conflict sensitive approaches. If the project team of Caritas Switzerland becomes aware of any inequalities or protection threats the necessary measures will be taken. All Caritas staff is trained in conflict sensitive approaches and gender sensitiveness.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

No

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

In a baseline and endline assessment conducted in all villages, questions of access to water will be asked. The results will be compared in order to assess the impact of the project in terms of access to water for the population.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Security is currently not an issue in the villages concerned. However, the situation remains volatile, reason for which the security situation will be re-assessed bi-annually to evaluate the safety of the beneficiaries and project staff. Caritas Switzerland has strict guidelines and security measures in place to avoid any unnecessary risk.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Questions about dignity of beneficiaries are included in the baseline and endline assessment.
