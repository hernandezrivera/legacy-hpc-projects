Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism, White Hands (WH) proposes a 12-month action that uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes emergency and regular food distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind & food voucher assistance modalities, and Bakery Support.
This program is a planned action submitted to Multiple Donors for funding, contributes to the first sector objective of the FSL cluster, which is "Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance," and aims to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and affected host communities with acute needs, living in the districts of Afrin, Jebel Saman, and A'zaz (Aleppo Governorate), and the districts of Ariha, Idleb, Harim, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur (Idleb) in NW Syria, in both the short to medium-term.
In the short term, WH is planning to reach 5,000 HHs/25,000 individuals with 20,000 in-kind RTERs based on their needs and reported situation. This is designed to be 2 distributions for exceptional cases in which beneficiaries do not have access to cooking facilities.
Equally, to meet the food security needs in the medium term, WH aims to assist 8,000 vulnerable internally displaced, returnees, and affected host communities' HHs (40,000 individuals) that meet the Food Security Program beneficiary eligibility criteria with regular monthly food assistance through the most appropriate modalities. This will include providing 3,500 HHs/17,500 individuals living in areas where markets are inaccessible or food is unavailable or unaffordable with in-kind food baskets for eight consecutive months and restricted food vouchers to a further 4,500 HHs/22,500 individuals with access to functioning markets in 8 distribution rounds, as recommended by the FSL Cluster and WFP.
The Bakery Support component provides approximately 15,000 individuals living in supported bakeries' catchment areas (mainly IDP camps) with free bread. This is achieved through subsiding up to 100% input (flour, yeast, and operational costs) of bread production, thus supporting five local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditioned upon Free bread production and distribution to IDPs daily and to be monitored by WH cash & markets and monitoring teams.
WH will identify targeted beneficiaries according to the following vulnerability selection criteria:
• Female-headed households (widows, divorced, GBV survivors).
• Child-headed households.
• Households with 6 family members and above.
• Households with pregnant or lactating women.
• Households with children under 5 years.
• Households with elderly/disabled family members (mental and/or physical).
• Households with low or no income (less than $20 per HH member).
• Households with poor shelter and living conditions.
• Households with significant debts.
Throughout this program, WH will identify the unique needs of men, women, girls, and boys and seek to maximize equality of treatment and access to services for all of these groups.
Moreover, WH M&E systems include accountability frameworks and have a continuously functioning complaints/feedback mechanism (CFMs) inside Syria that include direct mobile phone hotlines with Field MEAL Officer (including through WhatsApp/telephone/email/Facebook), and use of complaints boxes for submission of written complaints. Through CFMs, beneficiaries can safely provide feedback, report any project issues, and easily express concerns regarding the neutrality of WH.

The proposed targeted districts are currently facing a funding gap in Food Security Program, leaving many people in need without assistance.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 80,000 individuals (25,000 individuals provided with two-time emergency food assistance, 40,000 individuals supported with regular monthly food assistance, and 15,000 individuals supported with free bread distribution).

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO for Syria, 15M PiN for FSL, including 7.5M females & 4.2M PWDs.The WFP remote food security monitoring and recent food security and livelihood assessment in Syria (FSA, FSLA) estimate that seven in ten Syrian families is food insecure, with the highest concentration in Idleb (74%) and Aleppo (61%) Governorates, asthe compounded crises have touched every aspect of people’s lives, with access to food as one of the most pressing needs. Idleb Governorate continues to be impacted by the unstable security situation and the widespread economic crisis, which also hit local communities.Households, especially female-headed ones, reported facing food shortages and engaging food coping mechanisms to maximize the use of little available resources. For instance, households adopting Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) crisis or beyond food coping mechanisms [rCSI19+] reached as much as 50%. Furthermore, due to a lack of access to food or money to buy food, as many as 15% of HHs had to withdraw children from school, increasing risks for child labor and related protection concerns. 52% of HHs made difficult decisions of restricting food consumption for adults in favor of children, while 63% of HHs reported reducing the number of usually accustomed meals. HHs borrowing money to buy food reached as much as 58% indicating high levels of depleted means at the HH level. As many as 90% of HHs reported relying on markets as the main sources of consumed foods.

4 - Implementation of activities

1. WH is planning to reach 5,000 newly displaced HHs/25,000 individuals with RTERs within 72 hours of displacement, to cover the needs of 5- members’ HHs for one month. RTERs will be designed following the SPHERE standards and FSL cluster recommendations and aims to meet 2100 kcal/person/day for 5 days. 2. Provide 3,500 HHs/17,500 individuals with in-kind food baskets for 8 months and food vouchers with a value of 65$/HH/month to a further 4,500 HHs/22,500 individuals in 8 distribution rounds to cover a minimum of 70% of the SMEB as determined by the CWG and as per the FSL cluster recommendation, sufficient for the whole month for a family of 5 members (1550 kcal/person/day). 3. Support 5 bakeries (wheat flour, yeast, etc.) through appropriate modalities to enable 15,000 IDPs to access free bread. Activities will include Community Engagement, Market Assessment, Identification and verification of the beneficiaries, Identification of the vendors, Distribution of vouchers and in-kind FB to the beneficiaries, Redemption Period, Payment of Vendors, and Supervision & Monitoring through conducting the baseline & end-line assessments, on-site visits, beneficiary satisfaction surveys, ad-hoc monitoring of vendors and distributions, and PDM.The modality of response is determined by RAM. The most marginalized beneficiaries will be eligible for additional support (such as home delivery).WH will report its activities to the FSL cluster monthly using the 5Ws matrix formats.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). L: M/ I: H. MS: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set up, postponed, or canceled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that food/vouchers distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk2: For cash and vouchers there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. L: M/I: H. MS: Strengthen over-sight mechanism, develop a range of measures & internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption. Risk3: Tension between HC, IDPs & returnees. L: H/I: H. MS: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs & not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent & groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make an effort to target a % of the vulnerable HC in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism, conduct follow-up visits, and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: Food distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of food distribution points remains at a minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document as to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with extra workload in particular.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the ‎PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries ‎concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are ‎aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it.L: M/I: H. MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Assumption2: The project does not create distortions in the local economy.Risk2: As a consequence of the project, there is a shortage of food items in the area and the prices increase. L: M/I: M. MS2: Vendors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information regarding expected sales and the amount to be distributed to allow the supply of sufficient stocks. The price of the food items should be agreed upon with vendors and advertised to the beneficiaries. Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NWS. L: M/I: M. MS3: Follow strict preventive measures, building on the FSL guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity, All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions. Risk4: Aid diversion by armed groups, including looting of warehouses. Probability: L/Impact: M MS4: Invest in sound security measures at the warehouses, Train the staff on how to deal with aid diversion, Establish timely reporting mechanisms & info sharing with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters.
