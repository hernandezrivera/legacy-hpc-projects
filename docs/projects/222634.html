Project Summary

Approximately 4.2 million urgently need shelter and non-food items (NFIs) to meet their basic needs. This project seeks to address these needs through shelter, NFI, and Cash Voucher Assistance, focusing on newly displaced, returnees, and residents in priority areas, ensuring a timely and comprehensive response.
This project aims to deliver targeted SNFI assistance to 43,967 vulnerable households (263,802 individuals) across Abyei (33,000), Jonglei (69,000), Northern Bahr el Ghazal (57,000), Unity (50,802), Upper Nile (42,000), and Warrap (12,000) in South Sudan. The assistance will address immediate needs while promoting early recovery and resilience. IOM will coordinate closely with humanitarian partners, emphasizing information sharing, joint multisectoral approaches, and integrating HLP principles, as well as gender-, protection-, and conflict-sensitive strategies to uphold Do No Harm principles.
This IOM response will include:
Needs Assessments, Response teams and Distribution: IOM will maintain a rapid response team of over 25 specialists, including experts in protection, shelter, cash programming, and M&E, ready for deployment within 72 hours to address S-NFI needs in emergencies. The team will conduct assessments, registration, verification, distribution, and monitoring, collaborating with IOM’s HLP, WASH, Health, and Protection teams for an integrated response. IOM will conduct comprehensive needs assessments to identify vulnerabilities, mitigate risks, and, in coordination with the Cluster and Pipeline, provide essential shelter and NFI items.
Providing Cash and Voucher Assistance:
Delivering conditional and restricted SNFI assistance in South Sudan has been challenging due to diverse local shelter techniques, reliance on humanitarian aid, and hesitancy among small-scale vendors to engage with aid organizations. To address these challenges, IOM will adopt a flexible hybrid approach, combining cash assistance based on the Shelter NFI Sectoral Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) with in-kind distributions. This approach will coordinate with food assistance, protection, and WASH needs while mobilizing local vendors and skilled labor to boost SNFI supply availability.
The decision to scale up cash assistance will be contingent on the outcomes of a thorough market assessment to ensure that markets are functioning in affected areas. The results of this assessment will guide the implementation of cash-based modalities, ensuring that assistance is aligned with local market conditions.
The unconditional cash modality is inclusive in the current proposed budget but also in IOM’s MPC application. When local shelter vendors and skilled labor have the capacity to support higher-standard emergency and transitional shelter construction, and if HLP rights of households are ensured, conditional cash assistance may be provided in collaboration with WASH partners for integrated solutions, such as household latrines. However, this approach requires additional financial resources and technical expertise. Improved shelter solutions will be supported through a participatory approach, aligning cash assistance with market viability and the Shelter and NFI Cluster's strategy. This empowers households to address immediate needs, stimulate local markets, and accommodate diverse requirements effectively.
Exploring transitional solutions: IOM will prioritize transitional shelters to enhance resilience and reduce aid dependency, using climate-adapted, locally appropriate materials and disaster risk reduction measures. In areas with high IDP and returnee populations, IOM will collaborate with development partners to identify sustainable settlement options.
Improving conditions in camps and camp-like settings: IOM will work to enhance the living conditions of displaced people residing in camps and camp-like settings. This includes shelter maintenance and shelter repair.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face a complex and protracted humanitarian crisis driven by conflict, climatic shocks, and economic instability. In 2025, 9.3 million people 69% of the population require humanitarian assistance, with 4.2 million urgently needing shelter and NFIs, a significant rise from 2024. Key drivers include violence-induced displacement, escalating climate impacts, and economic challenges exacerbated by the Sudan crisis. The IPC has classified 46% of the population in Phase 3 or above, indicating the widespread nature of food insecurity, which directly correlates with the need for emergency shelter and NFIs.As of 29 November, OCH Situation Update, over 1.4 million people remain affected by flooding across 44 counties, with particularly severe impacts in Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Upper Nile states and the Abyei Administrative Area. More than half of the affected people are in Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states. Over 379,000 people are flood-displaced across 22 counties and Abyei. Many affected families are living in temporary shelters exposed to extreme weather conditions, heightening the risk of disease outbreaks such as malaria and cholera.Prolonged droughts and rising temperatures devastate livelihoods, heighten resource competition, and increase reliance on aid. These challenges, compounded by conflicts such as intercommunal violence, cattle raiding, and political instability, continue to displace families and disrupt communities heighten the urgency for emergency SNFI.The escalating impacts of the Sudan crisis have further compounded South Sudan's fragile situation, as per IOM DTM, Over 875,693 returnees have already crossed into South Sudan, with an additional 342,000 expected in 2025. Most of these returnees have settled in Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Jonglei states, arriving with minimal belongings and placing additional strain on host communities and creating an urgent need for transitional shelters and NFIs.Economic instability has worsened the crisis, diminishing purchasing power and leaving basic items unaffordable for many, making vulnerable populations heavily reliant on humanitarian aid. Shelter and NFI needs are extensive, with emergency shelters crucial for newly displaced populations and durable shelters with local materials and disaster risk reduction measures essential for long-term solutions. Beneficiary selection will follow the Shelter and NFI Cluster’s targeting criteria, prioritizing vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and the newly displaced, using data from IOM DTM and IRNA assessments. Essential NFIs like plastic sheeting, ropes, mosquito nets, blankets, sleeping mats, poles, and cooking sets will be provided to ensure dignity and improved living conditions. Cash and voucher assistance will also support shelter repair, aligning with market viability and enabling households to address their needs while stimulating local markets.The challenges to delivering aid are significant, including insecurity, ongoing violence, and flooding, which restrict access to high-priority areas. Poor infrastructure and seasonal rains further impede timely delivery of assistance. Despite these challenges, a coordinated and sustained response remains critical. The response strategy will combine in-kind support with cash assistance, emergency and durable shelter solutions, ensuring that both immediate and long-term needs are addressed. This approach aligns with the Shelter and NFI Cluster’s response strategy and ensures a flexible and effective response that enhances resilience, reduces dependency, and supports the transition from emergency relief to recovery and rehabilitation. By using cluster targeting criteria and integrating updated data sources, this intervention will ensure that resources are allocated efficiently and equitably, addressing the most urgent needs of displaced & vulnerable populations.

2. Project activities

1. Needs Assessment and RR Team: IOM will conduct comprehensive needs assessments to inform and guide its response efforts effectively. To ensure a timely and coordinated response, IOM will maintain a robust Rapid Response Team, capable of deploying across the country within 72 hours of an alert.2. Registration, Verification and distribution: For cash responses, IOM will ensure biometric registration through the DTM team to enhance accuracy and accountability. For in-kind responses, IOM will conduct token distribution and manual registration, followed by a request for item release from the S-NFI Core Pipeline, contingent upon the approval of needs and risk analysis reports by the S-NFI Cluster.3. Innovative and Sustainable Shelter Solutions (design, prototyping and construction): IOM seeks to implement a comprehensive shelter assistance program aimed at providing transitional and durable shelter solutions that are culturally appropriate. Bricks, bamboo, and thatched roofing are the preferred materials among the community, chosen for their adaptability to the local climate as well as to reduce dependency on Core Pipeline and promote local markets. This activity will be conducted following the guidance of SNFI Cluster, and in support of the SNFI Technical Working Group. 4. Providing Transitional / Durable Shelter Solutions through Conditional Cash Assistance: IOM aims to support IDPs, returnees, and residents with conditional cash grants to facilitate shelter construction based on pre-designed prototypes. IOM will cover 70% of the total shelter cost, with the remaining 30% contributed by the households through a participatory approach. The cash grants will be disbursed in two tranches, with the second tranche released upon verification of construction progress. This initiative will be directly implemented by IOM, ensuring compliance with HLP due diligence assessments.5. Shelter repair and maintenance: IOM will continue to enhance shelter conditions by shelter repair and maintenance in camps and camp-like settings, such as Sites A to E in Bentiu, and provide support to partners in Unity State, particularly in the Bentiu IDP camp, to address any gaps. 6. IOM employs a hybrid approach, combining in-kind distributions with unconditional, unrestricted cash assistance to address immediate shelter needs while maintaining a focus on sectoral outcomes. The cash assistance, valued at $80 per household based on the SNFI Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), enables households to procure essential shelter-related items, ensuring flexibility to meet diverse needs while stimulating local markets. Evidence shows that combining cash with in-kind aid reduces the likelihood of beneficiaries selling distributed items to address other priorities. To ensure effective shelter outcomes, IOM will:• Coordinate closely to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity with food assistance.• Integrate Protection and WASH considerations during joint targeting to address cross-sectoral needs.• Mobilize local vendors and skilled labor alongside cash distributions to enhance SNFI supply availability.This approach prioritizes sectoral outcomes, empowers households, fosters market engagement, and ensures culturally appropriate responses to immediate shelter needs.7. Market Assessment and Strengthening: IOM will support local SNFI vendors through cash-based interventions to strengthen local SNFI markets and promote cash assistance as a key response modality. Cash-related activities, including market and needs assessments, will be conducted to ensure feasibility and effectiveness. Where necessary, Cash-for-Work (CFW) initiatives will be incorporated to support livelihoods and enhance community contributions. This approach will address immediate needs while supporting local market functionality and fostering community resilience.8. Monitoring and Evaluation. 9. HLP support: IOM has dedicated HLP team to support HLP activities that has trained Shelter team
