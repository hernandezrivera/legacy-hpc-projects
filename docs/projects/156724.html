Project Summary

HIHFAD seeks to continue integrating nutrition related activities into the healthcare facilities it is currently operating. The intervention is intended to support the nutritional status for children under 5 years of age as well pregnant and lactating women across northwest Syria through the integration of the preventive and curative nutrition related activities into many primary, secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities across NW Syria that are operated by HIHFAD. The service package entails:
• Facility-based and outreach messaging about infant and young child feeding IYCF practices.
• Nutritional assessment for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years of age (Application of mid upper arm circumference MUAC and Z-scores among other assessment tools) and referral of undernourished cases to the appropriate facility as needed.
• Peer-to peer nutrition counselling for pregnant and lactating women.
• Peer-to peer nutrition counselling in terms of the infant and young child feeding practices for children 0-23 months
• Outpatient and inpatient management of moderate and severe acute malnutrition cases of children under 5 years of age.
The planned locations/ facilities are:
1. Atmeh (Idleb governorate/ Harim district/ Dana sub district/ Atmeh town) and Balyun (Idleb governorate/ Ariha district/ Ehsem sub district/ Balyun town) maternity and pediatric hospitals:
• IYCF counselling and messaging (Both peer to peer and group approaches).

• Outreach screening for malnutrition cases among children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women PLWs and referral of any suspected case into the relevant facility for further evaluation and follow up.
• Management of moderate acute malnutrition MAM cases among children U5 and PLWs.
• Management of severe acute malnutrition cases (Both complicated and non-complicated ones) among children U5.

Needs

As per the drafted 2019 HNO report for Syria, there is insufficient access to curative and preventive nutrition services, requiring interventions to be delivered at scale, especially skilled maternal, infant and young child nutrition support. Coverage of nutrition services continues to be insufficient due to limited available resources and need to be immediately scaled up, especially infant and young child feeding and maternal nutrition interventions.Lack of access to health services and harmful coping strategies such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition.Infant and young child feeding practices remain poor in Syria, with exclusive breastfeeding rates below pre-crisis levels at 24 per cent in 11 governorates. Barriers to appropriate feeding of young children include breastfeeding difficulties, illness of the mother, affordability, and lack of support from family. Coverage of IYCF services are largely awareness raising messages rather than the needed specialized services such as one-to-one counselling to better support pregnant women and caregivers on maternal nutrition and to safely feed their young children during the critical window of 0-24 months of age. Capacity of health and nutrition practitioners in specialized IYCF skills needs to be strengthened on a national scale.In terms of the areas where HIHFAD plans to implement nutrition activities within, the nutrition severity levels range between 3 and 5 out of 6 (Source: HNO data for Syria 2018), what potentially raise the need to continue with implementing nutrition related activities to address the needs of the targeted populations.

Implementation of activities

• Support facility and community based 1-1 counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices: As per the guidelines lately communicated by the nutrition cluster, HIHFAD will ensure investing more effort in terms of delivering peer to peer IYCF counselling rather than the group approach. To promote the optimal preventive practice, the implementation of this activity will take place at both facility and community levels. It’s imperative to mention that HHFAD will stick to the guidelines communicated by the health cluster in terms of SOPs and educational materials that will be discussed with the beneficiaries. In the same context, HIHFAD will ensure that the staff that are planned to conduct the counselling sessions possess the capacities required prior to conducting such sessions. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• PLW being reached with IYCF education in MBAs, health facilities, child protection sessions like mother-toddler and positive parenting, as well as community outreach: In combination with the providing the caregivers with the counselling about IYCF, HIHFAD will ensure that the caregivers are also provided with standardized messaging about the best IYCF practices. To ensure coordinated approach, all of the distributed educational materials will always be those adopted by the nutrition cluster. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• Provide Micronutrient supplementation to women and children for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies: As a preventive way of approach, HIHFAD will provide eligible women with Iron and folate supplements, and children under 5 with multivitamin supplements. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• Support facility- and community-based screening for acute malnutrition: screening for acute malnutrition is another approach of prevention HIHFAD will implement through all of the proposed facilities, where that will take place at both the facility and community levels. The screening will target children (6-59 months) as well as PLWs within the scope areas of the proposed facilities.• Support treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW: outpatient and inpatient management of acute moderate and severe malnutrition will continue to be available at both Atmeh and Balyun hospitals. While Mar Dibsi and Kalbeet PHC centres will provide the outpatient management only. As for Afrin PHC, the nutrition- related services will be limited to messaging, counselling, screening and referral.• Strengthen the capacity of public health staff on routine reporting and rapid nutrition assessment/SMART surveys: HIHFAD will make sure that the key staff at all of the proposed facilities are qualified enough to report on the cases encountered.• Strengthen/Establish Nutrition surveillance system: All of the proposed facilities have the required data entry tools to provide the nutrition- related inputs.

Protection Risk Analysis

The list of the key protection risks includes and not limited to the followings:(Abbreviations: LCs: Local councils/ HDs: Health directorates/ L: Low/ M: Medium/H: High)1. Beneficiaries are affected by conflict related incidences while seeking healthcare at the Facility.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Facility are based in areas included in the de-escalation zones agreement at adequate distances from gatherings of armed groups. Coordinate with the local authorities (Civilian LCs and HDs) to support the exclusion of the health facility from any possible clashes. Technical support by HIHFAD safety and security team. Training of staff on contingency plans. Ensure about the establishment of emergency exits within the facility.2. Patients are subject to harm due to low-quality healthcare services.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: HIHFAD follows strict HR recruitment procedures in terms of the qualifications and expertise required for medical personal. Also, HIHFAD consistently evaluate any gap of the technical capacities for its medical staff. Feedback mechanisms to be made available for the beneficiaries to ensure their participation in identifying any gap in the staff technical capacities that need to be addressed. In the same context, HIHFAD consistently seeks to ensure the standardization of the services provided in terms of medical equipment, medical supplies and pharmaceuticals.3. While at Facility, Beneficiaries are abused, harassed, undergo compromised privacy or dignity.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Child safeguarding training provided for all staff. Establishment of feedback mechanisms with maintained anonymity. Ensure individuals of vulnerable groups (children, women, elderly and people with disabilities) are accompanied while at the Facility. Ensure adequate female staff representation. Application of privacy screens and separation of medical examination theatres. Establishment of breast-feeding corners. Separation of the bathrooms by sex. Label tags to be demonstrated within the Facility. Establishment of adequate waiting areas and ensure adequate lighting of the facility is in place.4. Confidentiality of the information provided by the Beneficiaries is compromised.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: staff of the Facility adapt HIHFAD code of conduct. Tags about confidentiality of patient records and information to be demonstrated within the Facility. Maintaining patient information through well secured and strictly monitored modalities inaccessible to public. Establishment of feedback mechanisms with maintained anonymity. 5. Communities are affected by medical wastes produced at the Facility.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Securing the medical wastes prior to disposal. Application of medical waste management pathways for the purpose of proper disposal of them.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption #1Cross-border operations and humanitarian space are sufficiently preserved to allow implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Risk/s: Security incidents impacting border-crossings, terror attacks, cross-border shelling or clashes on Turkish soil. Consequences:Possible damage to relations with communities.Complications with assists transfer and delivery including medical consumables and pharmaceutical itemsDelays or stoppage of programming in multiple areas Mitigations:Options for procuring the needed supplies from inside Syria.Explore alternate official border crossings.Maintain good relations with local communitiesSecure stock in HIHFAD warehouses for emergency measures. Assumption #2The safety and security situation remain relatively stable in the project implementation areas in a way that allows staff to travel to and operate sufficiently. Risk/s:Increased suspicion/tension or armed clashes between the parties to the conflict.Airstrike raids or shelling bombardment. Consequences:Death or serious injury to the staff or the beneficiaries. Mitigations:Delays/stoppage of programming Reduce exposure to areas that are known to be targetedEnsure contingency plans are in place for cases of aerial bombardment and indirect fireEnsure that the facility has a safe room equipped as a shelter for the staff in case of conflict-related incidents. Assumption #3Targeted populations maintain adequate levels of cooperation, commitment and willingness to get engaged in the project activities. Risk/s:Rise of anti-NGO activities and loss of acceptance within the targeted communities. Mitigations:Insure that feedback and accountability mechanisms are in place Ensure stronger communications and relations with the local civil authorities.
