Project Summary

This six month action will target the newly displaced vulnerable residents’ and new returnees aged between 6-18 years as well as otherwise vulnerable or marginalized girls and boys at risk including children with disabilities(CWDs) that flee their homes from conflict affected areas that has been excluded from education in order to have a better equitable access to essential services, and promote their protection, safety and dignity in improved protective learning environments and enhance lifesaving learning strcuture.This action prioritize immediate critical life-saving education needs security, access and children safety . SC will make use of the education facilities as safe friendly spaces for the children during the school holidays. Once the schools re-open, SCI with its local partners will ensure the retention of both the displaced and non-displaced vulnerable girls and boys including those with disabilities. SCI through this project will intervene in Blue Nile, South Kordofan, Sinnar, Red Sea and Aljaziera states targeting a total of 407,372, 400,000 vulnerable and newly displaced children (196,000 boys,204,000 girls) 5% out of them are children with disabilities, 1778 teachers (871 male, 907 female) and 5594 OOSC (2741 boy ,2853 girl). This action will be executed into two phases, phase one from June to September 2023 through provision of services to the most vulnerable children and support water and sanitation infrastructure in schools with an integrated package of education and protection assistance and integrated interventions in Wash in school and provision of clean water and distribution of hygiene kits and provision of psychosocial support activities which includes access to life-saving information and survival skills, including awareness of hazards, better nutrition, protection from SEA and other forms of GBV, and child protection and well-being awareness. To support the traumatized children and enhance their psychological wellbeing. Phase 2 of this action will be from September until end of December, a mobilization campaigns will be conducted to register and enroll the newly displaced and vulnerable children into the established temporary learning spaces and schools near their gathering points and to facilitate children’s safe return to school in the longer-term, there is an intervention of construction of new gender sensitive latrines following the universal design in order to accommodate all children including CWDs and construction temporary handwashing facilities, in addition , children will be provided with feeding programs in close coordination with SCI feeding interventions in the targeted localities. .These interventions are combined with specific Education in Emergencies support including providing temporary learning spaces for displaced children, distributing teaching and learning materials, training teachers and facilitators and enhance their professional development and trained on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection. During phase 2, this action will support the innovative alternative learning modalities through ALP classes, which will be established in the newly constructed learning spaces. Mapping of referral pathways will be conducted to identify existing ones to support the most vulnerable children that will be connecting the schools with diversity of services for the children. The inclusion of children with disabilities is an integral component of this action, school infrastructure and newly established learning spaces will be constructed to accommodate the needs of children with disabilities, awareness on disability and inclusion will be conducted within the mobilization campaigns, and within the teachers training, they will also receive training on inclusive learning environment and disability. SC will follow a complementarity approach through building on EQUIP2 program ongoing in Kadougli -SK and WadAlmahi in BN.

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 ) in multiple cities across Sudan, including Khartoum, Al Fasher, Merowe, Nyala, Ag Geneina, Zalingi and El Obeid having devastating consequences for civilians. As of May 21, at least 1,042,114 individuals (209,136 households) have reportedly been displaced inside Sudan since 15 April, according to estimates by IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). In West Darfur, most of the residents of Ag Geneina town have left due to the fighting that erupted on 24 April; about 248,625 people (23.86% of total IDPs) are estimated to be displaced in the state. Overall Fourteen (15) out of Sudan’s 18 states have been affected by displacement. 319,194 people have crossed in neighboring countries. Since the outbreak of the conflict, an estimated 404,028 school aged children has been displaced (Reference: MSNA 2022, 38.77% disaggregation) 73% out of them are school going children (reference: Sudan ESSP 2019-2023 – 73% GER) and an estimated 15% are children with disabilities. The conflict has negatively affected the education of affected girls, boys and adolescents, including children with disabilities who face challenges in accessing inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment. In addition, the conflict exposes vulnerable children to a range of life-threatening risks such as GBV, human / sex trafficking and SEA recruitment by armed actors and child labor. This combined with school closures, will exacerbate and negatively affect school attendance for the new academic year that starts in September. In addition to pre-existing challenges related to insufficient learning resources and supplies, the crisis has left learners and teacher displaced and traumatized; damaged learning infrastructure and school spaces, and constrained access to schools (Revised HRP May 2023). According to Sudan conflict rapid need assessment conducted by SCI and UNICEF on May 2023 in Aljaziera state, 63,284 individuals displaced across the country, the conflict is deeply affecting important part of children’s lives. Some schools in safer areas are sheltering displaced families and examination is interrupted in a number of areas. Findings of the assessments indicated that conflict has severe impacts on learning opportunities. Some schools in safer areas such as White Nile, South Kordofan and Gezira are currently occupied by families fleeing Khartoum and Darfur regions, thus schools cannot be used for learning purposes, examinations interrupted in certain states due to the occupation of schools as well as conflict restricting movement and access to schools. Forced displacement of teachers and children. According to Acted global Emergency Response unit coordinated with Monitoring, Evaluation rapid need assessment in BN, Aljaziera, food items is not sufficient and has grown worse since the crisis began. With major supply chain disruptions throughout the country, the supply of hygiene items has dwindled, and KII respondents reported that costs are rising significantly, particularly in Gezira, Blue Nile. In terms of sanitation, assessment findings indicate that latrines and handwashing facilities have not been subject to significant damage following the crisis, but latrines are over -crowded in camps and collective centers in Blue Nile and Gezira state. Regarding protection, respondents from all states reported concerns around protection, safety, particularly as the conflict develops, and people become more desperate. Key Informants reiterated that informal sites lack security or clear boundaries, and many respondents indicated concerns around GBV and sexual exploitation (SUDAN CRISIS Rapid Needs Assessment: Blue Nile; Gedaref; Gezira; White Nile 11 May 2023- Acted organization)

2 - Activities/Outputs

The implementation of this project activities will be fully guided by and the revised HRP 2023, SCI HRP 2023 and Save the Children Quality Learning framework (QLF) that developed in consideration of INEE and Sphere. The response strategy of this action will adopt a two-pronged approach: first to support displaced children and second to support children where they live (original settlements) once security, access and children safety allows. The first set of activities are towards ensuring the accessibility of vulnerable or marginalized children (ages 6-18) to safe and protective learning environments through construction of more 20 learning spaces and WASH facilities through construction of 30 latrines, establishment of 80 temporary hand washing facilities and provision of safe drinking water to 80 schools . 2-Provision of all supplies that includes learning and teaching materials and recreational materials and hygiene sets, complementarity-feeding program. Provision of clean water and establishment of hand washing facilities in the newly established learning spaces will be ensured. 20 TLSs will be constructed following the universal design to include 1300 children from newly displaced children including CWDs and provide them with catch up lessons. In order to encourage teachers and ensure they will continue teaching, this action will provide monthly incentive to 1778 teachers, and this will massively contribute to the sustainability of the learning process and improvement of the learning outcomes. This action will provide a lifesaving skills to the newly displaced and returnees through capacitating 1778 teachers in on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection issues to support the traumatized children to have access to access to life-saving information and survival skills, including awareness of hazards, better nutrition, protection from SEA and other forms of GBV, and child protection and well-being awareness. This action will target 407,372 beneficiaries’ (264,792 IDPs, 20,369 returnees, 122,212 vulnerable residents)
