Project Summary

In line with the FSL cluster strategy for 2024, AFAA will contribute towards saving lives & building community resilience of over 184,050 beneficiaries (30,675HHs) through provision of in-kind and unconditional cash assistance, enhancing emergency food production though distribution of livelihood kits and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs through resilience building activities such as training and market support functionality. About 5.83 million (46.8%) of South Sudanese Population are experiencing high levels of food insecurity (classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (crisis or worse) due to insecurity, yearly flooding, drought and where the farming practices among the predominantly peasantry communities are very poor culminating in to problems of food insecurity, poverty and environmental degradation. Based on the latest IPC analysis and projections between December,2023-March, 2024, 1.71 million people are likely to be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 25000 people in IPC Phase 5 (catastrophe) critically food insecure and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3% of the population) in the lean season between April-July, 2024 will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in phase 4 (Emergency) and this is driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, influx of returnees, limited access to agricultural tools, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, limited access to basic services, capacity gap, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The project activities will be in line with the cluster Objectives “CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & 5).
CO2: Enhance & sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable & crop livelihood inputs, fishing & livestock.
CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.

1. Needs

Based on the latest IPC Data (Sept 2023-July, 2024), about 5.83 million people (46.8%) in South Sudan are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity(classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). In the first projection period of December,2023-March, 2024, 1.71 million people are likely to be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 25000 people in Phase 5 (catastrophe) With food assistance expected to decrease by 10-25% from already reduced levels in 2023 coupled with increasing humanitarian emergencies such as consecutive floods and ongoing violence point to the fact that projected resources available for 2024 will only be sufficient to cover eight months of need, with the impacts predominantly being felt by women, children, individuals with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) especially in Unity and Upper Nile States with acute malnutrition burden of 72%.According the South Sudan HNO 2023, the main drivers for this (1) a combination of shocks, such as climactic, pervasive environmental degradation, protracted armed conflict, access constraints, operational interference and economic collapse (including, inflation, increased poverty, disruptions to livelihoods, floods and droughts); and (2) household idiosyncratic stressors, such as GBV, lack of access to basic essential services, deaths caused by diseases or deaths of family breadwinners. Likewise, certain cross-cutting issues such nutrition, WASH – particularly in food preparation – and protection concerns, such as GBV, displacement and conflict further exacerbate stresses on food security. Although the international focus is shifting away from Food Security and Livelihood interventions, the severity of food insecurity is expected to worsen in 2023/2024 compared to 2022/2023. Since the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023, the influx of people fleeing the country has increased exponentially, with more than 75,000 individuals, including 14,000 South Sudanese returnees, crossing through 12 key border entry points along the Sudan-South Sudan border, as of 24 May 2023. The real numbers are likely to be higher as some people entered the country without registration, with the average number of people arriving daily recorded at 3,500 individuals. The conflict in Sudan has already affected South Sudan's economic outlook, particularly for northern States that rely heavily on imports from Sudan, leading to an increase in food prices and fuel costs. This could potentially exacerbate the protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population of the targeted States but also returnees arriving from Sudan and increase food insecurity, negative coping mechanisms and acute humanitarian needs. To address these needs, AFAA will continue to play a role of a strong FSL partner and conduct activities in line with the following cluster objectives: CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & 5) CO2: Enhance & sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable & crop livelihood inputs, fishing & livestock. CO3: Reduce dependency on food & agricultural inputs to support & strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes.

2. Project activities

To achieve these objectives, AFAA will 1. Undertake unconditional cash/ voucher transfers for the most severely food insecure (IPC 4 & 5, refugees and POCs), with blanket supplementary feeding for children under 5 and PWLs in Malakal, Panyijar, Longechuk, and Terekeka counties. 2. Distribute fishing & rapid response livelihood kits as part of an integrated response 3. Protect livelihoods through main season seeds & tool, fish & vegetable kit distribution in Malakal, Longechuk, Panyijar, and Terekeka Counties 4. Implement activities to help sustain community coping mechanisms and build resilience in; measures to combat: Post harvest losses (PHL); pest surveillance & control especially FAW, DL, & pesticides in IPC 3. 5. Support market functionality: scaled up support for market dependent households in Malakal, Longechuk, Panyijar and Terekeka counties. 5. Train and support agricultural extension workers to provide sessions and technical guidance to identified and selected farmers. 6. Establish and support VSLA groups from producing groups to improve on their household income - Establishment of Farmers Field Schools (FFSs). 7. Training of selected beneficiaries in postharvest handling techniques. 8. Establishment and training of project management and AAP committees made up of 60% women and 40% men. 9. Training of established fisher folk groups on improved fishing techniques. 10. Provision of water pumps, irrigation training/support, 11. Project Launch. 12. Establishment and training of cash distribution committee members on protection mainstreaming in cash intervention, their role in project implementation, and selection criteria, 13. Beneficiary communication, Community mobilization, sensitization & information sharing, 14. Beneficiary identification, selection, verification & registration.
