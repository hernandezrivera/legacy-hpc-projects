Project Summary

1. providing 180 awareness sessions and 204 outreach activities on different issues
including health and protection issues such as first aid, child labor, early marriage, and
GBV. The awareness sessions were built and developed to cover the following titles: Introduction, arterial hypertension, vital signs, infectious diseases, asthma, malnutrition, and diabetes, in addition to a full course of first aid (9 sessions).
2. Provide PSS to 3100 beneficiaries; IMDAT field team will conduct individual and group
protection support services for community members at Afrin. Marginalized and vulnerable
groups (PWD, OP and FHH) will be encouraged to participate, information will be made
available in all local languages, and staff will reflect diversity to enable a welcoming
atmosphere for all. the sessions kit material was built and developed to cover 9 titles: Introduction and definition, how do you know that you need psychological counseling, self-caring, dealing with suicide cases, dealing with stress, dealing with anger and sadness, parenting skills.
3. Provide childcare services to 3600 children. The services will be provided in the center
for the children of sessions beneficiaries. Provide childcare services for these children
allowing both mothers and women to attend either the awareness and/or PSS sessions.
iMDAT will follow the Save the Children handbook on the ratio of care givers to children.
At least two adult caregivers for 15 children aged from 2 years and above. child care
services will be conducted via outreach activities, children with disabilities will be included
in the child protection targeting plan. The childcare kit material was built and developed to cover the following titles: introduction session, building self-confidence, and child rights, honesty, tolerance, expressing feelings, building a friendship, stimulating imagination, respect and morals, and health awareness.
The Monitoring process will monitor the project implementation. The M & E team in the
field are well trained on the humanitarian principles and aware of the organization’s code
of conduct. As IMDAT uses the feedback and complaint mechanism from the beginning
of the project, it allows the team to mitigate and follow corrective measures during the
project constantly. All the data collected during the beneficiary selection and verification
process, baseline assessment, distribution evaluation and any other monitoring will be
analyzed immediately, and the results will be used to improve and mitigate any activities
if needed.M&E data will be collected and analyzed through Kobo Toolbox which will save
time and effort using smart tablets instead of paperwork. The tools will be designed together with IMDAT management, and the M&E team will be trained on the data
collection process using the Kobo forms. For any activity, iMDAT conduct assessment and
monitor it based on a statistical representative sample size.
in addition to that, iMDAT will attend the protection cluster, GBV and CP meetings and
submit the 4 WS on a monthly basis.
The main source of harm stems from a possible politicization of humanitarian assistance
by any of the parties to the conflict. To avoid causing any harm, IMDAT is well connected
with the communities it serves, strictly adheres to the humanitarian principles, and
actively engages all groups in the project. In line with the preliminary findings of the
conducted study on the challenges in providing independent humanitarian assistance in
NWS, IMDAT will communicate well with community leaders of different community
groups and ensures maximum transparency, especially with regards to its selection
criteria and process but also highlights its accountability versus the beneficiaries. The
self-targeting approach will also be employed to avoid causing any exclusion or harm to
the served community.

3 - Needs

the project was accepted in 2022-2023, so these six months are a continuation of the last year project. so far, the project covered 10175 beneficiaries from PSS, health awareness sessions, children protection services. The protracted nature of the crisis has weakened the protective environment, undermined resilience andchallenged recovery. Its cumulative impact has created high levels of psychological distress. During the 2022 MSNA, 73 per cent of heads of households interviewed reported that at least one household member had exhibited signs of psychological distress over the previous 30 days. The continuing impact of the conflict has depleted the resources and capacity for resilience of the population living in Syria, leading to an increase in harmful coping mechanisms. Protection risks in Syria continue to have a multifaceted and differential impact on all population groups. Displaced women, men, girls and boys in camps, collective shelters and informal settlements are particularly vulnerable and live in some of the hardest places to reach. Approximately 8.5 million people are in need of GBV assistance (a 1.2 million increase from 2022), of which 55.7 per cent are women and 37.5 per cent are girls. Women and girls in Syria are increasingly at risk of GBV. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 28 per cent of surveyed households87 (2 per cent higher than 2022), demonstrating the increasing cumulative toll on mental well-being.
