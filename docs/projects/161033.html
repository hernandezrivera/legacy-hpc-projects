Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan remains sudden, unpredictable and recurrent despite the overall stabilization of the political context. That is the reason why the RRM mechanism remains the appropriate way to address the needs to the most vulnerable and affected populations. The current capacities of the EP&R and other existing Rapid Response Mechanism systems are insufficient in comparison to the existing needs. Thus, the proposed project aims at contributing and scaling up the RRM mechanism.
Through this project, SI will be able to deploy rapid assessments to evaluate all the newly identified crisis: epidemic, movements of population, flood, or in life threatening humanitarian crisis like in Aburoc, Upper Nile.
These assessments will be mainly oriented to assess the WaSH needs and gaps, but will include an integrated approach strategy with comprehensive data collection regarding other sectors such as health, nutrition, food security or protection mainstreaming. These assessments will be carefully reported and shared through the WaSH Cluster and any other relevant forum. SI is already an active member of the EP&R working group and will share its main findings to its partners.
Afterwards, if needs are confirmed, the EP&R actors will decide collectively, according to the situation, to deploy a RRM response or to advocate towards other sectors or static actors.
Another aspect to this project is the deployment of full WaSH package responses. Indeed, depending on the findings of technical assessments, SI will improve the water supply, mainly through repair, rehabilitation, cleaning and chlorination of the water points. At the same time, SI will scale up the access to toilets with repair and rehabilitation of the sanitation infrastructures at household and institutional levels. Furthermore, WaSH NFI and MHM kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable in order to ensure the consumption of safe water. Finally, the community will be directly involved in the project implementation and will actively participate in hygiene promotion campaigns, awareness sessions and through the recruitment of Community Hygiene Promoters. All these activities will be dealt with a strong gender-sensitivity approach, to prevent GBV issues around the WaSH services and facilities and in respect of the Do No Harm principle.
The interventions will be based on specific triggers. Indeed, it can be expected more movements of population will happen during the dry season. The specificity of the RRM mechanism will allow to address the needs of the recent displaced population, either IDPs or returnees.
These movements, added to the insufficient rains registered for 2019 so far, will add pressure on the existing water sources. This could impact the refill of the ground water table or the availability of surface water, with potential shortage of drinkable water to be expected. Furthermore, the period of the project could partially cover the lean season when the population could be in vulnerable livelihood situation.
The RRM capacity is a strong added-value when it is well coordinated. A close follow-up of the needs and alerts, a clear communication and information sharing and an accurate analysis of the local capacities can enhance the efficiency and the relevance of this EP&R mechanism.

1 - Needs

Even with the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement in late 2018, the cumulative effects of the conflict, which has been lasting for half a decade, have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than 7 million people, drivers and multipliers of crisis have remained present over time. In 2019, the conflict continued to force people to remain on the move and undermined their access to assistance. An increasing number of IDPs is expected to seek durable solutions in 2020, although movement will continue to be fluid as people explore options for return, relocation or local integration. But some of these returns may not be sustainable due to insecurity.The conflict has continued to drive people’s WaSH needs, from blocking or destruction of WaSH infrastructure to limiting WaSH commodities in the market. Insecurity at times prevents humanitarian actors from accessing areas that have high WaSH needs, while the presence of armed groups can deter civilians from accessing existing WaSH infrastructure. As a consequence, and according to the 2019 HRP, nearly 6 million South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019.In term of water supply, only 29% of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns. The remaining 2/3 of the population are thus required to walk greater lengths to access water or are reliant on surface water or unprotected water sources, thus exposed to high risks of diseases. Furthermore, women and girls face increased risks of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines. The lack of access to sanitary materials is preventing women and girls to carry on with their daily lives, access to menstrual hygiene products and appropriate and dignified washing locations remain one of their key priority. Finally, it has to be noticed through HRP report that more than 90% of the population practices open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility.In addition, children will not have sufficient access to food and livelihoods in 2019. Between January and March, some 3.7 million are projected to be in IPC Phase 3, some 1.6 million in Phase 4 and 35,000 in IPC Phase 5. Conflict affected, displaced households and returnees continue to face alarming food consumption gaps, asset depletion and malnutrition rates. Access is restricted and many small pockets of populations are in hiding without access to services. Furthermore, these areas are likely to witness future displacement linked with lack of resources, pockets of conflicts, constant inter-community conflict, natural disasters etc.An integrated Emergency preparedness and Response model will ensure efficient humanitarian assistances. Indeed, the EP&R mechanism will ensure that the assistances are rapidly delivered on timely manner to relieve the affected population from further sufferings.SI has wide experience in South Sudan Emergency prepends and response thanks to the past implementation of similar projects in the country. With this this proposed action, SI plans to boost the existing WaSH capacities in the country in delivering urgent integrated humanitarian assistances.

2 - Activities

SI is planning to implement different activities to answer the most crucial WaSH needs identified. Firstly, WaSH and rapid multi-sectorial assessments will be deployed in frontline and hard to reach areas recently affected by crisis: conflicts, population movements, outbreaks, natural disasters, etc. These assessments will be conducted by SI’s RRM teams, to collect the immediate multi-sectoral needs, including WaSH, food security & livelihood, nutrition, health, shelters and protection mainstreaming. To prepare the deployment of the EP&R interventions, access and security will be deeply assessed. This activity will be strongly coordinated with other EP&R actors and Clusters, by following early warning system and alert sharing, which will ensure synergy and information sharing. The EP&R team will be deployed within 3-8 days after the Cluster’s request for a mobile response. In line with the EP&R strategy, SI could also launch a rapid assessment within 72 hours in case of AWD or cholera outbreak, in coordination with the WaSH cluster. Before the assessment is conducted, the communities will be involved and sensitized about the purpose of the partners’ presence and the assessment itself. These assessments will have a conflict sensitive approach in line with the Do No Harm principle by analyzing the possible unintended effects of an intervention to better mitigate them; in line with the gender issues mitigation in terms of violence, discriminations, etc. (cf. AAP). Considering the level of priority and needs, SI will produce the assessment report within 5 days after returning from the field. This report will be largely shared within the EPR partners, the Clusters and their members, and the different humanitarian stakeholders through the Clusters system.Once the intervention is validated, SI will deploy a WaSH package depending on the assessed needs and specific context. SI will design the response based on technical assessments and protection mainstreaming analysis. SI will strive to carry out consultations/focus group discussions with different groups of people, especially with people with special needs, such as women, girls, boys, people with disabilities and elderlies. The aim will be to identify and understand the specific needs and vulnerabilities, to ensure they are well taken into account, but also to identify the risks of negative impacts of the response and the solutions for an equal and fair access to the humanitarian program. When available, SI could work with local protection and/or GBV partners to optimize this gender-risk analysis.Concerning activities related to the access to safe drinking water, a detailed technical assessment will allow to decide between the different modalities of intervention, in line with the different emergency scenarios. The repairs and rehabilitations of existing infrastructures will be prioritized, notably to enable a fair ratio cost/efficiency. To ensure the provision of safe water during the works, SI will chlorinate all the water points. In case of emergency and without alternative, a SWAT system could be installed (pumping, treatment, storage and distribution of surface water). SI will provide the maximum of the materials from the Core Pipeline stocks, purchasing only what is not available from the pipeline. Water testing will be conducted before any rehabilitation and daily monitoring of the residual chlorine (pool tester) and weekly biological tests (H2S and delagua kits) will be carried out at water points and HHs level.On the sanitation side, SI will decommission, repair and rehabilitate latrines, at household and community level: schools, health centres... SI will decommission any latrine at risk because of collapsing, filling or over flooding. SI will particularly focus on the latrines causing the most important risks in terms of contamination and spread of AWD diseases. Then, the latrines will be repaired or rehabilitated. Finally, SI will carry out Hygiene promotion campaign

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The displacement-affected communities will be the main beneficiaries of the project. SI will target individuals impacted by the displacements, either directly, IDPs, returnees, or indirectly, such as host community having their access to essential services impacted by the influx.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The main objective of the EP&R interventions is to launch short actions and not to stay longer than 3 months with a sole purpose of life-saving activities. Because of this specific mechanism, the exit strategy and the project sustainability consists in: - working on a close coordination with the static actors present in the targeted areas,- looking for additional funds when a static and mid-term response is necessary,- advocating through the Clusters mechanism for deployments from other humanitarian actors and additional sectors.In addition, the exit strategy and the sustainability of the intervention will be captured in the technical solutions to be implemented, the involvement of local communities and authorities and through the coordination with static actors. The EPR intervention will contribute to SI’s global intervention in South Sudan, contributing to the resilience of the targeted population. Thus, when relevant and possible, SI will ensure a follow-up of the intervention through other projects implemented in the same location. In case SI has no static presence in the area, SI plans to handover the follow-up to another static organization.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the 2020 HNO/HRP objectives.In addition, the project aligns with South Sudan’s National Development Strategy (2019-2021). It addresses one of the 6 National Development Strategy Approach of increasing and expanding efforts to build resilience.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is suﬃciently safe and stable and adequate for project implementation. However, the overall political context will be closely monitored. Indeed, many security analysts (internal and external including at the United Nations level) think that areas around contentment sites are likely to face some security issues related to misconduct of the forces and lack of payment of salaries. in addition, any collapse in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement will worsen the protection environment displacing more people with restricted delivery of humanitarian aid and increased violence against women.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the sustainability of the project and its impact will be included at the design stage of each activitity. Overall, the project design includes sustainability/handover. Sustainability is entailed in the project plan, working mainly with the local of stakeholders Raja county. The project will ensure participation of local leaders and community structures as a capacity building strategy to ensure skills are transferred.More specifically, Water and sanitation management committee will be set up to ensure day to day running of the project. The water committees will be selected on commitment, and will be trained in order to give them the capacity to maintain the infrastructures once the project completed.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In order to mitigate the above-mentioned risks, SI will strive to gain acceptance from local authorities and stakeholders; will follow strict logistics and financial procedures; will streamline security risk assessments at all stages of program design; will gather up-to-date security alerts from various formal and informal sources and will adjust its actions to the context. Some measures are already in place, for instance, community awareness sessions about the principles and arrangement of the interventions are always planned before implementing SI’s actions, allowing spaces for Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (CFCM). In addition, SI staff are equipped with the capacities to negotiate access with the authorities. Access negotiation is based on a strong commitment on the respect of IHL and humanitarian principles. Specific trainings of the key people involved are conducted. Furthermore, SI will make sure its intervention will not put beneficiaries at risk. This is done by conducting risk analysis on WaSH infrastructures. When constructing boreholes or latrines, SI will always ensure it will be done in a safe place for women. Latrines blocks will be composed of different stances for men and women. During NFI distributions, SI will consider the distance from the accommodation to the distribution sites and ensure beneficiaries can reach back to their home before dark falls. Complaint desks on site will also allow SI to adjust its activities according to the beneficiaries’ feedback. Finally, post-distribution monitoring will measure the level of satisfaction on safety related matters at different times of the project.SI will include the HRP/Cluster recommendations in order to mitigate these risks.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Affected population and returnees’ point of view and opinions will be taken into consideration all along the proposed action to ensure its relevance and acceptance. They will be involved from the assessments to the monitoring of activities, including for the beneficiaries’ selection, the composition of kits and the implementation of activities. SI staffs are trained to ensure the needs of the different population groups are assessed. Before the intervention, SI will organize a minimum of 3 focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for the community consultations to ensure equitable access to the services provided. This feedback will be used to adapt the kits and the way to distribute, monitor and improve the quality of the work. It will ensure the appropriate uses of the distributed items and also improve the mobilization and participation of the population. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. For each location, SI will also create a distribution committee and a complaint committee with members of the community. They will be responsible for the link between affected households and SI. They will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community, etc. SI will set up a specific complaint/feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the complaints and feedbacks from the beneficiaries and take appropriate action. In this way, SI will be present to listen and register the complaints, to collect information and to answer the questions. If SI has already large experience of accountability to the communities and complaints responses mechanisms, the AAP for this project will be also aligned with the standardized community-led complaints and feedback.
