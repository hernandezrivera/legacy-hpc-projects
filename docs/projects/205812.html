Project Summary

Save the Children International intends to increase protective factors for boys, girls, adolescence, youths and women affected by conflict and climate induced shocks in Renk, Maban and Akobo counties of South Sudan. The proposed intervention will target most vulnerable children and families’ returnees and refugees affected by the Sudan and Ethiopia conflict and climate induced shocks in host communities and refugee camps.
According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, around 3.1 million children and adolescents face violence in South Sudan. The situation is currently compromised by the large number of the returnees and refugees coming into South Sudan with some integrated into host communities where there are pre-existing challenges of economic hardship and negative coping mechanisms which increase protection risks. From early 2023, South Sudan has been receiving an influx of returnees and refugees displaced by the ongoing conflict in Sudan and Ethiopia refugee camps. According to the OCHA (0ct, 2023), 341,967 people had been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since April 2023 due to the ongoing conflict. Of these, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children under age 18 with South Sudanese returnees accounting for 86% of all arrivals. In October, there was significant rise in the number of arrivals per day of up to 3800 individuals which was more than double the number received in previous months putting a resource constrain on the ongoing response. On the other hand, shrinking response services and conflict between Anyuak and Nuer Ethnic groups in Gambella Region of Ethiopia forced South Sudanese who were once staying in Ethiopian refugee camps to return home. The local authority and RRC in Akobo County reported approximately more than 23, 000 returnees to have arrived in the county during April to August 2023 to the centers and the surrounding villages without any humanitarian assistance. With Maban being the main county supporting refugees, the numbers have been soaring due to the displacements with Save the Children child protection camp programming recording a total of 36, 829 households and 183, 112 individuals across all four refugee camps.
Pre-existing challenges in South Sudan inclusive of intercommunal conflicts and flooding increases child protection issues resulting in high numbers of unaccompanied and separated children caused by displacements and abductions, negative coping strategies such as child marriages, dropping out of school, child labour and joining armed groups among others. The influx of returnees and refugees escalates the situation increasing need for a strengthened protective environment of an already vulnerable population and the new populations. Save the Children proposes to reach 28,983 beneficiaries (17,440 children (8,690 boys and 8,750 girls) and 11,543 adults (5,806 females and 5,737 males through child protection in emergencies interventions inclusive of focused and non-specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) using the HEART model to address trauma and distress; provision of case management services including cash based assistance, family tracing and reunification, alternative family care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children and strengthening of referral pathways; distribute dignity kits to vulnerable adolescent girls and women; provision of recreation and PSS services in child friendly spaces, awareness raising sessions on norms and practices that perpetuate and violate violence against women and girls (VAWG), strengthening community-based child protection mechanisms and the social service workforce. SCI interventions aim to provide lifesaving interventions and where possible integrating with other sectors and defining linkages with other actors supporting the strengthening of child protection systems.

1. Needs

The humanitarian needs across Republic of South Sudan continue to rise. The protracted situation is deteriorating as a result of conflict (internal/external) and endemic violence, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks, climatic shocks resulting in an exacerbated localized drought and flooding. The ongoing conflict in the neighboring country of Sudan and patches of intercommunal violence in Ethiopian refugee camps exacerbates the situation as this has resulted in an influx of returnees and refugees. This has negatively impacted on the communities` livelihoods, ability to adapt resiliently in times of adversity and degraded access to food, sanitation, hygiene, water and health services. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 estimated 5.53 million people in need of humanitarian services. Among those in need, the projected figure for children is 3.15M (42%) in need of all-inclusive child protection services (Case Management, MHPSS and Family Tracing and Reunification)To address these challenging needs, SC proposes to implement a 12-month multi-sector life-saving project with core interventions in Child Protection (CP), and also integrating SGBV, Multipurpose Cash Assistance (3% of the total budget), and non-focused community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to enable inclusive coverage of the humanitarian needs in the selected locations of Renk, Maban and Akobo counties.Renk and Maban is prioritized as level 2 and 3 with severity ranking of and 3, the logical rationale shows multifaceted protection concerns triggered by the current influx of returnees from Sudan, unprecedented flooding as well as suspected outbreak of measles and cholera. According to the HNO 2023, There are 117,138 children in need of case management services with integrated cash initiatives, 2343 separated children in need of family tracing and reunification as well as 146,423 children, adolescent and care givers in need of community based MHPSS. This has exerted pressure on the limited number of child protection actors working with limited resources to arrest and address the protection concerns.Akobo (including both Akobo East and Akobo West), Joint Inter-sectoral assessment report Sept 2023 estimated over 30,000 returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia, CPIMS+ Oct 2023 showed 1,102 (539 girls and 563 boys) in need of case management services and 415 (187 girls and 222 boys) for FTR services. Based on our observation’s domestic violence, boys and male youths are engaged as combatants in the conflicts with neighboring communities of Pibor and Ulang gave raise to other protection concerns like abduction/trafficking. These case scenarios make the community more vulnerable, yet service provision is very limited from handful of child protection actors.

Provision of Child Protection Case Management Services.• Identification of children at risk and with disabilities to provide individual specialized case management services and MHPSS including cash-based assistance. 1700 children (802 girls and 898 boys). • Identification and registration of unaccompanied and separated children 348 (178 girls and 170 boys) including provision of family tracing and reunification activities. • Rehabilitating child friendly spaces, youth centers, women and girl safe space to provide children with disabilities and at risk (4,630 children (2,410 girls and 2,220 boys)), adolescent 10,762 (5,360 girls and 5,402 boys) and caregivers 11,168 adults (5,548 females and 5,620 males) with focused and specialized MHPSS services.• Training of child protection partners staff 75(40 females and 35 males), local authorities 100 (62 females and 38 males), foster caregivers 50 (35 females and 15 males), community-based child protection networks 150 (72 females and 78 males) on key components of CPiE. • Development of all-inclusive age-appropriate IEC materials and awareness raising on key and relevant child protection issues with partners and communities. Targeted age categories I, e, 5-10, 11-14, 15-17 and 18+ with 216 sessions (1 session per month per Payam for 12months. Note.
