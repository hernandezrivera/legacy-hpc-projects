Project Summary

The ‘Education Support for Refugees Community’ project is designed to reach 3590 beneficiaries over the period of 12 months. IRC Bangladesh is currently implementing two EiE projects in camps (6, 8E and 22), where action research, design research and rapid need assessment was conducted to understand the existing context, where IRC also invested its global expertise on EiE. The project is developed based on IRC’s existing EiE working experience in Rohingya camps; community consultation; prototyping and different assessment from sector and ISCG. This project location will be camp 6, 8E and 22, with a total beneficiary of 3590 female 1776, male 1814)
The project objectives are aligned with education sector’s Objectives 1: Access; 2: Quality and 3: Community Engagement. The project will reach to its goal through community based learning facility and cross-sectoral shared learning facility. The project is developed base on ‘Community Consultation’ for tailoring the most effective assistance and will align with sector’s standard to ensure quality of education intervention with a clear focus on gender and age.
The key components of this project is early childhood development; informal education; life skills training; social and emotional learning. The project will work with learning facilitators; LCMC members and parents to develop their capacity, following sector standard. There will be activities with different stakeholders at community level following sector standard, visioning to crate ownership among community people.
The project will establish feedback mechanism (i.e client satisfaction survey, feedback box) to ensure IRC’s global approach of Accountability.
The project will work with adolescent girls integrating with IRC’s women protection and empowerment sector. The project will work for creating awareness among parents and community people to eliminate the traditional negative perception towards child with disability (CWD) and girl education. The project will also work collaboratively with IRC’s protection sectors to leverage the referral system intervention for the beneficiary.
Considering the availability of the space, the project will implement through cross-sectoral Shared learning facility, where the intervention will be in child protection and women protection’s existing facilities. The project will facilitate community based learning facility as well, mostly in home based.
The project will follow ISCG and education sector’s standard and guidelines during implementation, and will share all required data/information agreed with sector as sector’s partner. Additionally the project will also ensure compliance with government law and policy. At camp level, the project will work with prior consultation and guidance from CiC, site management and education focal person.
The project will identify the safe learning facilities for children considering the risk prone mapping developed by ISCG. IRC’s emergency preparedness team will orient learning facilitators, LCMC members, parents and children on DRR and emergency preparedness, so that they can take necessary actions during any emergency. The project is included in IRC’s emergency preparedness and response as well.

Needs

Only 76% of assessed facilities offered services for the 3-5 age group, compared to the 99% of assessed facilities providing services for children aged 6-14. (REACH/ENA 2019). The Multi-sector Need Assessment 2019 stated that, 69 % of the households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not attending any learning opportunities. Pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%) are the main factors preventing children and youth to fully access education opportunities (J-MSNA, 2019). Although 71% of the community (REACH/ENA 2019) consider that the education situation has improved in the camps over the last 12 months, children and parents are reluctant to pursue their education, especially as the Learning Competency and Framework Approach levels 3-4 are yet to be approved and around 30% of the children currently enrolled in level 2, will be enrolled at the level 3 in 2020.

Activities or outputs

The project mainly focus to align with Education sector’s activities, therefore the main activities under this project will be Objective 1: Access - • Provision of Early Childhood Education, Basic education, Life Skills training• Conduct education needs assessment• Equip learning facilities (measured as classrooms/learning spaces), including alternative learning modalities, for refugee settingsObjective 2: Quality – • In-service training for teachers and learning instructors• Conduct teacher competency assessment and develop teachers’ competencies• Training for teacher trainers • Train learning facilitators from the host and refugee community per the Teacher Professional Development Programme’s guidelines, endorsed by the Education Sector• Provide level-specific teaching material• Provide learning material (school kits), Recreation Kits• Provide girls and boys with textbooks per GIEP guidelines, education kits per Education Sector standards and according to appropriate age group• Develop and roll out learners’ assessment guideline and tools, such as ASER+• Conduct regular learning outcome assessmentObjective 3: Community Engagement • Parenting education, sensitisation protection concerns/systems (inclusion of siblings)• Management committee formation, considering academia, diversity and gender, • Parental awareness raising (for education / coexistence)• Awareness sessions on the importance of education/ Inclusive Education• Engaging parents and community stakeholders in ‘Participatory Monitoring’ to improve LC’s teaching-learning environment
