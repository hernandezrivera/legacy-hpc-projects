Project Summary

The security situation in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon remains unpredictable and fluid, meaning that IDPs are constantly moving to escape conflict and violence. According to the U.N, as from November 2019, close to 754 000 are internally displaced in the North West and South West regions. The crisis has come along with complex challenges in terms of access to good drinking water, consumables such as soap, detergents, buckets, jerry cans, male hygiene kits, female hygiene and dignity kits, etc., that are required for regular provision to IDPs because of their current location in bushes as well as their high densities in host communities. Worthy of note is also increased pressure on existing WASH services.
AMEF will provide IDPs & Host Communities affected by the crisis in Manyu and Kupe Manageuba Divisions within the SWR within a 12 Months’ period with basic WASH lifesaving support services, that will include increasing access to safe drinking water, facilitation of proper sanitation, public health promotion activities and WASH NFIs, with a specialized provision hygiene kits that include menstrual hygiene products, particularly re-usable sanitary pads. To get the message across to all beneficiaries, hygiene messaging and mass information sharing will be undertaken using cartoons/pictorial messaging and mother tongue translations in all intervention areas to promote lifesaving behavior change. The activities will be flexible to respond to different elements and needs related to the fluid context and basic needs would be prioritized and supplies tailored to suit the needs of 10 000 beneficiaries. A gender analysis will inform specific design of and identify sanitation needs of women, girls, men, boys, the disabled and persons with disabilities. All categories of persons will be consulted with regard to design, management and location of WASH facilities, as well as timing and location of awareness raising campaigns.
The proposed program has the overall objective to improve wellbeing (or living condition) and health status through provision of quality WASH services to IDPs and Host Communities.
The AMEF WASH team is comprised of all elements of WASH, with experts in WATER supply and quality, sanitation and public health promotion. New innovative methods, such as life saver cubes, will be utilized to enable water purification for mobile communities. AMEF will ensure a protection risk analysis and mitigating measures be put in place, through the provision of life saving WASH activities, AMEF will contribute to mitigate the risk of cholera outbreak but also ensure capacity to respond if an outbreak was to occur. According to a report released in November, 2019 Ndian Division is facing a cholera outbreak. Other Sub Divisions in the South West Region are at similar risk and is therefore requiring to mobilize efforts to mitigate potential cholera outbreak.
Protection interventions will be mainstreamed through the program with WASH interventions designed including training of gender inclusive WASH committees. All will be trained in construction, maintenance, repair and management of the WASH facilities. Men, boys and youth will be involved in order to mitigate conflict around WASH facilities to actively to reduce SGBV and to mitigate conflicts at Wash facilities ensuring ratios are respected and in coordination with other protection actors. All WASH facilities will be designed taking into account disposal of used menstrual hygiene products, accessible to children and the physically disabled, sex disaggregated, and clearly marked.
