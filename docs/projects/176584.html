Project Summary

Over the project period Jan - Dec 2021, this project aims to reach 48,949 beneficiaries (2% of the overall Health Cluster target) comprising women, men, boys, girls, the elderly, and those with disabilities in 2 Counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau and Jur River). Amref Health Africa intends to achieve 2% of the overall cluster target across the selected counties. This project will complement the current development projects in selected counties focusing on integrating essential health care services. Amref Health Africa will implement specific activities under the 3 Health Cluster Sectoral Objectives as outlined below;
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities
a. Conduct disease surveillance in the 2 Counties. Amref Health Africa will support weekly reporting through IDSR and EWARS for all the supported health facilities, including IDP Sites.
b. Establish one emergency team that will ensure preparedness and response to any outbreaks within the counties. Potential diseases of importance will include Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis, and Hepatitis E.
c. Conduct immunization services targeting vaccine-preventable diseases, both routine and campaigns to avert the effect of an epidemic-prone disease outbreak.
d. Integrate Covid-19 response in an emergency and routine health care delivery through the delivery of critical response activities by pillars (Cases Management, Risk Communication, and Community Engagement, Infection Prevention and Control (WASH/IPC), Continuous Training, Surveillance, RRT, and Case Investigation, as well as operational support and logistics. Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care and EVD
2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile, and community-based health service delivery systems
a. Support 15 health care facilities to provide integrated and quality static health care services, two hospitals (Wau teaching Hospital, and Meryhelp Hospital Stabilization Centre, CeMONC center), 5 PHCCs, and 8 PHCUs across the selected Counties.
b. Conduct facility-based and mobile clinic consultations and community-based consultations to diagnose and treat common diseases, including Malaria, RTIs, Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD), and non-communicable diseases, to reach an estimated 32,350 beneficiaries.
c. Provide BeMONC and CeMONC services at 5health facilities, 2 Hospitals, and 5 PHCCs.
d. Support the management of SAM with medical complications at Wau Teaching Hospital and Wau PoC Stabilization Center and in Bagari, estimated to reach 250 cases of SAM with medical complications.
e. Train health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. Amref Health Africa intends to train 40 health care workers in total, 25 clinicians and nurses in malaria, and 15 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries.
f. Conduct monthly and quarterly health facility WASH assessment, using the standard tool, and recommend WASH/IPC improvement; establish a network of Boma Health workers to provide community-based diagnosis and treatment of common childhood illness including Malaria, RTIs, and AWD and conducting health promotion activities and link them to health facilities,
g. Conduct quarterly supportive supervision to the supported health care facilities and at the communities where BHWs are providing life-saving services to ensure quality services are delivered across the project locations.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly
a. Establish and provide CMR and MHPSS services at 5 health facilities, 2 Hospitals and 5 PHCCs, and community-based awareness.
b. b. Training of Health Care Workers in CMR and MHPSS.
c. Identify/registration of persons with disability and provision of assertive devices along with other partners

1 - Needs

An estimated 56% of the South Sudan population live at least 5km away from the nearest health facility and have many difficulties accessing essential health care services (SSHHS 2008, NHSSP 2017-2022). Critical associated factors compounding access to health care include inter-communal and civil conflicts, flooding, and an underdeveloped healthcare system. Worsening the current health situation is the common sub-national conflicts, a second consecutive year of significant flooding, and the impact of the Covid-19 outbreak. The poor health indicators, such as a high mortality rate of under 5 children at 90.7/1,000 live births and a maternal mortality rate of 789/100,000 live births (South Sudan Mortality Statistics, 2016) continue to persist in some populations low access and use of services. More so, displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing life-saving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases and women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and childbirth. Over 2 years (2017 & 2018), immunization coverage for all vaccine-preventable diseases has persistently remained below 50% and has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks and or upsurge of epidemic-prone and other infectious diseases, including acute watery diarrhea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis, and hepatitis E compounded by flooding and food insecurity, presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality, especially to pregnant mothers, children under 5 years of age, people with disabilities and the elderly (60 years plus). Overstretching the health care system is the current ongoing Covid-19 pandemic with observed second waves far above 100 cases per day (MoH, Daily Updates, Feb 2021) and the looming threats of importation of viral hemorrhagic fever (Ebola Viral Disease) in the Country. The anticipated returnees would even pose a greater risk to Covid-19 infection with the ongoing revitalized peace process, an outbreak of other epidemic-prone diseases (Measles, Hepatitis E, AWD/Cholera), Malaria, and the importation of EVD in the Country. These latest developments on returning of IDPs and refugees to their homes of origin necessitate early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks while strengthening the current response to Covid-19 across all the response pillars.Cross-cutting issues like people with disabilities, mental disorders, HIV/AIDS, TB, and non-communicable diseases, including life-saving trauma care, are being cut-off from life-saving services. This has been related to contextual disruption of services, unpredictable health care funding in conflict-affected locations with increasing IDP burden, and a lack of integration of services across the different clusters. Whereas the Ministry of Health Launched Boma Health Initiative Strategies (BHIs) focuses on community-based health care systems to bridge the gaps in access to integrated health care services, the implementation is currently limited to the counties with good access networks for service delivery. An expanded approach to reach out to the most vulnerable populations (men, women, boys, girls, elderly and disabled) through the community health care system is critical to improving access to care under this response plan. Wau and Jur River health indicators fall below the national level. Amref Health Africa intends to fully integrate health care activities with nutrition, IPC/WASH, Protection and Food Security, and Livelihood while ensuring both static and mobile clinic services alongside community-based interventions to increase access and use life-saving care services in the target population. Further, protection interventions will focus mainly on the clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV and the provision of mental health care services using the MHPSS services protocols.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Providing information about PSEA to the beneficiaries is critical to enhancing community reporting of any SEA form committed with the beneficiaries. As a bottom line, Amref Health Africa has PSEA policies and practices that aim to prevent sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by its employees, and it provides that any allegations of SEA are responded to in a timely and appropriate manner. All new staff in emergency context are oriented on this policy before deployment to deliver health care services to the beneficiaries. It is the principle of operations that Amref Health Africa ensures beneficiaries are oriented on their responsibilities of reporting SEA through a confidential protection desk that is always made available and linked to CMR services and legal support. This is always done during various community engagement processes in the course of the project implementation. Amref Health Africa will also ensure beneficiaries education on SEA and reporting channels throughout the project period. The community-based feedback mechanisms to prevent and respond to PSEA will be established. New staff at all service delivery points (static facility, mobile clinics, and community-based health care workers) will be trained in how to handle such cases with the level of confidentiality they deserve.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Amref Health Africa provides an environment where beneficiaries have the confidence and support to provide feedback on health care projects being delivered to them irrespective of their age, sex, education or physical abilities. The most vulnerable are particularly allowed to express and share their views is the simplest way or in local language specific to the population group. This can be in the form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions and community meetings. The information collected will be analyzed and provided responses accordingly at different levels, and will also guide for the inclusion of missing components or change of course whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

AMREF HEALTH AFRICA provides an environment where beneficiaries have the confidence to provide feedback on health care services and interventions they are involved including the quality of services delivered to them and received. The organization supports the community with the best mechanism for them to do so, which can be in the form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions, and community meetings. The information collected is analyzed and any issues raised are responded to accordingly at different levels, and guidance is given to those concerned for the inclusion of missing components or change of course whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Amref Health Africa recognizes the value of community and stakeholder engagement as an essential part of project planning, implementation, monitoring, and collaborative decision-making in project processes. Under this service delivery area, Amref Health Africa will employ three (3) approaches to engage communities in project implementation; (i) Conduct Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, (ii) conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community-level health care services; and (iii) conducting defaulter tracing, follow up and referrals. (i) Conduct Monthly Boma, and Payam Health Meeting Amref Health Africa proposes to extend support to BHTs/Payam health administration for conducting monthly review meetings and activity planning to ensure effective implementation of the project. This forum will also provide feedback to communities as a way of transparency and accountability to beneficiaries. (ii) Advocacy/Public Campaigns for Utilization of Community-Level Health Care Services. Amref Health Africa will work with the SMOH, CHD, Payam health officers, BHWs, and BHTs to conduct various meetings/campaigns at County, Payam, Boma, and community levels. The meetings will target stakeholders and beneficiaries to understand this project's scope, including BHI components and strategies, and make sure the communities understand that BHI is part and package of the health care delivery system adopted to increase universal access to services utilization. Amref Health Africa will also use mass media platforms such as local FM radios and posters to advocate for community levels' availability of care services. This is expected to increase understanding and utilization of the services by all the vulnerable populations in the project areas. (iii) Amref Health Africa will support the BHWs to conduct regular Default Tracing, Follow up, and Referrals for EPI, TB, and HIV services, including for children on treatment for MAM/SAM. Also, the BHWs will be supported to follow up of ANC/lactating mothers, conduct early and prompt referrals of patients in critical condition and children under ve years with danger signs using bicycles, motorcycles, or using stretchers from the Community to health facilities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project aims to benet the host community, IDPs, returnees, and other affected populations. The project intends to ensure that the services provided do not pose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted by carrying out joint activities and integrating some of the services within the population targeted. The project intends to engage the local authorities in the proposed project locations for their total engagement and support during the project implementation to minimize protection concerns that may arise, such as Land disputes over project sites. Implementing these projects will also ensure equal access to men, women, boys, and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for providing CMR services to survivors of SGBV, Psychosocial Support with a high level of privacy and confidentiality. Various approaches such as Conducting Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community-level health care services and conducting defaulter tracing, follow-up, and referrals will be adopted to enhance community participation would contribute to risk reduction. A risk tracking system will be embedded into monthly reporting, data received analyzed, and corrective actions are taken immediately a high risk is identified. Also, prompt incidence reporting will be adopted and used in mitigating potential risk incidences.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

We designed the project to significantly address access constraints to basic services, including protection concerns of the most vulnerable section of the population, especially women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities. Although the political environment remains fragile, the revitalized peace agreement's implementation is beginning to take shape and there is currently relative calmness. This is anticipated to improve access to remote areas further, reduce risk of new displacement, and a subsequent stable system that will protect humanitarian workers and beneficiaries. This project's implementation will ensure equal access to men, women, boys, and girls to basic and essential services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

At the design stage, the project paid crucial attention to integrating health and nutrition services at all the proposed project sites in line with the BPHNS. This approach will enhance the early detection of children under 5 with Malnutrition and earlier initiation of treatment with reasonable cure rates. At the facility levels, the Stabilization Centers in Wau Teaching Hospital and Wau PoC will be maintained, and treatment of children with MAM/SAM with medical complications will be ensured. Coordination of activities with other partners implementing OTPs/TSFP services is critical to ensure timely and appropriate referrals of children with Malnutrition. At the community level, the Boma Health Workers (BHWs)/Community Nutrition Volunteers will be trained and deployed to continually conduct health and nutrition promotion activities including but not limited to household hygiene, personal hygiene, breastfeeding, how to introduce other feeds for children six months and above, use of locally available food materials to enhance diet, cooking demonstration among others. The BHWs/CNVs will conduct house-to-house screening of children using MUAC TAPES to identify those at risk and ensure earlier and appropriate referrals. Malnutrition drivers, including common childhood diseases such as malaria, RTIs(Pneumonia) and Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD), will be diagnosed and treated with ACTs, Amoxicillin, ORS, and Zinc, respectively. Strengthening the community health care system through this strategy will ensure that access to health and nutrition services is bridged, especially among the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach locations.
