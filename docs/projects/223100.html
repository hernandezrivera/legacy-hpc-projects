Project Summary


The influx of displaced persons from Sudan, including Chadian returnees, has exacerbated the already fragile health sector in eastern Chad. s. Returnees, many of whom are children or pregnant or breastfeeding mothers, are frequently located in remote and underserved areas, where healthcare infrastructure is minimal or non-existent. In response, IOM aims to implement targeted emergency health interventions to address the urgent needs of these vulnerable groups.
Through the deployment of mobile health clinics, IOM will provide essential healthcare services directly to the most at-risk returnees, ensuring access to critical medical care in hard-to-reach locations. The mobile clinics will offer a range of services, including maternal and child health care, vaccination programs, treatment for infectious diseases, and nutritional support. Special attention will be given to addressing the unique needs of pregnant women, breastfeeding mothers, and young children, who are especially vulnerable to the health impacts of displacement. Additionally, IOM will work closely with local health authorities and partners to strengthen the capacity of existing health systems, providing training, medical supplies, and technical assistance to improve the quality and accessibility of services for displaced populations.
By implementing these emergency health interventions, IOM aims to mitigate the health risks faced by displaced populations, reduce morbidity and mortality rates, and contribute to the overall well-being of returnees and host communities in eastern Chad. These efforts will not only provide immediate relief but also help strengthen the resilience of the local health system, ensuring it is better equipped to respond to future health crises.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

I. Strengthening PoE capacities to detect infectious diseases using quick check/diagnostics techniques and logistics1. Support active surveillance, including health screening, referral and collection of health and mobility data, and complementing supplies at POEs2. Support the development and dissemination of POE-specific standard operating procedures (SOPs) for detection, notification, isolation, management and referral, including development of training curricula and manuals. 3. Train immigration and border/port health staff on SOPs to manage travellers exhibiting symptoms of communicable diseases and on infection prevention and control, including simulation exercises to identify and remedy gaps and strengthen preparedness. II. Empowering government officials, health workers, immigration and border officers, security officers and other local authorities on mobility and health 1. support technical capacity-building for front-line border authorities and health officials III. Support technical and operational HR at national MoH and local health authority levels, at PoEs, and support cross-border coordination: 1. Conduct training, simulation exercises and on-job mentorship of health workers and immigration and border officials to enhance their index of suspicion and response capacity IV. improve readiness and response capacities for public health emergencies1. Strengthen Community Event-based surveillance by linking mobility information to surveillance data, particularly among border communities and migrant dense areas. 2. Conduct Participatory Population Mobility Mapping (PPM) Exercises, especially in areas of high incidence or outbreak, to identify high-risk transmission mobility corridors and areas to inform regional and national preparedness and response plans.3. Conduct simulation training exercises for local health officials on surveillance, preparedness and response to the outbreak of emerging and re-emerging communicable diseases 4. reinforce and improve waste management equipment and capacity that allow for strengthened preparation and response to potential future public health crises. V. strengthen through sustainable energy infrastructure and energy efficient upgrades to provide better health care services and enhance the resilience and well-being of vulnerable communities in humanitarian settings1. train key local stakeholders on operation and maintenance of renewable energy systems and future planning of similar interventions2. electrify off-grid health centres, replace diesel generators with solar photovoltaic (PV) and storage systems, procure energy-efficient equipment, and build the local capacity to operate and maintain these systems
