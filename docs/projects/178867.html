Project Summary

Through the proposed project Oxfam plans to enable the most vulnerable families in the targeted subdistricts to have access to the essential non-food items. Depending on local market functionality, people preferences, and availability of appropriate FSPs, distribution modality will be through cash, noting that Oxfam had performed market assessment in several communities in the proposed subdistrict and found that cash modality is preferable and feasible. The number of cash rounds will be given according to the CWG standards. According to the to February 2021 MEB calculation the value of monthly cash transfer will be 46USD per family, this value will be subject to change based on the periodical calculations made by CWG. The cash transfers will be given for up-to 8 months. In specific cases. Oxfam may merge the transfers; and therefore target less number of months with increased value. Oxfam will coordinate this with the CWG. Oxfam will target 6,800 HHs, at least 30% of which will be headed by females. This will benefit a total of 38,000 individuals in need. The monthly distribution value will be given in Syrian Pound using the Central Bank preferential rate at the time of distribution in Deir ez Zor (Mayadeen, Ashara), Aleppo (Jabal Samaan) and Rural Damascus (East Ghouta). Oxfam will coordinate with other humanitarian actors to avoid overlapping in and ensure complementarity in geographical targeting, their coordination will be done through NFI Sector, CWG and even through bilateral meetings. in Oxfam will make sure to engage the communities in the selection of the most vulnerable families. Therefore, Oxfam will form a community committee where at least 50% of participants will be women. The committees will be responsible in identifying the most vulnerable families. If the security situation and Covid 19 measures allow, Oxfam will perform 100% physical verification, if not, Oxfam will rely on random representative sampling approach. The aid delivery process, impact and market prices will be monitored regularly.

4 - Needs

According to HNO21,Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving,especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic items to keep them warm.Despite the improved stability, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 9% in 2021.Oxfam found that the crisis-affected communities are still in need for NFI support especially for IDPs& those who are spontaneously retuning to their home. Oxfam performed market assessment in DZ& Aleppo in Aug 2020,& currently Oxfam is performing an Markets& Livelihood Assessment in RD. Assessments findings show that cash-based assistance would be feasible for cash grants in the targeted subdistricts. Cash modality is aligned with the targeted household preference and majority households already reported using markets as their main source for purchasing food and NFIs. In addition, findings suggest that the market would be able to respond to an increased demand,traders have a good capacity to absorb the increase of demand by 100% &restock can be achieved in a short period of time.Besides,the markets in the targeted subdistrict has maintained good supply of commodities&items from main source &central markets &has enough stock available to fulfil the needs of beneficiaries.The main challenge is with the reduction in demand in these markets is mostly due to the reduced economical access&reduced purchasing power of the farmers.Cash injections will improve the purchasing power&subsequently positive impacts on the markets with the demand issues&may also enable these markets to become more resilient to challenges arising from COVID pandemic&overall country economic situation.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community mobilization, sensitization and committee’s formation. The project will be implemented in a manner that deepen connecting factors and minimizing the dividing factors in the targeted communities.Identification, registration and verification of most vulnerable HHs, IDPs and Host Communities, as per the defined selection criteria.Three tranches of unconditional Cash transfers will be provided to 6800 HHs, with at least 30% female headed households, through licensed and vetted FSPs.Monthly Price Monitoring and analysis to measure the impact of cash injection on the prices of essential commodities and services in the local markets.Process monitoring to measure the beneficiaries’ satisfaction with the cash delivery process, Oxfam MEAL team will perform daily DDMs during the whole distribution period. Also, Oxfam will measure the impact of the projects through PDMs where MEAL team will calculate the essential indictors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. SEA by humanitarian workers and potential other beneficiaries: strict feedback mechanism, and all staff will be thoroughly inducted on protection and safeguarding issues and redlines.2. Risk of violence (risks of potential GBV such as harassment in the place of distributions) and Covid 19 as a result of overcrowding: Ensure distribution is well-organized with strict crowed control and Covid 19 mitigation measures.3. Interference with beneficiary targeting, and diversion of aid: communities will be engaged in developing the targeting criteria, training of field team on targeting/vulnerability criteria as well as community mobilization, set community feedback mechanism, very strict verification mechanism in place. Oxfam will clearly inform all the relevant stakeholders about their targeting redlines and the consequences of breaching them.4. Beneficiaries data sharing/ protection: pushing back all the requests for data sharing and vetting of beneficiaries, if critical stakeholders will insist on their positions, Oxfam will suspend the activities and change the modality to voucher or in-kind.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: –Risk of compromised impartiality and independence-Occurrence of gender-based violence- The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds,or not enough funds to fulfil commitments &project objectives. - Potential of fraud &aid diversion,amongst Oxfam staff, FSP& partners -C-19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities&of humanitarian workers delivering assistance.-Movements restrictions as C-19 preventative measure by governmental authorities hamper the delivery of humanitarian assistance.Assumptions:-No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. -Security situation in target areas allow movement ability of Oxfam staff&volunteers to operate. -Oxfam is supported by relevant authorities to continue with the cash distribution programme. -C-19 related risks can be sufficiently managed.Mitigations: Protection risk mitigation:Oxfam will ensure the participation of local communities in the designing of all project activities through regular consultation with community committees on project activities& beneficiary selection&adapting its intervention considering the feedback received when needed. Help desks are always set up in all distribution points to gather beneficiaries’ complaints& feedback &provide instant responses.Also, Oxfam conduct PDM to ensure that beneficiaries’ feedback is well collected and addressed.C-19: Oxfam has C-19 SOPs& security management plan for office& field operations and it is mandatory to adhere to. Oxfam conducts continuous assessment of the situation to ensure risks are minimized.
