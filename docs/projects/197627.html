Project Summary

The intervention aims to respond to the significant need of implementing public infrastructure rehabilitation, road rehabilitation, and the creation of short-term job opportunities in NWS,
based on the previous experience in implementing similar activities, Global Communities will provide critically needed upgrades/rehabilitation/construction to the neglected and crisis-affected infrastructure as follows:
- Rehabilitation of 6km of the main roads including a new asphalt layer with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure and lighting required.
- Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the newly rehabilitated roads by hiring 10 teams of 6 members each to implement the planned works
- Establishing and training 16 rescue teams (6 members each) during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks
- Rehabilitating 5 bakeries in NWS.

3 - Needs

Displacements from areas still facing hostilities to relatively more stable areas are likely to continue placing a further strain on already fragile essential services and limited, employment opportunities, increasing the risk of intra- and inter-community tensions involving residents and displaced people. Likewise, returns will require the rehabilitation of services and infrastructure, resumption of economic activity, and, in parallel, initiatives to foster social cohesion among the returnees and host communities. “HNO 2023”In 2023, the ERL sector estimates 15.3 million people in need of early recovery and livelihood support. 50 percent are estimated to be women, 40 percent children, and 17 percent persons living with disabilities (PLWD). the reported PIN figures refer to 82% of the overall population having a gap between their income and MEB, a third of them are at the catastrophic severity scale; only 14.4% of the population have the ability to meet the basic needs, while 42.2% reported insufficient because of the inflation, 26.7% reported insufficient because of the income gap, and 8.1% as completely unable. That leads to adopting negative coping mechanisms like child labor, asset and savings consumption, debt, and relying on humanitarian aid.Across all population groups, the two main reasons limiting the ability to meet basic needs are the price increases of food items and other essential goods, followed by insufficient income, unemployment, and loss of jobs.

4 - Implementation of activities

Global Communities will implement:- Rehabilitation of 6km of the main roads including a new asphalt layer with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure and lighting required.- Implementing sidewalks/Road rehabilitation through 60 cash for work workers- Establishing and training 96 rescue team members during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks- Rehabilitating 5 bakeries in NWS. The activities will include; 1. Community/facilities selection and outreach: collecting detailed information about the specifics of intended infrastructures that require rehabilitation, through FGDs, KIIs, and Observations with all the stakeholders. 2. Topographic surveys for the selected locations, developing the BoQsand the technical specification. 3. Procurement process: through a transparent procurement process, advertising and receiving offers from the bidders, opening bids, TEC, and signing the contract with the selected contractor. 4. Implementation of the proposed rehabilitation and construction, and supervising the implementation of the rehabilitation and construction works. Along with implementing the activities, we will have ongoing monitoring for the implementation.Meanwhile, the selection of CFW workers will be through the beneficiaries selection approach and modality while the process will include registration and verification in light of the preselected selection criteria, considering the most vulnerable cases, their dues will be transferred in cash.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

There will be a probability for the following risks;Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members as well as the probability of Tension between host communities, IDPs, and returnees. This risk can be mitigated through 1. Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content. 2. Use recommended minimum standards in quality and specification of items. 3. identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. 3. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 4. The selection of the facilities is transparent, and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 5. Establish a feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be. 6. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 7. Make effort to target a % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs.
