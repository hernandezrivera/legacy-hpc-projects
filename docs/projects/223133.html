Project Summary

GFA: WFP will provide life-saving food assistance to the entire Rohingya refugee population through General Food Assistance. Beneficiaries will receive entitlements that can be redeemed from WFP-contracted local retailers across the camps. E-vouchers provide more flexibility, dignity and greater dietary diversity for the refugees while also creating market linkages with the host community and the wider Bangladeshi economy. To ensure an effective and efficient emergency intervention, WFP will preposition enough in-kind food to rapidly respond to the needs of approximately 30,000 households should they be displaced by monsoon, cyclone or conflict. In addition to providing large-scale food assistance, WFP makes sure to address the needs of the most vulnerable households through promoting income generating activities in the camps and host communities. WFP will provide complementary food vouchers to 390,000 extremely vulnerable individuals through Fresh Food Corners.
Resilience: WFP will implement natural resource managment acttivities aimed at enhancing natural resource management and disaster risk reduction, with a focus on community-based environmental protection. This will include initiatives such as roadside tree plantation on going care and maintenance of these trees, and watershed management practices including channel re-excavation and improvements to drainage systems. Similarly, under the green skill development, WFP will facilitate food production within refugee camps to bolster supplementary food security and improve household nutrition. This efforts includes the promotion of ecofriendly production materials through upcycling activities, particularly in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas. Furthermore, WFP will engage Youth in constructive activities that foster self esteem and resilience, thereby steering Rohingya youth away from anti-social behaviors and contributing to overall community resilience. Under the capacity strengthening and building resilience component the resilience and capacity of local communities will be enhanced by promoting food security through seasonal livelihoods and climate adaptative agricultural methods. This approach aims to improve agricultural production and post-harvest management. Lastly through the market linkage promotion initiative local supply chains and markets will be strengthened by establishing aggregation centers that enhance farm productivity and connect producers directly with markets.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Seven years after the initial influx, Rohingya refugees and their surrounding host communities face heightened vulnerability, marked by limited livelihood opportunities and increased reliance on external assistance. The 2024 REVA assessment reveals a troubling trend: for the first time since 2017, 10 percent of Rohingya households are categorized as having poor diets, with 70 percent classified as being in poor to borderline conditions. Alarmingly 24 percent of these households are unable to afford the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for food, even with existing levels of humanitarian support. A qualitative assessment corroborated significant disatisfaction among Rohingya households regarding the recent ration cuts. Furthermore, simulations indicate that should assistance be withdrawn entirely, a staggering 83 percent of Rohingya households would fall below the food MEB line, underscoring their critical dependence on humanitarian aid for survival. In Teknaf and Ukhiya, there are 209,094 Rohingy households, representing a population of 1,010,298. In 2021 and 2023, WFP supported 60,000 Rohingya households, with plans to assist an additional 30,000 through home gardening inititiatives in 2025. Promoting self reliance and enhancing livelihood opportunites for Rohingya refugees and vulnerable host communities is essential to mitigate the economic impacts of ration cuts and climate change, thereby improving food security for the Rohingya population and the Bangladeshi local communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WFP incorporates protection principles in designing and developing programme interventions in Cox’s Bazar to ensure that all activities target the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. Through robust contextual analysis WFP targeting crisis affected people including women, men, boys, girls, elderly people, person with disability as well as people with special need. WFP carried out various assessments and analysis, i.e., stakeholder analysis, gender analysis, vulnerability, protection and assessment of social and economic factor that create inequalities among Rohingya community and host communities. Selection for interventions and beneficiaries is informed by vulnerability monitoring and classifications, which are found in, REVA and FSOM, providing frequent updates on existing vulnerabilities and groups. These updates and assessments on vulnerable groups help programmes adjust their target groups while maintaining documentation on the impact of programmes on vulnerable populations. WFP has encountered protection referral and provide support through inter agency protection cluster and UNHCR to provide faster service to the affected population. WFP has followed PSEA guideline and inter agency referral pathway to report sexual exploitation and abuse, GBV and other forms of violence. Onsite protection monitoring, protection referral and follow-ups further complement protection assessments. However, continuous efforts are made to ensure that protection risk and concern is not overlooked throughout the programme implementation phases. Sectoral recommendations regarding protection are additionally adopted by WFP to enable a safe, secure, and dignified WFP service irrespective of sex, age, and diversity considerations.

2.3 - Age & Disability

WFP works to streamline age, gender, disability inclusion, elderly and other diversified people (including LGBTQI if found) to support the participation of all beneficiaries across its programmatic interventions, and to ensure their rights and safety from the diversity perspective. Trainings and capacity building exercises are conducted with WFP staff and cooperating partners on basic concepts, frameworks, and programmatic applications of diversity approaches. A number of information and communication materials are in use to ensure adequate awareness raising of the frontline workers, key staffs and ensure meaningful engagement of the diversified beneficiaries including LGBTQI. Every distribution site has segregated waiting areas, toilet facilities, and help desks managed by at least one female staff member/volunteer. At nutrition sites, priority is given to pregnant and nursing women with disabilities, and caregivers of children with disabilities. Person with disabilities will be identified by using the WGSSQ tools. There were presence of porter services for the child, women, elderly and persons with disabilities headed household considering the diversified vulnerabilities. WFP provides additional top-up cash support to child and elderly headed households to meet their basic food and nutrition. Assistive and flexible nutrition services are provided to both parents with disabilities and children with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP re-committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations (AAP) through adoption of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (C-AAP). WFP exercise active processes and actions that meaningfully place affected populations at the center of humanitarian response with the aim of providing assistance that meets people’s needs, elevates existing capacities and dignity, and helps strengthen long-term resilience and independence from humanitarian assistance. WFP’s priorities and approach to AAP is outlined in the Protection and Accountability Policy (2020), elaborated in the Community Engagement for Accountability Strategy (2021 – 2026) and mutually reinforces the Gender Policy (2022) and the Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020). WFP has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations. Protection assessments and the CFRM process protection issues in the community by collecting community feedback and responses in an organized manner. WFP will do capacity development training for partner to enhace their understanding on protection and AAP.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Green skills development is a key component of WFP’s resilience programme, focusing on circular economy principles, supporting climate-resilient local food production, and promoting waste reduction and environmental improvements in the camps. Rohingya refugee camps face high vulnerability to natural disasters due to deforestation, silt accumulation, and pollution. To address these risks, WFP is improving resilience by restoring natural resources, including slope stabilization, infrastructure upgrades, Cash for Work (CFW) initiatives, and community capacity building. By engaging in activities that support youth volunteers, the youth group will enhance their skills, expand their knowledge, and improve their overall quality of life.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

"YesWFP Resilience Programme aligns with the Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan, which underscores the need for holistic and comprehensive development targeting Rohingya refugees and host communities. The plan highlights the district's challenges of both humanitarian and development needs and priorities: (i) strengthening economic resilience, (ii) improving human capital and productivity, and (iii) promoting social cohesion. All initiatives are designed to be gender-inclusive, addressing the needs of children, youth, the elderly, and concerns related to gender-based violence, consistent with the proposal. Additionally, WFP conducts Community-Based Participatory Planning to ensure the programme accurately reflects community needs."
