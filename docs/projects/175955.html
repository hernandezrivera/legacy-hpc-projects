Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees living in the hard to reach areas of South Sudan. It will target newly displaced people, returnees and host communities affected by conflict, natural disaster and disease outbreak. The project will focus on providing emergency WASH services as per the cluster guidelines to the displaced persons including most vulnerable host communities taking into consideration do no harm, protection mainstreaming and Covid-19 guidelines. Focus will be on the repair and rehabilitation of water points, training hand pump mechanics, provision of hygiene and dignity kits and water treatment kits as well as hygiene promotion and messaging to raise awareness among the affected population, reduce the incidence of water borne diseases and to limit spreading of Covid-19. The affected population will also be supported in the construction of emergency latrines specifically targeting the most vulnerable and newly displaced people. The project will be delivered through a rapid response mechanism in project locations selected in line with the cluster prioritizing process.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with South Sudan humanitarian response plan (HRP) for 2021. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 14 days for full-fledged delivery of WASH services and other activities per location.
The WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with the Emergency
Rapid Response Mechanism (ERRM) WASH actors where implementation is based on the multi-sector assessments and team involving WASH, Shelter, Food security and emergency Education with protection as a cross cutting theme. In some of the locations, the WASH projects link up with other emergency projects to ensure the beneficiaries receive a comprehensive response package. The project will be implemented in coordination with other players in the sector especially UNICEF for information sharing and use of the cluster core pipeline supplies when needed as well as other WASH cluster members for joint assessments and response when appropriate. The project will also be coordinated with the WASH and Logistics clusters especially in prioritization of locations and delivery of supplies.
This Project is designed to mitigate GBV throughout the maintained and constructed facilities and through all the project activities.

1 - Needs

Access to safe drinking water: Shallow/hand-dug wells, or surface water (rivers or streams) are the main sources of water in South Sudan. Access to safe drinking water continues to be challenging, and water-borne diseases have spread due to lack of clean water and poor sanitation. According the HNO 2021, 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. There are 5.9 M people in need of WASH services, in which 50% of them are females, 53% are children and 15% are people with disability. As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, the limited access to WASH services impacts all population groups. Those in need are not restricted to one specific group. IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. As the movement and flow of returnees to South Sudan continue with people returning to their areas of origin and urban areas, demand on an already limited water and sanitation infrastructure is expected to increase in 2021.Due to distance to the few existing limited infrastructure, access to safe water during dry season drops drastically. With increased population in some areas due to displacement and reduced amount of water during dry season, it is expected that water induced conflicts will arise at the water collection points. In South Sudan lack of access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation pose protection risks for women, with the search to find a safe place to defecate and trek to collect water, women are exposed to the threat of violence and harassment. With reference to the WASH Severity Map 2021, only 50% of South Sudan households have access to boreholes/tap stands and at least 3 liters of water per person per day. Many counties across South Sudan have insufficient access to safe clean water, I.e. Canal/Pigi, 100% of HH don’t have access to sufficient safe clean water, 99.1% Panyikang, 98.2% in Ulang, etc. Talking about the quantity of water, only 61% of the HH reported having access to at least 3 liters of water per person per day, I.e. In Ulang only 12.8% of HH have access to sufficient amount of water, 38% in Mayom, 44.4% in Maban, etc. In addition to this, 16% of the HH reported protection concerns while accessing their preferred source of drinking water across South Sudan, I.e. In Ulang 55.1% of HH have protection concerns accessing their source of water, 46.3% in Ezo, etc. Moreover, due to lack of awareness regarding safe hygiene practices, a higher percentage of the affected population often have very low ability to effectively utilize improved water and sanitation facilities to prevent diseases. There is a dire need to raise awareness through promotional activities and training to enable effectiveness of water and sanitation improvements.For Sanitation, 77% of HH across South Sudan reported that they have no access to any form of latrines either Household or communal ones, I.e. In Leer, Yirol East no one has access to latrines, in Aweil South, Canal, Gogrial East, Tonj North only 0.93% of people have access to latrines, 1.92% in Kapoeta East, etc. In addition to this, only 17% of the HH reported owning both soap and an unbroken jerrycan/bucket with a lid across South Sudan, 70% of HH reported a child or adult with a self-diagnosed water/vector sickness in the previous two weeks and 14% of the HH reported access to sufficient water for handwashing and soap. Those figures, shows the huge needs for safe water, WASH NFIs, latrines and safe access to facilities across South Sudan.COVID-19 still considered as a global pandemic. Although that South Sudan has recently faced a high number of positive Covid-19 cases, there are many displaced people who have no WASH basic services. Therefore, mitigation measure should be integrated in all WASH activities across the country

2 - Project activities

CO3: Access to basic services in emergencies: (1) Water supply: Temporary or semi-permanent solutions for water supply: bucket chlorination and emergency rehabilitation of existing water points. Provide timely equitable access to safe and sufficient water for vulnerable and affected population. (Target 115,000 individuals). (2) Hygiene promotion at community level: Hygiene promotion at community level emphasizing on disease prevention (WASH related diseases and Covid-19), Safe excreta management, handwashing with soap and WASH NFIs usage demonstration. (Target 115,000 Individuals). (3) Sanitation: Temporary communal latrines and Households shared latrines with GBV mitigation measures implemented if justify by public health concerns to enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living. (Target 20,000 individuals). (4) WASH NFIs distribution: NRC will distribute NFIs based on the WASH cluster minimum package matrix. NFI packages will include water treatment products (depending on NTU) and for maximum of 3 months, buckets with lid with tap for storage, bucket with lid without tap or Jerrycan for transport, soap for 3 months and filter cloth. Second round of soap and HHWTS products distribution upon needs. Other items distribution will depend on the need and availability such as MHM kits. (Target 115,000 individuals). Targeting will be done based on the assessment report outcomes which might varies from one location to another. CO4: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation will be based on Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children for WASH activities adequately planned with the community to reduce the risk of violence when vulnerable groups, especially women and children access or try to access these services. Activities (1) Conduct site-level safety audit: NRC protection team will do protection and risk assessment for locations of intervention and will identify GBV and other protection concerns and will implement mitigation measures accordingly. All WASH activities will be conducted in consultation with the protection team to avoid any harm and to make the WASH activities and facilities safer. (2) Implement WASH protection mainstreaming: Based on the protection and risk assessment done by NRC for locations of intervention, mitigation measures will be implemented. Both WASH and Protection teams will engage the community including Women and children in infrastructure siting, collect community feedback, build robust and safe toilets, etc. MHM kits will distributed for women in reproductive age based on the protection and WASH assessments.

3 - Project indicators

NRC will measure the success of the project using different indicators, including Number of people supported with WASH NFIs, Number of people with safe access to clean water, Number of people with safe access to emergency latrines, etc. NRC will also support the WASH cluster in measuring the cluster related indicators. In addition, NRC will measure the HRP indicators such as (Indi No.3.1: Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent water, Indi No.3.2 Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent sanitation, Indi No.3.3 Number of beneficiaries reached with hygiene promotion, Indi No.4.1: Number of beneficiaries reached with the package of assistance ( conduct safety audit at site level). Those indicators will be captured and reported through regular reporting during WASH cluster meeting, 5W monthly reports and through sharing all the intervention and post distribution monitoring reports with the Pipeline and WASH cluster as well. NRC will capture all the mentioned above indicators using intervention reports, pipeline requests and Post distribution monitoring reports (PDMs).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All WASH intervention phases will be carried out by a WASH team that consists of one Male Protection Officer, one Female WASH officer and one Protection officer. NRC Protection officer will conduct PSEA training for different groups of the community including Chiefs, Local authorities, IDPs, Returnees and HC. NRC team will also ensure to engage both males and females from different age groups and people with disabilities as well. PSEA training will also be done during the AAP training since AAP training covers different topics in which PSEA is one of them.NRC protection office will also do community awareness raising in all locations of interventions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Every WASH team is composed of 1 male WASH officer, 1 Female WASH Officer and 1 Protection officer during every WASH assessment. They will visit the communities and will identify the most vulnerable community members in assessment, registration and distribution process. Feedback and Complaint desk will be sat up. Community complains will be recorded and responded during all the phases. Efforts will be made that no vulnerable community member is left out. Community committee will also be formed and trained on accountability to affected population (AAP). Community will be made aware that what support is to be provided by the project. Roles and responsibilities will be clearly explained. Community will be trained on AAP.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability lies at the core of this project. Prior to activity implementation, the implementing team will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders are involved in all stages of project development and informed of the feedback mechanisms available to them. Beneficiaries will be able to make confidential suggestions for improving the project, lodge complaints where they disagree with implementation. NRC will ensure that the project will systematically engage with all beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, including the most marginalised, to deliver information about assistance to be provided in such a way as to maximise meaningful participation and consultation. Information shared will include the scope of the proposed interventions, entitlements of those involved, clarify any targeting of vulnerable groups, and information essential to accessing companion services, as well as other relevant information. NRC will consult with communities about the optimal way to share information, and provide information in forms accessible to those with limited literacy and in local languages, to the extent that it is possible. Taking precautions to make the project as transparent and inclusive as possible will both service to make the program more inclusive and contribute to a sense of local ownership, as well as mitigate possible negative consequences of targeting or prioritising vulnerable persons and minority groups. NRC has utilised a variety of approaches to provide beneficiaries with information, including holding community awareness raising sessions, both general and targeted at specific groups according to gender, age, and other considerations. IEC materials used and messages shared will be tailored to meet the needs of these unique groups. NRC has also training community mobilisers to go door-to-door in targeted communities and share information, as well as sharing information over the radio. These and other approaches would be used in the current project, following consultation with communities about the best way to reach affected groups, and depending on the duration of the intervention (i.e. static versus mobile).Messages are both context-specific and tailored for gender and age-groups. In particular, NRC revisits all protection messages after each protection needs assessment, to ensure that messages are addressing identified needs and targeted to the most vulnerable groups based on the contextual needs. Messages are particularly tailored to reach children, people with disabilities, and older persons. NRC strives to include local context in its messages, such as alerting beneficiaries to existing referral pathways for complementary services. NRC also considers messages from a Do No Harm perspective, moderating messages as appropriate.The project will offer several complaints and feedback mechanisms, depending on the needs of the community. In initial assessments, NRC will consult with targeted persons about the best approach to establish CFMs and strive to be responsible and flexible in our implementation. NRC also intends to establish the digital community hub to strengthen information sharing, feedback and response mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback will be used for adaptive programming, learning, improvement and decision making. NRC will engage the community in decision making during all the project phases. During assessments, NRC team will conduct FGDs, KIIs and HH interviews in order to find out the beneficiaries needs and also to consult them on what type of assistant they need and they are looking for. In addition to that NRC team will also consult the beneficiaries on what is the best way to conduct distribution, which boreholes to be repaired, latrines needs, etc. and all those information will shape the intervention modality after verifying the need and ensuring do no harm principle. Distribution locations and methodology will be also decided on based on the feedback of the community during the FGDs, KIIs and HH interviews and based on the protection and security team recommendations as well.
