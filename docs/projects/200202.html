Project Summary

In line with the 2023 ERP strategic objectives focusing on: 1) the provision of essential humanitarian support to the most vulnerable people affected by the economic crisis and 2) supporting the response capacity of the Lebanese health and WaSH systems in coping with disease outbreaks, including waterborne diseases, Oxfam, in partnership with Utopia and Nabad, is proposing a 10-month project covering outputs 2.1 and 2.3 of the WaSH sector 2023 joined up framework. Specifically, the proposed intervention is designed to respond to outcome 2 of the ERP WaSH chapter - More vulnerable people in Lebanon are using safely managed drinking water and sanitation services whilst reducing health and environmental risks and improving water quality by increasing the proportion of wastewater that is safely treated – for a total of 523,772 beneficiaries in the North, Akkar, Bekaa, Baalbek el-Hermel, Mount Lebanon, Nabatieh and the South, through:
1. Upgrading 33 water pumps with solar systems
2. Upgrading 8 chlorination units to solarized operated chlorination systems
3. Distributing critical hygiene items – Menstrual Hygiene Items - to 33,000 individuals, alongside awareness raising
4. Raising awareness on water quality, conservation and safety

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection risks, especially sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and contractors, exacerbating tensions between refugees and host communities over assistance provided – potentially leading to violence or mass evictions-, reinforcing gender stereotypes if women and girls are not involved in discussions over WaSH topics, will be monitored throughout the implementation of the project. Mitigation measures: 1. Ensuring selected contractors sign Oxfam’s Code of Conduct; 2. Maintaining an Oxfam staff presence on site; 3. Obligatory safeguarding training for all Oxfam and contractors’ employees; 4. Establishing complaints mechanisms with existing community groups and focal points to raise awareness on SEA risks and adapt reporting channels; 5. Conducting key stakeholders mapping; 6. Ensuring endorsement of project by key stakeholders; 6. Community consultations with gender-segregated groups to integrate men and women’s recommendations in the implementation plan.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Oxfam applies an APP Framework, which foresees multiple mechanisms at the project level to meet commitments to all stakeholders. Oxfam runs several channels (in person, email, phone, website) that are communicated to the community in order to report issues of conduct, safeguarding, and fraud related to Oxfam’s work. Oxfam maintains a national Community Response and Feedback (CRF) phone hotline that community members can use to give anonymous complaints and feedback. All project staff are trained to receive community feedback in person and enter it via a mobile app into Oxfam’s digital dashboard. Sensitive complaints (non-safeguarding) are shared and discussed with senior management. Safeguarding and fraud-related issues are addressed by Oxfam’s Safeguarding Focal Points and are investigated by trained safeguarding investigators. Communities and any other stakeholder can also log complaints in a whistleblowing hotline run independently from Oxfam by a third-party organization.
