Project Summary

The project's objective is to provide safe and timely multi-sectorial services to internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities (HC), and non-displaced individuals affected by the ongoing conflict in high-needs locations. Education services are a critical focus due to the conflict's impact, with 9.1 million people needing education, including 6.5 million children (51% girls) lacking access. AORD aims to support 7,350 children (380M, 570W, 3,700G, 2,700B), including 735 CWD, by enhancing access to education through the provision of recreational materials, establishment of Temporary Learning Spaces, rehabilitation of 147 classrooms, installation of 50 handwashing facilities, provision of psychosocial support services, and establishment of referral pathways.WASH interventions target 23,500 individuals (4,606M, 4,794W, 7,191G, 6,909B, and 2,820 PWD) through activities such as drilling and installing three new hand pumps, installing two new mini water yards, rehabilitating two water yards, rehabilitating five water distribution points, providing water supply through trucking, constructing seven emergency latrines and three institutional latrines, rehabilitating seven emergency household latrines and seven institutional latrines, conducting vector control and solid waste management campaigns, providing training for 175 community members on latrine operation and maintenance (O&M), distributing hygiene kits to 1,400 individuals, conducting seven hygiene campaigns, and forming seven hygiene clubs in schools.Nutrition activities aim to reach 30,986 individuals through counselling for 5000 pregnant women and caregivers, dissemination of Infant and Young Child Feeding/Education messages for 4000 individuals, services for early childhood development, early detection of malnutrition among under-five children, provision of food-based prevention for acute malnutrition, management of severe acute malnutrition for children, training of state and locality staff, and awareness raising on different types of nutrition disabilities and available services.
AORD's efforts extend to provide GBV support to Ben through the 10360 Ben(2115 W, 2030 M, 3170 G, 3045B)establishment of Women Centers, rehabilitation of Women Centers, support for community-based protection networks, income-generating activities for women, GBV training, provision of dignity kits, and specialized GBV response services. Additionally, the project addresses general protection concerns for 5,290 beneficiaries through 420 individual protection assistance, cash assistance for 140 HHs, mental health and psychosocial support, the establishment of dedicated facilities, awareness-raising sessions, protection needs assessments, and provision of case management.
Child protection activities for 3,759 Ben(51% female) involve the registration of separated children, placement in alternative care, response services for those experiencing GBV, family awareness raising, structured PSS services, support for children with disabilities, community participation, and training on child protection. Conflict-induced livelihood challenges affecting 19.3M people prompt AORD's FSL interventions. Targeted activities aim to support 6,550 individuals (1,637M, 982W, 1,179B, 1,965G, 687PWD) by integrating FSL with WASH and Nutrition interventions, prioritizing life-saving measures and improving food security and life-sustenance. A comprehensive and integrated approach ensures the efficiency of implemented activities, contributing to the achievement of overarching objectives (CO1&2). The selected activities will support the most severely food insecure population groups focusing on life-saving by improving the food security status and life sustenance. The proposed multi-sectorial activities will be integrated with other sector interventions to achieve the overall goal. Comprehensives and integrated approaches will be followed during the interventions to ensure high efficiency of implemented activities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Escalating conflict and insecurity, pre-existing crises, and deep-rooted chronic child protection concerns have had serious, long-lasting, harmful effects on hundreds of thousands of girls and boys and their families in Sudan. The Protracted displacement, loss of assets, abuse, physical and psychological trauma, fractured societal structures, and chronic long-term economic, social, and health problems negatively impact not only children and their families but also child protection organizations themselves and their ability to deliver life-saving child protection assistance. Children with disabilities are constrained and challenged to access humanitarian services due to the limited data availability on how they are impacted and the lack of accessible information on the available services. In 2024, AORD is committed to adhering to the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AoR) and maintaining a community-based approach in its interventions and will continue to tailor the response to the fast-changing situation and the emerging needs through the following modalities: uniformed and functional child protection information management system, strengthened sub-national coordination mechanism, localization strategy, enhanced intersectoral and interagency coordination, and remote modality where access is a constraint. Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity during the implementation of the project. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS). In addition, AORD will focus on engaging with organizations of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action to establish systemic, meaningful, mutually reinforcing collaboration and to strengthen reach to more marginalized populations. Moreover, efforts in local empowerment and ownership of community-based child protection networks (CBCPNs) in all the targeted states shall be given due priority, including hard-to-reach areas.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

AORD is committed to ensuring that humanitarian assistance is effective and aligned with the principles of AAP. Clear, concise communication, input into planned activities, feedback on humanitarian assistance, and participation in decisions affecting the lives of affected communities are paramount. AORD will adhere to humanitarian standards, and in line with the "Do No Harm" principle, confidentiality methods for feedback will be identified and communicated appropriately to avoid exposing community members to further risks. As appropriate, mechanisms and approaches will be tailored to the needs of IDPs, Non-displaced people, and host communities. AORD is committed to aligning with the CHS focusing on standard number 5 Community feedback mechanism and response. To ensure the quality and accountability of the project, AORD will promote transparency, meaningful participation, and inclusion through the following steps: 1. Enable the targeted populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence 2. Share project information at an early stage and on an ongoing basis with girls and boys, parents and caregivers, community leaders, and government stakeholders; 2. Host regular consultations/ listening forums with established groups, including women, boys and girls; 3. Ensure partner communities are fully aware of PSEA, Protection and Safeguarding Policy including how to make complaints or raise issues. 4. Share information on progress and lessons in relevant coordination groups and key messages on child participation 5. Ensure a robust and accessible complaints mechanism and response procedure is put in place and appropriate for different groups. The mechanism and timelines for expecting responses and how complaints and feedback are managed to inform programming; 6. Ensure that all staff, and stakeholders, are familiar with existing referral pathways both to service providers for supporting identified cases and referring to other agencies. In facilities, posters and leaflets will be posted and shared on program objectives, key activities, and ways in which beneficiaries can provide feedback to the implementing partner. Feedback will be collected where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, boxes, and a dedicated call-in number and meetings. Compliance boxes at the community level are the mechanism put in place by AORD for suggestions and complaints(SGBV), ensuring proper periodic review, analysis, reporting, and documentation. AORD will share project information at early stages and maintain ongoing communication with girls, boys, parents, caregivers, community leaders, and government stakeholders. through capacity building and referral pathways, AORD will increase knowledge of AAP, GBV, and disability among service providers and community nutrition workers.
