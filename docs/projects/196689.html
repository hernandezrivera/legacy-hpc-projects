Project Summary

A business incubator is a program that aims to revive entrepreneurship and provide opportunities for marginalized communities, women and vulnerable people to demonstrate their potential as entrepreneurs. It provides a suitable and inclusive environment for these groups during the inception and protection stages of their projects, while also reducing incorporation costs for new entrepreneurs. The ultimate goal of the incubator is to present strong, sustainable projects to the community, particularly those led by marginalized communities, women and vulnerable people that have the potential for growth and development. This is particularly important for Syrian youth, as it enables them to establish, develop and grow their own independent projects, thereby contributing to the national economy and local output. Additionally, the incubator aims to stimulate creativity, innovation and the entrepreneurial spirit among marginalized communities, women and vulnerable people, by providing opportunities to develop projects in various economic, practical and technical fields. Furthermore, it helps in converting research, studies and creative ideas into real projects and products that can be marketed and led by marginalized communities, women and vulnerable people. Finally, the incubator also networks projects with various public and private sectors, and links them with investors and businessmen, specifically with a focus on those led by marginalized communities, women and vulnerable people.

3 - Needs

Sanad, in partnership with UN agencies such as FAO, UNFPA, and UNDP, conducted a thorough needs assessment to determine the specific needs of youth entrepreneurs in Syrian governorates of Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, and Hama for a business incubator. The assessment aimed to identify the challenges they face in starting and growing their businesses and the resources and support they require to be successful. Data was collected through surveys, interviews, and focus groups with youth entrepreneurs and potential entrepreneurs in these governorates, as well as an analysis of existing literature and data on youth entrepreneurship in the region. The findings were used to design and develop the incubator program, ensuring it addresses the identified needs and provides the necessary resources and support for the youth entrepreneurs to succeed. The assessment revealed a lack of access to funding, mentorship, and networking opportunities as the main challenges faced by youth entrepreneurs in these governorates. The incubator program was designed to provide funding opportunities, mentorship programs, and networking events to overcome these challenges and support the youth entrepreneurs in their business ventures.

4 - Implementation of activities

The business incubator launch activities will include a variety of events and initiatives to introduce the program to potential participants and other stakeholders. These may include workshops, networking events, and information sessions. The incubator will also have a formal application process for participants to apply and be selected. The monitoring and evaluation plan will involve regularly collecting data and feedback from participants, mentors and other stakeholders to measure the effectiveness of the program and identify areas for improvement. Specific metrics such as the number of participants, launched businesses and revenue generated by incubated businesses will be used to measure progress and impact. Regular check-ins with participants and mentors will provide support and guidance as well as identify any challenges. The data collected will be analyzed and used to make adjustments to the program as necessary. An evaluation committee composed of experts in entrepreneurship, and representatives from the incubator's partners will be established to evaluate the program's progress and impact and provide recommendations for future improvements. The monitoring and evaluation plan will be an ongoing process to ensure the incubator is meeting its goals and objectives, and to make necessary adjustments to ensure the program's success.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

During the project design phase, no risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) have been identified. However, Sanad recognizes the importance of preventing and addressing PSEA in humanitarian settings and has established policies and mechanisms to prevent and respond to any cases of PSEA that may occur during the implementation of the project. These include the development of a Code of Conduct for staff and partners, which sets out clear standards of behavior and guidelines for preventing and addressing PSEA. Sanad also has a complaint and reporting mechanism in place for staff, beneficiaries and partners to report any concerns or incidents related to SEA. Regular training on SEA prevention and response is provided to all staff, partners and volunteers involved in the project. Sanad is committed to maintaining the highest standards of conduct and will take prompt and appropriate action in the event of any breaches of its Code of Conduct or any reports of SEA.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Beneficiary accessibility and transportation issues: There is a risk that beneficiaries may face difficulties in accessing project locations due to transportation challenges or lack of infrastructure in certain areas, which may lead to low participation rates and difficulties in delivering services. To mitigate this risk, Sanad will work with local partners and stakeholders to find solutions such as providing transportation subsidies or working with local authorities to improve infrastructure. 2- Time-consuming governmental processes: There is a risk that project implementation may be delayed due to time-consuming governmental processes such as obtaining necessary permits and approvals. To mitigate this risk, Sanad will anticipate and plan for potential delays by building in extra time for governmental processes in project timelines and by working closely with local authorities and stakeholders to ensure smooth project implementation. 3- Economic Status: There is a risk that economic instability and fluctuations may negatively impact the project's ability to achieve its objectives and the beneficiaries' ability to access and benefit from the project's services. To mitigate this risk, Sanad will monitor the economic situation in the project area and adapt project activities as necessary and also lower high-cost activities to ensure the sustainability of the project.
