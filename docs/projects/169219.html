Project Summary

The project aims to ensure that stranded migrants, migrants in vulnerable situations including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), victims of trafficking (VoT) and vulnerable host community members affected by the Beirut Port explosions are safely identified and protected, in line with international standards. This will be achieved through mapping of services available for migrants; direct assistance of protection services; and comprehensive assistance including cash to meet immediate needs, MHPSS and health care services, legal support, return assistance and case management. This support will be provided in cooperation with NGOs. Furthermore, this project will enhance the safe identification and referral of those who are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, VoTs and those who have special needs among host communities, migrants and refugees communities to specialized services including to mental health services through the provision of capacity building and tools to frontline actors, and referral to existing MHPSS teams. IOM will also establish community support systems of trained community members in Psychological first aid and translate and distribute MHPSS leaflets. A team of informal interpreters in the most recurrent language of migrants will be trained in basic MHPSS, humanitarian principles, listening and translation skills for MHPSS services. Through this initiative, IOM will provide technical assistance to enhance the implementation of national standard operating procedures (SOPs) developed by the counter-trafficking working to identify, assist and protect VoTs.

1 - Needs

Estimates on the number of migrant workers (MW) in Lebanon range from 250,000-400,000, with large populations of nationals from Ethiopia, Bangladesh and the Philippines. A large portion of MW live in the damage radius of the explosion (including Geitawi, Bourj Hammoud and Ras El Nabaa) who have lost their homes, damages to their shelters. Based on initial rapid evaluation, IOM estimates that 24,600 migrant workers have been affected in a number of sectors of Greater Beirut. IOM has already confirmed that there are over 150 injured MW, 15 causalities. Reported needs by migrants to NGOs and embassies since the explosion remain similar to those that were identified before the explosion. This includes food,water, sanitation and hygiene, shelter, cash for rent, medical aid, PSS, personal safety and security and voluntary returns. Migrants in irregular situation or within employer sponsorship system (Kafala) find it increasingly difficult to access much needed and life-saving humanitarian assistance. The need to respond to the Beirut explosion in addition to existing limitations due to COVID-19 resulted in disruption of referral pathways- such as those for victims of abuse, VoT, and vulnerable children, leading to interruptions in case identification, referral, and protection processes. This has led to immediate protection gaps for vulnerable migrants who are often unidentified and/or unassisted. Over time and if not properly addressed this could contribute to the weakening of referral pathways and protection systems for migrant workers. Specialized service providers, such as NGOs providing shelter and case management services for VoTs, unaccompanied or separated children, and other migrants in vulnerable situations are facing unprecedented challenges in operations as they have insufficient supplies and staff, facilities are not adequately prepared to accommodate such crisis. Since the outbreak of COVID-19 , a significant number of migrants were prioritized return to their country of origin as one of their main needs. Loss of income and devaluation of the currency has reduced access to savings for migrant workers, limiting their ability to return on their own.

2 - Activities

Data collection including mobility tracking, thematic assessments on migrant workers needs in disaster affected areas by IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) to identify the most vulnerable migrants, host communities and refugees and their greatest needs. Provide training to existing MHPSS teams working with NGOs on identification and referral pathways for specialised MHPSS services for migrant workers. Train informal interpreters in the most recurrent language of migrants in the country including in basic MHPSS, humanitarian principles and listening and translation skills. Translate in the most recurrent languages of migrants and targeted distribution of MHPSS leaflets and social communications developed by other actors including the Ministry of Health (MoH). Provide training and tools to front line actors (embassies, communities, social workers, NGOs) on safe identification and referral of those who are at-risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, VoT those who have special needs among host, migrants and refuges communities to specialized services in targeted areas. Provide protection and comprehensive assistance including case management for at-risk migrant workers who are victims of violence, exploitation, abuse and trafficking, and those who have special needs. Caritas Lebanon will provide safe accommodation and counselling services to migrant workers who are identified as need this support. Provide voluntary return support to 1,250 stranded migrants, including associated case management and health assistance prior to departure. Provide cash assistance to cover immediate needs of the most vulnerable migrants affected by the explosion. Provide technical assistance and basic capacity building to enhance the implementation of national SOPs to identify, assist and protect VoTs.
