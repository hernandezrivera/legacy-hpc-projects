Project Summary

The project aims through its activities to support 10 schools in Maarat An-Numan and Kafr Nobol sub-district in Idleb province in formal and non-formal education, 7500 students (3405 females, 4095 males), 300 teachers and education personnel (90 females, 210 males) will be benefited from the project.
the project has two main phases:
1- Academic year:
GAN will support 10 schools in the targeted communities that accommodate about 5500 students (5-17 year) including (3405 females, 4095 males) and 52 students with disabilities, first of all, back to learning campaigns (BTL) will be conducted before the academic year starts, brochures will be distributed to raise the awareness of the importance of education flag
the risks of not enrolling children in schools., communities’ mobilization and posting a poster in the main facilities of targeted the communities will be conducted, in addition to lunch the campaign on the media, ASER test will be conducted to help us better plan and focus on the needed type of educational activities based on ASER results, also to capture the intervention impact, learning materials (stationery, pens, notebooks...etc.) and school bags will be distributed for students, the materials are disaggregated by age (5 to 11, 12 to 17) and sex (males, females), recreational activities also will be provided inaddition to running cost (such as fuel for heating, stationery for schools... ), GAN will establish one durable child-friendly space in each school (total 10) to conduct the PSS activities one session a week per class, ant potential drop-out of students will be followed up by the specialized protection staff and they will try to get them back to schools.
teachers will be trained on (child-centred and protective pedagogy, active learning, life-skills, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE minimum standards (MS)) in addition to provision teachers and education personnel (90 females and 210 males) with monthly incentives to attract the skilled teachers and improve the learning outcomes, PTA meetings to make sure a good level of coordination and engagement with parents which is also part of our accountability mechanism to make sure feedback from parents is taken and address,
the curriculum used in schools is the SIG.
2- Summer phase: the summer phase has two main activities:
2-1- rehabilitation of schools: GAN will rehabilitate the existed classroom in schools (about 80 classrooms in 10 schools) and the scope of rehab is windows, doors, walls, floor and schools’ yards in addition to providing the needed furniture like students’ desks, whiteboards, teachers’ tables, and chairs, WASH facilities will also be rehabilitated making sure that the facilities are clean, healthy, both gender (males, females) have easy access to it and adapting it with the disabilities.
These rehab activities will improve and increase the safe access to schools and ensure children are receiving education in a safe and protected environment. As parents might not send their children to schools due to the inappropriate school condition. rehabilitating the targeted schools will make sure that school absorption capacity is increased and more students can benefit its education services.
2-2- providing non-formal education for student in summer for 2 months for about 2000 students in 10 schools including (912 females, 1088 males): GAN will target the out-of-school students with literacy and numeracy classes and students who have poor education score with catch-up and remedial classes, GAN will distribute the learning materials (stationery, pens, notebooks...etc.), in addition to distributing recreational materials to schools and conducting recreational activities for students to motivate them.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced an estimated 2.1 million, over one-third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and over 120,000 teachers are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Schools are reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria, accounting for an estimated 32 percent of collective centres. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. Teaching capacity remains overstretched. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. This shortage, as well as irregular and under payment of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage for girls and the worst forms of child labor. More than 40 percent of the out-of-school children in Syria are between 15 and 17 years of age and have differentiated learning, skills development and livelihoods needs. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war-related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. Exposure to violence decreases attendance, leads to drop-out and affects learning outcomes by limiting concentration. In some areas, over half of the children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two-thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area.(HRP 2019) According to the data gained from HNO: the targetd areas (Kafr Nobol and Maarat An Numan sub-districts) are considered as hirgh severity scall (4) and the number of childern school age (5-17) in need 102,195 including 56,577 boys and 45,618 girls in addition to the total number of teachers in need is 2,129 including 1,179 male and 950 female, GAN is one of the limited organizations who work in these areas and support 8 schools which accommodate about 5500 students,according to the evidence and experience:- The rehabilitation of schools is still a challenge that contributes effectively to increase the safe access to schools and ensure children are receiving education in a safe and protected environment. As parents might not send their children to schools due to the inappropriate school condition. - Rehabilitating of classrooms will make sure that school capacity is increased and more students could benefit its education services, in addition to improving the learning outcomes,(sometimes 50 students or more existed in one classroom)- children are dropped out because of different reasons and the identified ones are poverty, displacement, lack of safety both at home and in school, and unknown reasons,- girls may not attend schools because of poor WASH facilities conditions and disaggregation issue, children with disabilities also may not attend schools because the schools are not adapted to receive them (lack of WASH facilities condition, stairs .. etc)- Girls and boys may not attend schools due to the lack of hygiene needs, so provision of hygiene kits taking into consideration different needs disaggregated by sex (boys and girls) and age will increase access to education.- responding to the needs identified will protect children for the risk of early marriage and child labor, recruitment and exploitation,GBV can result in specific disabilities for children: injuries can cause physical impairments; deprivation of proper nutrition or stimulus can cause developmental delay and the consequences of abuse can lead to long-term mental health problems.

Implementation of activities

-Provide children and youth with formal education: GAN is committed to providing the official curriculum issued by SIG which is called "curriculum B", in addition to measure the learning outcomes using ASER tool for Arabic, English and Math subjects. - Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs (including catch-up classes, SLP, remedial education, literacy and numeracy classes): GAN will provide the non-formal education for those who have poor education score or dropped-out in the summer (July and August 2019) for 2000 students including about 1020 male, 980 female. - Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns (media outreach, awareness raising activities, community mobilization): GAN will conduct 14 campaigns, 10 campaigns before the academic year and 4 campaigns before the summer phase, brochures will be distributed, and posters will be posted in the targeted communities, in addition to outreach posts via media. - Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms (temporary or permanent):GAN will contract with a rehab service provider from inside Syria to conduct the rehab needed for schools according to the deep need assessment that will be conducted, the rehab scope will cover classrooms, windows, doors, walls, and school yeard, - Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities: GAN will conduct a deep need assessment in the targeted schools according to the cluster guidelines document which is developed by the task forces recently, then after the contracting with the rehab service provider and conduct the needed rehab considering to finalize the rehab process before the academic year or proceed the rehab after-school to protect students, in addition to take into consideration the disaggregation between boys, girls and adapting the facilities with disabilities, - Provide students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags in formal and non-formal settings: GAN will proceed the procurement form Turkey or Syria, the technical specification of the items will meet the UNICEF standards and cluster recommendation and disaggregated by age (5-17) and sex (differentiate between girls and boys such as bag colors .. etc,), the distribution will be conducted according to the previous students' list and from the vendor to the schools directly. - Provide professional development to teaching staff (child-centred and protective pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life-skills, Curriculum B, ECCE, New Curriculum, psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE minimum standards (MS)):GAN will contract with a professional company to provide the needed training inside Syria, about 300 teachers (90 female and 210 male) will receive the training, pre and after questionnaires will be conducted (the protection risks like GBV will be considered in the contract with the company). - Provide teachers and education personnel with incentives: monthly incentives will be provided to the teachers and education personnel, the scale will be determined according to the cluster recommendation, the teachers are not entitled to receive incentives from another side, the teachers must sign the Duty of Care policy like the field staff and the Monitoring of incentives' payments will be conducted through ensuring that timesheets of personnel are complete, correct and meet the incentive of each staffmember in the approved budget. -Provide children with school-based psychosocial support programs in formal and non-formal settings: GAN will establish a Child-friendly-space in each school and equip it with needed materials, or conduct a maintenance for the existed ones, the PSS curriculum for children 5-17 will be provided according to protection cluster recommendation and one weekly seeion will be provided for students during the academic year. - PTA meeting will be conducted on mothly basis as a part of accountability with affected popula

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- access to the target schools locations.- the availability of the needed materials and equipment.- the good security situation,- access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries.Risk:1- the risk of shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies in the targeted locations or and infighting between different armed groups continues causing loss of access to the targeted locations inaccessible:Mitigation:GAN will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again.2- the heavy shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies for a long time (months) or the target locations becomes under its controlMitigation:Shifting program locations to more accessible areas in coordination with Education cluster, HFU, and other partners and relevel stakeholders.3- Communities and key stakeholders reject our project/plan;Mitigation:Include the local and IDP community in the design and the implementation of the activities of this project, conduct community mobilizationahead of the project promoting to its proposed activities and ensure the community acceptance. In addition to the close coordination of relevant stakeholder including MoE, EDs and EAs and MoU to be assigned by education authorities.4- A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the organization and negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria.Mitigation:As a registered NGO in Turkey, GAN is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitudetowards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, GAN will adapt their modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure continued assistance, although GAN has its operational offices inside Syria that can continue following up all the needed activities with the remote management from GAN headquarters in the neighboring countries.
