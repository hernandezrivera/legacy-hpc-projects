Project Summary

Through the proposed interventions HIHFAD is planning to continue the provision of
general and specialized health services through a network of health facilities into a
a comprehensive modality including Primary health services with 4 primary health centers
and secondary health services with 5 hospitals 2 Maternity and Pediatric Hospitals and 3 general hospitals. The mentioned facilities are also putting into consideration full access to people with disabilities through the prepared infrastructure of those facilities to make sure of access without any difficulties.
In addition, HIHFAD will continue operating 3 rehabilitation centers to enhance the quality of life of people with disabilities by the provision of physiotherapy sessions and a solid referral mechanism to other healthcare facilities if needed. Adding to the previous interventions HIHFAD focuses from a comprehensive perspective on providing specialized health services connected to the hospitals and outpatient services such as a dialysis unit in Armanaz Hospital and operating a Tuberculosis center which is the only of its type in Idleb governorate and prosthetics /orthotics Center to serve people with amputations.
MHPSS
HIHFAD is implementing MHPSS activity since 2017 in NW Syria through Integrate MHPSS services into health facilities including providing mhGAP consultations and distributing psychotropics through 15 mhGAP-trained doctors working in health fixed facilities.
Also, Individual Psychosocial Support sessions are provided using structured interventions (problem management plus PM+, thinking healthy program) through well-trained PSWs who have the psychological academic background working in fixed facilities and rehabilitation mobile teams as a part of a comprehensive rehabilitation approach including 3 specialties (physiotherapy, nursing, and psychosocial support).

3 - Needs

Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), children, elderly, PWDs, people with NCD (which are estimated to account for 45 percent of all mortality in Syria ), people with mental health and psychological issues, and those with injuries are the most vulnerable groups in need of emergency life-saving health services. Furthermore, 4.2 million women of reproductive age in the health sector PIN require healthcare assistance in order to access essential reproductive health services, including antenatal care, safe deliveries, postnatal care, family planning, and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care. Of the 501,783 live birth expected in 2023, 75, 267 of those women will need assistance to access emergency cesarean sections to save their lives and the lives of their new-born. Within the health sector PiN, 2.7 million persons are estimated to have a disability which places them at greater risk for exclusion from health services. According to the 2022 MSNA, households affected by disability were most likely to report living more than 1 hour from a health facility, paying for health care, non-availability of needed services, and lack of access to a health facility for persons with disability or mobility challenges. It is estimated that approximately 1 in 10 people in Syria lives with a mild to a moderate mental health condition, 1 in 10 children need focused mental health care and, 1 in 30 is likely suffering from a more severe condition. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

HIHFAD will make sure that the individuals of vulnerable groups are always accompanied by either their own caregivers or by pre-defined facility staff, so such individuals are additionally protected against any type of harassment or abuse while at the supported facilities. The facilities will be ensured to be well lighted and ventilated and the electric outlets will be rendered either high enough or provided with protecting means, so they are inaccessible to children. HIHFAD will ensure the privacy of the patients, where special attention will be paid to ensure the availability of safe and comfortable examination settings that supports the patient’s privacy. Furthermore, sex-separated toilets will be ensured available as well.To promote the protected environment for the presented beneficiaries, HIHFAD plans to conduct training on psychological first aid for the first-line humanitarian workers that are relevant to the proposed intervention. In addition, HIHFAD will ensure the availability of mhGAP and PSS as gender-focused services integrated into both proposed Health facilities. regarding COVID-19 Spread in NW of Syria during the project implementation HIHFAD focused to provide protection for both staff and beneficiaries and the mitigations were - Counseling group activities or reducing participant numbers. - Using online platforms for meetings and training. - train staff on IPC and provide PPE kits - worth mentioning that all HIHFAD staff are vaccinated by SIG teams


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Considering the high vulnerability of the project beneficiaries, HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per facility. In addition, the services will be provided on a free-of-charge basis for all those in need regardless of their individual gender groupings. HIHFAD will ensure the privacy of the patients, where special attention will be paid to ensure the availability of safe and comfortable examination settings that supports the patient’s privacy. Furthermore, sex-separated toilets and breastfeeding corners willbe ensured available as well. HIHFAD will make sure that the individuals of vulnerable groups are always accompanied by either their own caregivers or by predefined facility staff, so such individuals are additionally protected against any type of harassment or abuse while at the supported facilities. The facilities will be ensured to be well lighted and ventilated and the electric outlets will be rendered either high enough or provided with protecting means, so they are inaccessible to children.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The potential risks and the mitigation depending on the area's situation 1-Attacks on the civilian population and health infrastructure, which may result in the destruction of thefacility or the death of the project staff/Management: Review and adjust security measures on a daily basis Participation in the UN de-confection mechanism: communicating and pinning project sites as independent neutral humanitarian locations, in order to prevent bombing by international actors. Emphasis on neutrality, independence, and adherence to humanitarian principles and IHL 2-Intensification of the conflict, leading to delays in implementation, due to access restrictions Possibility: Low/Management: Temporary suspension of project activities and minimization of staffmovement Adjustment of time and the activity plan 3-Drastic changes in the context of the Syrian conflict, such as extensive changes in the front lines, leading to mass expulsions and/or access restrictions Possibility: Low/ Management: Provision of emergency plans e.g. through the deployment of personnel in mobile clinics in the event of a sudden IDP influx - Constant and accurate context analysis and situation assessment by the security focal point of the project partner HIHFAD.4- interruption of WHO supply line-Temporary closure of the border crossing between Turkey and Syria for humanitarian aid, leading to delays in implementation. Possibility: low /Management: - Adjustment oftime and activity plan
