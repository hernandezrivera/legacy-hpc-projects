Project Summary

Almanar is planning to contribute in the response for the urban refugees in Khartoum state, and South Sudanese Refugees in Khartoum, South Kordofan and East Darfur states, according to the needs, assessments conducted during 2019 year.
The overall goals of the project is to contribute in improving the livelihoods status of 360 urban women and their families from among the most disadvantaged communities in Khartoum state (Jabel Awlia, Shreq Alnile, Khartoum and Umbadda localities) through a comprehensive self-reliance, and poverty reduction approach as well as reducing morbidity and mortality of the affected populations caused by unavailability of preventive and remedial health services.
The project works in an integrated way and combines a comprehensive social development program with activities to increase livelihoods opportunities targeting most vulnerable women, thereby addressing both the causes and effects of women’s disempowerment. This will be achieved through building the knowledge and skills of men & women by raising awareness on their rights, supporting their groups to make their voice heard in demanding peaceful, providing vocational skills, literacy classes and secured life for their children and communities, and reducing barriers between women from different backgrounds and life experiences in order to contribute in strengthening a base for peace and reconciliation, coupled with gender training for men, leading to an improvement in the socio-economic status of the marginalized women and their families. In addition to strengthen the preparedness and capacity of national actors to fulfil their protection obligations within the overall humanitarian response.
Also the project will target the same localities which host the urban refugees in health component, ensuring live saving is exist for the urban refugees in Khartoum state. Beside the small target of health sector in 2020 RRP, Almanar will ensure the good health status of its FSL beneficiaries as well, through constructing of 2 facilities (1 in Haj-Yousif, 1 in Um-badda), providing medical treatment by qualified Health staff, provision of drugs and lab equipment, conducting Mobile clinic for the population in the catchment areas.
In addition, the project will seek and target the total of 25,000 South Sudanese Refugees in Khartoum, South Kordofan, East Darfur states with health intervention. The objective is to reduce the morbidity and mortality among South Sudanese refugees in their camps and outside the camps through constructing/rehabilitation of health facilities, providing medical treatment by qualified Health staff, provision of drugs and lab equipment, conducting Mobile clinic for the population in the catchment areas to reach most vulnerable and disable people.

1 - Needs

Sudan has a longstanding tradition of hospitality towards refugees and asylum-seekers. As of December 2018, UNHCR reports over 1 million refugees and asylum-seekers recorded in Sudan. This includes individuals officially registered by UNHCR and the Government’s Commissioner of Refugees (COR), as well as official estimates based on other government and UN sources. The Government of Sudan estimates that overall, there are 2 million refugees and asylum-seekers living in the country. Factoring in new arrivals and population growth, it is projected that 1.2 million refugees will be in need of assistance in 2019. This includes refugees from the Central African Republic (CAR), Chad, the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia, South Sudan, Syria and Yemen. Voluntary return is not an option for a vast majority of these refugees due to the situation in their countries of origin and resettlement opportunities remain limited.Syrian, and Yemeni refugees are considered as “brothers and sisters” by the Government of Sudan, and are not required to register with UNHCR and COR upon arrival. About 16,000 Syrian and Yemeni refugees are registered in Khartoum by UNHCR and COR for assistance purposes, with an additional 80,600 Syrian nationals recorded by immigration authorities entering the countryThe majority of asylum seekers and refugees arrive in Sudan with few personal belongings or livelihood assets and are in urgent need of livelihoods support to safeguard their food security while enhancing their self-reliance and capacity to meet their basic needs. Those living in camps and large out-of-camp settlements remain largely dependent on monthly food distributions. Little investment has been done in livelihoods programming for refugees, which further compounds food security issues. This is particularly in a context of ongoing food assistance pipeline breaks, as well as protection concerns, as it relates to the risk of child labor and exploitation, as well as women’s access to safe and legal income-generating opportunities. Available assessments indicate that income generating opportunities for women, as well as livelihood inputs such as agricultural seeds.For Eritrean refugees in Khartoum, a lack of livelihoods opportunities remains a key driver of onward movement and continues to be a critical gap. Many refugees have skills that can be easily and quickly transformed into livelihood options with some initial financial assistance and/ or training. Some of the options for livelihood in Sudan include vocational training, agricultural access, agri-business, fishing, and small business grants for those qualified. (Source: HNO 2019)The health status of newly arrived refugees and those in protracted situations remains a concern. A significant proportion of refugees and their host communities live in remote and often hard-to-reach areas, often with limited quality of health infrastructure. Understaffing, and drug and medical supply gaps are major challenges, resulting in poor quality of service delivery. 2018 mid-year indicators show that approximately 30 per cent of refugees in Sudan still lack access to primary health care services. Within the South Sudanese refugee response, access to health coverage varies from location to location. For example, access is estimated in Khartoum recent assessment indicates only about 25 per cent are able to access primary health care services. There is a need to strengthen quality, access and coverage of primary health, reproductive health and emergency care referral services. The integration of health services to meet the needs of both out-of-camp refugees and host communities is also a key challenge. (Source: HNO 2019)

2 - Activities / Outputs

The project is targeting urban refugees in Khartoum state with focusing on improving livelihood status as well as their health status in the Jabel, Awlaia, Shreq-Alnile and Umbadda localities. The livelihood component will be achieved in the 3 targeted localities in Khartoum state, through building their knowledge and skills of men & women by raising awareness on their rights, providing vocational skills, literacy classes and secured life for their children and communities, and reducing barriers between women from different backgrounds and life experiences in order to contribute in strengthening a base for peace and reconciliation, coupled with gender training for men, leading to an improvement in the socio-economic status of the marginalized women and their families. Also Analyze market through survey and assessment of vocational trades to identify viable trades, establish and develop women savings and loans groups, procure required materials and equipment. In addition to strengthen the preparedness and capacity of national actors with refugees needs and rights to fulfil their protection obligations within the overall humanitarian response. Also strengthening the referral system to other humanitarian support in the area.For the Health services for the urban refugees in Khartoum state, the objective will be achieved through construction total of 2 health facilities (1 in Haj-Yousif and 1 in Umbadda Locality), provision of medical equipment, providing the medical treatment and referral for cases to be treated either in centers or to be referred the Hospitals for the targeted beneficiaries (boys, girls, women and men) even PNS and elderly. To ensure the sustainability of the services capacity building will be provided for MOH seconded staff, Community members and Health care medical staff.For South Sudanese Refugees in Khartoum, South Kordofan and East Darfur States, Almanar will focus on improving their health status through construction/rehabilitation of existing health facilities, provision of medical equipment, providing the medical treatment and referral for cases to be treated either in centers or to be referred the Hospitals for the targeted beneficiaries (boys, girls, women and men) even PNS and elderly. Include mobile clinics for wide reach for the vulnerable SS. To ensure the sustainability of the services capacity building will be provided for MOH seconded staff, Community members and Health care medical staff.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Almanar through coordination with the local government authorities and humanitarian partners will incorporate protection principles with DO NO HARM and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in the implementation of this project by avoiding causing harm preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project activities which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks, ensuring equality by arranging access to impartial assistance and services without discrimination and paying special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services through the vulnerability assessment and identification vulnerability criteria. Through the CBPCs and consultation sessions Almanar will ensure accountability to the beneficiaries, where they can address their concerns and complaints. Through the awareness session and training workshops, networking and forums Almanar will support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empowerment.
