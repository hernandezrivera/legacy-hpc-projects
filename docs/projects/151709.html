Project Summary

UNICEF intends to support the Coordination in the WASH sector, essential to strategize and optimize the intervention of all actors in WASH.
After reviewing different assessments, keeping in view the situations in East, needs, capacity and comparative advantage, UNICEF is planning to respond the needs of 1.77 million individuals in the two conflict affected areas of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (GCA and NGCA), with provision of safe drinking water, improving access to safe sanitation and hygiene supplies, dissemination of critical water and hygiene related messages on communities located on both side of contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk, in Eastern Ukraine. This will be implemented through the water utility companies (centralized network of primary and secondary water suppliers) and decentralized structures. A particular focus will be made on WASH in social institutions (education/health facilities).
The different activities take into account the gender, age and disabilities specificities and promote equality, especially on gender. People reached with more than one activity are only considered once to avoid duplication in counting beneficiaries.

Needs

As the fifth year of Eastern Ukraine’s armed conflict takes its toll, the local population continues to be affected by regular on-going hostilities and its consequences with issues related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) as ones of the most acute. Reports, including the HNO, indicate that 3.2 million people (out of 3.6) whose water supply is affected by the conflict, require direct water supply and sanitation assistance in the conflict affected areas (GCA and NGCA). Conditioned by the on-going conflict, critical public water infrastructure located close to the line of contact (LoC) is put at risk on a daily basis of being targeted by on-going hostilities: 105 WASH incidents (shelling, shooting or other issues) directly affecting the water and wastewater infrastructure were reported over the past 12 months. Water companies such as VD and PVK continue to struggle financially further requiring support in the procurement of essential chemicals to treat water (with a significant increase of the cost of chlorine gas in 2018), and equipment/material to make critical repairs. The stoppage of water would also impact companies in charge of heating networks, with the potential for widespread population suffering as temperatures often reach -20 C between December and February.Poor condition of WASH infrastructure both on the institutional and community levels results in applying negative coping strategies among people (for instance storing water in buckets, with a potential for microbiological contamination). At the same time while the level of unemployment rises and the purchasing power drops, people face increased food and water insecurity, lack of access to healthcare and other impediments forcing them to leave places of their origins (WASH Cluster 2018).In 2018 the number of people living close to the LoC experiencing frequent water shortages, either daily or weekly was 27%. At the same time, due to lack of funding under the 2018 HRP, the number of people accessing trucked water dropped from 12% in 2017 to 7% in 2018 (REACH).According to WASH cluster, towns and villages near the frontline report problems with garbage village councils can no longer afford to pay for trucks to remove it. The same applies to sewage in remote areas along the contact line, where people also need to empty household level septic tanks; this is more difficult as pump trucks refuse to come, or charge a premium for the risk incurred. WASH implementing agencies reported observing difficulties for people to empty septic tanks near the Line of Contact, with people instead using 200 liter drums and other means to empty septic tanks manually. Old sewage networks and wastewater treatment facilities pose a significant risk of water contamination. Unaddressed water, sanitation and hygiene needs create serious health risks for the population (like diarrhea).There are 3,542 schools, kindergartens and vocational training colleges (670,400 children, of which 65% within the 20km from the LoC) in the 2 oblasts, of which 52% reported a need for improvements to substandard latrines (20% of all education facilities excluding kindergartens are not equipped with any privacy item – screens and/or cabin with doors), or inadequate water supply (Education Cluster 2018).Due to the lack of support to the main water suppliers in NGCA, the access to drinking water via piped supply dropped from 64% before the conflict to around 53% at the end of 2017 (REACH). Lastly, the access to hygiene materials remain challenging with some being not available in all locations in Donbas. The least available items being adult and infant diapers (ACCESS consortium 2018). Vulnerable groups (elderly, people with disabilities, woman-headed household, numerous children household…) are of particular focus. Meanwhile the cost of hygiene items increased on average by 10% from Nov. 17 to Feb. 18. Hygiene items prices in Donetsk city are appr. 25% higher than the Ukrainian average (WASH Cluster 18)

Activities & outcomes

Output 1: Coordination and advocacyWASH coordination mechanism continued for effective, efficient and accountable humanitarian response for affected people (Female, Male and children) in Eastern Ukraine.There is a need for further coordination on activities implemented in the WASH sector, identification of gaps, avoidance of duplication, standard setting, assessments and response strategies, technical guidance. As well as an advocacy work around 1) the targeting of water infrastructure by all parties to the conflict; 2) increased funding for water utility companies (international donors, govt); 3) facilitated & safe movement of utility workers and essential materials to complete timely repairs.Output 2: Support to service providers Main Water Service providers supported and strengthened, in coordination with WASH actors, to ensure continued water supply and sanitation services to the affected people in Donetsk and Luhansk, GCA and NGCAUNICEF intends to support VD & PVK directly, for 5 months Aug.-Dec. 2019, as a pre-identified gap already coordinated with ICRC & SDC with liquefied chlorine gas and chemicals for water quality improvements. A support to repairing water companies’ water/wastewater systems, with provision of spare parts, materials and equipment to facilitate them to repair critical water infrastructures, focusing on systems run by VD, PVK in NGCA, LV in NGCA, and smaller vodokanals, is planned. Wastewater systems, pipelines and treatment plants have also been impacted by shelling and blockages due to reduced water flow, will also be supported;Output 3: Support to institutionsBoys and girls and their families in different social institutions including education and health facilities have access to improved and sustainable WASH services Assessment and repairs to damaged water, sanitation and heating systems in health facilities, schools and kindergartens is essential to keep the essential services operational. A particular focus on gender and dignity will be made on toilet related activities.Provision of hygiene kits/voucher based assistance (baby, adult, family and elderly)Implementation of Hygiene Promotion activities to students, parents and teachers. Partnerships with the following CSOs, at least, will be established: AN, ADRA, PUI & TGH in GCA, DDC & WRF in NGCAOutput 4: Service provision to most affected people, including women and childrenAffected population has access to the efficient WASH services through provision of WASH supplies, emergency repair of WASH communal infrastructure/facilities.Short-term water trucking of potable and technical water (untreated water used for cleaning) will be initiated only when critical needs arise, complemented by bottled water distribution, in respect with SPHERE Stds;In parallel, increasing the resilience of the most vulnerable HH with water storage (water tanks or jerricans) or small-scale water systems (boreholes construction or source securing, water towers, distribution network, filtering device);Repair of water structures of decentralized water networks is planned, with empowerment of women in village councils;Desludging of septic tanks and solid waste removal activities are required especially in areas close to the contact line where access has been limited due to the conflict, and where costs of removal are now high. WASH activities supported by integration with livelihoods activities;Given the harsh situation at winter time, some winterization activities will be realized (provision of coal, installation of heaters, boilers…)Improved availability of essential hygiene items, through an e-vouchers programme in GCA, and distribution of hygiene kits in NGCA or in hard to reach areas in GCA. Programmes are designed to answer to the specific needs: women, elderly, people with disabilities... An appropriate hygiene promotion programme will be implemented: hygiene sessions on reproductive health & MHM to youth; for pregnant women; for elderly people
