Project Summary

World Vision will continue supporting A'zaz and Afrin districts in Aleppo governorate with emergency and sustainable interventions as follows:
1. To support lifesaving WASH needs in a number of A’zaz camps ( Bab Al-Salama, Shamaren, Sijjo, Al-Haramen and Al-Rayan with some of informal settlements (Telal Alsham, Alzaytoon, and AlAiwaa), interventions will focus mainly on
• support boreholes in those camps with operational and maintenance costs (diesel, spare parts ,maintenance, operators)
• Covering the daily needs of water by providing water trucking according to Syrian cluster standers
• Emergency repair and maintenance of WASH facilities
• Regular garbage removal activities within camps and informal settlements.
• Hygiene promotion sessions with regular distribution of hygiene consumable items.
2. Support resilience in some communities in A’zaz and Afrin districts, by providing WASH-related services:
• Support water stations with rehabilitation component.
• Provide operational and maintenance costs for targeted water stations
• Provide all technical support to water committees within these communities to enhance their capacity to operate the station properly and effectively.
• Support municipalities within these communities with solid waste management operational cost.
• Rehab and construction sewage and rainwater drainage systems within communities,
• Providing vehicles for jetting and suction of sewage pipes to enable municipalities to conduct repairing process.
• Hygiene promotion activities with communities in coordination with education sector and civil society organizations.

Needs

The North-west of Syria is marked by ongoing conflict and witnessed hostilities throughout 2018 including between NSAGs. This has resulted in civilian casualties, damage and destruction of civilian infrastructure including attacks on humanitarian assets and personnel, and waves of mass displacement that continue to have a significant impact on needs and vulnerabilities. There are some 2.8 million people in need including some 1.9 million IDPs, many of whom have displaced multiple times, Based on WASH cluster assessments conducted mid of 2018 there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements and host communities). Over the course of 2018, following a series of changes in territorial control owing to military operations and “local agreements”, the Syria humanitarian operation contends with three distinct areas with different humanitarian needs and access dynamics. While acute needs are found throughout the country, the presence of IDP sites of last resort in the northwest and northeast. A Space of Hope (SoH) study conducted in November 2018, provides insight into the main protection concerns and issues in North Aleppo for child, adolescent and women. In the assessed villages, 73% of respondents believe that there is a community committee who provide the basic services (such as water, sanitation, waste removal…etc) and ensure these services are available and functioning sufficiently. However, 23% of respondents reported no presence of this committee in their villages, leaving a large gap to respond to basic needs. Received data from the study also showed that the percentage of IDPs to Hosts was high in areas such as Azaz and Souran, due the high waves of displacement in the last 8 months, adding extra strain to basic services. The increase in population coupled with the unstable situation has also led to increased safety and security concerns, particularly for women, such as sexual exploitation. In the target area, and more generally across Northern Syria, women can be understood to have a subordinated role in society with poorer access to services and resources; and are more commonly subject to physical and gender-based violence and sexual violence (HNO, 2018). Women’s decision-making power is low at all levels, making the exercising basic rights (e.g. access to life saving WASH, health and social services; registering for humanitarian assistance; or claiming their property to secure shelter) extremely difficult. Boys are also under threat of GBV and sexual exploitation and older boys and young men are at risk of being recruited by armed groups and criminal gangs. The children and youth of Syria have also become collateral damage in a situation beyond their control. A report by the Global Child Protection Working Group , the global level forum for coordination on child protection in humanitarian settings, indicated that 98 percent of Syrian children surveyed reported deterioration in their psychosocial well-being and humanitarian agencies are warning of a potential “lost generation”. Further to this, a War Child report writes that the provision of support for the psychological and social well-being and recovery of children – ‘psychosocial support’ – is being overlooked, despite confirmed evidence that psychosocial support during emergencies is critical. In February 2018, World Vision spoke to 1,254 Syrian girls and boys; their stories included war-induced violence, displacement, and missing, separated and deceased family members. Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented through direct implementation using WV field staff as WV field team has a great experience within the previous 5 years where all staff have been hired form targeted communities in Syria consist of engineers and assistants and hygiene promoters which build better acceptance from the community and increase the understanding for the essential needs of those communities.The technical team will prepare all technical studies including BoQs, schematics and all other needed documents to start tendering process, these studies will be reviewed by WASH specialists in Turkey office and then will be sent to procurement for the public tender process, additionally, WASH staff will work closely with local authorities and community leaders to make sure all needs and project will reflect the actual needs of those communities.A number of volunteers will be hired for implementing some works inside the camps such as hygiene promotion activities, garbage collection and latrines cleaning.

Protection Risk Analysis

WASH is a valuable intervention thus, tensions can run high when interventions are made. Vulnerable individuals and groups may be unable to obtain their entitlement, or may have it taken from them by force. WVI will be assessing in advance and adopt mitigation measures, in terms of selection criteria and to minimize the risks, these should include adequate supervision of selection and service delivery, the involvement of local authorities/ councils might be required. Measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender-based violence or sexual exploitation associated with service delivery will be considered.Moreover, Sexual activity cannot be requested in exchange for humanitarian assistance and WVI must must NOT be party to any such forms of exchange. WVI follow a zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and other forms of abuse of power by aid workers or those collaborating in provision of WASH services (including community volunteers. Safeguarding policy, safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). Gender balance of assessment teams will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the distribution areas. In addition, it may be advisable to rotate distribution and assessment teams so that no one team constantly visits the same community. This will reduce the potential for exploitation by staff, as well as providing a greater opportunity for protection issues to be picked up by a broader range of staff with different expertise.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There is a risk of inter-community tension due to varied hygiene kit packages across members; WV mitigates this by standardizing hygiene and baby kit contents. Additionally, there is a risk of sexual harassment from unseparated and poorly lit bathroom facilities; WV mitigates this risk by providing IDP camps with street lighting and separate facilities by gender. WV recognizes the risk of assistance dependency, especially in the emergency context; as a mitigation strategy WV is building the community capacities to build and maintain structures and ensure sustainable provision of WASH services as well as shifting whenever possible to rehabilitation projects as opposed to emergency provision. There is a risk of staff members unjustly charging individuals for provision of services; WV mitigates this risk through sharing information with beneficiaries, especially women and girls, who may be subject to sexual exploitation. Finally, all WV's staff signs a code of conduct regarding this.
