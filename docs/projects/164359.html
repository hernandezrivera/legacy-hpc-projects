Project Summary

As shelter lead agency, UNHCR is coordinating the emergency shelter responses and implementing the 2020 shelter programme from inside Syria, as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey). Through this project UNHCR will support the communities of returnees in specific targeted areas ( some 1,357,000 persons) through emergency response and long-term shelter solutions, from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey).
Project indicator targets represent a sum of Syria and Gaziantep hub targets, outlined below:
The shelter Programme aims to improve housing and related community/public infrastructure addressing shelter needs of 15,000 vulnerable households/75,000 individuals through basic house repairs. Returnee communities will be supported with debris removal and installation of solar street lights in 67 neighborhoods to support 837,500 individuals/167,500 returnee families and host community.
Through emergency response shelter conditions will be improved for 67,500 individuals/13,500 households with 13,500 emergency shelters kits, as well as through provision of emergency shelter to 150,000 individuals/30,000 households ( tents distribution, camp construction and collective shelter rehabilitation).
UNHCR’s cross-border shelter programme aims to provide emergency shelter (tents) to 43,000 households/215,000 individuals and address shelter needs of 2,000 vulnerable households/10,000 individuals through basic house repairs through shelter material/kits. The component of Gaziantep hub constitutes 23% of the project.
The role and responsibility of the local and national authorities will continue in close partnership with MoLAE and partners (NGOs/INGOs) in the implementation of shelter and infrastructure works. Project management capacity and technical skills of 2,000 engineers from local government will be enhanced through a training.
UNHCR area-based approach, is well adapted to the immediate needs of communities, consolidating protection, shelter, access to basic infrastructure and basic services to ensure maximum impact through integrated integrated interventions in targeted communities. Affected population will be empowered and capacitated at all stages to participate in programme planning, needs assessment, implementation, monitoring and evaluation in order to design acceptable, appropriate, sustainable and culturally-sensitive shelter programmes.

4 - Needs

Based on the draft SNFI HNO 2020 summary, the shelter situation has continued to deteriorate due to the cumulative impact of the crisis, particularly in urban areas, putting pressure on others basic services and facilities, with a need for better quality and sustainable shelter integrated with WASH, health, education, and basic services. The overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people. Of this, 3.8 million (69%) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the north-east. There is significant overcrowding in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, where the total population exceeds 6.5 million people with more than 2.4 million internally displaced. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. In addition, some communities in 23 sub-districts in Aleppo, Hassakeh, Idleb and Rural Damascus (with five sub-districts comprised of over 80% IDPs - Al Tal, Tal Rafat, Dana, Sabe Byar and al Hol and Sabe Byar) are overburdened as they host more IDPs than resident populations with limited shelter and hosting capacity, where repair or rehabilitation are needed alongside a multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to basic services.The continuous increase in prices of construction material has significantly reduced the ability of the affected populations and returning populations to repair their houses. Damaged houses, coupled with non-functional public service networks in return governorates such as Aleppo, Dara’a, Al Qunitra, Hama, Ar Raqqa, Deir Azzor and Rural Damascus, form the main challenges for the population to settle back in their original areas. As an outlook for 2020 the draft HNO summary anticipates a further increase in displacement levels as a result of intensifying crisis in north-west and north-east Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project activities will be implemented through partners, while procurement of shelter kits will be conducted by UNHCR. For the long-term shelter assistance, the program will reinforce and upscale the sustainable, recovery-oriented shelter solutions through support to those returning to their original homes. The shelter response will also contribute to public infrastructure rehabilitation, including, but not limited to, debris removal, rehabilitation and upgrading of water utilities networks, distribution of solid waste bins and to ensure sustainable community development. The implementation modalities of the shelter interventions will follow established Shelter Sector guidelines. The following main activities will form the core of UNHCR’s shelter program: 1) Provision of emergency shelter, e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure, and distribution of emergency shelter materials and kits. 2) Rehabilitation of collective centres and transitional shelters (including: in-kind and physical repair). 3) Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/returnees/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for- work, labour, etc.). 4) Training of stakeholders on resilience and quality-oriented shelter skills and capacities. In 2020, UNHCR expects to reach 1.2 M individuals in Syria, based on following factors: 1) expected improved approval process following improved coordination between UNHCR and MoLAE; 2) access all over Syria is constantly improving, providing more areas available for our interventions; 3) UNHCR and partner capacity increased, new SOPs are in place and understood by our partners; 4) an increased number of infrastructure projects (almost 70% of the total target); these are community based and not at HH level interventions, benefitting the entire community where we implement our projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of increased community tension due to packages, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigation measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Returnees are exposed to Risk of eviction and/or exploitation with major protection implications, while there are also risks of rights violations, either of owners or occupants, and future disputes due to the difficulties in demonstrating ownership documentation (HLP).Mitigation measures: Gather available information locally through a structured due diligence assessment that would help to identify and prevent/mitigate HLP risks specific to each intervention. Risk of interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risk.Mitigation measures: Capacity building of Partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with GOS and Partners to mainstream shelter sector criteria implementation in beneficiary selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Eestblish an active and informed complaint mechanism is in place.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk description: Lack of access hampering needs and impact assessments and protection monitoring of the programs results in continuous relying on estimations, 3rd party reports and information provided by partners for planning purpose, which may not accurately reflect needs and impact being assessed largely through proxy reporting.Mitigation measures: Maintain and expand UNHCR field presence and monitoring capacity. Advocate with GoS on the need for systematic monitoring and continue the direct and indirect advocacy for access to safe areas. Risk description: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources.Mitigation measures: Implementing risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote monitoring arrangements. Train/Support Partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and communicating with communities’ mechanisms. Support partners to capacitate staff on fraud prevention. Risk: Potential for aid diversion/corruption leads to reduced assistance to the persons of concern to UNHCR and a decline in donor funding.Mitigating measure: SOPs for beneficiary selection and distribution process to be used by Partners. The findings and recommendations in partners’ and 3rd party monitoring reports reviewed and implemented. Risk description: Risk of limited national NGO capacity, particularly in areas directly inaccessible by UNHCR, resulting in inefficient programme delivery, inadequate assistance to beneficiaries and reputational damage to UNHCR.- Mitigation measures: Maintain partner selection and retention process. Advocate for simplification of approval procedures for PPAs with national NGOs. Conduct capacity building activities for local partners
