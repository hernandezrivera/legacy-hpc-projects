Project Summary

The proposed actions have been designed as a part of an overall Integrated Program aiming to respond to WASH, Health and Protection needs for the Southern and Western of Libya, Sebha, Murzuq, Ghat, Ubari, Aljfara, Azzawya, Zwara and Tripoli mantikas.
The protection components are integrated into Health and WASH Responses summarized further below. This programme will be implemented in partnership with Libyan NGO Migrace, as implementing partner, with support, training and monitoring from WW-GVC. This builds on a partnership which started in 2018. The main areas of intervention will be in the South and West of Libya, where WW-GVC is currently present in partnership with Migrace.

Using WW-GVC’s established Community Protection Approach (CPA) and Individual Protection Approach (IPA), this programme will build capacity within Libya for integrated protection programming through a focus on community engagement, training and awareness raising, and safe identification and referral. Through a participatory process WW-GVC will undertake multi-sector needs assessments at a community level. The multisector questionnaire will focus on information gaps and veriﬁcation, in order to extract the Integrated Protection System of Indicators (IPSI) which will establish indicators in 12 sectors benchmarked with international standards, a protection risk, threats, vulnerabilities, capacities, and dignity and safety indexes. The questionnaire also generates protection triggers through which WW-GVC will use to implement safe identification and referral of individuals or households with protection needs. Through assessments at community, household and individual levels, protection responses will be tailored to needs, bearing in mind gender, age and diversity, particularly in consideration of vulnerabilities based on diversity including displacement/legal status and on different support needed to access services. Individuals in need of protection assistance, and individuals with specific needs will be identified and referred to services on the basis of a mapping of services in the areas of intervention. Data will be recorded on WW-GVC’s secure online platform, which adopts PIM principles and is GDPR compliant. Furthermore, WW-GVC will coordinate through Protection Sector to support existing service mapping tools, and will work with intersector coordination mechanisms, with a view to engaging in and strengthening the common feedback mechanism. In terms of training, WW-GVC will build local capacities for identification and referral, through training trainers in the community, and through outreach to local authorities, non-protection actors and relevant community leaders, to embed an understanding of key protection principles and practices, including respect for confidentiality, informed consent, and do no harm, as well as raising awareness on issues like PSEAH and access to services.

- The health component (separated HRP proposal submitted to the Health Sector) will be focusing mainly on the rehabilitation of primary and secondary public health facilities by upgrading their basic services through the supply of the needed equipment, commodities and/or refurbishment. The support provided will strengthen the capacity of the health care facility staff through the delivery of personnel capacity trainings and workshops for efficient and inclusive management, operation and maintenance of the upgraded facilities. The project will highlight the inclusion of the most vulnerable groups in the areas such as IDPs, Refugees, Migrants, women and children, by facilitating their accessibility to the facilities and include staff training related to GBV and protection.

1 - Needs

The HNO notes that shifts in the conflict dynamics and the impact of the COVID pandemic in 2020 have exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Libya. The breakdown of the electricity, water, oil, and cellular network systems affect the provision of and the accessibility to life saving services. Movement restrictions curfews, closures of services, loss of livelihoods and limitations in social contacts have different impacts on diverse sectors of society, often worst-affecting the most vulnerable people, creating or exacerbating obstacles to information and services. With 49% of Libyan households and 42% of non-Libyans reporting movement restrictions in their Mantika, outreach through community engagement with information about services and the option of confidential referrals is needed. Community protection approaches are needed to build local capacity to safely identify, inform and refer people with protection needs, with due consideration to stigma faced by survivors and by people from diverse groups. According to figures in the HNO, there are more than 600,000 migrants and over 45,000 refugees living in Libya, and almost 400,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs). The on-going conflict and gaps in state control leave migrants and asylum-seekers and refugees at high risk of being trafficked and IDPs in fear of retaliation if they return to their areas of origin due to perceptions of affiliations with a given group. Lack of legal documentation adversely impacts migrants and refugees considerably, particularly East Africans, with a higher percentage of men (66%) than women (41%) reporting difficulties due to a lack of documentation. Discrimination also negatively impacts migrants and refugees, heightening the need to identify and ensure referrals/access to relevant service providers. The impact of the pandemic and of economic crisis has heightened the vulnerability of already-precarious households, increasing the potential impact of further shocks and the likelihood of resort to negative coping mechanisms if they are not identified and supported with protection services and other sector support as relevant. Negative coping mechanisms include depletion of savings, borrowing money, reducing food intake, selling goods and skipping, rent, returning to (unsafe) places of origin, women taking on informal jobs with a high risk of being exploited and abused. For children, there may also be heightened pressure for early marriage or for child labour being valued above the investment in education. Women, girls and their families cope with the fear of being exposed to GBV by reducing their movements which results in reduced access to education, livelihoods and assistance. The lack of access to formal education due to the COVID-19 pandemic has negatively impacted the physical and mental health and wellbeing of children, exacerbated by living conditions characterized by insecurity, poverty, exposure to explosive ordnance, limited recreational activities and inability to access safe spaces. Migrant children continue to be subjected to violence and exploitation including torture, abduction for ransom, child labour, sexual abuse, physical abuse, child-trafficking, and detention. With widespread explosive hazards as a result of the conflict, injuries leading to disability can be anticipated, especially if families return to highly contaminated areas. Persons with complex health issues and elderly people may be particularly isolated and unable to access services without additional support in the context of the pandemic.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project will be accountable to the affected population at each phase of the cycle project thanks to GVC’s Community Protection Approach (CPA; https://cpainitiative.org/). Population engagement is a process of empowerment, following the Ladder of Citizen Participation. Different AGD groups will be identified based on a sampling approach and classified according to a scale of prioritization. Attention will be paid to ensure that underrepresented groups are considered. A selection committee will be composed of the heads of targeted facilities, community representatives and GVC and its implementing partner Migrace staff to design the project. They will also be included in the design of the M&E tools. The beneficiaries will be included in the implementation of the activities (ToT of Community mobilizers; Personal from targeted facilities; FGDs during the implementation for potential readjustments etc.). A complaint mechanism will be put in place: 1) Complaint boxes made available in each targeted structure/community; 2) On the GVC project management platform, an online complaints form, in English and Arabic will be accessible. This platform has been developed in in compliance with the GDPR and the principles of PIM; 3) During the monitoring visits of the M&E officer, s/he will may will complaints with beneficiaries (i.e. including PwD, people who are illiterate and marginalized groups). A mechanism for identification, assessment and referral of families and individuals ensures meaningful access to assistance and services. A final evaluation will measure the impact of the intervention. An exit strategy will assure the durability of the project and the re-appropriation by the beneficiaries and the stakeholders. At last, GVC Libya will pay attention to apply GVC Policy such as the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, PSEAH. A reporting mechanism for cases of violence and/or abuse will be set up. GVC also aims at using the Common Feedback Mechanism.
