Project Summary

The project will reduce the risks of water-related morbidity, mortality and malnutrition amongst the 37,000 targeted girls, boys, women and men through increased access to water, sanitation and hygiene assistance. The project will increase sustainable access to water, sanitation and hygiene services through the implementation of three key responses; the drilling of two strategic boreholes, the mapping of water sources in five targeted districts and the rehabilitation of five community boreholes and ten shallow wells (shallow wells, deep boreholes or others) in Mudug, Sool, Sanaag, Bay & Bakool and Lower Juba regions. The proposed response will: provide emergency water trucking and emergency water tanks at strategic water distribution points; emergency latrines and latrine dislodging for communities and IDPs; water treatment services such as chlorination and routine water quality testing to the targeted beneficiaries. The project will strengthen the local water governance through the establishment and training of Water Management Committees (WMCs) at the community and IDP level. The committees will manage WASH facilities, conduct hygiene promotion training, carry out clean-up campaigns and/or cash for work solid waste disposal activities that improve the sanitary conditions in camps and communities. The committee will also carry out health promotion services such as; acute water diarrhea/ cholera prevention, water sanitation and safe handling procedures. The project will strengthen the governance structure through; the training of committees, the development of bylaws that outline how the facilities will be managed and the establishment of government oversight roles in order to enhance the participation of line ministries in the maintenance of boreholes. The project will address the WASH cluster and the 2018 HNO priorities for 2019. Particularly, SO4 - Resilience: Support the protection and restoration of livelihoods, promote access to basic services to build resilience to recurrent shocks, and catalyze more sustainable solutions for those affected, including marginalized communities. Address water shortage in the target locations by providing sustainable water access and hygiene and sanitation. Furthermore, the project will contribute to the reduction of the risks of water-related morbidity, mortality and malnutrition amongst targeted children and families through increased resilience and increased access to other services such as education and protection.
The project activities implementation process will be sensitive to environmental protection and the sustainability. for instance, there will be environmental and social impact assessment for any sites where new construction will be done and this will ensure the risk mitigation measures will be applied during the implementation process. There will be extensive community and other stakeholders’ involvement in the decision making as well as participating in the actual delivery of the project activities. The project will use the local available local materials for the construction of the sanitary facilities (latrines, hand washing stations). This will enable the communities to replace when those local materials are worn out or other villagers will adapt and replicate by constructing their on sanitary facilities using the local available materials.
The project activities will include disaster risk reductions, where the communities and the local authorities will be supported to map the local risks, hazards and there will be community own DRR plan that is in line with the WASH response.
The project will be integrated with other lifesaving responses such as food security, health, education, protection and ensure that these other repose strategies correlate and complement the project activities. The project activities will have gender and protection mainstreaming in order to address the potential exposure to address gender dynamics, risk or conflict as a result of the project resources. The project will be linked to the protection project where the cases of gender based violence will be supported, as well as strengthen the referral mechanism to other sectors such as food security, education, health so that other immediate needs outside the WASH scope are also addressed.
Adeso has been implementing WASH projects in Somalia-funded by several donors (SHF, OFDA, EU, SSF) and has overtime established a strong technical capacity to deliver large scale WASH emergency responses. In the 2017 pre-famine responses, Adeso implemented over $5 million WASH response activities in Somalia. Our strengthened capacity will ensure the proposed emergency WASH project will be successfully delivered.
The proposed locations are those that have been facing frequent water access challenges as result of limited or poor rainfall, security related displacement, limited water facilities and an increased number of IDPs that overstretch the resources of the host community. This project will reach 37,000 disaster affected girls, boys, women and men in the proposed locations with the total project budget of $1.5 million.

Needs

From the recently undertaken internal needs assessment by the Adeso field teams as reported in the month of September 2018 in the project target locations. It was noted that population is in dire need of WASH interventions especially women and young girls who mostly tend to household chores and small livestock as their male counterparts take the larger herds of cattle out to look for pasture and water. Among other challenges noted, it was reported that exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups (minority groups such as women, young girls, the aged, under-fives and physically challenged men and women) is contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian needs and lack of protection among some of the most vulnerable. At the same time, disease outbreaks such as cholera, other waterborne diseases and measles continue to lead to preventable deaths in Somalia. Across the country there are multiple threats to the health and resilience of the population due to inadequate WASH facilities. For water supply, the population is dependent on a mix of unprotected surface water sources and protected groundwater sources and there is limited access to clean water during the dry periods. Surface water sources are seasonal and usually contaminated as the community rely on traditional water sources. Many of the urban and peri-urban poor, including IDPs, pay up to 5 times more for their water than those in rural areas, The overall situation is compounded by the ongoing conflict in Sool and Sanaag regions. As a result of clashes in Tukaraq town, Sool region, since 15 May 2018 a reported 2,200 households, mainly women and children, fled their homes, leaving behind their assets. Currently tropical storm in coastal parts of Puntland are measures that will potentially further restrict the capacity of humanitarians to respond. The sanitation service provision was not given much attention by the successive armed groups that were in tandem controlling the district.

Activities

The above needs identified will be addressed through the establishment of new strategic water points, rehabilitation of the existing water facilities and improvement of the sanitation and hygienic practices. The project will improve immediate and long term access to clean and adequate water, improve sanitation and hygiene practices for the targeted disaster affected women, girls, boys and men. in addition, the project will strengthen the water local governance structures through training, bylaws development and linkage with the water line ministries. The project will take into consideration the clan and communities dynamics and minimize new management structures that may cause conflicts instead the existing local structures will be strengthened. The project key activities include; drilling of 2 new strategic boreholes, rehabilitation of dysfunctional 5 boreholes and 10 shallow wells, construction of 100 latrines (desludgeable types) and the hygiene and sanitation awareness campaigns. The project will provide emergency water trucking to those facing water access crisis with strategic placed water water tanks and provide water through water voucher system. The project will provide water treatment through chlorination and routine water quality testing activities at the water point, systematically and at the household level. This project will establish and train Water Management Committees (WMCs) at the community and IDP level to manage WASH facilities, conduct hygiene promotion and clean-up campaigns and/or cash for work solid waste disposal activities that improve the sanitation conditions in camps and communities. The project aims to carry out health promotion services on AWD/Cholera prevention, water sanitation and safe handling. The project will strengthen the governance structures through committee training and the development of bylaws that outlines different roles, enhancing the participation of government oversight roles of the line ministry

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed project locations are relatively safe and allow for humanitarian assistance. The project target locations are under the control of the local authorities and communities that have previously accepted humanitarian assistance. As such, there is a foreseeable stable environment in 2019 for continued humanitarian assistance. The project will address the government and community priorities as reflected in the community action plans, government strategic plans, the national development plan and the immediate needs of the community. By addressing the government and community priorities and needs, the project will link the government and local communities interests in the project, allowing for the acceptance, ownership and sustainability of the response. The implementation of project activities will be conducted through inclusive community participation, from the design of the proposed response to the implementation and the monitoring of the response delivery. The project will consider the local structures such the local communal committees, the local governance and the religious systems in place, to ensure that the implementation of project activities are in line with the local structures. The project will engage the local contractors in the rehabilitation and the construction of the project funded structures. The use of the local contractors will ensure there is local acceptance and local participation in the implementation process. All the project activities will be handed over to the committees as soon as its completed for the timely use. There will be capacity strengthening of the local communities in the management of the project structures. Bylaws will developed through a participatory process, which will guide the management and maintenance of the water structures, tariff collections and its uses, during the election process of the committees. The beneficiaries' entitlement will be ensured during the implementation of project activities and this will be clear to the parties involved. For instance, the project will make use of water vouchers, which each registered beneficiary will use when obtaining the water at the emergency water truck sites. The water voucher outlines the details of the beneficiary, the amount of water the household needs to obtain and the date. There will be a beneficiary satisfaction survey that will be carried out in order to get feedback and there will be post water distribution monitoring, which will monitor whether or not the beneficiaries are receiving the right entitlement.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project will undertake gender context analysis as part of vulnerability assessment in order to identify the different needs, concerns and priorities of women, girls, boys and men taking into consideration age and disability. The project will ensure that mitigation measures are put in place to address the risk of exclusion by facilitating selection and nomination of members of the Water Management Committees and hygiene promoters. During the selection Adeso will ensure that all groups in the community are well represented while maintaining gender balance and including people with disabilities, internally displaced persons, minority clans. Rights based and community based protection approaches that ensure 'do no harm' and inclusive participation of the community will be used to ensure that interventions and interactions address gender and power dynamics at the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project, thereby reducing the risk of exclusion and conflict over resources. Adeso has previously trained its staff on the Organizational Safety and Security Plan, Sexual Abuse & Child Protection Policy to ensure that they understand how to implement project activities in both secure and non-secure environments. This training has enhanced staff’s capacity in ensuring project activities are implemented in a non-discriminatory and impartial way, and promotes the safety, dignity, and integrity of the people receiving assistance. Adeso will continue to collaborate with the protection cluster through coordinated responses with various actors with diverse mandates and experiences and whose primary responsibility is to protect all individuals within their jurisdiction, especially the most vulnerable (women, children, elderly and people with disabilities) groups.the project will ensure inclusivity in the project delivery, and so will ensure minorities, marginalized and other interest groups are included during the targeting and implementation process.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The gender context analysis that will be conducted by Adeso will seek to identify the needs of men women, boys and girls considering age and disability. The identified needs will enable gender responsive design of the project. Adeso recognizes the importance of water for life and a source of livelihood and therefore ensures that all the field staff have been trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse which enables them to exercise high integrity and ethics while performing their duties. Adeso will facilitate the following in order to ensure that targeted populations are not excluded based on gender and age: Facilitate gender balance and active participation of men and women in the village, District and State water committees. Collect and use data dis-aggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. Communicate information through various means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels (ex. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms). the project will ensure there is gender mainstreaming in the delivery of the project activities so that women, youths, men and children are all included in the project activities implementation. lack of access to water affect differently -the women, men, girls and boys and therefore special attention will be given to the gender and age of the target population. for instance, Somali women and young girls tend to do more households chaos, fetching water and maintaining sanitation at household level. such cultural practices will be taken into consideration when deciding the locations and the design of the water and sanitation facilities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Through the gender analysis and conflict analysis facilitated by Adeso, a gender and conflict management strategy will be developed and implemented to ensure access to WASH services by minorities and vulnerable groups; such as female headed households, pregnant and lactating mothers, the elderly and people with disabilities. Drilling of boreholes and rehabilitation of existing water points is a strategic investment that can address the drivers of conflict through provision of water which is a critical resource and catalytic in nature to drive growth and improve livelihoods. The location of the water points will be well selected through a rigorous rights based and inclusive community approach that will ensure all groups in the community amicably agree on the location and management of the water points. Adeso will facilitate formation and coordination of village, district and State water committees that will be tasked with the responsibility of resolving conflicts as the committees are social contracts and coalitions that will foster trust and local ownership of solutions, all of which contributes to peace and stability in Somali society. The selection process will also ensure representation of all groups in the community as per the age, gender and diversity approach guidelines. conflicts and resources over resources are common in Somalia and is key reason for clan conflicts and displacement. The likely risk factors are exclusion during the locations, beneficiaries selections, limited wider community involvement, during the tender issuing and distribution of trucked water or the hygiene kits. The project team will embrace all inclusive discussion in the community right from the design of this project where the priorities have been captured. there will be committees -that represent all the communities and handle any emerging issues in very early stages to avoid conflicts as result of the project resources.
