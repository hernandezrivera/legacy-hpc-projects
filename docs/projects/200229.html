Project Summary

SIDC has been working with the most vulnerable populations(VPs) including, but not limited to, individuals from the LGBTIQ+ community, sex workers (SW), people who use and/or inject drugs (PWUD/ PWID), migrants, people living with HIV(PLHIV), youth engaging in risky behaviours and the families of all the aforementioned individuals.Reaching out to these individuals is focused on sexual, reproductive&mental health(SR&MH), GBV, harm reduction (HR) services and case management.
Throughout the past years, Lebanon has been experiencing a series of compounded crises beginning with the uprising of 2019, leading up to the Beirut Port explosion on August 4th, continuing with the hyper price inflation, the pandemic, cholera, presidential gap, and the devaluation of the LBP.These crises have exposed the structural gaps and fundamental inequalities that VPs are subjected to, hindering their access to basic needs, protection, social, and economic development and putting them at an increased risk of unemployment,unstable housing, violence, worsened MH, and food insecurity.
The worsened conditions have been particularly evident in assessments conducted with migrants. Findings within the MSNA (Nov 2022) indicated that migrants identified health support as one of the top three priorities,with safety and security being reported as a concern for all genders. GBV was also indicated as a concern whereby some areas are avoided due to their fear.Through outreach activities conducted by SIDC in 2022, 19% of individuals reached (from 1571) indicated they are survivors of GBV, while 23% presented with additional vulnerabilities related to drug use, sex work, and injecting drug use.
During 9 months,SIDC aims to:1)Reach 180 migrants through 9 group awareness sessions conducted over the duration of 9 months(1 full day session/month,reaching 20 different migrants in each session). Topics covered during the sessions include:MHPSS, legal topics,access to services,SRH,HR,GBV& substance use.2)Reach 3000 migrants to benefit from individual screening, counseling on how & where to receive support for SH,MH,HR,GBV,etc.2700 migrants will be reached through the field outreach activity&300 through the screening&awareness fair.The outreach team will be composed of VPs,with a focus on migrants.5 field visits per month will be done reaching 300 migrants.Moreover, SIDC, in coordination with partners within the migrant sector taskforce, will conduct a screening day through the implementation of a fair, reaching a total of 300 migrants.This fair will consist of booths on protection,health,SH,legal,shelter,food security&other relevant topics to be screened and gain knowledge on assistance pathways.Sector partners,based on their expertise/field, will be available within these booths to raise the awareness of the migrants on key topics& counsel them on how&where to receive support.An area will also be available to create a space for the children to play.3)80 humanitarians,frontliners &service providers will be trained on cultural sensitivity pertaining to SIDC's topics with a specific focus on migrants. 4)One study will be conducted to address the engagement of migrants in risky behaviour.Upon the completion of these activities, migrants would have increased knowledge on the aforementioned topics&available services.Finally,migrants will be referred to SIDC&partner organizations as deemed necessary.
The proposed project will ensure that migrants, with their additional specificities, are targeted and complement objectives set within the GBV and health sectors, as migrants will be ensured access to life-saving&life sustaining health services for those most vulnerable.
By collaboratively working together and including migrants, the whole project process would ensure that any and all risks that may expose migrants will be identified and mitigated against.A range of communication modalities will be made available to migrants to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SIDC will be providing 180 migrants with group awareness sessions on the topics of MHPSS, SRH, HR, GBV, risky behaviours,one session per month,over 9 months.This activity has been identified by migrants reached during SIDC's pilot project, as extremely crucial as they expressed needing a safe space to ask and better understand information about topics that are "taboo".Through this project, SIDC will also be screening and providing awareness to 2,700 migrants through it's field outreach team, which will be composed of individuals from marginalized communities, with specific focus on migrants. The outreach will be done over 5 field visits per month, reaching 300 migrants per month, over 9 months.SIDC, aims to collaborate with members of the migrant sector taskforce to conduct a screening and awareness fair. Through this fair, 300 migrants will have access to booths on protection, health, sexual health, legal, shelter, food security, and other relevant topics to be screened and gain knowledge on assistance pathways.Moreover,SIDC will train 80 frontliners on SIDC's topics with particular focus on migrants and risks they are exposed to. After which, a training guideline can be developed and validated by key stakeholders. Finally, SIDC aims to conduct a study on the engagement of migrants in risky behaviours given that SIDC's outreach efforts indicated migrants involved in substance use, sex work, injecting drug use, and unsafe sex.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Migrants are exposed to several security concerns, particularly those without documentation and who are illegally residing in Lebanon, therefore, staff members will be informed ahead of time on referral pathways to support migrants they come in contact with. Outreach workers have organizational cards with SIDC and MOPH logos for their safety. A training on PSEA is done to all previous and newly joining employees (full and part time).A hotline will be available to receive any immediate protection concerns the outreach staff may face from law enforcement, and inform beneficiaries that they may identify SIDC as a reference in the case they were exposed to any risk. Migrants including those with additional vulnerabilities, such as drugs users,injectors,sex workers, ex prisoners,PLHIV,& individuals from the LGBTIQ+ community are exposed to higher protection risks. Therefore, staff will provide the awareness, support, and required referrals.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

SIDC is committed to maintain and establish a system that allows beneficiaries (& others) the possibility to leave comments, feedback, complaints, & suggestions on our work in a secure,appropriate& accessible way,& allow complaints to receive a timely response.F&C mechanisms include regular service evaluations,complaint box within SIDC,and direct access to management for expressing any complaints needed. At the right up stage of the project, beneficiaries are involved through expressing their needs during the outreach that SIDC conducted and previous awareness sessions,contributing to service evaluations,& activity implementation.Community involvement takes place as community members will be acting as key informants to “hotspots” where the outreach activity can take place and they will be implementing the awareness themselves,preparing material,&they will take part in the study.Focus group discussions and satisfaction surveys are conducted, based on which activity within this appeal.
