Project Summary

The WASH Cluster has 61 WASH partners including 26 INGOs, 19 NNGOs, 6 UN agencies, 6 Educational institutions, and 4 local authorities. All partners operate at different levels in the WASH sector, contributing to several humanitarian planning initiatives such as HRP and WASH contingency plans.
UNICEF aims to ensure the maximum operational integration and technical harmonisation among these partners, which will be facilitated through the WASH Cluster. It also aims to enhance the WASH coordination system, mechanisms, and tools to ensure that: 1) the WASH humanitarian needs in Palestine are identified; 2) all the WASH partners develop their response projects to address these needs; and 3) all the proposed WASH responses satisfy the national and international standards and match national priorities.
Through this project, UNICEF, as Cluster Lead Agency, is proposing to implement several interventions that aim to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of the WASH humanitarian project cycle in Palestine: 1) needs and technical assessments to identify the most vulnerable groups with limited access to basic WASH services; 2) capacity building programs for WASH Partners to improve partners capabilities to address the identified WASH needs; 3) developing WASH coordination mechanisms and tools to ensure efficient communication between the Cluster partners and humanitarian country team; and 4) leading and coordinating the development of national and sub-national humanitarian response and contingency plans.
UNICEF will allocate three specialised staff members to handle all the tasks mentioned above at national and sub-national levels, under the supervision of the Chief of WASH and guidance of the Special Representative. These staff members will work directly with all WASH Cluster partners to ensure quality coordination of WASH interventions in Palestine, providing them with technical support.
The WASH Cluster coordination team will also carry out tasks related to monitoring, evaluation and reporting of the cluster partners planned, ongoing, and implemented activities.
It will also ensure the maximum accountability of WASH Cluster partners to the beneficiaries, local authorities, and the humanitarian country team. Moreover, the Cluster will support and encourage its partners to mainstream protection, gender, and socioeconomic vulnerabilities in their plans and responses to ensure the appropriateness of the assistance provided for all vulnerable groups.
Moreover, the WASH Cluster will track and monitor the availability of sufficient PSEA and feedbacks mechanisms and tools within the partners’ WASH HRP projects, besides the application of the relevant age, gender, and diversity mainstreaming in partners WASH plans and responses during 2023, and ensure that all the technical guidance, proposed and implemented WASH interventions satisfy the specific needs of these vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, more than 1.36 million Palestinians (650,000 in Gaza and 710,000 in the West Bank) suffer from limited access to WASH services. Their access to water, sanitation, solid waste services, and flood mitigation measures affects their quality of life and exposes them to several risks, most importantly health and protection risks. In Gaza Strip, the 2021 and 2022 escalations and the continued blockage enforced by the Israelis have negatively impacted the WASH sector; the restricted access to fuel and WASH infrastructure operation and maintenance materials and tools have reduced the capacity of 25 WASH service providers. As a result, 600 WASH facilities are affected. In the West Bank, the Israeli restrictions in Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem affect access to adequate WASH services for the Palestinian communities. The Israeli planning and building policy in Area C prevents Palestinian communities from developing their WASH infrastructures; in 2022, 20 demolition incidents against 36 WASH infrastructures and assets were recorded. Poverty, unemployment, and the national economic deterioration pose a threat to these vulnerable groups and will eventually affect the ability to properly apply WASH measures and practices. The low-income families in West Bank and Gaza strip are already adopting negative coping mechanisms to survive, such as reducing their water consumption, using unreliable and low-quality water resources, and dumping wastewater in open areas and agricultural lands. Moreover, since 2020 the situation was further exacerbated due to the outbreak of COVID-19. According to WHO, the provision of safe water, sanitation, and waste management are essential for preventing infectious disease outbreaks and protecting human health. Many households and institutions in Palestine have limited access to water and sanitation services and are not able to apply these basic and crucial recommended measures. Under the aforementioned circumstances, many national, international, and UN agencies along with the local authorities, are intervening to support these communities to improve their access to WASH basic services. According to its mandate, each agency and local authority deals with the WASH vulnerability based on its technical and operational capacities. UNICEF is taking the lead in coordinating all the WASH humanitarian responses being provided by different agencies. According to 2022 records, many of the cluster partners participate directly in developing and implementing the country’s humanitarian response plans, including HRP, inter-agency response plans, and contingency plans. In 2022, 30 WASH Cluster partners participated in developing the Gaza and the West Bank WASH contingency plan plans, and 35 partners participated in the 2022 HRP. Through the WASH Cluster, UNICEF is taking a leading role in coordinating partners’ participation in these plans and providing the required support to ensure maximum integration and harmonisation among its partners. Moreover, the WASH Cluster coordinates with its partners and the local authorities to ensure partners’ operation and technical harmonisation. The Cluster also supports the partners in identifying the most vulnerable groups, prioritising the targets, unifying the related technical standards, developing standard operating procedures (SOPs), and monitoring and tracking partners’ interventions. In order to utilise the partners’ full capacities, the Cluster is supporting its partners to develop their technical and operational capabilities to increase the efficiency of their responses. The WASH cluster has also provided age, gender, and disability (AGD) disaggregation of people in need in the WASH sector for its partners (Male 51%, female 49%, children 45%, elderly 3%, people with disability 2%) in addition to technical guidance on targeting with relevant age, gender and disability (AGD) disaggregation in the West Bank and Gaza, so that partners are able to provide the required AGD analysis for their proposed interventions.Moreover, the WASH Cluster has allocated gender focal point to support partners in the AGD mainstreaming during the planning and implementation of their WASH projects.

2 - Activities

In 2023, the WASH Cluster will operate based on its 6+1 core functions and will continue its effective engagement in the country’s inter-cluster platforms and coordination mechanisms such as national and sub-national Inter Cluster coordination Groups (ICCGs) and inter-agency assessments and plans. Moreover, the Cluster will adopt different vulnerability identification and response approaches, including emergency development nexus, linking relief, recovery, and development (LRRD), right, and needs-based approaches. The WASH Cluster in 2023 will coordinate and facilitate the following activities: - Activity 1: Updating WASH Cluster national/sub-national contingency plans: WASH Cluster will coordinate with its partners and the related public authorities to update the WASH contingency plans by updating the WASH partners’ response capacities in the West Bank and Gaza. Cluster and its partners will determine the most affected groups by these potential hazards, propose the appropriate WASH response, measure partners’ capacities to respond to these risks, and develop the related response triggers and SOPs. - Activity 2: Conducting/ facilitating WASH Cluster partners’ capacity building events: The WASH Cluster will evaluate the Cluster partners’ strategic, programming and technical capacities, thus, identifying their capabilities gaps and developing the related capacity building program. This program will include several topics, such as information management, monitoring and evaluation, advocacy and WASH programming. - Activity 3: Coordinating and providing Information Management (IM) support for the planning and implementation of WASH Cluster partners’ interventions: The Cluster will provide the required coordination, technical, and IM support to partners to ensure that all their WASH-related responses are designed and implemented according to the agreed national and international standards. The Cluster will provide the proper tools, including national and sub-national meetings, to ensure the integration of responses and eliminate any overlaps among partners WASH responses. - Activity 4: Monitoring the development, planning, and implementation of WASH partners HRP projects: using the new updated WASH Cluster monitoring mechanism, the WASH Cluster will keep tracking, recording, monitoring and reporting its partners’ performance and achievement during the planning, designing and implementation of WASH humanitarian responses. The WASH Cluster will also ensure that partners have sufficient resources to address issues related to maternal care such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services and ensures coordination with the PSEA focal point to timely address potential SEA concerns. - Activity 5: Developing the required WASH interventions’ technical and coordination standards and SOPs: WASH Cluster, with the support of the related technical working groups and the related national authorities, will develop unified technical standards and SOPs. These SOPs will ensure the technical harmonisation among partners and that all the provided WASH assistance satisfies the related national and international standards. - Activity 6: Support WASH Cluster partners’ programming in developing WASH response frameworks and road maps: WASH Cluster will cooperate with its partners to develop response frameworks in specific areas of interventions andtopics such as WASH in schools, WASH in HCFs, WASH advocacy and wash triple nexus. WASH cluster will facilitate the development of such frameworks and road maps based on updated assessments, and analysis to ensure prioritising the most vulnerable groups with the relevant WASH interventions. - Activity 7: Leading, facilitating, and conducting WASH need and impact assessment: the WASH Cluster will keep monitoring the WASH sector status in the West Bank and Gaza, and will assess any urgent needs, context change, extreme conditions that affect the most vulnerable groups accessibility to adequate WASH services. Moreover, the Cluster will update its HNO by collecting the related data from primary and secondary resources, which will be later shared by the Cluster along with evidence-based updates and information about the WASH vulnerability in oPt, this ensures that the decision was made with the collaboration and agreement of HCT, government, and international levels. - Activity 8: Coordinate for the mobilisation of the required resources for implementing the 2023 HRP WASH interventions: by developing a ranking system of the proposed WASH HRP interventions, the WASH Cluster will coordinate with its partners to ensure that the required WASH assistance will prioritise the most vulnerable groups. - Activity 9: Support WASH Cluster partners in mainstreaming protection, gender, GBV, PSEA, and PWD in their WASH projects through the development of protection action plan and gender, PWD mainstreaming checklist.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages. Cluster partners have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussions and will be continually involved in its implementation. Channels of communication for feedback, complaints, and information sharing will be established through satisfaction surveys and recording goods of services delivered to the end user so that assistance is delivered according to the principles of “do no harm”. UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Therefore the WASH Cluster will ensure that consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through WASH Cluster partners joining training and community interventions.
