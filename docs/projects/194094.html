Project Summary

This project aims to focus on the provision of emergency nutrition services for prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition amongst vulnerable groups of people (including children, women) escabated bythe ongoing fighting in Sudan. This is aligned to the Nutrition Sector strategic objective 1(Life saving preventive and protection intervention focusing among under-two) and Sector objective 2(Treatment of Severe and moderate among under-fives and PLWs ). Khartoum , North , West, South Darfur States . Our proposed treatment (both out-patient treatment and in-patient) of severe and moderate acute will target children 6-59months and PLWs) in prioritized localities and women. The project will scale up access to quality integrated life-saving nutrition preventive and protection minimum package services; Improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treat severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-fives, pregnant and lactating women; and improve understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition through evidence generating assessments and inter-sectoral analysis to guide planning and decision making. The nutrition services provided will include nutritional assessment of children under 5 years, pregnant and Lactating Women, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and other nutrition-related counselling, early years stimulation services for infants/young children, diet management and lifestyle modification for pregnant, lactating women, older people and patient with chronic diseases, distribution of IFA, Vitamin-A, deworming tablets in addition to SBCC activities for positive mindset change. Multi-sectoral nutrition sensitive interventions (WASH, Health, Food Security, social protection, education, protection) shall be integrated and ensure synergy and collaboration with sector leads aimed at strengthening holistic approach for addressing multi-sector dimensions affecting food, nutrition and health and social protection security and safety of children and women for improved nutrition outcomes among target population (Vulnerable Residents, IDPs, Refugees, Returnees mainly). The quality of and access to integrated nutrition services for at-risk groups will be improved by strengthening local capacity and linkages for provision of quality nutrition services and supporting community-based nutrition services at scale to the reach the last child in last miles of this project. The project areas' outcome will be more equitable, accessible, and higher-quality-high impact nutrition services for most at at-risk groups such as poor mothers and children.

1 - Needs

The current ongoing conflict has induced displacement of households, leading to increased vulnerability to malnutrition, increased inflation, reduced access to food, lack of quality water and poor hygiene situation, and increased risk of infections and communicable diseases such as measles. According to the revised HRP People In Need of Nutrition services are 4.6M with approximately 2.5m (50%) People Targeted and this requires a funding of approximately $350m of which $159.1M is needed to scale up life-saving assistance. The deterioration of the nutrition situation has led to an estimated about 35% increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hotspot areas, a 15 percent increase in IDP hosting states and a 10 percent increase in other localities. Interruption of nutrition monitoring and reporting impacting on the understanding of the evolving nutrition situation in most parts of the country particularly in the hotpot areas. The revised projects are estimated for seven months from June to December 2023 with the nutrition sector expected to scale up maternal and young child nutrition and scale up Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices among under-five children, prioritizing new hotspot and IDPs areas.HDPO will scale up early detection and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women Scale up and initiate responses in new emerging hotspot areas including IDP hosting states, focusing on the youngest to prevent increased morbidity and mortality risks

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under the current prevailing emergency context the project is designed to deliver three activities/outputs which include to: Scale up access to quality integrated Life-saving preventive and protection intervention focusing among under-two and treatment of Severe and moderate among under-fives and PLWs with special focus on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF/E) practices in emergencies through IYCF/E message dissemination of which the key activities will include conducting counseling of Pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months on MYCN as part of the breastfeeding initiative promotion and the celebration of the World Breast Feeding Week 1st-7th August will be part of the activities as well as Complementary food supplementation with MNP. Formation of Mother to Mother and Father to Father support groups. Special focus will be geared towards the strong messaging on the implementation, monitoring, and control of the International Code of Marketing of Breastmilk Substitutes which tends to be prevalent during emergencies. Further deworming of under-five children (12-59 months) and 2 doses of Vitamin A supplementation will be administered per child targeting while for Pregnant women during antenatal Iron and Folic Acid will be given to supplement the loss in the iron intake. Above all active case findings and mass MUAC screenings will be routinely carried out by the HDPO International network of volunteers who will also train the mothers on the family MUAC use as to enhance early detection and referral of malnutrition among children under 5 and Pregnant and lactating Women by mother MUAC (household level)There will also be Food Based Prevention of Acute Malnutrition (FBPM) (increasing access to nutrition foods) for 6-23mo and PLW through the distribution of CSB++ and as part of early detection of malnutrition through Growth Monitoring and promotion at health facilities for all Pregnant and Lactating Women and children 0-23monthsFor the sector objective 2 that is the curative component that involves the treatment of Severe and moderate among under-fives and PLWs will start with active case findings and referrals like above and other key activities will include Outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without medical complications for children 6-59mo where _____ boys and girls under five years with severe acute malnutrition without complication newly admitted for treatment in OTPs and treatment in Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP); and ____SAM children with medical complications supported with transport; ____caretakers of children with SAM with medical complication supported with food costs at SC; 2 SC sites rehabilitated and further a 5 days refresher training for Health workers will be conducted on inpatient management of SAM (SC)Other cross-cutting activities will include the integration of nutrition in Health, WASH, educational and Food Security/Livelihood as well as protection to address the underlying causes of malnutrition from the nutrition-sensitive lens. Improvement on understanding of the nutrition situation in prioritized localities with critical levels of acute malnutrition through evidence-generating rapid assessments and inter-sectoral analysis to guide planning and decision-making and monitoring visits conducted by the teams in the respective states. Capacity building through trainings and refresher trainings for HDPO and Government staff on management of SAM, MAM with and without medical complications and reporting. There will be enhancement of nutrition facilities with feedback and complaint mechanisms;

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Community-based needs assessments, community action plans, and direct use of funds by the beneficiaries are the basis of the project design. This is in contrast to projects that regard communities simply as recipients of nutrition services or as participants in health education interventions. The project will be implemented in one year, from 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2023. The first quarter-year activities will comprise recruitment of staff; review of baseline data for monitoring and evaluation; detailed project planning; and training of community facilitators and workshops for key implementing agents.
