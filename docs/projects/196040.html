Project Summary

"Arannayk Foundation (AF) will provide skill development training to Rohingya refugees to enhance their livelihoods and self-reliance in cooperation with the Government of Bangladesh, LSD, UNHCR, and other stakeholders. Beneficiaries will receive training in industrial sewing machine operation, cosmetology, and general electric and machine mechanics, which will help them with skill development in designated trades and the opportunity to be self-resilient while both at camps and return to Rakhine state. After coordination with LSDs, the beneficiaries will be selected from all blocks of 4E except G, all blocks of 8W and 20 Ext. As a part of the vocational training, the program will develop training modules on the identified trades aligned to international standards e.g., Myanmar National Qualification Framework (MNQF) and/or ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF) standards. Based on the trade, each of the vocational training courses would be 360 hours or more in duration, which will comprise both theoretical and practical sessions, and the project would arrange the provision of formal assessment of the training participants. Successful candidates would receive completion certificates. The certification would help the project participants in pursuing their future education/training, as well as in negotiating higher wages both in Camp and in Myanmar as skilled manpower.
Moreover, vulnerable host community beneficiaries will receive training, in-kind support, and job placement, resulting from stepping ahead toward resilience and self-dependent. After coordination with LSDs, the beneficiaries will be selected from all unions of Ukhia upazila and will receive training in industrial sewing machine operation, cosmetology, and general electric and machine mechanics based on the Bangladesh Government approved TVET curriculum. AF proposed activities are based on the communities’ feedback from the targeted communities and local authorities and will be accountable to them for the decisions made during the project implementation. The intervention was designed to ensure a long-term positive impact on the livelihood and skill development of the targeted beneficiaries and to increase market accessibility of the neighboring households and communities not directly targeted through the project. This community-based approach and market linkage assistance will ensure the acceptance and sustainability of the AF project.
Beneficiary selection will be based on vulnerability criteria (i.e. Female headed HH, presence of a disabled person in the HH, elderly-headed HH, HH affected by disaster recurrence, level of poverty, etc.) for all trades. Females will be favored for all the trades, specifically nursing, industrial sewing machine operation, and cosmetology, as they face more barriers to accessing training and livelihood opportunities. Youth groups will be prioritized simultaneously to ensure sustainability and the best effective results.
AF has a well-established Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and whistleblowing policy in Ukhia to receive beneficiary feedback regarding the assistance provided. The MEAL team will ensure that the appropriate mechanisms are in place through which the affected populations can measure the adequacy of the intervention and address their concerns. In case of protection needs, the case will be referred to relevant protection actors working in the area."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) in June 2022 revealed that high dependency on humanitarian assistance and zero access to livelihood and income-generating activities make the Rohingya households highly vulnerable, compelling them to consume below the minimum expenditure basket (MEB), two third of them relied on less preferred or less expensive food for at least one day, 79% are indebted condition and thus as a part of blanket program skill development will be the most decent solution to make the Rohingya population self-reliant both in the present scenario and if they got the opportunity to back at Rakhine state.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The proposed project by Arannayk Foundation will ensure an integrated approach during the camp selection process; suggestions from LSDS, Government officials, and counterparts stalk taking will be considered to avoid the same work in the same area. Moreover, AF will consider improving working relations with Government agencies and CiC and building up an operative bridging among Government officials, service providers, and beneficiaries to ensure the best use of resources in a coordinated way for the betterment of beneficiaries in a sustainable manner.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AF is in the process of becoming a member of the PSEA network. AF is fully compliant with safeguarding communities and stakeholders, having relevant policies in place to ensure safety and dignity, and apply to do no harm principles to the communities concerned. AF ensures meaningful access of all concerned beneficiaries and stakeholders and project staff/volunteers to the opportunities/facilities of the project and the organization itself, giving due consideration to AGD-related tailored risks. Moreover, the location, distance, and access routes to the distribution points will be made accessible to all community members, including the most vulnerable groups. AF will establish mechanisms to prioritize (e.g., priority lanes for the big que) those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, and pregnant and lactating women, including alternate collector mechanisms.

3.2 - Gender

Likewise, the other projects of the Arannayk Foundation Gender and youth issues will be considered for the food security sector for host communities. The project would ensure the participation of girls and women in the home garden, livestock, and IGA activities as they face more barriers to accessing training and resilient food security opportunities. Youth groups will be prioritized for the veterinary agent, local service provider, awareness sessions, and ERT formation to ensure sustainability and the best effective results. In addition to that, the participation of the members of families and persons with disabilities and LGBTQI would be prioritized. The project would have a strong team of GESI (Gender Equality and Social Inclusion) and a gender mainstreaming team to ensure the participation of women and persons with disabilities. AF will conduct periodical community-based inclusive approaches (e.g., PRA) and gender analysis to understand and identify the potential gaps in LSDS.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AF has safeguarded its people (participants, employees) in the first place by making their safety a primary consideration already at the level of the design of programs and enterprises. The prevention of sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, intimidation, violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect, and exploitation is, as far as possible, built into the work practices. Where the design and its implementation cannot reduce risks sufficiently, AF has established secondary safeguarding mechanisms, like selecting and training personnel, supervising and monitoring, establishing multiple reporting channels, investigation, and punishment. A dedicated PSEA committee is formed in AF, having a focal person to deal with all cases, including child safeguarding issues. AF has the mechanism to receive feedback from the children as part of accountability to them, maintaining separate complaint boxes and providing child-friendly information and avenues for reporting.

3.4 - Diversity

"AF supports its project participants by following humanitarian standards and principles. Therefore, prioritizing staff diversity and having a culturally relaxed and inclusive work environment is an integral part of its program. The project will also include Gender Diverse Population (GDP) considering their needs and interests, and training modules will be developed and facilitated accordingly. To ensure diversity, AF will be disaggregated all collected data by age, sex, and other diversity considerations based on the context; it will follow the participatory methodologies at each stage of the operations management cycle to incorporate the capacities and priorities of women, men, girls, and boys of diverse backgrounds; AF will approach to communicating with them, that are appropriate and accessible to all groups in any given community, and will establish and promote feedback and response systems, including for confidential complaints."

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"AF always ensures its accountability to the affected communities and keeps them at the center of AF’s work, decisions, and actions. AF will ensure two-way dialogue with the community, even during crisis responses. AF determines demands for information from the community and their presentation styles/formats in consultation with the clientele communities and accordingly establishes the information channels. Further, the AF implementation teams work closely with the PQ teams, which ensures appropriate tracking and monitoring of activities being delivered to the beneficiaries. For this purpose, AF uses various tools like complaint books/boxes/channels, feedback, and response mechanisms such as establishing a help desk, post-distribution monitoring (PDM), hotline, feedback hub, participatory review sessions, etc. AF recognizes that all relevant groups have a voice, including men, women, children, older persons, minority cultural groups, and people with disabilities.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"AF already has a contingency plan to respond to natural disasters. AF provides basic Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training to project staff, including community volunteers, to respond effectively to an emergency crisis situation. They also provide first aid training to all staff and volunteers as a part of their skill development. Arannayk Foundation, being mandated to conserve forest and biodiversity, is willing to adopt an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which will apply a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation, and will reduce environmental degradation in AF’s project implementation mechanism. "
