Project Summary

Ethiopia has a significant landmine problem with data from Ethiopia’s Landmine Impact Survey (2004) stating that 261 hazard areas remain within Ethiopian territory, with over 99% of this area lying in Somali Region. Here, landmines were laid close to, and often across, the international borders with Somaliland and south-central Somalia during the Ogaden War between Ethiopia and Somalia (1977-78) and Somali Civil War (1986-91).
The presence of landmines, especially anti-vehicle mines along the border, prevents improvements to the existing road networks, the amelioration of state security and border infrastructure (customs points, livestock inspection, quarantine areas, etc.), as well as proper demarcation and policing of the Ethiopia-Somaliland border. A lack of redevelopment of roads in rural areas means that drought-affected communities in Somali Region where no road development has taken place are significantly less well-equipped to deal with the effects of drought, leading to increased conflicts over scarce resources such as water and productive agricultural or pastoral lands.
Mine action activities, including the resurvey and demarcation of hazardous areas to present a more accurate picture of remaining landmine contamination, will contribute to increased social mobility, improved access to natural and social resources and enhanced resilience against climate and economic shocks. In addition, HALO will work with its national NGO partners to conduct needs assessments in communities impacted by ongoing community conflicts over resources, as well as amongst its own female employees to identify the most prevalent knowledge and skill gaps where we can have an impact.
The project aims to increase stability and security in the Ethiopia-Somaliland border region through the identification of hazardous areas and community conflict prevention. It will enable the recruitment, training and deployment of Ethiopian nationals to join the first ever humanitarian non-technical survey and EORE operations in the Ethiopia-Somaliland border region in Somali Region, Ethiopia. These mine action activities will also enable the facilitation of community-based projects conducted through local NGO partners to empower local women and reduce resource-based community conflicts in the region.
Specific Outputs:
• Recruitment and training of 18 Ethiopian staff (50-50 male/female) to be employed as survey team members, operations management, drivers, administrators, logisticians, mechanics and security)
• Deployment of two survey teams to identify and demarcate 100 confirmed hazardous areas within 12 months.
• Reduction in community-based explosive ordnance accidents through conducting 300 Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) sessions to 6,000 local people in mine-impacted communities within 12 months.
• Enhanced stability and social cohesion through needs assessments in 20 communities located in the Ethiopia-Somaliland border region and the implementation of conflict prevention initiatives in 10 communities within 12 months.

1. - Projects activities

Non-Technical Survey: NTS is the primary means for providing the evidence to support decisions to deploy technical assets. It is a process which is done in collaboration with various informants from those communities directly impacted by the minefield, ensuring that it is fully participatory and directed by the needs of the community. Resources directed to NTS will ensure that in future mine clearance activities will be directed to clearing areas with direct evidence of minelaying. Additionally, it will begin the necessary process of refining the landmine contamination data currently held by the Ethiopian MoD through the process of cancellation of areas where no evidence of landmines exists, enabling a more accurate depiction of remaining hazards and the resources that will be required to clear these areas. This will not only facilitate the prioritization and deployment of future operations, but will more importantly enable the clear marking of landmine contaminated areas around communities, creating a visible barrier between areas considered ‘safe’ and ‘unsafe’. All survey data will be submitted to regional and federal Ethiopia authorities via the Ministry of Defence in order to support the development and security activities of the administration. Explosive Ordnance Risk Education: The EORE team will provide risk education sessions intended to promote safe behaviour amongst recipients and to reduce the potential incidence of injury or death. This is particularly important for vulnerable groups who may be unfamiliar with the threat in a particular geographical area and unsure of what actions to take if they find something suspicious. Sessions will be targeted and tailored to vulnerable sectors of the population to ensure the greatest efficacy. EORE sessions are typically carried out in schools, churches or mosques or other communal areas. They are composed of both interactive and visual presentations to attendees and the provision of leaflets and other literature. They are disaggregated by age and sex in order to ensure that all presentations are tailored to attendees, the different social roles they embody and the resulting risks to explosive ordnance that they face.

The project will be conducted under the authority of the Department for Mine Action, which sits within the Ethiopian Ministry of Defence and which is directly supported by the United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS). HALO will coordinate an annual work plan through both the Department for Mine Action, the Somali Region Government and the Agency for Civil Society Organizations (ACSO) which monitors the work of all NGOs in-country. The project’s annual work plan will be drawn up in conjunction with federal and regional authorities for inclusion into national mine action, development and state security planning.Regular coordination meetings will be facilitated in Addis Ababa, in Somali Region capital Jijiga and in Gashamo District where HALO will initially seek to recruit staff in order to strengthen relations with district authorities and respect clan dynamics. These will enable an ongoing dialogue with national government partners and establish a clear prioritisation process, giving due consideration to security and development priorities of the Ethiopian state.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

During the non-technical survey of suspected hazardous areas and facilitation of explosive ordnance risk education sessions, HALO seeks information from a wide variety of informants, including local community leaders, household members, victims of accidents, and local land owners. For survey, this information not only allows HALO to clearly define the area of contamination, but to quantify its impact on the community and its different members. Information is also collected on the disaggregated (by age and sex) number of direct and indirect beneficiaries impacted by each minefield. Upon completion of NTS, the community actively approves the agreed hazardous area in accordance with HALO staff. For EORE, information on participants is also disaggregated by age and sex, whilst sessions are tailored to meet the needs of high risk, vulnerable groups within each community. Community members are provided with full contact details of HALO staff to pass on any additional feedback or concerns which may help shape the project at this initial stage. Communities are also provided with contact information of HALO’s community liaison point of contact so that they have a separate feedback chain within the organization who can be contacted should the community wish to raise an issue about HALO’s work or personnel. HALO also conducts individual socioeconomic Household Surveys and Community Surveys (including focus group discussions) within all mine-impacted communities in order to provide richer layers of data on demographics and consequences of living in close proximity to landmines and explosive ordnance. This data is collected by both male and female HALO personnel in order to ensure greater comfort and access to community participants. Following both facilitation of both survey and risk education sessions, HALO conducts internal reviews in order extract any lessons learned that can then be rolled into future project design.
