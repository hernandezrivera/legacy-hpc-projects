Project Summary

The Darna Al Aman project aims to support the safe return of the civilian population of Raqqa through the provision of integrated services of protection and primary healthcare. The protection component includes the enhancement of the space for women and girls already situated in Raqqa Al Hilal Maternity Hospital. Moreover, it includes the setting up of a child-friendly space in the same location, thus allowing better access to integrated case management services. A key element of the action will be the integrated training plan for local medical and non-medical staff through modules on the protection of minors, protection of women, gender-based violence and mental health, integrated with health services already provided.
Although, the project also plans to strengthen the decentralized health system of the area, providing basic health services in the surrounding area of the city of Raqqa. Finally, the project plans to develop integrated awareness campaigns on health and protection issues with a community-based approach.

4 - Needs

The volatile security conditions, the risk of sexual violence and kidnapping, together with the excessive distance from services and a complex socio-cultural context where female participation is reduced, represented the main obstacles to the implementation of protection programs targeting the most vulnerable subjects in Raqqa. These factors severely limit access to services, pointing out the need for a major engagement of the urban area. According to data collected by UPP with the UPP project funded by UNFPA, early marriage, psychological violence, rape and honour crime are the most frequent forms of GBV. There is also a gap in accessing medical care for GBV survivors and the need for female medical personnel trained in Clinical Management of Rape.Furthermore, CMR services are not always accessible within 72 - 120 hours after being subjected to violence, the crucial time window according to WHO international standards for providing life-saving care. Awareness-raising initiatives aimed at empowering children on key issues of protection and support for parents are also needed; although, there is also a lack of a child-friendly space and of case management services for children who survived exploitation and abuse with particular attention to unaccompanied minors and child labour. Finally, it is necessary to strengthen psychosocial support for children at risk. In Raqqa, the psychological trauma caused by the conflict afflicts many families and can cause long-lasting stress. That is known as toxic stress and adversely affects children and adolescents to cause behavioural changes, disease, hormonal changes and neuropsychiatric disorders. According to information collected by UPP, the use of corporal punishment and psychological abuse by parents is frequent. Hence the need for parenting support to spread stress management techniques and promote positive discipline models.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to improve the capacity of the WGSS in Raqqa AL Hilal Maternity Hospital, while piloting the integration of child protection services through a new CFS in the same area. The WGSS will continue its activity of Awareness Raising and PSS with the increased support of technical staff.During the first month of the project, a Protection / GBV expert will conduct an evaluation review with Focus Group to determine the modalities of the activities, and develop a community-based strategy.PSS counselors and social workers/caseworkers will be recruited to support both the WGSS and Al Hilal Maternity Hospital. An expert mental health consultant (Mental Health and Psychosocial Support -MHPSS) will provide support and training together with the specialists in GBV for the development of a curriculum/calendar of meetings to be offered to parents, women and girls. The Prevention Officers support the campaigns already started with the Darna2 project with the Community Health Workers (CHWs) to increase prevention and access to services in the community.Moreover, a Child Protection expert will train the staff selected by the local communities during the first months, assessing to determine the CFS strategy. Besides, meetings and Focus Groups / Key Informant Interviews will be held with representatives of the local stakeholders and specialised committees dealing with children and young people.A CFS Officer/team leader in coordination with the WGSS team leader will take care of space management according to the minimum standards mentioned above. In a second phase, caseworkers and psychologists in modality will give support in the field regarding psycho-social activities and referrals with Prevention Officers. Child-friendly activities with information on health, protection and rights will also be implemented.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

According to the Syria HNO ‘19 report, 501,503 people are in need of protection services in Raqqa governorate. According to the same report, gender-based violence (GBV) and in particular sexual harassment, early and forced marriage, domestic violence and IPV continue to negatively impact the lives of women and girls in Syria. The lack of psychosocial support services and specialized GBV life-saving services continues to represent the major gap in Syria and in particular in Raqqa where there are few specialized protection actors and these have limited capacity to receive cases. The analysis of the Whole of Syria GBV Area of Responsibility (2019, UNFPA and Gender AoR) confirms that the country faces "new realities in which gender dynamics have been significantly altered" and that GBV has a disproportionate impact on women and girls. Also according to Syria HNO 2019, 222,310 children need assistance in the Raqqa Governorate. These are at risk of violence, alcohol and drug abuse, school drop out, child labour or military recruitment, with potentially negative long-term effects. In Raqqa, out-of-school children and unaccompanied and separated children are more vulnerable to violence and the most common protection risks for them are early/forced marriage and child labour. Furthermore, there are limited child-friendly spaces and alternative care facilities. The conflict then caused a high number of child and female-headed households as well as the deterioration of social support networks.Finally, data collected by UPP in Raqqa between Dec. ‘18 and Apr. ‘19 confirmed low awareness of health and protection risks among returnees. The risks identified are linked both to external factors such as land mines, ERW and IEDs, food and water contamination, high crime rate and generalized violence, GBV and domestic violence, low health awareness during pregnancy.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During 2018 and 2019, security alarms continued in the city of Raqqa, and if the security conditions worsened, activities could be suspended. UPP has established adequate security procedures for its offices in Iraq and Syria in coordination with the INSO operator safety information system. Furthermore, information exchange systems have been activated with other NGOs and the UN agency and a specific hibernation and evacuation protocol. Also, for operations in both Syria and Iraq, UPP refers to the internally adapted manual "Security Risk Management NGO Approach".The territory of the NES falls into the red level and is therefore not accessible to Italian operators. All the operators were then trained with ad hoc courses for security in crisis contexts (with private companies offering NGO services) and are equipped with satellite phones and GPS. Risk assessments are carried out regularly for each mission to conducted out in risk areas. UPP security department is in charge of monitoring activities and ensures that information are regularly shared with the competent authorities and the International Coalition. All staff receive regular training courses on UXO/Mine.In the event of deterioration of security in the city of Raqqa, expatriate project staff will not have access to the town. Management will take place from UPP's Amuda and Dohuk offices. In any case, the project staff in normal conditions will be able to move on Raqqa only during the day. Exceptional evacuation plans have been studied to deal with the hypothesis of military escalation conducted by any of the multiple actors present on the field.
