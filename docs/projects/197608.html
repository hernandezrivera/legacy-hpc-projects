Project Summary

According to HNO 2021, among the 12.43 million people in need of health services in 2021, 1.41 million are children under five years old, while 3.32 million are women of reproductive age (WRA), including 498,480 women who are expected to become pregnant in 2021. An additional 536,753 older people are likely to require health services, as well as those with early-onset non-communicable diseases (NCD), which is estimated to account for 45% of all mortality in Syria.
The proposed project aims to respond to pediatric primary health care needs, aligned with nutritional services available in the form of community mobilization and outreach awareness for children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The continuation of the primary health care services, including health care services at static and mobile clinics levels, outpatient consultation to CBAs and children, dissemination of health promotion messages for health, immunization, and COVID-19, are key components. These health facilities (static and mobile) will also contribute to strengthening the health system and improving the nutrition status of very vulnerable populations in northern Syria at a challenging time with the specter of the COVID-19 threatening the already fragile health systems.

3 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, the impact of the prolonged crisis on people, services, and access in 2020 has resulted in a 21% increase in the number of Syrians in need of humanitarian assistance, from 11 million in 2020 to 13.4 million in 2021. Approximately 10 million (75 %) of the 13.4 million people in need (PIN) are women and children, including approximately 2.3 million females and males with disabilities aged 12 and up. Children with and without disabilities account for 44 % of those in need in Syria (6,1 million people). More than 600,000 children are estimated to be stunted, putting their physical and cognitive development at risk, and nearly 90,000 children under the age of five are acutely malnourished in Syria. 12.43 million people necessitate health needs analysis, which is based on the 2021 HNO for Syria and health database from Bahar health facilities with its different levels of health care throughout Afrin and Azaz districts. The largest proportion of total consultations was for gynecology-obstetric-RH services, which accounted for 28% of all purpose-consultations, followed by communicable disease at 26% and non-communicable diseases at 22%. The most common health problems are the following and were responsible for 81% of all NCDs: Kidney disease, DM, and HTN accounted for 35%, 25%, and 21%, respectively, which is estimated to account for 45 % of all mortality.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will last for 2*12 months phase, aiming to improve risk communication and community engagement in Azaz district through outreach activities. By using community-based health workers, engaging key influencers, and building local capacities, outreach activities can be more effectively mitigate risks to more efficiently establish the authority and trust required to rapidly mount responses. Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) refers to the processes and approaches to systematically consult, engage and communicate with communities who are at risk, or whose practices affect risk.the aforementioned objectives are going to be achieved through 8 CHWs teams of 2 members =16 community health workers (at least 50% of the CHWs are females) who will primarily target both IDP and host communities in Azzaz district, Aghtrin subdistrict The CHWs team will be based in Afrin clinic: HeRams code: B0203002 as a work-based station and they will be conducting the outreach activities in Aghtrin subdistrict

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk: Lack of privacy for patients, especially women and children, during treatments. Mitigation: Treatment in separated rooms and having female staff present.- Risk: Lack of hygienical standards and thus the transfer of communicable diseases to other patients (vulnerable groups) and staff. Mitigation: Maintenance of hygiene protocols and close follow-up of any violations against the protocols. - Risk: Lack of access to adequate health care, which includes physical, mental and reproductive health care and psychosocial support for people dealing with the effects of violence, including trauma and post-traumatic stress disorder. Mitigation: Use referral systems if health care cannot be provided and awareness sessions on how to deal with victims/ survivors of crime, violence and abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying the related procedures. Bahar ensures that the field team attends PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal point's responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of the online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complaints pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor and report if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The number of Covid-19 infected cases will decrease. Risk 1: Covid-19 and other pandemics and infections pressure the health system.Mitigation: Bahar team commits to using PPEs, makes frequent PCR tests (Bahar has many health centers in the area and a specialized Covid-19 isolation center, these facilities will provide service to our team), social distances in offices, commitment to Covid-19 SOPs by the programs teams. Bahar encourages teams - especially in winter when seasonal diseases like flu and throat aches are common - to look after their health extensively. Assumption: The fuel price will remain stable throughout the project duration.Risk 2: Increase of fuel prices and changes in the supply chain.Mitigation: Storing fuel amount in safe warehouses or contracting a certain fuel provider to keep us an emergency stock of diesel in his gas stations Assumption: The cross-border mechanism will be extended, or at least a closure will have no impact on the operation. Risk 3: Cross border movement restriction (goods and people)Mitigation: Contingency stock would be stored for 3 months of activities
