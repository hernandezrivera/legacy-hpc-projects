Project Summary

The return of TDPs in Tribal districts of KP province is still continuing. The health facilities in return areas of Kurram and Orkzai tribal districts are not fully functional and do not providing required PHC, MNCH services and NCDs complete. Returned communities are travelling tens kilometers to avail life saving PHC and MNCH services in their original areas. As per FATA vulnerability assessment report 2017, 64% of the population in FATA Agency and FR Areas faced difficulties in accessing health care facilities. The mean distance travelled by the population to the nearest health facility in FATA Agency is 7 km and in Frontier Region (FR) Areas is 19.5 km. Out of the women who had a child in the last three years, 24% reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services during their pregnancy. A significant proportion (40%) reported delivery at home, mostly with the assistance of traditional midwife (Dai). Around 20% of the households from FATA and 15% from FR areas reported that their eligible children were not vaccinated during routine EPI. Around half of the respondents (51% in FATA and 46% in FR areas) reported that during the last three months, they were unable to seek medical help even it was needed. Key reasons include the unavailability of relevant staff, service and financial constraint.
Over 80% of health infrastructures in return areas are severely damaged by the crisis and remain non-functional. Many of the health staff who were displaced especially the female health workers have not resumed work. This has negatively influenced the provision of Reproductive Health, Mother & Child Health care services in FATA and FR Areas. The situation of health services in Kurram and Orkzai tribal districts is more vulnerable due to sectarian issues in both tribal districts. The return of TDPs in upper Orkzai is still on going and also expected after the winter season.
HelpAge International already completed three projects in Kurram and Orkzai agency funded by UNOCHA and German donors related to increase access of vulnerable older people and Persons with disabilities for health, livelihood and specific needs.
HelpAge International is proposing following activities for returned people in Kurram and Orkzai tribal districts:
1 Repair and rehabilitate two health facilities in each tribal district; Kurram and Orkzai of KP province for making it inclusive for older women, older women, PWDs and other vulnerable groups.
2 Provide support in filling staff gap
3 Provision of essential medicines, drugs, medical equipment and supplies
4 Provision of awareness and safe delivery kits for safe deliveries
5 Establish laboratory services for basic laboratory tests and diagnosis
6 Establish referral system
7 Establish Infection prevention and wastage disposal system
8 Train health facility staff on MNCH, BLS, ACLS, NCDs and geriatric care
Through these activities, we'll ensure the access of all returned people to PHC, preventive MNCH and NCD services including older people, persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating mothers and other vulnerable groups.

Needs

As per Preliminary findings from "FATA Vulnerability Assessment 2017", conducted in August/September 2017 revealed that 64% of the population in FATA Agency and FR Areas faced difficulties in accessing health care facilities. The mean distance travelled by the population to the nearest health facility in FATA Agency is 7 km and in Frontier Region (FR) Areas is 19.5 km. Out of the women who had a child in the last three years, 24% reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services during their pregnancy. A significant proportion (40%) reported delivery at home, mostly with the assistance of traditional midwife (Dai). Around 20% of the households from FATA and 15% from FR areas reported that their eligible children were not vaccinated during routine EPI. Around half of the respondents (51% in FATA and 46% in FR areas) reported that during the last three months, they were unable to seek medical help even it was needed. Key reasons include the unavailability of relevant staff, service and financial constraint. Over 80% of health infrastructures in return areas are severely damaged by the crisis and remain non-functional. Many of the health staff who were displaced especially the female health workers have not resumed work. This has negatively influenced the provision of Reproductive Health, Mother & Child Health care services in FATA and FR Areas. In addition, only 11% and 22% respectively of responders in FATA and FR Areas have ever been visited by Lady Health Workers during the last three months. A significant caseload opts to visit health facilities outside of FATA. This trend impacts negatively on the finances of the household and practiced by families who cannot afford to meet the expenses. Access to safe water and sanitation is also poor with 45% of the population in return areas relying on unsafe drinking water. 36% Returnees have no toilets and practice open defecation. The presence of water-borne diseases is prevalent with 33% household reported diarrhea in FATA. In August 2017, an outbreak of dengue fever was reported from Bara Tehsil in Khyber Agency and FR Kohat with over more than 107,283 suspected cases and over 22,300 cases that tested positive for dengue fever as of 7th November 2017. Note that this outbreak is still ongoing and has been declared as a Grade 1 emergency according to the internal WHO grading system. Mental health problem is a big issue with [16-39%] particularly females reported mental health and psycho social issues (depression, anxiety post-traumatic stress disorder).HelpAge International also conducted assessment with its partners in November 2017. The findings of the assessment are 70% lack of health services along with non availability of health staff in BHUs, THHs and dispensaries reflecting towards less satisfaction with the quality and the level of services provided to communities. 70% of females in these areas cannot avail essential reproductive health (delivery) services due to the non availability of female doctors and trained relevant paramedical staff in the areas assessed. The existing staff of health facilities needs capacity building training in MNCH, Advance Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) and Basic Life Support (BLS). It was also noted that basic laboratory services were not available in the health facilities. There were no facilities to admit patients for observations and intervention procedures; this is another factor for accessing specialized health services such as reproductive health. Although 67% respondents stated that the stairs/steps are available in health facilities but without railing or grab bars along with non availability of ramps. Similarly, there was no Psychosocial support available for the elderly and PWDs in the health facilities. In all cases the most vulnerable persons are pregnant and lactating mothers, elders and persons with disabilities. Directorate of Health services FATA also endorsing the same needs in health sector.
