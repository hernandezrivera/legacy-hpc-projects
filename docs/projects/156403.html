Project Summary

Providing NFI and shelter opportunities or rehabilitation to the most vulnerable in Northern Syria even if they are IDPs or those vulnerable from the host community as emergencies or seasonal assistance such as the winterization activities.
The project has been designed based on the assessment that has been done by the end of the year 2018 by Violet and its partners and the needs of the people in Northern Syria for the basic humanitarian assistance especially the NFI and shelter or even the cash provision which contributes significantly in the provision of shelter and NFI for the newly IDPs.
The project aims to provide emergency response by NFI and shelter for the IDPs as following:
- 1000 Full NFI for 1000 newly arrival HHs
- 75 tents for 75 HHs
- 3,794 shelter kits for 3794 HHs
- 1877 Essential NFI kits
This previous assistance will be a rapid response in case any emergency, even displacement or natural distastes such as the affection of floods, and the increase for rapid response accordingly for those affected especially for those settling in the camps.
The project also provide seasonal assistance for winter as following:
- 2,000 Stoves for 2000 HHs
- 2000 Cash provision for winter for 1,000 HHs
To sum up, the project will contribute in the provision of shelter and NFI for 10764 HHs (53730 individuals) from the IDPs and those vulnerable from the host community.

Needs

As a result of the evolution of the crisis over the last 12 months, there has been a slight improvement in the overall NFI situation, but a worsening of the shelter situation.4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the SNFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. Against the backdrop of increased self-organized returnees in 2018, there are increased needs related to the repair and rehabilitation of damaged shelters and infrastructure as people seek more durable solutions. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2019 at the same time, of the 6.2 million long-term IDPs in Syria, many continue to face a range of barriers preventing return. Significant proportion of population are living with at least one shelter adequacy issue. Similarly, significant proportion of population living in rented accommodation have been unable to afford their rent in the last 3 months. This issue disproportionately affects IDPs, of whom 64 per cent are renting. In addition, host communities face significant pressures in terms of shelter capacity and adequacy. (HNO 2019)By the end of the year 2018 Violet conducted multi-sectoral need assessment in Idleb governorate and Northern Aleppo, A'zaz, Jarablus and Al-Bab to assess the needs of the IDPs there and the in general key findings of the needs under the Shelter and NFI sector were as following:*For shelter, all CFPs 100% express their knowledge of the percentage of their community live in each type of dwelling dwell, where 64% of HHs living in Solid finished house, 10% were living in informal camp /settlement, with the same percentage for those living in Solid finished apartment. Adding that about half of HHs could afford rental prices in the last three months as 33% of CFPs said, while 30% said less than 50% of HHs could afford it, and 12% said over that 75% of HHs could afford it.For the HHs were not able to repair their damaged or unfinished shelter past three months, were (very few less than 10%) as 42% of CFPs said, while 25% said (less than quarter more than 10%), and 17% said (about a quarter) with 16% for whom said (Non 0%) and for the reason that made the HHs unable to repair their homes, where 58% of CFPs said the reason was shelter and repair materials are too expensive, 33% said repairs require professional but cannot afford their service, 13% said they have no answer and 4% said other, were the reason was that the area is on hot clash line.The lack of heating was the most common adequacy issues faced the HHs according to 96% of CFPs, as that Lack of insulation from cold as reported by 54% of CFPs, and 50% said was Lack of lighting inside the shelter. *For NFI, 69% of interviewees confirmed that the most urgent NFI items needed are: 14% blankets\bedding, 14% kitchen utensils, 14% fuel for cooking, 11% adult clothes for females, 11% children clothing for female, 10% children clothes for males, 10% adult clothes for males, 9% for stoves and 7% for shoes.88% of CFPs said that the most common strategy the community using to cope the lack of NFI was borrowing money/buying on credit, 75% said spending saving and 54% said Selling household assets/goods.According to the majority of CFPs that the community preferred Unconditional cash distribution as a modality for any available NFI support, while 29% said NFI distributions.The top 3 winterization items priorities for Adults Over 18 were (85% Blankets, 85% Carpet and 84% Mattresses), while for Kids Under 18(61% Baby diapers,51%Trouser/Sweatpants and 39% Jumper/Sweat Shirt), the following figures other percentage for top items according the HHs respondents.

Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs. _1.1.1_ Provision of core and essential NFIs for 2800 HHs (This activity will contribute in life-saving and life-sustaining NFI support as rapid response in Northern Syria to the newly arrivals, as following: - Supplying the items will be done by Violet and its partner according to the ongoing project.- Shipping the items to Syria as a preposition.- Monitor the context .- In case any emergency a RNA will be done within few days.- Coordination with the other humanitarian actors.- Start the response.- Conduct PDM) 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter_1.2.1_ Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs (2000 stoves_ 2000 cash grant for winter)(This activity will contribute in life-saving and life-sustaining NFI support as a winterization response to the most vulnerable people even IDPs or host community, as following: - Conduct Need Assessment.- Coordination with the S/NFI cluster for winter response plan.- Start the response by Sep.- The NFI and winter clothes those will be procured by Violet partners and the distribution of the NFI and clothes will be done to the same beneficiaries as a comprehensive response considering the average of the family member is 6 members.- Regarding to the cash activity, the distribution will be through the agent.- Regarding to the NFI vouchers, Violet will contract with NFI vendors in the targeted areas so the beneficiaries will be able to redeem their vouchers.- Conduct PDM) 1.3 Provision of emergency shelter._1.3.1_ provision of tents for 75 HHs(This activity will contribute in life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support by providing tents for the newly arrivals or those don't have permanent shelter and hosted by others, as following: - Conducting need assessment and design the response.- Coordination with the I/NGO and stockholders, considering the HLP principles when selecting the land of the added tents within the formal camps in case the possibility to host more tents in the camp.- Receiving the items from Violet partner and shipping it to Syria warehouse.- Start the response of installing the tents- Conduct PDM) _1.3.3_ Distribution of emergency shelter materials/kits for 3749 HHs(This activity will contribute in life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support as a rapid response by providing shelter upgrade materials, as following: - Receiving the items from Violet partner and shipping them to Syria warehouse.- Monitor the context .- In case any emergency a Rapid Need Assessment will be done within few days.- Coordination with the other humanitarian actors to design the response.- Start the response based on the RNA report.- Conduct PDM)
