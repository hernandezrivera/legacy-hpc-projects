Project Summary

Needs:
According to the latest (October 2018) Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), as of 04 September 2018, there are approximately 921,000 Rohingya Refuges (215,796 families) in Cox Bazar. Out of these, 631,000 refugees are in Kutupalong and Balukhali Expansion Sites, 275,000 refugees in other camps and 15,000 refugees in Host Communities. This constitute one of the biggest refugee crises in the world at present. The majority of the population are women and children (52% are women and girls, 55% are children under 18).

Despite the response, An Integrated Nutrition Assessment (SMART) conducted in Kutupalong makeshift and Nayapara registered camp from 28 April to 28 May 2018, indicates the prevalence of acute malnutrition among all children 6-59 months of age is considered serious, if not emergency, according to WHO emergency threshold (12% & 13.5% respectively) . The survey conducted in makeshift settlements indicates that nearly half of children suffered from anemia (Hb<11.0g/dL), represents a severe public health problem according to WHO threshold (40%).
Data from a two-week recall of diarrhea, acute respiratory infection, and fever are concerning indicating a high disease burden among children under five. The prevalence of Diarrhea has reduced from 41.3% in November 2017 to 20.9% in May 2018 in make shift settlements while similar decline recorded in Nayapara RC from 34.3% to 23.9%. According to May 2018 SMART survey result the prevalence of Acute respiratory tract infection is 20.1% (Round 1-57.7%) and 21.5% (round 1-50.3%) in Makeshifts and Nayapara RC respectively. The same survey finding indicates that, the rate of fever without cough has increased from 25.2% to 40% in Makeshifts and from 16.9% to 40.5% in Nayapara within the six-month gap between the two surveys.
In July and August 2018, SLEAC assessment (quantitative and qualitative) was conducted by the Nutrition Sector in SAM (OTPs) and MAM services (TSFPs). A total of 10,742 cases were screened from five zones within the refugees camp. Results indicate that, despite considerable efforts, 27.7% (95% CI: 22.5-32.9%) of SAM cases were covered by OTP services and
34.1% (95% CI: 31.9-36.4%) of MAM cases were covered by TSFP services
There is a need to maintain the existing services, while continuing to explore scaling up SAM treatment through implementation of IYCF integrated Stabilization Centers (SC) and Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) programmes to address the needs of Rohingya refugee population. In addition, it is crucial to monitor the nutrition status and situation of the new refugee population for programming and adaptive response. Therefore, nutritional assessments will be conducted to determine the nutritional status of children and woman and compare it with Round 2 SMART survey result and plan for further assessments in the coming year to monitor the situation and respond accordingly.
Activities or outputs:
1. Screening, identification, refferal of children and PLW with acute malnutrition
2. Management of SAM & MAM
3. Inpatient Management of SAM with complicated cases
4. Prevention based program (IYCF)
5. Community Mobilization(Male Forum, Female Forum, Model Mother, Mother led MUAC, School Nutrition Program, IFA/MNP distribution)
