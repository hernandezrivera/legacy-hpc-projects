Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced complex emergencies that have left over 9 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance and protection. The humanitarian crisis is caused by several emergencies that include floods, influx of returnees from Sudan crisis, voluntarily returnees of South Sudanese from various neighboring countries and camps that has strained the already dire humanitarian situation in the country. Global economic recession increased emergencies and conflict across the globe has also greatly affected funding and thus reduction of humanitarian assistance and targeting only the most vulnerable in the community.
Flooding has been a serious cause of displacements and it has also destroyed property and farms lands, leaving the communities in dire need of assistance. According to OCHA reports on humanitarian situation over 1.4 million people have been affected by the severe flooding and over 376000 displaced.
TADO plans to support the affected populations in with life saving emergency Shelter and NFI interventions in Akobo, Uror, Nasir, Ulang and Tonj East Counties. TADO will target a total of 30,000 individuals (5000 HH) in the target locations that have been affected by conflict and effects of climate change.
TADO will work closely work with the communities and local leaders in identification, verification and registration of beneficiaries and cluster guidelines will be followed with protection mainstreaming taking a center stage in the programme cycle. Accountability to affected population principles will be implemented by formation of Beneficiary accountability committees (BAC) that will supervise the project implementation giving feedback and guiding the project staff on the s/NFI needs of the communities.
TADO will also coordinate with the core pipeline managers on the availability of core pipeline supplies after the approved caseloads by the cluster from the verification and registration of beneficiaries done. TADO will train the project staff on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, accountability to affected population principles and housing land and property principles so that the distribution of the NFI and shelter commodities will follow the core humanitarian principles of do no harm. During distribution TADO will work closely with protection partners, local authorities, and other key stakeholders in order to ensure that there is transparency and accountability in this exercise. There will be protection mainstreaming with safety audits of the distribution sites and other key activities like early distribution, prioritization of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure safety and security of the beneficiaries.
Post distribution monitoring processes will be done in coordination with the cluster and other key stakeholders to measure the impact of the interventions given thus improving service delivery.

1. Needs

According to IRNA conducted in Uror County on 16th to 17th September 2024, a total of 4086HH (24, 516 individuals) have been displaced by revenging flooding. A similar IRNA assesment done in Nasir County in Feb 2024shows that a total of 29340 individuals (4890 HHs) have been displaced internally within Nasir county. There has been intercommunal violence in Nasir County that has also displaced communities and houses torched. That has left the communities without shelter and in dire need of house hold Non-food items.Tonj East County inter agency assesment was conducted on 10-12th Sept 2024 conducted for across 6 counties of Warrap state, this was to assess the negative impacts on the IDPs and returnees and communities from the floods and conflict. Tonj East County among the most affected with 15000 individuals (2500HHs) severely affected by the flooding. The situation is likely to get worse due to rising levels of water from the rivers in additional to inter-communal violence especially in some parts of Tonj East County.In Ulang County on 29 August 2024 an IRNA was conducted to assess the effects of the floods which has also displaced communities of Ulang County According to the IPC analysis results conducted in November 2024, the counties that have been affected by the floods are also experiencing extreme food insecurity. This is making the situation even more precarious and calling for the need to support the vulnerable and affected populations with assistance in order to alleviate their situation and cushion them prom protection and health concerns concerns associated with lack of shelter.Lack of mosquito nets can also lead to wide spread infection with malaria that is a leading killer of children under five years in South Sudan. During the rain season, the infection rate of this disease is very high due to the conducive environment of the mosquitoes to breed. Distribution of mosquito nets alongside other household NFIs will greatly reduce the risk of contracting this killer disease and therefore drastically reduce mortality and morbidity rates and help in improving health indicators.The conditions above exposed IDPs and returnees’ people to extreme danger of water related illnesses due to lack of shelter and even house hold NFIs calling for urgent need to offer basic shelter and NFIs to the vulnerable and affected populations in the selected counties.

2. Project activities

This project will be implemented in line with the following subsequent activities: a) Provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind) e.g. Emergency shelter materials will be provided and purchases for IDPS and Returnees beneficiaries where there is market, Using local materials and labor to setup new shelter.b) Provide access to life-saving NFIs (in-kind), TADO NFI personnel will access and distribute life-saving NFIs such as blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets and mosquito nets to IDPs and Returnees beneficiaries to enable them to mitigate the harsh conditions facing them. This will be done after registration and verification of beneficiaries in line with the cluster guidelines. Beneficiary Accountability Committees will be formed to monitor the project implementation at all stages.c) Shelter upgrades (in kind) IDPs, returnees and host community whose shelter is dilapidated will be supported to repair/rehabilitate them using both local materials and plastic sheets provided d) Distribute essential NFI and emergency shelter in collaboration with the core pipeline and the cluster. The project intervention will be implemented in collaboration with FSL ,Protection and WASH partners to build a way of building resilience and promoting long term, Sustainable and accessible interventions for the beneficiaries they improve their livelihood.. e) Engage the community to adapt to the local ways to actively participate, Collaborate in Project planning meetings and consultations will be held throughout the intervention. Training and appropriate local context-specific messaging to create awareness and build resilience will be shared.f) Mainstream protection and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response to the beneficiaries and train them on accountability to affected population principles. g) Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response This will ensure that gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating chances of exacerbated vulnerabilitiesh) Mainstream land issues throughout the stages of S/NFI response as land is the most single factor of production and livelihood for sustainability. This will be done through initiation of Housing Land and Property guidelines to avoid conflict in the communities.i) Community engagement and consultation in the implementation of S/NFI response will be critical as a way of feedback mechanism.
