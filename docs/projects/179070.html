Project Summary

The project will build on previous achievements of the HIHFAD and continue to provide integrated supports to the vulnerable groups in an accessible way. The project integrates with other on-going HIHFAD projects funded by ECHO, German Foreign Offices and contribute to a broader coverage of comprehensive services integrated with General Protection, GBV, CP and MA .
Built on HIHFAD’s previous success in harnessing self-reliance among the most marginalized group as well as in promoting disability inclusive approach among the humanitarian organisations and the communities, the proposed project aims at increasing resilience of the persons with disabilities and IDPs through community and beneficiary participation.
The project will include following components.
1) Benefiting and empowering people with amputations directly through prosthetic supports in Northwest Syria.
2) Enhance participation of the persons with disabilities through the community inclusion networks and promotion of disability inclusion approach among non-protection actors/NGOs, such as Education, CCCM, WASH, FSL, ER.
3) Decrease barriers to livelihood opportunities through customized supports of their choices for the persons with disabilities with case managements, apprenticeship programme and engagement of the private sector.
4) Address GBV needs in health facilities by providing GBV training to health staff and supporting WGSS annexed to maternity and children hospital.
5) Provide training on PSEA for humanitarian workers.
Provision of 200 prosthesis
During 12 months, a P&O centre will install 200 prosthesis.
The beneficiaries will be selected from HIHFAD beneficiary list or through referral channels, after determining their ability to receive a prosthetic limb based on their K-level(a rating system used to indicate a person's rehabilitation potential).
The selected beneficiaries will undertake required physical rehabilitation at the HIHFAD rehabilitation centre prior to receiving prosthetics.
Transportation will be provided for the beneficiaries in need throughout the whole process
In addition to existing 14 community networks for disability inclusion, the project will add one more network, and all of them will further progress towards community-based rehabilitation (CBR) mechanism and act as ‘champions’ on disability inclusion via
- Engage schools to build disability inclusion capacity at education facilities through peer supports and child resilience activities
- Provide training and awareness sessions on inclusion of people with disabilities in communities
- Identify and refer people with disabilities
- Become an agent of advocacy in their communities for rights of people with disabilities
- Plan and conduct community events of their choices
- Conduct accessibility audits in selected camps/IDP sites/schools
Address barriers to socio-economic opportunities and livelihood through customized supports of their choices for the persons with disabilities with case managements, apprenticeship programme and engagement of the private sector.
- 500 beneficiaries and their families among the most vulnerable households will benefit through case management, protection assistance, referrals, and access to socio-economic activities.
- Engage the private sector on inclusive employment awareness raising and conduct barrier mapping)
- Apprenticeship will offer training and working experiences with other organization on substituted salary while the beneficiaries can gain competitive working skills and knowledge. Upon the completion of apprenticeship, HIHFAD will promote inclusive employment among other humanitarian agencies, and it will advocate cooperating organisations to retain them.
This project will provide GBV training to non-protection staff and Support WGSS that annexed to Atma maternity hospital.

Through this project HIHFAD will provide PSEA Focal Point training to 300 humanitarian, also will conduct PSEA awareness activities for targeted communities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: expectations of response/services in locations where services may in fact be limited/non-existent.Mitigation 1: Develop clear communication to manage expectations and receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information being provided Risk 2: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services. Mitigation 2: Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services. Risk 3: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance and medical capacity provided by humanitarian organisations. Mitigation 3: Coordinate with development organisations to ensure the establishment of local medical capacities.Risk 4 . 7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback
