Project Summary

The project aims to provide primary and secondary health services for the affected people in Idleb governorate in Syria. Violet aims to support 3 PHCs in Idleb governorate (Abu Thar, Samidoon, Janiet Al Bir) in Armanaz as well as supporting 2 ambulance system in Idleb and Maaret Al Numan. Additionally, Violet will continue supporting the maternity hospital in Ariha as well as continue training nurses to qualify them to work as midwives.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, Reliance on essential health services supported by the humanitarian response will continue across all of Syria throughout 2019, with health needs among all age groups, genders and population groups across all levels of health services provision, from preventive to specialist care. The vast majority of women report delivering in hospitals, with the exception of Quneitra Governorate, where nearly two-thirds of women reported delivering at home with a skilled attendant . Currently humanitarian health partners assist with approximately one-third of the estimated 1,580 deliveries occurring each day across the country, with nearly half of the supported deliveries consisting of a Caesarian section . Pregnant adolescent girls and their babies are at high risk of medical complications and death. They constitute a substantial high-risk group in Syria, with early marriage reported to take place in 57 per cent of assessed communities , and pregnant girls under 18 representing about 12 to 15 per cent of the deliveries taking place in health facilities, . In terms of access to family planning services, free contraceptives were reportedly available in 192 out of 271 sub-districts surveyed in 2018. This is consistent with the finding that 28 per cent of surveyed health workers reported the non-availability of organized family planning services in their community. Both health providers and community representatives highlighted the limited availability of clinical management of rape (CMR) as a services gap across Syria . Non communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. WHO estimates that approximately 12 per cent of the population have diabetes and 20 per cent have hypertension . Estimates for the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers together is around 6% while reliable data on the prevalence of thyroid disease, thalassemia and other less common NCDs is not available. Untreated non-communicable diseases lead to serious complications such as renal failure and disability due to stroke and amputations. Continuous professional development of existing health professionals was limited over the past seven years, which affected the quality of health services provided. Hospital-acquired infections were reported across Syria, as well as antibiotic-resistant infections. This complicates, for example, treatment of hospitalized trauma cases, or diseases such as tuberculosis. Due to the limited laboratory capacity existing across the country, verification of such events is limited.

Implementation of activities

throug the proposed project. Violet aims to implement the following activities:1 Provide essential primary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referral system through supporting 3 PHCs (Abu Thar PHC in Idleb, Jamiet Al bir facility in Armanaz, Samiddon PHC in Samidoon camp in Salqin and 2 referral systems in Idleb and Maaret Al Numan in Idleb Governorate .2 Provide the supported health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies3 Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. The project activities were designed to complement each other and with other Health activities that being carried out by Health partners and stakeholders, for instance, the maternity cases that need special care will be referred by the supported facilities (Abu Thar, Jamie Al Bir, and Samiddon PHC) to the maternity hospital in Ariha when applicable. Violet Maternity hospital was part of Ariha referral network. Over 27 health centers in Ariha district were part of this referral network.This referral system has offered the beneficiaries the best services and facilitated their procedure. about 3514 patients were referred among those health center. During 2018 Violet maternity hospital itself has received 340 referred cases from other health center. Through this project, Violet will activate the referral system through the ambulance to ensure the referral of the cases that needs special care to the specialized facilities in the governorate. Additionally, Violet will sign a MoU with the health sector stakeholders to ensure the referral system is activated.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Protection threat: Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:Likelihood Medium Impact: High. Mitigation measure: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria.- Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase. 2- Protection threat: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA. Liklihood: Medium Impact: Medium - High Mitigating measures:- A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.- Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver.- Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Border crossing between Turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistance Risks:- Indiscriminate attacks on civilians and health infrastructure (IED, VBIED)- Intensification of the conflict resulting in shifting frontlines, mass population displacements or drastic changes of the context- Theft of project equipment by warring parties following changes of frontlines in the project area- Exodus of professional staffTo address the risk of instability, contingency plans are made and include predominantly the emergency evacuation of staff and equipment and shifting the health services to where the community moves, using public buildings where available.security team carries out regular security assessments of facilities and closely monitors developing security situations to determine if facilities remain open and continue to provide full range of services or limited emergency services during periods of increased hostilities. policy towards SEA following internationally established protocols and standards for SEA protection. staff, interns and volunteers are trained in SEA protection, child safeguarding with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA and child protection concerns.
