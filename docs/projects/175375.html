Project Summary

According to OCHA, An estimated 4.77 million women, girls, men and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2021 and out of these, 2.3 million children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection services (South Sudan Humanitarian needs overview, January 2021). In response to the continued growing humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, Confident Children out of Conflict (CCC) is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving Child protection response services to vulnerable populations in Juba and Yei Counties of Central Equatorial State. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including: FTR, CB PSS, PSS and maintaining a functional interim care Centre for UASC and most vulnerable children in need of protection and response services in Juba Central Equatoria State. The proposed one year child protection in emergency intervention is designed to: • Increase access for UASC and OVC to life saving child protection services i.e. FTR case management process, Psychosocial support (PSS) services through CFS including Non CFS psychosocial support (CBPSS). Integrate protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the Child Protection in emergency actions to improve the overall context analysis and integration of protection principles in the intervention that will be scaled up to respond to the identified needs. • Enhance understanding and analysis among staff and communities and other actors on the child protection context and needs. CCC will utilize and increase on the existing internal emergency response capacity to staff and be able to provide timely, effective and efficient Child protection (FTR), PSS and ICC services for UASCs during the project period. This will offer an opportunity to provide much needed CPiE humanitarian services in Juba IDP urban settlements to areas with limited child protection services and in addition, strengthen Child Protection interventions in Gumbo, Mahad, IDP camps and the Host communities in Juba city. The child protection intervention has been specifically designed to be integrated within CCC’s existing child protection program in Juba City, which are aimed at strengthening community based protection mechanisms (CBPM) and facilitate access to information and appropriate services. • Through existing Community Protection Committees (CPC), provide a platform to establish community based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), Child Help Desks and Child Rights clubs (CRC’s) in primary schools to facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns, the development of community based mitigation plans, prevention messaging (e.g. on forced early marriages etc.) and child protection surveillance mechanisms. In addition, the CBCPMs will identify and refer vulnerable children in need of individual support. • Operate community based safe healing and learning spaces for children that facilitate their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development and provide parenting support. • Youth/Adolescents engagement to provide peer support, recreational activities and referral to other multi-sector support services. CCC’s own experience testifies to post-traumatic distress occurring in high numbers in war-affected children. Children affected by conflict need to be assisted to use and rely on adequate coping mechanisms, which include developing self-confidence and expression of emotions. An integrated approach to ensure broad community support for psychosocial support to both children and their caregivers is critical to ensure children are adequately cared for. CCC’s ongoing work through child friendly spaces will continue but this project will ensure they are more deeply rooted and owned by the community.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA, An estimated 4.77 million women, girls, men and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2021 and out of these, 2.3 million children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection services (South Sudan Humanitarian needs overview, January 2021). Food insecurity levels remain elevated in South Sudan with 5.8 million people expected to face acute food insecurity from December 2020 to March 2021 and 7.24 million from April to July 2021 respectively according to IPC (IPC Acute food insecurity and Acute malnutrition Analysis, Oct 2020 to July 2021, Issued 18, Dec 2020). Besides, Displacements remain high due to natural disasters (floods), conflicts and insecurity. According to IOM, between May and December 2020, DTM reported on 121 instances of spontaneous population movement due to natural disaster (floods), conflict and insecurity accounting for 442,668 IDPs across nine states in South Sudan. In addition, in reference to the 2021 HNO, since the outbreak of violence in December 2013, 26,18 (12,214girls and 13,970 boys) cases of unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), missing and other vulnerable children have been documented in South Sudan and only 6,348 reunified, with up to 4,000 children yet to be reunified (South Sudan Humanitarian needs overview, January 2021). Besides the above, more than 19,000 children in south Sudan are being used by armed groups and armed forces with only 3,677 released since 2015 (UNICEF). In addition, COVID-19 disrupted learning where approximately 2 million children currently enrolled in primary and secondary levels in South Sudan, are being deprived of education as schools are shut down (According to UNICEF as of 12, June 2020). Containment measures and school closures have exacerbated protection risks for children because some families resort to rely on negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. For instance, in 2020, CCC interim Care Center received over 15 cases of girls referred by CP partners or self-referral for protection from early forced marriage. Hence, children continue to be disproportionately affected by the crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence, exploitation and displacement. Meaning, the protective environment around children in south Sudan has been compromised.CCC is proposing to maintain its specialized child protection programming approach and integrating child protection measures across other humanitarian sectors/actors (Wash, Education, Nutrition, Health, food security and GBV) to prevent, mitigate and respond to protection threats facing children affected by the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. This project will seek to reduce incidences of child abuse and empower frontline workers in child protection through a mix of interventions at family/community and institutional levels. This project will be implemented in Central Equatoria State specifically in Juba and Yei Counties. IDPs in Mahad and Don Bosco camps and underserved host communities of Lologo, st. Mary cemetery, and korobo are targeted in Urban Juba. CCC is also expanding its child protection lifesaving services to other new locations such as IDP camp 1 and IDP3 (formerly known as POCs) In Yei county, CCC consulted the CP partners to ascertain the gaps in CP service provision and found out that partners such as Plan International, Action for Children and IPCS have operational presence only in Urban Yei, meaning there’s gap for CP service in the payams. With the formation of state governments especially central Equatoria, CCC will render CP servicers such as Case management, PSS, FTR, GBV services for minors in Otogo, Lasu, Mugwo and Tore payams. These are some of the communities that were mostly affected by the conflict in Yei.

2 - Project activities

Provision of FTR services for UASC identified in the targeted communities;CCC will identify, document and provide Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) and follow up services for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). To ease the FTR complex processes, CCC will set up FTR system in the targeted communities by establishing and strengthening existing community-based CP mechanisms: build the capacity of response network (CP committees, staff and volunteers, community leaders and CBOs) to help prevent and identify family separations as well as facilitate family tracing and monitor reunification of children. CCC will conduct awareness raising sessions for children, parents in the targeted communities and orient other actors such as education, food security, and health on how to identify UASC during their respective activities. Lastly, CCC will participate in IRNA to determine the needs and UASC caseloads in the targeted project sites.Provision of comprehensive case management services to UASC and OVC in the targeted communities;CCC will support extremely vulnerable children including UASC identified by the response network through comprehensive and age-specific case management services based on their needs. CCC Interim Care Centre (ICC) will remain operational in Juba providing accommodation, meals, medical care, clothing, and other assorted material assistance for the UASCs & OVCs identified or referred by other child protection partners. This will ensure the safety of children during transit for family reunification purposes and similarly, Foster Parents Association or group will be established in the targeted communities to be on standby for response as part of placement of UASC identified. Vulnerable children especially UASC, OVC and disabled with critical protection needs will be referred for specialized and essential services provided by other actors such as health and nutrition, education, food and livelihood. To ensure vulnerable children receive these services, CCC will work with other actors to set up ‘child friendly desks’ at registration and entry points during food distribution, health and nutrition and other services. In addition, CCC will participate in or establish case management referral pathways in Juba and Yei and case workers and volunteers will be trained on Case management. Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services through CFS and ECD activities in communities and schools; Structured community based psychosocial support activities through CFS and ECD activities will be provided for children of different age groups with the aim of facilitating their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development. The CFS activities will mainly center on structured indoor and outdoor play and sport activities. Basic numeracy and literacy will be provided to both children and parents. In addition, children who require specialized services will be identified and referred using the established and updated referral pathways. In conjunction with CBCPCs, CCC will integrate risk reduction action and monitoring plans to address and monitor the specific child protection concerns and also conduct awareness raising session on child protection in the targeted communities. When Schools reopens, CCC will mainstream child protection and psychosocial support activities in schools through capacity building events like training teachers and PTA/SMCs on PSS/PFA and organizing debates, music dance and drama activities with child protection risks mitigation messages. Child Rights Clubs (CRC) and Child Help Desks will be established in primary schools of the targeted communities to facilitate the identification of and response to child protection concerns. Quarterly, child protection awareness raising campaigns and trainings on PSS/PFA will be organized in schools targeting adolescents, youths, PTA members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CCC will disseminate PSEA information to beneficiaries through organizing a training workshop for Training of Trainers (T.o.T) for Community Based Protection Committees comprising of community leaders such as chiefs, camp leaders, teachers, police, and women group. These leaders are usually trusted by communities in conveying messages on social problems such as PSEA and CCC will engage them together with our GBV field staff to run awareness raising events which will increase community ownership of the issue. Focus group discussion (FDG) with women and children in the Women/Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) and Child/youth clubs and displaying PSEA posters with prevention and response messages in specific community gathering places are other strategies CCC will adopt to disseminate the information to the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Individuals living with disabilities;To ensure feedback from People with disabilities, the project staff will consider the type of disability that the beneficiary has and adopt the appropriate strategy of collecting information. Therefore, the project staff will make sure the feedback session is organized somewhere safe and accessible, adapt questions that can be answered with simple response or gestures such as YES or NO or Pointing, have an interpreter with someone from the family or friend and use various means of communication such as images and pictures, visual materials, role play and drawings to explain question and receive feedbacks.Individuals who are not literate;To ensure feedback from individuals who are not literate, the project staff will make sure an interpreter from the family or friend is present during an activity. Local dialect/language per project site will be used to ensure feedback about services or project activities from all participants. In addition, the project staff will use emotional mapping strategy of using ‘story boards or feeling dice’ (with faces of ‘happy, sad and angry expressions’) to receive feedbacks about the experiences or activities.Women and girls;To ensure feedback from women and girls, the staff will organize focus group discussions or individual interviews in the women and girls friendly space. The WGF space provides a safe and private space for women and girls to freely express their feelings about an activity or experience about the project. The project also offers opportunities to Women and girls to freely drop-in to field offices in Yei and Juba to give in their feedback about project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Provision of project information to the beneficiaries;At the beginning of the project, CCC will launch the child protection project to community representatives with the aim of explaining the objectives, approaches, staffing, duration, budget and the contact details. CCC will use notice boards in primary schools to share information about protection concern and school based CP referral pathways. Social events through music, dance and drama is also another way to be used to disseminate information on child protection. Posters with pictorial child protection preventative messages and referral pathways will be used in the communities during awareness raising due to the high level of illiteracy. Visibility items such as T-shirt, sign posts are ways CCC will adopt to share information about the project to the beneficiaries.Feedback or complaint Mechanisms;CCC will use both formal and informal mechanisms to receive feedbacks from the beneficiaries about the project. The formal level feedback will be collected through official consultation of beneficiary representatives such as camp leaders, chiefs, teachers, women leaders, and police among other community representatives through meetings, participatory monitoring, reflection and evaluation processes, and individual interviews. Suggestion boxes and child help desk will be introduced in the targeted community schools to enable children to give feedback to project staff on how to improve school outreach service. At informal level, beneficiaries (children, community members and leaders) can drop-in to CCC field office in Yei or main office in Juba where they are able to give private, one-to-one feedback or raise specific concerns and challenges. This mechanism provides a safe and secure space where discussions on sensitive issues, such as abuse and violence, can take place in confidence.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CCC will use beneficiaries feedback to make project decision to fit the needs of the targeted beneficiaries. Beneficiaries feedbacks for instance, about project design, implementation, will shape how the project will be delivered. For example, if beneficiaries complain that their source of livelihood is being affected by daily implementation of project activities such as PSS, CFS, awareness raising, then the project staff and management will device means of how to reduce the frequency of weekly activities.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Extremely vulnerable children identified (including UASC, OVC and disabled) in the targeted communities will be supported through comprehensive case management services. CCC will collaborate with medical service providers such as hospitals (Yei referral hospital, Al-shabba pediatric hospital, Juba teaching hospital) to refer children with complex medical conditions to receive specialized services. In addition, children and women with medical cases will also be referred to health actors operating within the targeted communities. The project will improve the psychological wellbeing of children and adults through psychosocial services. Children and parents will receive focused and non-focused PSS through CFS and ECD activities and positive parenting sessions. This will facilitate their recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote their cognitive, physical, social and emotional development and build a positive relationship between children and parentsTo ensure the safety and protection of children at risk or survivors of abuse, CCC will maintain a safe space providing residential care and rehabilitation services, including appropriate referral mechanisms and psychosocial support in Juba for young women and girls at risk and survivors of GBV. The project will also make link children (girls) involved in survival /sexual exploitation or at risk are empowered to develop alternative livelihood sources. In addition, together with GBV actors, CCC will ensure a safe protection environment to prevent the risk of GBV for young women and girls at risk and monitored in the underserved communities. CCC will also train frontline staff on GBV case management and PSS to respond promptly to incidents of SGBV especially sexual violence. Hence, the staff will refer and accompany survivors of violence especially girls and women to appropriate service including judiciary, police, health and mental health service providers.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The planning of this integrated child protection project has at its core opportunities for vulnerable population in the targeted communities for equitable access to cross-sectorial-sectoral basic services. Through the project, CCC will advocate and coordinate with other actors to ensure that; facilities, goods and services are accessible to all people especially the most vulnerable without discrimination and within a safe physical reach of all sections of the population. The Project will make sure special measures are taken to facilitate access for vulnerable groups to enable older persons, children, persons with disabilities and people living with chronic diseases to access and benefit from emergency programmes, services and activities on an equitable basis with the general population. For example, the project will coordinate with the WASH actors to ensure that the construction of water and sanitation facilities to increase access for persons with disabilities, using ramps, wider doors, etc. The project will engage and coordinate with other sectors such as food security and livelihood, education, health and nutrition to set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations and distributions so that vulnerable individuals such as pregnant women, people with infants, unaccompanied children and the elderly, sick, and those with disabilities are helped first. The project will also integrate in its awareness raising sessions information about services provided by other humanitarian such as to enable the vulnerable populations benefit from the cross-sectoral basic services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will mainstream clear nutrition information as part of CP prevention and response messages during awareness raising sessions. For example, clear information on the importance of exclusive breastfeeding in children up to six months, and continued breastfeeding up to 24 months will be included. During the outreach services, beneficiaries will be informed about the dates, times and locations of planned food distributions well ahead of time. CCC will coordinate with Nutrition actors to ensure that child-headed households and unaccompanied and separated children under the age of 5 years are given ration cards in their own names, and that they receive distributions of food as necessary. In addition, a nutrition specialist will be among the established community based focal points who will help to identify, register and report and refer malnourished children to nutrition actors within the community. This will help to reduce the prevalence of malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years in the targeted communities hence contributing to the reduction in global prevalence
