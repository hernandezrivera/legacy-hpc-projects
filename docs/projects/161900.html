Project Summary

The IRC seeks to improve the general health and well-being of approximately 110,000 internally displaced persons across Borno, Yobe and Adamawa (BAY) States. The main objective of this project is to ensure that people are protected from water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases through provision of access to safe drinking water, provision of safe sanitation and as well as ensuring people have the knowledge to protect themselves from WASH diseases.
The IRC will achieve this through improving access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene awareness, through the construction and rehabilitation of new and existing water points, construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, supply of WASH NFI's to vulnerable households and through hygiene messaging at IDP camps, host and returnee communities as well as in health centers, nutrition centers and schools settings. The IRC will also work through the health and nutrition centers which we envisage will help improve the service delivery as well as provide a platform for sharing targeted key hygiene messages to the patients and their care givers. Also households with severely undernourished children will be targeted for hygiene and water storage kits, which will be followed up with hygiene messaging in their homesteads with monitoring of their recovery within two months.
The IRC through the WASH sector, will also work closely with other partners to continue promoting menstrual hygiene management (MHM) in IDP camps as well as continue supporting in cholera response when needed in BAY States.
In Yobe and Adamawa state, the IRC will focus most WASH activities in health, schools and nutrition centers and also adopt long term recovery and development strategy in host communities. The IRC will build on current experience of together with RUWASSA of establishing, training and working closely with community structures which include WASH Committees and Community health clubs to promote community ownership of installed water and sanitation projects.
The IRC will continue to coordinate its activities with the WASH sector and work with the local and state level Ministry of water, Ministry of health and Ministry of education during the assessments, implementation and monitoring of its WASH projects.

1 - Needs

Since the year 2009, North-East Nigeria continues to experience effects of armed conflict that has also led to more than 27,000 deaths. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019 estimates 7.1 million people to be in need of humanitarian assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states, of which 4.7 million are in Borno state. Borno state now hosts 1,483,566 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 55% of which are women. Of the IDPs in Borno state, 55% have settled in formal IDP camps with the remaining 45% residing in host communities which has led to increased pressure on the already limited and overstretched water and sanitation services. Protection concerns continue to be experienced when there is inadequate access to services and the HNO 2019 estimates 1.81 million women and children as being in dire need of protection. The OCHA – Nigeria HNO indicates that of the people in need, 3.6 million people are in need of water, sanitation and hygiene across BAY states of which 1.12 million are IDPs. BAY states are endemic to diarrheal diseases such as cholera which occurs yearly and in the year 2018 over 18 local government areas (LGAs) were affected with over 4,250 cases and 26 deaths recorded. Currently there is ongoing cholera in Adamawa state that has so far recorded 818 cases with 4 deaths (Situation Report, Adamawa State Ministry of health, Report #64) that the IRC and other WASH partners are responding to. The HNO 2019 also denotes acute needs in ASkira Uba, Gwoza, Magumeri and Monguno LGAs. The IRC’s routine monitoring and KAP surveys, indicates lack safe water, hygiene and poor sanitation exacerbates malnutrition among vulnerable groups including children under five, pregnant or breastfeeding women. The IRC also witness overcrowding in most camps due to the sporadic attacks by AOGs, increasing the risk of spread of waterborne diseases. This coupled with heavy rains accompanied with heavy winds continue to destroy the temporary infrastructures resulting in protection issues particularly to women, girls and children when using damaged facilities. The HNO, 2019 confirms that only 38% of latrines are gender segregated with 31% lacking locks. Also, water access in 73 IDP camps across Borno state stands at 14l/p/d which is below the sphere standards of 15l/p/d. Also the latrine to persons access ration in these IDP camps currently stands at 72 people per latrines which is below the emergency sphere standard of 1 latrine drop hole per 50 people. With most IDPs having displaced multiple times including in November 2018, they have mostly arrived in these IDP camps without essential NFIs including water storage kits that are important for survival. Women and girls continue to experience huge challenges as they lack the critical NFIs for including ones for adequately managing their menstrual hygiene needs which are in most cases not prioritized. Although WASH and protection partners continue to provide menstrual hygiene materials, FGDs have confirmed that these materials are not enough to cater for the majority of women and girls who make the biggest population in the IDP camps. Lack of soap also continues to be a huge challenge for IDPs in Camps making them not able to maintain their personal hygiene and health in general. Another vulnerable group of persons includes pregnant women and under-fives who often arrive in these settlements in undernutrition state. The HNO, 2019 cites that majority of children arriving from inaccessible areas contribute to a GAM prevelance of 15% and above in two LGAs in Yobe state, and 11% and above 9 LGAs in Borno state. These children if exposed to inadequate water, sanitation and poor hygiene conditions might lead to severe undernutrition which could lead to loss of life.

2 - Activities

The IRC seeks to improve and maintain water, sanitation and hygiene awareness coverage across 10 LGAs in BAY States. The IRC will achieve this through support from donors and establish and work with community structures to promote adequate community engagement and sustainability of installations as well as hygiene knowledge. The IRC will drill and equip new motorized boreholes and rehabilitate existing motorized boreholes in IDP camps, Schools, health centers and nutrition centers. In host communities and returnee areas in Yobe and Adamawa state, the IRC will prioritize hand pump boreholes for ease of maintenance by community members. The IRC will continue with operation and maintenance of all motorized hybrid borehole systems in IDP camps through the provision of diesel, engine oil with continued chlorination of the water systems. For each water point constructed and rehabilitated, the IRC will establish and train a WASH committee that will be linked to the LGA level Ministry of water and natural resources. The IRC will construct gender segregated communal latrines and bathing shelters in IDP camps and informal settlement while following the WASH sectors recommended standard of one latrine drop hole to serve a maximum of 50 individuals and 1 bathing cubicle to serve 100 individuals. The IRC will ensure all latrines are gender marked and lockable from the inside. The IRC will also install a privacy wall to all female latrine and bathing facilities. One door in each communal facility will be constructed with an access ramp and doorway to allow for the disabled and elderly to use with ease. The IRC will work with WASH com members to maintain the latrines and bathing facilities. In host communities in Adamawa and Yobe states, the IRC will build on current experience and construct household latrines through community participation where community members were mobilized to contribute labor and locally available materials with the IRC only providing hardware materials for the concrete slabs. Each latrine facility will be provided with a hand washing station with users through community volunteers encouraged to refill the hand washing stations with water as needed. In host communities, the IRC will encourage community members to construct tippy taps in their homestead. The IRC will work closely with the education sector partners to provide basic WASH services in schools across Adamawa, Yobe and Borno states. The IRC will provide new motorized and hand pump boreholes, rehabilitate existing motorized and hand pump water points, construct latrine facilities that promote Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) for women teaching staff and girls as well as provide MHM and hygiene messaging through the establishment and training of school WASH clubs. The IRC will improve service delivery in Nutrition and health centers through the construction of new and rehabilitation water points, construction of sanitation facilities and medical waste management facilities. The IRC will also use this platform to share key hygiene messaging through brief hygiene sessions to the sick and their care givers that will be conducted by trained Community health workers (CHEWs). Also the IRC will target households with children discharged from the Nutrition centers with WASH NFIs. The IRC through coordination with the WASH sector will continue to coordinate its hygiene messaging and IEC materials to pass key hygiene messages to the IDP populations. The IRC will develop and supply IEC materials, identify and train Community hygiene volunteers (CHVs) in IDP camps. In host communities the IRC will train community health clubs (CHCs) and work closely with mother to mother support groups. The IRC will target vulnerable households in camps for the supply of water storage and hygiene kits. This will target new arrivals as well as top-ups for the existing populations. The IRC will also target women and girls of menstruating age in IDP camps and schools with MHM kits.
