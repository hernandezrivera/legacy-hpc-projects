Project Summary

The project proposes to improve access to safe water, sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices in an effort to improve the health conditions of 16,000 ( 8,759F, 6,600M and 640 elderly) drought affected individuals including internally displaced, marginalized riverine and vulnerable host women, men, boys and girls in Belet Xawa, Ceel Waaq, Dollow, Garbaharey and Luuq districts in Gedo region. Among the target beneficiaries will be 2,400 persons with disabilities.
The recurrent drought in Gedo has largely contributed to inadequate population access to potable water and resulted in frequent disease outbreaks, mainly AWD/cholera. WASH conditions are particularly poor (inadequate access to water to meet basic needs, lack of access to latrines, reliance on poor quality water sources). An assessment by REACH in June 2020 shows 93% of the households in Gedo have a WASH Living Standards Gap (LSG), with access to improved water sources below 41%. The region is served by small, seasonal, open water sources which are rapidly depleted with the recurrent droughts, leaving communities vulnerable to new outbreaks as they lack access to water for consumption and personal hygiene. Further, the assessment shows that 36% of the households have no access to a functional and improved sanitation facility. Serious protection concerns, and rights violations persist, where persons with disabilities, children and women are among the most affected social groups due to compounded vulnerabilities. The REACH DSA in March 2021 further shows that people with disabilities (100%), children (50%) and women (50%) are the top 3 groups reportedly facing impediments in accessing latrines.
As part of the WASH cluster’s efforts to address the critical WASH needs in priority populations, this intervention will respond to the WASH gaps in the five target districts through the construction of two (2) solar powered water systems with piping to communities, rehabilitation of two existing shallow wells and training of 4 five-member water management committee. In addition, 8 existing high volume water points will be provided with fuel subsidies for three months during the hot and dry Jilaal season while water trucking will be done for 2,000 persons in rural villages for three months. The project will also construct150 universally design emergency latrines with handwashing stations and distribute 150 latrine cleaning kits as well as 3,000 hygiene kits through Cash Vouchers. Hygiene promotion messaging will also be conducted in all the five districts.
Trócaire will collaborate with Somali Humanitarian Relief Action (SHRA) and Active in Development Aid (ADA), national NGOs with strong experience in implementing WASH projects in Gedo, in the implementation of this project in the five target districts.

1. - Projects activities

1. Construction two of solar shallow wells and piping to communities in Dollow - This will target two marginalized riverine villages in Dollow to provide communities with sustainable access to safe water. This will also include operation and maintenance support for the first 6 months of the project.2. Rehabilitation of two existing shallow wells in Dollow IDPs and Gedweyne village.3. Conduct training of 4 Water Management Committees composed of 20 members on management of water resources.4. Provision of fuel subsidies to 8 water management committees – the fuel will be provided to high volume water points running on diesel fuel during lean seasons (3 months during the Jilaal Season) when water availability is low and prices are high, to ensure availability of water at all times and relieve cost burden to IDPs and rural host communities.5. Conduct emergency water trucking for 2,000 persons in Ceel Waaq and Garbaharey for three months – through this activity, Trócaire will support 2,000 individuals through water trucking in the neediest rural villages in the five districts during the hot and dry Jilaal season (January-March). This will provide 15 litres of clean water/person/day for 90 days in line with the SPHERE standards. Trócaire will also ensure water supplied through this activity is chlorinated to WHO standards of 0.2mg/l, using chlorine obtained from the WASH cluster’s Regional Supply Hub in Luuq.6. Construction 150 emergency latrines with handwashing stations to ensure 3,000 individuals have safe and comfortable access to universal design latrines that are disability friendly and sufficiently close to their dwellings. 7. Distribution 150 of Latrine Cleaning Kits through cash vouchers to the beneficiary households of the 150 latrines – beneficiary households will be issued with cash vouchers which will enable them to purchase the latrine cleaning kits from pre-selected vendors in the target districts. The complete latrine cleaning kits will be composed of 1 bucket 20-liter capacity, 1 mop bucket, 1 mop with handle, 1 soft brush with handle and a pair of heavy-duty gloves.8. Recruitment, training and incentivization of 50 (25M, 25F) hygiene promoters in Gedo – the hygiene promoters will undergo a five-day training based on the Global WASH cluster’s training package for Hygiene Promoters. Persons with disabilities will also be recruited as community hygiene promoters to ensure their meaningful participation in the project. The trained hygiene promoters will conduct behaviour change hygiene promotion activities across the 5 target districts.9. Conduct clean up campaigns – The project team will mobilize community sanitation groups to conduct three clean-up campaigns in the five target districts to enhance clean environments and reduce disease incidences from indiscriminately disposed solid waste. During these sessions, hygiene promoters will raise awareness on the public health benefits of living in a clean environment.10. Distribution of 3,000 hygiene kits to 3,000 IDP and vulnerable host households in the 5 districts – this will be done through cash vouchers in line with the WASH cluster's recommendations to use Market-Based Programming (MBP). Beneficiary households will therefore be provided with cash vouchers which will be used to purchase the hygiene kits from pre-identified local vendors in the respective districts. Each complete hygiene kit contains 200 aqua tabs, 5 bars of 800g soaps, 1 Jerry can 20lt capacity, 1 Bucket 20 litre capacity, 4 cotton panties and 3 packs of disposable sanitary pads for menstrual hygiene management by women and girls of menstrual age.11. Reproduction and distribution of 1000 IEC materials for hygiene promotion – the IEC materials will be prepared in the local language and will be utilized as part of the resources to be used by hygiene promoters during their routine hygiene promotion sessions.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of CVA to deliver WASH interventions whenever feasible is recommended by the WASH Cluster and is a focus of the cluster in the 2022 HRP. The approach as well, has the advantage of stimulating local markets while enhancing the rapidity and flexibility of the WASH responses. Trócaire prefers this approach for the distribution of hygiene kits and latrine cleaning kits since it reduces the unit cost of delivering the items.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Trócaire has been using CVA approach for the distribution of hygiene kits in Belet Xawa, Dollow, Garbaharey and Luuq for the last two years. The proposed activity will build on the success of previous similar interventions and borrow from lessons on the viability of the modality in the proposed locations. Trócaire has extensive experience in conducting market assessments and will conduct market assessments to guide the feasibility of this activity where necessary.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistanceLikelihood - MediumImpact – HighMitigation – Trócaire, through its ongoing integrated humanitarian interventions, has been working with traditionally marginalized riverine communities and other minority ethnic groups in Gedo. This project has tailored some interventions that will enhance access to WASH services for these minority groups and will further adopt a do no harm approach through which various actions will be taken to ensure inclusion of these minority groups in the proposed interventions. These will include ensuring meaningful participation of minority groups in the project by consulting and adapting interventions to meet the different identified needs of these diverse groups within the target population. To achieve this, Trócaire will employ its comprehensive Feedback and Response mechanism that allows a two- way interaction between the organization, its partners and project participants. This will be through face- to- face conversations, suggestion boxes placed at strategic places, the monitoring of information that is collected through a toll-free line, and community leadership structures. Information will also be collected during regular monitoring activities by both MEAL and program teams.2. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. Likelihood - LowImpact - HighMitigation – Trócaire has strong institutional processes and policies on PSEA, which includes the IASC Guidelines for Integrating GBV interventions in Humanitarian Action throughout all stages of our programming. All Trócaire staff are expected to always uphold the highest standards of personal and professional conduct to protect beneficiaries. In addition, Trócaire has built the capacity of the management and program staff of its local partners on PSEA. The project will put in place measures to prevent sexual violence by actively engaging women and girls, along with men and boys, in community-based groups and incorporating their views in the project implementation. WASH facilities will be sited appropriately from dwellings in line with the SPHERE standards. Additional design considerations have been made for latrines which will be lockable from inside and not only separate but separated for males and females especially in IDP camps. Finally, awareness will be created on the existing GBV hotline managed by Trócaire through which appropriate support can be accessed.
