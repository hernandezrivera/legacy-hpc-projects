Project Summary

With the proposed project, CHADO intends to provide lifesaving child protection response and prevention services to a total of 11240 people (4500 girls, 3800 boys, 2500 women and 1240 men. This intervention aims at ensuring the provision of critical child protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable girls, boys, women and men in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas through provision of comprehensive case management to 450 boys and girls and Identificatify, document and provide FTR services to 150 UASC. CHADO will also provide safe referrals (e.g health, NFIs and education) services to most vulnerable boys and girls.
In order to reduce the psychosocial distress faced by boys and girls as a result of protracted exposure to conflict and violence, CHADO will provide individual/group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services) to the most vulnerable and in addition conduct focus group sessions on parenting skills to 100 parents/guardians ( 70 women, 30 men).
In addition, the project aims to Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience through strengthening of community based child protection mechanism. Therefore CHADO will establish 5 Child Friendly Spaces in Mandeng (Nasir), Maiwut Center, Luri Rokwe (Juba), Nyagkot (Rumbek Center) and Malou Rumbek East. Child Friendly Spaces will be used as an entry point for children and parents/guardians to receive wider services e.g PFA, PSS and recreational activities, that will help them gain a sense of normality thus mitigating the possible negative developmental impacts.
The Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) will be staffed with qualified Child Protection staff as well as community frontline staff/volunteers with the appropriate set of skills and interest in working with children. Frontline/community workers will undergo regular trainings in child protection in emergency, Psychosocial Support, case management including Child Friendly Space management, followed by ongoing mentoring and coaching by CHADO Child Protection staff.
As part of strengthening community based child protection networks the project will further implement awareness raising activities, including public drama sessions around child protection in emergencies. This will include components of SGBV to ensure that children have access to the relevant information and services. This initiative will involve peer-to-peer education initiatives through the Child Friendly Space. Additionally, adolescents will be supported to develop and lead on initiatives to increase local understanding of child protection risk factors and what kinds of steps children themselves can take to be protected from abuse, violence and exploitation through specific workshops and safety audit exercises.

1 - Needs

On 30th May 2019, the U.N Security Council renewed sanctions on South Sudan for another year including an arms embargo, assets freeze and global travel ban. This came after five years of the most recent conflict that has forced nearly 4.2 million people to flee their homes in search of safety, majority whom are children. While the intensity of conflict is reduced and clashes contained to certain regions including the 31st July to August 5th intense fighting that erupted in Maiwut between the Cie-Wau clan and opposition forces, forcing about 10,000 people to flee their homes to villages around Maiwut town (UNOCHA). The conflict has caused disruption of livelihood, displacement of refugee returnees, separation of children from their families, loss of lives, high risks of recruitment of boys and girls into the armed groups, weakened community based child protection structures causing urgent need of child friendly spaces, PSS, case management and reunification of UASC services. United Nation reports indicate that all parties to the conflict have repeatedly violated international humanitarian law and perpetrated serious human rights abuses, including gang rape, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls and recruitment of boys and girls. MSF report on flood in Upper Nile states that thousands of people have been stranded and cut off from humanitarian assistance, this includes an estimated 22,507 individuals in Nasir who have been wholly or partially displaced, separated children from their families during displacement, potential risk of conflict between the host communities and IDPs over limited resources, overcrowding at the borehole points causing women and girls to stay out late at night hence risks of SGBV, destroyed livelihood by the floods hence families can’t support their children with basic services like food, shelter, education and Psychosocial Support. (CHADO with partners on ground IRNA report)Lakes State has also experienced particularly high levels of violent conflict, threatening stabilization and security throughout the State’s eight counties. Tension is largely driven by inter‐ethnic clashes over resources (UNDP). The years of conflict, displacement and underdevelopment have limited people’s livelihood opportunities of the population that is 80 percent dependent on pastoral activities and have to move to other payams and states to look for grass and water especially during the dry season. CHADO form O reports and Integrated Community Mobilization Networks (ICMN) across the eight counties, state that children face child labor, child marriage and others not attending school due to inter communal conflicts. Juba is host to a high number of street children majority of whom are male within the age of 10-14 years and originally from urban areas, with extended families of low socio-economic status. Most of them go to the street for employment purpose, followed by parental loss, child abuse, strict regulations at home and commitment of offence. Children survive by engaging in works such as selling wares, shoe shining, collecting rubbish, collecting empty bottles for re-use by local beverage makers, washing cars, and others beg or steal, They face challenges such as drop out, drugs abuse, lack of food, inhumane treatment such as torture, rape in the streets including arrest (MAF SSD). Children lack access to sufficient Psychosocial Support and case management services, furthermore there’s need to strengthen/establish community based child protection networks including child help desks and child friendly spaces in all the locations mentioned . CHADO is on ground in all the eight counties of Lakes state implementing a communications for Development (C4D) project funded by UNICEF and would like to scale up child protection services in the Rumbek East and Center. CHADO currently has a sub office in Rumbek Center. CHADO has also established strong presence in Nasir implementing a GBV project.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims at ensuring the provision of critical child protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable girls, boys, women and men in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas through identifying, documenting and providing FTR services to 150 UASC and connect to them to other services e.g health, livelihood and education. In order to reduce the psychosocial distress faced by boys and girls as a result of protracted exposure to conflict and violence, CHADO will provide individual/group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services) to 15,000 vulnerable boys and girls. CHADO will further train 500 community front line workers on child protection in emergency and additional 500 (350 women, 150 men) community/front line workers on PSS , case management and mental Health and Psychosocial Support service provision for children. Furthermore CHADO will conduct focus group sessions on parenting skills to 500 parents/guardians (400 women, 100 men) and raise awareness on child protection including dissemination of lifesaving information on available services. CHADO intends to strengthen the community based child protection structures through establishment of a total of 5 Child Friendly Spaces (WGFS) one in each location of Mandeng (Nasir), Maiwut Center, Luri Rokwe (Juba), Nyangkot (Rumbek Center) and Malou (Rumbek East). Child Friendly Spaces will be used as an entry point for children and parents/guardians to receive wider services e.g PFA, PSS and recreational activities, that will help them gain a sense of normality thus mitigating the possible negative developmental impacts. CHADO will also conduct 6 mass awareness raising sessions on Ebola for communities to increase their knowledge and how they can be linked to trained health workers in case Ebola erupts. lastly CHADO will train 250 UASC on vocational skills including brick making, sewing and carpentry. In addition CHADO will promote relevant, coherent, coordinated and integrated humanitarian responses driven by the assessed child protection needs of the populations to promote integration and multi-sectoral programming with GBV, General Protection, Health, Nutrition, NFI, FSL and Education service providers. In coordination with GBV service providers messaging will be tailored on effects and cost of child abuse and GBV on the survivor and its related cost to the community to enhance prevention of child abuse and GBV. CHADO will liaise with the nutrition partners in providing nutrition education to pregnant and Lactating Women (girls) during Antenatal care. CHADO will also coordinate with FSL partners to ensure UASC caregivers and pregnant and Lactating Women (girls) are provided with vegetable seeds and encourage home vegetable gardening to diversify diet and lastly CHADO will work hand in hand with Health partners in ensuring good health and better living of boys, girls, women and men and also promote hygiene in schools in full coordination with Education and WASH partners. Thus the resultant outcome is that the target girls, boys, women and men including the aged and persons with disabilities, will have a reduced caseload of diseases and enhanced resilience against shocks.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Rights and dignity of all boys, girls, women and men irrespective of origin, background, tribe, religion or political affiliations will be equally respected and legally recognized and project activities will be accessible to all persons of concerns without discrimination. CHADO will ensure services are provided in a safe environment in which boys and girls can feel safe and respected and can access child protection services without fear of exploitation or abuse by any aid worker, and in which aid workers themselves feel supported, respected and empowered to deliver such assistance in working environments free from sexual harassment.Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) will not be tolerated; they are an unacceptable breach of the fundamental rights of the beneficiaries we serve and of those with whom we work as well as a deep betrayal of CHADO core values. Beneficiaries will be involved in the monitoring of programs and their points of view on the success and impact of the project will be central to any evaluation. Sexual exploitation and abuse by anyone associated with the provision of aid constitutes the most serious breach of accountability, and beneficiaries will be able to raise complaints and call for appropriate protection measures against such abuse, as well as be informed of the results of investigations on these complaints.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will have equal representation and participation of all displaced persons including PWDs, who will be able to access and benefit from the project activities. Activities will also be implemented in locations that are easily accessible to all beneficiaries including PWDs and all barriers to inclusive participation will be mitigated through active engagements with beneficiaries to determine how best services can reach them. CHADO will also give account; through sharing of information in an effective and transparent way across women, men, boys and girls. This will include giving information about the project, its roles and responsibilities, entitlements and targeting criteria, as well as how to provide feedback or how to raise complaints. Information will be shared in a way that communities can actually understand it, can be empowered by it and become active participants in the project life cycle.Lastly CHADO will be held to account by ensuring communities have the opportunity to assess and, where feasible, alter or sanction CHADO actions. Their view on the appropriateness and quality of the service or response will be consistently sought. This will also involve consulting communities on what they think about the quality of the response.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CHADO intends to work hand in hand with the local child protection structures, community based protection systems including the local authorities through strengthening their capacities on child protection in emergency and after project completion, conduct a handover ceremony for community these structures to continue implementation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The entire project is designed to reduce morbidity, mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in severity levels 3 and 4 and ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity levels Intesctorial objective SO1 and SO2. The activities are also aligned with protection cluster objectives of ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience including enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other population PO1, PO2 PO3. Furthermore the project is aligned with CERF life saving criteria of promoting early action and response to reduce loss of life, enhancing response to time-critical requirements and strengthen core elements of humanitarian response in underfunded crises. CHADO will conduct all activities according to the above objectives and follow all humanitarian minimum standards according to the sphere handbook including protecting all persons from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is safe and suitable for activity implementation to enable long term impact of the project after project completion. CHADO will also keep vigilant and keep a lookout of new and existing crises in the locations and beneficiaries will be equally informed of the security situation. Activities will be implemented in secure locations that do not cause any more harm to the beneficiaries including staff and furthermore CHADO will work with community based child protection structures including the local authorities, strengthen their capacities through multiple trainings on child protection in emergency as such, these communities will be able to provide services in the long term.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The exit strategy will be that all project facilities including the child friendly spaces will be handed over to the community frontline workers and relevant project stakeholders who will be able continue service provision. CHADO will work with community based child protection structures including the local authorities, strengthen their capacities through multiple trainings on child protection in emergency as such, these communities will be able to provide services in the long term.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Experience from South Sudan highlights key risks that require careful and measured approaches. These include: exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence caused by living in highly insecure settlements, communities leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of getting assistance humanitarian assistance as a pull factor and risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services.CHADO will make monthly security forecast analysis and child protection assessments to mitigate on possible risks of girls, boys, women and men living in highly insecure locations and adopt measures like providing services before dark, encouraging boys and girls to stay in school and not go wander around the villages, preventing exposure to armed groups. CHADO will also work with child protection community structures to help identify the most vulnerable boys and girls, register them and provide them with services and also work with the structures in identifying the most vulnerable beneficiaries that were not initially planned for and come up with service provision plans and how services can reach them. CHADO will conduct surveys on how community members living in some insecure locations can be reached with humanitarian assistance without causing harm to beneficiaries or the staff and also where possible hold peace talks with warring sides to allow safe passage of humanitarian workers to reach the beneficiaries. CHADO will use local content human resources especially persons from targeted communities to build trust while also being transparent to all project stakeholders in providing project objectives, targets, outcomes, period of project, who are being targeted as primary beneficiaries, how the project will benefit the community, CHADO will be transparent with all persons of concerns through thorough consultations and involvement of all beneficiaries including PWDs and elderly.
