Project Summary

This project aims to reduce the vulnerability of conflict-affected populations, including vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host community men, women, boys, and girls, who have been significantly affected by the conflict and the subsequent waves of displacements in Libya. This will be achieved through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to internally displaced, host community, and returnee households across Libya. MPCA aims to improve beneficiaries’ access to essential goods and services of their choice, enabling them to meet their basic needs.

1 - Needs

As a result of the protracted conflict in Libya and the constant population movement that is a characteristic of it, many populations in Libya – including host community members, returnees, and displaced persons – face heightened vulnerability. According to the results of the 2020 REACH MSNA and from ACTED’s ongoing protection monitoring activities in Libya, prolonged displacement situations have drained the financial resources of IDPs, host community households, and the hosting cities, particularly in the case of multiple displacements. Vulnerable populations are forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as reducing the number of meals per day, reducing expenses on essential health services or education, or even begging or exploitative work. Women and female-headed households in Libya are also at particular risk of food insecurity and malnutrition, and therefore more liable to resort to emergency level coping mechanisms.

2 - Activities

To support vulnerable conflict-affected populations, ACTED proposes to provide MPCA to internally displaced, host community, and returnee populations across Libya, to offer an immediate response to address the basic needs of vulnerable beneficiaries. Increased financial capacity will allow beneficiaries to address their basic needs in a timely and dignified manner. This project will provide MPCA to 580 vulnerable households. ACTED will work with local CSOs, LibAid, MoSA, and municipalities to collect lists of vulnerable households in need of assistance. To select beneficiaries, ACTED will screen these households through phone interviews and household visits, using the questionnaire and vulnerability criteria established by the CMWG. The MPCA will be in line with the cash transfer value, determined by the CMWG, and will depend on household size. MPCA will be provided to beneficiaries in 3 instalments. ACTED will use the e-card modality for cash distributions. The e-card modality allows ACTED to ensure strict control mechanisms, with full capacity to track the flow of funds from end to end. ACTED will coordinate with local actors on distributions, namely LibAid and municipalities, and distributions will be held in line with COVID-19 regulations. During distribution, beneficiaries are provided with information on the use of the e-card, how they can receive support in case of a lost or stolen e-card, as well as information on the available helpline.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED has a dedicated Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM), consisting of two helplines and an email. The helplines are operated from Benghazi, covering the Eastern region of Libya, and from Tripoli, covering the West and the South of Libya. This allows both direct beneficiaries and community members in all of ACTED’s intervention areas to reach out to ACTED directly via phone or email in order to express their concerns, grievances, or inquire and give feedback on ACTED’s programs. ACTED will ensure that beneficiaries are sensitized to the existence, the purpose, and the processes of the CRM and will adapt interventions accordingly to both specific information and emerging trends identified through the CRM.
