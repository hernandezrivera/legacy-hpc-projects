Project Summary

In 2021, conflict was the primary cause of displacement in the country. Particularly, the conflict that started in December 2020 in Tigray region with a spillover effect in the neighboring regions of Afar and Amhara in mid-2021 caused strife, limited access to basic services and displacement of a total of 2.11 million IDPs with 1.8 million in Tigray according to DTM Overview 2021. Following the conflict, despite the tremendous efforts by humanitarian partners to assist the most affected population limited humanitarian access, cash deficient, lack of electricity and telecommunications, inflation, hike of price of basic goods and services, inadequate fuel access, and movement and entry restriction to Tigray exacerbated the crisis.
At the moment, there are 259,000 displacement affected populations in Tigray regions Eastern Zone. To fill the gaps identified in Eastern Zone, through this intervention, ZOA will address the immediate shelter and NFI needs of 8,721 IDP and Returnee households or 47,963 individuals (with the assumption of 5.5 household size) throughout the intervention Woredas selected in the Zone. Overall, a total of 2,616 (14,389) men, 3488 (19,185) women, 1221 (6,715) boys and 1,395 (7,674) girls will be reached. In Freweyni town 1,309 households (HHs) beneficiaries will receive NFI V1 kits and 145 households will receive NFI V1 disable kits for highland. In Gulo Mekeda, 3,223 HHs beneficiaries will receive NFI V1 kits, 50 households will receive Repair Kit for Returnees and 364 households will receive NFI V1 disable kits for highland. In Saesie, 1,145 households will receive NFI V1 kits and 127 will receive NFI V1 disable kits for highland. In Erob, 2,071 HHs will receive NFI V1 kits, 50 HHs will benefit from Repair kits for Returnees and 236 HHs will receive NFI V1 disable kits for highland.
This intervention is in line with the shelter cluster’s strategic objective of provision of safe, appropriate, and critical lifesaving Emergency Shelter and non-food items to displacement affected; improving the living conditions, physical protection, and safety of the displaced population and provision of safe, appropriate lifesaving Emergency Shelter Repair Kits to returnees. The proposed intervention will address cross-cutting themes through activities such as awareness raising on protection and gender mainstreaming and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in collaboration with organizations to reinforce referral systems in the project sites. Moreover, through the establishment of strong Complaint and feedback mechanisms beneficiaries will concerns and issues will be addressed in time and in a safe manner. Our beneficiary targeting approach will encourage the active participation of women, girls and other marginalized groups.
Furthermore, this intervention will be complemented with WASH and Food security and livelihoods activities to address the most urgent needs of the conflict-affected returnee and IDP communities in Tigray region. The intervention also includes a capacity strengthening element in both the WASH and Food Security and Livelihoods sectors. ZOA will provide some resources, as well as training to relevant government stakeholders, to capacitate them to fulfill their roles as duty bearers and service providers towards their constituencies.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ZOA will ensure that the beneficiaries, local government representatives and key partners actively participate in the design, implementation, and monitoring of the proposed intervention. The close coordination and collaboration will ensure that duplication of efforts are reduced and interventions are carried out in an integrated manner among S/NFI partners and other clusters. Most importantly, coordination through the national and sub-national cluster systems will be strengthened. At the national cluster level, ZOA’s Shelter Specialist and other Humanitarian response team members will ensure active cluster participation and reporting on ReportHub on latest project progress updates. Similarly, at the sub-national level, the Tigray Programme Area team will continue their active participation in the Shelter/NFI cluster by sharing latest updates, bringing forward operational and programmatic challenges and reporting. Furthermore, the identification, verification and distribution of planned shelter response packages will be supported by IDP and returnee representatives and pertinent government officials in the region and target Zone and woredas. The same stakeholders will be engaged in Supportive supervision and monitoring tasks. Furthermore, with support from the MEAL Office in the programme office and the MEAL team in the Country Office led by the MEAL manager timely monitoring of activities to measure progress against Outcome and output indicators and PDMs will be undertaken. The overall oversight will be led by the Area Programme Manager with support from Project Manager and other relevant project staff.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

ZOA is committed to Accountability to Affected populations. This is manifested in the Accountability Framework of the Organization which clearly lays out the Complaints and feedback reporting and handling procedures. The complaint and feedback response mechanism will be established in consultation with the target community considering their level of literacy and access to the various mechanisms especially for women, girls and disabled community members. Once the mechanism is agreed, Complaints and Feedback Response systems that are safe and confidential will be established. The MEAL officer with the support of Programme Area Manager will take the lead in the establishment and operationalization process. Complaints will be addressed at the field level in a timely manner by relevant staff in keeping with the confidentiality commitment . Our Code of Conduct (CoC) will be shared with project beneficiaries during information sharing sessions to ensure target communities are aware of expected behavior of ZOA staff who are also trained on the CoC. Complaints on the breach of the CoC and other complaints that demand the attention of Country office senior management will be escalated using our internal complaints reporting mechanisms guided by our Integrity Framework. The CoC clearly indicates that there is Zero-tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Any SEA case will be reported directly to the Country and Headquarters Integrity Coordinators for immediate investigation. Hence, it is guaranteed that beneficiaries complaints will be properly handled at all levels in adherence with appropriate age, gender, safety and confidentiality considerations.
