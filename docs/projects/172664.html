Project Summary

Sudan has one of the highest rates of malnutrition in the region, around three million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition out of which an estimated 570,000 have severe acute malnutrition (SAM) (Draft HNO 2021). Through this project, UNICEF aims to increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition curative and preventative services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6-59 months) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) among highly vulnerable populations including IDPs and vulnerable residents. In 2021, UNICEF will continue to support the provision of nutrition curative interventions through strengthening the quality of services and scaling up Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) while working also on securing pipeline for essential nutrition supplies such as Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF), therapeutic milk, Resomal, OTPs drugs & Anthropometric measurements). Through this UNICEF will target around 330,000 children suffering from SAM and will procure 396,000 RUTF cartons. To ensure early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition, UNICEF will work on strengthening outreach activities through activation and training of new and existing community structures, including community outreach volunteers and Mother Support Groups (MSGs).
UNICEF will also support the provision of other specific nutrition interventions through the promotion of infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices. In 2021, UNICEF will continue to exert efforts in scaling up IYCF program to ensure the counselling of 990,000 pregnant women and mothers and caregivers of children less than two years (0-23 months). At the community level, IYCF counselling will be carried out by MSGs, while at the health facility level one-on-one IYCF counselling will be carried out by trained health care/IYCF counsellors. UNICEF is working on strengthening the linkages between CMAM and IYCF program, to ensure that SAM children upon discharge from the OTP are referred for nutritional counselling through community-based MSGs & vice versa.
To alleviate the risk of micronutrient deficiency in women and children, UNICEF will continue to provide vitamin A supplementation targeting 6,770,972 children (6-59 months) and iron foliate supplementation targeting 990,000 pregnant and lactating women.
Through this project, UNICEF will continue to support health system strengthening through efforts to strengthen supply chain management of nutrition commodities at all levels (national, state, locality and facility level). Besides building the capacity of front-line health workers and other CMAM related cadres on CMAM program and CMAM monitoring to ensure the delivery of high-quality nutrition services. UNICEF will also support the strengthening of nutrition information systems to provide accurate and timely data from OTPs and MSGs.

1 - Needs

After decades of long-lasting civil war, tribal conflict and natural disasters, Sudan continue to experience a myriad of factors impacting the food security and nutrition trends. The situation has recently been exacerbated by political instability and deteriorating economic situation marked by soaring inflation rates (170% in 2020), a spike in the average price of local food basket by nearly 200% compared to 2019 and overall shortages in fuel, food and medicine.This deteriorating macroeconomic situation is worsening economic conditions for all Sudanese people, especially vulnerable families and children aged below 5. Approximately half of the population are at or below the poverty line and food insecurity remains alarmingly high. Currently (June to September 2020), an estimated 9.6 million people are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). This is the highest figure ever recorded in the history of the IPC in Sudan compared to previous years increasing the vulnerability of the population, including household nutrition security levels (SUDAN IPC ACUTE FOOD INSECURITY ANALYSIS).Sudan also continues to experience a protracted displacement. In 2020 around 2.5 million people were displaced, out of which 423,291 are children under the age of five (Draft HNO 2021). Currently, 55,000 IDP children are facing severe food insecurity which puts them in a very high risk of short-term nutrition outcomes (acute malnutrition). The five Darfur states besides South Kordofan are currently hosting the highest number of IDPs who require humanitarian assistance (Draft HNO 2021).Malnutrition is chronic in Sudan and over the past three decades, undernutrition among children under five continued to report high records. It is a threat to public health, increasing health care cost due to illness and limiting educational achievement due to high repetition and early drop-out rates which in turn limits the opportunities of economic development in the country. The national prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) is 14.1 per cent, reaching above 15 per cent threshold in 7 out of 18 states in Sudan, indicating a very high level of acute malnutrition (WHO) (S3M II). Approximately three million children under 5 suffer from acute malnutrition annually, with nearly 570,000 suffer from SAM (Draft HNO 2021). This means that up to two in ten children in each state are suffering from short term nutritional deficiency and are eleven times more likely to die. It is anticipated that current malnutrition levels will be further exacerbated by COVID-19.The rate of exclusive breastfeeding at six months is 61.5% reaching its lowest (20%) in Central Darfur (Um Dukhun locality) (S3M II). The timely introduction of adequate complementary feeding is limited with only 24% of the children receiving age-appropriate diet diverse meal and 63% receiving adequate meal frequencies (S3M II). The low exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding levels reflect suboptimal infant feeding practices. This situation is particularly harmful to children 6-23 months old, who are at the stage of rapid growth and have high nutritional requirements. Around one million mothers need appropriate IYCF counselling services (Draft HNO 2021).

2 - Activities/Outputs

To address the high burden of SAM in Sudan, UNICEF in collaboration with F/SMoH and nutrition sector partners will support the provision of nutrition curative services targeting around 330,000 children (boys and girls 6-59 months age) through the expansion and the support of the operational cost of OTPs in compliance with COVID-19 guidance. To ensure the delivery of high quality of nutrition services, UNICEF will strengthen the capacity of front-line health workers and other CMAM related workers on CMAM programming and monitoring. UNICEF will also focus on the timely provision of life-saving treatment to 330,000 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition through the procurement of 396,000 cartons of RUTF (1 carton per child plus 20% buffer). Through community volunteers and MSGs, UNICEF will strengthen outreach activities to ensure early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition before they become seriously ill. Community volunteers and MSGs will be linked to the OTPs this will facilitate the mobilization of communities in the catchment area and strengthen referral and follow up. UNICEF in collaboration with F/SMoH and nutrition sector partners will continue to support the provision of preventative nutrition interventions by promoting optimal infant and young child feeding practices through IYCF program in compliance with COVID-19 guidance. Around 990,000 pregnant women and mothers and caregivers of children less than two years will be targeted for IYCF counselling at the community level through MSGs and at facility level through IYCF counsellors. UNICEF will also strengthen the linkages between CMAM and IYCF program, to ensure that SAM children upon discharge from the OTP are referred for nutritional counselling through community-based MSGs& vice versa.UNICEF will exert efforts in alleviating the risk of micronutrient deficiency in women and children. UNICEF will provide vitamin A supplementation targeting 6,770,972 children (6-59 months) and through antenatal care services in collaboration with the health sector, UNICEF will provide iron and folate supplementation targeting 990,000 pregnant women and lactating women.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The proposed project will promote women empowerment through supporting mother to mother support groups at the community level and training them on optimal IYCF counselling and active case finding- skills that will help them become more independent and empowered. Furthermore, this project will focus on recruiting and training female community nutrition volunteers as they are more accepted at the community level when carrying community mobilization and house-to-house outreach activities. Beneficiaries safety and protection is one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing its staffs and implementing partners to raise awareness on Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence not only those may be happening within its staffs bit also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at organizational level to put in place this mechanism. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: - The children, women, men and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone or tolerate behavior that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH - An Organizational Culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero tolerance for SEA and SH, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented. - Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights. - Swift and Credible Investigation and Sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNICEF will keep Accountability to the Affected Population centered to planning and response. UNICEF will work on strengthening supply chain management of nutrition commodities at all levels, besides building the capacity of front-line health workers on CMAM program and CMAM monitoring to ensure the delivery of high-quality nutrition services. UNICEF will also support the strengthening of nutrition information systems. Hence, UNICEF will invest in capacity building of local partners and government personnel in key components of nutrition, in addition to strengthening the information system which will directly contribute to the strengthening of the health system in Sudan and ensure the sustainability of our interventions. Furthermore, UNICEF in collaboration with F/SMoH and nutrition sector partners will work with affected communities on behavioral change communications to improve awareness on optimal infant and young child feeding, hygiene and care practices. Hence, we are working on capacitating communities with the appropriate knowledge and practices to raise healthy and well-nourished children, which in the long run will contribute to the economic development of the country.UNICEF is also an active member of the inter-agency platforms and is committed to making significant-quality contributions to it. at the Global level, the UNICEF Executive Director has assumed the role of Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Champion for the prevention of SEA and SH for a one-year period. As part of this role, UNICEF has led the development and adoption of an IASC Champions Strategy on the protection from and response to SEA and Sexual Harassment.
