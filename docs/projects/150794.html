Project Summary

Since the mass demonstrations began, the cumulative number of people killed and injured by the response of Israeli forces to the mass demonstrations, keeps growing on a weekly basis. This takes place within the context of an already under-resourced and collapsing health system, suddenly exposed to an overwhelming number of trauma patients suffering from tear gas exposure, gunshot wounds and explosions. Yet, the Gaza trauma system is based on a “scoop and run” pre-hospital policy, the tertiary hospitals are overloaded and there is very limited post-operative and rehabilitation capacity. Furthermore, the trauma services in Gaza have been supported by multiple players deploying international and national emergency medical teams in an ad-hoc and fragmented way.
In response, the World Health Organization (WHO) aims to support the Ministry of Health by continuing to strengthen the Trauma System at its weakest points.
In addition to the work already being developed, WHO will work in partnership with the MoH to address priority gaps in the trauma pathway:
1 – Establish an EMT Coordination Cell (EMT-CC) within the MoH. This coordination cell will ensure that all deployed EMTs meet a minimum standard and are coordinated and directed to the appropriate health facilities. The EMT-CC will play a fundamental role in the emergency response to the current crisis.
2 – Ensure a procurement and supply process for trauma equipment and emergency consumables
3 – Develop information tools that better capture and demonstrate the actual caseload and system constrains.
4 – Coordinate the implementation and development of a unified limb reconstruction unit in Gaza

Needs

Since the start of the mass demonstrations, the Health Cluster have conducted a weekly analysis of the situation and issued Health Cluster/ WHO Situational Reports, which can be accessed here: http://healthclusteropt.org/pages/3/situation-reports . These reports routinely list the critical needs for trauma care. Since the 30th of March 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase in Palestinian casualties. Until the 6th of October, 205 people have been killed and 22,267 have been injured.From this total, 10,768 were treated at the Trauma Stabilization Points (TSPs) and immediately discharged and the remaining 11,499 injured patients were transferred to the hospital for treatment. This takes place within the context of a health system on the verge of collapse, suddenly exposed to an overwhelming number of trauma patients suffering from tear gas exposure, gunshot wounds and explosions. The tertiary hospitals are overloaded and there is very limited post-operative and rehabilitation capacity. In response, World Health Organization (WHO) supported the Ministry of Health to enhance the trauma management system by upgrading key elements of the pathway. One particular area of support was the strengthening of the national emergency medical teams (EMT) capacity, in the form of Trauma Stabilisation Points (TSPs) set up by the Ministry of Health, in surge support to the Palestine Red Crescent Society. A total of ten Trauma Stabilisation Points were established across Gaza, close to the Israeli perimeter fence, where the mass demonstrations are held. Over the course of the 22 weeks, WHO worked with the MoH and PRCS to evolve the TSPs from an improvised treatment point for lightly injured patients, to a mandatory triage, treatment and stabilization point for all casualties. In addition to the TSP upgrade, numerous key interventions by WHO are strongly contributing to the development of a trauma management culture, across the trauma pathway, from the point of injury to rehabilitation, up to reconstructive surgery and patient rehabilitation. Another example, is the establishment of the Gaza Trauma Working Group, led by WHO and co-chaired by the Ministry of Health, which involves active coordination and liaison with partners delivering trauma services in response to the recent crisis. Moving forward, WHO is now bridging the emergency response with health systems development through proactive trauma preparedness and strengthening of the whole trauma management system.

Activities

Taking into consideration the natural history of traumatic injuries, capitalizing on the work that is currently being developed, and based on lessons learned from past experiences, the key priority activities can be summarized in four different axis. Development of the EMT Coordination Cell (EMT-CC) - Due to the chronic situation in Gaza several emergency teams from around the world have been offering their assistance to the MoH. Making sure that these teams meet the minimum standards of care, select them, task and monitor their activities is the role of the EMT-CC. This is the command and control center for all matters related to Emergency Medical Teams either national or international ones. The set-up of this coordination cell is critical for the future of EMT operations in OPT, and in Gaza in particular, as it will give the MoH a powerful tool to optimize, coordinate and integrate emergency health activities being performed by a multitude of actors.Support the development and operation of the Trauma Stabilization Points (TSPs) - So far, almost 50% of all injured patients have been treated and discharged at TSPs thus decreasing the hospital caseload burden by the same amount. In parallel with the physical ongoing upgrade, It is critical to keep supplying the current national EMTs (Trauma Stabilization Points) with the necessary consumables (trauma kits), allowing them to keep playing the critical role of front line medical provider.Development of an integrated Health Emergencies Information System (HEIS) - Information is the key for better decision making, future planning and optimal allocation of emergency resources. The current Health Information System in use in Gaza is not prepared to deal with acute emergencies and escalations of violence. As a consequence, a lot of information is lost and not captured at the different levels of the trauma pathway. The lack of good quality patient data is then reflected on the low quality of data currently available to populate the different dashboards at an operational, tactical or strategical level. The development of an integrated Health Emergencies software, will provide the framework for proper patient tracking from point of injury to rehabilitation, and a much better understanding of the system weaknesses and strengths. Analysis of the information provided by the HEIS will facilitate the design of targeted interventions and shape the whole emergency response.Development of a unified Limb Reconstruction Unit and a Center of Excellence for the Treatment of Osteomyelitis - In the current Gaza context, limb injuries are very common, accounting for 62,1% of all the injuries that arrived to the hospitals since the beginning of the demonstrations (1233 upper limb + 4837 lower limb). Additionally, continued advances in weapon technology have resulted in wounds with more extensive composite tissue losses, often complicated by ongoing osteomyelitis (chronic bone infection). It’s estimated that a high percentage of these cases, specially those resulting from gunshot injuries, will need further care, as this was already the case after the 2014 war. So far, reconstructive resources are scattered across Gaza and there are no unified protocols for treatment. The creation of a unified limb reconstruction unit, would bring together under the MoH leadership, all the different partners currently performing reconstructive procedures in an ad hoc and non-coordinated way. This important step would centralize knowledge while rationalizing human resources, time, equipment and costs. Visiting medical teams would also be able to focus their interventions in a centralized way, maximizing training opportunities in particular for the younger resident staff.

Indicators

Selected indicators include: • Number of international EMTs coordinated and deployed according to the EMT minimum standards• Percentage of EMTs reporting their activities online, on a weekly basis• Total number of patients admitted at the TSPs• Number of patients treated and discharged from the TSPs• Number of patients stabilized at the TSPs and referred to hospitals• Number of acute trauma surgeries performed• Number of physiotherapy sessions performed• Number of early reconstructive surgeries performed• Number of patients screened for reconstructive surgery• Number of late reconstructive surgeries performed
