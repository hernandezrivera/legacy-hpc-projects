Project Summary

This project is set in Northern Bahr el Gazal and Warrap States. It will target a total of 10,000 women and men and their families, through a two-pronged FSL intervention aimed at integrating resilience in the relief and recovery actions proposed herein. Action Against Hunger agriculture, food security and livelihood activities (as a component of our strategic objective to prevent undernutrition and promote resilience of nutritionally vulnerable population) are designed to save lives of the most vulnerable, while improving food security and protecting and restoring livelihoods (nexus).
Women and farmers targeted for the project will receive extension services support from the FSL Department of ACF, as well benefit from the Cash for Assets modality in establishing community assets and group savings. They will also receive appropriate skills training. Selection will be done based on the criteria of vulnerability and capacity, in catchment communities of the ACF USA nutrition and FSL programs.
FSL staff trained as VSLA Trainers will provide VSLA training after establishing the groups. In Warrap, we will train our nutrition staff to offer similar support to the groups in the state. These groups will work together for 12 months and cash out their savings at the end.
Through this project’s first component, we will target the mothers and caregivers of children admitted into the OTP and SC, notably members of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSG). They will receive immediate support in the form of urgent agricultural inputs and basic training, which will protect and restore their basic livelihood assets. This will lead to increased production of food, increased food access and dietary diversity at the household level. Kitchen gardening will target members of MtMSG - 408 women in total - focusing on training Lead Mothers and having them as examples and agents of change for good nutrition sensitive cultivation practices. The groups will also be able to sell any surplus vegetables/food produced within the program and use the collective income for any other activities of their choice. Groups that perform well in working together and that are generating income will receive training in Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA) methodology. Some of the income generated from the gardens will go into personal savings for these women.
The project will also work with men in the community, by way of engaging farmers in restoring community production and equally promoting the establishment of VSLA schemes among the male farmers (CFA component). This will create more value for the communities and ensure the do-no-harm principle (avoid that women are perceived as having money or assets and therefore exposed to GBV / control of assets by the patriarchal structures).
Cash for Assets component will directly contribute to the improvement of the food security and nutritional status of vulnerable communities in the project area, and to enhance rural development through the creation of productive community based assets. The CFA program helps to meet the immediate food needs of vulnerable people by having them build or boost assets that benefit the whole community, thus building the resiliency of individuals and communities in the face of climatic and conflict shocks. This component of the project consist of the creation of community assets (roads, dykes, multipurpose ponds, planting trees and cultivation of arable land. Principal target groups are vulnerable households in 6 payams and 24 Bomas within Aweil East State. Of this number, 56% are female-headed households and 44 male-headed households. Similar approach applies to Warrap.
The two project components will work in synergy, creating added value and optimizing the use of resources at all stakeholder levels.

Needs

Despite efforts of partners, the food and nutrition security situation remains critical in states such as Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) and Warrap. Although these areas were not directly affected by conflict, food supply has been disrupted due to closure of the border with Sudan and insecurity in other areas of South Sudan, which impact supply routes and limit the availability of food in the markets. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal, all counties will remain classified as in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or above, with Aweil East in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). 510,000 persons will be in Phase 3 (Crisis) and 285,000 in Phase 4 (Emergency). By December 2018, Aweil East county will remain in the Critical phase for acute malnutrition. AAH SMART surveys conducted in Aweil East show a GAM rate of 21%. This trend will be difficult to reverse in the coming years, hence requiring concerted efforts of humanitarian and development partners within HRP framework.As of May 2018, NBeG had some of the highest reported food insecure populations in the country with an estimated 74.3% of the population categorized as in Crisis or Emergency (IPC Phase 3 and Phase 4) or approximately 1,040,000 people. IPC Phase 4 outcomes continue due to extremely high levels of food insecurity, limited income-earning opportunities, high morbidity, poor infant and young feeding practices, and limited access to services. Access to safe and reliable water also exacerbates the nutrition situation throughout Aweil East. Our Sept. 2018 Gender Analysis in Warrap (Gogrial West) found that a lack of education/literacy, lack of access to productive resources and health care, and climatic and conflict shocks result in women’s and girls’ disadvantaged position and use of precarious coping strategies. Having little control over one’s sexuality, pressure on mothers to bear additional children puts added stress on household food and nutrition security and limits the ability to breastfeed.Action Against Hunger’s SMART survey in July 2018 placed Food Consumption Score at 63.4% of households experiencing poor food consumption. The survey also placed household diversity score at “low” (consuming 3 or less food groups in the 24 hours before the survey) for 51% of households. Women must gather home construction materials, fetch water and firewood, bear and care for children, and do HH chores. Men look after cattle, and share cultivation and construction duties with women. Although men are primarily responsible for partaking in paid labor, most women in Warrap partake in paid employment/livelihoods activities, usually due to their husbands’ suffering from mental illness or forced recruitment into armed forces, creating an additional burden on their workload. GBV often results from men finding it difficult to cope with unemployment and inability to provide for families. When present, men have control over all HH income, including that generated by women; however, women must cater for all HH expenses (including food) based on the allocation the man provides. Thus, a woman’s lack of decision-making power and control over assets combined with her responsibility for household affairs during times of severe physical insecurity poses a significant threat to household food security and nutrition status: a situation which reflects the vicious cycle of gender inequality, conflict, poverty, and malnutrition. Therefore, in direct response to this reality, and in line with WFP Strategic Outcomes 2 and 3 for South Sudan, the proposed project will increase access to food and dietary diversity levels by providing opportunities for women and men in at risk communities to strengthen their agricultural knowledge and capacity. Additionally, access to sources of food will improve through the enhancement of community infrastructure, providing for increased supply of nutritious foods.

Activities

This project will work in Northern Bahr el Gazal and Warrap States, with 10,000 beneficiaries (60% women). Women targeted for the project will receive extension services support and VSLA training from ACF FSL Department. ACF Warrap nutrition staff will be trained to offer similar support. Groups will work for 12 months and cash out their savings at the end.The project will identify and register group members (Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and Farmer Groups), verify arable plots and HH garden space that will be cultivated. We will procure vegetable seeds (or source them from FAO) and proceed to train the groups and commence the cultivation practices. A committee from the Bomas in each Payam selects CFA component participants and group farming sites. Assets are identified through Community Based Participatory Planning. The group clears arable land, ACF staff survey the land into feddans; group leaders distribute feddans to households. Town-hall meeting validates the choice.Through community-based MtMSG, lead mothers and community volunteers, home health gardens (tomato, onion, okra, cucumber, watermelon, corn) will be put in place. Extension services will demonstrate cultivation techniques. We envisage rapid appropriation of this practice, as it can play a significant role in improving overall HH consumption. Lead mothers will act as ‘demonstration plot’ owners and thereby influence others within support groups.Next step will be to introduce women groups’ idea and initiate self-organization into cooperatives, based on women’s interest, availability and cultural aspects to ensure the do-no-harm principle. Currently, there are very few or no female cooperatives in the target areas; this could be an impulse to women’s economic empowerment in a way that supports and uses the nutrition sensitive agriculture, water resource management and sustainable cultivation techniques to produce vegetable crops for own consumption as well as for sale in local markets. Training on vegetable marketing skills and record keeping will accompany the cultivation work. Formation of VSLA groups will follow this initial stage. Project staff will monitor progress and supervise the groups, advising them on cash management and good savings practices.Working with farmers’ groups will focus on sustainable agriculture and resource management (notably water and soil enrichment techniques), as well as on crop diversification and introduction of nutritious crops such as ground-nuts, sweet potatoes, yams, beans, watermelons and sorghum. We will include intercropping techniques, climate sensitive cultivation practices and disaster risk reduction planning based on the priority risks. This planning will involve the whole community, incorporating in the appropriate way the views and observations of women, girls, men and boys on: a) what the risks are, and b) what mitigation measures should be undertaken. ACF will request Treadle Pumps from FAO.The farmers will work on climate change adaptation through resilient production approaches, learning about soil management, water management, irrigation, dykes and dams, water storage ponds and other techniques. This interaction will also involve pastoralists and explore ways of reducing conflict between the groups through integrated and commonly implemented resource management. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) provides the seeds and tools for the project, distributed through Input Trade Fair (ITF).Cash based programming (e.g. agricultural input vouchers for tools and seeds) will complement resilience and development actions. The program will consider offering smallholders cash protection for seeds, to avoid negative coping strategies. The CFA component will consider larger community projects aimed at securing arable land, providing irrigation for farmers or acting as protection / mitigation measures against natural disasters and effects of climate change. WFP will support the work through cash transfer support.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will inform beneficiaries about the contents, purpose and nature of the project activities. They will also receive specific information related to technical skills the project will help them develop (cultivation practices, nutrition sensitive agriculture, climate change, gender analysis, VSLA, etc.) The project will inform beneficiaries about the project inputs as well as other information relevant to the achievement of the project objectives. This and other information relevant to the achievement of the project objectives is shared via community-level Project Management Committees (made up of Community Leaders). We will also share other information relating to ACF’s operations/services in the region, which may be relevant to stakeholders.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Infrastructure and cultivation-related messages delivered to beneficiaries in training are specific to the context of Aweil East County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Gogrial West in Warrap. Productive asset-building activities are tailored towards differing gender and age groups, and therefore their messages are delivered specifically to corresponding gender and age groups. For example, less labor-intensive activities target women groups so to not impose greater burden on their already high workload of household duties. . The messages around these kinds of productive asset-building activities are tailored to inform on the balance between these and women's household activities. For farmers and women, the information will be structured respectively around their roles, expectations and literacy levels, gradually challenging the gender stereotypes in communities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. It sets the standards of conduct and behaviour required for the creation of an equitable workplace and accountability to beneficiaries. Additionally, the organization conducts SafetyAudits, which specifically look at PSEA as a form of Gender-Based Violence and its forms and prevalence in the community. Its results enables us to identify mitigation measures and implement them in all emergency activities.Additionally, PSEA risks are included in Pre-Response Assessments conducted before each deployment of the emergency response team. Results of the Pre-Response Assessment are cross-referenced at Cluster Level to ensure that all risks have been captured.Although the project is not specifically for protection, one of the foreseeable outcomes will be stable income for the beneficiaries; especially during the lean season. Personal income for the women and relevant information provided through the project will help the women seek support from each other and from relevant authorities.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The preceding years’ versions of this program will inform its 2019 iteration. Payam and Boma leaders are consulted as they are the direct representatives of the residents of these regions. Additionally, members of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry are involved in project design and implementation. Our sustained presence in the area has already resulted in trust relationships between the organisation and community, which facilitates the entry and discussions, leading to common conclusions and improved ownership.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Action Against Hunger is committed to ensuring that its interventions are timely and appropriate to meet the needs of its beneficiaries throughout its implementing sectors in South Sudan. To ensure that Action Against Hunger is Accountable to its beneficiaries and the community it serves it has developed a feedback mechanism integrated into current program activities to solicit feedback from beneficiaries and stakeholders.The three groups that we are soliciting feedback from are:1. First-line program implementers (including volunteers and field staff)2. The local communities where our programs operate3. The direct beneficiaries of our programsFirst line implementers work directly with beneficiaries on a day to day basis: they can often provide the most useful readily available information regarding the challenges beneficiaries face as well as how programs are functioning, what is difficult, and what needs to be changed.A total of nine (9) Feedback Mechanism tools have been developed for program staff to use at the field level. Five have been identified as “minimum standards,” which should be implemented at the program site level. 1. Monthly staff meetings between first-line implementers & field office staff, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries at the site level, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate, 5. Fraud Hotline Number.Depending on the urgency of the issue, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers on a monthly basis during the monthly meetings. Specific responses can also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the PM or national/ program level. Urgent issues are addressed immediately by the Field Coordinator and Program Manager through appropriate channels (community meetings, MinAgri)

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project specifically targets those who do not traditionally have equal access to decision-making power and productive assets, such as female-headed households as well as other women household members. Women's access to productive assets is usually restricted: hence the project will take practical steps in the do-no-harm spirit to gradually increase this access and empower women in the community. Farmers usually face problems accessing cultivation equipment and are unable to expand their production. The project will address this through provision of animal traction or tractor hire for larger surfaces.The CFA component of the project involve work that is physically demanding and intended for able-bodied women/men only. This is taken into account during selection. Households with no work force cannot benefit from the CFA component, but we will considered them for other aspects of the proposed action. This is a barrier but is a requirement of the design in the project.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some of the issues highlighted by the women and people with special needs interviewed as potential causes of violence, abuse, coercion, exploitation, neglect, etc. include the lack of social support groups, the lack of access to personal capital, savings, loan scheme and proper reporting mechanisms. The project integrates experiences of men and women from the community. Discussions with communities during the initial stage of the project will help develop strategies to enable women and youth participation, prioritizing female-headed households as direct beneficiaries. Project monitoring, using Action Against Hunger's Results Based Management approach, will facilitate an enabling environment for women's participation. We will organize focus group discussions with women and men on current livelihood and care-giving roles, especially targeting households with malnourished children U5. Regular SADD data collection will help assess results and challenges in the integration of women and youth. Community accountability and feedback will be ensured through focus group discussions and individual interviews. Additionally, there will be equal representation of both men and women at the leadership level in Project Management Committees (e.g. equal representation of gender groups in all functions). Programme tools will be developed with respect to gender sensitivity (e.g. making use of visual communication materials for those women who are illiterate). Gender indicators will be integrated within the monitoring framework. Efforts will also be made to promote women’s membership and participation in local decision-making bodies and committees, which have been traditionally dominated by men. Within the project management structure at the community level, there be equal representation of both men and women. The organization will also ensure recruitment of more female staff.

Within our monitoring procedures, participants (mainly females and people with special needs) are asked, if they have at any time experience or heard of a fellow participant having difficulties accessing any of our services including getting registered, receiving inputs, receiving training as part of the project package, or getting to any of the committees set up to ensure the smooth delivery of the project.

Yes

Action Against Hunger in South Sudan conducts Safety Audits and Gender Analyses in order to assess protection risks associated with all of its programs. Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

We know that hunger and undernutrition are preventable. In a world where climate change and conflict will challenge even the most resilient among us, Action Against Hunger will be a global leader in advocating for resources, practices and policies that will restore the dignity and potential of people affected by hunger. ACF staff shall always demonstrate respect for individuals by ensuring individual actions and interactions with others are guided by respect and a determination to restore dignity to beneficiaries / persons of concern.The engagement with beneficiaries and stakeholders at all levels complies with these core principles and behaviours. At regular intervals, the project assesses the status of participating women in terms of their role in the community, their feelings about belonging, self-worth and ability to engage in ways that were not possible. The project will also assess the dignity of men as related to project activities and community dynamics, by organising similar types of focus groups and ensuring that we challenge stereotypes in a non-confrontational manner.
