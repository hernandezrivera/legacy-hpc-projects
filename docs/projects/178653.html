Project Summary

In line with the priorities of the HNO and identified gaps and needs in the selected areas, the project aims at improving livelihood opportunities of vulnerable unskilled workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor Governorates, such as women heads of households (HH) and young adults between 18 and 35 years old breadwinners of the family. The intervention will be implemented in rural areas of Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. These are considered extremely poor and economically affected areas, in high need of support. The action is designed to train people providing them with knowledge and tools; they will be eventually re-inserted in the work market, boosting the local economy and assuring the sustainability of the intervention. Beneficiaries will be identified through a comprehensive and participatory approach, which involves also the analysis of data collected by WWGVC in the same areas in the past years through the conduction of non-formal education activities. The typology of the trainings, with 3 to 6-months length, will be decided through an initial market study to identify the most successful topics, with the involvement of local/national authorities. Trainees will be provided with individual professional kits allowing them to self-create their job. Specifically, the intervention is composed of two phases: (i) plan, design, and conduction of vocational trainings to identified 800 beneficiaries, and (ii) facilitate their regular employment access through linkages with workplaces and distribution of individual professional kits. The target of the intervention are adults breadwinner of the families, identified as the most vulnerable and in need of such support. The identification phase will rely on the analysis of data collected through previous WWGVC implemented projects about families with out-of-school children. Specifically, the intervention will privilege households with out-of-school children due to economic reasons, and/or with children at risk of dropping out, aiming not only to create a job opportunity, but also to facilitate the children’s school re-insertion and/or school retention. The initiative will have an impact that will not only directly benefit the head of households, but dramatically improve the economic condition of their entire families, with positive impacts to close relatives and neighborhoods.

4 - Needs

Securing livelihood is one of the most critical defies faced in Syria. According to ICRC estimates (2020), 80% of the population live below the international poverty line of $1.90 per day. People are adopting harmful coping strategies (reduced food consumption; reduced hygiene practices; accumulation of debt) due to lack of livelihoods. Adolescents are exposed to exploitation and abuse, with girls also forced to early marriage. Divorced women and widows are at risk of stigmatization and discrimination. Pressure to provide for their families can lead to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at the workplace. In rural and underserved areas of Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor unemployment particularly affects the age group between 18 and 30 (exceeding 30%), and 75% of women are excluded from access to work opportunities (WWGVC assessment, November 2020). Thanks to back-to-learning campaigns at HH level (door-to-door visits) run by WWGVC in 2018-2020 within education projects, data were collected on out-of-school children in the targeted areas. Results show that 32,9% of children in Aleppo and 84,5% in Deir-Ez-Zor are out of school due to economic reasons, confirming families’ weak economic conditions. Livelihood in the city of Deir-Ez-Zor has been significantly impacted by the protracted siege,electricity outage,shortage of fuel and water and significant destruction of existing infrastructure. Unemployment rate is reported to be high and remaining income sources were salaries for civil servants(less than 20% of the current population) and casual labor. Due to low wages and high needs, child labor is observed to be rife. Finally, in rural areas in both governorates mines make farming land partly inaccessible.

5 - Implementation of activities

A1)Vocational training for vulnerable unskilled workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor (800 people, 400/governorate). 1.1-Market study to map the most neededvocational trainings.1.2-Identification of potential beneficiaries. The database developed by WWGVC with data on out-of-school children will be analyzed to identify the most vulnerable families. The program will address vulnerable people, preferring women HoHs (18-35), as well as families of out-of-school children and/or with a high probability of dropping out 1.3.Conduction of vocational training in cooperation with the DoE for Vocational Training for 3-6 months depending on the topic. Training subjects to be identified during the first phase of the project through the market survey. However, the first findings show that the most successful training topics are informatics (maintenance of PCs/mobiles), electricity works, tailoring/sewing, shaving/makeup, welding/metal forming, carpentry.A2)Regular employment accessed for 640 workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor (80% of the trained individuals ). Most individuals in the selected age range are willing to become self-employed. A target of 80% is considered appropriate and achievable, as it combines both trainees who will set their business and those who will work as employees supported by the project activities below. 2.1-Identification of companies in need of workforce: definition of job offers, required profiles, and a number of workers. 2.2-Support to access the job market. Trainees will be put in contact with relevant companies by support in the preparation and sharing of CVs. In addition, all trainees will be supported in self-job creation with the distribution of 800 professional kits in line with acquired skills.
