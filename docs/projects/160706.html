Project Summary

CEDS is proposing a one year emergency food security and livelihood project that aims at meeting the immediate food needs of conflict affected people by providing emergency livelihood support to increase food production and availability to the targeted beneficiaries thus reducing malnutrition incidences and related morbidity. The project will target 4,000 households, 2,000 households in Aweil East and 2,000 households in Aweil North. The targeted beneficiaries will be provided with seeds and tools including fast maturing and nutritious vegetables seeds to improve their immediate food production. The targeted beneficiaries will be trained on good agricultural practices, conservation agriculture and kitchen gardening. Each beneficiary will be provided with 7 kg of groundnuts seeds, 7 kg of sorghum seeds, 50g of okra seeds, 50g of kudura seeds, 1 piece of hoe and a piece of malloda.

1 - Needs

After more than five years of conflict, South Sudan remains one of the most food-insecure countries in the world (Food Assistance Fact Sheet - South Sudan, Sept. 2019) . The FSNMS bulletin of 2017 for instance reported that South Sudanese households are facing the worst food security situation in the horn of Africa . The rampant violent conflict in the country coupled with population displacement and restricted movement has among other factors disrupted trade and markets and also hampered cultivation activities thus aggravated food insecurity and humanitarian needs. An estimated 6.35 million people, about 54% of South Sudan population are currently classified in crisis or worse acute food insecurity (South Sudan, IPC report Sept 2019). Among those affected, an estimated 1.7 million people are facing emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in catastrophe (IPC phase 5). In Northern Bahr EL Ghazal, Aweil North and Aweil East Counties included, an estimated 560,000 individuals, about 59.3% of the population, were reported to be facing crisis and emergency acute food insecurity . Further to this, Relief net in August 2019 classified Northern Bahr el Ghazal to be facing critical to extremely critical levels of global acute malnutrition. Though it is projected that number of people facing crisis or worse acute food insecurity in South Sudan will decrease to 4.54 million (39% of the population) between September to December 2019 as a result of improved seasonal harvest, the number is projected to increase to an estimated 5.5million people (47% of the population) between January and April 2020 as a result of the depletion of the food stocks. In Northern Bahr El Ghazal, an estimated 450,000 (47.6%) of the population will be facing crisis or emergency acute food insecurity between the September to December 2019 but the number is projected to increase to 545,000 (58.0% of the population) between January and April 2020 (SS IPC AMN Sept 2019). A food security and nutrition assessment carried out by CEDS in September 2019 in Aweil North and Aweil East noted that as at the time of reporting, only 38.9% of the households had extra food in store that could last for an average of about 1.13 months. At the time of the assessment, 22.2% of the households reported to have been receiving food assistance 8.6% of which rely solely on the food assistance as their main source of food. The households mean reduced coping strategies index stood at 17.03 with male headed household reporting better coping strategies (rcis of 16.8) as compared to female headed households (rcsi of 17.24). Relying on less expensive food (97.3%), reducing the number of meals per day (87.2%) and reducing portion on meals 73.1%) were the most practiced food coping strategies by the households. As at September 2019, the mean household’s food consumption scores stood at 34.4 with males headed households (fcs 36.3) noted to be significantly better compared to female headed households (fcs 32.2). One in three households (37.6%) were categorized in the poor food consumption group while 35.3% in borderline and 27.1% in acceptable food consumption groups. With the current prevailing food consumption indicators and the projected IPC classification for January to April of 2020, it is evident that the food security indicators among households in Aweil north and Aweil East will further deteriorate. A consultation with the households and the community leadership noted that though almost 80% of the land in Aweil North and Aweil East is arable and fertile but chronic food insecurity among households is contributed to partly by lack of skills on good agricultural practices that result to poor harvest and also lack of farm inputs including seeds and tools. Thus this emergency food security project will go along way to help the targeted beneficiaries to meet their immediate food needs.

2 - Activities

The project will begin with sensitization of the state ministries, local authorities and the targeted villages to prepare ground for beneficiary registration. The beneficiaries will then be given seeds and tools with each beneficiary getting 7kg of sorghum seeds, 7kg of groundnuts seeds, 50g of okra seeds and 50g of kudura. The targeted beneficiaries will also get a hoe and malloda each. 400 lead farmers will then be trained on good agricultural practices who will then train and support the targeted beneficiaries working together with the government extension workers in the targeted villages. The good agricultural practices that will be taught include planting, weed control, post‐harvest handling and conservation agriculture practices. The targeted farmers will also be trained and supported by the lead farmers to set up kitchen gardening. Additionally the targeted beneficiaries will be educated on good nutrition practices. Monitoring activities will also be carried out by the project officer and the M&E officer.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The project will work together with the ministry of agriculture at the state and local levels to ensure that the government set systems to support agriculture are incorporated into the project. The project will also use government extension workers who are based at the Payam levels to support activities of this project. The extension workers will work together with the lead farmers who will be trained in order to train other farmers to ensure that the targeted beneficiaries are trained and supported in applying the good agricultural practices that will be taught. To ensure that the project conforms to systems set by the state ministries, CEDS will ensure that the seeds that will be distributed to the beneficiaries are procured from only suppliers who have had their seeds tested for germination by the ministry.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project will not target either IDPs or Refugees but will target the returnees and host community in both Aweil East and Aweil North. The proposed project is in line with the South Sudan policy on humanitarian response plan and strategic objective which is to "protect the rights and uphold the dignity of the most vulnerable" and contributes to the sector specific objective which is to "protect and promote livelihoods to enhance coping mechanisms and improve access to food".

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project will be implemented in Aweil East and Aweil North in Northern Bar el Ghazal which has been one of the most peaceful and stable states in South Sudan over the years. Though there was a slight insecurity situations in August this year, the calm has been restored as the rebelling forces that was forced out from the north and moved into the region where clashed and forced out of the area. The area remains safe and secure for humanitarian intervention.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CEDS incorporates sustainability in all the projects it implements as it understands that the targeted communities will have to build their lives beyond the humanitarian support that it offers. In this project CEDS will work with the government extension workers in supporting the various agricultural activities that will be carried out. This will ensure that the farmers are supported by the extension workers beyond the project period. Locally produced seeds will be procured and distributed to the farmers in order to ensure that the farmers are able to access the seeds even after the project. CEDS will also work together with and continuously consult the state ministries of agriculture to ensure that they are part of the activities that will be implemented in order to ensure that they learn from the project and are able to continue to support the farmers. The government extension workers will also be trained together with the lead farmers in order to equip them with knowledge that they will use to support the farmers beyond the project period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To mitigate the risk that the beneficiaries may lack access to complaints and feedback mechanisms, CEDS already has an accountability officer who will ensure that beneficiaries are offered platforms to share their complaints and feedback. The risk of impartiality by stakeholders in beneficiary selection is also likely to be experienced but CEDS will ensure that comprehensive consultation with the various stakeholders and that the selection criteria is made clear to all. Community based targeting will entirely be used to minimize stakeholder interference. To minimize the risk of women and girls being not included in consultation and decision, CEDS will organize different consultation forums for the different genders and groups which will give opportunity to women and girls to share their views to be included in the implementation of the project. CEDS will also through the gender officer create awareness among the beneficiaries on the importance of giving both women and girls opportunity to share their views on various project issues and their involvement on decision making.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CEDS already has a communicating with community officer who is dedicated to ensuring that project information is shared with the beneficiaries, beneficiaries are consulted and feedback from the targeted beneficiaries are collected and acted on through different forums such as community meeting and focused group discussions. The officer will organize focused group discussions with different vulnerable groups such as women, girls, the disabled and the illiterate to get views and feedback from them that will be included in the project implementation. Community meetings targeting these vulnerable groups will also be held giving them opportunity to receive project information and share their feedback. The officer will also be available to listen to individual beneficiaries whenever the matter requires secrecy. A project feedback committee will be formed at every targeted village and consisting of a representative of the youths, women, the disabled and a local leader. These people will be selected by the targeted beneficiaries in every village. They will be receiving feedback from the beneficiaries and have meetings to deliberate the feedback collected under the chairmanship of the communicating with community officer. The beneficiaries will also be provided with a hotline that can be used to call and report any feedback or complaint. All the feedback and complaints collected will be shared with the project implementers to incorporate them in the project.
