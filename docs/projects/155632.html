Project Summary

This project aim to improve the access to sustainable WASH services in Communities hosting CAR refugees in the East, Adamaoua and North region of Cameroon. The main target are communities hosting refugees and particularly villages with new arrival and villages with no functionnal water point

Needs

In general, the populations of the rural areas and those living in region affected by humanitarian crisis in Cameroon live in an environment characterized by a lack of access to the Water and sanitation infrastructures unsafe environment. This situation affects not only the host population but even more displaced people who must in addition seek livelihoods for their vital needs.In the face of the ongoing socio-political conflict in the North West and South West regions (marked by the displacement of about 350,000 people), the weak funding of the 2018 Humanitarian Response Plan and the persistence of the underlying factors (insufficient investment in the water sector, poor infrastructure management, increased demand for WASH services as result of an influx of populations, etc.), the humanitarian needs for 2019 saw a quantitative increase (25% of number of more people in need) and geographic (8 out of 10 regions in 2019 compared to 4 out of 10 regions in 2017). For the year 2020, the analysis of the evolution of the socio-political context does not make it possible to predict a decrease of the humanitarian needs.For this year 2019, Regardless of the conflict zone, the two main problems identified and related to the sector are:• Refugee, IDP, returnee and host / local men, women, girls and boys do not have access to safe and sustainable drinking water for their needs. • Displaced, returned or host men, women, girls and boys live in an unsanitary environment marked by the presence of human excreta in the open air. This situation similarly affects both male-headed and female-headed households.In the East, Adamawa and North regions, the most critical needs are access to drinking water supply, hygiene and sanitation facilities for populations living in refugee sites ( Gado, Ngam, Borgop, Lolo, Mbile, Minawao) in which the minimum standards are not reached. Another category of people in need of humanitarian assistance is mainly individuals living in the 43 communities hosting 15,181 Central African refugees and without any water supply infrastructure, which forces communities to drink from the water. surface and from unimproved sources. This situation of lack / absence of water points within and off site mainly affects women, girls and boys who are involved in the search for water within households.There is an urgent need to provide Water services to these communities along with hygiene and sanitation services.
