Project Summary

IOM will coordinate and co-lead the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group, alongside Internews and the CCCM Cluster. This effort will support the broader humanitarian community by working towards greater transparency and accountability in humanitarian decision-making, providing technical guidance to humanitarian partners on communication and community engagement (CCE), and supporting the Clusters in analyzing progress towards their accountability to affected populations (AAP) objectives.

The CCE Working Group will provide a platform for a wide range of humanitarian partners in their efforts to analyze community perceptions of humanitarian services in South Sudan, and in developing the guidance necessary to improve the accountability of aid actors to affected people. The Working Group will also serve as a forum for the Clusters to find support in strategically reviewing their CCE and AAP work.
The overarching aims of the CCE Working Group are to improve access to information about humanitarian assistance across the response, and to support programs and agencies in developing meaningful ways of enhancing community consultation, closing feedback loops, and instituting reliable complaint mechanisms that address the concerns of affected people.

1 - Needs

Communication and community engagement (CCE) refers to a range of activities, including information-sharing, consulting communities during each phase of the project cycle, and promoting the active participation of communities in decisions that affect their lives and well-being. At the core of CCE is the concept of a ‘two-way dialogue’ between humanitarians and crisis-affected people, whereby those impacted by humanitarian decisions have a voice and are afforded meaningful opportunities to communicate with aid actors and to receive feedback on their concerns. CCE starts from the basic premise that information is aid, and that robust CCE activities increase the overall effectiveness of humanitarian actions. This is because CCE involves sharing of information in a timely manner, using communication modalities in line with community preferences, and strives to include the most vulnerable and traditionally marginalized groups in decision-making. CCE considers the particular informational needs of women, persons with disabilities, older persons, and youth, to be of critical importance to a humanitarian response. Moreover, regular and sustained CCE is more likely to make potential violations of ‘do no harm’ apparent, so that aid actors can adjust their response and activities to reduce the likelihood that interventions will cause harm to communities. While there are numerous CCE interventions ongoing in South Sudan, many lingering problems remain, particularly when it comes to sharing best practices and building upon lessons learned. The activities of the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group are therefore critical interventions, for which support is required for strong coordination by agencies with significant experience operating in this area.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The CCE Working Group will work collaboratively with the PSEA Taskforce to support humanitarian partners in implementing PSEA recommendations. The WG will support in the development of more functional CBCMs, which will aid beneficiaries in reporting SEA violations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As a common platform serving Clusters as well as communities, the CCE Working Group will provide guidance on communication strategies that enhances the participation of women, persons with disabilities, youth, and older persons. By providing analysis of existing CFMs, the CCE Working Group will be able to provide specific technical guidance to partners to ensure that feedback from the most vulnerable groups in society is collected.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The CCE Working Group will operate as a platform to provide systematic and strategic review of current information-sharing practices, as well as a platform through which to examine the functionality of existing CFMs. The CCE WG will support humanitarian partners in providing information about various programs and agencies, and will aid in the strengthening of CFMs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The CCE Working Group will work closely with humanitarian partners to provide guidance on closing the feedback loop and will encourage regular and systematic review of community perceptions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

As a platform for information sharing and analysis, the CCE Working Group will work towards standardized and harmonized practices for collecting complaints and feedback, in order to mitigate against the risk that beneficiaries do not know about CFMs, lack trust in the mechanisms, or do not have meaningful ways to file complaints. By promoting meaningful access to CFMs, communities and humanitarian partners can benefit from targeted program adjustments, also enhancing the standards of AAP and meaningful participation. Additionally, by providing technical support to the Clusters on CCE and inclusive engagement, the CCE WG will promote and mainstream disability inclusion, women’s participation, and the involvement of youth in decision-making. The CCE WG will also promote the mainstreaming of PSEA, in collaboration with the PSEA Taskforce, and will highlight best practices in CCE data collection to avoid data protection breaches.
