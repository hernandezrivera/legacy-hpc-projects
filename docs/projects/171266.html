Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children in Area C in the West Bank to safe access to water and sanitation. The interventions will contribute to reducing the targeted communities WASH vulnerability with focus on children and persons with disabilities (PWD),especially girls as they are considered the most vulnerable and more in need of these critical services. The proposed interventions will target unserved and underserved communities with essential WASH services that will also protect the most vulnerable families under threat of forcible transfer. The intervetions will focus on improving the communities’ access to: 1) adequate and safely managed water services; 2) sanitation services and 3) WASH facilities in schools; 4) safe and adequate tankered water in underserved communities including schools; and 5) increased storage capacity of the unconnected households. All interventions will be implemented in parallel with the provision of hygiene materials and hygiene awareness sessions in schools and communities.
The project interventions were developed based on the WASH Cluster eligible actions for HRP 2021, and targeting the most vulnerable communities according to WASH 2020 vulnerability mapping. Therefore, UNICEF and its implementing partners are proposing to implement:
• Construction/rehabilitation of water infrastructures (pipes and cisterns) (19 communities).
• Construction/rehabilitation of household wash facilities (latrines and handwashing stations) (55 of households)
• Construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools. (21 schools)
• distribution of tankered water to most vulnerable unconnected communities including six schools in Area C for at least five months, developing capacities of water distribution chain stakeholders at local, regional and national levels.
• Provision 459 household Poly Ethelene (PE) water tanks in most vulnerable unconnected communities in Area C benefitting from the tankered water.
• Provision of hygiene materials and hygiene awareness sessions in schools and communities. (21 schools and 24 communities)
UNICEF and its partners will adopt several implementation methodologies that consider the targeted communities’ locations, proposed response technical standards, and UNICEF's accessibility and partners to these communities. Therefore, UNICEF and the partners will adopt and consider beneficiary/community implementation mechanisms for household interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of household WASH facilities and cisterns. Implementation through suppliers/contractors will be adopted for WASH facilities in schools and water community water infrastructures activity.
Moreover, the proposed interventions will consider the targeted groups' socioeconomic, protection and COVID-19-related vulnerabilities. UNICEF will target the communities under the risk of demolition and forcible transfer in Area C, the families under the poverty line, and the communities highly affected by the COVID-19 outbreak. The proposed interventions will improve the targeted groups' capacities and resilience to deal with the challenges mentioned above.
UNICEF and its partners will carefully consider the special and persistent needs of women, children, elders and PWDs. Thus, these vulnerable groups will be consulted during the planning, design, and implementation of the interventions. Therefore, UNICEF and its partners will propose the sufficient response of these groups that consider the different social and traditional characteristics in the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, more than 1.6 million Palestinians suffer from limited access to WASH services, of which 390,000 are living in vulnerable communities in the West Bank. Most of them are also suffering from other protection, social and economic challenges, such as restrictions in Area C, high poverty rates, and the increased number of COVID-19 cases. In the West Bank, the inadequate water resources and water infrastructures affect the access to water for many Palestinian communities. The Israeli government controls the water supply to the Palestinian communities, and the water losses on the water infrastructure in the West Bank reaches more than 40%. According to WASH Cluster vulnerability mapping, more than 80,000 families in the West Bank suffer from a low water consumption of less than 30 l/c/d. These families are obliged to adopt several negative coping mechanisms to satisfy their water needs, such as purchasing expensive tankered water or depending on other unreliable water resources. An NGO conducted in September 2020 an in-depth field assessment that showed that 29 unconnected communities and six schools in West Bank have a water consumption of less than 50 l/c/d and are dependent on water trucking during the summer season. Considering that access constraints in Area C pose challenges for the water trucks to reach remote localities, this is usually coupled with subsequent increases in the final cost of water up to 40 NIS/m³. UNICEF and its partners have conducted WASH vulnerability analysis in 24 communities and 21 schools in the West Bank. These schools and communities are suffering from limited access to WASH services; about 50,000 persons (including 11,000 students) are either not connected to water networks, their water consumption is less than 30 l/c/d, or using inadequate WASH facilities. WASH services in schools are considered a key component to provide a healthy and conducive learning environment. Although the Ministry of Education in coordination with WASH Cluster partners targeted around 250 schools as part of its COVID-19 response, 140 schools are still lacking sufficient or adequate WASH facilities, affecting the access to water and sanitation for more than 50,000 students. This results in students overcrowding around the available facilities and the associated protection, privacy, and dignity complications students, particularly girls, are suffering during the use of these facilities.30,000 children in these communities and schools are experiencing daily suffering due to their WASH vulnerability. Their low water consumption affects their daily hygiene practices, and the lack of sufficient sanitation facilities affects the students' willingness to attend the schools. Women and girls are also suffering in two different dimensions: 1) quality of life, due to restricted access to drinking water and subsequent obstacles to access to hygiene practices, especially considered the threat related to COVID-19 pandemic, and safe menstrual hygiene management; 2) sense of safety, due to the long distance covered to fetch water, they are exposed to settlers’ and Israeli Forces’ harassment violence, a situation worsened by the frequent movement restrictions in response to the COVID-19 crisis. In Area C, many communities are affected by the restricted Israeli construction regime in the area. 33 incidents took place against WASH infrastructures in Area C during 2020, which affected access to water and sanitation for many households who were depending on these infrastructures.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will deal with the source of WASH vulnerability in communities, such as the unavailability of water, unavailability of water storage capacity and the inadequate WASH facilities in households and schools. UNICEF will keep coordinating with relevant authorities at all phases of this project to ensure that the communities are targeted based on actual needs and vulnerabilities. UNICEF and its partners will organize meetings with stakeholders to coordinate the activities and the locations, and avoid gaps and overlaps. Local councils in the targeted locations have been identified as partners for collection of data related to the baseline and the endline. UNICEF will lead the advocacy efforts towards key stakeholders and the International Community, linked with the provision of the following WASH intervention implemented by partners in full coordination with relevant authorities. 1. Rehabilitation/construction of water networks for communities: Construction of pipelines and filling points for Bedouin and herding communities: the pipelines will be connected to the municipality systems. 11 communities in Area C will have improved their access to water through transmission pipelines and water networks; This will be implemented by contractors or village council members and beneficiaries and will build upon their knowledge of the area and of the potential risks involved. 2. Construction/rehabilitation of household cisterns for vulnerable families with special attention to female-headed households and families with disabled members in Area C;. A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted including female-headed households and families with disabled members.This will include chlorination campaign and distribution of chlorine tablets to the community to improve the quality of new storage capacity. 3. Improving access to safe drinking water: This activity will be composed of the following two components: 1) 18 wheeled water tanks shall be rehabilitated to ensure safe drinking water to communities which will improve the communities resilence. 2) Provision of two chlorination systems for communities in Area C. 4. Improving access to adequate sanitation and safe household/personal hygiene: This activity will include three components: 1) rehabilitation of 55 household sanitation facilities at five different communities in Area C; 2) provision of 23 household latrines for two communities in Area C; and 3) promoting hygiene and sanitation practices with gender and protection mainstreaming integrated. 5. Water infrastructure operation and management plan in Yatta city: through developing hydraulic map and technical capacity building training to ensure fair water distribution amony the city nighberhoods. 6. Rehabilitation/construction of WASH facilities in schools/institutions. This will include rehabilitation/construction of new sanitation facilities, latrines, and water fountains facilities in targeted girls schools for students aged between 5 – 17 years old, based on Ministry of education standards for schools water and sanitation facilities. 7. Improving access to safe drinking and domestic water services, at the household level through provision of trucked water and PE water tanks. This will improve their resilience and strengthen COVID-19 preparedness of targeted communities by improving their access to water with an integrated protection approach by working at two levels: 1) distributing tankered water to most vulnerable unconnected communities in Area C including six schools for at least five months and providing 459 Household PE water tanks in most vulnerable unconnected communities in Area C benefitting from the trucked water, and 2) developing capacities of water distribution chain stakeholder at local, regional and national level in strict consultation with relevant authorities to improve water governance and accountability of Village Councils and Joint Service Councils.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project’s main focus will be to provide the needed services in a safe and dignified manner. The project will instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measure to meet “Do No Harm” requirements. All staff involved in the projects will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in UNICEF’S Code of Conduct and related policies including ChildProtection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The planned project considers protection as key cross-cutting approach along the design, putting special emphasis in the community participation and empowerment considering the needs of some groups of the communities that are in a vulnerable situation due to gender, age, disabilities or any different element. The “do no harm principle” will be respected and promoted in each action and the design of the project will implement the Community Protection Approach (CPA) that ensures community participation (promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children) in the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in an increased empowerment at the individual and community level. Aligning with non-discrimination policies, protection and vulnerability concerns and community participation, the identified activities are implemented through an approach that foresees the involvement of all community members. Implementation methodology is led by community members through a participatory approach. In addition, the action engages the targeted communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. This approach is built from a multisectorial point of view, which allows to understand deeply the needs and the possible responses of the community, considering the specific vulnerabilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The planned proposed interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWD to safe water and sanitation through the provision of safe water, tankered water, increasing the HH storage capacity, andrehabibilitation of WASH facilities in schools. While most of public schools in the West Bank need extensive infrastructure work, yet, girls’ schools are the most vulnerable and in need of such services due to girls’ personal hygiene needs. The project will target these schools, and provide the most needed WASH services. UNICEF and the partners respect a non-discrimination approach through ensuring the equality in the access of the outcomes and benefits of the project, some of the activities will directly promote the gender equality and the rights of children as a key element in the development, safety and dignity of the community itself. This approach will have a crucial role in the direct contribution to the beneficiaries’ coping strategies development. In this sense, some of the activities will promote these coping strategies in which is expected that young people have a crucial role. Coping strategies will not only be promoted through beneficiaries involvement with local councils and stakeholders, but also in a family and individual level acting through the different trainings and awareness campaigns as direct agents of change spreading the importance of the different roles in the community and sharing the load of care-work needed in order to prevent the spread of the different diseases.
