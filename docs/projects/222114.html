Project Summary

Health services is key in reduction of mortality and morbidity rates in South Sudan that has one of the highest rated of mortality and morbidity globally that can be attributed to weak health systems and other compounding factors related to conflict and climate change. These factors have had over the years affected the quality of health service delivery and thus affecting populations and increasing the people in need of health care services.
Attacks on healthcare is a major challenge to health service delivery as vandalism on health facilities and violent attacks on health care givers have hampered efforts to deliver quality health service delivery and continues to hinder investments in the health sector. This has led to poor health indicators as with very high maternal mortality rate, high infant and child mortality rates and low functionality of health services contributing negatively on the health service delivery.
Populations continue to walk long distances in search of health services and this has negatively affected the well being of the vulnerable populations. Displacements caused by intra and enter communal conflict as well as flooding in some parts of the country continue to affect health service delivery. In 2024 over 1.4 million people have been affected by flooding and over 359000 people displaced by the ravaging flooding that has overwhelmed the health systems in the country.
Disease outbreaks like cholera and measles continue to ravage the country that is still recovering from the devastating impact of COVID 19 pandemic and this urgent need to strengthen health systems and thus quality health service delivery.
This project will therefore improve the access to health services in Pibor, Bor South, Aweil center, Rubkona and Kapoeta East counties by provision of quality health services in line with the basic package for health and nutrition services through primary health care units and mobile health interventions to reach the vulnerable communities in the hard to reach areas.
The project will ensure delivery of health services through out patient consultations, reproductive health services that will include antenatal and post natal care services. Skilled assisted deliveries will be conducted by skilled health personnel, CRSF will also ensure that there is provision of EPI services throughout health service delivery to improve vaccination coverage in the community. The EPI services will also target zero dose children and under immunized to ensure that they are fully immunized and thus reducing vaccine preventable infections.
CRSF will also ensure that management of trauma and injury cases is done and referral of the critically injured patients to other facilities will be done. Other non communicable disease management like mental health services, management of hypertension, diabetes and detection and referral of cancer cases will be done.
CRSF will offer integrated services to all children under five years through screening for malnutrition. Vitamin A supplementation and deworming will also be done to the children visiting the health facilities. Pregnant and lactating mothers will as well receive nutrition services. Protection mainstreaming will be done with case management of rape and referral pathways for survivors of gender based violence.
CRSF will build the capacity of health workers both at the facilities and county health department to ensure that there is delivery of quality health services through well trained and informed staffs.
All the activities will be done in collaboration with the health cluster national and subnational level with weekly reporting on IDSR and EWARS and 5W reports monthly. Coordination with the county health department and other key stakeholders in the county and stated will be done fully.
CRSF is targeting 15000 (3500 male, 5000 female, 3500 girls, 3000 boys) in 5 counties with integrated health services through static and mobile interventions in 3 PHCUs and 2 Mobile units.

1. Needs

Health needs in the country remains quite huge due to weak health systems, lack of enough qualified medical personnel and dilapidated health infrastructure that led to lack of health services. Natural disasters and internal conflict have led to displacement of populations with flooding a major affecting over 1.4 million people and displacing over 356000 people in 22 counties (Flood snapshot Nov 2024).According to recently released IPC results, between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) (IPC Nov 2024). This situation compounded by poor health systems in the country and threats of wide spread disease outbreaks overwhelming the already strained health sector. South Sudan maternal and child health indicators have not significantly improved over the years and have remained very poor. The maternal mortality rate of 789/100000 live births according to UNICEF 2024 are among the worst performing globally. Child mortality rates are not better with key statistics showing the rates of 96/1000 live births still among the worst performers globally and therefore efforts to improve access to quality health services can be critical in improving this very important indicators.The Vaccination coverage in the country is below the WHO recommended threshold with the overall country vaccination coverage of 49% very low compared to the 80% recommended by WHO. According to the IPC report Nov 2024, Kapoeta East County has over 26000 people in IPC 4, Pibor has over 89000 people in IPC4, Rubkona county has 104000 people in IPC 4 and other two counties are having significant number of people in emergency IPC situation. Food insecurity is a major cause of health concerns as it leads to malnutrition and further opportunistic infections.Rubkona County is currently experiencing displacements due to flooding with over 24000 people affected by the floods (Flood Snapchat Nov 2024). health infrastructure has been greatly affected and supply of essential medical supplies continue to be affected by the flooding. Proximity to Renk county that’s currently having an active cholera outbreaks makes the situation in Renk County in need of urgent intervention.Pibor County has also experienced protracted crisis with interna conflict causing displacement of populations and thus exposing boys and girls men and women to adverse conditions that leads to exposure to diseases like respiratory tract infections. The county has high numbers of people with acute food insecurity who need support with integrated health care services.This is therefore calling for efforts to improve access to health services in the country to ensure reduced mortality and morbidity rates.

2. Project activities

• Outpatient consultations for common illnesses in health facilities and mobile units• Trauma and injury management and referral for severe cases to higher level health care services.• EPI services to infants and under immunized children with target of reducing vaccine preventable illnesses.• Vitamin A supplementation for children under five years old• Screening for malnutrition to all pregnant and lactating mothers and children under five years referral of the children to nutrition centers.• Management of Sexually Transmitted Illnesses in the Primary health care units and the mobile health units • Management of childhood illnesses like malaria, Diarrhea and pneumonia and other respiratory track infections• Infection prevention and control in line with and proper medical waste disposal.• Outbreak prone disease surveillance and prevention awareness• Health education on early health seeking behaviors and disease prevention measures.• ANC and PNC services targeting mothers to attend 4 antenatal care visits and a post natal care visit within the first two weeks after delivery.• Skilled deliveries by health workers targeting to have the deliveries done in the health facilities • Referrals of the complicated cases to higher level care.• Strengthening the health systems by supporting the county health departments and the ministry of health through coordination meeting and other key engagement meetings with the ministry officials• EWARS and IDSR reports to ensure all the heath data is reported to the support health service delivery in the country that help in good decision making• 5W reports to the health cluster• Mental health services and referral pathways for GBV survivors
