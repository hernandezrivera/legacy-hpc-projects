Project Summary

As the crisis in South Sudan entered its 5th consecutive year in 2018, 5.3 million people, (42% of the population) are in need of life-saving food assistance, an increase of 40% over 2017 of people facing severe food insecurity soon after crop harvest. East Coast Fever (ECF) disease has claimed more than 20,000 heads of cattle in the Jonglei state and its neighboring States (Lakes, Warrap and Equatorias) in the past 5 years. Losses caused by the disease are due to high mortality rates are estimated to be at USD 134,325 annually . Survival among the predominantly pastoral communities (displaced and host) has been buoyed up by food aid which is presently contributing up to 30% of food consumed at household level, and increased utilization and liquidation of livestock products and livestock assets among livestock owning households. Livestock derived food is making up to 60% for host groups and 81% for IDPs (up from about 42% in 2013) of the food basket in 2017/18. The pressure on livestock keepers to exchange their animals for food for survival has spurred a rebound of livestock markets in Western Jonglei (covering Uror, Nyiror, Duk, Twic East and Bor South). However, the concentration of livestock in confined spaces has significantly enhanced the spread of communicable diseases among livestock, resulting in losses of up to 35% in areas without services. The objective of the action is to improve or sustain access to animal source food and related income for crisis affected individuals at risk of malnutrition particularly children and women. This is aimed at impacting on health and nutrition of the affected pastoralists, thereby saving lives that may be lost through attributed excess morbidity. The project will target 7000 households in Bor South County, comprised of IDP, host and returnee groups with support to local institutions and mechanisms for delivery of Animal health and public health services. VSF Germany will employ two livestock protection interventions – vaccination and treatment using the Community based Animal Health Service approach. These exercises are carried out in accordance to the annual dry season vaccination plan issued by the Ministry of Animal Resources and Fisheries (MARF) & FAO which is based on region specific disease seasonal calendar. The exercises will involve mass vaccination of 100,000 heads of livestock and about 25,000 cases of treatment against East Coast Fever disease. VSF Germany will use its disease surveillance and response systems. Cattle camp chiefs communicate herd disease situations systematically to their Community based Animal Health Workers (CAHWs). This is then reported to Animal Health Auxiliaries, to Stock Persons and to Veterinarians who carry out preliminary investigations and where necessary make outbreak reports to VSF and MARF. MARF with support of VSF and FAO carries out confirmation investigations and defines immediate and longer term intervention plans.

Needs

Awareness campaign to the target community on East Coast Fever disease effects and controls: The targeted beneficiary in the Bor South County needs to be mobilized and sensitized on the objectives of the intervention and the specific activities to be conducted towards control of ECF. The community needs some education on the basics of ECF disease control especially on control of livestock migration, isolation/quarantining, proper carcass disposal, control of disease vectors (ticks) and importance of vaccination. The communities should be sensitized on the importance of their participation including the necessity to build cattle crushes with locally available materials so as to improve the efficacy during vaccinations. There is need to reduce reliance on foreign aid for animal health provision but allow the veterinary services to be rendered on cost recovery basis.Community animals health workers (CAHWs) capacity enhanced through trainings on extension and East Coast Fever (ECF) disease control methodsThere is need to identify ECF specific CAHWs based on standard CAHW selection criteria in close and active involvement of the Bor livestock owners. The CAHWs should be trained to diagnose the ECF and other common emerging diseases. The CAHWs should be trained to use the medications provided correctly, giving the right dosage to avoid under or over-dose, and practical demonstration on weight evaluation and drugs dosage and administration. Training should be done by a technical personnel and should use the government approved CAHWs training guidelines and manuals. Special emphasis is required to improve on CAHWs capacities in dissemination of extension messages on the ECF disease control. Curative animal health services specific to ECF and other common diseases: East Coast Fever (ECF) is a major problem on both the eastern and western banks of the Nile from south of Juba to north of Bor town. The disease has claimed more than 20,000 heads of cattle in the Jonglei state and its neighboring States (Lakes, Warrap and Equatorias) in past 5 years. Losses caused by the disease are due to high mortality rates estimated to be at USD 134,325 annually . Fear and outbreaks of ECF often leads to conflict among pastoralist in attempts to protect their animals from those fleeing from infected areas. Control of the disease has eluded veterinarians and requires a concerted effort of application of contemporary veterinary medicine using parvaquon and ethno-epidemiology. The concentration of animals in confined localities has also led to increased incidences of the disease causing a lot of losses. Livestock protected against ECF and other common diseases through mass vaccination Immense pressure to save lives and livestock assets has continuously driven livestock keepers to safe enclaves which are usually swamp or marsh lands, reducing spatial space and concentrating livestock and humans within close proximity for unusually prolonged duration of time. This fomented in many cases widespread outbreak of ECF in various cattle camps in 2017, mainly along the catchment of the Nile and its tributaries. Apart from causing several livestock death, it may deal a blow to the crucial sale of cattle to Ethiopia and Sudan with significant negative impact on food security of livestock dependent groups. Vaccination against ECF will appropriate in controlling the disease.

Activities

a. Mobilization and sensitization of communities on the effects and control of East Coast Fever Disease The beneficiary communities in Bor counties will be sensitized on the objectives of the intervention and activities to be conducted. They will be educated on the basics of ECF disease control including control of livestock movement, isolation, and proper carcass disposal, control of disease vectors and importance of vaccination. Target communities will be made aware that veterinary services are supposed to be rendered on cost recovery basis and whenever possible (depending on the situation) treatment shall be paid for so as to ensure sustainable delivery of animal health services in the target areas. VSF G will ensure compliance with the Livestock Emergency Guidelines and Standards (LEGS) at all times, community awareness campaign to sensitize the local communities on the project objective is conducted, cost recovery for the diseases treatment is implemented as planned, and sensitize and mobilize of the community to build cattle crushes in order to improve quality of the vaccination in done. b. Identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs),conduct a training needs assessment (TNA), training delivery and deployment of selected CAHWs to their respective locationsThe selection of CAHWs will be based on standard CAHW selection criteria and active involvement of the beneficiary community. The CAHWs shall be trained to diagnose ECF diseases as well as relevant emerging diseases. They will be trained to use the medications provided correctly, giving the right dosage to avoid under or over-dose, and practical demonstration on weight evaluation and drugs dosage and administration will be an integral part of trainings to be implemented. Training shall be done by the VSF G technical personnel using the government approved CAHWs training guidelines and manuals. c. Procurement of the CAHWs Kits (ECF and other common diseases) and prepositioningVSF G will provide the treatment kits that contain drugs, dispensing equipment (e.g. syringes and needles) and other relevant items. VSF G will procure the inputs and avail them to the CAHWs in the field in good condition. VSF G will ensure the ECF Kits are distributed to the targeted CAHWs according to the distribution plan. We shall compile the summary distribution data in both hard and soft copies.d. Vaccination for prevention and control of outbreaks VSF G will vaccinate the targeted number of cattle towards preventing ECF infections. The vaccination shall be undertaken as per guidelines for disease control coordination with the Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries (MLF). Each animal (cattle) shall be vaccinated against at least three diseases during the project period including ECF. VSF G will also support the cold chain system in the field through provision of technical support, cold boxes and vaccine carriers. e. Treatment of clinical casesVSF G will support CAHWs in treatment of affected animals using the appropriate treatment drugs and asking for payment whenever conditions permits. Livestock keepers shall be encouraged to report sick animals to the CAHWs as soon as ECF clinical signs are detected so that treatment is commenced early. f. Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of ECF diseases trends in the target areas. VSF G shall investigate all ECF outbreak rumors received in the targeted areas. The cases will be reported to the government authorities and the community will be kept informed of any suspected ECF disease outbreak reported. VSF G will carry out the preliminary investigation of the outbreaks and report to the authorities using the relevant disease investigation forms and procedures .CAHW supervisors will be trained to use the disease investigation form.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The projects main objective is to reduce the high mortality rates of livestock accelerated by the East Coast Fever disease infections that is Bor context prone. The pastoralists who are being facilitated to return to Bor South from the Equatorias are resisting because of fear of imminently high mortalities in Bor from East Coast Fever.Ticks specific to transmitting the disease is prevalent to the project location. The government wants them to return so as to leave behind a conducive environment for return of farming communities to Equatoria. Their presence in Equatorias is affecting peace and also agricultural production due to pastoralist-farmer conflict. All the activities shall aim at addressing the project objectives hence designed to prevent any further escalation of the disease that will lead to more migrations of the pastoralists. Messages and information provided to beneficiaries during training and community dialogues shall be specific to creating awareness and addressing the control measures towards reduced infections.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In the project design phase, through Community Dialogues, the program engaged with men, women and young adults in the planning stage to advocate for increased coverage of the program to remote areas where ECF is prevalent. The program further advocated for prioritization and involvement of female headed households during vaccination and treatment events so as to accommodate their work schedule thereby reducing a lot of inconveniences to them. Intervention scheduling, monitoring and feedback planning will be carried out with all groups, including old persons through Community Dialogue meetings. Equal participation will be maintained during monitoring, new assessments and at end term evaluation. Data collection and reporting will continue to be disaggregated by gender. Despite previous low ratios, efforts will be maintained to improve and involve all genders among field staff and CAHWs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The feedback mechanism for the program is based on the traditional system of entitlement where rights to community or clan resources are declared in public meetings. In this case, entitlements to the program services are pronounced at Community Dialogue meetings before the start of any activity and procedures agreed on. The redress system involves first a beneficiary making complaints or concerns to the CAHW as the direct service provider. Beyond this, the cattle owner takes their issue to the cattle camp leader or the clan chief who takes up the matter with the CAHW team leader. If necessary the CAHW supervisor, the Field Officer and the Project Officer are involved. The second channel is through the local administration starting from the chief to the Payam Administrator; then the Relief and Rehabilitation Commissioner; and further to the County Commissioner. This process is also based on the fact that all the relevant authorities are participants in the Community Dialogues. From the level of RRC and the commissioner, concerns are made to the Country Directors.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

the project will work closely with the community leaders and members to ensure maximum participation during field visits to collect data. Through consultation and engagement, the community members will support filling the monitoring forms. the monitoring forms will capture equity in access to vet services in terms of sex, age, disability, ethnicity, vulnerability and locality. Monitoring visits by project and program managers are routinely carried out to verify field reports. They will capture data on small ruminants, poultry and dogs separately and also include information and education to children and women regarding ECF, general Livestock husbandry, zoonosis and safety. The planned beneficiary focused monitoring will entail conflict dynamics of each beneficiary community and group in regard to revenge attacks, cattle raiding and politically instigated/motivated animosities that may pose security risk to beneficiaries as well as service providers and sharing of relevant information with staff, local leadership and other stakeholders. The community will be involved during monitoring missions to provide information and their views to discrimination, abuse and exploitation with particular attention to women, children, older people, people with disabilities, minorities. CAHWs will be detailed to seek and serve these people in their communities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the present context of conflict and insecurity, access to water for domestic use, firewood and market for animal products by women are significantly constrained and highly exposes them to risks of exploitation. This is further compounded by increased absence of male heads of households forcing the females heading household to directly get involved in males activities like grazing. The programs intends to enhance equal and safe participation of women through training in basic community health skills that will encourage them to part of the community resources caretakers. During community dialogue sessions, the male participant shall be sensitized on the importance and values of allowing female participation in the community development. The program will further encourage equal participation during activities implementation planning ensuring that all member are involved at self convenient levels. The girls will be trained on basic diagnosis skills to the ECF disease hence support in the surveillance and reporting of the disease since in most cases they are frequently involved with the livestock during milking and grazing of the young stocks.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring process shall ensure it captures the extent of participation and involvement of the beneficiaries to the program. Through the monitoring forms, the program will capture data on how small ruminants, poultry and dogs separately were supported with the community animal health providers. They will also include measure of level of information passed to the children and women regarding general animal health and husbandry.
