Project Summary

In light of the protracted nature of the crisis, the proposed intervention aims to respond to both emergency and post-emergency needs of conflict-affected communities in the Southwest and support long-term solutions to protracted displacement over a period of 36 months. Through a combination of protection, shelter/NFI and livelihoods interventions, DRC will provide a comprehensive response at all phases of the displacement cycle, transitioning from emergency to early recovery. DRC will build on its access strategy to design this two-pronged approach programme, focusing on the emergency needs in both hard-to-hard and peri-urban communities; and on post-emergency needs of populations living in less sensitive peri-urban areas.
To ensure that the most at risk are targeted and can benefit from DRC’s expertise based on needs and vulnerability, DRC’s approach will identify all beneficiaries of all sectoral interventions of this programme via the protection team, using protection criteria, and in order to reach protection outcomes. Once the identification of individuals’ needs will be done, DRC will internally refer the most vulnerable individuals to the different sectors. The integrated approach of this programme could be further enhanced by using multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to cover the multi-faceted needs of extremely vulnerable households, which DRC ambitions to pilot in peri-urban areas. For the shelter/NFI component of the action, DRC used a combination of socio-economic vulnerability and sector specific vulnerability criteria to identify not only those without an adequate access to shelter and NFI items but also those suffering the greatest financial barriers to meeting their basic needs. The socio-economic vulnerability ranking prioritised households with disabled or chronically ill family members, pregnant and lactating women, single and female headed households as well as those supporting unaccompanied minors. Distribution sites are chosen together with the community with the safety and accessibility of beneficiaries in mind. Special attention is given to the needs of vulnerable groups such as elderly, people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and children headed households to make sure they are able to access the distribution site and are given priority during distribution. Distribution days are previously agreed upon so as not to interfere with activities such as market days.
The proposed approach is based on the “Do No Harm” principle and is conflict-sensitive, with activities tailored to the specific context of Southwest Cameroon. One of the pillars of this principle is to ensure local ownership. DRC will build the capacities of Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPC) and Protection Focal Points (PFP) already formed during previous actions and identify members in new communities of intervention. In addition, Community-based Targeting Committees (CTC), respecting a status, gender and age balance will promote local acceptance and transparency in the beneficiary selection process.
DRC’s engagement with civil society is at the heart of its programming and part of the “Go local” initiative promoted by DRC 2025 Global Strategy. In the Southwest region, DRC has been partnering with the NGO LUKMEF since 2021 and initiated a partnership with Grace Chin in 2022. DRC seeks to expand and strengthen equitable, long-term, and strategic partnerships with local NGOs to provide humanitarian assistance in the most effective manner and to foster local capacities. To realise this ambition, DRC is to launch a Call for Expression of Interest.
The programmatic strategy’s key objective centres around providing continuous support to the conflict-affected populations in DRC’s current intervention areas (Fako and Meme divisions) while also expanding to the Kupe-Manenguba division.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Between September and November 2021, DRC conducted a household survey in Meme and Fako Divisions, assessing over 4,500 individuals to identify the most vulnerable families to benefit from DRC’s food assistance activities. In addition to crisis levels of food insecurity, the assessment highlighted the dire shelter conditions of the target IDP populations. The assessment found that 72% of those assessed either had no access to shelter, sleeping out in the open, or only had access to a makeshift shelter. Furthermore, 86% of the surveyed population reported a loss of income/employment or productive asset because of the crisis, resulting in financial barriers to accessing their essential basic needs. DRC will expand its geographical coverage and start implementing activities in Kupé Muanenguba for the first time based on the high needs and lack of actors operating in the area. Findings of the MSNA conducted in August 2022 indicate that conflict-affected populations in Kupé Muanenguba include internally displaced households, returnees, and the host population. In terms of Shelter/NFI needs, many IDPs are renting (76% in Tombel and 23% in Nguti) and 1% in Bangem. The average cost of rent is XAF 3,500 and IDPs are oftentimes unable to pay it regularly and face the risk eviction. The displaced population is in increased need of shelter and NFI as many live in makeshift shelters lacking minimum standards of privacy and protection. In addition, these shelters are damaged and the displaced fear that they will collapse during rainy periods. This situation is a risk factor for children and women, who are exposed to disease and gender-based violence. Essential household items are insufficient (cooking utensils, sleeping materials, water supply equipment in priority), which make the living conditions of conflict-affected communities even more difficult. In addition to secondary data collection, DRC will conduct its own multisectoral needs assessment in Kupé Muanenguba to better inform the proposed design of the action. This will include a rapid protection needs assessment as well as a Shelter/NFI needs assessment that will also inform the NEAT+ analysis to ensure that the action includes Minimum Environmental Requirements. The attention to environment will notably be crucial through the NEAT+ analysis to adapt the intervention for Shelter/NFI. This will include ensuring sustainable supply chains, promote the use of sustainable materials and certified suppliers, and reducing to the maximum the material CO2 footprint as well as the land footprint. The proposed intervention will build on the findings from the last PDM conducted by DRC to gather feedback from beneficiaries on the NFI vouchers and shelter kits distributions in Kosse and Mabeta in July 2022. It comprised 120 respondents on a total population of 400. - The intervention allowed to respond to unmet needs as 88% of the surveyed beneficiaries indicated they did not receive similar SS/NFI from another NGO and 93% of the respondents reported that they received their NFIs when they needed it the most.- 84% of the sampled respondents live in Carabot shelter, 13% in makeshift shelter while the rest live in other types of shelter like bamboo houses.- The first 6 most urgent items needed by respondents were pots, zinc, mattresses, nails, blankets and tarpaulin. - 38% of the sampled HH rated the quality of shelter items to be ‘very good’, 56% ‘good’ and 6% ‘fair’.- 62% of the sampled HH rated the quality of NFI items to be ‘very good’, 31% ‘good’ and 6% ‘fair’.- 100% of the respondents reported the distribution did not cause any security problems after DRC distribution team left. NFI and shelter respond to specific protection risk related to denial of right to live with dignity and have the security of a shelter. It also greatly reduces protection risks such as GBV, physical violence and other human rights violations.
