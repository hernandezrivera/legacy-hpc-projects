Project Summary

Men and women, boys and girls with disabilities are at heightened risk of violence and abuse before and even more during humanitarian crisis. Already, prior to the crisis, they often face disability-based discrimination and rights violation in the different social spheres of life. In the majority of societies, persons with disabilities have started to address those violations and have gained increasingly influence over the decisions affecting their life through for instance representative organizations. As a result, legal and policy frameworks were adapted and/or newly developed to address systemic barriers and promote equal rights enjoyment. This shift towards a rights-based approach to disability-specific discrimination and rights violation has arrived in the humanitarian field only recently, gaining increasing force in 2016 during the humanitarian summit in Istanbul. In the subsequent years, more evidence highlighted the gaps experienced by persons with disabilities during humanitarian crisis. First humanitarian operational policies were developed or adapted to guide and hold humanitarian actors accountable to shift gear and address the gaps in access to and meaningful participation experienced by persons with disabilities during humanitarian emergencies.
The project will further advance disability-inclusive humanitarian action by supporting the operationalisation of the IASC Guidelines in humanitarian programming and the humanitarian system. To achieve this, CBM intends to train local and international humanitarian actors on the inclusion of persons with disabilities in the humanitarian response in line with the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action.
