Project Summary

This project seeks a 12-month period starting January2021 to provide Critical and Lifesaving WASH Services to Vulnerable Populations in hard to reach areas of Aweil South County Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Tonj East, Tonj North in Warrap state The overall objective of this project is to provide integrated multi-sectoral interventions in WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Health and Protection to vulnerable and affected populations to be implemented by Humanitarian Aid for Change and Transformation (HACT) This project is designed to provide intervention starting at nutrition facilities targeting the Households of – severely malnourished child” aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle by improving access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities Under Health and WASH component HACT will work to Reducing prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of outbreak by transmission contexts upon epidemiological investigation working in coordination to the Health partner in the targeted location. HACT has been on the frontline in COVID 19 awareness and basic hygiene promotion to combat the spread of COVID 19. This project will continue to provide the right information on Controlling the spread of COVID 19 through appropriate WASH response. HACT response will involve WASH-specific activities for GBV mitigation including safety audits on existing and new WASH infrastructure along-side referral services to survivors. In perennial floods area, HACT will provide access to basic services in emergencies involving Sustained access to water with flood-resilient infrastructures targeting Tonj East, Tonj North, and Aweil South County area with high WASH vulnerability. Provide permanent access to water and sanitation services for IDPs, returnees, hosts, and chronically affected by floods to build resilience to communities likely to suffer from famine according to the IPC report of October 2020. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risks associated with poor WASH conditions in the proposed counties, and it also will complement existing long-term Protection, Health, WASH, FSL, and Education activities that implementing partners are executing in the targeted Counties.
The project will provide safe water to the community through the rehabilitation of boreholes and the distribution of MHM kits to women of reproductive age and adolescent girls.
Monitoring of the cross-border movements of South Sudanese refugees spontaneously returning to South Sudan and provision of lifesaving GBV, Child Protection, Psychosocial Support, and Trauma healing services.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been confronted with many challenges. These include large population displacements, prolonged floods, skyrocketing hyperinflation, protracted civil conflict, serious food insecurity, and effects of COVID 19. The latest Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS) indicates direct correlations between inadequate WASH services, Malnutrition, Food Insecurity, and disease with communities exhibiting high levels of malnutrition and food insecurity lacking adequate access to safe water and sanitation facilities. FSNMS reporting only 15 percent of HHs reported ownership of three key WASH items - buckets/jerrycans, soap, and mosquito nets. These key indicators show that WASH infrastructure coverage alone, for instance, water points and latrines, is weak in the targeted locations. The counties targeted fall under IPC 3 and above phase classification projections for the earlier part of 2020. Chronic WASH needs continue to surface in parts of NBeG, and Lakes mainly affecting women and children majority of these multiply displaced. OCHA estimates close to 488,096 persons in 23 counties targeted are in need of humanitarian support due to adverse effects of floods by September 2020. Torrential rains in the months of August – December 2020 resulted in massive flooding thereby affecting access to basic needs. The areas targeted host vast numbers of IDPs, which despite integration into host communities, tend to display higher chances of vulnerability. WASH cluster projections for 2021 and vulnerability assessments estimate a total of over 463,853 persons will be in need of a Famine response package for WASH in 10 priority counties. HACT targeted counties include Aweil East NBeG, Tonj East and Tonj North in Warrap state with a total 110, 914 population of need targeted with famine response package Remoteness and access issues in/to locations of NBeG have further worsened the already dire situation with the majority of the locations assessed lacking essential WASH infrastructure, communities reliant on unsafe water due to inadequate safe water sources. Less than 30% of the communities assessed reported their main source of drinking water is an improved source (borehole, tap stand & water-yard) and available in under 30 minutes, with over 70% of the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the main source of drinking water is an unimproved source (river, swamp, unprotected well), (REACH, 2019). Water points are shared by both animals and humans leading to breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitation and repairs. Cultural practices and attitudes towards excreta disposal and personal hygiene have further undermined efforts to achieve WASH prospects. Villages assessed displayed crude methods of excreta disposal thereby raising risks of water-related diseases. Less than half of the population uses latrines, due to unavailability, safety concerns, knowledge, and attitudes amongst other challenges. Protection risks have been increased to girls and women due to insensitivity in gender during WASH project implementation. Girls and women stray into bushes for convenience due to a lack of adequate, safe, and dignified sanitation infrastructure, with cases of harassment reported especially amongst IDP populations. Safety at water points makes women and girls resort to nearby contaminated water sources. A lack of dignity, hygiene, and NFI kits further hampers access of females to common basic services such as education. There’s equally a direct correlation between poor WASH and malnutrition especially amongst under 5s and pregnant and lactating women. (HACT 2020 February) Insufficient WASH facilities have played a big role in the rise of water related and diarrhea diseases, which have undermined efforts to reduce levels of malnutrition High prices have made it difficult for the population to access WASH consumables

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project provides beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through AAP committee in place and Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in place. Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is incorporated in all training manuals and also form part of staff induction including staff code of conduct. All project volunteers are selected from the affected population and are provided with training and information on PSEA and referral pathway. The project will employ a range of most effective strategies for preventing abuse. These will be those that promote positive roles and valued status for people with disabilities throughout the community. These are; Community inclusion, Enhancing valued status, Raising awareness, Supporting families and cares, Creating safer services, and Building a person's confidence.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HACT has designed this project to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through an inclusive AAP committee and community feedback and response mechanism in place with well-trained staff to handle the exercise. The project will conduct regular field monitoring visits. During these visits project staff will hold guided focused group meetings, with project beneficiaries. This aimed at receiving feedback on the level of satisfaction of project implementation, and the project outcomes

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

We emphasize transparency in project implementation by directly involving the community in every stage of the project to ensure a clear understanding of the objectives of the project, expectations and stakeholders. HACT has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies, and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions. HACT ensures the facilitation of the provision of feedback from affected people on the services. Suggestion boxes are fixed at all HACT field offices to maximize inputs from communities. Information will be available to local communities in local languages; Teams are recruited with attention to a balance of women and men, cultural diversity, and age. Staff, volunteers, and consultants, both national and international, are provided with adequate and timely inductions, briefings, and clear reporting lines that promote positive organizational behaviors and enable staff to understand their responsibilities, work objectives, organizational values, accountability commitments, key policies, and local context. HACT works with partners and other stakeholders to ensure the needs of the most vulnerable are addressed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project is designed to use the beneficiaries’ feedback to reach the most vulnerable persons in the community. Our common forms of feedback from the project beneficiaries have professionaly on may occasions come to the project team notice through M/E repots, interactions with beneficiaries at service points. Each time this information comes to our notice, we align it with our Risk management framework { Asess; measure, mitigate and monitor}; project senior management use this to devise solutions based on our risk matrix and engage priority stakeholders to improve the project outcome. For instance; risks related to poor targeting and missing representation of most vulnerable households in any round of WASH NFIs distribution; the post-distribution monitoring exercise helps us to fix the risk and target the beneficiaries in the next round of distributions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

HACT will work to mitigate beneficiaries’ exposer to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services through AAP committee information sharing that seeks to reduce such associated risks including PSEA. This response includes Community Response and feedback Mechanism handled by trained staff with a clear referral pathway working in coordination with existing humanitarian actors in the project area. 90% of the project staff will be from project locations who understand project area and security situation and impending disputes.HACT gives the main concern on applying and committing to Do No Harm principles through all project design, activities, and results. HACT will save no efforts on following these principles and mainstreaming the protection through this project life cycle, including consulting with the stockholders, coordinating with partners and cluster, ensure a safe and productive environment, providing equitable access to project services without discrimination, and promoting the social cohesion. HACT will therefore apply the following principles during risk protection and application of mitigation measures. 1.Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: HACT takes great concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. But being safe is not enough if people do not have their dignity: people need to feel valued, have a sense of self-respect and feel they have some control over their lives. Prioritizing safety and dignity can also help to prevent or minimize the possible unintended negative effects that our interventions can cause for beneficiaries. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, HACT will seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Conflict and divisions within society often emerge from discrimination resulting in individuals or groups being unable to access services. Barriers to access can exist in different forms, including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity, and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: HACT will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Mechanisms for collecting feedback will use a combination of channels. Channels include active channels, where project beneficiaries perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); reactive channels, where project beneficiaries have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines); and finally, ‘open channels’ where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. 4. Participation and empowerment: HACT would strive to put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and beneficiary-focused. Special attention will also be paid to ensure the participation of and consultation with, the most at risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. Humanitarian programming should also seek to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including (not exclusively) the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.Protection risk awareness is one of HACT's key tool used to ensure humane access to humanitarian assistance where ever there is Need. Therefore our staff and external service providers are expected to adhere to our protection policies within humanitarian response.

We will ensure that disability issues are considered in all programming and where disability-specific actions and programming may also be requiredHACT recognizes that mainstreaming is simultaneously a method, a policy, and a tool for achieving social inclusion, which involves the practical pursuit of non-discrimination and equality of opportunity. HACT will recognize persons with disabilities as rights-holding, equal members of society who must be actively engaged in the development process irrespective of their impairment or other status, such as race; colour; sex; sexual orientation; language; religion; political or other opinion; national, ethnic, indigenous or social origin; property; birth or age’. Children with disabilities will share most basic needs and facilities with other children in society, so HACT recognizes mainstreaming as the most cost-effective and efficient way to achieve equality for persons with disabilities. An evaluation of Norwegian efforts to mainstream disability; for example, found that such efforts were poorly designed and insufficient.
