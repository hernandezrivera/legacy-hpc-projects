Project Summary

The project objective is to respond to the Health and Nutrition cluster objective to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
This project aims to provide urgent response to immediate health needs by ensuring access to health services for the most vulnerable patients (pregnant, lactating women, neonates and children under age of five; boys and girls) through the provision of medical supplies, and targeted outreach postnatal home visiting services.
The project will respond to the key components of the Health and Nutrition cluster emergency response plan, namely procurement of essential medical supplies to increase the availability of drugs, disposables, and medical equipment to ensure accurate diagnostics and treatment for high risk pregnant and lactating women including victims of or at risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), newborns and children under the age of five (boys and girls) across the five governorates in the Gaza Strip. Furthermore, access to quality newborn and child health care will be improved through further enhancement of health workers’ (doctors and nurses; females and males) capacity on lifesaving health interventions, including essential newborn care, and provision of early intervention services to children with developmental delays and disabilities and strengthening communities’ preparedness, response and resilience, especially during crises. The project will be implemented in close coordination with Health Cluster partners including WHO, UNFPA, MAP-UK and local NGOs to ensure complementarity of the interventions for the improvement of maternal, newborn and child health care.
The total number of women and children that will benefit from the HRP funding will be 62,100 across the five Gaza governorates over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that 17,000 high-risk women and caregivers and 45,000 children under age of five (22,950 boys and 22,050 girls) and 100 health professionals (doctors and nurses; female 60 per cent and 40 per cent males) will benefit from urgently needed lifesaving health interventions.

1 - Needs

The total population in need in Gaza, 627,070 (49 per cent are female), and over 72,000 children under age of five are at high risk of life-threatening conditions (e.g preterm, low-birth, congenital diseases, acute respiratory infections and water-borne diseases, violence), developmental delays and disabilities. The health sector has been over-stretched to respond to the high numbers of casualties from incidents at the fence which has led to an estimated 22,500 cases of not being able to access (fully or at all) essential healthcare services. This includes response to the health needs of high-risk pregnant and lactating women and young children under age five. According to the Ministry of Health (MoH), of the total annual pregnancies in Gaza, 27,600 are at high risk, with 23 per cent of preterm births and around 10,000 neonates (MoH Gaza Birth Report, 2017) in need of transfers to neonatal intensive care units for immediate health care and early intervention. Furthermore, in the Gaza Strip, there was an increase in maternal mortality from 2017 to 2018, from 5.9 to 16.7 deaths per 100,000 live births respectively, with 63% of mortalities ante-partum suggesting significant gaps at the primary healthcare level. The increasing trend of maternal mortality is likely to continue in 2019. Adolescent girls in Gaza are at particularly high risk, as 28.6% of adolescent girls are married before the age of 18 and 25.1% of women aged 20-24 had a child before the age of 18. Maternity and neonatal wards in Gaza are overstretched due to their limited numbers. This leads to the early discharge of mothers and their babies within 2-3 hours after birth and with no access to post-natal home visiting services (PNHV), which reduces opportunities for detection of potential medical complications and provision of lifesaving interventions. Recent findings of the external evaluation of the UNICEF’s supported PNHV programme proved the effectiveness of the PNHV interventions for high risk pregnant lactating women in Gaza. The report’s findings argued for the relevance and efficiency of the PNHV targeted interventions particularly for the most vulnerable and hard to reach pregnant and lactating women and new-borns in Gaza. In addition, the inconsistent supply of medical items negatively affects the quality of care. Gaza’s Central Drug Store (CDS) reported in August 2019, 69% of essential maternal and child health drugs were at zero stock. There were 516 essential medicines at less than one month’s stock while 40 per were completely depleted, and 30 per cent of the 853 essential disposables were at less than one month’s supply (HNO, 2019). Access to quality maternal and newborn health care has further deteriorated due to decreased electricity supplies per day undermining basic health care services already compromised by the blockade, recurrent hostilities and restrictions on the import of medical supplies. Therefore, the life of 10,000 newborns which require immediate neonatal health care interventions might be severely jeopardized with intermittent and insufficient electricity supplies. Gender equity in the Gaza Strip is already compromised. As reported by the Health Cluster, the influx of injured in the context of the “Great March of Return” demonstrations has exacerbated the already fragile situation of the health system in the Gaza Strip and will negatively impact the response to high-risk pregnant and lactating women, where medical services, equipment, consumables and drugs are diverted to respond to injuries at the detriment of those needed for this target group. This project is designed to contribute significantly to the gender equality and gender-sensitive programming focusing on the needs of gender-specific vulnerabilities faced by high risk pregnant and lactating women and their new-borns and boys and girls under the age of five.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will partner with the relevant stakeholders, NGOs and Health and Nutrition Cluster agencies to address identified needs and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and child health interventions through the implementation of a comprehensive package of services in the selected 8 maternity and neonatal hospitals, clinics and communities in 5 Gaza governorates. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with implementation of the WHO’s, UNFPA’s and MAP-UK’s HRP 2019 projects and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the Gaza Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. In order to reach the 2020 HRP objective “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities defined under the Health and Nutrition Cluster (HNC) will be implemented: Activity1.1. Ensure that people have access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring that clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available at all times through provision of essential supplies for neonates, children under five and high risk pregnant and lactating women, training, community awareness and establishing a clear referral mechanism for obstetric and newborn emergencies by working at the community, primary healthcare and hospital level focusing on the access restricted areas (ARA) which will be implemented by local NGOs . This will be fully coordinated with WHO, UNFPA and MAP-UK complementing their intervention in maternal, newborn and child health. UNICEF will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting. Programme officers will conduct regular field visits to the project sites and will review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to systematically ensure quality of the interventions during Programmatic visits. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. The programmatic visits will also assess bottlenecks and barriers, challenges and lessons learned. Data will be collected, analyzed, and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Gender will also be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved. Early engagement of selected communities particularly from North Gaza, Rafah and Khan Younis will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Target beneficiaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. Many mothers who have been consulted during the focus group discussion have reiterated the needs for targeted PNHV services in hard to reach areas of the North Gaza, Rafah and Khan Younis, awareness sessions on nurturing care (e.g. health, protection, responsive feeding, early learning), and intervention services for children with disabilities. During the project implementation, channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance to the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.
