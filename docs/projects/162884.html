Project Summary

This project aims at building the resilience of the poorest, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children. It targets 1,500 communities and 329,000 vulnerable children (50% girls and 2% children with disabilities) with below breakdown:
• IDPs Children: 54,000
• Returnees Children: 23,000
• Vulnerable Residents: 252,000
The project should provide integrated and sustainable assistance through interventions that promote resilience amongst affected populations and strengthen human security. The whole child approach using the Child Friendly Schools (CFS) framework needs to be clearly reflected with the following five thematic components: (i) Inclusiveness; (ii) Effective Teaching and Learning; (iii) Safe, Healthy and Protective Learning Environment; (iv) Gender Responsiveness; (v) Community and Child Participation.
The activities will be implemented under three outcomes as follows:
Outcome 1: More out-of-school children, especially girls, marginalized groups and those in the most vulnerable situations, are enabled to access quality pre-primary, primary and alternative learning opportunities with zero tolerance to hazards
To increase enrolment and reduce dropout of children from schools, multitude of interventions will be provided in a customised package based on actual underlying reasons. Target schools will be supported to enrol additional 50,000 Out of school children, to open ALP classrooms for 50,000 children and to establish ECE centres/ pre-school classrooms for 75,000 Children. Also, 6,580 Children with disabilities will be targeted in an integrated manner whether through training of teachers, providing specialized supplies, adding rams to Classrooms and latrines, etc...
Schooling supplies will be provided in form of Teacher kits, student kits, Recreational Kits, ECD kits, dignity Kits for girls, uniform sets, books and school furniture for a total of 329,000 children to attend schools. Cash assistance to 100,000 vulnerable children (1,000 SDG per school year) and income generating activities to women-headed households and to children with disabilities will be provided to support continuity of learning.
Outcome 2: Individuals, Education providers, parents and communities have improved knowledge, skills and pro-social capacity to contribute to quality learning outcome.
Building capacity of 1,500 communities and civil society to promote, enable and sustain behaviour change related to access to quality education. 22,500 PTA members will be trained on School Improvement Planning SIP before being supported with school grant (max 270,000 SDG per school) for income generating activity that will cover local school feeding, teachers’ incentives (ALP, Pre-School, School), school running costs and at least 50% reduction in school fees.
Also 1,500 school clubs will be formed and trained on upshift programme to build skills and create opportunity for the most disadvantaged young people and empowers them to identify challenges in their communities and create entrepreneurial solutions to address them. Also, the package of Unlocking literacy programme including its assessment pre- and post will be scaled up to support quality learning at 1,500 schools/ communities
Outcome 3: More communities have strengthened mechanisms to protect children from violence, abuse, and exploitation including FGM/C and child marriage for inclusive, safe and protective school environments.
The integrated approach of Education and Child Protection will be reflected in all education activities. It targets increasing awareness of 150,000 education personnel and community structures on the CP issues with signing of Code of Conduct (CoC). Moreover, safe referral pathways and complaint mechanisms between CBCPN, PTA and Education personnel will be established with estimated 6,000 cases referred to CP agencies/networks by education staff each month from the target 1,500 schools.

1 - Needs

Sudan has recorded steady progress in education, from an increase in primary school attendance to gender parity in primary classrooms. However, achieving Universal Primary Education remains a challenge. A recent report (UNICEF) concludes that Sudan has one of the largest numbers of out-of-school children in the Middle East and North Africa region. It’s estimated that more than 2.9 million children at primary school age (6-13 years) are not in the classroom. Only 76 per cent of primary age children attend school; in secondary that figure dips to 28 per cent. Access is particularly a problem in the eight conflict-affected states (Darfur states, South and West Kordofan and Blue Nile). The school enrolment rate in these states is currently 47 per cent, which is far below the average enrolment rate in the rest of the country (which stands at 72%). The Humanitarian needs overview for 2020 has identified almost 1.6 million vulnerable children in basic school age 6-13 years of which 132,642 are South Sudanese Refugees children. This project targets 404,215 children of which 75,215 are South Sudanese Refugees children which represent 25.4% of the total people in need under education sector.The inability to pay school fees - even though school is free according to government policy - prevents poor families from sending children to school. With low investment by government into education, schools are forced to apply school fees to cover the running costs for the school, as well as the incentives for volunteer teachers who are not paid by the Ministry of Education. Even if all the barriers to education would be somehow hurdled, once in the classroom the quality of learning is below the required standards. Teachers are often untrained, under supervised and unequally distributed between rural and urban areas. Instruction doesn’t lead to learning, the school environment is unfriendly or unsafe with inadequate separate sanitation facilities for boys, girls and teachers and materials tend to be not available or have inappropriate content. The Education Sector Strategic Plan (2018-2022) state that in order for Sudan to achieve a hundred per cent Gross Intake Rate, an additional 38,875 classrooms are needed. This implies that many of the existing schools need to be rehabilitated and new ones being build. The Sudanese education system is marked by high dropout rates making the achievement of universal primary education a big challenge. Retention rate in basic education dropped from 67 per cent in 2009 to 62 per cent in 2017.In 2017, there were a total of 218,000 primary education teachers, 11.4 % being volunteers. While the national Teacher/Pupil Ratio (TPR) based on Government teachers is 1:36, the uneven distribution of teachers results in a TPR as high as 1:63 in West Darfur state and as low as 1:17 in Northern State. Additionally, there is a weak correlation between the number of students and the teachers deployed to schools. As evident from the available date, the extent to which the distribution of teachers is based on school size is only 32 %. It is observed that schools with 1,000 learners can either have five or twenty teachers. Concerning teachers’ qualifications, the Education Sector Analysis (2018) shows that only 26 % of teachers meet the minimum requirements for teaching. There is an apparent shortage of textbooks with multiple children sharing books. At least four students share a science book, two students share a mathematic book and three students an Arabic language book. The results of the National Learning Assessment (2015) show persistent challenges in learning outcomes in basic literacy and numeracy skills. Despite being in grade three, 40% of children assessed show no awareness of any relationship between letters and sound. Less than half of those who can read comprehend what they read. In numeracy, less than half (46%) of the children can correctly solve level one addition and only 40 % can solve level one subtraction.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The integrated approach of Education and Child Protection will be reflected in all education activities and contributes to enhancing the protection of children in school environment and beyond, increasing their well-being, and improving their performance in education and other aspects of their lives. It allows for collective action and strategic advocacy aiming for safe and quality education for children in Emergencies. Increase awareness of Child protection issues, establishing of referral system and complaint mechanism are crucial results of this output. It targets increasing awareness of 150,000 education personnel and community structures on the CP issues with signing of Code of Conduct (CoC). Moreover, safe referral pathways and complaint mechanisms between CBCPN, PTA and Education personnel will be established with estimated 6,000 cases referred to CP agencies/networks by education staff each month from the target 1,500 schools.
