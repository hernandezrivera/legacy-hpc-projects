Project Summary

This project aims to improve the living standards of people affected by displacement in Cabo Delgado by improving/ supporting their shelters and providing household items. This project will target mainly the displaced and returned people, however, as part of the affected people, the host communities will also receive assistance to ensure the living environment is safe and dignified.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project targets both displaced, non displaced and recently returned populations with priority to the most vulnerable and in need, including those living in host community and IDP sites. The disaggregation by sex/gender, is contemplated in the project as part of the selection criteria to select the most vulnerable groups, such as children head of household, women head of household, women lactating, etc.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The overall project is integrated, bringing together three sectors, Shelter, Livelihoods, and protection through the provision of legal identity civil documentation. For the standing purpose, shelter & NFIs, the project will focus in Mueda, Mocimboa da Praia, Nangade, Muidumbe, Quissanga and Meluco districts in Cabo Delgado. This support will include an emergency shelter kit, tools and technical advice to facilitate access to covered living spaces that protect the safety, health and well-being of displacement-affected populations. As the project includes early recovery shelters as well as shelter upgrades, this second component, will be provided only to the returned families. NRC will provide technical support for (i.e.) stronger roofing anchoring, orientation for better ventilation, and locks to improve safety and security. Household items (NFIs) are essential to protect the health and well-being of the targeted population and are fundamental contributors to the process of starting a longer-term recovery will also be provided to the displaced as well as returning families. The implementation will preferably be delivered through cash/vouchers and whenever not possible through in-Kind

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Using community-based approaches and safe and inclusive programming, NRC will implement this humanitarian project through and with the affected community. NRC integrated protection-specific questions in each assessment and selection criteria to ensure prioritization of safety, and dignity, and avoid doing harm throughout shelter activities. The safety needs of men, women, boys, and girls will be identified through assessments prior to shelter interventions. In alignment with SPHERE standards, the privacy and safety needs of women and girls will be particularly important in the design of space usage to reduce any protection risks. Some of the highest protection risks reported in camp settings include lack of privacy, risk of intruders, and sexual violence, due to lack of lighting at night. NRC teams will engage in consultations through relationships with community leaders, FGDs, and representatives of different ages, gender, ethnic, and marginalized groups to ensure intentional safe and inclusive approaches to prevent and reduce exposure to protection risks and promote safety and dignity. Through community engagement, NRC will identify approaches that do no harm and promote gender equality, ensuring the inclusion of women in decision-making, community consultations, and design. The GBV risks will be mitigated throughout programming and informed responses will be identified in consultation with communities through FGDs and ongoing staff engagement to monitor any potential harm or threats.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

To ensure accountability to the affected people and create community acceptance before the implementation of the project, NRC will conduct community engagements to present the project to the communities. This includes discussing the design of shelters and planning distributions of NFIs to identify accessible locations and times for distributions that accommodate all individuals in a safe and dignified manner. In addition to the community engagements, the activities will be monitored by project teams to proactively ensure meaningful access and through post-intervention monitoring to assess potential gaps or community feedback to inform implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Through FGDs, assessments, and registrations, NRC will identify and include the PwD in the decision-making and ensure their protection and inclusion in all shelter and NFI-related interventions. During distributions, People with Special Needs /PwD’s committees will be included or ad-hoc focus groups will be consulted for the revision of shelter design, adapting sectorial shelter designs to their specific needs. To reach out to a representative sample of PwDs who might be unable to attend consultation sessions in the community, the NRC team will take care to include household visits at their dwellings during assessment, planning, implementation, and M&E activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Due to the nature of the proposed activities under this sub-sector, the localized environmental impact is relatively low. However, NRC prioritizes the use of Cash to give flexibility in best respond their unique needs including usage of few conventional material for the roofing, and walls such as mat mixed with Portland cement. This approach reduces the usage of items such as plastic tarpaulins. Shelter materials such as bamboo and wood will be procured locally through authorised suppliers by local authorities, who have been interested and insisting on the use of sustainable and controlled sources, mitigating the environmental impact. Other mitigation measures have already been taken by local authorities and humanitarian partners, such as the limited/reduced number of families per site or the use of efficient cooking stoves, in order to reduce the environmental impact in the surroundings of each reallocation site.
