Project Summary

Needs: The intersectoral severity of needs according to the HNO 2024 is extreme (level 4) for almost all districts in NWS. The top five priority needs reported by households were food, household NFIs, access to health care, and hygiene items, linked to sharp price increases for food, rent, and water. Activities: 1) Providing 8 rounds of MPCA (100 USD/HH/round) Targeting will include vulnerability and eligibility criteria as prioritization of female, child or elderly headed HHs, HHs containing PWD for 2200 HH, high dependency ration, HH with PLW, etc. 2) 4 rounds of MPCA (100 USD/HH/round) will be distributed, reaching 2.950 individuals (small business support). The 4 rounds of complementary MPCA for 590 HH bridges the gap until the businesses of the target group are operational. The targeting will include people with existing skills and previous experience who are able to set up a business, have literacy competencies and willingness to grow the business.
For monitoring purposes, WHH will use the harmonized PDM tool and will employ a multitude of monitoring activities such as field visits, KII and FGDs, PDMs.
The CRM system will be available for staff and beneficiaries to raise their concerns via WhatsApp, phone line and box. Communication with communities will be undertaken, and different consultations on eligibility and vulnerability criteria will be conducted.
PRA, incl. PSEAH; R1: Violation of PSEAH or CSG, CoC principles by staff. M1. Anti-corruption policies in place; CoC policies and training provided; Complaint and investigation mechanisms are operational; Presence of PSEAH FPs, trainings, visibility materials in the offices. R2) Risk of not reaching the most impacted members of the target population M2)clearly set eligibility criteria and tools and have adequate MoV; CBP committees as entry points, also for referrals; CRM mechanisms operational for BNF to contact the organization; Targeting those at heightened risk (intersectionality, vulnerability);Distribution systems are consistent, accessible by all groups and knownR3) Risk that BNF are not reaching durable solutions and can become vulnerable again M3) Linking BNF with livelihoods opportunities; Exit strategies in place. R4) lack of acceptable documentation and lack in means of obtaining them among BNF M4) Safe and informed referrals are made to legal actors. R5) increased tensions and GBV incidents M5) Women and other at-risk groups are involved in the design of programming; ensure at-risk groups have the same access to services to avoid tensions in the community which can result in tensions in HHs; capacitated staff. R6) Certain groups can’t access the distribution/redemption centers/locations of sessions due to barriers M6)Venues are evaluated to be central and physically accessible; Staff trained on inclusion principles. R7) Due to deterioration of the economic and/or security situation increase in forced evictions M7) Linking those affected with reception centers; Provision of cash/voucher for eligible cases. R8) Psychological distress due to conflict and worsened living conditions M8) Staff trained to refer BNFs with additional PSS needs; Provision of PSS groups in communities; Psychoeducation sessions provided
