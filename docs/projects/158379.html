Project Summary

This is a second part of a multi-sectoral project implemented by WHH in Azzaz covering food security and WASH. WHH delivers immediate support to people in need in the Euphrates Shield areas. The intervention has two components: (1) distribution of multi-sectoral (FSL-WASH) vouchers to internally displaced persons (IDPs) living in camps and (2) Trainings of Trainers (TOTs) on hygiene awareness and practices followed by hygiene awareness campaigns in the camps. This project entry focuses on the hygiene aspects of the project.
The first component of the project aims at covering the basic needs of IDPs in camps in the areas of food security and hygiene through multi-sectoral vouchers. Beneficiaries will be able to redeem the food and hygiene items from a list based on nutritional dietary and basic personal hygiene needs. The voucher value will be 60$ per HH per month and the each HH will receive it for three months. The activity will take place in two cycles and in each one 1,000 HHs will be targeted. In total, 2,000 HHs will benefit from the activity. The distribution will take place in IDP camps through blanket distribution. Vendors will be selected based on the proximity to the settlements and their supply capacity as well as the quality and price of items.
To complement this, the second component aims at increasing hygiene awareness in the IDP camps. Taking into account women empowerment, female camp members will be selected from the same camps and trained as hygiene mobilizers and develop a hygiene awareness campaign plan in the TOT. The TOT will also cover protection issues, allowing the mobilizers to recognize protection cases and act on them. This activity will also take place in two cycles; in each one 10 women will be identified and trained followed by the implementation of the hygiene awareness campaign plan over a period of two months. It is targeted at women specifically, as they are mostly the ones who take care of the children and the hygiene and sanitation of the settlement places. Also considering female hygiene needs, women are more vulnerable. In a second step, the hygiene mobilizers will be sharing the information they learnt in the training by implementing the awareness campaign, which will include household visits inside their camps. Lastly, they will reflect on their experiences and gather lessons learnt in a report. They will also have the chance to reflect on their experiences and discuss lessons learnt in a one-day-session with the trainer of the TOT in a follow-up session after performing the HH visits. The hygiene mobilizers will receive incentives for their work.
By training and engaging female hygiene mobilizers from among the target population WHH supports further engagement and participation of women in the humanitarian response. In order to create a combination where people in need will be able to redeem hygiene items through the vouchers and learn practical knowledge on hygiene, both components address the same target group.
The selection of camps will be based on humanitarian principles, where the most vulnerable camps will be targeted based on the less access they have to food and hygiene items. The project will be implemented inside IDP camps since the people living there had to leave their belongings behind, which puts them into a more vulnerable position. Assessments show that there is a functioning market in the area, however, lack of financial inputs of people limit people to access basic food and hygiene items; people either procure, borrow or work for food. Considering the dignity of people living in camps, the vouchers will allow them to redeem basic food and hygiene items. The distribution will be a blanket distribution in order not to cause any tensions among people in the community.

Needs

According to WHH assessments from July 2018, about 450,000 people reside in the districts of Northern Aleppo (Azzaz, Al Bab, Jarablus), 150,000 of them are IDPs from other regions in conflict-torn Syria, about one-third (50,000) reside in camps. Azzaz is the district with the highest number of IDPs. The Turkish authorities directly addressed WHH with regard to the high needs in the region. Two rapid assessments have been conducted by WHH in A’zaz, Aleppo governorate, between the dates 19 December 2018 – 10 January 2019. The main objective of the assessments was to gain an overview of the humanitarian situation in the informal camps in the targeted areas. The assessments focused on specific sectors which are food security, NFI and Hygiene items based on the scope of WHH activities. Key Informant Interviews have been conducted in 23 informal tented settlements in A’zaz, and in 6 central retailer-shops in A’zaz. A market condition assessment was conducted in April 2019. An overall compilation of humanitarian data regarding NW Syria was produced by the WHH MEAL department on 12 April 2019.Children and women make up 73 per cent of camp populations in north-west Syria. The disabled and children cannot access distributions because of overcrowding during distributions and the weight of the basket. There are also problems related to access to water, causing problems among beneficiaries. There exist high levels of family separation, with women becoming the primary family breadwinners as a consequence of the absence of male family members. Affected women and girls face distinct protection risks, including various forms of GBV such as sexual exploitation and abuse and early and forced marriage. Restrictions on mobility, access to resources and participation in the humanitarian response also varies across different sex and age groups. Women and girls, as well as persons with disabilities, may face barriers to accessing information, education, community participation and decision making at all levels. IDP and returnee female-headed households have more difficulties to afford a majority of hygiene items in comparison with female-headed households in host communities. For instance, affordability of baby diapers for female-headed households is an issue for 17 per cent of returnees and 8 per cent of IDPs, compared to less than 1 per cent of host communities. Markets across the country generally remain functional, and the majority of hygiene items are available for purchase. The majority of households were able to access adequate soap and hygiene products, but displaced household were less likely to access them compared with non-displaced households (76.0 per cent vs 84.7 per cent) and among female-headed compared with male-headed households (76.1 per cent vs 81.9 per cent). Lack of female hygiene products and clean water are reported to have led to health problems for women, including fungal infections, dermatological problems, and chronic pain. The major barrier to accessing hygiene items is therefore the severely reduced purchasing power of families in Syria, particularly those that are displaced, enhancing the need for market-based WASH programming. The functionality of markets on one side and the reduced purchasing power of vulnerable groups can be tackled by the voucher program for food and hygiene items, which WHH proposes in this project. Protection issues will be identified and can be addressed through hygiene mobilizers, which WHH proposes to send to the target camps. Continuous efforts are required for WASH staff to be trained on basic GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly refer GBV survivors.

Implementation of activities

The project is in line with the cluster response strategy of the water, sanitation and hygiene cluster. Through voucher distribution, these can be used to redeem hygiene items in accordance with the needs of the beneficiaries. The voucher distribution is sidelined by hygiene awareness activities. In order to reach the IDP communities in such a security-sensitive environment WHH will conduct a TOT for female hygiene mobilizers, who will later on perform household (HH) visits in their IDP camps as part of a hygiene awareness campaign they develop during the TOT. This is in line with WASH objective 2 of the HRP and related suggested activities: “WASH partners will consider market-based programming/assistance that includes cash and/or vouchers as viable option to mitigate the decreased purchasing capacity of most deprived families.” The camps will be selected in coordination with AFAD, and consequently checked by WHH team-members according to vulnerability criteria. As newly arrived IDPs are amongst the most vulnerable, camps with a high proportion of newly arrived IDPs will be specifically targeted. Within the selected camps, WHH will perform blanket distributions of vouchers in order to avoid conflicts between the camp members. This is based on the recommendations by AFAD. With regard to the voucher distribution, the HHs will receive their vouchers at the beginning of each month and have ten days to redeem items according to their needs. The list of pre-selected items is based on the recommendations of the Cash Working Group and adapted to the two sectors targeted here in consultation with the Cash Working Group. It will include items like bulgur, rice, olive oil, sun flower oil, lentil, salt, bathing soap, sanitary pad, baby diapers, laundry/dish soap, toothpaste. However, it will only be finalized and can be shared after the selection of the venders was completed taking into account item availability. The item list will then be shared with both, vendors and beneficiaries (BNFs). At the end of the redemption, the vendors will submit all the vouchers with the list of items that each BNFs redeemed, and WHH will make the payment accordingly to each vendor. For the hygiene awareness component, selected female camp members will discuss hygiene issues and learn practices to address these issues in the TOT. They will be trained on how to teach others and raise hygiene awareness within their communities. The trainer will support the participants in developing a hygiene awareness campaign plan, which includes HH visits in their own camps. In order to be able to consider protection concerns, they will also discuss sensitive protection issues and learn how to identify them as well as what to do, when they come across such cases. The training will last three to five days and be followed-up by a one-day session on lessons learned after the campaign. The HH visits will be monitored by the A’zaz team and reported to the Gaziantep team periodically. The coordination of the hygiene mobilizers will be organized by the project coordinator under the guidance of the Gaziantep team. After completing the HH visits, the hygiene mobilizers together with the A'zaz team will prepare a report which includes lessons learnt as well as recommendations for further interventions. The hygiene mobilizers will receive incentives for their work.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: WHH keeps operating directly and/or remotely in A’zaz even if a political change of control occurs during the implementation of the project.Associated Risks: - Possible security problems in areas of intervention, impacting movements and security of teams and beneficiaries. – Turkish authorities may not renew the authorization of implementation for WHH in case of major political change (e.g. GoS may claim; new OAG formation/s). - NGOs workers and/or facilities can be targeted during plausible security problems.Mitigation measures:• Relocating to a safer area is preceded by an in-depth access mission from the Welthungerhilfe Security advisor. Formal approval from the Security advisor for daily movements and daily security updates for relevant staff/consultant.• Remote management procedures have already been implemented in other project in Syria, allowing WHH to carry on implementation without disruption in case of evacuation.Assumption 2: Commodities to be procured remain available in Azaz at a competitive process matching quality requirements and prices estimated under the project. Roads inside Syria remain open to allow the transport of staffs. WHH is able to continue recruiting independently staff/consultant with the required level of qualification to carry out the project activities.Associated Risks: - Closure of trade roads and border crossing point due to clashes or political change impacting provision of required goods. -Roads to areas of intervention cannot be used due to lack of maintenance or damages due conflict activities or weather conditions. - Tentative interference from Local Authorities in WHH’s contracting or recruitment processes.Mitigation measures: • Diversification of suppliers as well as regular procurement and pre-positioning in advance. • Solid procurement and recruitment processes are in place.Assumption 3: No major change toward NGOs acceptance is observed in Azaz during the project implementation.Associated Risks: - Refusal of WHH’s project implementation by Turkish authorities and community leaders or violence erupting during activities implementation due to lack of acceptance of the project.Mitigation measures: • WHH has set up CRM in Azaz, so that stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues and easily express concerns. • WHH works as well closely with all actors on the ground in Azaz including Turkish authorities, camp managers, and obtains all necessary authorizations needed to conduct the activities.Assumption 4: The project does not create distortions in the local economy.Associated Risks: - As consequence of the project, there is a shortage of food items in the area and the prices increaseMitigating measures: • Vendors identification at the initial stage and provide vendors with necessary information to allow the supply of sufficient stocks. • Prices will be agreed upon in advance and advertised to beneficiaries. • Regular market monitoring.
