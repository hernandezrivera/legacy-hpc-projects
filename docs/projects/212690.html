Project Summary

In line with HRP SO1, DDD’s project for 2024-2025 aims to save lives by strengthening access to lifesaving and life-sustaining essential primary health services, protection referrals and infectious disease response for the most vulnerable people with high-severity needs and limited access to regular services in underserved urban settings and camps in Aleppo and Idleb. They will receive comprehensive Essential Health Service Package: PHC, SRH, and MHPSS through 9 fixed clinics provided by doctors, nurses, midwives, pediatricians, psychologists and CHWs.
There will be increasing focus on outreach services, including health promotion activities in PHCC, communities and people’s homes by CHWs. This will focus on people with chronic conditions, people living with disabilities (PwD), and at-risk pregnancies. In- demand MHPSS services will be expanded, with improved MHPSS consultations, dedicated safe spaces for women and children, and strengthened referrals to third-parties. In Idleb the services will be complemented by the medical waste management for DDD facilities and other providers in Idleb. PHCC services will be complemented by free medication procured by DDD's medical supply center complimentary to donations from WHO, addressing a significant gap in the region.
A total of 274,798 individuals (on avg. 57% female) from an estimated 392,247 catchment population will indirectly benefit from the intervention in 2024. The program will support 9 PHCCs: 3 PHCCs in Afrin (1. Afrin: B0203070 ! Afrin PHC 9; B0203087 ! 2. Jalma PHC: B0203087 ! DDD Jalma comprehensive PHC, 3. B0203069 ! Jandairis PHC 3.). And 6 DDD PHCCs in Idleb (1. Qah PHCC: G0703049 ! Qah village clinic/ Qah village; 2. Al Salam PHCC: Alsalam camp PHCC/ Alsalam Camp!G0703101; 3. G0703047 ! Aldoaa camp PHCC/ Aldoaa Camp; 4. Kelly PHCC: G0700164 ! DDD Kelly PHC, 5. G0703184 ! DDD Sarmada PHC; and Kemmune PHCC: G0703215 ! kemmune PHC).
The project has been developed to respond to needs identified by the community itself through DDD’s engagement mechanisms and strong links with local communities, as well as its MSNA in 2024. DDD's MEAL framework will continue to ensure results-oriented implementation, measure impact and adjust activities for improved quality. The MEAL plan includes direct supervision and monitoring at field level through 2 offices in Qah and Jandairis; monthly monitoring visits by coordination staff based in Antakya; constant tracking of consumptions of drugs and consumables; monthly reporting of situation, workload, morbidities, beneficiaries reach, and indicators progress; and data disaggregation by gender, age, host/IDP and disability. Annual program evaluations are also conducted to assess impact and any changing needs.
DDD’s close coordination with relevant structures and actors in the region has also informed the project design, to ensure that services are well-integrated and relevant. In the 2024-2025 period, the established coordination is planned to continue with Health, CCCM, and Protection Clusters, as well as Cholera/COVID-19 Taskforce, MHPSS WG, SRH Technical WG, NCD WG, the Access WG, the logistics WG, and the NGO Forum and AFAD meetings. In addition, activities will continue to be supported by cooperation with other partners: Mentor Initiative for leishmania diagnosis and treatment, Shafak for nutrition services, SRD for referrals, UOSSM for MH-GAP, Horan Foundation for ambulance services (Jandairis only), and SIG for vaccination.
DDD, as part of the Medecins du Monde network which has been responding to the health needs of IDPs and host communities in Dana sub-district since 2012 and in Afrin since 2018. Support for these 9 facilities in 2024 is essential to maintain availability of services to these populations in need. DDD had already been forced to close 4 PHCCs because of funding gaps since 2022.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NWS (Idleb and Aleppo) still the largest increase of health sector needs (PiN - HNO 2024); Afrin and Jandairis have critical gaps. Needs include: Access to free PHC services including NCD, child immunization and MUAC. CDs remain a priority with infection risks such as Leishmaniasis. Demand for home-based PHC/SRH is a priority, particularly for vulnerable groups such as women, WRA, elderly, and PwD: 23.5% of DDD HH have one PwD while women account for avg. 57% of DDD beneficiaries. -SRH access: Increased aid dependency and disrupted healthcare system has decreased the wellbeing of WRA: 30% of DDD respondents in the 3 locations require SRH services (incl ANC, PNC), while access to obstetric and new-born health care continues. -MHPSS: 75% of DDD Households (HHs) in target locations reported MH issues but only 8% accessed MHPSS professionals; avg 53% cannot access safe spaces for women and children and ongoing need for GBV referrals and specialized medical services. - Community outreach to increase beneficiary knowledge of health, COVID-19, Cholera and communicable diseases, and services must be improved. PHC services must be accompanied by the safe removal and management of medical waste in Idleb, where it is burned close to HHs. Essential Health Services Package (SRH, MHPSS, MUAC, NCD, CD, Community Outreach, GBV first-line support, referrals) in addition medical waste management. Services are directly implemented by DDD staff in Afrin and Idleb PHCC and Labs. Syrian Program Coordinator based in Istanbul (with support staff) oversees all Syria staff and NWS activities. Field offices in Qah and Afrin are managed by field coordination team in Antakya. PHC services are provided by qualified health care staff (doctors, pediatrician, gynaecologists, midwives, nurses etc) and PHC testing provided by lab technicians all overseen by the Medical Coordinator. Referrals are made by case managers to protection actors for GBV, CP, and other specialized care needed. Community Health Workers (CHWS) inform beneficiaries of available health care services, health issues Cholera and MUMPS. Two incinerators are operated for enhanced waste management in Idleb and be available to safely dispose of waste for DDD and partner facilities. PHC equipment, medical consumables and PHCC supplies are procured and shipped from DDD's medical supply center in Turkey to Syria. Regular cross-border monitoring is conducted to monitor activities; monthly collected data is disaggregated by gender, age and disability to improve services. Monthly exit interviews facilitate beneficiary feedback, satisfaction, and challenges for service access. Complaints Mechanisms are accessible to beneficiaries, stakeholders, and staff of different linguistic, literacy, and PwD.
