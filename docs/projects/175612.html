Project Summary

The response intervention focuses on provision of basic essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards and based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable groups like women, children and person with disabilities in Kapoeta East, North, South, Budi, Torit, Ikotos Counties of Eastern Equatoria and Maridi and Ibba Counties of Western Equatoria States targeting 114,825 (14,196 Male, 10,382 Female, under 5 years, 20,653 boys, 19,618 girls above 5 years, 21,619 males, 23,576 female between 16-49 years and 2,387 males and 2,394 Female between 50 -80 years) with health emergency support of different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected population, including COVID-19 pandemic context.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment & Needs has strong presence in these county for the last 4 years and involve the communities and HPF Implementing Partners along with the County Health Department in the planning of emergency health services through County Health and State Cluster coordination mechanism at state level to coordinate, collaborate, complements ongoing effort for responsive project design to implement, monitor and review activities.
The action will also support to streamlined and strengthened referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies. Referral pathways will be linked to the community and facility (primary, secondary and tertiary) based health care system to provide continuum of care.
In order to protect both patient and highly needed health staff, OPEN will encourage and support full of IPC/WASH measures including COVID-19 prevention and management activities as COVID-19 is expected to have a long-term impact on health service provision. Furthermore, inter-cluster coordination mechanism at all levels will work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other sectors including nutrition, food security and livelihoods, WASH, and protection clusters. Looking at challenging constrains in terms of resource mobilization, OPEN will implement Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (PHCC and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers., Conduct routine Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response during the implementation period, Support Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and Provision of BeMONC services with Support of RH commodities from core pipeline and building the capacity of skilled birth attendants, Procure 5 per cent of essential health supplies as back up for Core Pipeline supplies from WHO, Ensure IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health services. Conduct supportive supervision visits with County Health department for technical and operational assistance to heath facilities to ensure quality provision of health services and support community health volunteers to provide Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives in counties of Kapoeta East, North, South, Budi, Torit, Ikotos, Maridi and Ibba.

1 - Needs

The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report released in December 2020 estimated that periods between October to November 2020 , an estimated 457,000 people (4 1.6 % of the population) of Eastern Equatoria Counties faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity , of which 348,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 109,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity . During this period, all the counties of Budi, Torit Ikotos, Kapoeta east and South were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, except for Kapoeta North which were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity with the main drivers of food insecurity included insecurity stemming from political in stability, high market prices, loss of employment due to the COVID 19 pandemic, poor water and sanitation conditions, high cost of living due to the devaluation of the local currency against the dollar, serious illnesses , crop pests and diseases , high prices of fuel, and flooding especially in Maridi and Ibba Counties that resulted in to public health factors such as disease outbreaks, unavailability and low utilisation rate of health, WASH and nutrition services. Therefore, severe acute food insecurity will negatively impact the health of the affected communities through increased infectious diseases transmission due to insufficient macro-and micro-nutrient intake, with consequent decline in immunity. Children, pregnant and lactating women, and people living with HIV will be hugely affected given the prevailing health systems fragilities are exacerbated by lack of health care workers, poor water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management and expansive malnutrition necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health services to mitigate disease spread and enhance appropriates access and utilization of health care services to all most vulnerable population. The number of people targeted for healthcare services based on multiple factors including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Seasonal outbreaks of common diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a challenge to a health system which is already fragile._________________IPC_South_Sudan_Key_Messages_Oct_2020-July_2021.pdf

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The front lines health care workers recruited by OPEN are oriented on PSEA policy and signed as annex to the employee contract together with Condition services term and on monthly basis, The staff will provide PSEA information to beneficiaries in our various project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

OPEN will place a suggestion box and a compliance redress desk in each health facility that will receive all the complaints from the community either in written form or oral on daily bases during the working hours of the health facilities. These complaints will be channeled to OPEN to attend, address or forward to the next stakeholder. The feedback shall also be through both formal notification and oral communication to the community. OPEN will maintain confidentiality of the persons submitting feedback

The local Boma leadership structures, counties local government and other stakeholders shall timely participate in joint monthly and quarterly monitoring and evaluation of this intervention of recommendations drawn could be used to fill in the gaps

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

8.11. Mitigation Measures

OPEN’s sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment & Needs during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. OPEN also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment & Needs normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc.
