Project Summary

This project aims to provide emergency l WASH assistance to 145,560 beneficiaries (29,694women, 28,530 men, 44,541 girls, 42,795 boys) to address the scarcity of water supply,sanitation services and hygiene promotion for populations affected by armed conflicts, and natural disaster in target communities, and to contribute to the 2023 - revised WASH humanitarian Response in line with the revised 2023 PHRP strategic objectives: SO1: Provide timely multi-cluster life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity, and SO2: Mitigate protection risks and respond to protection needs through humanitarian action. Under 2023 HRP, the proposed RI’s emergency response will be in line with the cluster priority and immediate response which focus on (stabilization and restoration) and will ensure scaling up of the water trucking in areas where supply is disrupted including health facilities and the establishment of distribution points, including in gathering and transit points. Additionally, essential WASH supplies and NFI emergency sanitation and handwashing facilities will be provided in hotspot locations where RI is operating. Hygiene promotion and household water treatment practices will be promoted as an immediate alternative, especially for those reliant on surface/river water.
Through the proposed project, RI will reached people affected and those internally displaced due to ongoing armed conflict with provision of comprehensive WASH services and will also focus and on safe water provision for the Health facilities (through water trucking or pipeline connections), WASH NFIs distribution including dignity kits for women. RI will aim at rehabilitating rather than constructing water facilities in order to utilize the available resources effectively and reach the targeted IDPs and host communities in timely manner in Aljazirah, Blue Nile, North Darfur and Khartoum states. Renewable energy sources, like solarization of water yards, will be given priority during the phases, of project implementation in order to avail durable solutions and ensure continuous availability of safe water supply and reduction of the operation cost. Strengthening and capacity building of WASH staff and the Water user committees (WUCs)will be part of the proposed interventions. This will be done through formal training and ongoing coaching and support supervision of WASH project sites. The proposed WASH assistance will be integrated in health, nutrition, protection/GBV and other livelihood interventions. RI will work in collaboration and coordinate with other sectors including health,nutrition, FSL and protection/ SGBV.
By the end of the phase of project implementation, RI will rehabilitate 57 water points (9 water yard rehabilitation, 18 water yard solarization, and 30 handpump rehabilitations) and will, where needed, provide water provision using water truckingfor a number of health facilities in the localities of El fasher , Mellit, El Melha, Sarafomera,ElTaweisha, Um Kadadah, Kalemendo, Kebkabiya/Sortony, Alliet/Allite Camp in NorthDarfur; Wadmadani in AjJazirah , in located to be identified in Khartoum, El Roseires, El Damazine, Baw, Geissan, and Wad Almahi/Camp6 in Blue NileStates, where there is significant inflows of IDPs fleeing the conflict. Based on the ongoing armed conflict and assessment s of the WASH situation, RI will scale its WASH programme to do other localities where new IDPs and host communities will be in need of emergency WASH response. Overall a total of 142,848individuals will benefit from the water supply service provision provided humanitarian corridor is opened in the target locations. Furthermore, RI will reach about 6,000 people (1,224 Women, 1,176 Men, 1,836 girls, and 1,764 boys) withSanitation activities, and 145,560 people (29,694women, 28,530 men, 44,541 girls, 42,795 boys) will be reached through hygiene promotion activities in the target localities.

1 - Needs

According to the revised 2023 Humanitarian Response plan, the humanitarian needs acrossSudan is catastrophic. Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF has killed hundreds of people, forced morethan 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 736,200 people(out of which 368,000 children) inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict. In addition to large-scale displacement, the conflict has shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care and other basic services for millions of people. High insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices has compromised humanitarian access to key locations . In Sudan, the WASH needs have increased significantly in urban centres, which were very thinly covered under the 2023 HRP initially. Existing displaced persons, including the most vulnerable residents,and an increased number of displaced people in new locations, including international borders, are a matter of significant concern. The already limited water sources are under extreme pressure due to increased population concentration in new IDP concentration points, which carries inter-communal tensions and conflict risks; skyrocketing cost of fuel has resulted in a 300 to 400 per cent increase in the operation of fuel-reliant water supply systems as well water trucking. The rainy season will further heighten the risk of outbreaks in the coming months. Water-borne diseases and epidemics are a threat due to the deteriorating WASH situation, including no waste disposal services, water safety, poor hygiene, disruption of the supply chain, and access concerns. According to the WASH cluster, BN and ND are priority 2 while Aljazirah and Khartoum are priority 1. As per the Rapid Needs Assessment report of NRC for Wad madani locality of Ajjazirah state, 40% of the women didn’t have containers for water fetching or storage. Many of the locations affected by the current conflict has already protracted IDPs, with current waves of IDP arrivals further straining the limited basic services like water sources and sanitation facilities, overburdening the health facilities and generally exacerbating humanitarian conditions. The already limited water sources are under extreme pressure due to increased population concentration in new IDP concentration points, which carries inter-communal tensions and conflict risks; skyrocketing cost of fuel has resulted in a 300 to 400 per cent increase in the operation of fuel-reliant water supply systems as well water trucking (As per RI ongoing monitoring and observation, only those water points with solar system are giving continuous service without interruption). As the Sudan rainy season is approaching, it will further heighten the risk of outbreaks in the coming months. Water-borne diseases and epidemics are a further threat due to the deteriorating WASH situation, due to the high accumulation of solid wastes in the villages, the increased rate of open defecations, absence of water safety measures, poor hygiene, disruption of the supply chain, and access concerns. As per the WASH cluster meeting minutes of 21May (report from Health Cluster), indicated that across Sudan diarrheal disease is the first most reported cases of the functional health facilities and hospitals.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In line with WASH cluster’s strategy, RI will ensure the restoration and availability and continuity of WASHservices to health and nutrition facilities and populations that are in urgent need of WASH services. To achieve high impactresponse, RI will provide an integrated response of all three components of WASH for the affected communities. RI’s WASH intervention will remainan integral part of preparedness and response for disease outbreaks and pandemics(cholera, AWD, Hepatitis E, vector-borne diseases, COVID -19, etc.) and will maintainconstant coordination with FMOH and WASH sector. Using in integrated approach, RI willensure that WASH response is integrated in health facilities, nutrition facilities (OTPs/TSFPs, and Stabilization centers), and in communities hosting a high number of SAMcases and those in need of urgent emergency WASH intervention. In addition, a minimumresponse package of WASH kits and hygiene promotion activities will be implemented inthe target localities. Additionally, RI will reach out with priority WASH interventions inschools and child-friendly spaces, where activities will focus on awareness creation,dissemination of hygiene promotion, promotion of handwashing with soap, constructionof household emergency latrines, and raising awareness on the importance of safe andclean water for the household and community (as the current conflict forced people to use unprotected water), and to protect school going children fromwater borne diseases. RI will mobilize communities to construct latrines as much aspossible. In addition, social mobilization approaches will be used in hygiene promotioninterventions focusing on the installation of handwashing facilities with soap and water,hygiene risk communication, and community engagement activities. This will makecommunities more resilient to recurring shocks. Community capacity to operate, maintainand manage WASH facilities will be strengthened through forming and training Water user committees with the required managerial, technical training and related equipment. Withthis background, RI will prioritize rehabilitation, upgrading and ensuring sustainability ofexisting water points rather than new water point constructions. Therefore, the proposedproject's contribution to safer water access revolve around rehabilitation and upgrading ofwater points as well as community water users capacity building, water quality testingand monitoring. In total, RI will rehabilitate 57 water points (9 water yard rehabilitation, 18 water yard solarization, and30Handpump rehabilitations) and Water supply by watertrucking for a number of health facilities in localities of El fasher , Mellit, El Melha,Sarafomera, El Taweisha, North Darfur; Wadmadniin AjJazirah , Khartoum,El Roseires, El Damazine, Baw, Geissan, inBluenile States, to improve the water availability in RI supported health facilities in theabsence of other technology options and emergency needs. In total, 142,848 individualswill benefit from the water supply service provision. To improve the access to sanitationservices, RI will also intervene in the same localities with the construction of individual emergency household latrines, Health facility VIP latrines. Accordingly, RI willbenefit a total of 6,000 individuals at the localities of ND,AjJazirah , Khartoum and BN states. Under thehygiene promotion intervention in target localities, RI will conduct regular hygienepromotions including MHM sensitization, construction/installation of handwashing facilities, support disposal of solid and medical waste through construction of incinerators and waste disposal sites, and distribution of Hygiene kits whichincludes MHM component. The hygiene promotion intervention will reach 145,560 individuals.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

RI will focus on Ground water monitoring, sanitary survey, water quality monitoring and surveillances with the framework of integrated water resource management to avoid any impact on environment and natural resource. In addition, RI's strategy will focus on rehabilitation and upgrading works rather than new water points construction; this will help in avoiding any environmental impact. Before any intervention, RI will do appropriate assessments to avoid any risk of contamination due to latrines or other wastes near the water points. RI’s Water supply strategy focuses on renewable Energy (solar) than dependence on fuel for generators which have eventual impact on the Environment. All waste management disposal facilities (like incinerator and solid waste disposal pits) will be fenced and all essential measures will be taken to avoid pollution to the environment and natural resources.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI will mainstream minimum protection standards to ensure equitable and safe access to WASH programs and facilities. In addition to setting locations of community water points based on hydro-geologic conditions consideration, sociopolitical considerations will be factored in to ensure the location is accessible to the entire community, and that the WUC is representative. Safety and dignity are key considerations for sanitation activities, and each household will be involved in the construction of their emergency household latrines (EHH) with technical support from RI WASH team to account for the unique needs of its members, and it is worth to mention that the hygiene kits will incorporate MHM items. All beneficiaries will be selected according to set criteria and in consultation with the local authorities and the beneficiaries' community representatives. The hygiene promoters, CHWs and CNWs will receive hygiene promotion training that considers how to tailor messaging to diverse groups. RI will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout the project’s WASH activities. Priority will be given to the children, the disabled, female-headed households, and beneficiaries with cases of acute malnutrition. When selecting beneficiaries for latrines construction, beneficiary households meeting the vulnerability criteria will be prioritized. In the selection of sites for the rehabilitation of water yards and hand pumps, priority will be given to locations with high percentages of vulnerable households. For the training of WUCs, RI will ensure that there is representation from vulnerable community groups and that the WUCs develop processes to ensure that vulnerable community members are able to access water points easily. Throughout the project planning and implementation phases, RI will continue to monitor the communities to ensure that vulnerable households are benefiting from the proposed WASH programme.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding, to address issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for the beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy and sign the Safeguarding policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system by putting a mechanism which will strengthen inter - agency coordination to facilitate learning of best practices and ensure a strong and well coordinated referral mechanisms such as referrals of GBV cases. RI will enhance capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedback to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and improve quality of program for the project beneficiaries, and encourage recipients participation in each stage of the project management cycle. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations and future programming.
