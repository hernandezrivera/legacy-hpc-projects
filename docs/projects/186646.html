Project Summary

Tdh has been working in Rann communities since 2017, as a major actor in the provision of MNCH services. However, a critical gap has been identified in the lack of access to nutrition services within the IDP camp and the HC. The proposed project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality due to severe and moderate acute malnutrition in displaced populations and acutely vulnerable host communities by improving the availability, utilization and quality of essential preventative and curative nutrition services for boys and girls under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Through the proposed project, Tdh will reinforce early detection of malnutrition through active screening of children between 0 and 59 months, will refer and treat children with SAM to Outpatient therapeutic feeding program and stabilization center. In addition, Tdh will set up support groups (mother to mother and father to father) at the community level. Lastly, Tdh will set up MIYCF corners and provides skilled IYCF support to caregivers.

1 - Needs

Rann (Kala-Balge Local Government Area), composed of 37,842 individuals, is a landlocked location suffering from massive humanitarian needs since 2016. In Nigeria (ranked at 8.5 in the Children’s Climate Risk Index), Rann is one of the most isolated locations prone to flooding resulting from the rainy season, punctual water release from Cameroonian dam which results in cutting the road access and regular harassment by insurgents (latest attack on October 28th, 2021). The attack led by a Non-State Armed Group took control of the entire city for a few hours in August 30th, 2021 (resulted in displacement of 1,769 individuals to Cameroon). All these factors make of Rann one of the most challenging areas operationally in humanitarian contexts in Borno State. Heightened access constrains and annual flooding that destroys hectares of cultivated fields, hinders road access from Ngala to Rann during the rainy season (road access only from December to June in best case scenario and from February to June at worst) and put the population at very high risk of malnutrition and WASH-related diseases prevalence. The IOM DTM Round 37, indicates that the humanitarian situation in most of Nigeria’s north-eastern states (Borno included) is currently approaching famine levels, characterized by high food insecurity, malnutrition, and exposure to disease outbreaks. More specifically, in Kala Balge LGA malnutrition cases among children are on the rise, with an increase between 3 to 5% of cases of severe acute malnutrition (SAM). WFP “Cadre harmonisé” analysis for June to August 2021 has placed Kala-Balge in CH level 4, with 30,000 beneficiaries of GFD, 1,800 children from 6 to 23 months and 2,400 PLW. As such, an estimated 12,766 IDPs are in need nutrition assistance (Nutrition sector PIN 2022 analysis). With the GAM rate at 12.9%, SAM rate at 1.9% and PLW GAM rate at 13.4%. SAM burden for children 0- 59 months is 1,488 children, SAM with medical complication 149 cases, MAM (6- 59 months) burden is 4,845 children and PLW MAM burden is 406 women. IYCF burden is estimated to be 3,027 cases. As for the host community, 8,103 persons are estimated to need nutrition assistance with the GAM rate at 12.9%, SAM rate at 1.9% and PLW GAM rate at 13.4%. SAM burden for children 0-59 months is 800 children, SAM with medical complications is 80 cases, MAM (6- 59 months) burden is 2,785 cases, and PLW MAM burden is 285 women. IYCF need is estimated to be 2,126. In addition, risks of widespread cholera outbreaks persist, with cases of AWD already being reported and, according to a UNICEF surveillance report, Kala-Balge LGA already reported between 10-30% increase of diarrhea among children.

2 - Activities

Tdh will apply a community-based approach, by engaging the CHVs in Mass MUAC screening and Family MUAC approach to ensure early detection and referral of children below 5 years malnutrition cases. Identified SAM cases will be referred to the OTP for management. CHVs will engage the community in awareness creation sessions, MIYCF activities, and mother-to-mother, father-to-father support groups activities including cooking and gardening demonstrations, FDG, and IYCF skilled counselling. Tdh will set up and equip the OTP in line with the nutrition guidelines. SAM cases without medical complications (MUAC ≤ 115mm- or Oedema) will be admitted at the OTP, systematic treatment, and RUTF will be administered, and growth monitoring conducted bi-weekly. Children will be discharged on attaining a MUAC ≥125mm for 2 consecutive visits without Oedema and no medical complications. Tdh will set up and equip a 10-bed capacity Stabilization Center (SC) unit within Rann General hospital. SAM cases with medical complications referred from the OTP will be admitted to the SC. Due to insecurity, Tdh will adopt a day-care model to provide services at the SC in line with the sector guidance and protocols and adhere to COVID 19 IPC guidelines.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will ensure women's participation while selecting the community health volunteers to ensure it is gender-sensitive. In addition, the timing of health promotion sessions and other committee meetings will be considered to ensure suitability. Close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by Tdh to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material, and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation, and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding of specific issues, if needed, topics will be re-discussed. Tdh ensures that the guiding principles are respected in the planification and implementation. Indeed, Tdh will honor safety and security, by ensuring the safety of the survivor, child, and family and will maintain the awareness of safety and security of people who are helping the survivor. Tdh will also respect the confidentiality of the survivor, child, and family at all times. Relevant information on the case will be shared with other humanitarian actors for helping purposes if and only if the survivor agrees to it. Tdh will indeed seek patient consent before engaging in any response or sharing of information. If the survivor is not able to read and write, he/she will provide his/her consent verbally. Tdh will also comply with the respect of the patient: the patient will be provided with information on options available to them and Tdh will maintain a non-judgment behavior. Through awareness-raising, Tdh engages the community fully in understanding and promoting gender equality and gender power relations that protect and respect the rights of women and girls. Tdh will also ensure equal and active participation by women and men, girls, and boys in assessing, planning, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating programs through the systematic use of participatory methods Tdh will ensure that the promotion among the staff of Tdh’s safeguarding principles.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Tdh ensured that the accountability to the affected population was engaged at the project-designed stage. The needs assessment, which was conducted among IDP, host community, and returnees enable to reveal specific challenges faced by the community (as developed in the dedicated section), which enabled Tdh to make the informed programmatic planning to be accountable to the beneficiaries that are developed in the proposed project. Tdh actively seeks the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming. It is composed of community leaders, religious leaders, representatives of women, youth, and other social groups, and a representative of all ethnic groups present in the community. Therefore, it contributes to routine transparency and two ways of communication. It also encourages and facilitates a space for communities to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance received, while paying particular attention to the gender, age, and diversity of those giving feedback. These feedbacks will be obtained through a suggestion box, and questionnaires. The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such asFGD, interviews, and feedback boxes.
