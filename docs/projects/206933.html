1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project considers the latest trend of population movement in 2023 looking not only at areas of displacement (Pemba, Ibo, Montepuez with 46,226 targeted individuals), but as well on areas of return (Mocimboa da Praia, Quissanga, Palma with 30,093 targeted individuals). The project will have a strong focus on DRR and capacity building to reduce possible consequences of natural and conflict caused disasters, while providing emergency response. The DRR actions will focus mostly on more densely populated areas (capital of the districts) to increase awareness, but as well to pre-identify and possible reinforce structures that can be adapted to temporary host displaced people, evaluating the accessibility, as well as the capacity to host. Linkeages with possible livelihood/CFW intervention will be set up to guarantee minimum standards in terms of safety and accessibility. Where it is not possible to identify structures, different options and approaches will be considered to provide emergency shelter assistance with particular effort dedicated, where possible, in enhancing existing individual structures (i.e. in 2023 AVSI improved host communities houses of families hosting IDPs) being them of host community or own shelter abandoned during the displacement. Considering vulnerable categories, particularly elders and people living with a disability, a special effort will be put in improve accessibility.Capacity building on resilient shelters will have a prominent role, particularly in areas of return, where it could be expected/hoped that the population could settle for good. When suitable, the use of cash for shelter or NFI assistance will be piloted, to reduce delivery time and implementation costs, as well as to mitigate risks of "misuse" of resources caused by the high mobility of the population or excessive use of natural resources. In line with AVSI policy for AAP, pre-assessments and post distribution/execution monitoring will be conducted for each intervention and the results be used to adapt the program in the future, or adjust what can be adjusted as the project advances.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will ensure protection mainstreaming process in order to ensure DO NO HARM approach, promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in all actions. In order to guarantee these principles, not only staff, but all people involved in the project (including artisans, beneficiaries, suppliers) will be trained on the principles and procedures in place and when necessary will be required to will sign the reference documents such as the Internal Code of Conduct, the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA) and the AVSI Children Safeguarding Policy documents as well as the AVSI lead protection mainstreaming checklists. Through DRR actions, a particular effort will be put in place to identify, with community members, all potential risks and main resources in the area of intervention. In order to avoid conflicts between IDPs (new or extended), returnees and host communities/resident population, targeting for individual assistance will be done in consultation with the communities, defining together the criteria of vulnerability and ensuring that all the different groups are represented in the consultations. This will also ensure the use of the principles of impartiality and neutrality: no distinction of nationality/provenience, race, religion, social condition and political affiliation will be made and no sides will be taken in any dispute. Protection monitoring will be guarantee through routinely monitoring access to services by women, girls, boys and men through spot checks and discussions with communities, and promptly address the obstacles to equal access.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Gender equality and women’s empowerment are preconditions for effective and sustainable development and the enjoyment of universal human rights. The project will ensure IASC GAM within the different phases of the project: - Need Analysis set, in order that all and the right people are understood and counted - - Tailoring and adapting the interventions according to gender and age - Ensuring a gender and aged balanced participation on the feed-back process The project shall make ensure gender equaility and inclusion criteria are used. AVSI promotes and encourages active participation of women. As such, the project has a strong commitment to ensure the full participation of women in decision-making and implementation of its programmes including in beneficiary selection, implementation, and monitoring to the greatest extent possible within culturally appropriate contexts. Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the entire project. For all constructions, community members, and women in particular will be consulted on the proposed models, as well as on the structure’s orientation and placement; women with previous knowledge on construction practices will be prioritized in the selection of artisans. All artisans and daily workers will receive a basic PSEA training before initiating construction, provided by trained AVSI protection personnel. The project shall collect and report program monitoring data which includes sex- and age-disaggregated data of beneficiaries and will ensure that such informations are used purposely to improve all groups and age inclusion, participation and satisfaction. This project will follow AVSI Foundation SOP described in the AVSI FOUNDATION ACCOUNTABILITY POLICY on ‘feedback and accountability mechanism’. Newly recruited and old staff are always oriented and receive refresher trainings to stress the importance of the opinions and views of communities concerning program implementation. Beneficiaries and the population at large is also made aware of the forms available to provide suggestions and complaints. This project will adopt a community-based and participatory approach. The selection criteria will be developed in consultation with the communities and will aim to upgrade living-conditions within a settlement and when possible through the construction of new shelters. AVSI will conduct the assessment according to the agreed criteria, which will be reviewed and verified by the local authorities and the representatives of the communities. In AVSI’s experience, this has proven time and again to be particularly productive, as not only people’ concerns and priorities are taken into account.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In the present project proposal screening, surveillance and participation of people with disabilities have been included as key activities and indicators. During their implementation, people with disabilities will be recorded (disaggregated by gender and age) and given special attention. An ad hoc activity and indicator will focus on improving shelter condition, or re-adapting shelters for people with disability. This translates into prioritizing the above mentioned target population as to the assistance for Shelter, NFI and Protection, especially for those cases having motor disabilities to which AVSI Foundation will provide logistics support. AVSI Foundation will, when possible, organize specific studies on the inclusion and conditions of persons living with a disability and will utilize such studies to devise inclusive strategies on round table with PwD as well as local communities. AVSI Foundation will ensure the implementation of sensitization sessions throughout its activities to ensure that these people are supported within thsere community and given special attention when coordinating and planning activities with local SDGCAS (Serviço Distrital de Genero, Crianças e Acçao Social) and partners. This action is facilitated by the fact that AVSI uses a multisectorial approach and regularly tries to implement shelter programs hand in hand with protection (and often livelihood) interventions.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

AVSI aims to promote a sustainable and healthy environment in order to improve the wellbeing of the community. Given the experience of AVSI Mozambique in energy efficiency-related projects, methods to save charcoal and wood during cooking will be also promoted so to sensitize the target population on preventing deforestation. This in turn will positively impact family finances by incurring savings on wood and charcoal. In addition, , the use of local materials for construction will be prioritized. This will help promote an environment-friendly approach by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. When possible, renting options, maintenance of existing structures or local procurement of shelter materials will be conducted to ensure a positive contribution to local economies and reduce enviromenal overheads associated with transportation. At the same time, however, technical quality is often varied and at times below the required standards because of: - Limited capacity to deliver bulk shipments of local products - No authority for quality certification of wooden products or manufactured products - Limited industrial capacity and many small suppliers on the market.Moreover, with strong community engagement and participation on rehabilitation activities, the project will put specific attention regarding the site selection and sourcing of materials, favoring the use of local and renewable resources and where possible promoting good environmental practices. Furthermore, AVSI can rely to an internal Policy on environment on May 11th 2020.
