Project Summary

The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that over 16.7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance across Syria. Syria remains a protection crisis. Children continue to be killed, women and girls continue to fear for their safety and 7.2 million Syrians remain in displacement. The protracted conflict in has severely limited access to civil documentation, leaving a significant portion of the population undocumented or under-documented. Lack of documentation hampers freedom of movement, access to services, and increases the risk of statelessness. Housing, Land, Property (HLP) issues, particularly for women, exacerbate protection concerns. Legal assistance and awareness-raising are critical to address these challenges.
The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC)’s comprehensive protection focuses on direct provision of information, counseling, and legal assistance (ICLA) to Persons Affected by the Crisis and Displacement (PACD). NRC aims to enhance knowledge and response capacity among duty-bearers, promoting the integration of ICLA with Shelter, WASH, Livelihoods, Food Security, and Education programs. NRC’s Protection programming aligns with Sector Objectives #1 and #2, aiming to improve the protection of crisis-affected populations, advocate for durable solutions, and strengthen technical capacities at national and community levels to address protection risks and needs.
Direct Assistance to PACD: NRC's direct assistance services target PACD facing legal challenges related to civil documentation, housing, land, property (HLP) rights, and access to essential services. Key priorities include issuance of basic civil documentation, catering to the most vulnerable, and preventing forced evictions. Special attention is given to female-headed households, children without proof of legal identity, and those facing HLP concerns.
Increased Knowledge and Capacity: NRC undertakes legal analysis, research, and technical assistance, providing training modules on legal identity, HLP, and employment law. Community leaders, including formal and traditional authorities, receive capacity-building interventions. Emphasis is placed on resolving HLP, employment, and labor disputes, with a commitment to protecting the rights of women, girls, and persons with disabilities.
Focus on Vulnerability and Inclusion: NRC places a gender, age, and disability inclusion lens on its protection interventions, recognizing the heightened vulnerabilities of specific groups. Advocacy is integral to NRC's activities, emphasizing that civil documentation should not be a prerequisite for receiving assistance. Tailored interventions and capacity-building efforts aim to empower communities and duty-bearers.
Target Group: NRC prioritizes IDPs, returnees, host community members, Iraqi refugees, Palestinian refugees, stateless individuals, and those at risk of statelessness. Protection activities aim to address rights to legal identity, civil documentation, HLP rights, and access to essential services. Vulnerability criteria include gender, age, disability, ethnicity, and religion.
Implementation: NRC implements protection programming directly, emphasizing the importance of house-to-house verification processes and community-supported targeting approaches for beneficiary selection. Thorough cash feasibility studies guide the decision-making process, especially concerning the provision of assistance through cash. NRC implements these interventions directly. In exceptional circumstances, when access to communities is limited, NRC collaborates with partners, respecting anonymity for security reasons.
Monitoring and Evaluation: NRC maintains a robust monitoring and evaluation framework, conducting regular reviews of awareness-raising and training materials. The organization actively contributes to Protection service mapping, ensuring accurate and up-to-date information. Ongoing reviews inform program adjustments, advocacy efforts, and preparedness measures.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NRC's implementation is guided by an in-depth understanding of protection threats in local areas and the relative vulnerability of different beneficiaries to those threats as well as their capacity to cope. NRC conducts risk analyses for the areas where NRC activities are implemented, taking into account a large range of views, including staff, beneficiaries, other organizations etc. NRC implements its Project Cycle Management (PCM) approach throughout its projects to track and address any challenges on time, ensuring the ultimate outcome of the activities. NRC includes robust lessons learnt sessions in its programming to ensure that insights into effectiveness, efficiency, internal process and programmatic integration, impact, and sustainability are identified. NRC also has in place an elaborate Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policy with a clear complaints channel to ensure any instances of abuse are reported and handled conclusively. This comprehensive protection response by NRC underscores the organization's commitment to addressing legal, documentation, and housing challenges, contributing to the overall protection and well-being of crisis-affected populations in Syria.
