Project Summary

Limited access to quality education in a protective environment remains a challenge within Syria and requires concerted efforts among education members to enable an effective response. In 2019, there were over 150 education members across the three hubs operating from within and outside of Syria, coordinated through Save the Children and UNICEF. The complexity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria demands larger investments in the existing coordination structures to enable a more structured, accountable humanitarian education response, and to facilitate more effective partnerships with education members (NGOs, INGOs and UN agencies) to better serve affected populations.

4 - Needs

Building on the investments of previous years, in 2020, the education sector will continue to prioritize strengthening the capacity of the education system to deliver and sustain education investments. The Education Sector aims to improve the capacity of education members and will implement capacity development programs that provide targeted and needs-driven opportunities for local-level members for both professional technical development and action-oriented learning and equip them with the knowledge and skills required to plan, implement and coordinate high quality emergency education responses. As the crisis in Syria enters into its ninth year, the complexity of the humanitarian response demands larger investments in the existing coordination mechanisms namely, in Damascus, Gaziantep and North- East Syria, as well as a Whole of Syria overall coordination system that ensures coherence across the various hubs. Through this project, UNICEF and Save the Children as Education Cluster co-leads are aiming to strengthen coordination across the hubs to ensure:- Dedicated sector leadership and coordination to ensure a more strategic and coherent response in support of national led efforts;- Increased evidence-based advocacy efforts;- Increased planning, preparedness and response capacity and ability across the hubs;- Demonstrated accountability (for sector wide performance);- Enhanced ability of the WOS architecture including the Strategic Steering Group to lead a more strategic and coherent response, and better influence how available resources are prioritized.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project covers both the staff costs related to the coordination functions, including dedicated information management and coordination capacity, as well as the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: (a) support service delivery based on needs; (b) inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis; (c) plan and implement cluster strategies; (d) monitor and evaluate performance; (e) build national capacities; and (f) support advocacy efforts. At the WOS level and under the cluster co-leadership of UNICEF and Save the Children, concerted efforts will focus on enhancing the effective coordination among the hubs namely through:- Ensuring effective Information Management in all hubs, and at a WoS level; - Development and dissemination of timely information materials - Strengthening evidence-based advocacy on attacks on education- Promoting the exchange of best practices and technical standards;- Representing and advocating on behalf of, the sector in the relevant fora, at hub and WoS level and at intersectoral level, including key donors and stabilization members among others; - Facilitation of capacity building initiatives to strengthen capacity of education members across the hubs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Children in insecure areas are denied their right to education due to lack of capacity and ability of education members to respond. Mitigating measure include, ensuring that education members are adequately trained on contingency planning and response, in addition to strengthening linkages with other clusters and promotion of duty of care commitment.Risk: Children and education personnel are unduly exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) from aid workers. Mitigation measures will include the familiarization and dissemination of education members with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies and promote within education members to adopt policies within their organization.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Potential of conflict escalation remains high in some parts of the country with reduced access by education members to provide quality education interventions. As a mitigating measure, the cluster will ensure that education members are adequately supported through capacity building packages that strengthen preparedness and response, including strengthening linkages with key sectors.As per the 2020 HNO, attacks on education facilities are increasing, particularly in Northwest Syria. While education members have limited influence on preventing attacks on education, in 2020 the education cluster will focus its efforts on mitigating the impact of attacks. This includes strengthened reporting, advocating that schools in insecure locations are structurally strengthened, safety protocols are in place, preparedness trainings regularly practiced and that psychosocial support services are provided.Additionally, children might be denied their right to education due to lack of sufficient funding for coordination, impacting reliable and timely data highlighting education needs and priorities. Mitigation measures will include timely evidence-based advocacy efforts by WOS co-leads with the donor and wider humanitarian community.
