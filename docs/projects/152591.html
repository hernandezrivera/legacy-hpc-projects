Project Summary

During the time of emergency, families separated, children girls and boys are greatly affected psychologically by war as they flee for safety, their daily routine are greatly disrupted, environment altered and missing out on their friends cause distress. Children are exposed to violence, child abuse, neglect, child labor, and physical violence due to their low ability to protect to protect themselves. This project is design to provide immediate child protection in emergency services to conflict affected children boys, girls and foster care givers in Maiwut. UNCDR child protection team will work closely with the community based child protection networks to identify and document separated, unaccompanied and missing, identify suitable foster care families for most vulnerable unaccompanied minors, while coordinating with other actors to initiate adult tracing to facilitator the reunification in accordance to minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian action. Lifesaving messages to prevent separation, mine risks, will be integrated in child protection activities to minimize cases of separation and risks through community gathering, churches and organized awareness campaigns.
The prolonged effects of conflict and displacement in Upper Nile state has majorly affected women and children in locations which prior to the current situation, where already below thresholds in protection. The county targeted in Maiwut has populations of 118,082 people, (NBS, Apr.15) of which more than 50% are IDPs. (IOM DTM, ’16). Upper Nile state has been recorded as hosting the greatest IDP sites without access to work. The target group includes unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed forces and armed groups, children victims of or at risk of GBV, domestic violence, physical and sexual abuse, GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV. According to Humanitarian Bulletin South Sudan Issue 15/10 October 2017; following increased threats of insecurity and imminent clashes on 6 September, actors implementing protection interventions temporally suspended activities with approximately 50,000 people (women, girls, boys, men, elderly and disabled) were displaced at risk of facing critical protection threats including GBV, family separation, and boys and girls targeted with armed forces and armed groups.
Based on the Data obtained from the protection cluster, the present program covers the humanitarian gaps which have been identified with the aim of guaranteeing a minimum package of protection services for the conflict-affected population. Assessment findings in Maiwut underline a high level of UASC, child survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation and GBV cases. IDPs children are not attending school and considering the high presence of armed actors, they represent an easy target for armed groups' recruitment. There are not recreational activities in the area and the psychosocial needs of the children and young people are not addressed. There is timely need for protection services (on gender-segregated latrines, access to hygiene and dignity kits, etc.): from the Focus Group Discussions held with IDP community, GBV concerns included early and forced marriage, domestic violence and SGBV case. Not any service available in order to guarantee PSS to the GBV survivors. UNCDR’s current program in Maiwut highlights that child recruitment; abuse and exploitation are still main concerns. UASC have been identified and there is still consistent need to proceed with further identification, registration and reunification with their families. In the area of Maiwut, CP interventions are very limited while the needs are constantly increasing. Thus, UNCDR will scale up its activities in protection in order to complement the current multi sectorial interventions and address the gaps in coordination with the CP and GBV sub clusters.
