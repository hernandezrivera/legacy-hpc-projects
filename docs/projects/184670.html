Project Summary

Due to prolonged drought caused by failure of three consecutive seasons, water availability has deteriorated. Since January 2021, extreme water shortages, widespread reliance on water trucking, and high-water prices have been reported in most pastoral and agro-pastoral areas in Gedo region. Reports from the regional WASH cluster lead and partners show that in Baardheere, Belet-Xaawo and Ceel-waaq districts where people depend on water catchments which are temporary sources, 100 percent of these water sources have dried with currently no Deyr rainfall in the region. The situation has increased the number of IDPs as people migrating from their original locations as they seek water and basic services for survival. Water scarcity threatens to amplify conflict, food insecurity, and poor health and sanitation conditions. Based on the humanitarian context, there is an urgent need to provide water, sanitation, and hygiene services in all target districts which accommodates both IDPs and non IDPs that requires urgent support. A rapid assessment conducted by LLG in target districts in October 2021 indicated the beneficiaries walk an average of 15 kilometers from their dwelling to the water point against recommended standards of a maximum of 500 meters. On average a household of average, six members can fetch and utilize less than 20 liters per day instead of the recommended 60 liters. Knowledge of hygiene messaging and the inability to afford hygiene kits have slowed down progress in improved hygiene.
The response plan aims to improve the health conditions of targeted IDPs and host communities through better access to clean water, the use of latrines, and increased hygiene promotion practices based on the need analysis above. It emphasizes implementing sustainable water systems, enhancing protection in WASH, and enhancing the quality of outcomes. In Baardheere, Belet-Xaawo and Ceel-Waaq IDP camps and host communities, an increase in access to safe water will be done through upgrading a water treatment system previously constructed by LLG in 2016 and 2017, Supported by Concern worldwide and the Government of Somalia through Funding from the World Bank and FCDO in all target locations. The focus will be to upgrade the existing infrastructures such as wells with pumping unit at the intake and rising main and existing water treatment unit and pipeline extension to water points, construction /rehabilitation of underground tanks to strategic locations in the three target districts. All the water points will be metered, and water utilized will be paid to the private water company managing the water supply system. This will reduce the walking distance of the target IDPs & non-IDPs and reduce queuing time at the existing water points. A water management committee will be formed at each water point to be in charge of hygiene aspects of the water points. To increase access to latrines, 150 latrines will be constructed (120 household latrines and 30 latrines for people with special needs/People with Disabilities) all fitted with handwashing facilities. Solar streetlights will be strategically located in locations where the latrines will be constructed to provide lighting to women, girls, men, and women using the latrines. 40CHWs (20 Females and 20 males) will be recruited and deployed to cover IDPs and host communities in target districts. The CHWs will be charged with health promotion and safe for adoption and emulation of safe hygiene practices through radio (mass media) and house-to-house visits and will be complemented with the distribution of 1,500 hygiene kits which will be sourced from WASH cluster and provided to the most vulnerable households. A total of 21,799 individuals with 1,373 beneficiaries with special needs/disabilities will be reached. The action will further support affected communities with water trucking through community water voucher targeting population of 15,234 households from the host communities in target districts.

1. - Projects activities

1. Water point construction or rehabilitation-: Supply of potable water through a pipeline to IDP Communities: This activity will involve the supply of water through a pipeline to IDP communities in the two camps. Each IDP will be provided 15l/p/d for drinking, hygiene, and domestic use targeting 6,565 IDPs individuals. 2. Water point construction or rehabilitation that will support 21,799 individuals including IDPs and Non IDPs-: Water infrastructure Constructed (5 Number two shallow wells with solar system and 3 underground tanks): This will comprise electromechanical pumping systems, at the intake and rising main, 3-5km Distribution main from the elevated tank, and two water points one constructed in Baardheere and the other one Belet-Xaawo target strategic locations. 3. Capacity building (water committees and WASH training): A total of 64 people will be trained. Training of three Water Committees on Operation and Maintenance (O&M) and WASH training for 24 WES committee members and 40 Community Hygiene Workers (CHWs). Operation and maintenance tools will be supported with all three committees for wells and is aimed at ensuring smooth running of the project after completion and handover to the community. It will also ensure the sustainability of the project. The Water Management committee will comprise 24 members (15 men and 9 Women) democratically selected by the community. The three days training will be undertaken at the water treatment unit site, Baardheere, Belet-Xaawo and Ceel-Waaq for three days. The key objective of the training will be to equip the committee members with knowledge to operate, maintain, and manage the water points and solar pumping system. by overseeing day-to-day operations and setting policies, such as whether and how much to charge for usage to cover future maintenance costs. Water management committees will also promote health and sanitation education in the community by passing on the knowledge members gained during training, as part of project implementation. The committee will be trained on general maintenance of the system and to troubleshoot should a problem arises during normal operation. In addition, the role of a water committee is expected to extend establishing leadership roles for women within project requirements and helps to facilitate a shift in attitudes on gender and traditional roles, allowing for greater social mobility. Trained committee members will also be issued with tools and equipment for Operation and maintenance. Participants will be issued with stationary during training. Three days training of Community Health Promoters will be conducted to improve the knowledge and skills of CHWs in the dissemination of health and hygiene messages. the training will target selected members living within the community to disseminate hygiene messages and will target 21,799 individuals. Training will focus on transmission routes and key barriers to prevent AWD diseases among the community, Household water treatment, handwashing steps among others. The UNICEF/WASH cluster manual will be adopted for the training. 40 CHWs comprising 20 men and 20 women will be targeted and trained for three days. Trained CHWs will conduct house-to-house visits and through mass gatherings in the community passing hygiene messages 21,799 individuals. 5. Targeted most vulnerable 4,500 IDPs and non IDPs will have improved access to secured, gender disaggregated and accessible latrines with solar lights for protection in the strategic locations of Baardheere, Belet-Xaawo and Ceel-Waaq. 6. water trucking through community water voucher to 15,234 individuals of host communities will be implemented during jilal and xagaa period which are dry spell for 100 days in two dry seasons at an estimated cost of 95 USD per loads and 1,924 loads of water will be delivered during the two dry seasons in 2022.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, Minimum Inter-agency Standards for Protection Mainstreaming and Do No Harm principles, the project will ensure accountability and minimize harm to all beneficiaries at all stages of the programme cycle. Further, LLG has a Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), LLG Code of Conduct and Safeguarding policies - which all LLG employees, partners, contractors, and consultants must sign up to during their contract of employment and engagement. The policies set out and clearly articulates the standards of behavior expected including to ensure Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) when interacting with programme participants in the locations where LLG implements programmes. Staff and other contractors are regularly sensitized on these safeguarding policies. LLG commits to deliver relief food assistance with a consistently high level of safety, accountability, accessibility, and participation, right from its first interactions with the community through to the end of the intervention.At the onset of engagement with communities and stakeholders, LLG will conduct sensitization sessions on its fully functional CRM while also re-confirming that they can access the available feedback channels used. Posters with the channels and information on how to lodge complaints will be mounted in strategic locations in the community. In line with the handling procedures, complaints categorized as sensitive are channeled to the Programme Director per practice, to ensure they are addressed appropriately. Protection issues from beneficiaries will also be monitored regularly as part of the PDM processes as well as the regular tracking and updating of the protection risk assessment/analysis in the project locations. LLG uses available mobile phone company provided technology to send short messages and voice messages to target beneficiary households about entitlement before the transfer of assistance and after transfer informing about transfer, its purpose, source of assistance, CRM information and zero tolerance to corruption and fraud. LLG will tailor its CRM dissemination material – e.g. printed posters, leaflet, SMS content etc. and in-line with proposed project and its contractual obligation to comply with WFP policies and guidelines. In the target areas, LLG has adopted conflict sensitive programming. LLG continuously assesses the potential impact of its programme in creating or resolving conflicts. LLG takes consideration of conflicts linked to selection of project location, resource transfers, operational activities like selection of vendors, hiring of staff or enumerators, visibility material, lack of interaction with local authorities etc. LLG has integrated various conflict sensitivity approaches into the project in order to adhere ‘Do No Harm’ principles. This includes but not limited to, extensive interaction with clan elders and linking with existing conflict mitigation systems, well balanced hiring of staff, communication chains, responsive CRM mechanism, coordination and interaction with local authorities, clan sensitivity, robust procurement and sourcing policies and procedures, community and stakeholder engagement throughout project cycle etc. LLG Programme Director charged with the responsibility to ensure adequate application of protection considerations in the project will work closely with the programme team on protection and safeguarding to provide technical support to the implementation team.
