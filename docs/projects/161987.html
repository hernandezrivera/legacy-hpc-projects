Project Summary

The Rohingya, who sought refuge in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh are in dire need of Protection and other life-saving humanitarian assistance. Among the 910,000 Rohingya in the camp settlements, women and children comprise approximately 78%, and 31% of the families have persons with specific needs. Most Rohingya in the camps continue to be in immense need of humanitarian services and face formidable challenges, although they have found a safe haven and demonstrate resilience. The lack of a refugee protection framework in Bangladesh leave the Rohingya vulnerable to harm, abuse and exploitation and expose them to serious protection risks. Their restricted mobility, coupled with limited access to civil documentation and formal livelihoods and education, render most of them dependent on humanitarian aid, stymie sustainable solutions, and weaken the protective environment. Violence, criminal activity, the threat of natural disasters caused by the monsoon and cyclone seasons, wide-spread poverty, and limited access to scarce resources impact Rohingya and host community populations.
In response, the protection project that IOM proposes herewith for the 2020 JRP has interlinked activities under the Protection Sector’s strategic objectives 1, 2, 3, and 6. The cross-cutting activities that IOM will conduct focus on community-based protection at the camp-level through direct interaction with beneficiaries, awareness raising and sensitization; case management for extremely vulnerable individuals and victims of human trafficking; protection monitoring activities; and protection mainstreaming through capacity building activities. IOM will use existing facilities in Camps 8E, 8W, 9, 18, 19, 20, 20Ext, 23, 24, and 25 for activities linked to Sector Objectives 1, 2, 3 and 6. Many of the counter-trafficking activities that IOM will conduct in 18 camps and 10 host communities aim to achieve the Sector’s third objective, along with sustainable impact and improved social cohesion.
Drawing on experiences from the IOM Protection programme that has expanded considerably since 2017, IOM has conceptualized and will implement the activities for 2020 according to age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar. IOM’s approach to Inclusion will identify, understand and support the affected segments of Rohingya and host community populations through meaningful access, participation, and engagement in decisions and action that directly impact the well-being of the individual, family, and community.
For disaster preparedness, IOM will work with Site Management, WASH and Shelter Sectors to identify and relocate with consent extremely vulnerable individuals before a disaster. For disaster response, IOM will verify the needs of EVIs, who are in collective centres after a disaster, and coordinate services. IOM will serve as the focal point for the Protection Emergency Response Units in 8 camps.
Most activities described in this proposal will be directly implemented by IOM Protection staff. The IOM counter-trafficking team is mobile and covers the entire district. Some activities will be conducted by IOM through a few implementing partners that contribute a specific expertise and complement but do not duplicate IOM’s Protection presence or work. IOM will continue to serve as the Protection focal point in 8 camps, be a proactive member in the PWG, the PW Task Team, the PSEA Network, and co-chair with UNHCR the Protection Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group.
IOM programming will benefit 99,082 Rohingya and host community men, women, boys, girls, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, and expression in Cox’s Bazar in a way that does not harm, assists based on needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety and dignity."

Needs

The complex needs and challenges persist despite extensive, multi-sectoral humanitarian operations that have improved the overall conditions in the camp settlements. The needs and challenges also underpin the analyzed findings published by UNHCR, IOM, UNICEF and other partners in reports and assessments which indicate that continued efforts for advocacy and community-based protection that respects human rights and promotes meaningful access and social cohesion between the different community groups, conflict mediation particularly for civil matters, and safety are necessary. According to IOM’s Protection Monitoring in 2019, Rohingya refugees need more support to obtain meaningful access to basic services. Barriers to access include proof of identity or registration status to receive aid, lack of information, opaque processes, camp topography, neglected facilities, persons in positions of power who control access or impose “fees” for services, and bad experiences that instill a reluctance to try to receive services again. Rohingya who are elderly, persons with disabilities, and women with specific circumstances like pregnancy, lactating, single headed households were consistently cited as in greatest need of access. The reports by IOM and partners note some perceptions about and reactions to the current context, including the use of child marriage, Mahjis to arbitrate matters - often for a fee, and labour opportunities in and outside of the district that might be harmful. The report findings suggest a tremendous need for humanitarian actors to improve safety measures, meaningful access to basic services, and make a concerted effort to address emerging Protection risks. There is also a need to continue counter-trafficking efforts through awareness raising, capacity building of government and humanitarian actors, and victim identification and assistance because of the complicated trafficking dynamics affecting Rohingya and Bangladeshis in the district. According to IOM’s anonymized case data about victims of trafficking, 70% of the cases were Rohingya women, girls, and boys mainly trafficked for labour exploitation. 30% of the cases assisted by IOM were Bangladeshi men, women, and children also trafficked for forced labour. The IOM caseload is most likely not representative of all trends, as very few Rohingya men who are victims of trafficking are represented in the entire caseload and other partners report assisting Rohingya women and girls who were trafficked for sexual exploitation. Assessments completed by IOM and actors cite how boys and girls are exposed to different types of harm. For example, boys were engaged as child labour in markets, shops, agriculture, construction, and fishing, while girls were at risk to child marriage and domestic work. According to UNICEF, Bangladesh has the fourth highest prevalence of child marriage; a trend that humanitarian actors have observed in the camps and Rohingya may say is necessary for the child’s safety and socio-economic reasons. The humanitarian crisis has overwhelmed the local community, which was already struggling for sustainable self-reliance. The Bangladeshi population in Cox’s Bazar has limited socio-economic opportunities, mostly connected to traditional agricultural and fishing sectors. For decades, there have been challenges related to food security, nutrition, access to healthcare, employment and sustainable livelihood options. All these factors – poverty, limited access to services and stable livelihoods – are known to increase the risk of exploitation and recruitment for illicit activities by organized criminal groups. Mounting tensions within the different refugee groups, the host communities, and the authorities increase the vulnerabilities and thus the various needs of Bangladeshi and Rohingya men, women, boys and girls and heighten their risk to harm, and diminish their dignity and fulfillment of human rights.

Activities or outputs

The proposed protection activities aim to reduce threats and vulnerabilities while increasing capacities, including those of duty bearers. Sector Objective 1: IOM will continue protection monitoring through direct observation and weekly FGDs and KIIs with beneficiaries to collect and analyse data on risks, incidents and trends in 10 camps. The analysis of the fluid camp situation will be used by IOM to address risk and threats, while informing humanitarian operations. IOM will advocate for and contribute expertise and guidance that addresses Protection issues discussed during various coordination meetings, when interacting directly with beneficiaries or stakeholders, and when IOM identifies a gap that could harm the affected population. IOM will advocate through the humanitarian sectors and with authorities who can guide decisions and actions. Sector Objective 2: IOM will continue strengthening community-based protection mechanisms in 8 camps by interacting with beneficiaries and empowering them through training and participatory activities. The tailored activities will support beneficiaries to safely identify and mitigate protection concerns and threats, exchange positive strategies for reducing and managing exposure to harm. IOM will conduct outreach sessions for Rohingya and host community members, camp authorities and partners to strengthen social cohesion and raise awareness on various Protection issues, promote Protection principles and “do no harm” practices and inform about available services. IOM will facilitate the identification, referral and follow up for victims of trafficking and other Extremely Vulnerable Individuals. Using appropriate screening tools, IOM will identify individuals who have Protection needs or are facing difficulties in access to basic services. Consultations with the beneficiaries to assess immediate needs will enable IOM to coordinate the appropriate services. IOM will refer identified cases in need of specialized services to IOM’s integrated protection specialists, relevant partners, or services like Health and Shelter/NFI. The counter-trafficking activities that IOM will aim to achieve Sector Objective 3 and are in line with the national legislation and plan of action. Many of the activities will be coordinated with relevant authorities and humanitarian partners and target Rohingya and host community members. The activities will reinforce the protection response, prevention through community outreach and capacity building of local authorities and humanitarian actors. IOM will continue technically supporting the government to lead the counter-trafficking committees. For Sector Objective 6, IOM will continue protection mainstreaming to promote age, gender and diversity inclusion by conducting regular refresher trainings on protection principles and Inclusion techniques for IOM staff, implementing partners, contractors, and relevant stakeholders. Regular field visits will assess how well Inclusion measures are incorporated into the design and implementation of activities by IOM sections like Protection, Site Management, WASH, Shelter and Health. IOM will continue to improve disability inclusion across all sectors in IOM’s camp area of responsibility through sensitization and operationalizing the action plan, and linking persons with disabilities to programmes to encourage participation. Accountability to Affected Populations initiatives shall continue with specific Rohingya groups and surveys with individuals who received Protection services to assess satisfaction level and receive feedback. The Community Feedback Mechanism, which IOM Site Management launched, will be promoted during FGDS, KIIs and outreach activities. IOM Protection checks in daily with the CFM desk about complaints requiring action. IOM shall continue the PSEA Champions in the camps and training about sexual exploitation and abuse and the obligatory SEA reporting system for staff.
