Project Summary

PUI Action aims to enhance the recovery of local communities, including migrant populations on the move and returnees through inclusive access to quality basic services.
The specific objectives are:
S.O1: Basic Services upgraded in the areas of the project through supply of equipment and/or refurbishment
S.O2: Service providers and local stakeholders’ personnel capacity built in efficient and inclusive management, operation and maintenance of the upgraded facilities
S.O3: Awareness raised among the target populations on inclusive access to basic services and key protection information.
The action will target the following health facilities in the east of Libya:
• Children Hospital (Benghazi Municipality)
• Attiah Alkaseh Hospital (Al Kufra Municipality)
• Bezima Al Jadidah Polyclinic (Al Kufra Municipality)
• Rebyana Medical PHCC (Rebyana Municipality)
• Clinic number 3 (Al Bayda district number 3)
• Althawra Teaching Hospital (Al Bayda downtown centre)
PUI will effortlessly continue to reach out and assist conflict-affected communities and will target the following direct beneficiaries:
31 service providers and local stakeholders, 45 health facility staff, 36 community mobilizers, 36 key community members, service providers and health staff, and 19,440 local community members, IDPs, migrants and refugees.
Throughout the Action, PUI will still work closely with authorities and partners to improve local capacities, acceptance and coordination of the intervention. Humanitarian principles will guide the Action to promote safety, dignity and rights of affected people, and mitigate exposure to additional risks through conflict sensitive and do not harm approaches.

1 - Needs

The 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan estimates that 526,000 individuals are in need of health assistance in Libya. Seven years into the crisis, people across Libya have been increasingly impacted by the deterioration of public-sector services. The dysfunctional public health system has resulted in a lack of access to primary and secondary healthcare. In the public health system, severe shortages of health staff, medicines, supplies and equipment have been compounded by years of under-investment and a lack of transparency and accountability.The proposed Action is designed to address the combination of insufficient offer of health services, low demand, and access barriers by offering a three-pronged approach, with the overarching aim of fostering recovery and stability by creating a culture of inclusiveness in the supported health facilities, in close synergy with other services provided by humanitarian and development actors. PUI will address gaps in Service Availability and Readiness of the selected health facilities by providing material assistance in the form of key equipment and building maintenance/rehabilitation. Additionally, PUI will improve the quality of services offered by enhancing the professional competencies and awareness of staff. In order to increase the demand and the inclusiveness for services, the consortium will rely on a strong outreach and behaviour change strategy to make sure all targeted communities are aware of the services available to them, and that the supported health facilities guarantee full access.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.2 - Increased tensions and conflicts in the community due to perceived or actual unequal distribution of assistance:- The specific groups most likely to be affected: Marginalized groups living in the area (including specific communities/ tribes, people with no legal documentation ) due to intercommunity conflict in the area - Migrants and refugees living in the area / tensions with host community.- Mitigation measures identified to reduce the risks of increasing tensions between the communities: Involvement and participation of the communities in the definition of the needs - Regular communication and consistent messaging towards national and local counterparts as well as the beneficiaries and the population widely on the humanitarian principles which are the fundamentals for the proposed intervention. - PUI will focus on ensuring that migrants, refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities have similar access to services targeted in an inclusive manner. The supported facilities will be accessible to all vulnerable groups identified including children, elderly, chronically ill patients, PWD, and pregnant and lactating women. This approach will also improve social cohesion as all individuals would have equal access to the same services without threat of legal, safety or physical violence or discrimination because of age, sex or status.4.4 - Project results in Increased exposure to COVID-19 transmission: - The specific groups most likely to be affected: All Persons in direct contact with PUI staff during the conduct of activities - PUI Staff - All people can get COVID 19; however, certain groups appear to be at greater risk of severe illness and death; Old people and people with pre-existing medical conditions, women and girls (highest risk of exposure to illness, gender inequality in accessing health services...), / vulnerable persons to the spread of COVID 19 due to barriers to acces health services. (migrants, refugees, marginalized groups, people facing economical issues...).- Mitigation measures to reduce the risks related to exposure to COVID-19 during the conduct of PUI Activities: Provision of assistance only by essential staff - Staff are protected with PPE - Preventive measures are respected (based on WHO recommendations) - Staff are trained on COVID 19 and preventive measures. Through integrated protection and health project, PUI aims to reduce the risk of spread of COVID and improve access to health, notably by providing awareness to communities on preventive measures related to COVID-19 risks, dissemination of information regarding access to health services and provision of primary healthcare.

PUI commits to uphold fundamental human rights, and is continuously seeking to increase the input of Affected Population (AP) in shaping the assistance through incorporating systematic engagement of the AP in different phases of the projects.-Need assessment: Active participation and engagement of the AP are considered main pillar in designing and implementing qualitative/quantitative data collection processes.
