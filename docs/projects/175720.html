Project Summary

The WASH Situation across Sudan remains dire. Generally, South Sudan has remained in protracted crisis in 2021 due to instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential services provision. According to Humanitarian need overviewed by OCHA, It projected that living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. This will be made worse by Covid-19 pandemic, floods, and intercommoned conflicts arising from cattle raiding, housing land and property. Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound and 77% practicing open defecation. The number of reported cases of acute watery diarrhea and acute bloody diarrhea have spiked dramatically in 2020 and early 2021. According to cluster partner mapping, there are serious WASH gaps in the counties of Pariang, Panyijiar, Koch, Rubkona, Guit, Tonj North, Tonj East. Coalition for Humanity has identified this need and would like to serve these counties. Similarly Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Rumbek North,Longechuk,Abiemnhom,Koch, Maiwut, Ulang, Akobo, have reported lowest coverage of water sanitation and hygiene facilities with a majority practicing open defecation and accessing unsafe water.
This project is in line with cluster objectives
• CLWSH/CO3: Access to basic services in emergencies,
• CLWSH/CO4, WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation
• CLWSH/CO5: Provide permanent access to water and sanitation services for IDPs, returnees, hosts and chronically affected by floods and (pre-)famine conditions
• CLWSH/CO6: Improve access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities
Coalition for Humanity will address these gaps by
1. Rehabilitating of water points, distribution of WASH core pipeline items like aqua tabs, water storage facilities, hand washing facilities.
2. The project will also construct and rehabilitate latrines in public institutions within the community where IDPs seek temporary shelter during floods.
3. The project will identify vulnerable girls and women of child bearing age and provide them with MHM kits.
4. As an exit strategy, the project will train hygiene promoters on importance of latrines, water treatment, GBV risks at water points.
5. The hygiene promoters will create awareness and sensitize communities on water treatment, proper use and maintenance of sanitation and hygiene.
6. The project will establish and train water resource management committees that will take over the maintenance of the rehabilitated water points beyond the project funding.

1 - Needs

Across South Sudan, the WASH Situation remains dire. Generally, South Sudan has remained in protracted crisis in 2021 due to instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential services provision. According to Humanitarian need overviewed by OCHA, It projected that living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. This will be made worse by Covid-19 pandemic, floods, and intercommoned conflicts arising from cattle raiding, housing land and property.The situation has been compounded by localized instability and lack of sufficient investment in essential service provision. According to HNO and IPC 2021, More than half of South Sudan population, 7.24 M people are in need of humanitarian assistance. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic, 1.6m people remained internally displaced and 2.2M are refugees. The IDPs, refugees and returnees are putting pressure on the limited resources by host communities.Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound and 77% practicing open defecation. Majority of the population 5.6m have limited access to water, sanitation and hygiene services. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. The number of reported cases of acute watery diarrhea and acute bloody diarrhea have spiked dramatically in 2020. By the end of October, 593,000 acute watery diarrhea cases were reported, a 60 per cent increase compared to 2019. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. In 2020, 16 per cent of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns.Poor infrastructure, limited investment in WASH facilities is some of the barriers to access to Water sanitation and hygiene. Floods have also submerged the available boreholes. The IDPs are being hosted in places with limited access to water and sanitation, this has become a health hazard. According to cluster partner mapping, there are serious WASH gaps in the counties of Pariang, Guit, Tonj North, Tonj East. Having presence in most of those locations, Coalition for Humanity has identified this need and would like to serve these counties. Similarly Leer, Mayendit, Mayom, Rumbek North, Maiwut, Ulang, Akobo, Longechuk, Koch,Panyijiar, Rubkona and Abiemnhom have reported lowest coverage of water sanitation and hygiene facilities with a majority practicing open defecation and accessing unsafe water. If these needs are not addressed, the poor sanitation and hygiene is increasing the disease burden and can be catastrophic. Coalition or Humanity seeks to address the above needs in the selected counties though a WASH project integrated with protection/GBV risk analysis, mitigation and management at water points. 2 - Project activities

1. Construction and Rehabilitation of water points: Coalition for Humanity will construct/ rehabilitate and disinfect existing dysfunctional water sources in order to improve access to clean sufficient water in the community. 2. Household water treatment: Identification, registration and verification and distribution Aqua tabs, jerrycans, water storage containers to vulnerable house households. 3. Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities: Construction/ rehabilitation of community latrine blocks segregated by gender. The latrines will be constructed in institutions like churches, schools, health facilities where IDPs are currently residing due to floods4. MHM kit distribution at community and Institutional level: Coalition for humanity will identify vulnerable girls and women of child bearing age, distribute mistral hygiene and dignity kits. 5. Community and institutional hygiene promotion: Coalition for Humanity will organize targeted outreach on importance of proper sanitation and hygiene, water treatment and proper menstrual hygiene management. awareness will take place door to door, at water points and in community meeting. Community members will be educated on proper water storage, water treatment. This activity will be achieved through the use of community dialogue tools, fliers, and posters. 6. Training of Hygiene promoters: Coalition for Humanity will Identify hygiene promoters per affected Payam and train them on water quality monitoring, importance of hygiene and sanitation, water treatment and menstrual hygiene. The hygiene promoters will be assigned to reach households with hygiene information and monitor water treatment behavior, monitor access to latrines and hand washing facilities.7. AAP/HLP/Protection/GBV safety audits: Coalition for Humanity will conduct AAP/HLP/Protection/GBV safety audits at water points and distribution sites to monitor the risks that are likely to affect women and girls on their way to collect water.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff and enumerators, all humanitarian staff, community leaders are taken through 1 day training on PSEA. The community leaders will be selected across all Payams, after training, they will sensitize the community members on their rights and reporting mechanisms on any cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. during community mobilization, the community members will be sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited. They will be given direct contacts to call, women leaders will be appointed to collect feedback on any cases of SEA and forward to Coalition for Humanity

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will involve people with disability and people with special needs at project inception and needs assessment. People with disability groups will be mandated to monitor and report any challenges facing people with disabilities. The project has designed a house-to-house monitoring form to be used by community leaders and hygiene promoters, the form will be used to follow up on households that are not able to use suggestion box, they will provide means in which they can be contacted by phone, or will give verbal feedback to leaders and hygiene promoters to document. The Post distribution survey will be administered verbally and entries made by literate enumerators

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Coalition for Humanity has engaged with various communities affected baseline survey, focus group discussions, community consultations before and during the development of this Project. Moving forward, Coalition for Humanity will continue engaging the communities through risks assessment, WASH GBV related risks discussions, community engagement meetings/Consultations (CEC) and PDMs involving the affected community before and during the project implementation. The community needs will be verified through focus group discussions and Key Informant Interviews with leaders. A project inception meeting will be held with local authorities involving women, men, boys and girls to ensure participation and feedback. People with disability and the minority will also be invited during inception meetings to ensure their needs are captured, and they will have a role to paly in project implementation, monitoring and reporting. A feedback desk and complaint box will be set up during distribution with beneficiaries able to register complaints. Coalition for Humanity will adapt participatory monitoring and evaluation. A post distribution survey will be conducted with the affected community. The survey will seek feedback on the conduct of activities and satisfaction by the affected persons. Affected community will make recommendations for future

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from community will inform the WASH risks, most needy Payams, Bomas, most affected population, and this will inform prioritization at community level. Feedback on needs beyond this project will be referred to other partners in the county who have capacity to handle cases beyond this project. Further feedback will inform change in strategy, and future proposals

8.11. Mitigation Measures

There is a risk of external stakeholder interference, Coalition for Humanity will conduct a stakeholder engagement meeting at the start of the project to define objectives of the project, their role and responsibility. The meeting will spell out mechanisms on how beneficiaries are identified. Coalition for Humanity will hire non partisan enumerators to support registration and distribution process. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity will ensure latrines constructed are segregated by gender and are disability friendly. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, Community leaders are also trained on PSEA . The project will raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised. Community will be provided with Mobile numbers, Thuraya to call in case of any complains on sexual exploitation and abuse. women leaders will be selected and mandated to report any issues related to GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse affecting girls and women. The women will give feedback through GBV and PSEA referral pathways or directly to Coalition for Humanity Staff, CH head office and partners.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Increase supply of household water through rehabilitation of boreholes increases access to drinking water, access to water for food preparation, access to water for kitchen garden irrigation hence improved access food and food security hence decreasing populations in IPC 3, 4 5

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project targets vulnerable people with disability, elderly, women, unaccompanied children, as priorities to receive WASH items. All latrines constructed will be segregated by gender and will be disability friendly.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Lack of access to sanitation facilities, and lack of access to safe and adequate water leads to malnutrition and diseases. Poor sanitation and hygiene has been linked to intestinal worms that augment malnutrition among children under the age of 5. The improvement of access to clean water and sanitation reduces the intestinal worms hence reducing the rate of malnutrition.
