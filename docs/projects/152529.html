Project Summary

This project seeks to improve the psychosocial well-being of women in Leer County. A network of mother-to-mother ‘Cascade Groups’ is supported by staff trained in appropriate modalities of mental health and psychosocial support. Child-friendly spaces complement Medair’s integrated programming in Leer.

Needs

The crisis in South Sudan is considered to be primarily a protection crisis. Protection concerns related to conflict and violence continue to be the primary drivers of displacement. As of October 2018, 1.96 million people were internally displaced (draft HRP 2019), living in formal and informal settlements, host communities and in hard-to-reach areas. Prolonged conflict continues to impact the physical and mental wellbeing of vulnerable populations. 5,732 cases of SGBV were reported in 2017 (draft HNO). The recurring conflict and brutal violence in Leer County that began in 2014, with exacerbations continuing into 2018, has included killing, rape, abduction and loss of homes and livelihoods for thousands of people, as well as large-scale displacement.There is a growing body of reports regarding the extent of the trauma experienced and witnessed by the population within Leer. These reports describe an alarming picture of an almost uniformly traumatized population that has suffered individual, family and community losses. Up to 90% of people interviewed lost at least one relative in the violence and 75% of interviewees indicated that their family was experiencing at least one symptom of trauma (Protection Cluster report 2016). Focus group discussions conducted in 2016 showed that 66-100% had experienced/witnessed physical violence by a stranger and 25% had experienced/witnessed sexual violence by a stranger. Assessments by protection and MHPSS actors in Leer in 2017 and 2018 paint a similar ongoing picture of SGBV and trauma, and also reflect the severity of the current MHPSS needs in this area.Given the extreme trauma exposure there is a need to support the psychosocial well-being of this population, through the provision of mental health and PSS services. If appropriate services are not provided as a preventative measure, the ability of the population to cope with further shocks will be severely compromised. Medair’s protection programming in Leer will be integrated alongside its ongoing nutrition, health, and WASH programme.

Activities

MHPSS: • Regular supervision of the PSS team by the Psychosocial Support Project Manager.• Equip and supervise two PSS Officers to support 13 PSS Assistants in provision of psychoeducation and life- and stress management skills to Cascade Group. PSS staff trained and supervised in the provision of PFA, Community Based Psychosocial Support (CBPS) and Life Skills to recognize and manage mild mental health needs, and link beneficiaries with appropriate services.• Train at least one PSS Assistant per nutrition site to lead integration of PSS activities into the cascade group network and facilitate nutrition site activities including child friendly spaces and discussions with groups of mothers on coping mechanisms. • Provide training to all PSS Assistants (CG promoters) and lead mothers (Cascade Group Volunteers) on leading peer to peer support groups, SGBV referrals, behaviour change communication and provision of psychosocial support and referral. • Provide training to existing WASH, health and nutrition staff on psychological first aid (PFA).• Continue to implement a peer-to-peer group module on psychosocial support topics for use in cascade group networks, for example positive coping mechanisms, how to stay safe and to reduce stigma with regard to mental health and SGBV.• Increase community awareness of available mental health and SGBV services through Cascade Groups. Consider use of focus group discussions and/or barrier analysis to investigate the factors influencing uptake of such services.• Revise supervision system with quality checklists for supervision of PSS activities including child friendly spaces at nutrition sites, cascade group PSS sessions and CGV group PSS meetings with neighbour women, and PSS Assistant follow up home visits for mothers needing more support. • Conduct a pre and post qualitative mother’s support group participant’s coping mechanism survey on randomly selected peer group participants.• Link psychosocial support focal points with community nutrition workers for periodic home visits. • Recruit, train, equip and supervise up to two Health Managers and two Health Officers to detect, diagnose and manage selected mental health conditions as per mhGAP-HIG guideline.• Co-ordinate with protection, psychosocial and other GBV actors to establish confidential, safe mutual referral pathways as appropriate and feasible.• Actively participate in and coordinate with other actors in the MHPSS working group at Juba level.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Cascade Groups’, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback gathered from the Care Groups. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Further information is shared within Medair’s child-friendly spaces. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, equipping community members with the knowledge and skills to treat their own communities ongoing.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All IEC material is tailored to the relevant population group, with translation into local languages when required.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Through leading the cascade groups, the lead mothers and PSS assistants provide a ‘safe space’ within the communities in which sensitive concerns such as PSEA can be disclosed in confidence, and fed back to the organisation safely and effectively.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Although ‘usual constituencies’ is not fully defined, Medair undertakes stakeholder analysis and conducts focus group discussions with all relevant stakeholders, as informed by this. The Cascade Groups themselves have played, and continue to play, a key role in representing the views of communities to Medair on a regular basis.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The Cascade Group network itself forms an ongoing feedback mechanism from the community to Medair. Additionally, regular meetings with communities are undertaken throughout interventions to elicit further feedback and to respond to concerns raised.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Medair’s peer-support ‘Cascade Groups’ provide a natural route through which the views of the community are continually engaged. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to elicit their opinions and advice regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, creating a further network of local contacts through which community feedback is sought. Additionally, Medair conducts regular ‘knowledge, practice and coverage’ evaluations, using local staff to conduct these exercises which elicit information from communities regarding health needs and behaviours. Barrier analysis is utilised to engage community participation in overcoming obstacles to accessing services and practicing healthy behaviours.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community have and continue to be consulted regarding Cascade Group meetings and activities, to ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to increased risk through this intervention. Medair works with the community to ensure that the CGs themselves are understood and accepted by the community. All needs assessments include protection components. The largely community-based intervention design reduces the potential protection risks faced by women and children involved in travelling to facilities. The siting of child-friendly spaces is directed by protection-related factors.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Medair seeks to ensure that the all mothers who could participate in a CG are able to do so free from intimidation or discrimination. The CG participants, known as ‘neighbour women’ are residents within the same community. Communities will be consulted to ensure that choice of location for the CG meetings does not disadvantage any group.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The CG participants are women, though the acceptance of the CGs and the behaviour change intended requires acceptance by the rest of the community. Medair does work with the community leaders to ensure that they understand and value the CGs and the behaviour changes that the project seeks to introduce. Staff are trained in protection-sensitive programming as part of Medair’s standard briefing procedures. Medair regards confidentiality very highly, especially for those who present with SGBV. Same sex service providers will be selected when possible and staff are trained in Psychological First Aid. Medair liaises with existing protection partners to identify and refer cases requiring more specialised protection support.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Medair will monitor the age and gender breakdown of those accessing services, comparing these with expected breakdowns based on demographic statistics. If discrepancies are found, Medair will use focus group discussions and community meetings to understand, address and mitigate access barriers.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Medair ensures beneficiary safety through the access and protection considerations as mentioned above, but also by ensuring that beneficiary confidentiality is maintained appropriately, with particular sensitivity to SGBV survivors. Medair liaises with existing protection partners in all locations to identify and refer cases requiring specialised protection support. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of safety through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity is one of Medair’s values. As such, it is an organisational priority in all interventions and staff are trained to understand what this means in how to treat beneficiaries. Examples of this include using private and confidential spaces wherever possible for clinical examination and counselling. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of dignity through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.
