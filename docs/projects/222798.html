Project Summary

Access to health services in South Sudan is greatly hindered be increased needs of health services by populations and lack of functional health facilities and trained health personnel. In the recent years, the country has experienced numerous measles outbreaks and its still recovering from the shocks of COVID 19 pandemic that greatly shock the health systems.
Currently the country is experiencing a wave of Cholera outbreak in upper Nile state As of 28 November 2024, a cumulative total of 1,007 suspected cholera cases have been reported in 11 counties in 5 states. Malakal County accounts for 62% (623) of all cases, followed by Renk at 21% (201). There are also reported suspected cases in Ulang and Panyikang Country. This can be attributed to the serious flooding that has engulfed the country apparently affecting over 1.4 million people and displacing more in 22 counties across South Sudan.
This project will address the health needs of the affected populations by ensuring that they have access to health services according to the basic package for health and nutrition services. TADO will offer integrated health services in 6 counties of Nasir, Ulang, Uror, Akobo, Kapoeta East and Tonj South Counties.
TADO will ensure that the population are reached with curative services though OPD consultations in 6 PHCUs and 4 Mobile units targeting over 36000 (8000 men, 10000 women, 9000 boys 9000 girls) people in the 6 counties. Through these interventions, the beneficiaries will also be reached with live saving reproductive health services, ANC services and skilled birth deliveries attended by skilled and trained health personnel. The deliveries will be done in health facilities.
Immunization services are key in preventing the spread of vaccine preventable infections, TADO will ensure that it improves the vaccination coverage in the 6 counties through immunization of children under five with vaccines and target zero dose children who are displaced by floods and also have fled the Sudan conflict.
The project will ensure that there is delivery of health services through referrals of the severe sick persons to higher level health care facilities. The referrals will also include pregnant mothers who are due for delivery and are having prolonged labour. Children under five will be managed using integrated management of childhood illnesses guidelines that will support the children and ensure that they are managed for diarrhoea, pneumonia and malaria.
The project will also ensure there is health promotion geared towards improving health seeking behaviours thus has been a major issue in supporting health service delivery. Growth monitoring for children who come to the health facilities will be done and referral to nutrition centres done for the children identified with malnutrition in the health facilities.
There will be integration of protection in the services provided by ensuring that survivors of gender based violence have access to case management of rape, mental health and psychosocial support will be done and referral to legal aid where possible.
In order for the health workers to perform their duties well, TADO will build the capacity of the health team with key trainings that include infection prevention and control, IDSR and case management of rape. Other trainings like BHI modules will be done to the BHW who will support the project in delivery of health services at boma level.
Support supervision visits done in collaboration with the county health department will be done to the health facilities that are supported by the project and ensure health workers in the health facilities are able to offer quality services and gaps corrected.
TADO will do reporting on EWARS on weekly basis and also 5W reports to the cluster that will ensure important health data is disseminated to help make important decisions on health service delivery.

1. Needs

The health needs in South Sudan are immerse as the country is having protracted crisis attributed to conflict and effects of climate change. This includes flooding that is currently in the country and has been a major issue of concern over the last 4 years. This year alone, over 1.4 million people are affected. Flood waters bring along health concerns that include increased malaria cases, increased watery diarrhoea and increased respiratory tract infections.Currently, the country is struggling to contain an active cholera outbreak that has already affected 11 counties predominantly in Upper Nile state and expected to spread to other counties. As of 28 November 2024, a cumulative total of 1,007 suspected cholera cases have been reported in 11 counties in 5 states. Malakal County accounts for 62% (623) of all cases, followed by Renk at 21% (201). There are also reported suspected cases in Ulang and Panyikang Country.Measles outbreaks have also been a major challenge in the country and as of Epi week 32, 2024, 3,160 suspected measles cases were reported, 173 (5.5%) lab-confirmed, 41 reported measles deaths, and a case fatality rate of (1.3). (WHO outbreak situation report wk. 32 2024. This vaccine preventable disease targeted for elimination has been a major public health concern in the country. This can be attributed to low vaccination rates and influx of un immunized children who have been displaced by conflict in various parts of the country and also from the Sudan conflict.Akobo County has an active measles outbreak and according to the county health department a total of 5 cases have tested positive for measled from the national public health laboratory in November 2024 (Akobo Cluster minutes Nov 2024). This can be attributed to low immunization coverage and movement of populations from Ethiopia and also those fleeing conflict in Sudan. Nasir county has also had its share of health challenges as returnees from Ethiopia and Sudan trickle in on transit to other parts of the country. Conflict has affected health service delivery in the country with IRNA conducted in August 2024 showing that over 15000 people have been displaced due to internal conflict and houses torched. These displacements affect the delivery of health services.Due to the active cholera outbreak in upper nile state, Nasir County is classified as a high risk county for the cholera outbreak and the other counties are at risk of the outbreak. Poor WASH facilities in the counties have significantly contributed to the outbreak and poor health seeking behaviours lead to increased risk to increased morbidities in the country.The country is also facing acute food insecurity and according to IPC report released in Nov 2024, over 6.3 million people are food insecurity and over 2.1 children under five years are having acute malnutrition out of which 650000 children with Severe acute Malnutrition and over 1.1 million women of childbearing age also facing malnutrition. 1.7 million people are expected to be in IPC 4 up to July 2025. (IPC Nov 2024 report). This puts pressure on the health systems since malnutrition exposes the children to other health issues like diarrhoea and lowers their immunity thus increasing susceptibility to infections. Akobo, Nasir, Uror and Kapoeta East counties are in IPC 3 and 4. Kapoeta East has 26000 people in IPC 4, Akobo has 35000, Uror 61000 and Nasir has also 61000. This are people in extreme need of quality health services to improve health indicators.The capacity of health workers in the health facilities remain low due to lack of training facilities in the country. The staff turnover from is also very high due to low pay and delayed payment in the health facilities. Information management is which is a major pillar in health system strengthening remains a major issue with delayed reports that often make delayed response to health emergencies and thus spread of disease outbreaks.

2. Project activities

• Immunization services against vaccine preventable infections for children under 15 years. This will include training vaccinators on Immunization in practice.• Mental health services and referrals• OPD consultations and referral of the patients that need inpatient care to higher level health care facilities• ANC and PNC services• Injury and trauma management.• Sexually transmitted infection management including case management of rape • Skilled birth attendant deliveries.• Health Education at the health facilities and in the communities • Nutrition screening for pregnant and lactating mothers and children under five years old including referral to nutrition centers for children identified with malnutrition.• Case management of rape • Distribution of contraceptives and other reproductive health services to women and men of reproductive age• Infection prevention and control in the health facilities• Training of health workers on Infection prevention and control, integrated disease surveillance and response, integrated management of childhood illnesses and BHI module for the boma health workers• Joint support supervision to the health facilities in collaboration with the country health departments.• Timely reporting on EWARS, DHIS2 and 5W to the cluster.
