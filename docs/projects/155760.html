Project Summary

The communities of Orakzai Agency are affected by a conflict that has left them in a position where external support and interventions are depended on. If the need is not met by government agencies or civil society organizations, the chances of mass displacement and an increase in potential security risks in the area and, even at the wider level, will escalate. Enabling children to return to school is a crucial part of normalizing life after a crisis. The project will critically focus on identifying out-of-school learners from Orakzai Agency and prepare them to ‘catch-up’ to their peers through getting re-integrated into formal education. The project will provide parents and guardians of out-of-school children access to relevant training and other opportunities to further compensate and remove economic dependencies on their children. Considering them key duty bearers, the capacity of government officials and representatives of civil society organizations will also be built to ensure a formal response mechanism is well established and strengthened to serve the vulnerable communities.
The project consider children’s education is not only an effective to introduce improvements in gender in-equalities but also to hasten development and break the intergenerational cycle of hunger and poverty. The proposed project will holistically address the multiple barriers to children, with special focus on girl’s, education. Thus, the basic tenet of the project is that the bottleneck (the “push factors”) around children’s access and completion of education can only be overcome by providing and integrated approach (the “pull factor”) that tackle multiple factors that inhibit children’s education.
Proposed project will also have the potential of transforming behaviors of children and communities in conflict affected areas where the need of their engagement for self-development and psycho-social assistance is highly needed to come out of the implication that conflict has caused to their lives. The project will not only capacitate them to understand and adopt the alternate practices for their improved growth, rather it will combine them as community networks to sustain and emerge as self-help groups that have the understanding of their available resources and potential and how these can be taken into account while in such circumstances.

Needs

According to the “United Nation’s Gender In-Equality Index – 2017” Pakistan is ranked as 133 out of 151 . In-equalities are reported as more evident in, previously known, KP TRIBAL DISTRICTS agencies, where female headed households are more likely than male headed households to be poor and less educated. This can be explained in part due to the specific impediments women face in having access to resources and education. Along with it, cultural practices are another barrier to women empowerment in the area. There are compelling evidences to show that the girls are, comparatively, more at risk that boys. The prevailing cultural practices and social restrictions are considered more applicable towards women or girls that provides the basis of women staying out of business and have no access to resources and even their basic rights as well. Assessment revealed 78% of the girls being out of school in Orakzai Agency.Retention Rate in Government Schools: KP TRIBAL DISTRICTS has been reported as an area with worst ratio of primary school enrollment of children which is further compromised at middle and secondary levels. According to the Education Sector analysis report, the Gross Enrollment Ration (GER) is 51% that includes 70% boys and 33% girls at Primary level in, previously called KP TRIBAL DISTRICTS Agencies. Assessment revealed it being the lowest in Orakzai Agency which reflects the overall enrollment as 34% boys and 22% girls get enrolled into primary education. While approaching towards the retention of these enrolled children, the assessment concludes with 29.03% at primary education. Further, the findings indicate that 69.5% of the teachers did not receive any post-service training from any of the government / non-governmental educational bodies.An unfriendly school environment with scarce resources and in-equate methodologies is another critical barrier in promoting children’s access to basic education. The assessment clarifies that only 05% of the schools have are in satisfactory conditions, but not considered as ideal ones for the children even. 95% of the educational facilities are damaged in the area. Although most of the building structures are observed as available but it highlights the need of major structural rehabilitations to stat the buildings are protective and safer for the children. The agency education Officer informed that 493 schools are available in Orakzai Agency, out of which, 52% are for girls and 48% of the schools are for boys. It is found, in this regard, that 39% of the schools are fully damaged while 59% of the schools are reported as partially damaged. Lack of transportation for Teachers, in-effective monitoring, supervision and accountability lead towards and increased absenteeism of teachers, poor quality education & learning, low-enrollment and high drop-out rates.WASH: It is found that mostly female and children are held responsible to fetch water from their nearby rivers. Streams. Correspondingly, the 68% of the female and 38% male responsible for fetching water. The 55% children are already included in fetching water, moreover they are feel insure as they must travel miles to get Water from available sources. Open defecation is widely being practiced that carried real health, safety & security implications for the communities. A designated place for defecation is available for only 21% respondents despite the nature of the latrine facility availability, while 79% revealed that they don’t have any designated place for open defecation. After the crises, 98% mentioned that there are neither availability of private latrine nor at communal level in their area. As per the Orakzai TD assessment findings, it is noted that common diseases are Cough/cold, malaria, diarrhea and skill infection. Majority people around 54% are using medicines at household level for the diarheal treatment, 25% are going with traditional healers, only 23% are used to with hospitals/clinic and 7% are familiar with ORS.
