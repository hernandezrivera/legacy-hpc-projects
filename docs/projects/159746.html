Project Summary

The proposed intervention will target crisis-affected populations in Bay, Lower Juba, Galgaduud and Gedo with 504,000 people in IPC 3&4 combined and in need of humanitarian assistance. The beneficiaries will be targeted from both urban and rural centres of vulnerable and displaced persons in Bardera, Garbaharey, Kismayo, Caadado and Baidoa that are currently considered nutrition and health hotspots and are projected to have a Critical GAM.
The action will continue utilising cash – based programming to address immediate food for the targeted households (both Conditional Cash transfer (CT)and Unconditional Cash Transfer UCT). UCT action will target 2000 households (12,000 individuals) (60% women) - disabled, women headed households, very elderly household heads, that are unable to do heavy manual labour and are not able to meet the MEB and not able to meet the minimum living threshold of 2100Kcal per person per day and therefore will not be able to participate in CFW activities. The UCT beneficiaries will receive 50 percent of the MEB for the purchase of a basket of goods from functioning markets, calculated to include food and other essentials for a period of three months during the major lean period of Gu/Deyr seasons. The markets in the targeted districts and villages are fully functional, and that basic foods are available. Thus, the cash transfer modality is seen as appropriate in this context. SADO has preferred the use cash transfers since this modality has demonstrated ability to catalyse the growth of local markets, compared to food aid which, when there is no actual food shortage in the local markets, may adversely affect the markets by reducing food prices and therefore risk increasing the vulnerability of food producers and traders. Furthermore, the experience of SADO and others also shows that cash-based programs in appropriate circumstances are less costly and better adjusted to people’s needs and preferences than the distribution of commodities in kind. The MEB is based on the latest Cash Working Group (CWG) recommendations for each of the regions. Targeted households choose the items they buy, but past experience suggests that the bulk of their purchases will be food; therefore, this intervention contributes to the nutrition and health of target beneficiaries. Children and pregnant or lactating women will benefit greatly from this activity because they are generally the most vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition during lean periods.
For CT beneficiaries, SADO will target households who can participate in labour work, yet remain very vulnerable to food insecurity. The project will engage 800HH (4800 individuals) in CFW activities of rehabilitating community assets including rehabilitation of river embankment among others. The cash received will be used to purchase lost assets therefore restore livelihoods. The daily wages will be based on agreed work norms in line with food security cluster standards, but the total monthly wage will vary according to the prevailing cost of the minimum expenditure baskets across the target district. There have been adjustments in the MEB for the above regions due to increased food prices attributed to poor Gu rains received in 2019.
As well, appropriate farm inputs (seed and farm tools and equipment) will be provided to 500 riverine farmers (3000 individuals) recently affected by drought in Gedo to restore and protect their livelihoods to utilise the Deyr rainfall 2019. SADO’s response will help stabilize crisis affected communities and prevent populations from migrating to urban centres in search of food and other services. SADO will also train 60 committees on DRR. The proposed activities have been aligned to Food Security Cluster objectives and strategic priorities. SADO will build on its successful experience implementing similar SHF funded project in Galgaduud regions in Caadado and USAID project under food for peace in Bardera and Elwak regions of Somalia.

Project Activities

IDP households throughout all the four regions of intervention have suffered great loss of livelihoods as a result of the cumulative effects of the drought and conflict induced displacement. As a result of the drought, many households have exhausted their livelihoods and coping mechanisms and have resorted to negative coping strategies such as reducing the number of meals or selling assets to scrap by. The loss of assets and livelihood opportunities for IDPs further deepens their vulnerability, rendering them highly dependent on humanitarian aid. The latest assessment by SADO and partners in Gedo indicate that food and water are the priority needs for most households (HHs) and Household level food insecurity continues to deepen due to conflict and drought based displacement. Most rural and urban HHs are in borderline or poor food consumption categories (FCS) respectively. The IDPs in Galmudug face huge food insecurity and protection related crisis particularly those in Dhusamareeb and Caadado as they are expecting possible eviction after the election. An endline survey by SADO at the conclusion of the SHF project in Caadado indicate that urban IDPs are the most affected with 70% having having poor or borderline FCS. HHs continues to survive on less diversified meals further undermining HHs nutritional status. In rural pastoral in Gedo and Galgaguud regions, loss of livestock, destitution and displacement are reported with local regional government authorities estimating high loss of livestock in the districts and have sent for urgent humanitarian appeal.Activities Activity 1. Conduct livelihoods and food security needs assessment in targeted areas to determine the gaps and needs. FGDs will be done separately to ensure that the views of women and men were captured. Activity 2: Unconditional cash transfer to 2000 households (12000 persons) who will be receiving a monthly cash valued at 50% MEB for a period of 3 months. Activity 3: Conditional cash transfers to beneficiaries on cash for assets to rebuild their asset base i.e. work and receive animals or farm inputs - A total of 800 households (4800 persons) will participate in CFW activities for rehabilitation of 15 selected community assets and benefit from 30 days of CFW opportunities at a daily value of USD as determined by daily labour rates of the area. Activity 4: Provision of agricultural seeds and farming hand tools to 500 riverine and agro-pastoral farmers (3000 persons) Activity 5: Provision of irrigation pumps and start up fuels to 50 riverine farmers (300 persons) in Bardera districts in Gedo Region giving specific consideration to farm families headed by women-led families.

Mitigation Measures

SADO will ensured that the protection environment is maintained by utilizing the checklist developed by the Somalia Food Security Cluster (FSC) members. Activities will be implemented in a non-discriminatory and impartial manner that promotes the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. This will be supported by the establishment of the local village relief committees. SADO will ensure that targeting focuses on vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities and older people, women and children who are particularly susceptible to food insecurity, through unconditional food assistance using vouchers.SADO mainstreams protection principles into all programs by incorporating protection concerns from assessment to implementation of the interventions. The Do No Harm approach utilized by SADO creates harmony between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is achieved through awareness raising and joint targeting and selection of the most vulnerable HHs to participate in the project. SADO maintains consistent and accurate messaging about selection criteria, resource availability, prioritization of assistance, and recognition of the need at the community level to reduce misunderstandings and to maintain stability in the project implementation area. Protection measures will be implemented throughout the project ensuring the needs of men and women, the elderly, children, and the disabled are taken into account. These measures include, among others: • Sex and age disaggregated data as well as conditions of vulnerability; • Sensitizing the CoC regarding Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (SEA) to project staff and RCs. • Beneficiaries will be sensitized to the nature of the assistance & an accountability system will be established for feedback and reporting; • The timing and location of beneficiary registration will be organized to accommodate women’s roles as caregivers and the needs of vulnerable groups; • All project monitors will be trained on protection issues. Furthermore, SADO has adopted CoC and therefore will ensure that all its staff have been sensitized on and have signed CoC, which is consistent with the six core principles of the IASC Task Force on PSEA including implications for violations. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the CoC and will benefit from sexual exploitation and abuse prevention messaging, and will receive information on report violation.A Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will be set up during project initiation stage with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards and procedures. They will be provided with brochures, banners and pamphlets containing these standards and for those unable to read or in the case of children will receive visual illustrations from SADO field based staff on the accountability standards. A toll free telephone contacts will be provided through which they can call and register their complaints. A database will be developed by our IT staff in which all the complaints will be recorded and appropriate redress taken within the shortest time possible and recorded as well. All complaints will be responded to within a maximum period of 72 hours and the responses will take into consideration the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys. This field based CRM database will be analyzed on a monthly basis. During meetings with project beneficiaries, 2 desks will be set up, one manned by a male staff and another by a female staff to receive feedback from beneficiaries. Child friendly accountability standards will also be displayed in these meetings.

Please provide further explanation

SADO ceased operating in areas under the control of the non-state controlled militia groups. In the targeted project areas under the control of government in Galgaduud and Gedo regions. SADO has been having unrestricted access and has not faced any security challenges that may hinder resilience / durable solutions in the project locations. SADO will monitor the security situation on daily basis and if possible adjust outreach schedules as needed while consulting with government institutions and local communities through Village Relief Committees. During project launch and design, SADO will engage the local actors and stakeholders to discuss the long term interventions in the project areas. As such, the livelihood component of the project is intended to bring back the livelihoods of the farmers who lost pumps and tools during the flooding. The provision of farm tools is therefore considered a long term solution for improving food security and ensuring resilience. The beneficiaries will also be informed of their entitlement during project sensitization and mobilization sessions. The VRCs will inform the beneficiaries about the entitlements and a complain and feedback mechanisms will be established. As well, MOU's will signed with the community for asset rehabilitation. SADO will ensure a proper clan balance in the VRC as well as a gender balance.
