Project Summary

The aim of the project is to strengthen health information systems for emergency response and resilience across the country. The information generated from the health
information systems provide up-to-date information on functionality of facilities, availability of services, and accessibility of beneficiaries. All health partners are/would
report including those operating public, NGO, private health facilities. To ensure quality health information products; WHO conducts joint monitoring visits with MoH to health facilities to follow up on HIS issues at health facility level (reporting, challenges, needs…) in addition to HeRAMS internal and external evaluation exercises.
In north-western Syria Reliable, timely, and good-quality data is particularly important to inform decisions in crisis situations and complex environments such as northwest Syria. Health Information Systems (HIS) generates data at regular intervals from health facilities. The information generated enables health program managers and other health stakeholders to monitor, evaluate, and improve health system performance, and to make informed decisions. Since the beginning of 2019 HIS started by generating core health indicators with technical support from WHO and built the aggregated data forms involving the most important and frequent health aspects, this includes number of consultations either in clinic or emergency room, types of consultations, communicable diseases, and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health, hospital workload, rehabilitation, blood bank workload, dialysis forms, with regular monthly interactive reporting along with COVID 19 hospitalization data.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Throughout the Syria crisis, national health information systems were disrupted, and the need of information has increased. The most affected areas, such as hard to reach and areas with severity scale more than 3 were considered the most challenging in terms of providing timely, relevant and reliable information on the available health resources and services for decision making.2. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement and donors’ support.3. Lack of data on disability and sensitive gender issues may impact proper planning and response to the needs of such vulnerable groups. 4. Violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers. According to the HNO 2020, Attacks on health care have left 14.5 million people in 42 districts below emergency standards for hospital beds.5. up to 70% of health workers has left Syria and many others have been injured or killed.6. Regarding the health system risks the recent assessments indicate the need for referral capacity, including ambulances to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to lifesaving care.7. Information systems’ data is stored in WHO servers. While data high availability is considered.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: - Lack of information about the health situation in some hard-to-reach areas. - Limitation of financial support to meet the needs of strengthening the health information systems with health partners - Use standardized tools and assessments to assess patient satisfaction and other issues.- Lack of professional staff in hard-to-reach areas which may result in reduce the quality of information.- Difficulties in obtaining timely approvals for comprehensive assessments in high-risk areas. - Non-renewal of UNSCR may affect cross-border delivery of medical supplies and equipment, Mitigation: - Conducting site visits by HIS-FPs especially hard to reach areas, reconciled areas, and regained areas to assess the health situation with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.- Allocating sufficient resources to meet the needs of strengthening the health information systems with health - Using standard tools developed by WCO, such as “monitoring tool of delivered assistance by WHO” and others.- Strengthening the capacity building of the staff across the country focusing on hard-to-reach areas at different level on HeRAMS and other HIS areas. - Linkage of the emergency health information systems into the planned national HMIS strengthening initiative to collect health burden in all areas.- WHO is in continuous discussions to hand over parts of its interventions to international INGO.
