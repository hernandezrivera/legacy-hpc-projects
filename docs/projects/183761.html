Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to improve community access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services and facilities for vulnerable populations, including IDPs, Returnees and Host Community in South Kordofan, Blue Nile, Khartoum, Central & West Darfur States in Sudan and reduce the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic by improving community access to hand washing facilities and supplies.
The project targets 394,760 crisis-affected people (91,144 women, 88,550 men, 109,183 girls, 105,882 boys) in 8 localities of Central Darfur (Zalinge, Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Bendesi, Mukjar & Umdukun), 7 localities in West Darfur (Genina, Kernik, Kulbus, Habila, Beida, Jabel-Moon & Sirba), 6 localities in Blue Nile (Kurmuk, Geissan, Bau, Wad Almahi, Tadamon & Damazine), 4 localities in Khartoum (Bahri, Khartoum, Um-Durman, & Jebel Awlia) and 7 localities in South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, Abu kashola, Dilling, Gedir, Heiban, Tadamon, & Talodi).
Firstly, this project aims to improve access to basic water services for crisis-affected populations. Under this objective, the interventions ensure drinking water from an improved source with a collection time of not more than 30 minutes roundtrip, including queuing. People targeted with basic water services will benefit from new installations and maintenance, operations, and rehabilitation of existing handpumps, water yards, protected wells including check-dams, and hafirs. KAP assessments by WRS in 2020 show that in the dry season, women and children walk far distances to fetch water. Women and girls face explicit risk of sexual violence as they go about their daily task of collecting water. Therefore, to mitigate such risks, the project will ensure that water sources are close to homes. Furthermore, the project will ensure that the different committees either established or strengthened by the project consist of at least 40% women. Secondly, the project aims to ensure improved access to adequate, protected, and gender sensitive sanitation services for conflict-affected populations. Interventions will ensure the installation, rehabilitation, and use of ventilated improved pit latrines (VIP), pit latrines with slabs, composting toilets, and flush/pour pit latrines where possible. Household latrines (basic), latrines shared between two households (limited), communal latrines, and institutional latrines will be considered. For sustainability, community volunteers will be trained on CLTS. To ensure effective behavior change, community based hygiene promotion volunteer networks will be established and supported to disseminate hygiene messages and monitor household hygiene practices.
Thirdly, to ensure access to hygiene promotion services, water quality and environmental health interventions will be provided to 208,000 crisis-affected populations. The targeted population will receive hygiene promotion messages focusing on handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage. Half of the targeted population will be provided with soap and jerry cans. Handwashing facilities will reach people in communal setups. Women and girls who are part of the newly displaced population will receive personal hygiene materials such as reusable sanitary pads or culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene supplies. Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected children, along with their households, will be reached with WASH supplies such as soap, jerry cans, buckets, and point of use water treatment materials, including aqua tabs and PUR along with appropriate hygiene promotion messages in the OTPs and Stabilization Centers (SCs). People located in the epidemics and flood-prone areas will be
reached with PoUWT and water treatment at the source, followed by water quality monitoring to check on the contamination levels. Environmental health interventions, such as solid waste management and vector control, will be implemented in risk-prone areas.

1 - Needs

The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation has deteriorated due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, decreased revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs due to an increase in fuel prices in 2021According to the HNO (2021), Sudan's protracted crisis continues to create water, hygiene, and sanitation needs for vulnerable people. The key drivers of WASH needs are the deepening economic crisis, conflicts, weak WASH services, poor knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to WASH, poor community governance of WASH infrastructure in rural areas, floods, climate change, inadequate and inconsistent availability of soap for personal hygiene, poor hygiene practices, open defecation due to absent and limited latrines, frequent breakdown of water supply infrastructure due to overuse, and erratic fuel supply . The data from the state MoH in Central Darfur indicates that diarrhea is still the major cause of mortality and morbidity of children, attributed mainly to poor access to clean water supply, hygiene, and sanitation. According to the KAP survey result conducted in Umdukhum (June 2020), 54% of the population are practicing open defecation which poses grave public health risks, including the transmission of water-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhea, dysentery, hepatitis A and typhoid. There are also high WASH needs related to hygiene and water safety in flood-prone villages of Central Darfur (for example) 3,718 pit latrines and 12 VIP latrines collapsed in West Jebel Mara locality due to the heavy rainfall (July- September 2020). According to IGAD’s assessment and report on the extent and impact of floods in Sudan as at 6th September 2020, Sudan was affected by floods that caused havoc. According to the report from the Government of Sudan as of 6th September 2020, more than 500,000 people were affected, over 100 lost their lives, and about 100,000 houses were reported to have been destroyed or damaged with many families rendered homeless. With a high possibility for heavy rains expected in the coming months, the challenge is far from over and all preparedness measures need to be bolstered. The extensive floods affected over 80 localities and villages, from 16 out of 18 States of Sudan including Khartoum, Sharg El Neel and Jebel Awlia localities that are being targeted by this project. Collectively, over 1,000 households were destroyed and an estimated 24 people lost their lives in Khartoum State. COVID-19 exposed WASH gaps in urban areas of Khartoum, Blue Nile, South Kordofan, West and Central Darfur states, which faced problems due to wide disparities in access to and use of WASH services. Most of the schools and primary health care facilities have limited access to WASH facilities and services. Records showed 64% of the schools in the targeted localities have no gender segregated latrines which provide dignified service to boys and girls. Menstrual hygiene management is often not prioritized, negatively impacting girls' attendance and enrollment in schools. IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents will need continued WASH support. In many rural villages, these population groups walk over five kilometers to fetch water, especially during the dry season. Based on the SMART Survey findings, an average of 64% of HHs are collecting water from unprotected water sources including the covered hand dug wells, hand pumps, and water yards both in the summer and rainy season. The use of surface water “Hafir” increased from 20.5% in the dry season to 31.6% in the rainy season. Only 20% of the respondents in Umdukhum reported that they are practicing water treatment at the household level, which includes a high probability of exposure to water borne diseases. The survey depicted an average water consumption of 7.1L/per person/per day, which is way below sphere minimum standards and WHO’s recommended 15L/person/day in an emergency context.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project takes the maximum care not to cause damage to the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activity implementation. The environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed in a manner that is sensitive to the environment, and that necessary mitigation measures were taken as per the guidelines. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles, WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create community awareness on the environment. The trained community members will further create awareness to the beneficiary communities on the efficient use of water resources and protection of the environment. Besides, the water management committees will be monitoring ground water for water projects supported by the project and WR, to avoid depletion of ground water and to take joint measures in enhancing the recharge of ground water. Emphasis has been given to installing solar energy instead of supporting diesel energy, because solar is environmentally friendly, cost effective, and efficient. In addition, the ferro cement screen household latrine is environmental friendly technology which will ensure no trees are cut, rather that the technology will utilize sand mineral which is readily available in the area. The side slabs are thin and portable, and reusable in their lifetime as opposed to wooden superstructures. Additionally this project will address the issue of environmental health by establishing solid waste management committees among the targeted villages. Furthermore, through FSL sector WR is supporting tree nurseries in the project proposed localities and the seedlings produced in the nurseries will be planted in the project operational areas.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

While providing WASH services and facilities, this project will consider girls' and women's security and safety. Their engagement will be critical while identifying water points and new water sources to save them travel too far to unsecured places to fetch water. Furthermore, this project will ensure that the communal and institutional latrines are gender-segregated, safe with latches on the doors, and easy to access in secure places. The project will link women and girls to income-generating women's groups toreceive training on making sanitary pads and soap. Additionally WRS will install and construct suitable facilities to suit the aged and disable population. WRS will uphold the five minimum commitments, including consulting or engaging the affected population in assessments, designs, implementation, monitoring, and inclusion of women and girls. Efforts will be made to strengthen feedback mechanisms and post distribution surveys.
