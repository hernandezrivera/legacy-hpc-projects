Project Summary

White Hands (WH) proposes a 12-month action that uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes agriculture and livestock interventions, using an in-kind, voucher, and cash-for-work (CfW) assistance modalities.
This program is a planned action that contributes to the second sector objective of the FSL cluster, which is "Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives." It aims to increase the resilience and livelihood opportunities for the most vulnerable male and female farmers and livestock breeders of both IDPs and affected host communities living in the sub-districts of Afrin, Jandairis, Azaz, Mare, and Daret Azza (Aleppo Governorate) and the sub-districts of Harim, Dana, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Qourqeena, Armanaz (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria, to revitalize of agricultural and livestock sectors in the target locations in the medium-long term.
The program comprises the following Key intervention areas:
1. 4,500 Targeted households (1500 olive farmers and 3000 winter & summer crop farmers) are able to resume agricultural activities, increase household income, and improve consumption through i) provision of agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; olive harvesting and pruning, and ii) improving/restoring agricultural infrastructure (for example irrigation/drainage), IV)Cash for harvest, V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat.
2. 1,500 Targeted households (1,000 sheep breeders own1-10 sheep/or goats and 500 cow breeders who own one cow) living in the sub-districts of Jandairis, A'zaz, Harim, Mare', and Daret Azza are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through i) provision of livestock inputs (compound fodder- mixed (forage + pellet)); ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (2 campaigns, 10500 head per campaign owned by 1,500 livestock breeders).
3. 500 Targeted households (500 women & men over 18 years, of them, 200 of the host community and 300 IDPs) stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community-related value chain infrastructure, disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest & vol control, olive trees harvesting, Purification of wheat fields, and afforestation.
4. Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Mills, bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads, and silos that will benefit 2,000 HHs/10,000 individuals.
WH will identify and conduct a multi-sectoral needs assessment to verify needs, priorities, and gaps, determine the severity level, and identify specific vulnerable groups within communities. To ensure the most vulnerable households are selected, WH conducts a socioeconomic vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated vulnerability scores. The beneficiaries will be prioritized according to clear vulnerability and eligibility criteria.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 8,500 households/ 42,500 individuals (4500 HHs supported with agriculture interventions, 1500 HHs supported with livestock interventions, 500 HHs supported with Cash for Work assistance modality, and 2,000 HHs benefited from the rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure).
Note: This is a planned intervention (No secured fund to date).

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO for Syria, 15M PiN for FSL, including 7.5M females & 4.2M PWDs. Syria’s multiple crises have severely affected the agriculture and agri-food sectors, and supporting its resilience has become an urgent national priority. It is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies but also a result of issues with water scarcity and availability, damaged infrastructure and productive assets, disrupted services, and scarcity of energy resources, especially diesel and electricity. In addition, the high prices of agriculture production inputs, especially fertilizers, herbicides, and others, continue to limit agriculture input use by farmers and adherence to standard seed and dose rates. This results in reduced actual yields, reduced crop productivity, and reduced food availability. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed crop production systems but also irrigated crops such as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables. The unfavorable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, and high prices, especially imported feed. This trend is likely to continue if the 2022/2023 rainy season is not favorable.

4 - Implementation of activities

1. Support 1500 Livestock breeders who own one cow or a maximum of 10 sheep/goats with compound fodders. Each sheep/or goat will be supported with 0.75kg of compound fodder & each cow will be supported with 10kg of compound fodder daily, sufficient for 4 months. 2. Establishment of 4 mobile veterinary clinics & provision of veterinary services and vaccinations campaigns (Enterotoxemia, Smallpox, and Foot-and-mouth). 3. Distribution of agricultural inputs through the most appropriate modalities for 3000 winter & summer crop farmers & supported them with NPK & Urea fertilizer 46% and 50 L of fuel for supplementary irrigation. 4. Support 1500 small and mid-sized olive framers (owned 3 to10 dunum) with agricultural inputs, agricultural services, and harvesting support (each olive farmer will be supported with 100 kg compound NPK, 2 L pesticides, 1 Scissor, 1 Hand Saw & 12 tins (capacity of 16 liters). 5. Delivery of training program on the best practices for livestock breeding & GAPs. 6. Wheat, olive, and agriculture infrastructures will be rehabilitated. 7. The procured wheat from farmers will be processed into flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize bread prices and increase the availability & accessibility of bread in the targeted communities. 8. CFW: WH seeks to improve or protect agriculture and bread-related infrastructure or assets by implementing CFW activities, which will enable the provision of 500 labor opportunities ($6/day/beneficiary).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). L: M/ I: H. MS: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set up, postponed, or canceled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that inputs/vouchers distribution is completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk2: Child labor (involving children in CFW or farming activities). L: M/I: H. MS: WH will be sure that the staff working on this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding and child protection. Risk3: Tension between HC, IDPs & returnees. L: H/I: H. MS: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs & not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent & groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make an effort to target a % of the vulnerable HC in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism, conduct follow-up visits, and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: Agricultural/Livestock Inputs distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women (exposure to GBV risk), elderly, and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design, and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of food distribution points remains at a minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document as to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with the extra workload in particular.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the ‎PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries ‎concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are ‎aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it. L: M/I: H. MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Risk2: As a consequence of the project, there is a shortage of program inputs in the area & the prices increase. L: M/I: M. MS2: Vendors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information regarding expected sales & the amount to be distributed to allow the supply of sufficient stocks. The price of the items should be agreed upon with vendors and advertised to the beneficiaries. Risk3: Markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/or supplies. L: M/I: H. MS: The market situation will be monitored regularly by program team. In case of the situation changes, WH would consider relocating the area of intervention where the required conditions are met. Risk4: For CVA, there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. L: M /I: H. MS: WH will strengthen the over-sight mechanism, ensure staff are aware of Code of Conduct, develop a range of measures & internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption, outreach to the affected population, and ensure the presence and information of complaints mechanism.
