Project Summary

Improving the self-subsistence for women headed Households. Multi-field action of agricultural income generating activities in the rural area of Idleb. This project is an active and already funded project, following the HNO 2019/20. For this reason we implement our activities in Idlib Gov. Train 237 women head of household including 3 teachers out of 3 schools in techniques agricultural and livestock breeding. Support 237 women head of household including 3 teachers out of 3 schools in the 3 targeted villages start business self-sustaining through the production of fresh vegetables (120 greenhouses set up 3 of them in the 3 selected schools) and of sheep animal derivatives (4 Sheep per family and 4 Sheep per school). Support 10 families consisting of women head of household are trained and started activities inherent income-generating to agriculture for a project pilot through production of fresh vegetables (10 greenhouses set up) and 10 families with animal derivatives sheep (4 Sheep per family).

4 - Needs

- Improve food security conditions of IDPs and host communities in Idlib governorate. In particular the livelihood opportunities of female HHs- Increase the efficiency and productivity of agro and zoological sectors *Field assessment 30/12/2019 The aftermath of the Turkish incursion in North-east Syria resulted in the displacement of thousands of people, most notably farmers – further reducing primary food production. Throughout the country, persistent insecurity and random attacks on humanitarian personnel in 2020, also limited accessto highly food insecure locations. These results highlight a notable increase in the number of food-insecure people across both rural and major urban areas in Syria. Adult women and men are specifically strainedand impacted by food insecurity as they frequently engage in adverse coping mechanisms to ensure that their children have enough food to eat. Furthermore, from the assessed households, 31 per cent of the male-headed households adopted crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies compared to 35per cent of the female-headed households, who are often placed in a situation where they are at risk of being exploited due to their status. The lack of technical services coupled with the absence of emergency interventions focusing on integrated pest and disease management and the unavailability of Syrian wheat varieties are further affecting farmers in North-west Syria. . Livestock breeders’ remain vulnerable since their animals are at risk of disease outbreaks; with limited pasture availability and high feed costs, many livestock farmers are forced to sell-off their animals, thus adversely affecting overall livestock production levels andcompromising the long-term livelihoods.

5 - Implementation of activities

Train 237 women head of household including 3 teachers out of 3 schools in techniques agricultural and livestock breeding. Support 237 women head of household including 3 teachers out of 3 schools in the 3 targeted villages start business self-sustaining through the production of fresh vegetables (120 greenhouses set up 3 of them in the 3 selected schools) and of sheep animal derivatives (4 Sheep per family and 4 Sheep per school). We decided to distribute 4 sheep per family because of the lesson learned from previous projects and because of the specific situation of the beneficiaries, mainly female HHs living in rural areas. The number took into consideration the required space for breeding the animals and the capacity (technical and financial) to take care of the sheep in the medium long term. Support 10 families consisting of women head of household are trained and started activities inherent income-generating to agriculture for a project pilot through production of fresh vegetables (10 greenhouses set up) and 10 families with animal derivatives sheep (4 Sheep per family). Improve the knowledge of 600 livestock breeders and 600 farmers in the area. They receive training on techniques e products to increase the their ability to produce plants and sheep breeding for produce more food e increase their resilience e sustainability.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

SGBV and Child labour are the main risks that beneficiaries or humanitarian workers may be exposed to through the implementation of the project’s activities. The mitigation measures would be providing technical and in-kind support for the beneficiaries till the income generating activities are well established, providing PSS to children and create a referral path for those suffering from SGBV and child protection issues.Humanitarian workers will be protected by our Duty of Care Policy.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Given the volatility of the context the security situation may change in any moment. New IDPs arrivals are a concrete risk. The project would mitigate the risks if they were to materialize readapting the budget structure up to a 15% as per donor regulation and will reconvert the functions of the field staff to support PIN.
