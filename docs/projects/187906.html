Project Summary

NES population is still facing an extreme density of explosive hazards contamination and confronted with the damage of a 10 year-conflict: Mines, Explosive Remains of War (ERW) and Improvised Explosive Devices (IED) are forbidding the rehabilitation of cities and usage of land. Humanitarian response itself is also heavily affected by hazard contamination, especially making it more difficult to start rehabilitation projects.
80% of the city of Raqqah was destroyed by the offensive led against the Islamic State. Construction work is halted by the persistent presence of mines, especially those difficult to access. All stabilization activities, whether humanitarian or government-led, must include a specific clearance intervention defined as hazardous or confined work depending on the situation. The investigation and clearance phase is imperative and a prerequisite to resume recovery programs in complete safety.
The intensity of fighting also caused a high concentration of pyrotechnic pollution in the Euphrates and irrigation canals, which represents both a technical difficulty to intervene and a proven danger for the surrounding population. The significant drop in the level of the Euphrates this year, the combined effect of water blockage by Turkey and drought, threatens mines that were previously submerged to reappear on its banks frequented by the population. The rehabilitation of irrigation canals, which must be carried out urgently in order to mitigate the effects of droughts, is severely hampered by the known or suspected presence of munitions.
Between 2017 and 2020, HAMAP-Humanitaire (HAMAP) came to strengthen the capacities of two local Syrian organizations by training, mentoring and monitoring non-technical surveys (NTS) and explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) teams, deminers/searchers and medics. Successive military and sanitary crisis in NES, have made our team face major challenges. However, thanks to the will and the unceasing efforts of our teams, we proved to be an enduring, adaptive and a resilient organization in the most difficult times in NES for the past years with impressive achievement records, acknowledged and hailed by the local authorities, NES Forum and many NGOs.
In 2020, HAMAP reorganized its operations and chose to open its own local office in Raqqah. Under the responsibility of the coordination and three EOD demining experts, more than 50 staffs have been recruited and trained to start operational activities including NTS, EORE and clearance. The ongoing project is taking place from September 2021 until May 2022 with the ambitious goal to provide two specialized clearance trainings for local deminers thanks to EOD trainers with more than 20-year experience.
The main objective is the strengthening of local capacities, skill transfer and to adapt technical trainings to the specific needs identified in the area. As such, each expatriate function is doubled by a local assistant, who benefits throughout the project from daily mentoring. The activities carried out so far have mainly focused on the governorate of Raqqa, but also episodically in the governorate of Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor, NES’s most contaminated area.
HAMAP’s new project would aim at supporting the local population, internally displaced people and humanitarian organizations by providing three EORE/NTS teams, and two specialized clearance teams in order to clear mines, explosive hazards located in perilous, confined and underwater areas.
The project directly tackles 2 of the 3 strategic objectives to "save lives" and "enhance protection" by preventing explosive hazards ordnance-related risks. Furthermore, Mine Action is one of the main areas of response priorities
within the protection cluster and focuses on at least two aspects of the project:
- Expansion of existing humanitarian mine action capacities and improve the quality of services.
- Delivery of tailored risk education messaging

4 - Needs

People are still at risk of an explosive device accident throughout NES. Towns and rural areas have been exposed to contamination, whether through fighting or voluntary entrapment by armed groups. It is absolutely necessary to continue raising awareness to allow the safe return of IDPs and other humanitarian aid activities are necessary to tend towards decent living conditions for the populations and displaced persons.After the takeover of the governorate by Kurdish forces, a large amount of pyrotechnic pollution and old ISIS stockpiles have already been removed in the city and its surroundings. However, the most inaccessible areas such as destroyed buildings and tunnels are still heavily contaminated, slowing down rehabilitation activities in the city. During its presence in the region, IS had the inhabitants build numerous tunnels under the city of Raqqa and underground galleries in the vicinity of Tabqa to protect themselves from attacks and to move around without being seen. These tunnels have been very little explored and represents a threat for the local population, children and the infrastructures on the surface. HAMAP teams have already identified at least a dozen tunnels previously used by IS.Similarly, irrigation canals, wells and the Euphrates River have not been included in mine action and the need for clearance must now be assessed. HAMAP considers it is necessary to provide highly skilled clearance teams in Raqqah so that humanitarian aid can operate safely.

5 - Implementation of activities

HAMAP conducts NTS which allows data to be collected from the population and potential beneficiaries of suspected dangerous areas. HAMAP carries out demining operations in the the governorate of Ar-Raqqah and episodically in Deir Ez Zor thanks to demining teams. 4 diver deminers will be trained by to diver deminers experts during spring for future underwater clearance in the Euphrates and irrigation canals already identified previously. Similarly, 10 deminers will be trained by 2 EOD experts for special operations in confined and perilous areas including climbing exercises. Those trainings require specific material to be forwarded to NES with the support of HAMAP local office based in Erbil. HAMAP conducts risk awareness workshops with 3 trained EORE teams for the benefit of the population in the city of Raqqah and its periphery. Activities focuses on camps and schools to reach the most vulnerable population. HAMAP offers support to NES humanitarian actors, local and international, facing significant presence of pyrotechnic pollution sharing request forms to be forwarded by each organization for analysis and immediate action. NTS / clearance conducted by HAMAP teams unable them to safely operate their own humanitarian assistance activities. Upon request, HAMAP can provide specific training for the benefit of NGOs beneficiaries and staff related to prevention and risk education.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mine action activities are highly sensitive and require precaution and protection measures for both staff and beneficiaries with regards to hazards risks, community tensions on lands rights and gender-based violence. Vulnerable groups and minorities might not have the same access to clearance and heighten tensions over clearance services: HAMAP is directly offering support to humanitarian actors implementing projects in non-cleared areas with EORE activities for both their beneficiaries and staff as well as NTS / clearance activities upon request. This approach helps to better assess needs based on all stakeholders’ analysis. Familiarization may cause the beneficiaries (civilian population) and deminers from HAMAP staff to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards: Coordinate with local authorities to explain importance of RE/clearance and to ensure access to vulnerable groups, ensure adapted and tailored EORE sessions with the expert team, and organize surveys one month after the session took place. Due to the sensitive nature of mine action activities undertaken, often perceived as a military environment, gender-based violence can be exacerbated and occur under these specific circumstances: female staff and beneficiaries will easily access a complaint mechanism to report any inappropriate behavior and harm. Coordination with specialized agencies will be facilitated to address GBV issues.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HAMAP is committed to ensuring that all personnel related to its work, whether they are its staff, local community, beneficiaries, suppliers and partners are treated with respect, dignity and compassion. We are committed to the Basic Principles on Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse established by the United Nations Secretary-General in 2003. HAMAP has a policy of zero tolerance towards SEA. All HAMAP employees and related personnel are expected to uphold the highest standards of personal and professional conduct at all times, and to provide humanitarian assistance and services in a manner that respects and fosters the rights of beneficiaries and other vulnerable members of the local communities.During the project design phase, HAMAP will maintain an environment that prevents SEA and promotes the inclusion of all employees, beneficiaries and stakeholders. HAMAP’s policy includes a robust recruitment and training courses in the planning process to mitigate the risk of SEA.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Armed Conflict: If active fighting should resume; the daily security monitoring will allow to anticipate and foresee an evacuation/sheltering of every team. Full fuel tank vehicle with driver 24/24 7/7. Anytime shifting of the daily travel routes, curfew at 8 PM and no going out in the evening. 2 armed guards flank the operations, and everyone is equipped with bulletproof vests. Kidnapping: All activities are operated in teams. No isolated staff, each team has a designated driver who can perform an extraction at any moment. Demining operations are overseen by armed guards and living places and workplaces are protected 24/24/ and 7/7. Traveling by foot and alone is forbidden. Health protocol (caution concerning water and infectious diseases): Drinking water tank for a daily use, cook in the Raqqah base prepare the meals once a day for all team returning from an operation. Kit with basic medicines and first aid supplies, under control of the health support team, clinics and hospitals are identified in case of need of care and for serious cases a medical evacuation procedure is set up. The Raqqah workplace base as well as the living places allows quarantine if needed. Sensibilization workshop about safety measures will be given for every team and the material needed (gel / water and soap / masks etc.) will be provided. Health support teams can establish monitoring process / testing et oversighting of the staff. Propensity to natural disasters
