Project Summary

Access to food in Gaza remains a challenge for a majority of refugees struggling with deteriorating socio-economic condition compounded with recurrent hostilities and the COVID-19 emergency. Soaring rates of unemployment and limited income are key drivers to poverty and food insecurity among the population. Through the Cash for Work intervention, UNRWA aims to support Palestine refugees in Gaza coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees. These will be equal to more than 1,430,000 work days, and will inject over US$ 17.6 million into households, generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short term job opportunities will include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions. UNRWA developed a Standard Operation Procedures amidst COVID-19 emergency to ensure that beneficiaries under the Cash for Work programming have access to enhanced occupational health and safety norms for the work they agree to undertake. It identifies the functions and manner in which the Cash for Work modality can be deployed for UNRWA activities safely and in a dignified manner in order to continue providing income generation opportunities to refugees to achieve livelihood objectives.
The project is directly addressing the HRP Strategic Outcome 3: the ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.

1 - Needs

The on-going blockade and intermittent hostilities as well as the intra-Palestinian conflict compounded with COVID-19 emergency are continuously deteriorating all aspects of life in the Gaza Strip. Restricted access to land, water, production inputs and markets constrain productive capacities and limit economic opportunities (HNO 2022). According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 44.7 per cent during the second quarter of 2021. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 64.0 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2021), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 63.8 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2021). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped more than half of them into food insecurity. The poverty rate increased from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017 (SEFSec Survey, 2018), and is expected to have increased to 56.4 per cent as a result of the May hostilities (World Bank, 2021: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/07/06/the-rebuilding-of-gaza-amid-dire-conditions-damage-losses-and-needs). This is despite of 53 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza reported having received the assistance of some type since the beginning of the escalation according to MSNA findings conducted in July 2021, while 94 per cent reported wanting to receive assistance in the future (HNO 2022). People cope with food insecurity mainly through borrowing food and/or money. UNRWA has been experiencing the influx of people desperately seeking for jobs, even for a temporary employment such as Cash for Work opportunities. Currently, an average waiting time for a family to receive such opportunity is more than four years.

2 - Activities

Through its Job Creation Programme (JCP), UNRWA will support Palestine refugees coping with protracted threats and shocks and mitigate food insecurity through offering short-term employment opportunities to approximately 14,000 poor Palestine refugees. It equals to more than 1,430,000 work days generating almost 5,000 full time job equivalents, and injects over US$ 17.6 million into local economy. The full-time job equivalents (FTEs) are determined based on the workdays generated, which means that one FTE may be filled by 4 beneficiaries working 3 months each. The short-term job opportunities would include more than 10,000 unskilled positions, more than 3,800 skilled positions and around 170 professional positions. Examples of unskilled positions include school attendants, guards and food packers at distribution centers; skilled positions include midwives, nurses, accountants and teachers; and professional positions include medical officers and engineers. Priority will be given to applicants from households who have assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$ 3.87 per person per day. As a part of the Agency-wide Gender Mainstreaming Strategy, UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women (1,588 out of 3,970 positions) and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth between 18 to 29 years old (4,000 out of 10,000 positions). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. The salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and CfW unskilled and skilled wage range from NIS 46 (US$ 14) to NIS 66 (US$ 20) per day for 5 workday per week. Also note that although salaries may be indicated in US dollar, beneﬁciaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of ﬂuctuations in the exchange rate. The JCP ﬁeld monitoring team will ensure appropriate placement of refugees, and will monitor work attendance and performance. In order to continue to provide income generation opportunities to refugees to achieve livelihood objectives amidst emergencies including COVID-19, UNRWA developed SOPs identifying the functions and manners in which the Cash for Work opportunities can be deployed in a pandemic environment safely and in a dignified manner. In order to minimize risks during the work placement, the beneficiaries will receive the same work protections (PPE) and training afforded to staff, and beneficiaries with pre-existing medical conditions are not exposed to “front line” functions. Furthermore, beneficiaries are afforded the opportunity to complete their employment opportunities assigned in the event they must quarantine or isolated or due to other factors. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

As part of UNRWA commitment to transparency and accountability of its operations, UNRWA’s Job Creation Programme, as managing function of CfW intervention, continues to improve channels of communication with Palestine refugees and refugee communities through various mechanisms, such as complaint channels in relation to specific services ensuring the monitoring the quality of services provided, and dedicated help lines to answer questions, and through UNRWA’s Area offices available in Gaza city, North and Middle areas, Khan Younis and Rafah.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations throughout the Gaza Strip, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms. The feedback may be directly addressed to hosting programmes and departments for positions within UNRWA, or the beneficiaries may address their feedback to the monitoring team during their visit.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations with a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestine refugees' rights. An integrated humanitarian response, with the inclusion of Cash for Work, is directly related to a growing focus on resilience to withstand and recover from crisis. The cash support reduces current risks by providing relief to strained families and enhancing refugees’ sense of dignity and self-reliance. Gauging the impact of such very-short temporary assistance is impossible; especially in a volatile and chronic-emergency Gaza context; however, without such cash assistance, the vulnerability of poor families will be worsened and hence this entire project will contribute – among other factors and interventions- towards “resilience.”The salary provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneﬁciaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of ﬂuctuations in the exchange rate. UNRWA aims to meet gender mainstreaming targets through ensuring gender equity in providing jobs by developing and implementing projects and initiatives that are appropriate and sensitive to local culture to absorb the large number of female JCP applicants, in course of conducting needs assessment to identify opportunities that were both aligned with actual needs and viewed favorable by prospective female employees.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all Palestine refugees who are seeking for assistance. However, in terms of the Cash for Work opportunities, the priority will be given to applicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$3.87 per person per day, considered as food insecure. Targeting criteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). Offering CfW opportunities to unskilled women in Gaza remains one of the main challenges for this intervention, mainly due to cultural constraints. To overcome these challenges, some of the available CfW opportunities specifically targeted women. Moreover, new job opportunities were created to help absorb more vulnerable unskilled women in other working fields, such as school attendants, guards, cleaners, and food packers at UNRWA distribution centres. According to PCBS report (Labor Force Survey, Q2-2021), while the unemployment rate of women in Gaza remains high (64.0 per cent), the unemployment rate of refugee women (61.6 per cent) is lower than non-refugee women (69.6 per cent). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. With the limited prospects of employment opportunities in Gaza, the Agency through its Job Creation Programme, managing the Cash for Work, addresses these economic hardships and ensures that families are self-reliant. The provision of CfW opportunities are expected to allow families to cover some of their basic expenses, to improve living conditions and contribute to their copying mechanisms and resilience.
