Project Summary

CAID propose to take a holistic “One School” approach to building safer schools and improving educational outcomes for students in Deir-Ez-Zor.
Practical rehabilitation of school structures will be accompanied by targeted support and capacity building of teachers and psycho-social programming for students. This will improve reintegration for returnees and IDPs and build social cohesion. The needs of students and teachers are interlinked and have been significantly altered post conflict. Our multifaceted approach focuses not only on facilities, teachers, and students, but also on the wider community and the important role they play in school safety and educational attainments. We will not only ensure that schools are structurally safe and accessible to the current needs of students but will also support emotional wellbeing and ensure that school communities have safe spaces to address trauma. We will also upskill teachers to improve their ability to build positive classroom cultures.
This project will be aligned with the HRP 2022 - 2023 and will coordinate with the Education cluster, providing updated reporting and coordination with all actors. The rehabilitation works will operate during school holidays to ensure minimum disruption to education in line with do-no-harm principles.
Improving educational outcomes:
In implementing this programme, practical rehabilitation of school structures will be accompanied by psycho-social programming for students and targeted support and capacity building of teachers. To achieve positive impact, we will implement across three workstreams:
• Workstream 1: Reconstruction and Rehabilitation: We will target 115 schools for this program. Through our previous rehabilitation work conducted in previous years we have identified needs of these schools. The 115 schools are in 16 communities in Hajin (communities: Abu Hamam, Abu Hasan, Bahra, Gharanij, Hajin, Kishmah); Susat (Al Baguz, Shafa, Upper Baguz); Thiban (Abu Hardoub, Darnaj, Eastern Jarda, Shinan, Sweidan Jazira, Tayyana). Through further community consultation and coordination with the EWG, we will ensure that the programme is aligned to the current needs of the students and teachers in those schools, including improved disability access. There will be around 285,000 beneficiaries (students, teachers, parents and school staff) across the project 65,000 students and teachers in each year. The beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age and disability status. Teachers needs will be incorporated into the activity design.
• Workstream 2: Psychosocial Support: We will implement the I DEAL psychosocial support (PSS) programme, a Syria context-specific training for children designed for public use by War Child to both children and parents together to aid recovery from traumatic experiences from armed conflict and displacement. There will be scheduled monthly talks in schools on mental health, and base PSS social workers in schools and support teachers, with a roving PSS team to target all the targeted villages. Protection measures will ensure that schools are always functioning as safe spaces for young people within their communities. The beneficiaries for this workstream will be 60,000 students in each year. A reintegration program improving returnee children's access to formal education in Baguz will also be implemented in parallel partnering with 8 schools in Baguz.
• Workstream 3: Teacher Training: To make schools fit for purpose again, we will support teachers with training and mentorship to empower them with tools to do their jobs effectively with appropriate teaching materials. The beneficiaries for this workstream will be 5,000 teachers in each year. This project will ultimately contribute towards improved safety, quality, and performance of primary and secondary education across the target communities. It will support school rehabilitation, teacher training and the provision of learning materials and PSS for students.

4 - Needs

A REACH survey carried out in June 2021 in 1144 communities throughout north-east Syria found that the most common barrier to education was a suitable school environment. The lack of suitable school infrastructure and disruption caused by the war has meant there are a shortage of schools that can operate which leads to extreme overcrowding. Teaching and school administration capacity remains overstretched and school personnel are overburdened and under resourced. Children with disabilities are disproportionately underserved in NES and the closure of special needs schools means that ill-equipped mainstream schools are often the only viable alternatives. The psycho-social scars of war also manifest themselves in varying ways among children who have experienced extraordinary trauma throughout their lives, and many struggle to adjust to the school setting. The HNO 2021 also identifies that teachers face diverse and complex learning needs in classrooms overcrowded with children of different ages and varied levels of education attainment without adequate remuneration or essential capacity building.A lack of adequate schools and resources and high levels of poverty throughout NES have made school an unattainable prospect for many families. Education services are often absent and families struggling to survive often resort to harmful coping strategies such as early marriage and child labour. Teenage boys are vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of community tensions. Mitigating measures: The program will be open to all population groups among both teachers and children that suffer from severe trauma. We have established and developed relationships with education committees in the targeted schools and will liaise with other actors, the Education WG, and education councils to ensure that the most vulnerable children attend the PSS program. To safeguard the beneficiaries and further reduce community tensions, there will also be no branding of the organisations implementing the works at the school. We will also ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed which will include local education actors in the assistance too; 2. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to locations of services. Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse among staff towards beneficiaries; GBV and child protection Measures taken to mitigate this risk - CAID Best Practice Guidelines agreed by all stakeholders; Clear anti-fraud, corruption, abuse and harassment; bribery andcorruption clauses included in contractual commitments and policies; ; Ensured stakeholders knew where to report incidents of harassment, abuse, fraud, bribery and corruption on the whistleblowing hotline by distributing cards with numbers.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project has a Community Response Mechanism (CRM) where participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about the school and project activities and staff and systems via email, whatsapp messaging, telephone and designated complaints boxes open to caregivers and children. Continually over the two year project, the facilitators and advocacy staff will be responsible for disseminating CRM cards to beneficiaries and their caregivers and give clear guidelines on why and how all feedback should be reported. CAID and Khotoat's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are in line with the principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Khotoat safeguarding and child protection codes of conduct are vetted by CAID HQ DD team. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate risk analysis and mitigation measures identified together with the partner which includes the promotion of female staff at the school to ensure a space of safety. PSEA focal points will be trained by specialist staff on safeguarding and PSEA measures and appointed to the schools who will engage with the wider PSEA network in NES in liaison with the PWG. They will be responsible for enhancing prevention strategies. Khotoat staff will be trained on checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in activities. Further, all personnel will adhere to and sign the CAID PSEA code of conduct prior to contracting.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of Injury during rehab works (ERW, Hazardous Materials) Mitigation - Site safety assessment completed by engineer; Inspection reports to be completed prior to undertaking work; No works undertaken unless signed off by CAID technical expert; No works undertaken at sites with suspicion of severe ERW threats; Clear SOPs for dealing with ERW discovery; Clear SOPs for managing hazardous materials.Risk of injury on site, safeguarding, injury from debris fill sites (public risk) Mitigation measures - Site cordon established prior to commencing work; Concentrate work on periods of school holidays; Site condition report submitted for areas.Risk of increased insecurity in the programme delivery areas. Mitigating measures: Operations will not focus on areas with resurgence of unrest; Contingency planning in the event of a deteriorating security situation; Security manager recruited to support operational field team; Ongoing, proactive monitoring of security and conflict status. Risk of operational delays (Weather, extended curfew, security checks) Mitigating measures: Selection of contingency sites to offset access or operational constraints; Ongoing contingency planning and operational reviews; Scrutiny of operational scheduling by project manager; Early reporting of potential or actual delays; Evaluation of quality of available alternative PPE/machinery, and equipment prior to contracting.
