Project Summary

7.7 million Zimbabweans are facing acute food insecurity as a result of drought and are in urgent need of assistance to save lives and protect livelihoods. In addition to vulnerabilities induced by drought and economic factors, the COVID-19 pandemic is affecting livelihoods systems for the most vulnerable including migrants, host communities and communities of origin in unprecedented ways. Since the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic in March 2020, a total of 24,024 Zimbabwean migrant workers have been forcibly returned to the country. This sudden influx of migrant returnees in communities of origin in Matabeleland South province is creating additional food insecurity and livelihoods burden on already food insecure and vulnerable communities and has the potential to lead to further displacement or outward migration. Most districts in Matabeleland South province experienced severe drought conditions in the 2019-2020 season, adversely impacting on cereal harvest, with most households having not more than three months food stocks available. Matabeleland South also experienced the highest number of cattle deaths as a result of the drought. Remittance inflows from relatives abroad also significantly decreased by more than 50% for a majority of households, coupled with COVID-19 travel restrictions and border closures, most households were left unable to receive food support from outside the country, further worsening their food insecurity. All the target districts for this intervention (Beitbridge, Bulilima, Mangwe and Gwanda) have been classified in IPC phase 3 requiring urgent food assistance and livelihoods support.
The International Organization for Migration (IOM) in Zimbabwe through this project seeks to urgently mitigate the deteriorating food security and livelihoods situation of returned migrants and their communities of origin through provision of agricultural inputs support and livestock rearing support in a way that builds their resilience to future shocks, prevents and stem negative coping strategies including irregular migration.

1 - Activities

1. Provide drought-tolerant seeds for cereals (maize,small grains and legumes) as well as fertilisers ( and related training) in four high migrant sending districts of Beitbridge, Bulilima, Mangwe and Gwanda – The intervention will seek to provide input seed and fertilizers to 2000 returned migrants households and 3000 households in high migrant communities of origin in the target districts. Each beneficiary household will receive an agricultural input package consisting of 1x 10kgs bag of maize seed, 1x50kg bag of Compound D fertilizer and 1x 50kg bag of Ammonium Nitrate (AN) fertilizer.2. Provide small livestock (restocking, improved breeds) and other inputs materials (and related trainings) to 2000 returned migrant and their households in Beitbridge, Bulilima, Mangwe and Gwanda districts. IOM will provide start up small livestock (boer goats) to returned migrants and their households as a measures of creating households assest to mitigate livestock lost to drought, improve household nutrition diversity and counter the push for negative irregular migration. Each beneficiary household will receive 2x female goats and have access to 1 buck. Beneficiaries will be grouped in a manner that 13 households will be able to share 1 buck. All the goats provided will be between the ages of 7 to 10 months old. Beneficiary identification and selection will be done in an open and transparent manner through community based planning consultative processes involving the participation of community leaders and the whole community. In order to foster long term multiplier effects on the who community, the beneficiary households will be expected to pass on the goats to other households once the goats six months after the goats have given birth. This will have a cumulative effect of increasing the number of ultimate beneficiaries while building the number of goats at the community level.3. Support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management in Bulilima, Mangwe, Gwanda and Beitbridge districts - In order to avert livestock deaths due to disease. IOM will collaborate with the Department of Veterinary services in the procurement and provision of livestock vaccines for the target communities. IOM will additionally train beneficiaries and at least 2 Community Animal Health Workers on livestock disease surveillance and management . The Community Animal Health Workers will be given the responsibility of looking after the community bucks that wil roam around with the flocks . All the goats will be vaccinated against Pulpy Kidney and dosed as per veterinary department’s expert advice.4. Provide training, extension and advisory services to extension officers and farmers focusing on good agriculture practices with emphasis on climate-smart agriculture. IOM will provide support to extension and advisory services in the four target districts on good agricultural practices with an emphasis on smart agriculture.5. Provide agriculture cereal seed and fertilizer inputs to 200 households while working on rehabilitation of six dip tanks in Bulilima and Mangwe districts. Each of the beneficiary household will receive 1x10kgs maize seed, 1x50 kgs Compoud D fertilizer and 1x50kgs Ammonium Nitrate Fertilizer. 6. Facilitate the rehabilitation of 6 dip tanks in Bulilima and Mangwe districts.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project will through the Community based planning consultative approach include sessions on project risk analysis and mitigation at the community level. The Community Based Planning approach has at its heart inclusive participation and empowerment of beneficiary communities. The Community based planning process is a participatory approach, implemented in partnership with local government, that ensures the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities and aspirations of the whole community inclusive of returned migrants , are incorporated into ward based development and recovery plans. Through this mechanism the project stakeholders including beneficiaries will jointly participate in identifying potential risks that may militate against successful implementation of the project. Through the CBP consultative forum stakeholders at the community level will identify risk mitigation strategies and activities for implementation. In this respect using this approach will ensure that project beneficiaries are not passive recipients of assistance but are actively engaged and included in making key project decisions. The proposed project will thus encourage meaningful access to assistance and participation and empowerment of people. The approach therefore integrates protection for the most vulnerable groups within communities and ensures that their voice is heard and that they benefit from development and recovery interventions implemented in their communities. To ensure greater accountability and feedback from the beneficiary population, IOM will ensure establishment of community level project monitoring teams at the project site level to ensure inclusive participation of beneficiaries in monitoring and evaluating progress on project implementation. Additionally, IOM has established a tollfree hotline to facilitate submission of beneficiary complaints and provision of feedback. IOM will promote use of this toll free line through posting information on the toll free line in public spaces in the target districts. IOM will also periodically conduct community feedback meetings at which project beneficiaries and the community at large will be given the opportunity to raise their concerns and be provided with feedback on the project.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

IOM recognizes that the adverse effects of climate change and environmental degradation are among negative drivers of irregular migration in the project target districts. IOM has been engaging in research, policy and operational activities in the area of migration, environment and climate change since the 1990s, in support of national, regional and international efforts to address human mobility challenges associated with environmental factors and climate change. Through this project IOM will minimize the negative impacts that the project activities may have on the environment by ensuring that no trees are cut and that sustainable agricultural tillage practices are used which do not lead to unnecessary soil erosion and siltation of water bodies. IOM will ensure environmentally sustainable use of pesticides and livestock dipping chemicals used during the project activities. Furthermore, the project will promote food security and enhance the resilience of target communities while contributing towards preserving the environment and preventing forced migration that results from environmental factors. The project will also provide assistance and protection to returned migrants in target districts who have been forced, among other factors, to engaged in forced migration due to environmental and climate change factors.
