Project Summary

The project's purpose is to reduce malnutrition effect in Dhusamareb and Adaado district. Project activities will include; 1. Training of health facility and community workers on appropriate IYCF practices to provide support and counselling; 2. Facility and community based counselling for and awareness raising of breastfeeding and complementary feeding to women of child bearing age; 3. Screen all under-fives and PLMs visiting the health facilities for services for 12 sessions with 1 per month; 4. Provide lifesaving nutrition services for children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women with severe acute malnutrition at Dhusamareb Hospital; 5. Admit and treat severely malnourished children with complications at stabilization centre in Dhusamareb; 6. Training of health facility and community workers on IMAM guidelines; 7. Community mobilization and awareness sessions on IMAM programming; 8. Facility based and community based screening for acute malnutrition; 9. Training of Health workers on Nutrition information system (documentation, reporting and analysing data for decision making); 10. Establish community nutrition surveillance system. The project target will include; IDPs - 5000, Host community - 3000, Female - 5000, Male - 3000, Children - 5000 and Adult - 3000. The project will ensure that the nutrition needs of both men and women, girls and boys are taken into consideration during implementation. Children under 5 will also be given special attention to avert the severe acute malnutrition.

Mitigation Measures

The most vulnerable group to malnutrition are IDPs due to their vulnerability to food insecurity and inadequate health provision. They risk being profiled by the host community due to attention being given to them as opposed to the host community members. To avoid any form of exclusion in the project, TUOS will employ a Do-No-Harm approach principle and help the community to select project committees which will comprise of all the target group representatives and make the whole process as community driven. Beneficiaries targeting and selection committee will be formed from each group, that is host community and IDPs. A selection criteria will be agreed upon before the project starts and discussed upon with the donor for approval. Emphasis will be given to the most vulnerable individuals and households such as IDPs regardless of their age, gender and clan. TUOS will conduct due diligence of the list provided by the selection committee to ascertain the inclusiveness of the target beneficiaries. Periodic monitoring and evaluation exercise will be conducted with the aim of checking the changing dynamics in the target area and need for changes in the project implementation. Nutrition interventions may fail to reach the most vulnerable and, in some cases, may actually lead to further harm especially among the women and children. The risk of exclusion based on gender stereo type and age is prevalent in the community. The project targets pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months as well as vulnerable men and boys with malnutrition conditions. The selection committee will be trained on the selection criteria as such to promote inclusion of all gender and age group in the target community. Pregnant and lactating women and children under five years of age have special nutritional requirements and suffer severe consequences from undernutrition. Experience has shown, however, that other groups need to be involved in nutrition programmes as well to ensure that assistance for women and children is effective. Male family members have a strong influence on the feeding practices and eating habits of mothers and children. They also influence the healthcare practices and beliefs of mothers and other caregivers. The effectiveness of nutrition programmes can therefore depend on the active involvement of these groups. TUOS will collect sex- and age-disaggregated data on nutrition programme coverage, including: percentage of girls and boys aged 6-59 months treated for SAM; percentage of girls and boys under five, PLW in the target group covered by supplementary feeding programmes and treatment for moderate acute malnutrition; percentage of women, girls, boys and men who are still unable to meet nutritional requirements in spite of ongoing programming; exclusive breastfeeding rates for girls and boys; timely introduction of complementary food by age and sex and percentage of men and women benefitting from nutritional behaviour change interventions. Women and children are the most vulnerable group during clan conflict and violence in the target area. To mitigate the effect of being adversely affected by the conflict when it occurs, TUOS will be working with community security team to protect women and children from attack. Also nutrition services will be provided at the community level or IDP centers thus avoiding the need to travel for such services. Also in times of conflict, TUOS will set up temporary hold up warehouse in the community to avoid supplies cut off and sustain the nutritional services to the beneficiaries. TUOS will also be working with community elders in the target areas such that the community elders themselves will be negotiating among the warring groups to allow services to continue if any of the target areas will be affected.
