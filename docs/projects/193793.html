1 - Needs

Close to 1.9 million Palestinians across the oPt experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and displacement, including forcible transfer. Through this project, the Protection Cluster will coordinate responses aimed at addressing the following specific protection risks that arise in the context of the protracted protection crisis in the oPt:1. DETERIORATION IN PHYSICAL WELL-BEING, DEPRIVATION OF LIBERTY AND ACCESS RESTRICTIONSIn 2022, the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, continued to witness an increase in the use of force by Israeli Forces, resulting in an alarming increase in Palestinian injuries and fatalities, including children. Settler violence is similarly on the rise, with numerous settler attacks occurring in the presence of IF or even with their active support. Impunity remains a central concern across the oPt, including for possible violations of IHL in the context of hostilities in Gaza. Also of concern are the suspected arbitrary arrests, detention, and ill treatment, possibly amounting to torture, of Palestinians by the Gaza de facto authorities, who are held sometimes for extended periods of time without charge or conviction or based on vague charges. Israel as occupying power continues to violate its responsibility to ensure basic needs, including through the enforcement of its ongoing military blockade – which may amount to collective punishment. 2. INTERNAL DISPLACEMENT AND RISK OF FORCIBLE TRANSFERHouseholds in Area C, East Jerusalem and the H2 area of Hebron city continue to face a coercive environment, placing Palestinian individuals and communities at risk of forcible transfer due to a range of longstanding Israeli policies and practices. A number of significant legal and administrative developments in the West Bank, including efforts affecting the settlement of land title in both Area C and East Jerusalem, new building permit and access regimes in Area C and the Seam Zone respectively, as well as the application of policies that have led to a sharp rise in so called “self-demolitions” in East Jerusalem, and an increase in seizure and confiscation of structures and material goods in Area C. These developments are expected to further intensify the coercive environment in 2023, hinder the delivery of humanitarian assistance, and result in greater humanitarian suffering. In Gaza, since 2008 and following subsequent escalations until August 2022 more than 17,000 housing units were destroyed and 20,000 were damaged leaving a third of Palestinians displaced many of whom were already registered refugees. Related needs, include a significant increase in demand for legal aid support for affected households, including for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to issues of denial of resources, custody of children and the loss of identity documents due to destruction of houses or while taking refuge.3. PSYCHOLOGICAL DISTRESS AMONGST CHILDREN AND VULNERABLE GROUPS Child protection needs in the oPt have been exacerbated by the increase in conflict-related violence, particularly since April 2021 continuing through 2022, and the continuing effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2021, 2,934 grave violations were verified in Israel and the State of Palestine . Direct acts and threats of violence, including killing and injuring of children, as well as arbitrary arrest and unlawful detention, and other impacts of the coercive environment on of children all have a negative impact on the mental wellbeing in particular amongst children,. This which can lead to increased school dropout and child labour, reinforcing other child protection threats and leading to further deterioration of mental well-being. Key forms of conflict-related violence endangering children’s rights and contributing to needs include: Across the oPt children experience high levels of violence at home, with 90% of children facing some form of violent discipline at home (92.3% boys and 97.9% girls). It is estimated that three per cent of children aged 10 to 17 are engaged in child labour. 4. Gender-Based Violence It is estimated that 1.6m people will need GBV protection assistance in 2023, with the highest number of identified people in Gaza. Women and girls face widespread and varied forms of GBV, including intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, early/forced marriage, incest, denial of resources, psychological abuse and risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. These risks are further exacerbated by conflict, and exist in a context where GBV services are limited and access restricted due to limitation on movement by the occupation. Socio-cultural patriarchal norms increase the risk of GBV, and contribute to the disproportionate impact of these contextual factors on women and girls. Women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women, internally displaced women and women and girls living in refugee camps are at particular risk of violence.5. EXPLOSIVE REMNANTS OF WARThe May 2021 and August 2022 escalations have left Gaza severely contaminated with explosive remnants of war (ERW), explosive ordinance. A year after the escalation, out of 16 deep buried bombs (DBB) that were located in Gaza in 2021, 10 were cleared and 6 DBB/ unexploded ordnances are still buried in the rubble of damaged buildings or in farmland posing immediate risks to PaPalestinians, hindering safe movement, humanitarian access and access to basic services. Men are more vulnerable to life loss and injuries due to their larger engagement in the public sphere, including participation in the provision of first response services, construction work and scrap metal collection. Concurrently, children are particularly vulnerable due to their curious nature and tendency to explore their environments; since May 2021 escalation, 77% of the reported ERW victims were children.

2 - Activities

Given the large membership of the cluster and the broad scope of the issues coordinatedunder the cluster, sufficient human resources are essential to ensure leadership andcoordination, as detailed in the project budget. In line with below-outlined activities, andworking with the chairs of the Protection Cluster sub-groups and with the Clustermembers, the coordination team will: 1. Provide vision and leadership for the ProtectionCluster, based on consultations with members and stakeholders; 2. Undertake day-to-daycoordination of Protection responses, including convening meetings, information sharingand ensuring Cluster inputs to humanitarian processes; 3. Co-Chair the Legal Task Forcein the West Bank, 4. Ensure coordination with the work of the PC sub-groups and AoRs.Related activities include ensuring coordination between working groups to ensurecomplementarity 5. Identify and propose action to address gaps, including in informationgathering, management, analysis and coordinated response to priority protectionconcerns, with a focus on increased analysis on gender-specific vulnerabilities andgender-specific impact of ongoing violations of IHL and IHRL, and persons with disability;6. Strengthen detection and referral for protection cases; 7. Strengthen ProtectionInformation Management under the protection cluster and feed into inter-sectoralresponse monitoring including 4Ws, demolitions and settler violence response trackers; 8.Coordinate joint protection monitoring and information analysis; 9. Coordinate jointhuman-rights based advocacy for affected populations under relevant priorities of theHCT Advocacy Strategy, including through liaising with UN human rights procedures andmechanisms, and link Protection Cluster advocacy to the work of the HCT AdvocacyWorking Group; 9. Through the Legal Advice Group, coordinate the discussion ofquestions of international human rights and humanitarian law, and the provision ofinternational law analysis to the HCT; 10. Through the Legal Task Force, which OHCHRwill co-chair with NRC, ensure that up-to-date information, legal advice, and analysis onemblematic cases, trends and protection risks is available to the ICCG and the HCT; 10.Work with the other clusters and sectors to mainstream protection, in order to ensure thatAccountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments are fulfilled and thatprotection is made central to the humanitarian response in all stages of the HPC.Activities in this regard include trainings and technical support to the provision ofguidelines, checklists, and coordination of operational responses to protection issues; 11.Build capacity of cluster members to strengthen communication with and accountabilityto affected populations (AAP), including through beneficiary feedback on protectionresponses, and specifically, by establishing a tracking tool to monitor progress on AAPthroughout the Humanitarian Program Cycle for all funded HRP projects under Protection;12. Facilitate regular support to Protection Cluster partners on PSEA and related capacity building, monitor compliance with minimum standards on PSEA 13. Needs assessments: work to identify baseline data and needs of persons with disabilities in the context of humanitarian action; support the efforts of working groups to address assessment gaps as identified in the HNO; 14. Under the lead of the ProtectionCluster gender focal point, provide support to cluster partners in gender mainstreaming alltheir activities, including by providing capacity-building support to partners to enhancegender sensitivity in the planning, implementation and monitoring of projects submitted tothe HRP; 13. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners; 15. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners; 16. Prepare Protection Cluster submissions to the 2023 HPC, including consultations with partners and AoRs on needs, priorities, development of new intervention areas and HRP project vetting; 17. Update West Bank and Gaza contingency plans for the Protection Cluster, and feed into Inter-Agency contingency plans and emergency preparedness as required.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Protection Cluster is continuously engaged with Cluster Partners and the broader humanitarian response through the ICCG and the HCT, on ensuring the principle of Do No Harm remains central to all project planning and interventions in the oPt. HRP and HF project submissions are carefully vetted to ensure adequate risk analyses have taken place, and regularly provides support to other Clusters, primarily through its engagement with Protection Mainstreaming Focal Points, to ensure the tools and capacity are in place for all sectors to adequately address Do No Harm considerations.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Equal and impartial access to aid is highly prioritised in the vetting and assessment of HRP and HF projects submitted to the Protection Cluster. The Protection Cluster will hold regular technical support sessions on accessibility, and has partnered with Disabled People's Organisations (DPOs) to ensure adequate representation and technical expertise is available within cluster discussions, in project vetting and for general support of cluster partners. In 2022, the Protection cluster will prioritise initiatives aimed at addressing current assessment and information gaps particularly with regard to persons with disabilities in the oPt, including on their specific and compounded needs, to complement information gathered in 2021 through the MSNA. In addition, Protection Cluster team members will partake in capacity building initiatives, primarily facilitated through the Global Protection Cluster, to ensure team staff have the technical capacity to continue to provide adequate support to cluster partners.
