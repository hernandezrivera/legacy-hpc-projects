Project Summary

This project aims at providing timely and sustainable, equitable access to safe and suﬃcient water to vulnerable and affected populations. This aim will be obtained through integrated WASH in nutrition, WASH in health and WASH-focused gender-based violence (GBV) mitigation activities. Target groups are IDP’s, host community and the returnees, based on the needs. The total number of beneficiaries targeted is 250,000 individuals.
Interventions will include:
(1) Safe water provision through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children;
(2) Provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) supported at the institutional level and in IDP sites. At the household (HH) level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged, with GBV prevention aspect taken in the consideration;
(3) Hygiene promotion (hand-washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain and jerry can cleaning campaigns);
(4) Capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs);
(5) Distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR etc.) for the most vulnerable people in the community;
(6) Providing beneficiaries with solar lamps to mitigate GBV threats related to WASH.
Static and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EP&R) interventions will include a detailed multisectoral needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies of the affected population. The project design aims to integrate with other sectors for a holistic response, including Education, FSL, Protection and Shelter NFI with cash-based modality, focusing on shelter, skills training, and resilience building. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to the affected population, ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different age groups, people with disabilities, etc. Cash modality will be considered where feasible.
Activities will be closely planned with protection (GBV) and health/nutrition partners so that the water supply, hygiene and sanitation promotion activities are implemented to mitigate WASH-related GBV and malnutrition in the affected community. PAH is applying for Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 in five sectors i.e. WASH and Shelter NFI, Education, FSL and Protection targeting the same locations (where possible) to support the community with the integrated multisector approach. For the mobile responses, PAH will coordinate at the national level with relevant actors. PAH participates in the UN OCHA humanitarian coordination to obtain information about humanitarian needs and access issues. Immediate information sharing is done from a Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) and an Inter-Cluster Working Group (ICWG) supported by EP&R focal point dedicated by the partners for quick decision-making on EP&R team’s deployment. The ongoing coordination with EP&R with support from WASH cluster will be of high value for sharing and receiving the updates. On the ground, activities will be closely planned with nutrition, protection and education partners so that the water supply, hygiene and sanitation promotion activities are implemented to support nutrition centres (and health centres where possible) and mitigate the risks of GBV in community and institutions. Activities in schools will be implemented through the establishment of School Hygiene Clubs and improvement of sanitation facilities like latrines, hand washing facilities to provide adequate privacy and mitigate the risks of GBV.

1 - Needs

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of the country cause displacement of community and the economic downturn which has put a further strain on already limited WASH services. Based on PAH data collected in 2019 some 90 per cent of the population do not have access to improved sanitation. Half of the population does not have access to improved water sources. According to "WASH cluster 2020 humanitarian needs overview" sectoral analysis, it an estimated 5,243,000 million South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services in 2020. These people include IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees who are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. In 2019, 38 per cent of households reported access to an improved water source in less than 30 minutes distance, without facing protection concerns. The remaining 62% of the population across the country either relies on unimproved or surface water sources (35%), takes more than 30 minutes to reach the improved sources (24%), or is able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but faces protection concerns while accessing the source (3%). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Greater Upper Nile - 59%. Access to sanitation remained low, with 15% of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound and only 13% of households reported ownership of three key WASH items - buckets/jerrycans, soap, mosquito nets. Findings from needs assessments conducted by PAH and other partners shows that, in most parts of the country, people are faced with lack of access to safe adequate water, poor hygiene practices and sanitation conditions, food insecurity making people prone to morbidity and mortality resulting from waterborne diseases and malnutrition, especially children under five. Recent needs assessments in Terkeka Central Equatoria state on 11 to 19 September, shows a high hunger rate in the region, related to ongoing insecurity and erratic rainfalls in 2018, with many children and women, reported malnourished. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate in Tali was found to be 17.3% of the children aged 6-59 months screened by Nutrition team. This rate is above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Other locations like Yirol East of former Lakes State has an estimated 10,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). According to the WASH 2020, HRP severity mapping, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition for children between 6-59 months and households affected by relevant health issues like AWD, ARI eye infections is highly prevalent in most parts of the country like Pibor with 84%, Yirol East 80%, Kapoeta 81%, Aweil East with 84%, among others. Taking into consideration the severe floods in the majority of the country in the second half of 2019, which results are difficult to be foreseen yet, the scope and size of WASH needs will raise significantly.Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water, using communal latrines and accessing menstrual hygiene products. WASH-related GBV is more prevalent in Western Bar el Gazal, Raga county, Eastern Equatoria in Ikotos and Budi, Ulang Fashoda and Maiwut in Upper Nile, among other locations. Inappropriate and dignified washing locations remain as critical needs. WASH needs are also high among IDPs in protracted camps and non-camp settings including returnees. While host communities are also in need of WASH items and facilities, with an increasing proportion of people returning to counties of origin resulting from the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, the already limited water sources may become overstretched as the demand increases. These key findings show that WASH infrastructure coverage alone, is weak in South Sudan, however, the impacts of poor WASH coverage is measurable through a multi-sectoral view.

2 - Activities

1) Conducting WASH assessments with in-depth protection analysis using risk equation (with key focus on WASH, Nutrition, Health and Protection). PAH will cooperate with REACH, who provide platform for gap analysis and needs across the country and this will support rapid decision making. MOU developed by EP&R partners will ensure more coordinated, multisectoral assessment will be done with partners specializing in various sectors. 2) Baseline survey will be carried out by PAH MEAL team to establish the situation prior to the intervention to provide basis for evaluating the achievements of the intervention.3) Procurement and pre-positioning of project supplies such as borehole spares, latrine slabs, digging tools and soap. GBV related WASH NFIs such as torches and dignity kits will be procured for GBV mitigation. Depending on availability, items will be procured from the local markets in the project locations. 4) Rehabilitation/repair and development of new water points. Rehabilitation will focused on the water points that are most beneficial to the community in terms of access. This will be consulted with women in FGD. Consideration will also be given to water points at institutions – such as health/nutrition sites and schools. Locally recruited pump mechanics will be trained and water quality analysis will be conducted. PAH will focus on the inclusion of women in the mechanical training, with the target of at least 30%. In order to ensure the proper maintenance of water points, water user committee will be established and 60% of the committee members will be women. 5) Provision of solar lamps for HH to reduce GBV risks related to WASH. Provision of solar lamps, dignity kits and soap will be targeting HH most at risk of GBV. Targeting will be done jointly with GBV partners and may include GBV survivors. Targeted beneficiaries will include IDPs, mothers to SAM screened children, among others will be provided with vouchers for soap which will cater for three months’ supply of soap. Where possible, depending on the development of local market, the CBI modality will be implemented. That may strengthen the local vendors potential but also encourage them to provide new items on the regular basis 6) Distribution of WASH NFIs.PAH will rely on core pipeline and CBI for the items to be distributed. The targeting criteria will be defined and eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries will be used. Information on entitlement will be disseminated before and during the distribution. Meaningful access will be ensured to enable people reaching the location of the service, receiving and benefit from the service. Separate queues will be set up for elders, pregnant women, other. Carriers will be considered for people with limited mobility. Pictograms of the entitlements will be available for children, illiterate people and people with impaired hearing.Safety audits will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries and during distribution. Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to attend to complaints of beneficiaries. 7) Protection training and awareness raising will be provided to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics water user committee, women groups and general community leaders. PAH will work closely with protection partners on ground. 8) Construction/improving sanitation facilities at institutions by fixing the structures with proper doors and inside locks to enable privacy and mitigate risk of GBV. 9) Community hygiene promotion activities through house to house visits, sessions, campaigns will be carried out at community and institutions like schools, health and nutrition centers. 10) Monitoring activities including protection-sensitive project monitoring by PAH MEAL and program management unit to ensures imbalances are regularly checked and corrected early. Post Intervention Monitoring will also be carried out.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. PAH has high standards of conducting assessments and distributions, always concerning rights of beneficiaries and in full accountability. Before the activities are conducted, the beneficiaries are mobilized and made aware of the project activities, of their rights and selection criteria, what they will get and what is not in the project. Local authorities, chiefs and the entire community are enlightened of the selection criteria and how the registration is done. Beneficiaries will be asked to sign on the forms as a confirmation for receiving assistance. Distributions and other activities are conducted with high sensitivity to the special needs of women, children, elderly and disabled. PAH put strong attention to cultural norms and local law, acts always within the law of South Sudan, while humanitarian standards and international human rights are our highest guidepost. "Do no harm" principle is fully respected. All beneficiaries and affected population are informed about compliant mechanisms, there are multiple ways of asking questions, introducing queries and complaints, which are always considered and answered by our staff. Personal data of beneficiaries are protected according to European standards (GDPR). PAH professional Monitoring Team pays special attention during post-intervention monitoring to check the respect of rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. Vulnerable internally displaced people are the primary beneficiaries of the PAH project. PAH will involve affected people right from the assessment in the process of needs identification and activities will be designed to ensure meaningful access to services provided based on the needs of the displaced people. To ensure that services are provided to the targeted beneficiaries, PAH will also use people from within the affected people to deliver assistance and implement the project activities, for example, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, enumerators among others will also be selected from among the IDPs. Locations for WASH facilities and distribution sites will be decided together with the affected people to ensure meaningful access. In locations where IDPs are settled with the host community, IDPs PAH activities will target both IDPs and vulnerable host community whose resources are stretched with the influx of internally displaced persons for example where there is a need for WASH NFI distributions, vulnerable host communities will also be targeted. Water sources will be repaired/constructed to benefit both IDPs and the host community WASH activities in institutions will target both IDPs and host community users.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. PAH will work in close relationship with local communities focusing on traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level with local leaders and community leaders like the Boma chiefs, Payam administrators among others ensuring proper information sharing. 6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. This project aim at provision of timely lifesaving WASH integrated responses with nutrition, health and gender-based violence mitigation to the most vulnerable communities affected by conflict, disease outbreak, in areas of high malnutrition. Integrated response will reinforce protection and community capacity building to increase coping mechanism which contributes to the national cluster objectives of reducing morbidity, mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in severity levels through response that will mitigate the risks of GBV, decrease the prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children and decrease proportion of population facing IPC 3, 4 and 5 levels. Project activities will be aligned to cluster objectives of providing timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees and, mitigating WASH related gender based violence.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project focuses on beneficiary’s safety through protection risk analysis survey, safety audit tools exercises and access negotiation supported by UNOCHA. PAH field security protocol will be used to analyse security situation. The security updates in the field before any intervention will be shared via humanitarian hubs, with close coordination with UNDSS to receive and report security incidences. PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level This will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and house hold surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. The protection environment is challenging, due to high needs in the affected population. There are big groups of people requiring professional assistance in terms of healing traumas, GBV case management, child-protection, disabled protection etc. PAH, as very experienced in work across South Sudan, has good capacity in the management of activities in such an environment. The monitoring team has protection-related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery. Where distributions are conducted, demonstration on the use of items is done and safe disposal of packaging materials is encouraging during the demonstrations. PAH mainstreams protection throughout the project ensuring that activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In this project, PAH aims at enhancing the resilience of the community by reducing future risks for targeted communities. This project will focus on capacity building of partners in targeted locations especially the national NGOs by having joint trips and activities more especially in emergency response and community institutions such as water user committees to enhance self-management, pump mechanics to build the capacity to maintain and repair water points, handing over of hand pump repair toolkits to local leaders, and community hygiene promoters to keep conducting social and behavior change communication sessions. PAH will focus on re-establishing traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level and work with local leaders and community leaders. In locations with functional markets, PAH will use available markets to procure project materials.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. For example, eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries for WASH NFI distribution will be used and the following categories of beneficiaries will be considered: - female-headed households, elderly-headed households, child-headed households, unaccompanied children, people with less or no community link, people living with disabilities, elderly and people with chronic sicknesses, pregnant and lactating women with little support, people at community institutions, disease-affected population, children and women, responsible for fetching water. During the registration for distribution, PAH will endure door to door registration to ensure that even those who are not able to come to the registration points are registered including their vulnerability status. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. PAH will ensure that decision pertaining location of WASH facilities are made in consultation with women and girls. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up During registration/distribution, PAH do registration from household to household and will ensure that order is maintained by involving community leaders, crowed controllers and creating separate ques for men and women, children and different categories of people. The protection risk assessment will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to attend to complaints of stakeholders (direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers) at all stages of the project. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff, as well as invited to share their experiences and suggestions with PAH’s monitoring team or program staff. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback directly with PAH staff, through complaint desks, community leaders, etc. information shared will be used for improving actions/activities, advocacy for further services from relevant actors in situation where PAH is unable to respond to the needs including referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention. Positive feedback will be used for documenting success stories. For people living with impairment like the those are immobile, PAH team will consider visiting households with the help of the hygiene promoters to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language.
