Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
MC will improve access to basic water services through the identification of critical gaps and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure and solid waste management, in particular in areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ), and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement (Dana, Daret Azza and Atareb in Idleb and north Aleppo). A special focus will be given to water quality assurance. MC will actively seek solutions for operation and maintenance (O&M), through support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of water supply rehabilitation. Water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return. Where no other option exists, MC will provide access to safe drinking water through trucking for a period up to 3 months until alternative low-cost and sustainable solutions are implemented. Water interventions will be complemented by solid waste management services. Additionally, MC will support sanitation through repairs to community-level sewage networks and desludging of septic tanks in camps. Hygiene promotion, using carefully tailored messages, and the distribution of hygiene kits will complement WASH infrastructure rehabilitation, water trucking, and solid waste management interventions.

Needs

NES: Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signaling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. Across Syria, one in three households is now headed by women, which highlights different needs. Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and large-scale destruction pose a serious risk to the physical safety of civilians, housing, land and property (HLP) and for the restoration of basic services.Across NES, Mercy Corps estimates there are some 2,078,682 Syrians in need of urgent WASH support. Destruction of communal infrastructure, including damage to the power grid and disruption of specialized supply chains, has resulted in non-functional water systems and collapse of water treatment and quality assurance. 60% of HHs do not have access to water and an additional 35-40% of HHs only have access to some water. An unregulated private sector has filled the gap left by the absence of a centralized water system, but is inadequate, unaffordable and often low quality. Private water trucking places a considerable burden on HHs who are spending up to 25% of their income on water. The lack of sanitation and hygiene services has already led to an outbreak of acute bloody diarrhea in DEZ and typhoid in Al-Hole and Areesha camps. 41% of KII respondents indicated that access to water is the most important issue while 30% and 29% mentioned that quality and quantity are the main issues respectively.NWS:With multiple waves of IDPs, humanitarian conditions deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarized zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movement.In NWS, governorates with the greatest number in acute WASH need include Aleppo and Idleb. MC assessments revealed severe damage to pumps, generators and piped infrastructure impacting water quality, quantity, and availability. With public water systems operating at less than their pre-conflict capacity and unable to ensure the same water quality, the HNO estimates 40% of the population have water, sanitation and hygiene needs. Only 44% of the communities in Atareb, 60% in Daret Azza, 24% in Dana have access to water for 1 or 2 days/week, forcing people to opt for expensive water trucking. Water shortages have reduced coverage of safely maintained sanitation facilities. Toilets are often overcrowded as host communities continue to host IDPs in their homes and sanitation networks remain damaged without repair. In a specific assessment conducted by the Assistance Coordination Unit (ACU) in Harim district in Idleb, 50% of the communities reported extremely high prices of over 500 SYP/1.10 USD per cubic meter of water. The assessment also revealed that women fare worse than men in this difficult environment. Across sub-districts in Harim, only 38% of women reported that water distribution process was fair, in comparison with 47% of men. A rapid gender analysis conducted in March 2018 by CARE further revealed that women and girls fear sexual harassment when accessing communal water facilities, particularly in camps. This confirms that women suffer disproportionately in accessing water services, especially with the increase in the number of female-headed households due to the conflict.

Implementation of activities

1 Repair and rehabilitation of water systems: MC will identify critical service gaps and needs for rehab of water stations and networks. Technical assessments in consultation with the water directorates will inform BoQs design. Staff will facilitate FGDs with men, women, boys, and girls to identify protection risks faced by each in accessing WASH sites, which will inform the design. MC rehabilitates existing boreholes, supply/distribution networks and repairing/constructing elevated water tanks. Rehab works will ensure the distance to water points is ≤500m, with queuing time less than 15 min and filling time max 3 min/20 liters, or as locally agreed; a sufficient water supply to cover drinking, cooking and personal hygiene needs; water is safe with no fecal coliforms detectable/100ml sample and water treatment to FRC of 0.5 mg/l and turbidity <5NTU.2 Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems: MC will provide capacity development and O&M support for municipal water team through provision of monthly maintenance costs and necessary materials and equipment, incl generators, pumps, fuel tanks, diesel, disinfectants, and spare parts for periodic maintenance. MC will conduct routine inspections engaging local/civil councils and Water Directorates. Rehabilitated infrastructures will be handed over to the municipal services upon completion.3 Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems: MC will provide garbage services and provide communal bins in informal settlements, camps and urban settings. MC will work on longer term solid waste management by mobilizing communities through hygiene promotion in reducing and reusing waste. MC will work with municipalities to ensure proper disposal of the waste to the disposal points. MC will also ensure there is no contamination of groundwater. Periodic visits will be conducted to see if waste is dumped with no health risks. As an exit plan, MC will develop longer term solutions for continued solid waste management by the municipality; these may include cost recovery options and involvement of private vendors, etc. MC will further conduct small-scale sanitation rehabilitation to cover interventions such as cleaning the blockage of the inspection holes, replacing the damaged parts of sewage networks, constructing new manholes and extending the network to connect new HH. MC will contribute to improved sanitation in camps and informal settlements through desludging of septic tanks.4 Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion: MC will conduct hygiene promotion activities to raise awareness of safe hygiene practices and distribute hygiene kits. MC will conduct a Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey to identify KAP gaps in hygiene, water and sanitation. The surveys will be conducted with men, women and young people, and analysis will consider differences in needs to be addressed. MC will train community volunteers to organize hygiene awareness sessions and activities through participatory approaches, prioritizing those communities that have had hygiene related illness outbreaks. 5 Improved access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services: Where no other option exists, MC will support emergency water trucking services. The duration of water services in emergencies will be for a maximum of three months based on agreed Syria context minimum standards. Full water quality analysis will be conducted with the water supplier in advance. Trucked water will be chlorinated and FRC levels will be checked throughout the entire supply chain.6 Market-based assistance for WASH services: MC will distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to conflict-affected households based on regional SMEB values, which include hygiene items, and hygiene vouchers. MC coordinates all above programs with the NES WASH Working Group and the Northwest Syria WASH Cluster based in Gaziantep, Turkey.

Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff and partners will be trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all.1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for distributions.2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. MC utilizes appropriate and transparent targeting techniques, prioritising the most vulnerable and those at risk, ensuring services will be delivered without discrimination, ensuring inclusion of minorities. Mercy Corps has invested heavily in the tools and capacity to understand the conflict and power dynamics of where we work through regular analysis at the of communities through to regional geopolitical politics to ensure all interventions are conflict sensitive and in line with a DNH approach. Our approach is grounded in analysis and, accountability (ensuring transparent and inclusive targeting, participatory design, and transparent feedback and complaints mechanisms. Mercy Corps staff and partners are representative of the demography in each area and have close community ties which facilitates community acceptance and aids accountability. Staff and partners are trained to ensure conflict sensitivity approaches are mainstreamed across interventions.3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Similarly, where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavour to engage with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. 4. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneficiaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents. In line with protection cluster guidelines, information can be triangulated using local verification processes. Protection interventions can increase stigma and risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps support project designs which mitigate risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps also is supporting projects targeting youth and adolescents and persons with disabilities who are historically excluded from target interventions.
