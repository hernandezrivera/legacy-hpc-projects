Project Summary

For over a decade now, the insurgency situation in the North East of Nigeria has negatively impacted the overall living conditions of the population. Critical infrastructures, livelihood support activities, access to healthcare amongst others have been partially or completely destroyed. Recently, a new wave of security challenges in some remote areas of Yobe and Borno States have caused population displacement leading to an increase in the the number of LGAs that are in crisis phase from 8 LGAs in October 2019 to 11 LGAs in the March 2020. This has put pressure on the already limited local coping strategies thus increasing the demand on resources to respond to their needs. Similarly, restrictions due to the spread of COVID-19 has imposed additional challenge for households to access their livelihood activities thereby impacting on their access to food and other needs. Food security partners already spent longer time in the distribution of food and cash‐based support due to the need to maintain social distancing. This raises additional challenges, especially in high-risk security areas. Such challenges could lead to desperate measures like the outright sale of assets as a negative coping strategy.
With Yobe State recording 13 confirmed cases of COVID-19 as of May 4, 2020, it is obvious that the containment measures (like the lockdown) by the Government will be tightened thus putting more pressure on livelihood support structures of the affected population.
The restrictions on movement to curb the spread of COVID-19 disrupt households’ livelihoods and this will increase the vulnerability of the population especially those that live on a day’s earnings and female-headed households. Also, the policy of Government to allow markets to open twice a week for people to restock foodstuff and other essentials critically challenges the social distancing protocol for curtailing spread. This is because on such open days, there is usually a scramble for buying and selling. GHIV Africa believes that with supplemental support for household income and livelihood activities, social distancing will become more effective. Moreover, while others are still living in denial of the existence of the virus, there are others with misconceptions about the prevention and mitigation of COVID-19 such as traditional beliefs in the intake of some particular food as effective treatment and containment of the Virus.
GHIV Africa will strengthen the resilience of IDPs in host communities in selected LGAs of Yobe State through the provision of cash assistance.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in the Northeast of Nigeria has brought untold misery to the vulnerable population who are now in dear need of protection services. A lot of efforts have been put in place to mitigate the effect of the crisis. However, the outbreak the global of COVID-19 pandemic has increased the vulnerabilities of people especially women and girls. In Nigeria, as of April 25 2020, 1,182 cases have been registered with fatalities across the country. This has resulted into imposition of restrictive measures by the government authorities with intention to contain the widespread of the disease. The lockdown situation has increased prices of commodities which will make things even more difficult to low income households and will bring economic hardship vulnerable populations especially on women which may expose them to exploitation and abuse. Experiences have demonstrated that where women are primarily responsible for procuring and cooking food for the family, increasing food insecurity as a result of the crises may place them at heightened risks of intimate partner and other forms of domestic violence due to increased tensions in the household. According to the Strategic Framework-COVID -19 Northeast Nigeria, 1.6 persons are in need of early recovery and livelihoods assistance in the Northeast of Nigeria. With current situation of COVID-19 spreading rapidly, the number of cases keeps increasing every day, this means more stringent measures to flatten the curve of the pandemic in the region and this will increase the hardship on vulnerable populations. Already, there is inaccessibility to basic services, market disruptions and price fluctuations. This will greatly affect low income earners and those who depend on day’s earnings. All these needs to has to be addressed in order to contain the spread of the virus and also to help the communities to avoid negative coping strategies. GHIV Africa is targeting vulnerable IDPs in Host communities in selected LGAs of Yobe State for this project. Our activities will focus on cash distribution, trainings, and awareness and sensitization on COVID-19.

2 - Activities

The project idea is to support two ERL Strategic Priorities which are: 1) Contain the spread of the COVID-19 epidemic and decrease morbidity and mortality; and 2) Decrease the deterioration of human assets and rights, social cohesion, food security and livelihoods. In view of this, GHIV Africa will conduct the following activities:1. Consultation with community stakeholders across the project locations. 2. Mapping of IDPs and host communities to identify 2000 households hit by drastically reduced incomes, especially female headed households with unstable incomes (daily workers) as well as the poorest households with little savings. 3. Conduct an assessment of business viability in the targeted communities in order to provide tailored training and strategic support to the selected beneficiaries in areas of basic business principles, finance management, bookkeeping, savings and loan schemes, and ethics. 4. Profiling and selection of 100 women, men, boys and girls for two cycles of three-days training in their preferred livelihoods skills, and provision of start-up kits to the trained beneficiaries. 5. Procurement and distribution of start-up kits to 100 women, men, girls and boys according to their choice of skills for which they were earlier trained 6. Mobilization and training of 400 women, men, girls and boys for awareness creation and sensitization on COVID-19 in the local languages across communities, with messages on myths and facts, signs and symptoms, stigmatization and care-giving, general hygiene promotion and social distancing in public spaces and in the home. 7. Coordination of door-to-door COVID-19 prevention campaign by the mobilised 400 women, men, girls and boys to reach 2000 households across targeted LGAs 8. Profiling and selection of 400 persons living with disabilities needing immediate support to recover from the severe consequences of the lockdown and provide them with immediate livelihood support through cash transfers. 9. Provision of cash grant to 1600 women and girls headed households for investment in income generating activities to enable them recover from COVID-19 pandemic. 10. Profiling and selection of 400 women, men, girls and boys for cash for work activities targeting environmental hygiene promotion in the host communities. 11. Advocacy meetings with community stakeholders (community leaders, religious leaders, youth leaders, etc) on promoting gender equality and women’s participation in the management and recovery process of COVID-19 and other community engagements to strengthen social cohesion, while respecting the social distancing protocols.
