Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, World Vision Syria Response (WVSR) will provide cash value vouchers to food-insecure households in Idlib governorate. The project will improve access to adequate food through cash vouchers to all targeted beneficiaries in the targeted locations, providing life-saving assistance to those who need it most, especially children and pregnant and lactating women.
WVSR will WV will leverageLeverage its respective expertise and experience to d provides food assistance to an estimated 10,000 households in camps within Harim District (with a focus on al-Dana) using a conflict-sensitive approach.
For food assistance, WV align tis intervention with the harmonized FSL- CWG CVA transfer values and provide food value voucher assistance of 60$ per month to food-insecure households in underserved locations with the highest severity levels of food insecurity for 8 Cycles/Months. The transfer calculation are build based on the rational that this value is approximately 70% of the daily kcal intake (around 1550 Kcal per person per day in a monthly family food ration) as per the FSS guidance. It is also reflective of 70% of the SMEB food only proxy tbasket. The targeted beneficiaries will receive their monthly support through a closed loop e-voucher system, with the option of adding direct cash supplied by shopkeepers or local hawala agents where permitted. Community sensitization and awareness activities on how to use the e-voucher modality, as well as two-way accountability, feedback and communication channels (see more information below), will form a key element of the project implementation.
The merchant vendors network necessary to serve the beneficiary caseload will be drawn from a selection of retailers and wholesalers on the basis ofbased on their proximity to beneficiaries and the quality and cost of goods they supply. The merchants vendors will be monitored and adapted as required by the intervention.
Within the scope of this action an in-store shopping modality is proposed in which beneficiaries are topped up with a USD equivalent value (Points) that they can spend within a retail network. Merchants exchange goods for Points using the WVSR LMMS system that records every transaction performed by the Merchantvendors. Vendor settlement of accrued Points is effected either as cash settlement or via appropriate bank settlement routes. Nonetheless, other modalities including direct cash or goods order (click and collect) can be facilitated through the LMMS service, should the operational context necessitate a modality change.
Prior experience and current behaviors of affected populations all speak to the appropriateness of voucher assistance, which is preferred by the recipients as a dignified and safe modality, while also being cost-effective (lower logistics costs) and supporting local markets and trades. The local markets have been exceptionally resilient, accessible, and responsive throughout the crisis and expected to remain so in the near future. The primary risks on the economy stem from the high inflation of the Syrian Pound and Turkish Lira but this weakness of local currencies mitigated via: (a) the use of U.S. Dollars in programming and (b) the use of cash-value vouchers, which has the further benefit of reducing the pressure on US Dollar liquidity needs.
Since 2019, WVI has implemented direct cash assistance to 13,546 households across 60 IDP camps in NWS, with a total value of $2,150,170 in cash transferred to beneficiaries. WV has taken a cautious yet bold approach to introducing cash assistance, and has proven that it can sustain consistently high standards safely at scale with short lead times in the challenging and rapidly changing context. In the case of FSL, WVI has worked most closely with the Humanitarian Relief Association (IYD).

4 - Needs

Syria is experiencing the worst humanitarian crisis in modern history, characterized by increasing food insecurity and high malnutrition rates due to the combined effects of the ongoing-armed activities and the complex geo-political landscape. While the March 2020 Idleb ceasefire agreement led to an overall reduction in hostilities and large-scale displacement, conflict has intensified throughout 2021 along frontlines across the northwest that have not shifted significantly since 2020 and is driving humanitarian needs to a significant extent. Political division, exclusion and the lack of access to justice mechanisms have continued to disenfranchise people and to limit their ability to address their needs in a sustainable manner. In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. This has resulted in the unprecedented internal displacement of the population, especially towards the camps and surrounding areas of North West Syria (NWS). In total, an estimated 4.5 million people in NWS are in need of humanitarian assistance, and this number expected to increase in 2022.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be in line with the FSL cluster and the CWG priorities and recommendations. A food voucher value assistance of 60$ per month will be distributed to food-insecure households in underserved locations with the highest severity levels of food insecurity for 8 Cycles/Months. WV team will conduct DM and PDM (3-4 weeks after each round) activities for each round. Prior to distributions, finance confirms FSPs delivery capacity and their ability to source enough appropriate bank notes. The market system will be monitored on periodical biases and the data will be used to validate/triangulate with the REACH monthly market findings. In addition, base, mid, and end line assessments will be conducted to better understand the impact of the intervention on both the CVA recipient HHs, and the market system (commodities and FSPs). The vendor’s network necessary to serve the beneficiary caseload will be drawn from a selection of retailers and wholesalers based on their proximity to beneficiaries and the quality and cost of goods they supply. The vendors will be monitored and adapted as required by the intervention. WV will coordinate closely at area level with FSL cluster and other humanitarian actors to avoid duplication of the assistance and align assistance modality with the local community references and needs. WV will ensure the submission of monthly 4Ws and sharing assessment tools and findings with FSL cluster and CWG.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Abuse of power by distribution actors Mitigation: Ensure all staff sign code of conduct and receive training on WVSR s anti-fraud, whistleblowing, safeguarding and PSEA policies -Safeguarding Reporting Framework and CFM in place -At least 1 female in each verification team. Risk 2: physical harm at distribution points: Mitigation: Well-organized distribution to avoid overcrowding. Staff training on distributions and crowded management. Checklists at distribution sites. Priority lines for vulnerable persons. Risk 3: Discrimination of community groups and only a few groups benefit in the targeted communities Mitigation: Engage community committee in beneficiaries selection ( with diverse members of the community to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered, Select staff for all activities (especially volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile Risk4: Men and Women may come into conflict due to the mix of men and woman in the same distribution period, Mitigation: Take into consideration the culture and norms in each location through divide the distribution creating a separate queue for women and men during distribution to avoid crowding or harassment, Separated rooms for any additional services. Ensuring there are female staff members from the implementing organization present during distributions, and setting up women-friendly spaces at asset distribution sites.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WV adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA and it is committed responsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men – are treated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WV field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which is led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WV field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle Furthermore, WV has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WVSR uses a standard online platform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WVSR will prioritize having a harmonized risk mitigation approach based on the CVA risk mitigation task force for NWS in which WV played a key role in its development. General security situation in areas of operation becomes unstable causing operations to cease. WV has in place a safety and security structure, a security plan and a dedicated safety and security department to monitor and advise on safety. WV’s security team continuously monitors the security situation in the area of operation in close collaboration with the UN and inform operations accordingly. WV has a capacity to shift between assistance delivery modalities to adapt to the security context and limit the repercussions of lack of beneficiaries access to assistance. WV will monitor as well key technical risks such as any considerable market pricing fluctuation and the availability and quality of food items available at vendors. In order to mitigate these risks, WV rely on clear SOP to adjust the assistance in case of a market fluctuation based on an agreed threshold with the cluster. Also, WV will work on agreed food items basket with contracted vendors and not limiting beneficiaries with the number of vendors they can go to. This is along with leveraging monitoring visits to ensure mainly the availability and the quality of foods provided.
