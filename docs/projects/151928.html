Project Summary

The protracted conflict in eastern Ukraine is now at its fifth year with no end in sight yet. So far it has caused the displacement of 2.6 million persons and 35,081 casualties, of which 10,303 people killed , and a considerable destruction of infrastructure. The Line of Contact (LoC) separates now the government controlled areas (GCA) and non-government controlled areas (NGCA) of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. The Government of Ukraine (GoU) has registered 1.5 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), of which 800,000 are estimated to be permanently living in GCA. Over 1.9 million Ukrainians are living within 20 km zone along the LoC on both sides, and they are among the most vulnerable affected by the conflict.
Due to the protracted conflict food insecurity and disrupted livelihoods remain to be the main issues affecting hundreds of thousands of IDP and local population in eastern Ukraine. Over 2019-2020 period NRC will continue its comprehensive support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected population in GCA to enable their (1) access to food, (2) restoration of productive assets, and (3) employment and income generation opportunities.

Needs

Prior to the conflict, the Donbas region was the breadbasket of the east, producing grains, field crops and horticultural produce for national market and export, while mining, metal-processing and chemical industry used to employ the major share of work-aged population. The ongoing violence and displacement have severely affected the economy of both oblasts, causing a massive loss of jobs and incomes, market disruption and restrictions in the flow of goods and services. This has led to conflict-affected populations being unable to maintain their livelihoods, with basic standards of living deteriorating drastically. Disrupted livelihoods in eastern Ukraine and affected populations’ inability to regain economic security are still the main issues that require a well-coordinated response by both international and national actors over mid-term. Further, due to low incomes and high actual food basket costs, access to food remains a challenge, particularly for people along the LoC. As their savings and resources have been largely depleted, many have resorted to negative coping mechanisms. • According to the FSLC’s 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), there are nearly 400,000 people in need in GCA (185,815 along the LoC, 213,411 in other GCA areas), among whom 69% are women, 14% children under 18 and 54% elderly (60<) people. • Further, 267,000 people in GCA are in urgent need of food assistance, whereas approximately 13,000 are severely food insecure. The HNO estimates that up to 406,000 unemployed work-aged people, including IDPs, are in need of livelihoods assistance• The 2019 HNO has determined that food insecurity levels in GCA have decreased compared to 2017, with 9% of the population considered moderately food insecure and 1% severely food insecure. However, in the areas close to the LoC, the share of the population with moderate or severe food insecurity reached 15%.• Finally, the 2019 HNO has found that 30% of female-headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption, and therefore continue to be more vulnerable than male-headed households in GCA.• According to the FSLC’s Trend Analysis – Food Security and Socio-Economic Situation Report (March 2018), food insecurity for households led by people aged 40 to 60 years is waning - in February 2018 it was 26%, while in June 2016 it was 11%. For households headed by the unemployed within this age group the food insecurity level was 37%. • The Cluster has found that the unemployment rates continued to grow between 2015-2017, reaching 17.4% in Luhansk and 15% in Donetsk oblasts in January-September 2017, while the national average was 9.7%. However, the employment rate of IDPs is only 48% on the national level (IOM NMS March 2018). • 78% of households in GCA in February 2018 resorted to negative coping strategies, which is a considerable increase from 53% in June 2017 (FSLC 2018).• According to Market Monitor Review January-June 2018 (WFP July 2018 ), the actual food basket costs in Ukraine increased significantly between June 2017-June 2018 and has reached the highest historical level of UAH 996 in June 2018, a 11.4% higher year on year.Destroyed agricultural infrastructure, and limited access to land due to land mines/ERW, affect production in areas along the LoC (NRC January 2016/February 2018). Since many of the communities and agricultural households have lost their primary sources of income, they lack capacity to undertake investments or even repairs required.

Activities & outcomes

NRC has been implementing activities addressing access to food and early recovery in east Ukraine GCA since 2016. Project activities are aligned with relevant GoU strategies (e.g. IDP Integration Strategy, State Targeted Recovery/Peacebuilding Programme, etc.) and attempt to create synergies/linkages with the activities of recovery/development actors (HDN). Targeting primarily the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict-affected host communities along the LoC, beneficiaries will be selected from different age and social groups prioritizing elderly, female-headed and/or households with 2 or more children, persons with disabilities/medical cases and with no regular incomes. 1. The most vulnerable conflict-affected households have access to food: • Season: winter months, when needs are greater,• Conflict-affected households identified and registered as per the FSLC vulnerability criteria, prioritizing those residing along the LoC. • Amount of cash grants established in line with FSLC and CWG’s recommendations.• Cash transfer set up with public sector participation (Ukrposhta) and direct delivery to beneficiaries, to minimize the risks,• Selected households receive monthly unconditional cash grants for meeting basic food needs, • Post distribution monitoring (PDM) carried out 2 weeks after distributions.2. Restoration of agricultural productive capacity for vulnerable conflict-affected households along the LoC: • Early spring: Provision of agricultural inputs to selected vulnerable households for re-establishment of agricultural activities. Modality: cash, only exceptionally in-kind, • Year-round: Rehabilitation of agricultural productive assets such as irrigation systems, greenhouses and orchards. through food for assets type of assistance, • Year-round: Agricultural training and field advisory assistance provided through individual consultants or organisations specializing in provision of agricultural/extension services. 3. Early recovery assistance: • Season: Year-round, • Enhancing local household economy and incomes/job-creation for vulnerable population via supporting establishment and sustainability of micro, small and/or medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) through cash grants, • Support of skills training and building capacity of relevant employment/business-support service providers, with priority given to women, unemployed youth and young adults, • Production, management and marketing capacity building of income-oriented rural/producers’ organisations. Geography: With priority given to the LoC areas, the assistance, except #2, will be implemented in the whole Luhansk GCA oblast and in northern raions of Donetsk oblast GCA. Priority areas are along the LoC.Coordination: NRC will continue working closely and coordinating with the FSLC throughout the project’s planning and implementation.Gender Mainstreaming: NRC will ensure that there is active participation of women, girls, men and boys in all project activities. As women are the mainstay of day-to-day family subsistence in Ukraine, the programme will promote their role as producers and providers of incomes. Female-headed households will be prioritized and majority of the project beneficiaries will be women. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated as follows: Elderly (60+; male/female); adults (18-59 male/female); children (5-17 male/female) and children (<5 male/female).This programme is designed to promote equitable access to food and livelihood opportunities to the elderly, adults, boys, girls, men and women, taking into consideration unique needs of individuals/social groups. Participatory approach will be used in analyzing needs, programme preferences and in identifying the most vulnerable.Advocacy: NRC will continue advocating for access to land for livelihoods (demining of farmland, cessation of the use of property by the military, access to documentation), as well as for unrestricted access to markets on the NGCA side by LoC farmers.

Indicators & targets

Objective 1: Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable groups affected by the conflict (6,000 beneficiaries-individuals)• % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) at end of implementation (Target 70%) • Number of beneficiaries receiving cash (target: 6,000)• Total value of cash for food and basic needs distributed (target: $500,000) Objective 2: Ensure sustainable food security of the affected population through improved agricultural production - Restoration of agricultural productive capacity (1,680 HHs/4,200 individuals)• % of beneficiary households who report/are observed using food production inputs as intended within 3 months of provision (Target 90%)• # of households (male/female) receiving agricultural inputs (target: 1,200/3,000 individuals)• # of assets restored or maintained to targeted beneficiaries, by type and unit of measure (e.g. hectares/m2 of land, length and type of irrigation systems restored) (target:10)• # of households (male/female) benefitting from rehabilitated assets (target: 480/1,200 individuals)• Quantity of input items distributed, as percentage of planned (target 99%) Objective 3: Early recovery assistance for the conflict-affected populations for sustainable livelihoods (360 HHs/1,300 individuals)• % of targeted households that increased income as a result of assistance provided (Target 70%)• Total value of cash and in-kind assistance for income generation and employment creation distributed to targeted beneficiaries (target $250,000)• # of vulnerable HHs (male/female) receiving start-up capital to start/rehabilitate micro/small businesses (target: 200)• # of jobs created, in addition to recipients’ HHs - for women, youth and adults (male/female) (target 300)• # of people - women, youth and young adults (male/female) benefitting via improved incomes or employability (target 600)• # of people - women, youth and young adults (male/female) benefitting via improved access to/and or skills training or business-development services (target 300)• # of organisations benefitting via improved capacity (target 10)• # of people (male/female) benefitting via improved incomes and/or access to markets through the supported organisations (target 400/160 HHs) Protection mainstreaming indicators:• % of feedbacks/complaints responded to or forwarded to the appropriate actor (Target 100%)• % of the affected population reporting being aware of how to use the feedback and complaints mechanism (Target 90%)• % of beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner (Target 90%)• % of beneficiaries reporting they understand how humanitarian services were prioritized and selected (targeting criteria) (Target 80%)Monitoring and Evaluation: Output and outcome monitoring will be done throughout the whole project. Beneficiaries will be randomly sampled and post distribution monitoring (PDM) carried out after each distribution cycle. Data will be collected through focus group discussions as well as household interviews. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated as follows: elderly (60+; male/female); adults (18-59 male/female); children (5-17 male/female) and children (<5 male/female). NRC will also carry out a baseline studies as well as an end of project evaluations. Protection Mainstreaming and AAP: A full protection risk analysis (tensions, landmines/UXOs, abuse, exploitation, GBV, access by women/at-risk groups) will be carried out and mitigation strategies developed before the commencement of activities. NRC policy places a heavy emphasis on ‘rights based’ programming, in which accountability plays an important role. NRC has set up a Complaints and Feedback mechanism which is independent from programme operations.
