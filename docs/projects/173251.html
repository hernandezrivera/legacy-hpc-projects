Project Summary

The magnitude with which the COVID-19 epidemic has attacked highly developed countries is a clear warning of the possible impacts that this virus can have on countries with less capacity and development, such as Venezuela. Venezuela cases are increasing drastically. Although the government has imposed radical quarantine in most of the states in Venezuela, due to the economic crisis, the Venezuelan population has not been able to adhere to the COVID-19 preventive measures.
In addition, over 76,000 migrants have returned to Venezuela with a deplorable health and economic condition, many of which have been exposed to COVID-19 cases/ patient with no protection measures.
The capacity of the Venezuelan health system is limited compared to the needs required to fight the pandemic, due to the shortage of qualified health personnel and necessary supplies and reduced training in use of equipment. Currently, healthcare workers are on the front lines without gloves and/or facemasks, which have caused a high mortality rate among them. In addition, failures in running water, sanitation and hygiene services in health facilities reduce the ability to prevent and control infections.
As of August 16, the authorities have confirmed 33,755 cases of COVID-19 in the country, 22,700 people recovered and 281 deaths. While figures are the lowest in South America, doctors and opposition politicians warn that the true caseload is likely far higher given testing delays.
As a result, International Medical Corps Venezuela proposes a program that will prioritize Miranda (4,905 cases), Apure (2,660 cases) and Distrito Capital (8,722 cases) states as proposed intervention areas for this project.
According to statements made by the governor of the state of Miranda, 80.54% of the positive cases are concentrated in 11 parishes of the state: Ocumare Del Tuy, El Hatillo, Charallave, Chacao, Guarenas, Baruta, Leoncio Martínez, Guatire, Las Minas de Baruta, Los Teques and Petare. Private Hospitals in East Caracas do not have enough capacity to receive COVID-19 patients. Miranda falls under the second states that has the highest number of cases.
The state of Apure is located in the southwest of Venezuela, and shares borders with Colombia (southwest). Before the COVID-19 pandemic, local habitants of El Amparo (Páez municipality) used to cross the border through the Arauca River, motivated by the needs to cover essential and for trade of medicines and food coming from Colombia.
According to official statements, since mid-May there has been an exponential increase in "imported cases" presumably because of the Venezuelans crossing illegally the border using what is known as “Trochas” or clandestine routes, Táchira, Apure, Zulia and Bolívar. At least 7.000 migrants returned to Venezuela through the José Antonio Páez International Bridge during the month.
Currently, the daily number of Venezuelans crossing the border from Colombia to Venezuela is 300 people an average per day. All the migrants crossing the border are placed in one of the 32 temporary shelters (PASI) located in Apure state. Currently there are approximately 3.300 people interned in these facilities. On average, the daily entrance reaches 125 people (July estimates).
IMC is currently supporting two sentinel hospitals that are overwhelmed with cases and have lost some of the health personnel. As the needs are huge, the grant will be to:
Distribute PPEs (4 targeted hospitals, 3 PASI)
Provide COVID-19 training to health and non-health personnel at targeted health facilities:
Apure hospital: 150 HP and 30 Non-HP
HOSPITAL LEOPOLDO MANRIQUE TERRERO: 100 HP and 30 non-HP.
Luciani: 30 non-HP.
Distribution of hygiene materials to health personnel in 4 targeted hospitals.
Distribution of COVID-19 IEC (provided by PAHO) in the 3 PASI
Awareness campaigns for beneficiaries at the health centers in Miranda and PASI in Apure.
The proposed duration of the project is three (3) months.
