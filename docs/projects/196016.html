Project Summary

The project will provide primary and secondary healthcare services to refugees and surrounding host communities in Cox’s Bazar, based on the “Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for Primary Healthcare Facilities in Refugee/FDMN camps, Cox’s Bazar” and the “Standard Operating Procedure for Patient Referrals to Secondary and Tertiary Healthcare Facilities for Refugees/FDMNs, Cox’s Bazar”. The project will be aligned to the Cox’s Bazar Health Sector Strategic plan 2022-2024.
Services will be delivered through 10 primary healthcare centers (PHC), 5 health posts; 5 drop-in centers for key and gender diverse populations; 1 physiotherapy center and 1 specialized hospital in Ukhiya. The project will include 3 mental health in-patient centers for short term stabilization of clinical mental health cases, two within PHCs in the camps, and one in Ukhiya specialized hospital.
The health facilities will be accessible by older persons and persons with disabilities (PWDs) and accompanied referrals from households supported. Services will be tailored to the needs of PWDs including availability of appropriate equipment. Mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) will be provided integrated into primary healthcare facilities and at community level, with new modalities of Integrated Adaptive Therapy applied. The use of trained refugee community para counselors will bring care closer to the community. Integration of MHPSS with nutrition, WASH, protection, GBV and other sectors will be strengthened.
The project will cater for referral costs and ambulances for its partners, and referral costs for emergency cases in half the camps.
A robust community health system will ensure health promotion, disease surveillance, early identification and response to health hazards e.g., diseases of epidemic potential; and contribute to community resilience. Through coordination of the community health working group, harmonized community-based health interventions by partners in the response will be ensured; and capacity building of CHWs, IEC materials and toolkits for CHWs provided. To support case management of outbreak prone diseases, the project will assure 2 infectious disease treatment centers (IDTCs) in the camp and at Ukhiya specialized hospital, transitioned from the Isolation and Treatment Centers for COVID19. The IDTCs will be used as health facilities and activated as per need, enabling immediate response as the minimum human resource and equipment will be available. Stockpiling of medicines and medical supplies & support to emergency medical teams will be ensured.
The project will engage refugees in service delivery, through monthly facility-based health forums where the community gives feedback to providers. Camp health focal points will coordinate with various actors at camp level and monitor health services in the camps and health facilities using existing UNHCR and Health Sector monitoring tools.
Monitoring of performance through age and gender disaggregated data collection and analysis systems e.g., the community-based health information system, UNHCR integrated refugee health information system (irHIS), compliance with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare’s (MOHFW) District Health Information Systems -2 and the Health sector’s 4Ws, Early Warning Alert and Response System etc. will be undertaken, and data used to generate evidence to guide interventions.
The project will coordinate with the health sector and all associated working groups including the Sexual and Reproductive Health W, Epidemiology WG, MHPSS WG and Emergency Preparedness and Response committee.
Integration of services with other sectors will continue, aiming at partners implementing integrated health and nutrition services by the end of 2025. Integration will be fostered with WASH, protection, GBV sectors/sub-sectors.
The MOHFW will gradually take over services in the specialized hospital in Ukhiya, assuming full responsibility at the end of 2024.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The health status of the refugees and surrounding host community remains stable but prone to emergencies and in need of improvement. As of August 2022, the crude mortality rate in UNHCR’s area of operation was 0.3/1,000/month, however, maternal and neonatal mortality in the camps is high, with 54 maternal deaths reported as of September 2022 (Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) Working Group), with still births and neonatal deaths representing 21% of all mortalities (Health Sector bulletin, August 2022).Health facilities supported by UNHCR attended to 400,259 consultations as of August 2022, with respiratory infections representing the largest cause of morbidity. As of September 2022, the unprecedented incidence of dengue cases in the year resulted in over 13,800 cases and 19 fatalities reported. There was a surge in skin diseases, specifically scabies, and in August 2022, a rise in conjunctivitis was seen across the camps. Cholera and diptheria cases were detected sporadically throughout the year. There is need for continuous community engagement to limit the spread of communicable diseases, collaboration with WASH and other sectors to foster prevention and response; and strengthening of case management capacities. The threat of vaccine preventable diseases is a result of sub-optimal vaccination coverage and access to vaccination by children aged below five years requires prioritization. As of September 2022, the community health force in all the camps has referred over 930,000 refugees for health services including 55,105 with symptoms of COVID-19, 8,863 with symptoms of dengue and 97,139 for management of skin diseases, highlighting the significance of the community health system.The proportion of women delivering at health facilities is 73% as of September 2022, below the Health Sector’s target of 80% for Cox’s Bazar camps. The proportion of women who have received at least four antenatal visits at the time of delivery continues to be very low, with an average of 17% as of August 2022 for all the camps, and 68% in UNHCR supported facilities, below the Health Sector’s target of 80%. Postnatal coverage is also low, at 67% in UNHCR supported facilities, and is of particular concern as most maternal mortalities occur within the postnatal period. Concerted effort is required to significantly impact maternal health.Patients in need of secondary healthcare are referred to facilities outside the camps, and as of August 2022, UNHCR has supported 4,379 such referrals. The largest cause of referral is trauma/other injuries and surgical cases. These referrals include life-saving interventions. As of September 2022, over 6,700 clients received clinical mental health consultations in UNHCR supported facilities. There is need to adapt more new advances in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) that have been proven to be effective and invest in MHPSS research to guide interventions. The Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment conducted by REACH in May 2021 revealed that persons with disabilities reported facing barriers in accessing services especially due to the distance to facilities and need for assistance to reach facilities. Additionally, older persons, especially females reported a lack of involvement in community forums. A study on SRH conducted by IOM, “MaBoinor Rosom” revealed that women and girls face cultural barriers in accessing contraceptives, and adaptation of positive cultural practices within facility maternity care would contribute to motivation to deliver in health facilities. Sustained engagement of men and boys in SRH issues and accountability of health service providers were concerns raised by women and girls. The need to be accountable to affected persons is essential, to ensure they are aware of their rights and providers are able to meet the recognized essential minimum standards.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is aligned to the Health Sector’s rationalization exercise carried out in 2022 and will adjust the number of health facilities to fit within the results of this exercise. In the next years further commitment to rationalization of services will be ensured, with the aim of having integrated service delivery as a more cost-effective and sustainable model. The project will promote integration of health and nutrition services by planning for implementation of these services within a camp by one partner or by supporting partners who implement both health and nutrition together. To support this approach, all health and nutrition facilities will have key activities incorporated into their workplans, that foster integration of health and nutrition services for children and pregnant and lactating women. Where feasible, the project will promote delivery of facility-based health and nutrition services within one or adjacent facilities. Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services will be provided integrated in primary healthcare facilities and nutrition centers, phasing out standalone MHPSS service centers. On the community health front, the project will promote the polyvalent community worker approach where community health workers will provide both health and nutrition services. This will begin in one camp and gradually grow across other camps where feasible.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will ensure that MHPSS are availed within the protection service delivery areas including child protection services, GBV services and with community-based protection. Health facilities will promote access to persons with disabilities including through infrastructure enhancements and service provision. Engagement of religious leaders and faith leaders, GBV case workers and child protection staff to enable support to vulnerable populations will be ensured. The project will utilize the global distribution tool for health services where it is beneficial e.g., for COVID-19 vaccination thus linking registration services with health services. Through community-based feedback mechanisms, the project ensures that the various needs and diversities of the population and persons with disabilities are taken into consideration in planning and service delivery.

3.2 - Gender

The project design will incorporate the needs of all refugees including gender diverse populations and persons with disabilities. Health facilities ensure their services are provided confidentially by having segregated waiting and consultation rooms and in-patient facilities, and HF-based forums with community members will include separate sessions for women and girls to respond to their specific needs. Scale-up of the GBV Quality Assurance Tool at health facilities and clinical management of rape survivors will support GBV prevention activities. Gender diverse populations will access healthcare at the drop-in centers; men and boys will be separately engaged through courtyard sessions; women and girls will participate in forums that engage the community on health matters, the participation of women as leaders will be encouraged; voices of older women and adolescents will be sought to inform programming and equal access to services across all genders will be promoted.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will provide services for specific age groups; specific needs of different age categories will be considered when planning health services, including separate consultation rooms and in-patient facilities for children and adults, identifying health vulnerabilities of different age categories and responding appropriately; adolescent SRH services will be availed at HFs with adolescent friendly spaces maintained to respond to adolescents's needs; the community health workforce will support older persons who need healthcare services by escorting them or arranging transfer between HFs and their house; ensuring their reproductive health is safeguarded, programs for women of reproductive age will be strengthened. UNHCR is committed to preventing child abuse and exploitation of the refugees in the health programs; all partner staff will be trained on child safeguarding principles to understand the standards and conduct expected from all staff and volunteers working at HFs.

3.4 - Diversity

Recognizing that populations are diverse in nature, the project will take ensure participation of the different groups that exist other than by age and gender. Programs to ensure equitable access to healthcare for gender diverse popualtions and persons with disabilities will be supported. Cultural and religious norms, ethnic minorities and other diversities will be taken into consideration in planning programs. The various groups will be included and participate in programming and communication mechanisms will be maintained to enable a feedback loop.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Communities will be engaged in decisions affecting their lives throughout the cycle of the project. Health facilities will maintain feedback boxes and hold regular facility based consultative forums to obtain face to face feedback and engage directly on matters affecting the community. Household visits by community health workers will elicit issues affecting access to healthcare. Rohingya speaking volunteers will be nominated to interface with clients seeking healthcare at facilities, to enable them to provide feedback freely. Consultative forums will ensure all segments of the community – women, men, boys and girls, persons with disabilities and gender diverse populations are engaged and informed of their rights and entitlements. Adolescents will be engaged through adolescent friendly spaces while men will be engaged through courtyard sessions. A tracking system of all feedback and required actions will be maintained and followed through by camp and community health focal points.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Health facilities will be maintained with semi-permanent and re-enforced structures to protect against environmental hazards. The project will utilize solar power in health facilities to reduce carbon emissions. The activities of the community health workers will promote health and increase resilience of the community to disasters related to disease outbreaks. Facilities will maintain fire response capacity to disable fires that may begin at facility level. The project will support emergency medical teams that will respond rapidly to health emergencies and hazards, thus limiting negative impact
