Project Summary

Overview of the Affected Population Acute malnutrition is a major public health problem in Somalia and disproportionately affects children under the age of 5, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Malnutrition is the result of years of conflict and violence, disease outbreaks, drought, locust infestation, recurrent flooding and population displacements. In addition, other factors such as high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, food insecurity, low access to health services, poor access to water and sanitation, poor maternal nutrition, and high disease burden contribute to malnutrition rates. Poor feeding practices for infants and young children are also strongly linked with undernutrition. According to the latest findings of 2021 post Gu results of FSNAU, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at the national level has remained at a serious level of 11.1 per cent weight-for-height z-score. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, the cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of 5 years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently. Out of this, 295,515 children under the age of 5 are likely to be severely malnourished and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM). In addition to this, around 245,909 PLWs will be at risk of acute malnutrition. CARE will target Boys and Girls(4500 SAM,10500)and 5200 Pregnant and lactating women. CARE life-saving nutrition intervention which will be linked with Health, WASH and food security programmes to provide Gender based violence, outpatient treatment, immunization, disease surveillance, sexual reproductive health through the health sector which will complement the nutrition intervention in all fixed and mobile sites. CARE will also provide Cash voucher assistance for SAM cases through Food security programme. The interventions will be linked to the cluster objectives of; 1.Provide equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases 2.Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions. 3.Strengthen nutrition situation coordination, monitoring and surveillance, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response. CARE will work with MOH to support health system strengthening and improve the scale up and sustainability of emergency nutrition and health program in their operational areas. CARE will implement the nutrition program adopting the CMAM (community based management of acute malnutrition) model for the early detection and treatment of U5 Children with acute malnutrition and in light with the new COVID 19 program adaptations measures to stop the spread of the virus. Deliberate targeting will be done persons with disability and minority groups. Activities will include; 1.Screening and referral of children aged 6-59 months and PLW with acute malnutrition. 4500 children identified with severe acute malnutrition will be admitted to Outpatient therapeutic program (OTP); 15000 children and PLW (pregnant and lactating women) with MAM will be admitted to (TSFP); and children with SAM with medical complications will be referred to the nearest SC. 2.Provision of IYCF prevention services to 26900 children under 5, PLW and other care givers attending the nutrition and health program. 3. Provision of Micronutrient support to 15000 PLW and children<5 attending OTP, TSFP and IYCF services.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Individual and Group IYCF counseling: IYCF counseling will be provided to 14000 care givers who seek assistance at MoH facilities and those reached by Community Health Workers (CHWs). CARE will focus IYCF-E activities in target regions based on recommendations from MoH and avoiding duplication with other NGOs. CARE will provide IYCF-E counselling both to individual mothers with specific feeding problems and caregivers in the 35 fixed health facilities and mobile teams. A trained midwife or nurse will conduct individual counselling and mothers will self-refer, be referred through group counselling or identified by healthcare providers themselves. Activity 2: Identification, Screening & referral of SAM and MAM cases: CARE will emphasize early detection of malnutrition through facility and community level screening conducted by Community Health Workers (CHWs), nutrition assistants/measurers and mothers themselves. While CARE will carry out mass screening while using Family MUAC approach where possible to identify early signs of malnutrition in their children using a simple to use Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tape. Activity 3: Admission and treatment of MAM cases: Following the screening process, 10500 Boys and Girls aged 6-59 months and 5200 PLWs meeting the admission criteria based on the Somalia Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) guidelines will be admitted to the TSFP. Once admitted, the program will provide them with ready-to-use supplementary foods (RUSF), oil and fortified Corn Soya Blend (CSB) rations on a biweekly or monthly cycle for a maximum of 8 weeks depending on the distribution schedule of the site. The program will provide all children with a monthly ration of 30 sachets supplementary RUSF and PLWs will receive 7.5 Kg of CSB and 0.75 Kg of oil according to the WFP TSFP rations scale.Activity 4: Admission and Treatment of SAM cases (without Complications): 4500 Boys and Girls presenting with SAM upon screening will be admitted into the OTP for treatment for a maximum of 12 weeks (3 months). According to the Somalia IMAM guideline those who will be admitted to the OTP must have an appetite (pass the appetite test), be clinically well and alert, and have MUAC of <11.5 – 12.4 cm or weight for height score of <-3 or oedema of grade 1 and 2 (+++). All children with medical complications, no appetite or with severe oedema grade 3 (+++) will be transferred to the nearest stabilization center for inpatient treatment.=. Children in the OTP with common childhood illnesses will be treated by a qualified nurse using the IMCI guidelines. Activity 5: Nutrition, Hygiene and Health Education: CARE’s CHWs will provide integrated nutrition, hygiene and health (NHHP) promotion sessions to 26,900 care givers of both gender including persons with disability, minority groups both on site as well as at the community level. The CHWs will work together with hygiene promoters where possible from the WASH program to achieve scaled impact of the education sessions. CHWs will be trained on NHHP using the UNICEF-developed Manuals, and messages will be tailored to the context after analyzing the disease patterns and consultations documented in the health facilities, to ensure key messages are relevant to the local context.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

CARE will also liaise and coordinate with community leaders and government line ministries to further improve access and minimize possible threats. CARE has been working and continues to work in the proposed project regions and has successfully engaged local communities to mitigate against access/security risks. The organization has strong community acceptance in the target locations which will enable successful implementation of proposed project. However, competing interests from both local administrations and community leaders, Land tenure, disagreement on project resources, procurement processes, and project participant selection can, if not managed properly, contribute to the escalation of conflict thereby create access challenges. Being acutely aware of this and experienced in operating under such conditions, CARE will use a conflict sensitive approach to mitigate such risks.The following key mitigation steps will be employed; •Agreement on a common approach to project participant and site selection across all project areas•Thorough involvement of communities throughout the project cycle so as to increase local ownership of the project•Deliberately involving minority groups, person with disability in protection design, beneficiary selection, implementation and monitoring•Engage skilled national staff from project areas with a high level of community acceptance and who fully understand the dynamics of conflict and access challenges in the target region.•Carrying out regular security assessment to to mitigate risks
