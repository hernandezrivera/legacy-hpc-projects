Project Summary

In line with Cluster guidance, the objective of this response is to save and sustain lives through the provision of timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. PIN plans to provide different shelter and NFI kits to meet the priorities and different needs in North East Syria, notably distributions of Sealing-off & partition kits (SoK) and emergency shelter kits, kitchen kits Winterization kits/cash. PIN will also contribute to sector SNFI sector objective - Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure through repair/rehabilitation of housing and related community/public infrastructure. PIN’s response will be grounded in needs assessment data, contextual understanding and scenario planning. PIN will target locations based on needs assessment data, highlighting the most critical needs, vulnerabilities and gaps. In areas where security, access, and market functionality permit, and needs assessments indicate cash-based approaches would provide a more appropriate response, PIN may shift NFI/Winterization assistance to value vouchers to assist the targeted population. Value voucher will only be provided in cases where needs assessments show that NFI/winterization items are priority needs, to mitigate the risk of these being used for other purposes and where possible, PIN will strive to provide them together with other aid such as food assistance under complementary donors to ensure that all key priorities are met. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide most needed assistance to such groups through: Employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct need, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to priorities inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border, to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021 draft, the overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, witha corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people. Of this,3.8 million (69 percent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areasare concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the northeast.Recent displacements in both North West and North East Syria put increasing strainon already over-stretched and limited resources, including shelter access. Based on NWSSitrep (OCHA, 2020) currently, almost half of those newly displaced are living with hostfamilies and rented houses while 32 percent are living in camps or individual tents. Inparticular, the winter weather is adding another layer of urgency to the situation withflooding frequently affecting camps and informal settlements and many families seekingshelter in unfinished buildings. With the ongoing displacement, concomitant lack ofshelter options, and the sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement,protection risks have increased, specifically related to gender-based violence (GBV), childprotection (CP), and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in the IDP sites andmakeshift shelters, in addition to a lack of WASH facilities, will put women and girls atparticular risk for GBV. Evidence overwhelmingly demonstrates that households resort toincreasingly negative coping strategies to meet basic needs whilst preparing for thewinter – including reducing food intake precisely at a time when increased calorificconsumption is required to cope with the cold conditions. PIN found a significant drop inFood Consumption Scores across beneficiaries during the cold winter months.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed PIN’s response under the Shelter/NFI sector will be implemented to save and sustain lives by providing timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. PIN will also aim to support more sustainable housing solutions through improving housing and related community infrastructure. PIN plans to conduct the following activities: Sealing-off & partition kits (SoK) and emergency shelter kits will be provided to households in informal settlements, collective centers, and damaged returnees houses received any shelter assistance and live in inadequate shelter conditions. SoKs will provide thermal comfort and protection from the climate and ensure privacy, safety, health and enable essential household and livelihood activities to be undertaken. The emergency shelter kit will address acute shelter needs in camps and informal settlements; both in response to first phase displacement and prolonged sub-standard shelter situation in camps and informal settlements. The kit is made of materials that enable a more flexible use and can be re-utilized by IDPs in case of multiple displacements or return to their place of origin. New arrival & kitchen kits will be provided to IDPs, winterization kits/cash will be provided to IDP and returnee households in pre-identified camps, informal settlements and housing units during the winter of 2020–2021. Cash handouts for winterization are particularly efficient and effective since they empower people to make decisions regarding their priority needs; whilst reducing delivery time and costs. Selected beneficiaries will be supported with light and medium repairs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feeds into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1) MM: needs based targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; use of Washington groups Questions (WGQs) and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by theLCs.; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, wide advertisements and communication channels for CFRM, 2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs.3) R:PSEA, 3)MM: Trainings of all staff; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating thatassistance is free. 4) R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: Internaldistribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with Security teams for timingand place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered waiting areas, with seating (if possible); Different queues for men/women, PIN home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties if needed; kits are grouped to lighter boxes to facilitate easier transportation. 5)R: Shelter infrastructure is not accessible to all/safe, 5)MM: Health facilities are rehabilitated to be safe to use for people with disabilities (platforms, ramps) pregnant women, children and elderly,

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch toremote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff,offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding byarmed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. Ifspecified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry outinternal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, informrelevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek torelocate the intervention ;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line withprinciples. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize onprinciples of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to othertarget areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe andprincipled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy withlocal authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums,and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. Ifsuch efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acuteneeds; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If bordercrossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods,PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitateaccess; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming.
