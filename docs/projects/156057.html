Needs

Unexploded remnants of war (ERW) remain a critical threat across Syria. Especially as returns to newly retaken areas mount, explosive remnant contamination is an increasingly urgent threat, estimated to affect almost half of all people currently in Syria (HNO 2018). Explosive devices are commonly hidden within household items such as fridges and children’s toys, heightening the risk to civilians. Children are a particularly at-risk group due to their tendency to investigate interesting objects. The full scale of contamination across Syria is not fully known; for now, mine clearance operations are restricted to military actors within Syria, slowing the pace of response. Deaths of civilians as a result of ERW incidents continue to be reported, for example those of 5 children in Rural Damascus in December 2018. However, the scale of the problem is likely under-reported. Non-technical survey (NTS) and explosive ordnance removal (EOR) is a critical need to decontaminate areas and remove life-threatening risks to civilians. While threats remain, risk education is essential to increase the capacity of at-risk populations to self-protect. Particular target groups are agricultural workers, those who regularly travel long distances, and children. Boys and men are a particular at-risk group, with evidence suggesting that 85% of ERW victims are male.

Implementation of activities

DRC/DDG will deliver training of trainers (ToT) to teachers registered with the ministry of Education (MoE). Facilitators will be recruited and trained by DRC/DDG/DDG, both technically and in pedagogical techniques appropriate to different age and sex groups, including children. Content is also tailored to the specific risks of different contexts in Syria and utilizes standard curriculum materials prepared by the UNICEF and MOE- who have been leading the RE response in Syria, The sessions stress awareness on threats of explosive hazards and reporting with the primary aim of minimizing direct interaction with mines, ERW and IEDs, thereby preventing casualties. Accordingly, adaptive coping behavior is encouraged, including avoiding at-risk areas, and high-risk behaviors (such as letting children play in areas which have previously seen ground conflict) is flagged. These teachers are expected to deliver Risk Education to around 20 children per month. Should approvals from the Government of Syria (GoS) for NTS and/or EOR activities be received, DRC/DDG will recruit field teams to directly implement these activities supported by in-county international experts. DRC/DDG/DGG retains a presence in Damascus and is able to rapidly scale-up operations should approval for direct implementation of RE be granted. Each teacher trained is expected to reach at least 30 children in his/her respective school (indirect beneficiaries). Target locations for this project are approximate and will likely be updated as the project is implemented. This is because DRC is not able to specify target locations for this project beyond the governorate level as (i) teachers for ToT are referred by MoE from a wide range of areas across agreed governorates; and (ii) as approval for NTS/EOR has not been received from the GoS, the scope and target areas for these activities has not yet been determined. Should DRC/DDG receive approval, the target areas would be closely coordinated with UNMAS in addition to national authorities.Discussions on curriculum and modalities for activities will be held with all relevant stakeholders including UNMAS, UNICEF and relevant GoS authorities in terms. Currently all RE would be delivered through ToT of MoE teachers who would then solely target school children. However, given the need to cover other at-risk groups (e.g. agricultural workers, adolescent/adult males who make up 85% of explosive hazard victims in Syria) DRC will also attempt to negotiate approval to directly implement RE with a particular focus on these groups. DRC will also coordinate with other country efforts with Syrian refugees (Jordan, Lebanon etc); we will coordinate with UNMASS, UNICEF, as well as other DRC/DDG county units on this.Youth are explicitly targeted under this project, making up at least 50% of the intended caseload. DRC targets teachers for RE ToT as this group has regular access to children/youth and can deliver RE during the course of formal education. DRC will also liaise with MoE to have teachers who have received RE ToT train teachers delivering the Self-Learning Program (SLP) to out-of-school children and youth under other DRC projects. DRC has highly flexible funding for this project, with the donor open to funds being redirected for e.g. direct implementation of RE, an increased allocation going to NTS/EOD. This will enable DRC to scale up/down and change activities without issue. The main external constraint affecting implementation of this project is therefore approvals.

Protection Risk Analysis

PR1: Risk Education sessions are not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment. Risk: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: sessions delivered directly by teachers who are usually members of the local community themselves. Culturally-sensitive pedagogical techniques taught to teachers by experienced and qualified trainers. PR2: Implementation of NTS/EOR activities creates false impression that areas are safe even if they have not yet been fully cleared to international standards. Risk: High; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: RE focuses on positive adaptive behavior only and encourages beneficiaries to avoid risk-taking behavior, cautioning that even areas which may be commonly assumed to be “safe” could still be contaminated. Communication with key community figures to message that areas are still unsafe until they have been declared cleared in accordance with international standards. PR3: Rejection of DRC/DDG’s presence by local community. Risk: Low; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: ensure that all necessary approvals from GoS are obtained prior to intervention; communication with key community figures for example mukhtar, head of municipality, school principles, to introduce DRC’s mandate and explain nature, scope, and purpose of activities. PR4: Exploitation/abuse by staff or other service providers. Risk: medium; Impact: high. Mitigation measures: clear and regular communication with beneficiaries, including reinforcing the message that humanitarian services are free of charge; regular consultation with beneficiaries through group discussions; operation of complaint mechanism, including regular advertisement of the system to beneficiaries; training to all staff on code of conduct and PSEA; adherence to all of DRC’s global minimum standards and guidelines for protection.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Should any of the planned areas of intervention become inaccessible due to safety concerns, DRC/DDG would consider alternate locations in line with its standard targeting criteria and procedures. 2. Should exchange rate fluctuations between donor currencies and the Syrian pound mean that DRC/DDG had less actual funds available in Syria; and/or, should this project not be fully funded, DRC/DDG may be unable to deliver all activities as planned. DRC/DDG would prioritize those communities in most urgent need of Protection assistance according to verified data from the field. 3. Should all necessary approvals not be received from the GoS to implement NTS and EOD, DRC/DDG would implement risk education only. However, should DRC receive NTS/EOD approvals the large majority would go towards this. if not, we would use these funds to solely fund RE with the back-up option of transferring funds to other DRC/DDG operations in the region.
