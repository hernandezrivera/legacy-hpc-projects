Project Summary

The project aims to fill critical gaps in sexual reproductive health services delivery for conflict affected population and enhance provision of essential quality SR health services according to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for RH standards. The project will target sexual reproductive health needs of women, and men and increase access to quality maternal and newborn care, HIV and STI prevention and treatment, comprehensive services provision to survivors of GBV and SGBV/rape and improve integration of SRH service into primary health care level. The major geographical focus of the project will be at Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts GCA, areas along the contact line, Lugansk and Donetsk NGCA (through project partners).

Needs

As confirmed by the 2018 HRP 2.2 million people in eastern Ukraine are in need of essential healthcare and disease prevention. Needs assessment has shown that damage to healthcare facilities in the crisis affected areas, especially close to the Contact Line, leaves thousands without access to critical services, including SRH (HRP reported a total of 160 health facilities to be damaged/destroyed since the beginning of the conflict). While the continued loss of human resources (staff shortage of up to 80% in some areas doctors specialised in women’s health) and the breaking down of referral systems ultimately adds to the decreasing quality of care and patient’s safety.Moreover, access to the essential healthcare, including reproductive health services is limited by the compromised travelling ability of the majority of the affected population in the areas close to the contact line due to the security reasons, unavailability of the public transportation and very often unaffordability. Women and children (more than 60%) are disproportionately affected by a severe reduction in health services. Pregnant and lactating women continue to face compromised access to reproductive health and referral services, to antenatal care and post natal care and safe birthing practices. This exposes the pregnant women to a high risk of pregnancy related morbidity and mortality. Throughout the last 6 months, health authorities in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts are reporting a considerable increase in the non attended antenatal care for pregnant women and an increase of deliveries outside of health facilities (over 300 cases recently). Anemia rates among pregnant women in affected areas are relatively high (30%). Access and knowledge of supplementation is low. Donetsk and Lugansk GCA maternities providing service to big number of woman from NGCA - 30% of deliveries.There is a strong evidence of a marked uptick in sex work, including among adolescent girls, and an increase in the number of unwanted pregnancies as well as abortions among underage girls in Luhansk GCA (up to 10 cases monthly compared to 1- 2 cases before the crisis for the entire oblast). The deterioration of the economic situation, combined with the destruction of community ties caused by the conflict and displacement, have compelled some people to use harmful survival strategies and coping mechanisms that may increase the risk of sexual violence and trafficking. MHPSS care is largely not available due to the lack of trained care providers and non functioning referral system, as well as legal constraints; health services for GBV survivors, including post rape care, enhanced referral pathways, is either not available or not user friendly. Without access to quality reproductive health services, they face an increased risk of life threatening complications, including pregnancy related morbidity and mortality. The high risk of increased sexual transmission of HIV and STIs in the affected regions adds to the needs in the area of reproductive health. Estimated 32,800 adults in GCA, NGCA and areas of high concern in need of STI treatment. Around 18,000 potentially unrevealed HIV infected in NGCA: every 2nd potential HIV case is not revealed. At the same time, local healthcare authorities report the lack of diagnostic equipment and treatment supplies..Since the onset of the crisis, UNFPA Ukraine is leading the humanitarian response to reproductive health needs of most vulnerable women and adolescent girls, providing life saving supplies and ensuring accessibility of medical services to those in need, in particular in remote areas and near the contact line. However, the budget cuts triggered by the US defunding decision in April 2017 has put an acute pressure on the work of UNFPA in Ukraine in the area of Health. UNFPA has managed to sustain its humanitarian interventions until June 2017 using its limited internal emergency fund resources.

Activities & outcomes

Activity 1: Supply essential medicines, commodities and equipment to provide SRH services (emergency obstetrics, SGBV, STI and HIV prevention:Procurement and distribution of male condoms. A total of 2,000,000 male condoms will be procured by UNFPA (RH Kit 1A). Half of condoms will be distributed by 100% LIFE Network in Donetsk and Lugansk NGCA. UNFPA would target services and NGOs that have outreach to socially vulnerable groups, multifunctional maternity hospitals, and also services for PLWH and population at risk of HIV and STI (SWs, MSM and PWUD) including AIDS centers, Family Planning clinics and STI clinics. Procurement and distribution of RH kit 5 for Treatment of Sexually Transmitted Infections. A total of 25 RH kits #5 (for treatmenf of STIs) would be procured by UNFPA. The kits would be distributed at already existing service provision centers (FP centers, STIs clinics, youth centers, and maternity wards). For quality of STI services, capacity building of service providers will be required in order to make syndromic treatment available as part of routine clinical services for patients presenting for care. Procurement and distribution of post rape kits #3 A total of 20 kits will be procuredProcurement and distribution of RH kits 8, 9, 11A and 11B, medical equipment and disposable for: Management of Complications of Miscarriage; Suture of Tears (Cervical & Vaginal) and Vaginal Examination; Referral Level for RH Reusable Equipment and Drugs & Disposable Equipment. 30% of RH kits will be delivered and distributed among NGCA maternities through Donetsk and Lugansk Perinatal centers (as UNFPA did it in 2016 and 2017). Activity 2: Raising awareness of the affected community on the availability of services.UNFPA will coordinate the service providers and NGOs and other relevant stakeholders to raise the awareness on the available services through production of thematic IEC materials on contraception and safe motherhood and childhood, SGBV and HIV/STI prevention. Activity 3: Syndromic diagnostic and treatment of STIs training for service providers.With the STIs prevalence rate growing, UNFPA has started trainings on syndromic treatment of STIs in order to increase the outreach and make available STIs services (offered traditionally by STIs clinics and cabinets). UNFPA will continue training of ObGyn, include general practitioners and other health professionals during 2019 by conducting 15 of such training. Activity 4: Increase capacity of primary health care level providers in provision of comprehensive prenatal and postnatal service. Training for family physicians on provision of comprehensive care to woman during prenatal (regular examination and tests, anemia prevention, hypertension/pre-eclampsia ) and postnatal periods (breastfeeding support, depression screening, and contraception counseling). UNFPA will conduct 7 of such training.
