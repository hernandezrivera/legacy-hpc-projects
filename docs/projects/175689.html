Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the protective environment for children and access to child protection services in 7 counties of South Sudan that have been categorized as catastrophic or extreme. These include; Pibor, Yei, Morobo, Kajo-Keji, Rumbek Centre, Torit and Kapoeta East and will target 60,000 beneficiaries (16,848 girls, 15,552 boys, 14,352 women and 13,248 men). Consisting of most vulnerable communities (including children with disability - 15% of the target beneficiaries), IDPs and returnees.
Plan has been operational in all these proposed locations and currently implementing CPiE projects. The main activities will include provision of comprehensive case management services (including cash support for most vulnerable cases) and referrals; identification and registration of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and provision of family tracing and reunification services (FTR). Plan will work closely with the UASC working group, SCI and UNICEF and where possible ICRC on FTR. Remote and static psychosocial support to children and their caregivers, and community based mechanisms approach including radio messaging, door to door awareness raising on key CP issues/concerns, available CP services and trainings on CPiE.
All these interventions will adhere to Covid-19 measures and guidelines. Plan will adhere to Case Management adaptations guidelines for Covid-19 and MHPSS guidelines for Covid-19 response.
Further, the projects will be integrated with Nutrition, Education, GBV and Food security which are also ongoing sectors within Plan. E.g dissemination of key messages that prevent child labour or family separation during food distribution as well as setting up child help desks in Schools and Nutrition sites.
Plan will coordinate with the CPWG at the field level as well as the CPSC at the National level to ensure effective delivery of CP services. As a co-lead in Pibor and Yei CPWG, Plan will continue coordinating with National NGOs as well as International NGOs and maintain close relationship with Ministry of Gender Child and Social Welfare. Plan will intentionally include NNGOs (IPCS, GREDO, CHADO) staff in Case Management, PSS/PFA trainings, awareness, advocacy, assessments and coordinated referrals.
Through its interventions Plan will adhere to centrality of protection by reducing the threats and exposure to harm on boys, girls, men and women. This will be achieved by working together with other sectors to ensure a holistic protection approach, working with community members to understand local strengths & solutions to protection issues, coordinating with other agencies and Government and observing the principle of do no harm.
Plan will also have a sustainability or transition plan to ensure that in all areas of operation, there is sustainability of the gains achieved. This will be through working with community based organizations and structures and building their capacity.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO, 8.3M people are in need. Of which 54% are children. In 2020, communities were hit hard by intensified conflict, major flooding and the impact of Covid-19. Children and adolescents continue to be exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups (3,207B, 578B – HRP), family separation (12,506G and 14,342B – CPIMS Feb, 2021), psychosocial distress, sexual violence and exploitation. Women and girls continue to face extreme levels of GBV & PSS distress. The Covid-19 outbreak and measures put in place exacerbated this situation; the restriction of movement has led to difficulties in accessing critical CP services e.g about 2.4M children are out of school (HNO) as at 2020. In addition, people coping mechanisms have weakened due to the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative coping mechanisms e.g. child labour and forced/child marriages leading to more than 355 teenage pregnancies (radiotamazuj). Pibor is IPC 5 (catastrophic) with multiple protection concerns compounded by inter-ethnic and age set fighting amidst Covid-19. There are 202 boys and 169 girls in need of case management services including FTR (CPIMS). As a cultural practice, child marriage and child abduction is rampant (Joint Partners CP Rapid Needs Assessment for Jonglei, 2020), followed by domestic violence, especially among adolescent girls. Boys and male youths are engaged as combatants in the conflicts. Based on our observations, due to the catastrophic food insecurity ongoing in Greater Pibor, children are engaged in harmful child labour, child neglect from caregivers and communities in PSS distress. GBV and gender inequality is also persistent in Pibor due to negative cultural practices. In 2020, Pibor witnessed increased violence against aid workers preventing vulnerable communities from accessing services. Moreover, during the rainy season, roads become impassable rendering humanitarian assistance very challenging. Yei (including rural Yei): Plan conducted a Multi-sectorial assessment (Dec.2020) and FGDs with children revealed that hunger (21%), physical violence (17%), lack of basic needs and services (8%), death of relatives and neighbors (8%) and also Covid-19 are the main cause of PSS distress. Based on our CPIMS caseload, 144 boys and 235 girls are in need of case management services including FTR (12 UASC). In rural Yei 100% of the FGD participants said that girls got married at a 14-17 years. 67 of KIIs said there was SGBV and child labour ( farming and domestic work) cases in the communities. As Yei is on the border, there is a high chance of influx of refugees’ returnees and IDPs from Uganda/DRC and other neighboring counties respectively. Thus, creating pressure on the already limited resources. Kajo-keji – According to FGDs with adolescent boys and girls aged 10-18 years (Dec.2020), lack of services – including CP services (25%) is the main cause of PSS distress among children, followed by lack of food, physical violence by family members and closure of schools each at 14%. Child labour due to Covid-19 was also witnessed. Morobo – FGDs with adolescent boys and girls indicated that hunger (19%) and physical violence by family members (15%) were the main causes of PSS distress among children. Other CP violations reported included child labour, recruitment and use of children and child marriage for girls aged 14-17 years. Torit – As of 2018, Plan reached 84 (42G,42B) children through case management interventions and 1,367 (599G,768B) through PSS activities. The CP needs remain very high as only Plan is implementing CP. Further due to Covid-19 pandemic, there is increased CP abuses, child labour, teenage pregnancies (over 49 cases). Kapoeta East – As of January 2021, Plan has registered 120 (73G,47B) for case management services, and 2,522 (1236G,1286B) attending PSS activities. There are a lot of child marriage cases with over 12 teenage pregnancies due to Covid-19. Few CP partners on ground.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

1. Plan will conduct a 3 days’ trainings to all staff on PSEA and Safeguarding. Afterwards they will sign an acknowledgment form committing themselves not to abuse beneficiaries.2. Also, before conducting any activities, Plan staff will provide orientation on PSEA/Safeguarding to beneficiaries and also where to report in case such an abuse occurs3. Printing and dissemination of PSEA/Safeguarding messages and posting them in community centres, police, market, near boreholes so that beneficiaries are reminded of what and where to report.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through community meetings and FGDs, beneficiaries who cannot write, will be able to provide their feedback as they will be allowed to talk and express themselves freely. Also, persons with disability will be intentionally targeted as part of the feedback mechanism. The FGDs will be gender-disaggregated, men and women separately and also boys and girls separately.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

1. Plan will hold a kick of meeting at the beginning of the project. The meeting will include key leaders and community representatives. Where they will get all information about the project targets, objectives and activities.2. Awareness raising about the project objectives and activities will also be conducted to sensitize the community members.3. We will also conduct quarterly review meetings jointly with beneficiaries to review the progress of implementation/achievements incorporate any feedback to ensure improved programming.4. Through FGDs with adolescents in the CFSs Plan will be able to gather information on how we are conducting our activities and also make changes where there is need of improvement.5. Setting up of help desks in our CFSs, Nutrition sites, during food distribution and in Schools where community members can report any issues relating to project implementation.6. We already have a hotline in all locations targeted that beneficiaries can call in case of any complaints on how we are implementing our projects. The complaints are registered and feedback is provided on a monthly basis. 7. In schools, Plan has set up suggestion boxes to enable students and teachers report any complaints they may have. These are checked every month and feedback provided to the schools.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Based on the feedback provided through FGDs with children or CBCPNs, members or community review meetings, Plan will integrate them into project activities to ensure effective project delivery. For instance, if the CBCPNs suggest that their need megaphones to enhance their work, Plan will consider to procuring the megaphones.
