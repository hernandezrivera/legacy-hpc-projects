Project Summary

"The Government of Bangladesh endorsed two sets of frameworks comprising one relating to refugee vocational skills development and the other to refugee volunteering services within the camps. The National Skills Development Framework for Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities opened space for building the capacity of refugees by developing skills which are portable, commensurate with livelihood opportunities in Rakhine State and in support of their voluntary return and sustainable reintegration in Myanmar.
Mukti Cox's Bazar is targeting 500 beneficiaries where camp-400 and host-100 individuals for LSDS sector in 2025. Under this project, Mukti will facilitate training on Accelerate Adult Learning such as skill development training on handcrafts production and linkages with relevant stakeholder, development of skills on demand driven and marketable trades for youth employment and Youth led Green enterprise development (Zero waste management/Reduce, reuse and recycling for enhancing green environment and Youth employment) . Mukti Cox's Bazar has a plan to provide support under this project Refugee-400HHs and Host-100HHs including youth, disability and new arrival refugee population and will cover refugee camp- 1E, 13, 17, 19, 21 and 22 and also from host community union Palongkhali under Ukhiya and Hnila under Teknaf Upazila. This project will assist refugees in acquiring transferable and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp contexts in Bangladesh. While this initiative enables refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives in the camps, it will also support sustainable return and integration (in Myanmar). It includes their engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities, and community works, with the utilisation of acquired competencies for livelihood options. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees for durable solutions.
In this project Mukti Cox's Bazar will provide support for skills and livelihood activities for creating income options. It will include 400 individuals from camp for Accelerated Adult Learning and Non Formal Technical training. Project will ensure input support after training to the skill gained host beneficiaries. Besides, the project will provide 100 host community individuals for non-formal training. Under the Non-formal training Mukti Cox's Bazar will cover different trade like Mobil Barbering-50 individual, Bamboo Craft-75, Jute craft-75, Weaving Knitting handicraft-100 and Soap making-100 for camp beneficiaries and for host Bamboo Craft-40, Jute craft-30, Weaving Knitting handicraft-30 individual. The participants of this intervention will be youths and adults, including men and women, others and persons with disabilities from segregated age groups. Project has planned organize market linkage workshop with the concern private sectors for strengthening profitable business of the beneficiaries.
"

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"All food security and vulnerability indicators were significantly impacted by ration cuts in the camps from March 2023 due to a funding crisis, compounded by uncontrolled inflation, foreign reserve crises, and the war in Ukraine. The situation changed drastically, and the findings of the 2023 assessment were alarming. For the first time since 2017, 10 percent of Rohingya households were classified as having poor diets, and 70 percent were in poor to borderline conditions. Additionally, 24 percent of Rohingya households could not afford the minimum food basket, remaining below the food Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) despite current humanitarian assistance levels. There was significant dissatisfaction among Rohingya households regarding the ration cuts. In 2023, overall vulnerability2 continued its upward trend in both the Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities, resulting in a much higher prevalence than in pre-pandemic years (Source: REVA-7). Economic vulnerability among Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar increased by 3 percent compared to 2022. Economic vulnerability among Bangladeshi community households increased to 40 percent in 2023 compared to 30 percent in 2022. Both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households are struggling to secure sufficient food and are frequently resorting to negative coping strategies to meet their food needs. About two-thirds of households in the camps (67 percent) and half of the households in the Bangladeshi community (50 percent) relied on less preferred or less expensive food as their most frequently used coping strategy. The diminishing trend of relying on livelihood-based coping is alarming. In contrast, 50 percent of Rohingya and 40 percent of Bangladeshi community households borrowed money to meet essential needs, while 40 percent of Rohingyas versus 25 percent of Bangladeshis bought food on credit. Both communities reduced non-food expenses, such as education, health, and clothing, to prioritize food needs (Source: REVA-7). In 2023, labor force participation9 witnessed a significant decrease compared to 2022, despite an overall increase in unemployment, as evidenced in REVA 6 (Figure 17). The employment rate also decreased among Bangladeshi communities compared to 2022, indicating exposure to economic shocks within the community. In both Rohingya and Host communities include unskilled day labor, agricultural day labor, and roles as camp volunteers/workers, and salaried employment Rohingya HHs average monthly income of BDT 5,658 and Host BDT 17,674. The lower earnings among Rohingya households were attributed to lower wage rates and fewer available workdays. Particularly at risk are children under 5, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, and adolescent girls and boys. Given the extremely limited livelihood options for Rohingyas, nearly all individuals are vulnerable, each with unique needs and capacities. Therefore, establishing a comprehensive, interagency approach is imperative. This approach should include developing prioritization and targeting methodologies to identify Rohingya individuals and households most at risk of suffering from impacts on protection, nutrition, health, and other essential services in the face of significant reductions in humanitarian assistance (Source: REVA-7)."

2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"Mukti Cox's Bazar always includes protection principle into program and principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid and also mainstreaming in the project cycle. Mukti will continue to promote a culture of accountability to affected populations, prioritizing a community-based approach and age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming to ensure more impactful and sustainable protection outcomes. Mainstreaming and meaningful community participation should be the key priorities, and Mukti Cox's Bazar will ensure that participants arw enjoying their rights on an equal footing and can participate fully in decisions that affect them. Project will take every opportunity to proactively engage with beneficiaries and every stage of the programme cycle must include diverse representatives from within the affected community.
