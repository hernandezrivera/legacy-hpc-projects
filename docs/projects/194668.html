Project Summary

HAA will provide life-saving WASH activities aimed at meeting life-threatening, severe needs of the population of, host communities, IDPs and returnees. This project will be implemented in Akobo, Ayod, Mayendit, and Canal/Pigi counties which was affected by floods, sub-national conflicts, high malnutrition rates, disease outbreaks and GBV threats.
HAA have static presence in these locations with flexibility to address needs across the whole counties, as part of crisis modifier strategy. This project aims to support the provision of access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and aims to reduce gender-based violence-related issues.
Since women and girls are the primary, direct users of WASH services, the project will contribute to GBV mitigation among the targeted population.
This project will implement life-saving integrated WASH, Nutrition and GBV mainstreaming activities, targeting 90,570 individual beneficiaries suffering from inadequate access to safe drinking water, inadequate sanitation and poor hygiene conditions.
The interventions will include but not limited to:
• Provision of WASH Standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to parents /caretakers of children screened and confirmed with/ for SAM in the nutrition centers
• Provision of safe drinking water in the nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM;
• Enabling of vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate;
• Provide gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers
• Provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design;
• Safe water provision through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children;
• Hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns);
• Capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs);
• Distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community;
The activities will include a detailed needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies of the affected population. The main priority is to provide emergency WASH necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH-related diseases, contributing to malnutrition and limited access to water. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected population by ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive and responsive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, including people living with disabilities, etc. With the involvement of our M&E department, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through an existing beneficiary feedback mechanism, to address GBV and protection threats in WASH. The project activities will follow strict COVID-19 protocols and SOPs to avoid any spread of the pandemic

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022), the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. The report notes that covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, such as poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. People’s access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low in South Sudan. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and lack of accessibility to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions, with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E. The COVID-19 pandemic and climatic conditions exacerbate poverty and general living standards for many South Sudanese.The South Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Dec 2022-March 20203 projection indicated acute food insecurity, high levels of malnutrition and persistent trend of prevalence of WASH induced diseases in the floods affected Counties. Canal Pigi presents Population under IPC 5 and a further deteriorating conditions across Akobo, Ayod and Mayendit Counties. The lean season shows acute food insecurity trends showing constantly high levels of acute food insecurity with situation hardly changing in 2023 compared to 2022. Literature review of assessment reports indicate, due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. The Inter-Agency Needs Assessment (IRNA) report 13th - 15th March 2022 Canal Pigi County flagged out the deteriorating Humanitarian situation triggered by floods that resulted into an upsurge of WASH induced diseases; Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Acute Respiratory Infections, Skin infections, and trauma injuries on account of either snake bites, Accidents, and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). The semi-permanent health infrastructures collapsed as such the static health facilities are dysfunctional Equally, Akobo County was severely devastated by the recurrent floods that lasted for 4 consecutive years; 2019 through 2022. The Findings from the May_ IRNA _2022 Akobo County reports indicated that most of the schools, Health facilities, and stabilization centers/nutrition centers were submerged in floods. This has affected the routine service delivery capacities of these facilities. Ayod County is worst devastated by both armed conflict since 2019 and recurrent floods. The WASH infrastructure is limited and only accessible at the County Headquarters. There is wide spread inter-communal violence and most WASH facilities are dysfunctional In Mayendit County, flooding caused forced displacement of 48,291 individuals. The affected populations are majorly from the four (4) Payams of Boor, Maal, Tutnyang and Mayendit HQ located in the South. During the IRNA assessment conducted between 11th -15th Aug 2022 in the area. The 13 (hand pumps) water points available are also submerged in water and silted, 18 Latrines collapsed, WASH NFIs are unavailable, and there’s widespread open defecation

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community consultation and Inclusive Participation: Community Participation and Inclusion: During the project's design, Health Action Africa (HAA) will conduct and facilitate a consultative forum/workshop in each selected County. The participants will be constituted from the local stakeholders comprising the local authorities such as the office of the County commissioners representatives, The County Relief and Rehabilitative Commissions representatives (RRC), County Health Department (CHDS), community representatives, youth groups (males and females), representatives of persons with special needs, and humanitarian partners. This forum/workshop allows women, men, boys, and girls from various backgrounds to participate in meaningful programming on common issues affecting the vulnerable persons facing persistent health insecurities. Communication, Information sharing and transparency: The project will ensure all persons of concern, women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds, have access to timely, accurate, and relevant information on (i) their rights and entitlements and (ii) the project activities. HAA will also communicate and share information in languages, formats, and culturally appropriate media for and accessible to all community groups through health promotions and campaigns. Feedback and response: HAA will adopt the tools from the Community engagement and Community working group for effective feedback mechanism. These tested tools are the community score cards. The Community scorecards will moderate reviews, allowing people of concern to provide formal and informal feedback. Information will be received and responded to systematically, taking corrective action as needed. Health Action Africa (HAA) will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels accessible to all persons of concern and appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. HAA will allocate human and financial resources to ensure that input from persons of concern is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner and collaborate with partners in feedback referral and response processes (wherever appropriate). Organizational learning and adaptation: Interventions, planning, priority setting, course corrections, and evaluation are informed on an ongoing basis by the views of persons of concern. HAA will learn from continuous engagement with communities of concern and adapt interventions and programs in response to new knowledge gained through community participation and feedback, both in the short and long term
