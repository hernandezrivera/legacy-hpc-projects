Project Summary

The lack of proper and sufficient follow-up of vulnerable children and the inadequate capacity of the state services (MINAS) still constitute a big challenge to their care. Many of the MINAS social centers are located several kilometres away, which affects refugees’ access to social and protection services. The social workers working in goverment social centres are also unable to reach refugees' sites as there are no available transportation means. Furthermore, most of the social centers have insufficient qualified personnel to support the population in need, with approximately 1 staff for 377,637 people. One of the consequences of these gaps is that refugee children are not meaningfully reached by the protection activities carried out by MINA’s social centres. Moreover, with the exception of the village of Mbai-Mboum, most of humanitarian interventions in the North and Adamawa regions are focused on the refugee sites which only host 30% of the total refugee population, leaving the other 70% living in off-sites without assistance. The decrease in funding and unbalanced services provision jeopardize children protection and increase the violation of their rights.
The situation of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) is still of concern in the East, Adamawa and North regions, in particular in the North where ICRC is not present due to insecurity. Although family tracing and the IDTR process are already established and continue with the support from ICRC and UNHCR, the number of reunified children remains limited.
From January to October 2020, only 211 birth certificates were issued by authorities via late procedures. Despite the efforts made by IMC to increase children’s access to birth certificates, birth registration remains a critical issue due to, among other reasons, the lack of registers and other needed equipment as well as the insufficiency of qualified secretaries at civil status centers.
To address these gaps in 2021, IMC plans to support targeted communities and empower them through capacity building to enable them develop resilience capacity local child protection mechanisms. IMC will also continue working with line ministries especially the Ministry of Social Affairs (MINAS), and relevant humanitarian actors in order to enhance existing coordination platforms and synergies towards child protection.
Since the outbreak of the Central African crisis years ago, IMC has been addressing GBV concerns in East, Adamawa and North regions through the GBV prevention and response interventions in coordination with relevant line ministries including MINPROFF, MINAS and MoH. The overall objective of this project is to intensify actions that contribute to the reduction of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) in targeted communities through a survivor centered approach will beneficiaries full involvement. The project adopts a broader approach based on the rights of women and girls in a perspective of institutionalization and sustainability of action. On the one hand, it aims to increase the government's capacity to coordinate the fight against GBV in all its components while working in synergy with local structures and humanitarian partners. Furthermore, it will aim to have a significant impact in terms of prevention. In this context, the specific objectives of the project are: to engage men, women and young people to address unfavorable social and cultural norms regarding gender-based violence and to promote behavioral change; to ensure the institutionalization of integrated GBV management services; and to strengthen the effectiveness of regional and departmental coordination in order to allow actors to put in place appropriate frameworks, to ensure quality and to respond to GBV. In order to achieve the abovementioned objectives, activities will focus on promoting behave change and provide comprehensive case management.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Child Protection:Facilitate the access to birth certificates: In 2021, IMC will jointly work with BUNEC to provide support to Offices and Secretaries in East, Adamawa, and North regions through a training on the legal procedures for birth registration and the transcription of birth certificates. IMC will also provide office supplies to the civil status centres. Moreover, IMC, in collaboration with UNHCR and the councils, will advocate with BUNEC to facilitate the distribution of registers to civil status centres in accordance with the identified needs. Facilitate the production of birth certificates:IMC will continue to facilitate the issuance of birth certificates by relevant services by providing them with appropriate support.Sensitization activities on birth registration importance and risk of statelessness:In 2021, IMC will continue to support the existing children’s clubs established in 2019 to carry our sensitization on birth registration and the risk of statelessness. IMC social workers will conduct weekly educative talks on the children’s rights and importance of birth certificates with club members. IMC will also involve children’s clubs in sensitization sessions in primary or secondary schools and during the International Day of the Rights of the Child (November 20th)Prevention and response services for children at risk :Provision of case management services for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC): Working in close collaboration with UNHCR, UNICEF and MINAS, and according to the child protection Standards Operating Procedures (SOPs), a case management process will be conducted for all unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in different sites and 100% of identified UASC will be assisted. IMC Social workers will conduct best interest assessments (BIA) for all UASC and other children in need and their specific needs will be addressed through an individualized management plan. IMC plans to conduct Best Interest assessments (BIA) for UASC and other vulnerable children over the project period. IMC will continue to closely work with existing community-based protection structures such as parent committees and protection committees in the identification, referral and mediation for children at heightened risks or victims of abuse and violence. Joint supportive supervisions:IMC will continue to organize, in collaboration with UNHCR and MINAS, joint supervisions in the social centers. One joint supervision will be conducted at the end of the year in the social centers GBV Prevention and Response:Reinforce existing protection committees:In 2021, in Adamawa and North, IMC will revitalize and train the sixteen existing women's committees and husband schools in targeted localities. Outreach for GBV prevention risk mitigation:To improve outreach, with the support of IMC’s GBV Coordinator, IMC community mobilizers with volunteers will develop sketches and image boxes on topics such as: forced marriage, rape, parental responsibility, girls' education, the effects of sexual and physical violence on children, the importance of personal hygiene, self-esteem, women's leadership, gender roles, and positive masculinity, etc. Provision of psychosocial counselling and case management services to GBV survivors:In 2021, IMC will continue to provide psychosocial support to GBV survivors in the targeted areas in the East, Adamawa and North region. Provision of support services to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls:Women and girls who will benefit from these dignity kits will be identified by IMC social workers, based on a vulnerability criteria, with the technical support of GBV Supervisors. Community mechanisms to prevent and protect against GBV wil be established and made functional at the local level. IMC will also strengthen the capacities of community-based structures and associations to set up community mechanisms for conflict prevention and management.
