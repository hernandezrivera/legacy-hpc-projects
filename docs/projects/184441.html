Project Summary

According to FSNAU report, many parts of Somalia are experiencing prolonged drought conditions and water shortages since June, with thousands of people affected, according to the WASH Cluster. In Puntland, locals particularly in the coastal areas of Bari, Nugaal and Mudug are facing water shortages. Drought conditions have reportedly affected nearly 36,000 people in Dangoroyo and Qardho district in Bari region, with local authorities appealing for humanitarian assistance. A double climate disaster in April and May 2021 aggravated the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. More than 80 per cent of the country is facing moderate to severe drought conditions when the gu rainy season began, in late April. The rains did little to alleviate the conditions; instead, they triggered a new wave of severe flooding that affected 400,000 people in 14 districts including districts in Hiran and M/Shabelle. Thus, in this project, AADSOM will provide emergency and sustainable WASH services to the crisis affected IDPs and host communities in Puntland and Hirshabelle states of Somalia, targeting around 28,000 crisis-affected populations who have not safe WASH services. At the inception of the project, AADSOM will hold workshops to the prominent community stakeholders including women, disabled persons, marginalized communities, and IDPs to familiarize them the modalities/objective and roles such as project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement, and roles. Side meetings with girls, boys, and women will also be convened to ensure their different needs are addressed. AADSOM will present the project documents to cluster members to avoid overlaps, following the cluster discussions. The project will reduce people’s needs, risks, and vulnerabilities and increase their resilience, to achieve over a period of 3-5 years. They are designed to strengthen coherence and complementarity between humanitarian and development efforts, and ultimately aim to reduce needs and vulnerabilities to a point where humanitarian action is no longer required. In this project, AADSOM will:
(1) Provide lifesaving safe water trucking to 2,000 IDP households in Bari and Hiran & M/Shabelle regions, benefiting 12,000 men, women, girls and boys including disabilities and most vulnerable households who have no access to safe water for a period of 2 months; as an exit strategy, the project will rehabilitate 2 boreholes, 8 berkeds and 16 shallow wells; conduct training to 26 WASH committees and 52 water sources' operators aimed to keep the sustainability of the water sources and increase the community ownership, as cross cutting, the community training will include DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms; conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases. To reduce the walking distance of water fetching for women, children and disabilities and avoid the risk of GBV against women and girls, the maximum distance of the water sources will be 500m to the settlement.
(2) In order to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls and discourage open defecation, AADSOM will construct 200 gender sensitive and flood proof latrines including 20 latrines with disabled people, and 6 twin blocks institutional latrines with hand washing basin with equal access to men, women, boys and girls including people with disabilities and marginalized communities.
(3) AADSOM will carry out hygiene promotion and COVID-19 prevention and protection campaigns participating women, men, boys and girls in all groups to reach 28,000 people. 30 CHPs who are trained through this project, will carryout the HP and Covid-19 awareness campaigns. Finally, AADSOM will distribute 2,000 HP kits to 2,000 IDP HHs who are at risk of AWD and/or HHs with malnourished children and pregnant & lactating women.

Conduct two workshops to 200 (100 men and 100 women) community stakeholders including women, disabled people, and minorities to consult and familiarize them with the project objectives, activities, feedback, and complaint mechanisms for Accountability to the Affected People (AAP); 2. Provide safe drinking water to 2,000 households for a period of 60 days, representing 12,000 men, women, and children in flood-affected vulnerable and underserved communities including people with disabilities and minorities through water voucher, each household receives 45 liters/day (7.5 liters/day/person) in accordance to SPHERE standard. 3. Conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination, and routine water quality testing to the 226 water sources to reduce the AWD/cholera diseases. 6. Construct 6 twin block flood-proof and gender-sensitive institutional latrines with lockable doors and hand-washing points at health posts and schools benefiting around 3,000 (705 men, 675 women, 795 girls, 825 boys including disabilities). 7. Construct 200 flood-proof and gender-sensitive IDP latrines with lockable doors and hand-washing points including 20 latrines designed to be accessible for disabled people, benefiting around 6,000 (1350 women, 1410 men, 1590 girls, 1650 boys including people with disabilities). Conduct training to 20 CHPs (10 women and 10 men) on hygiene promotion practices and COVID-19 mitigation procedures using WASH cluster and UNICEF manuals. 10. Distribute 2,000 HP kits to 2,000 IDPs who are at risk of AWD/cholera diseases and malnutrition. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

(1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: Mitigation measures: During the registration of the project beneficiaries, the local authorities, community leaders, elders, women groups, and minorities will select the project beneficiaries with the guidance of AADSOM through the agreed selection criterion; the distribution location will be near the beneficiaries' settlements; the distribution will be done in the presence of the community stakeholders. 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities.Mitigation measures: AADSOM will ensure that the marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes during the project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. To meet the specific needs of women separate meetings will be held with the women and girls respectively facilitated by female staff. Targeting is a joint process involving communities representatives. During the sensitization meeting, action plans will be drawn with a clear division of roles and responsibilities. The capacity building of the beneficiaries is planned to empower them to participate fully in the implementation process. Local vendors will be given priority for contracted works and supplies and the project will promote participatory monitoring and monthly feedback to all stakeholders to enhance accountability. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Mitigation measures: AADSOM will ensure that the project implementation process is accountable and transparent to the community and to the stakeholders including women, people with disabilities, and underserved communities. One of the ways of achieving this is through the establishment of a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of the project services. The key objectives of AADSOM’s feedback and complaints mechanism include: Recognition and protection of the beneficiaries’ rights; Preventing and mitigating risks before they escalate; Building and maintaining good relations and dialogue with the community; Promoting accountability towards the stakeholders (beneficiaries, local authorities, donors and partners); Identifying weaknesses and areas for improvement in the service delivery. The complaint mechanism contributes towards creating a safer environment for the most vulnerable members of the community as well as increasing their voice during the implementations of the project. AADSOM complaint mechanism is accessible to everyone and beneficiaries are mobilized regularly. In addition to using the feedback and complaint mechanism, the project shall deliberately involve the beneficiaries in the implementation cycle. IDPs and host community leaders, elders, women, men, religious leaders. Local stakeholders, local authorities have specifically been consulted during the project implementation cycle. Sensitization and coordination meetings will be held before and during the project implementation with the local authority and communities in targeted locations to create awareness on project objectives, activities, target locations, expected outcomes, scope, and type and selection criteria for beneficiaries and beneficiary entitlements.
