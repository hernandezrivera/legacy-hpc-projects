Project Summary

The project will address human resources required for coordination and information management to enhance health sector coordination for equitable health response for people in need, with the emphasis on strengthening protection and increasing access to sustainable and resilient health services in Syria. It also covers the sustenance of health sector coordination mechanism at country and hub level. The project will ensure effective and efficient carrying out of health coordination core functions. The cluster plays vital role in collecting, analysing the data and production of information products. The main interventions of the cluster will be supporting service delivery to the people in need, mapping of partners presence (4Ws), information management, health needs assessment, informing strategic decision of HCT, developing HNO and HRP for the health sector, advocacy on behalf of cluster members, contingency planning, monitoring and reporting on monthly basis, and ensuring accountability to the affected population.
The cluster coordinate work of various technical working groups and task forces, including COVID-19, advocacy and communication, MHPSS, NCDs, trauma and disability, community health workers, dialysis, tuberculosis, referrals, sexual and reproductive health and health information systems.
The project is aims to maintain coordination structures at Whole of Syria, central and hub levels. Whole of Syria health cluster coordination plays a vital role in coordinating all the hubs and unifying and integrating the data and advocacy cross the hubs.
The coordination structure exists at Whole of Syria, Damascus, Gaziantep and the hubs (Aleppo, Qamishli, Deir ez Zor, Homs and Lattakia).
The effective coordination at all levels will ensure quality delivery of health care to 12.4 million people in need of assistance in all the governorates.

4 - Needs

There is dire need to maintain and support the health sector coordination structures at Whole of Syria central (Damascus and Gaziantep) and hub level (Homs, Lattakia, Allepo, Deir ez Zor & Qamishli). The coordination plays important role in ensuring timely, effective and predictable response to the health needs. WHO aims to develop the capacity of health partners in various areas such as sector severity scale, needs assessment, response and contingency planning, the health resources and services availability monitoring system (HeRAMS), attacks on health care, and the “Who Does What, Where and When (4Ws)” matrix, and information management. The health sector has more than 200 international and national NGOs and observers. Health cluster/sector (HC/S) requires continues support and build up within IASC parameters with focus on provision of lifesaving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance with an emphasis on those most at risk and in need, including cross border and crossline interventions in accessible areas through the designated crossings. HC will continue to strength coordination and health information systems to improve the effectiveness of life-saving health response for people in need, identiﬁcation of gaps and improving health system capacity for support of continuity of care and strengthen community resilience and response to IDP movements and disease outbreaks, planning and monitoring. There is a need for the HS to continue to advocate for the safe and sustained delivery of humanitarian aid to all parts of Syria: 1) securing crossline and cross-border access for supplies; 2) enhancing the protection of medical facilities and and humanitarian workers 3) securing patients’ access to health care.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Mitigating measures include applying the designated de-conﬂiction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conﬂict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, using SSA. Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase. Gender Based Violence GBV: Health services are often the ﬁrst, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. Mitigating measures include: Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a lifesaving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services. Specialized health staff should be trained on providing ﬁrst-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identiﬁcation of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Identiﬁcation of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support. Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. Duty of care: Mitigating measures: Establishing “duty of care” policies detailing clear implementation procedures.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks may be linked to the implementation of some activities and in case of WHO Field Presence Office in Gaziantep to the fact of non-renewal of the UN Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) after July 2021. In this case some of the activities which are continuing beyond July 10, 2021 are at risk. Therefore, WHO Gaziantep hub is working in cooperation with other UN agencies on the way how to identify potential implementing partners to take over some responsibilities as a Transition Plan.WHO’s functions in the areas of health coordination, HIS, technical support and capacity building are very important and don’t involve cross-border movement and not impacted by UNSC Resolution. But what is more important since WHO has been instrumental in providing lifesaving support for cross-border operations supplied almost entirely through cross-border operations from southern Turkey. Thus, it is expected to finalize most of procurements before 10 July 2021. Gaziantep hub is accelerating efforts to pre-position supplies available before July 10, 2021. Similar situation is with the role of Syria Immunization Group (SIG) which is coordinating its operations within Health Cluster coordination and in the light of COVID-19 response it is critical not only for the routine immunization in NWS, but also for the COVID-19 vaccine roll out in NWS.In GoS controlled areas there may be delay in granting visas for the international staff or approvals for the monitoring visits. This will be mitigated by timely submission of the request with proper documentation and advocacy at high level. The sensitivity in crossline communication will be mitigated using Whole of Syria Cluster coordination and by engaging with all the stakeholders transparently.
