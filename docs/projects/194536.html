Project Summary

Concern ensures provision of the Ministry of Health (MoH’s) Basic Package of Health Services (BPHS) to communities that are not covered by Health Pooled Fund (HPF) partners in North Bahr al Ghazal (Aweil South, West and North Counties), Unity State ( Rubkona and Guit Counties) and Kajo-Keji County in Central Equatoria. Concern will support mobile sites that will provide primary health care for outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhoea and coordinate with partners to ensure Concern is targeting those locations where there are critical gaps in provision of basic services. Concern will also support the SMoH and CHDs to provide the EPI services in Health facilities not covered under HPF 3 through seconding one EPI Worker in identified health facilities, to reduce the risks of communicable diseases. This project will be integrated with other Concern’s multi-donor funded programme that will help in coverage of health services is wide geographic area. Concern’s mobile teams will focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas. In NBeG, these are generally remote areas that are more than 10km from fixed health facilities, where the mobile team will conduct weekly outreach services to 10 locations (Aweil west: 05, Aweil North:05). A mobile team comprised of Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and EPI worker. In NBeG State, the project will support 9 static health facilities ( Aweil North : 05 and Aweil South: 04) not under HPF3, Concern will continue to provide monthly incentives to SMoH staff, medical supplies and capacity training to those seconded SMoH staff in Aweil South and North Counties. In Kajo-Keji, the approach will be provision of Health services in 4 hard-to-reach locations where limited services are being offered, either by the Ministry of Health or other Health partners, however this team will work within the framework of existing facilities where possible. In Kajo-Keji based on coordination with County Health Department (CHD), Concern will also continue supporting 3 static health facilities in Kajo Keji County with paying SMOH staff incentives, provision of medical supplies and staff capacity development. In Unity, Concern will continue to support 2 PHCCs ( Guit PHCC and Rotriak PHCC) by paying incentives to MOH staff, provision of medical supplies and capacity building of staff.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate with the number of people in need is growing due to multitude of crises, including the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, continual climate shocks, dwindling economic conditions, and most recently with extreme flooding and spiralling costs of food and fuel due to Ukraine crisis have created a renewed famine risk. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout. Amidst the high level of public health risks and malnutrition crisis, the health system in South Sudan is fragile and is still unable to cope and respond to emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. As per WHO, the government funding for health is low at less than 2% of the national budget, and out of pocket spending accounts for around 54% of total health expenditure. The 2022 HNO indicated that nearly 60% of the functioning health facilities are supported by humanitarian organizations but many of them are close to displacement sites and not accessible by established communities (such as Aweil North). Similarly, only 63% of the population can access health facilities within one hour of walking while 80% reported barriers in accessing health services (including geographic distance, insecurity and gender norms). The women, children, older people and people living with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Communicable diseases are the leading causes of deaths in children under-five, with malaria being a major contributor to the high burden of malnutrition. The weak health system resulted in low immunization coverage, leading to an outbreak of vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles outbreak reported by health cluster HIV infections remain high with estimated new infections at 18,224, of which about 3,500 were children below four years of age (UNAIDS, 2015). South Sudan continues to bear some of the worst health indicators in the world, with Under-five, infant mortality, and neonatal mortality rates estimated to be 98, 63 and 40 per 1,000 live births, respectively, reflecting a heavy disease burden for South Sudanese children . Main factors include limited access to high-quality care in pregnancy, and during and after childbirth, shortage of skilled health professionals and cultural beliefs that prevent women from seeking timely obstetric care. The Health Pooled Fund (HPF) is the largest donor-financing mechanism for healthcare in South Sudan, the funding cuts from the donor resulted in the immediate suspension services provision in number of health facilities in remote communities.

2 - Project activities

The project will implement the following activities to contribute to Health cluster objectives; O1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services: In NBeG (Aweil West, South and Aweil North Counties) Concern will support 10 mobile clinics in remote locations and 9 statics health facilities as well as Boma Health initiatives with 300 Boma Health workers (BHWs). In Kejo Keji County, Concern will support 2 PHCCs and 1 PHCUs as static health facilities and will continue with mobile clinic in 4 hard to reach areas. In Unity, Concern will continue to support 2 PHCCs ( Guit PHCC and Rotriak PHCC) . These health facilities will provide Basic Health packing as per south Sudan MoH guidelines. In all the static health facilities, Concern will second SMOH staff and pay their monthly incentives as per the South Sudan MoH standardized rate. In all the mobile sites of Concern and supported static Health facilities, Concern will provide surveillance report and response to any suspected outbreak immediately, Concern will train and support EPI Vaccinators in their supported health facilities to conduct outreaches in hard to reach areas so as many children are vaccinated as per immunization schedules, the vaccinators shall use vaccine carriers to collect vaccine from CHD cold chain and Concern will continue to support CHD in immunization campaign at all times. Clinical staff shall be trained on Clinical management of rape and they will be referring cases to the partners dealing with CMR and shall keep confidentiality. O2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases: Concern will provide health services through 14 static health facilities (Kajo Keji: 03, Aweil South: 04, Aweil North: 05 and Unity: 02) and 14 mobile clinics (Aweil North: 05, Aweil West: 05 and Kajo Keji: 4) in remote areas not covered by HPF partners. Both and static health facilities and Mobile clinic site will provide health services as per MoH’s BPHS to communities not covered by HPF partners. Concern will provide health services like outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhoea in coordination with SMoH and CHDs. Reproductive health will be part of BPHS provided to ensure attendance by pregnant mothers in remote communities at ANC, skilled birth attendant deliveries and attendance at PNC. Community health activities will focus on improving behaviors that reduce the risk of common diseases and will be linked with nutrition and WASH interventions. Concern’s health teams will continue to respond to scale-up health responses that will be integrated with its nutrition response. Although Concern does not implement specific services for survivors of SGBV and mental illness, it will continue to respond to scale-up a health response that will be integrated with its nutrition response, Concern has a referral system in place and CRM to identify and refer such cases to other Health and Protection partners. SCO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms: Concern will work toward health system strengthening including capacity building and collaboration with CHD. Concern will continue with regular community consultative meetings and ensure equitable distribution of limited resources like health facilities and mobile clinic sites. Complaint response mechanism will be in placed in order to provide timely feedback. Frequent and regular joint supportive supervision visits with CHD will be conducted on quarterly basis. On job, training will be provided to the health workers during the joint supportive supervision while action plan will be shared with health facility in charges for follow up. Concern will continue to hold quarterly stakeholders review meeting in order to review the achievements, challenges and plan for the next quarter.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understands different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at health sites will be used to avoid exclusion of access to feedback mechanism. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, selection criteria for mobile sites and entitlements to receive health services. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the health clinics delivery points.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, scope of services, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. Concern will ensure that health clinic staff is gender balance. Concern regularly coordinate with protection partners and safeguarding/protection issues are referred the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The Boma health committees will be formed to ensure consultation with target community and their involvement in program implementation. Caregivers attending mobile sites and health facilities will be asked to provide feedback on quality of the services and Concern will improve the service delivery according to the feedback from target community. Concern will consult with target community, local representative and relevant government authorities in selection of sites for mobile clinics. The Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcomes and seek feedback for future improvements. The lesson will be documented and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Although NBeG is relatively stable, there is still a high risk of GBV. In Central Equatoria and Unity due to on-going conflict there are considerable protection risks to women and girls including sexual and gender base violence and men and boys including forced recruitment into armed groups. The locations of mobile clinics will be identified in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe areas. The timing of mobile sites and service provision consider that the beneficiates can reach back to their home during day light. Concern will sensitize the community and make sure that the target population is aware about free health service provision, scope of program and staff is gender balance as for as possible. Concern will incorporate conflict sensitivity to ensure that the programme does not have negative impacts on conflict or social dynamics. Consultation with communities will be conducted during the programme and sensitisations on targeting being solely based on medical needs and entitlements to health assistance will be done. This will reduce misinterpretations of the programme and provision of health services. Referrals will be made of protection cases identified during mobile sites or the CRM. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
