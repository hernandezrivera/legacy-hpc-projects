Project Summary

After nine years of conflict, instability and economic collapse, hundreds of thousands of persons of concern to UNHCR are in need of protection in Libya in 2019. In response, UNHCR will improve access to basic needs, services and information through integrated protection assistance at the household and community levels in the west, east and south of the country.

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of conflict in 2011, Libya continues to suffer from the impact of a protracted political crisis, economic decline and intermittent hostilities. Despite a few positive trends in 2019, the humanitarian situation in Libya remains fragile as the absence of governance and rule of law contributes to widespread insecurity and criminality with an increase in abductions, targeted killings, robberies and trafficking. Within this environment of extended insecurity, the population at large is vulnerable to protection threats, especially IDPs and returnees who, it is reported, are increasingly resorting to negative coping strategies to effectively meet their basic needs. According to the HNO 2020, the compounded political, security and economic factors have severely affected the abilities of families to withstand the impacts of the protracted crisis and ensure their basic welfare. Without access to hard currency, a decline in purchasing power due to inflation, low or no income available to meet their basic needs, and exhausted savings during displacement, vulnerable households suffer the most from the effects of the protracted crisis. With absent or broken social networks and institutions, many conflict-affected groups lack the capacity for self-protection and self-reliance. The unstable political context, adverse economic situations, ongoing violence and protracted displacement represent core barriers to building resilience of the affected populations. IDPs and returnees have experienced years of exposure to violence both in their homes and communities, as recently as the Tripoli clashes beginning in April 2019. The trauma of conflict, and hopelessness created by protracted displacement are psychological stressors. UNHCR has identified that that the need for psychosocial support for conflict-affected groups is high.

2 - Activities

Aims to strengthen the protection pathway for the population of concern which will include protection monitoring and information management, protection assessment, information dissemination and awareness, training to mobilize key stakeholders as well as protection response in terms of integrated PSS, cash for protection and community-based protection. Protection monitoring: The protection analysis must be carried out continuously to shape priorities for the humanitarian community. To do so, protection monitoring is an important tool to identify protection risks faced by conflict-affected populations, to inform protection interventions and advocacy. Collect and analyse information relating to physical, material and legal safety encountered by displaced and other affected population; Inform humanitarian programming and advocacy through identification of protection needs, gaps and trends; Generate a response from relevant stakeholders through referral and/or advocacy actions; Refer identified protection cases. Outcome 1: The population of concern has strengthened protection from the effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring, prevention and protection response services (individual protection assistance (IPA), PSS); Outcome 2: Vulnerable households have increased access to basic needs and essential services through the distribution of multi-month cash assistance, and referral of identified individuals with specific needs to essential services; Outcome 3: Conflict affected communities in Libya are more resilient to the stresses associated with population movement (return and displacement). Finally, communication with communities will be mainstreamed throughout the project as both an approach and outcome.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Volatile security context prohibits meaningful access to target locations or country for an extended period, resulting in unmet targets. Due the local context, lack of resources, security and government capacity, there is a lack of specialised service providers in some targeted areas, which may result in less successful referrals, as well as frustration among beneficiaries over the lack of assistance provided. Households refuse to participate in assessments given the lack of corresponding service provision by the authorities and international community. Volatile security context endangers safety of staff and beneficiaries during protection monitoring and cash distribution activities. Duplication due to multiple actors working in the same geographic location. Local and national institutions/ministries revise their regulatory approach to INGO activities within the country, revoke registrations and/or place restrictions on key activities (such as protection monitoring) due to their sensitive nature. Remote management issues

5 - Mitigation measures

Use networks of community focal points which will be able to continue to inform protection monitoring activities remotely should physical access not be possible. Monitors the security context in Libya and advise staff on movement in the field. Maintain country safety plans and will activate these plans as the context requires and will therefore be well adapted to the changing environment. If unable to access a target location for an extended period of time, possible actions include partial or full suspension of some activities, substitution of a new location, and or change in modality of implementation. Ensure protection monitoring tools based on required data and will not collect unnecessary information or register/open cases for which there are no services available thereby risking further harm. Communicate with local authorities and beneficiaries throughout implementation to manage expectations of assistance delivery. Monitor staff movements prior to field activities and will activate their respective security plans ensuring safety of staff. DRC will work with local NGOs in the south to ensure community acceptance and continued access to vulnerable IDPs and hosting households in those areas. Finally, coordinate access to the local areas with the appropriate authorities to facilitate safe movement.
