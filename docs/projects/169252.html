Project Summary

The impact of COVID-19 pandemic, along with the months of protests have caused a major education crisis. Prior to the blast, 1.2 million children have been affected by the school closure. and faced many challenges in adapting to the distant learning modality. The infrastructural capacities, and digital gaps have put strains on the educational system, the parents, and their children in adapting to this new learning modality. The economic crisis with the inflation of prices, have also put a strain on the challenged families to pay the tuition dues for the academic year 2019-2020. Accordingly, this has affected the burdened schools in covering the salaries of teachers who have doubled their efforts in coping with a new modality of teaching. The tragic blast that ripped through Beirut has highly affected the educational system, through causing a damages in a high number of schools and vocational training centers.
Through the “Schools Rehabilitation and Support in the areas of Burj Hammoud, Rmeil, and Ashrafieh” project, Anera will holistically respond to the assessed needs on the education sector through 1) rehabilitating 10 private-subsidized schools with minor damages, and 1 private-subsidized school with identified moderate damages 2) Providing online education support to 200 school teachers and 2,000 students 3) Distributing educational supplies and hygiene kits to 14,400 students. This holistic response approach will strengthen the affected schools, staff and students to eventually break the cycle of vulnerability caused by the various interlocking factors.
Anera, which has always put in place the appropriate means to ensure access of students to high-quality education is well-positioned in responding to the identified educational post-blast needs. Anera’s annual back to school campaigns provide thousands of students with the essential educational supplies. Throughout the COVID-19 pandemic Anera has been one of the leading NGOs in moderating an online educational platform to out of school youths under a project funded by UNICEF, and it has always set the efficient procurement, capacity building, and monitoring procedures as a priority to support its implemented education programs. In this program, Anera has ensured that tutors delivering English classes remotely undergo mandatory certified online trainings to facilitate their access to the remote tutoring modality. In response to the blast, Anera has identified around 500 mildly damaged houses, has repaired 50 of them, and is in the process to cover the repairs of the identified target. Anera’s experience in this shelter project, keeps it readily equipped with the needed technical resources to rehabilitate mild to moderately affected semi-private schools.
This project will be implemented in compliance with the education SPHERE standards, through the guidance of the interagency education sector, and UNICEF’s, UNESCO’s and MEHE’s recommendations.

1 - Needs

Preliminary reports on the August 4th explosion in Beirut from Lebanon's Ministry of Education and Higher Education have identified 120 public and private schools that have suffered varying degrees of structural damage. Damages include broken windows, doors, and fundamental infrastructure destruction. In total, UNESCO estimates that 55,000 Lebanese and non-Lebanese students are currently unable to attend school because of the damages. The blast also left 300,000 people in Beirut and neighboring areas homeless, many of whom have taken temporary residence in shelters established in schools. This adds challenges to the possibility of online schooling for these students, as children may not have consistent access to reliable power or internet. The need to remove as many barriers to education as possible in Lebanon is fundamental to ensuring the rights of the most marginalized communities are protected, especially in the months following the explosion. Lebanon is the highest per capita host of refugees in the world. Since the start of the Syrian civil war in 2011, it has experienced a dramatic influx of Syrian refugees and Palestinian refugees from Syria. The arrival of these refugees, combined with the established communities of Palestinian refugees the country has hosted since 1948, has taxed already strained education systems. Before the explosion and the current pandemic, some 39% of Palestinian teenagers from Lebanon and 64% of Palestinian refugees from Syria left school before their education was complete. Furthermore, pre-explosion estimates put 52% of Lebanese citizens and 83% of Syrian refugees in Lebanon below the poverty line. Education is one of the only avenues that many youth have to break the cycle of poverty that their families have been trapped in. Access to education also combats negative coping mechanisms that disproportionately affect vulnerable refugee and female youth, such as forced, early, and childhood marriage. A recent UNICEF report projected 40.5% of Syrian refugee women in Lebanon aged 20-24 had been married before the age of 18. Anera’s “Schools Rehabilitation and Support in the areas of Burj Hammoud, Rmeil, and Ashrafieh” addresses these interwoven challenges by renovating schools, which provides the infrastructure needed to keep a generation of children from leaving school. The project aims to assist as many of the projected 100,000 children displaced as possible and will reduce strains on schools which are currently being used to shelter a portion of the upwards of 300,000 people total that have been displaced by the blast. Furthermore, the proposed project will cover school supplies (including tablets and internet cards) for students at these schools. The schools supplies are especially needed, given the ongoing depreciation of the LBP and the severe reduction to imports through the Port of Beirut post-blast will make these school supplies scarce and cost-prohibitive for these already vulnerable children. Ongoing telecom and power outages will make online education all the more challenging for students that have already been out of in-person education since March. The internet cards will ensure that students will have consistent access to the internet and will reduce families’ expenses for educating their students.

2 - Activities

I- Schools Rehabilitation 1- Anera’s shelter emergency team which has identified shelters with mild to moderate damages, and has started with the repairs of 50 houses in the same area zones of the affected schools, will be in charge of coordinating with 10 mildly semi-private schools, and 1 moderately damaged semi-private school. The data entry assistant will ensure documenting the damages/ school, and the repairs performed. Anera has pre-identified the moderate damages in the The Wardieh school in Burj hammoud. An assessment will be conducted by Anera’s emergency team by the upcoming days to select the 10 schools with mild damages. The assessment will cover schools in the priority areas: Ashrafieh/ Rmeil, Burj Hammoud/ Nabaa, Sinl El fil, and Modawar. Additional areas such as Bashura, Basta, and Zukak AL Blat, or where needed can be included in the assessment. The assessment tool includes sections covering: Information on teachers, students, and damages related to: physical, structural, equipment, and furniture needs (based on the available budget). The assessment will include identifying 5 schools in need of capacity building training and provision of e-learning support. Upon the assessment results, we will identify the schools in need of equipment, and based on the allocated budget (20,000$)/ school we will cover the rehabilitation, and it will may include replacement of damages. 2- BoQs will be prepared by the emergency engineers teams and will contract pre-qualified subcontractors to implement the needed works3- The contractors will be responsible of repairing the school damages under the close supervision of Anera’s site supervisors who will monitor the process of assessment, procurement, and restoration of the schools. II- Online Learning Modality Support 1- Anera’s education technical advisor will conduct 20 training sessions to 200 teachers selected from 5 affected schools supported with rehabilitation. Each session will be conducted to 10 teachers. The advisor will assess the familiarity of the teachers with the online learning techniques, and will provide the essential training to better equip the teachers with the needed teaching skills. The teachers will take the free certified “ How to Be an online tutor in 24 hours” and " How to Design an Online Course in 24 hours" through “Hamad bin Hamad” online training. The data entry assistant will ensure taking teacher’s attendance, and documenting all the forms associated with every implemented session. The project coordinator will monitor the capacity building program. 2- The students of 5 affected free-subsidized schools will receive tablets and internet cards. The selected number of students/school will be around 400 students/ school. This will ensure 2,000 students’ uninterrupted access to education. The data entry assistant, will ensure recording and documenting the distribution data, and the project coordinator will monitor the access of the students to the educational platform through contacting the parents in a planned monitoring schedule. The schools trained teachers will remotely teach the students on how to use the tablet, and have a Gmail account during the 1st week of the academic year. III- Education Supplies and Hygiene Kits 1- Anera’s high experience in delivering school supplies and hygiene kits, will allow the distribution of hygiene and stationary kits to a total of 14,400 student distributed over 20 schools. The supplies will be delivered to the schools. School administrates will be in charge of contacting and distributing the supplies to the students. The data entry assistant will ensure recording and documenting the distribution data, and the project coordinator will ensure timely distribution of supplies.
