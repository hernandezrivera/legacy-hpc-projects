Project Summary

SEMA will provide quality lifesaving secondary and trauma care in Idleb Surgical Hospital for patients suffering from conflict and non-conflict related injuries, as well as specialty care for management of chronic diseases, major and minor surgeries, intensive care, and emergency services. IRC prioritized this facilitie in collaboration with SEMA due to its established services, high demand in its catchment population, and urgent need to maintain service given limited donor resources. This reflects a strategic commitment to sustain coverage for the most critical medical hospitals currently funded by ECHO.

Needs

The 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that over 13.1 million people need urgent humanitarian assistance inside Syria with 11.3 million requiring health assistance, 4.6 million nutrition assistance. Attacks targeting health facilities have resulted in fewer functioning facilities and healthcare workers, as many have been killed or fled the country. Women and girls, people with disabilities, older people, people with chronic diseases, children and infants, and pregnant and lactating women, suffer disproportionately from lack of access to quality healthcare, as do those in need of mental health and psycho-social support (MHPSS). Civil society organizations have stepped up inside Syria to address these problems, however many lack the organizational and technical expertise to respond effectively.Specifically Idleb Surgical Hospital is considered one of the biggest hospitals in NWS and the biggest one in Idleb city, given the provided services in terms of specialties and scale. According to the most recent needs assessment Idleb city has a catchment population of 700,000 that in dire need of SHC services giving the limited resources of income for people residing in this area, due to eight years of continuous conflict. Furthermore, the hospital is considered as a referral destination from Idleb surrounding countryside, especially for major surgeries , since the hospital is equipped to provided complicated surgeries with high quality outcomes.

Implementation of activities

The implementation of the planned activities will completed by supporting the hospital with needed running costs and providing incentives for the facility staff. Furthermore, the planned activities will be provided by the following departments supported in the hospital:Outpatient Clinics: The OPD includes the following clinics (Orthopedic surgical clinic - Neurosurgical clinic - ENT surgical clinic - Ophthalmology surgical clinic - Urology surgical clinic - Vascular surgical clinic - Plastic surgery clinic (trauma) - Maxillofacial surgical clinic - General surgical clinic) Inpatient Department: Where major and minor surgeries are implemented, in addition to hospitalization of patients. ICU Unit: The hospital contains 6 operating ICU units. ER Department: Where all trauma cases are received and provided with emergency services, then referred to the needed specialty. Laboratory and Blood Bank: The hospital contains a well equipped lab to perform the needed tests in the hospital, in addition to a blood bank that collect blood donations to be used inside the facility. X-Ray Department: The hospital contains a well equipped x-ray department with a variety of different services including Mammography. RH Department: Provides gynecological consultations, deliveries both normal and caesarean, family planning, and gynecological surgeries. Pediatric and Neonatal Department: Provides pediatric consultations and neonatal health care services and equipped with 11 operating newborn incubators.

Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. Furthermore, Idleb city is considered as a conservative community in Syria. In order to tackle these problems SEMA initiated a GBV case management team inside the hospital, to receive referral cases from all departments, especially, the RH department where detecting such cases is rather easy, after implementing several trainings for the hospital staff on how to provide a clinical care for GBV survivors, and on how to detect and refer potential cases. Also, SEMA prepared the hospital to receive beneficiaries with disabilities, in order to provide access to the most vulnerable groups.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SEMA has its own procedures and strategy which aim to prevent humanitarian assistance diversion to members of the armed groups and maintain the humanitarian principles by using the security triangle (acceptance, protection and deterrence). SEMA is committed to “JOP protocol for engagement with conflict parties”. Also, all major stakeholders identified through stakeholder analysis who potentially can affect the project's implementation are consulted regularly and appropriate rapport/ relationship is maintained.SEMA has experience of safely delivering services in high-risk areas. Where risk Assessments are carried out for all operational areas to inform program design and allow appropriate budgeting to support mitigation measures.Over the past period, SEMA has worked on training and building the capacity of the staff in Liaison and Humanitarian Access Department, and a plan for broadening the department is in place.SOPs have been gradually prepared for all key procedures and they have been well understood by staff. These include the prohibition of the presence of firearms and the use of armed convoys. As well as, the Communication protocols including communication trees and incident reporting, in addition to, the securing of sensitive information. Safety management plans are developed for each location that includes the provision of basic safety equipment, the communication and logistic support required; Functioning communications equipment and at least one back-up method are maintained for all offices and operational locations.Contact with staff and all offices are maintained on a day-to-day basis through the use of appropriate communication technology. Training on these has been provided to the most staff. The risks are analyzed, and analyses are appropriately communicated and used to inform the operations department.Clear guidance and measures are in place to protect and secure sensitive and confidential information through both physical and electronic means.In case any emergency incident happens, SEMA staff inside Syria are required to comply with safety and security plan. The organization maintains and analyses an accurate and reliable flow of information relating to the context. In addition to that, all safety and security incidents are reported in writing to the designated authority and partners in a timely manner and followed up appropriately.A plan B for each operated facility was prepared, which is included in a comprehensive contingency plan of all SEMA’s offices and facilities. This includes as well, Hibernation plan, and relocation plan for each facility considering many possibilities might happen. A detailed plan exists of when and how and who will do the relocation if needed.
