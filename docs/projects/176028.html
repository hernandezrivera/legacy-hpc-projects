Project Summary

In South Sudan, an estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, including 313,391 children (163,913M,149,476F) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children (562,697M, 516,170F) with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW).124 In addition, some 21,000 out of 286,000 refugees in Maban, Pariang, Juba and Yambio counties are anticipated, and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition. IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for December 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.1 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the same period 4,000 people were likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, out of which 11,000 were in Pibor Administration Area, and 13,000 were in Tonj North County in Warrap state. Malnutrition needs are greatest in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states where number of acutely malnourished children under-five and PLW caseloads are highest, also with more than 50 per cent of their respective population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity.
World Vision International(WVI) will implement lifesaving nutrition intervention in Warrap, Central Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Upper Nile. The proposed nutrition project will be integrated with health, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Through the 2021 HRP, World Vision will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. The response will expand on nutrition specific interventions, as well as reinforce integration and linkages with other clusters to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional child care and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. It will prioritize areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and displacement coexist.
In 2021, WVI intends to achieve the following nutrition cluster objectives: 1) Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5; 2) Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5; 3) Enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, support returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas.
To materialize the nutrition cluster objectives, WVI will provide lifesaving nutrition emergency interventions through CMAM and maternal infant and young child nutrition practice by targeting under 5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the former Warrap, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria states. The proposed emergency nutrition interventions will be implemented in the communities, at the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Therapeutic Programme (TSFP), and Stabilization Centers (SCs). WVI is requesting USD 2,149,701 to reach 51,049 people for this sector.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict which started since December 2013 in the Republic of South Sudan has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population, and stretched the population’s coping capacity. Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs, this instability limits sustainable development.An estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, including 313,391 children (163,913M, 149,476F) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children (562,697M,516,170F) with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW).124 In addition, some 21,000 out of 286,000 refugees in Maban, Pariang, Juba and Yambio counties are anticipated, and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition.125 In addition, some 21,000 out of 286,000 refugees in Maban, Pariang, Juba and Yambio counties are estimated to be malnourished in 2021, including 4,986 SAM children (2,555M, 2,431F) 12,047 MAM children (6,198M, 5,849F) and 3,957 AM-PLW. There is a slight increase from the 1.8 million people who needed treatment for acute malnutrition in 2020. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for December 2020, an estimated 6.35 million people (52.6% of population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.1 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During the same period 4,000 people were likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, out of which 11,000 in Pibor Administration Area, and 13,000 in Tonj North County in Warrap state. Malnutrition needs are greatest in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazel states where number of acutely malnourished children under-five and PLW caseloads are highest, also with more than 50 percent of their respective population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity.The prevalence of GAM among children under-five is standing at a very high level of 16.2 percent, according to the lean-season FSNMS survey conducted in 2019. The COVID-19 situation, sub-national violence and floods that led to population displacement, coupled with a decrease in coverage/uptake of nutrition services exacerbated the nutrition situation. Globally, it is estimated that COVID-19 could lead to raising of child wasting prevalence by 14.3 percent if actions are not taken. With Support from UNICEF, WFP, GAC, GOG, governments of Japan, Korea, among others World Vision International South Sudan has been able to provide lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions in Upper Nile, Warrap, Central and Western equatorial state, where WVSS is supporting, in coordination with the nutrition cluster, the Ministry of Health, State Ministry of Health, County Health Departments and local authorities, 105 nutrition sites ( OTP/TSFP) and 5 Stabilization centers. In 2021, WVI intends to continue providing lifesaving nutrition services in these areas With this background, in 2021, WVI will implement lifesaving nutrition interventions which will be integrated with health, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Through this proposed project, WVSS will aim to achieve the following Humanitarian Response Plan Specific Objectives: - Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5.- Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5.- Enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, support returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas
