Project Summary

This project is titled: Addressing Cameroon’s Food Insecurity Crisis through an integrated approach to Food Security, Health, Nutrition, and WASH in the South-West region of Cameroon. It will be implemented by a Red Cross with the support of the International Federation of the Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC). The estimated budget is 1,00,000 US dollars for a period of 12 months starting from January 2024 to December 2024. This project will be implemented in the Southwest Region specifically in the Fako division. The project is targeting 10,000 direct beneficiaries and 100,000 indirect beneficiaries. Benefits will be in the areas of Food Security, Health, Nutrition and WASH in all regions. The aim of this initiative is to contribute to addressing food insecurity, health and WASH challenges for internally displaced populations and vulnerable hosts of the Northwest and the Southwest Regions. This project meets the SDG 1 and SDG 2, to eradicate poverty and to end hunger, through the provision of direct livelihoods support to beneficiaries and care for malnourished children under 5. It also promotes SDG 3 to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for the beneficiaries through quality health care for populations affected by displacement (IDPs, refugees and hosts) in the supported area. The project also meets the SDG 5- Gender equality through livelihood support to women making the financially sustainable and the SDG 6 – Clean water and sanitation through WASH training and the construction of boreholes.
Background:
Cameroon is facing a complex and unprecedented humanitarian crisis. Over 4.4 million people need humanitarian support and the food security situation in Cameroon is becoming more serious with more than 2.6 million people needing urgent food security interventions. The country has been heavily affected by three complex crises for several years now: the armed conflict between the Non-State Armed Groups (NAG) and the Regular Army that has intensified in the North West and South West regions (NOSO); the NAG insurgency in the Lake Chad Basin causing an influx of refugees from Nigeria, internal displacement in the Far North region, and the influx of Central African Republic (CAR) refugees in the Adamawa and North regions. OCHA estimates that in 2022, 3.9 million people needed humanitarian assistance. Of these people, 2.4 million were estimated to be severely food insecure over the period from June to August 2022 . Between 2020 and 2021, pre-existing vulnerabilities were exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. OCHA, in January 2019, declared 440,000 people as internally displaced with 220,000 in the North-West and 200,000 in the South-West region and the number keeps increasing as the crisis continues to go unresolved.
Project Goal/Main Objective:
This project aims at supporting the Cameroon Red Cross Society (CRC) in responding to the climate and conflict-induced food insecurity crisis by saving lives, protecting livelihoods, and promoting early recovery of the most food-insecure people.
Specific Objectives:
1. To contribute to achieving food security as a response to climate and conflict induced food insecurity.
2. To improve health, Nutrition and WASH services to the displaced population
Target
This project has as target area is the Fako division Southwest region of Cameroon where we have carried out a number of activities recently and have already trained volunteers and a regional office. It targets 1000 persons for CASH activities, 1000 for farm livelihoods support, 2000 consultation outpatient consultations and 5000 women for nutrition activities. This project also plans to reach 10,000 people with WASH and construct 20 boreholes.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Needs In the conflict-ridden areas of the NOSO, most poor households have already exhausted their food stocks. "As a result of the higher-than-average commodity prices and lower than average agricultural and pastoral incomes, food consumption gaps are widening and the use of unsustainable coping mechanisms (borrowing, sending children away to be fed outside the home, buying on credit) is increasing. Some households are forced, to ensure the daily feeding of their children by giving them out as housemates, nannies, salespersons or making them to hawk on the streets at tender age to provide food for their household. Most important is the fact that most of the rural populations that were involved in farming to meet the food needs of the urban population have abandoned their farms due to insecurity. In addition, most of the farmers are now internally displaced thus further reducing the food supply due to fall in manpower in the conflict areas which served as the breadbasket of the country.14 countries, including Cameroon, have been identified as most at risk of food insecurity. This proposal will go a long way to remedy the situation in an integrated approach the equally cares for health, nutrition and WASH needs in the community. Food Security • Provide agricultural livestock and fishery support to 1000 IDPs and host population • Provide unconditional food, cash transfers/Cash+ to 1000 returnees, IDPs and host community 1000 Health Support epidemic preparedness and response• Put in place epidemic alerts systems. • Enhancing community engagement and adherence to vaccination in affected areas• Train 100 community health workers trained and/or supported for epidemic surveillance. Ensuring availability of essential healthcare in areas affected by the crisis• Consult 2000 patients in outpatient consultation. • Support 10 health facilities Nutrition• Training of volunteers on IYCF counselling• Provide received IYCF counselling / awareness to 5000 women. WASH • Construction of 20 of boreholes • Construction of 20 taps in schools• 10,000 persons reached along awareness raising campaigns or hygiene promotion of good wash practice.Strategy/Approach: The project will have 4 phases which involves: 1) Community engagement and identification of beneficiaries, 2) Training activities for health workers, volunteers, and beneficiaries, 3) Cash, Health, Nutrition, WASH services and material item offered to the beneficiaries and 4) Evaluation of the project and report writing. Key stakeholder and community leaders shall be engaged and made to identify key beneficiaries in of the project based on standard criteria and then volunteers shall be deployed to confirm these persons fall within the specified criteria. Volunteers shall then be trained on cash distribution, CBS EPIC, CEA. Support shall be provided to for interior health centers to carry out massive outpatient consultation campaigns using existing health facilities and workers in the interiors. Basic health materials and equipment shall be provided to these health centers which will accompany the Red Cross in its massive health campaigns. Motivation shall also be provider to the health workers during the campaign days. The last phase shall be an evaluation of the project to ensure it met its target and report writing. As per the IFRC Regional Operations Strategy, household targeting will be based on IDP’s food security status; economic criteria; nutrition criteria; social vulnerability criteria (pregnant and lactating mothers and/or children under five, older persons, chronically ill persons, children or adolescent-headed households, persons with disabilities, internally displaced households). The overall target is 10,000 people IDPs and host population in the South-West-Fako, regions of Cameroon.
