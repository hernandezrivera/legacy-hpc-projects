Project Summary

In 2019, Tonj East (TE) registered school enrolment at about 28,000 out of the 47,010 expected in primary schools (Tonj East and Twic Need Assessment Analysis Report, 2019). Different reasons explain the discrepancy which varies between boys and girls. While boys are co-opted in to cattle rearing and armed groups, girls face the pressure of early marriage, pregnancy, and the burden of domestic work. This’ made worse by cattle raids, food insecurity, and communal fights between rival communities in TE. Assessment report indicate 2500 still displaced in 10 payams (Warrap 1 Village Neighborhood Assessment Catalogue, DTM IOM Displacement Matrix, March 2019). Maiwut and Longechuk are both classified under food security emergency. Over 45,000 returnees were registered in Longechuk in 2019. In Maiwut, armed conflicts have left more than 18,000 displaced and education services severely disrupted. All reports of assessments in TE, Maiwut, and Longechuk document lack of learning shelters, absence of basic school supplies, and dysfunctional SMCs and PTAs.
In TE, World Vision covers 5 out of 11 payams. The five include Ananatak, Makuach, Pagor, Paliang, and Paweng. Local authorities have expressed need for other agencies to provide education assistance in other locations that are in greater need. IOM Displacement matrix documents 3/10 locations having few basic school supplies while the rest have none. UNKEA will select most in-need schools from either payams in Wunlit, Palal, Malual-cum, Maparah, Pannhial, and Mayen. In Maiwut, UNKEA will work alongside Nile Hope (NH) to outstretched locations within IDP catchment populations. This will include Jotome, Olang, and Kigila that are currently not covered by NH. In Longechuk, UNKEA will work alongside CMD to meet unfulfilled gaps that have been articulated by local authorities. UNKEA plans to support the IDP and returnee population in Malual Gahor and Gueng that has no education support despite several children within the population.
This twelve months’ intervention costing $770,420 will target 18 schools with an average of 6 schools in each county. A total of 11,178 beneficiaries, 5030 males and 6148 females will be targeted in locations with greater number of IDP populations. Specifically, 10,800 pupils (5940 girls and 4860 boys) are targeted. A proportion of 4471 IDPs, 3912 host communities, and 2795 beneficiaries are planned to receive project support. The proposed project will reach 3261 in Longechuk, 4406 in Maiwut, and 3511 in TE. Interventions will be tailored to benefit children with special needs. Protection messaging will be critical and shall be integrated in activities within the school environment. UNKEA shall engage nutrition, health, WASH, and FSL actors to contribute complimentary assistance to the targeted schools to obtain optimal outcome and results that benefit majority of children.
The intervention will establish and refurbish 18 TLS, distribute learning and instructional materials to 18 schools, conduct training for 342 school management (162 SMCs) and volunteer teachers (180). As an added component to benefit education, UNKEA will leverage on its existing cash-for-work resources to rehabilitate learning facilities. This intervention also proposes to provide hot meals to 20 schools most affected by food insecurity. A further indepth assessment will be undertaken at the onset of the projects to identify and prioritise locations/schools with highest concentration of IDP populations in order to ameliorate deplorable state of livelihoods. Maiwut and Longechuk has been considered high risk locations of food insecurity projected at emergency levels in the first quarter of 2020. Provision of school meals will provide an enabling environment to ensure learning's unhindered due to hunger. Youth will be engaged to monitor food preparation, storage, and delivery of meals. Regular tracking of pupil attendance will be done in all the schools in order to document project impact.

1 - Needs

Years of protracted crisis in South Sudan has left social services in deplorable state. Education remains most affected with over 2 million children estimated to be out of schools (Report on Out-of-School Children, UNESCO 2018). The combined impact of political conflicts, floods, and food shortage has made it significantly difficult for learning to take place in schools. Girls remain the most disadvantaged due to pressure of marriage, challenge of pregnancy and domestic duties (Nation Wide Assessment Report, 2018). In the most recent IPC food security projections, 5.5 million people are expected to be in food security crisis (IPC Phase 3) and above in the first quarter of 2020, (IPC Phase Classification, August 2019). This is likely to compound the already challenging situation. Comparative evidence from schools with food meals and those without food meals reveal an average of 100% difference in school enrolment and attendance. Schools with meals double their previous records (After action review of emergency school meals, Save the Children 2018). Longechuk and Maiwut are classified under emergency levels (IPC Phase 4) while Tonj East is at crisis level of food insecurity. This challenge is exercabated by disruptive floods and armed conflicts. In the most recent assessments, floods ravaged community and household assets in Longechuk and Maiwut leaving the population to flee to safer locations as IDPs. Floods displaced over 17,000 from 7 locations in Longechuk (Longechuk Emergency Report on Flooding Incident, Sept. 2019). In Maiwut, armed conflicts that erupted in July and Augut 2019 caused displacement of 18,802 (Rapid Joint Assessment, Jekow area Maiwut Aug. 2019.Tonj East has 112 schools. Unfortunately, only 47 schools are currently functioning albeit with limited enrolment due to the above reasons. An assessment conducted by UNKEA indicate an average of 427 pupils out of an expected total of 850 (Tonj East and Twich Needs Assessment Analysis Jan. 2019). Out of 20 sampled schools, only 7 have access to essential school supplies. Even still, the assessment report show that schools that received supplies could not have their supplies last for a complete term. In Longechuk, teaching materials and other school supplies were submerged in water as people fled to safety amidst heavy flooding. In Maiwut all except two schools (Jekow and Pinythor Primary Schools) are non-functional. July/August armed conflicts and September/October floods have made difficult to resume normal livelihoods.Consistently across the three counties, UNKEA noticed remarkable absence of child-friendly spaces, nonexistence or poor state of temporary learning shelters, and inadequacy of scholastic materials. Majority of schools have nonfunctional PTA and SMCs. Absence of teachers due to delayed incentives have further demotivated learning activities in most schools. Volunteer teachers exist but require regular incentives to allocate their time and commit to deliver learning in the schools. Due to fragile and unstable situations, there is weak oversight by the main state/county authorities. Despite the above situation, the general trend in Upper Nile is characterized by returnees from neighboring Ethiopia and Sudan. Since mid-2018, Longechuk and Maiwut have continued to receive high number of returnees due to the prospects of the signed South Sudan peace agreement. The returnee populations have overwhelmed the limited social services in these counties. Learning shelters have been overstretched without commensurate input on erecting of new shelters. There is need to mobilise communities to rehabilitate public schools, provide training to activate PTCs and SMCs, deliver learning materials, and ensure teachers receive requisite training and basic facilitation. UNKEA proposes to focus on these three locations in order to deliver learning in safe and child-friendly environments.

2 - Activities

Several factors explained above threaten delivery of education to majority of children in South Sudan. The proposed intervention will address supply barriers to education and obstacles to demand for education services. Specifically, UNKEA shall deliver the following emergency support services; 1) Construct/refurbish temporary learning shelters in locations with population concentrations. A total of 18 temporary learning shelters shall be erected using majorly locally available materials with additional project support. Six will be established in each county. 2) The project will also provide training to teachers on basic skill packages. UNKEA has a professional teacher whose experience will be utilized to deliver training to all site volunteer teachers in both counties 3) As a basic component for learning, the project will utilize existing education pipeline support to supply learning and instruction materials to 18 schools in the three earmarked counties. Teachers will be guided on how to use and apply instructional materials to pupils. 4) Specific trainings will be delivered to both the primary teachers association and school management committees. UNKEA has training materials that have been tested and used in previous education projects. These shall be reviewed and utilised for training of the PTAs and SMCs. 5) The project will also provide training on protection through child clubs and through teachers who will facilitate continuous support to pupils in ensuring there is follow up within each school. 6) UNKEA shall also provide school meals to functional schools in order to sustain learning and maintain pupil attendance in the schools. Studies have proved that presence of food or meals in schools maintains pupil attendance and improves concentration of learners. 7) As an additional component, UNKEA will use its cash for work programming to prioritize rehabilitation works in public schools - including erecting or refurbishing temporary learning shelters, improving drainage in schools, and waste disposals.In delivering this intervention, UNKEA will work with youth to mobilize communities, monitor school feeding programs, provide volunteer teaching services, and to disseminate life-saving information to pupils and parents/care takers. The project develops and disseminate lifesaving messaging in schools and surrounding communities.Deliberate attempts will be made to support education departments or authorities responsible for education services in the counties. This will include tailored training for focal persons in each county, strengthening coordination mechanisms for all actors and overseeing delivery of standardized contents.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project legally recognizes the rights of the beneficiaries. All beneficiaries have the right to access to education services, to complete information, to be served with dignity and the right to refuse or accept any support provided through this intervention. The project will thus strive to educate the beneficiaries on their rights using the community dialogue meetings. All the engaged educationworkers and project staff are required to sign up to a code of conduct, and are expected to do no harm during provision of services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Displaced affected people are among the key beneficiaries of the project. During project design, the project staff conducted consultation with various stakeholders among whom were the displaced people. Selection of facilities, and outreach sites were guided by accessibility of the services to both the host community and displaced people. All services provided by the project are free of charge and thus accessible by anyone. In addition, the structured and frequent consultative dialogue meetings will ensure that the perspectives of displaced people continue to filter through and guide the subsequent project activities and interventions. UNKEA conducted both formal and informal consultations. The formal consultations included 20 focused group discussions (2 Maiwut, 3 Tonj North, and 2 Longechuk), in-depth interviews with key informants that included commissioners, payam administrators, leaders of women groups, representatives of people living with disabilities, a youth representative and 5 semi-structured dialogue meetings with community members in all the target implementation areas. In total, 600 people were consulted of whom 129 were displaced people.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to government/community systems. This project will be anchored to the county education department. An education focal point person for each county will be trained and equiped to monitor and oversee education related activities in the county. The project will use the standardised remuneration guidelines including teacher incentives rates approved by ministry of education. Training will be provided to teachers and volunteer teachers based on national training curriculum in partial stages. A consolidated attendance and participation of all training stages shall be considered sufficient to award of certificates. This is in line with the national policy on capacity building for teachers/education personnel.The reporting system shall follow guidelines and channels provided for through the government ministry of general instruction and education. In delivery of activities earmarked in this intervention, the project will empower and work alongside the county education departments of respective counties. This is intended to strengthen mandated government agencies in assuming their roles more competently both in medium term and long term.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the South Sudan national strategic plan that prioritizes access to quality basic services to promote education. The main objective of the project is to contribute deliver of education services to vulnerable communities that include IDPs and returnees. The South Sudan national protection policy articulates delivery of such assistance to disadvantaged groups or segments of the population. Further it also pronounces itself on the use of locally resources resources and emphasis on capacity building to youth and community members in order to enable them withstand future shocks and stresses. This interventions shall utilise local based procurements in order to promote productivity in targeted counties. A combination of both human and non-human resources required shall be sourced from beneficiary communities as first prority. The South Sudan national protection policy considers combined approach of providing assistance while strengthening capacity of individuals to increase their productivity and ability to manage future challenges. At conclusion of the project, established structures and local built capacities will remain. Skills gained by the education personnel will continue to serve the population long after the the project is closed.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The recent peace agreement has resulted in some safety and stability providing opportunities for people to return to their homes. For example, large numbers of returnees have been registered in Longechuk and others in Maiwut and Tonj East. Engagements between oppostion and government actors have paved way to allow unhindered access and movement of humanitairan workers and items. Movement options have been widening to enable access to affected populations who are in most need. Local institutions of police and community policing are gaining traction. The buy-in of different protection measures by armed groups even in viotile conditions are conducive for delivery humanitarian assistance. The project has integrated protection within its activities to ensure that no harm is done to the individuals and communities served. At all times, interventions will taking heed of the best interest of the people and ensuring that the people are served with dignity. The project will also benefit from UNKEA's integrated protection approach that mainstreams protection across regular sectoral activities. In Longechuk, UNKEA has already supported development of two (2) protection plans as means of creating an enabling environment for interventions in 2020. Similar initiatives will rolled out in other project locations proposed for this intervention.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project interventions are sustainable and will easily be handed over to the government structures. The project is working to strengthen government recognized structures such as the community education departments, and is also providing incentives based on the recommended harmonized incentive that wasproposed by the government. Joint integrated supervision and monitoring will be conducted and this brings together both the project staff and other government actors. The project is also working closely with the relevant government structures through the MOGEI coordination mechanisms to streamline access to some key school supplies and equipment and that supported schools are included in the MOGEI. The project is strengthening government structures such as the community education departments, structures that are envisaged to take on greater role and responsibility in the future. The project will not implement parallel systems but will strengthen the existing systems such as the HRIS management information system.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNKEA conducted a Protection Risk Assessment with the aim of doing no harm. The assessment indicated a low risk of harm that would arise from inappropriate conduct of staff during delivery of education assistance to disadvantaged populations. UNKEA also acknowledges the risk of not being sensitive to the cultural expectations of the communities during service delivery, GBV from sharing of school WASH facilities, and long distances to available education services that will require girls to walk distant places. The risks will be mitigated through ensuring temporary learning shelters are established in locations with most concentration of targeted population, ensuring availability of relevant stakeholders during consultations, education personnel follow universal precautions at all times, and women/girls are consulted progressively on assistance provided. The project will also ensure that privacy and confidentiality of information is prioritized at all times through provision of filing cabinets, and discretionary options to channel complaints and feedback during project execution. To further strengthen the protection component, UNKEA’s protection specialist will be tasked with conducting regular assessments to identify emerging gaps and propose strategies to mitigate the risks.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will adopt plural communication channels that will include low technology and non-technology options to cater for both sensitive and nonsensitive feedback, and will ensure that the chosen communication channels are relevant and inclusive to even the most vulnerable populations such as women, girls, and people living with disabilities and illiterate people. The Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems (suggestion boxes, notice boards), interpersonal communication through trusted liaison persons, and anonymous calls using the telephone and or email where possible. Feedback will be solicited both in person especially for those people that cannot read and write, but also using other means such as telephone, and writings. In addition, the project will conduct regular systemic assessments of appropriatechannels of feedback as guided by community preferences. Regular feedback/ complaint mechanisms will be incorporated through the project life span; at the schools, suggestion boxes and pupil satisfaction interviews will be used; in the community, quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various community members. Project achievement will be posted on notice boards both at the schools and at the county offices. The project in collaboration with the beneficiaries will identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison people. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the field and also at the organization headquarters. All complaints will be investigated in a systematic method ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times. The project M&E system is designed to capture pupil feedback through thescheduled pupil interviews and qualitative analysis.
