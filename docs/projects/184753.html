Project Summary

Garbaharey district is one of the most affected in the ongoing analysis of nutritional situation in Gedo Region and by extension Somalia. Somalia 2021 Post Gu Seasonal Food Security and Nutrition Assessment indicates that in Jubba pastoral the number of children with MUAC < 12.5 CM (%) is 13.8% and MUAC < 11.5 CM (%) of 3.4% and the child morbidity rates of 27.8% one of the highest in the country. Already, the federal government and the Jubbaland government have declared drought of a serious concern and national disaster which must be confronted with adequate response mechanism.
The project aim is to increase access to basic nutrition services among PLW and children between the age 6 - 59 months in Garbahare district, in Gedo region, Somalia. To accomplish this feat, it will conduct screening and provision of life saving and preventive nutritional supplements to the children and the PLW. Furthermore, facility and community based (BNSP) program will cover both curative and preventative interventions targeting children U5 years (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women.
The project will facilitate identification, referral and treatment of cases of SAM at facility-based as well as mobile OTP teams. All the while, the project will refer complicated cases of SAM to SC and MAM to TSFP. The project will also include routine deworming and immunization services. The organization runs MCHN and provides drugs to PLW at their facility in Garbaharey run by a trained and qualified pharmacist. As such provision of these aforementioned nutritional services will also be overlaid by the strengthened health services at the MCHN which has also been strengthened to cover mild and moderate health complications for children and PLW. Furthermore, the organization has provided access to drugs at the MCHN chemist and entered into agreement with several suppliers to ensure adequate requisite support is available at all points in time. This will provide a full package of community and facility-based nutrition, health and WASH services. Preidentified caregivers will be trained equipped to provide counselling on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) to strengthen the resilience of PLW and improve nutrition of infants between 0-23months. Moreover, the project will ensure children betwee 6-59 months receive vitamin A supplements in the first quarter.
To emphasize on the resilience aspect, provision of sustainable skills in WASH & hygiene to support the prevention of infection and/or fatalities related to poor hygiene especially in PLW will overlay. Women will be capacitated on breastfeeding and Infant Young Feeding Program (IYFP), which are key sustainability and resilience approach as well as basic hygiene programs.
The organization will also work in synergy and coordination with other actors at the cluster and field level to ensure referrals, information sharing, feedback and coordination provide effective management of nutrition within the district. The organization will continue its partner-level attendance of meetings and sharing important data.
Beneficiaries selections will be targeted including the vulnerable communities. The focus will also be made on women-headed households as well as vulnerable families in IDPs. The beneficiaries' selection process will be inclusive and conducted after extensive social mobilizations and communications about the project. Special focus will be made on the disabled through supportive framework for movements and access. For other forms of visibilities, the organization will make intentional effort to identify people with special needs in the community and prioritise their nutritional and health needs during the social mobilization, screening and profiling the beneficiaries.

1. - Projects activities

GEWDO will adopt the following standard activities for project implementation; (1). Identification, referral and treatment of children suffering from severe acute malnutrition through the community, facility-based approaches and OTP Mobile teams. Complicated cases of SAM will be referred to SC and MAM to TSFP centres. Mother-led MUAC approach will be used to identifying and diagnosing malnutrition by mothers. Mothers will use MUAC tapes to measure the Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC). Mother led MUAC training will be conducted to support mother’s to screen, detect oedema and self-referral of their children to nutrition sites. Efforts will be made to ensure integration of OTP and TSFP sites where possible. To ensure the sustainability of IMAM interventions technical local capacity will be strengthened for integration of IMAM into the Primary Health Care system through the functional MCH facilities. Additionally, the capacity of nutrition staff will be strengthened for effective service delivery at facility and community level. In coordination with the GBV-Subsection and health, survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) identified at the nutrition sites and catchment areas, will be referred to multi-sectoral services. (2). Prevention services will include deworming for children 12-59 months and pregnant women in the 2nd and 3rd trimester, counselling and promotion on infant and young child feeding. Community mobilization and screening as well as micronutrient supplementation for PLW (multiple micronutrients and Iron folate) and children 6-59 months with Vitamin A supplements. The program will promote home-based fortification to enrich the diets of young children 6-23 months through the distribution of micronutrient Powders. (3). Provision of routine immunization services to children and pregnant women in this project. (4.) The community resilience approach will be overlaid with basic service provision of key health, WASH and nutrition basic services for prevention, promotion and referral and will be done by trained community workers. Moreover, preidentified caregivers will be trained and equipped to provide counselling on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) to strengthen the resilience of PLW and improve nutrition of infants between 0-23months. Additionally, the project will ensure children betwee 6-59 months receive vitamin A supplements in the first quarter.(5). Provison of awareness, sanitation and prevention materials against Covid-19 will overlay all project activities.
