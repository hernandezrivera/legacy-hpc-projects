Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to work for the reduction of maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality as well as the provision of medical assistance and care to survivors of GBV. This will be achieved by ensuring (a) the availability, access to and utilization of integrated quality SRH services and information for women, men, girls and boys; (b) building the capacity of health workers and community health workers CHW; (c) reactivate and activate services in areas where no SRH services are in place; (d) ensure availability of essential equipment’s, medicines and supplies for delivery of SRH services and family planning;(E) Ensure delivery of complementary comprehensiveness service including the nutrition, MHPSS and NCD for targeted people in needs ;(F) strengthening referral mechanism with other actors and improve the coordination in national and sub national level.
UNFPA will take a Whole of Syria (WoS) approach with implementation taking place from Damascus and cross-border services from Turkey, achieving a country-wide response. . All 14 Syrian governorates will be targeted.
Vulnerable population groups in need to be targeted will include: Women and girls of reproductive age, Internally Displaced Population (IDP), host communities and returnees, adolescents as well as people living with disability (PwD).
The following key objectives will be pursed:
(1) Increasing access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need:
• Increasing access to primary health care through the provision of essential primary health services to cover the health care needs of vulnerable populations, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH).
• Improving access to secondary and tertiary health care for high risk pregnancy, emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) cases, and acute cases which cannot access treatment in the area in which they reside, particularly rural areas.
• Strengthening and consolidating medical supply chains, ensuring availability of safe and quality medicines for essential primary health care, obstetric care, infectious diseases including HIV to prevent transmission from mother to child and family planning methods.Pre-positioning of emergency supply stocks at warehouse and health facility level in line with contingency scenarios for reduced access and increased level of hostilities requiring RH kits , pharmaceutical items and supplies.
• Supporting essential repairs, rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities providing public and specialized services, in particular in areas of most acute need , as well as facilities that receive referrals from areas of acute need.
(2) Strengthening health sector coordination and HIS, with an emphasis on enhancing protection and increasing access to health services and continue coordination within and across hubs, including thorough joint contingency and preparedness planning for changes in context.
(3) Engage in activities that support continuity of care, strengthening of community resilience, response to IDP movements and shifts in control; and beneficiaries participation all over the country.
UNFPA will continue to monitor the implementation of these activities based on the five principles of accountability to affected populations as well as UNFPA’s global results based monitoring framework. Monthly supervision visits will be done jointly with the partners in the national and sub national level by UNFPA staff and personnel subject to access and approvals. Third party monitors are used in the cross-border response. Standardized tools including the supported services and supervision checklist are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic registration data will be collated at least monthly. Beneficiary feedback (incl. complaints box, FGDs, exit interviews) will be collected where possible.

4 - Needs

According to Health cluster available data ( 2019), health needs in Syria continued to be exacerbated by access constraints, damage to health facilities, and insufficient human resources. In addition, many areas that witnessed escalation in hostilities and changes in control are suffering from disruption of critical health services, access constraints, and an increased need for humanitarian interventionsThe Health Resources Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) has reported that 46 % of health facilities across Syria were either nonfunctional or partially functional.In addition, health providers and community representatives have highlighted the limited availability of clinical management of rape (CMR) as a services gap across Syria. The number of people who are in need of health assistance in Syria is estimated to be 12.0 million. Females make up 72 per cent of the population in need (PiN). Women in reproductive age have been identified as a particularly vulnerable group and make up 31% of the population, which equates to roughly 5.2 million women who are in need of Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescence services, HNO 2019, an assessment on disability among adults in western Aleppo, Idleb and Raqqa indicated an average prevalence rate of 30 per cent. Furthermore, the assessment revealed that an average of 45 per cent of surveyed persons injured during the Syria crisis are expected to sustain a permanent impairment. Persons with disabilities are vulnerable to exclusion from the humanitarian response, despite their specific or additional needs. Over 60 % of persons with disabilities state that their disability has been exacerbated by the crisis, with a lack of access to healthcare and difficulties in meeting their basic needs listed as the most common reasons.Furthermore, restrictions on access as well as unreliable availability of financial, material and human resources have prevented health partners from rapidly scaling up response.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNFPA will continue to work for the reduction of maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, to provide medical assistance and to prioritize RH and GBV integration through the provision of GBV specialized services in each supported health facility as well as the provision of GBV prevention services. This will be achieved by ensuring the availability, access to and utilization of integrated quality SRH services and information for women, men, girls and boys; building the capacity of HW, CHW and midwives; reactivate and activate services in areas where no SRH services are in place; ensure availability of essential equipment’s, medicines and supplies for delivery of services and family planning; Ensure delivery of complementary comprehensiveness service including the nutrition, MHPSS and NCD for targeted PIN ; strengthening referral mechanism with other actors and improve the coordination. Vulnerable population to be targeted includes: Women and girls of reproductive age, Internally Displaced Population (IDP), host communities and returnees, adolescents and especially adolescent girls and people with disability (PwD). UNFPA will ensure meaningful participation at all stages of the operation's management cycle. UNFPA will continue to monitor the implementation of these activities based on the five principles of accountability to affected populations as well as UNFPA’s global results-based monitoring framework. This will ultimately help to ensure quality, compliance and the identification of gaps and achievements, for lessons learned as well as corresponding corrective actions to be taken. UNFPA Syria has sub offices and network of field coordinators in all governorates who are conducting regular site visits and monitoring activities that care included in operational cost. The Turkey hub has included third party monitoring.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

UNFPA carried out a risk analysis for its operations and for this project, with the following being identified as key risks: Escalation of violence – includes targeting of health facilities, increased international displacement and limited access to basic services. Lack of access - Difficulty to monitor programs; difficulty to establish/provide services/supplies in insecure areas. Large Population Displacements –lead to overburdening of actors on the ground who are delivering services. Weak partners and lack of partners - This is applicable inside Syria. Partners have restricted movements if they do not receive approval from Authorities in the ground. Staffing restrictions – caused by delayed Visa/long recruitment procedures, increased outward migration and internal displacement of service providers. High turnover of staff – this particularly relevant to key government staff and results in the delay of implementation of activities. Procurement risks -sanctions have made it difficult to procure locally and long lead time of international procedures affecting delivery schedules. Fraud / Aid Diversion. Lack of acceptance of sexual terminology in SRH programs – Sexual health is a taboo subject and is often not prioritized in emergencies UNFPA maintains strong contingencies for the above mentioned risks and is leading efforts to develop contingency plans within RH/GBV subclusters/ working groups, including changes in access. This involves regular monitoring partner activities and the security situation in its areas of operation via UNFPA monitoring teams as well as third parties. It also includes the establishment of UNFPA’s own internal risk management framework. The risk assessment framework will clearly highlight the risk and assumptions and how to mitigate risks. Furthermore, UNFPA maintains necessary flexibility to move resources across implementing partners also provides flexibility to its partnerships for emergency response and preparedness.
