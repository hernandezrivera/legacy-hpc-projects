Project Summary

Samaritan's Purse will continue supporting households (HHs) by protecting and building productive assets and restoring/creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain life. The project is in accordance with the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs targeted activities for 2022. The program developed a comprehensive list of vocations (over 35 market-relevant skills training areas) that beneficiaries will select. In order to expand the varieties of apprenticeship providers, the program will first identify the businesses/apprentice providers and match trainees to the training providers. This will minimize the risk of saturating the labour market with a few types of trades, e.g. barbers and tailors. Besides, project beneficiaries have not been exposed to a wide range of vocational and apprenticeship skills training options. Therefore, the urban livelihood project will focus on two Specific Objectives (SO):
SO1: Strengthening small and medium-size businesses with high growth potential creates jobs for unemployed or underemployed youth. Samaritan's Purse will allocate grants for the 23 most viable business plans (Small Medium Enterprises). In addition, SP will explore possibilities to support at least 1 Large Business Enterprise in addition to the small enterprises. Mentoring and support of business owners on the growth journey will be provided although the programme cycle. The businesses selected will receive essential soft skills and business management training, including writing a budget and calculating revenue, offered to all business owners. Selection criteria: 1) based in Hassakah city, 2) defined as small businesses, 3) willing to attend mandatory sessions (business planning & budgeting), 4) willing to take between 1-5 apprentices for five months, 5) not receiving aid from another NGO for their business. See the image with a list of viable business opportunities within NES.
SO2: Increasing employability of unemployed youth in the local economy through market-relevant vocational training. The project will accept 46 apprentices and provide a stipend for six months of work (22 days/per month). The apprentices will be matched with the businesses according to their skills and experience. During the program, apprentices will receive on-the-job coaching from the businesses. As a result, the program will create a pool of skilled laborers that the market could absorb. Basic soft skills and business management training (formal five-day training and on-job learning), including writing a budget and calculating revenue, will be offered to all apprentices. In addition, apprentices working in a sector requiring more skills may be provided additional trade-specific vocational training, such as construction-related skills. The program is largely guided by the Early Recovery Livelihood working group guidelines and the good practices of other existing humanitarian actors in the program area. Samaritan's Purse will develop context-relevant training monitoring tools to track progress in skills development. Selection criteria: 1) not currently employed, 2) non-consistent employment in the last six months, 3) priority to IDPs but not exclusively, 4) no other income in the HH. SP will provide a start-up kit at the end of the program, i.e. construction toolbox, sewing machine, to the apprentices graduating from the program. The kit will encourage those who want to start their own business.

3 - Needs

The project aims to increase the local market's resilience by improving living standards through enhanced livelihood and IGA for the urban population. The program's objective reflects the need to scale up actions to prevent further deterioration of living conditions and reduce aid dependency. Life skills training for youth 18-30 years are expected to improve their employability and income-generating opportunities. The programme will target vulnerable youth in urban Hassakah. The apprenticeship skills training is non-formal, practical, and adapted to the needs and realities of the beneficiaries. The project will prioritize market-relevant apprenticeship courses with high prospects for youth employability within the programme context. Samaritan's Purse is a member of the Cash Working Group NES. Business grants (cash) will be given to eligible individuals/groups that meet the vulnerability and social welfare criteria and standards set by CWG and ERL WG in NES. Cash transfer values and stipends paid to apprentices will be in harmony with the local wage rate in NES.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to protect and build productive assets by restoring/creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain life by strengthening businesses with high potential growth creating jobs for unemployed youth. Increasing employability of unemployed youth in the local economy through market-relevant vocational training. The project will accept 46 apprentices and provide a stipend for six months. The apprentices will be matched with businesses according to their skills and experience. Samaritan's Purse will undertake post-distribution monitoring to generate lessons and guidance to improve program implementation and ensure positive outcomes are achieved for the targeted beneficiaries. Eligibility and targeting criteria will be explained to all program stakeholders within the area. Local council members and authorities will be involved throughout the program cycle and partnerships built, and communities will be engaged for cohesion purposes. Market assessment is done to inform all procurements related to the project. The choice of modality applied by the project is informed by market functionality and vendors' capacity assessment prior to the project's design. The program team also undertook livelihoods and labour market assessments to inform the program's priority enterprises and employability skills to be developed.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Beneficiaries being harassed to and from distribution sites (for example, beneficiaries are at risk because they may be in need of transport to/from the distribution sites, which makes them vulnerable to those who may commit coercion or aim to exploit the beneficiary sexually)• Access issues at distribution sites (e.g. poor lighting, little room to modify the queuing system if needed)To mitigate these risks and ensure a high level of service delivery as a whole, the quality of activity implementation is monitored by two complaint mechanisms, as well as through SP quality control at distribution sites. Beneficiaries have the opportunity to leave an anonymous complaint at the distribution point via a complaint box or by making a formal complaint at the HAO office. SP then determines how to improve and build further the capacity of the local partner. Samaritan's Purse regularly visits distribution sites to monitor distribution practices. Samaritan's Purse receives feedback from beneficiaries via Syriac Cross on commodity and services. Protection is streamlined through this program by ensuring that all SP staff is involved in the project are trained in managing and referring specific gender and protection concerns that may arise in the program and have a good operational awareness of protection risks and issues.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries exploited/abused by SP/SC staff members, vendors and other actors participating in the implementation of the projects. This risk is mitigated by background checks on all personnel, partners and vendors. Safeguarding training for all staff is provided, protection messaging is displayed at distribution points as well as reporting systems. There is a feedback and complaints processing department to support the mitigation of this risk. Our implementing partner, Syriac Cross (SC), work on the ground to collect and verify anonymous feedback (and if not anonymous, they will anonymize the information) and provide a weekly summary to Samaritan's Purse. The feedback is discussed and acted on, with SC and Samaritan's Purse working together with their respective safeguarding and protection focal points to follow up on PSEA issues. Additionally, Samaritan's Purse has the following measures:• Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation. •Provide training to all beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to ensure understanding of safeguarding and lines or reporting. •A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon.•Reporting mechanisms for each project•Hotline

•Access issues (closure, disruption) might make border crossing (Feshkabour- Semalka) inaccessible. - Protocols for remote working, including remote programming and support to the national staff. •Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. - Ensuring strong communication lines with the Forum and KRI to obtain approvals in a timely manner and with NES staff and partners to ensure there are no gaps in programming.
