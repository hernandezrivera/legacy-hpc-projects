Project Summary

Humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and long standing emergency in the world driven by natural hazards and disasters. Some chronically impoverished and conflict ridden communities are so vulnerable that even minor shocks, which continue to increase in frequency and impact, have devastating effects on their lives and livelihoods. The latest food security reports shows persistent food insecurity gaps in the country, the reports revealed wide spread a cute food insecurity and malnutrition across many parties of Somalia. 2.1 million People are in crisis and emergency (IPC 3&4) situations and faces considerable consumption gaps instigated by the extended hunger period following the poor performance of the 2019 GU season rainfall. On the contrary 4.2 additional people are stressed (IPC 2) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non food needs. The current situation is as a result of the below average rains during the Deyr 2018 (October to December) followed by Harsh weather conditions during the dry spell 2019 Jilal (January-March) season and the delayed and poor performance of the 2019 Gu (April-June). This has further been compounded by lack of livelihood opportunities and limited recovery from the extended impact of the severe 2016/2017 drought. Erratic distribution of rains, as well as low river levels led to significant reduction in the area planted and yield of cereal crops. GU cereal harvest failed in most regions, leaving the worst agro pastoral and riverine families unable to meet their minimum food needs given significantly below average food stocks and income from seasonal agricultural employment. The prolonged drought negatively impacted livestock leading to poor access to milk causing huge accumulations of debts among poor households as they struggle to feed their families and retain their remaining livestock. Deteriorating livestock body conditions rendered livestock market prices significantly low constraining the ability of poor families to meet their daily food requirements and purchase water for their animals. ( Ocha Somalia humanitarian bulletin, Sep 2019).
The proposed intervention seeks to respond to the food needs of the affected households in Galgaduud, Lower Juba, Bari and Sool region of Somalia with essential livelihood packages such as cash, voucher, livestock treatment and feed in order to strengthen and protect the livelihood assets of the poor and most vulnerable households.HOD implemented livestock projects with FAO in the past and hence has capacity . HOD seeks to use integrated food security packages which will respond to both immediate and the long term needs of the affected population. The intervention will support 1,730 households with total population of 10,380 persons (Women 1,980, men 720, boys 3,220, and girls 4,360). The transfer value will be based on the current regional MEB. The proposed project is cost effective because 81.7% of the project budget will go to the project beneﬁciaries making the project cost effective. Accountability on cash tracking will be achieved through the use of vouchers which will contain the name and unique identification of each beneficiary. A competitively selected Money vendor will also be engaged to process payments for beneﬁciaries in order to reduce risk associated with the cash and to enhance accountability in the cash transfer process. The use of cash is informed by the availability of well functioning market system. It also responds well to local needs during crisis. A post distribution assessment for the beneﬁciaries will be done to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism/grievance mechanism will also be put in place for beneﬁciaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneﬁciaries are receiving the right amount. Market data will be collected on regular basis to monitor cash impact on the market

Project Activities

1.1: Mobilize, identify and register and transfer unconditional cash transfer to 310 households with a population of 1,860 (330 women, 120 men, 750 girls and 560 boys) with malnourished children, lactating women, and pregnant women, elderly, sick and new arrived IDPs, identiﬁed by community leaders and women groups to prevent a deterioration of the situation from (IPC 3 to IPPC 4). Each household will receive USD$ 100 for 3 months 1.2: Support to 750 households with total population of 4,500 persons (800 women, 380 men, 1800 girls and 1520 boys), through voucher food voucher. With each household receiving food voucher valued at US$70/ for 4 months. Female-headed HHs will constitute approximately 60% of the beneﬁciaries HHs. 1.3: Support 450 rural households with total population of (2,700) persons (500 women, 130 men 1,320 girls and 750 boys) through CFW so as to increase food access to vulnerable households and rehabilitate community productive assets and enhance water availability for 48 days intensive CFW. Each beneﬁciaries will receive USD$100 per month. 1.4: Provide veterinary services to 18,000 goats and shots for 500 households representing 3000 persons (350 women, 120 men, 1250 boys and 1280 girls) and further support 200 vulnerable women lead HHs with restocking of lactating goats with each household receiving 10 lactating goats. The aim is to replenish household head size for those who lost their animals during the drought in order to boost their resilience and improve access to nutrition and reduce dependence on external support.

Mitigation Measures

1. Protection of civilians: i) Distribution points and routes and the areas where rehabilitation of community productive infrastructure will be done shall be located in central areas that are known to be safe and have been cleared of any landmines, not near military installations or near conflict areas or in areas known to be infested with robbers. ii) The distribution will be done door to door as much as reasonably possible iii) All distribution and community infrastructure rehabilitation activities will be done during daylight between 10.00 AM to 3.00 PM. iv) Route guides shall be stationed at various locations to safely guide the beneficiaries to the distribution points. v) The shortest route to the distribution point shall be used. 2. Exclusion and marginalization risks: i) Data about beneficiaries shall be segregated by age, gender, physical ability/disability and group identity (minority or dominant) ii) During invitation to community sensitization meetings, messages will be sent through door to door, social media and posters to youth groups, parents, community elders to invite boys and girls to the meetings. ii) During meetings an opinion of women, boys and girls will be sought before conclusions are made on a matter. Iv) Beneficiary registration will be done according to categories such as women, people beyond 60 years old, youth, physically disabled, pregnant and lactating women and minority clan. v) Those who are most likely to be marginalized or excluded in distributions such as women and those who do not have documentation will be made part of the project implementation team to champion the rights of such groups. vi) All stakeholders will be involved in the selection of beneficiaries so that no vulnerable and deserving beneficiary is left out and all who can benefit are informed of the details of the project.3. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: i) All project workers will sign a code of conduct which prohibits interference in the beneficiary selection process, involvement in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. ii) A stakeholder engagement plan will be prepared and all stakeholders will be invited for community sensitization meetings for the project. Selection of beneficiaries will be conducted after an agreed-on criteria by all stakeholders iii) At distribution points, female staff shall be in charge of women and girls’ queues iv) A feedback and grievance mechanism shall be set up to pick out issues arising from stakeholders v) Distribution teams shall be rotating among the various groups of stakeholders. vi) In the rehabilitation of community productive infrastructure, women and girls will be granted flexible working hours to accommodate other duties they do such as cooking for the family.4. Protection mainstreaming risks: i) Beneficiary lists and personal data shall be kept confidential and safe from unintended use. ii) The location of distribution points and access routes shall have enough space for orderly distribution and will be guarded by security staff to ensure the safety of beneficiaries. iii) Distribution will be done twice in a month and during the day. To minimize safety risk and to ensure beneficiaries do not face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their rations home, undercover escorts shall be deployed during distribution days. iv) The ration size will be determined to be appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them v) Working in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food distributions vi) Avoiding community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets, and inputs.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed proposal is designed to respond to the most immediate needs of the poor and destitute food insecure households by stabilizing their livelihoods through conditional and unconditional transfer. The intervention will rehabilitate community productive assets such as water catchments, irrigation canals and strategic feeder roads linked to markets with the aim of enhancing food production as well as water availability and accessibility for both human and livestock consumption. This will ensure resilience among the affected communities. The proposed project will strategize in the protection of livelihood assets of the targeted vulnerable households through restocking to increase livestock herds among the poor vulnerable and drought affected households in order to enhance access to food and nutrition. The project will target 18,000 goats and shots for treatment and provision of feeds in order to ensure their resilience against diseases and the effect of droughts. Training and awareness on issues of nutrition, hygiene promotion and water harvesting techniques will be incorporated in the project in order to enhance the capacity of the community members and the project team members
