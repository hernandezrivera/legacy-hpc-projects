Project Summary

The project will prioritize the most vulnerable groups at the IDP camps and people with disabilities in the communities, In addition to the emphasis on the participation of the beneficiaries themselves and the community members to harness inclusive and protective environments and increase the resilience of the affected population through the following activities:
• The project will be continuing in support of the 15 pre-established inclusion networks, the networks’ members will continue actively engage in the following tasks:
- Plan and provide training and awareness sessions on the inclusion of PWDs.
- Identify and refer PWDs to the needed services.
- Advocate in their communities for the rights of PWDs and design and conduct community events.
• The project staff will train community focal point on the Inclusion of PWDs, in addition, to conducting consultation /FGDs with affected population and protection community networks to ensure meaningful participation in the intervention (before/after interventions) and collect feedback about the inclusion of PWDs and their rights, and how to activate the inclusion in the communities.
• Case Management supports beneficiaries of PWDs who will be identified through all project activities and their families by offering PSS sessions, protection assistance, referrals, specialized service, access to socio-economic activities, livelihood kits accelerated education.
• Through peer support group sessions activities which will be conducted by protection community networks members with disabilities, for PWDs, and for caregivers of children with disabilities, the project aims to reduce psychosocial problems effects that prevent a PWDs from using his/her abilities through sharing emotional and practical experiences, and lessons learned between participants during a peer support session, which can develop and support plans for more Independence and self-reliant.
• Survivor assistance through physical rehabilitation, nursing, PSS, and speech therapy for PWDs 50% of them are war injured verified by VDC surveys and data collection.
• This project also aims to enhance the psychosocial well-being and protection of children and caregivers through conducting inclusive psychosocial support structured group sessions for children, and parenting skills program
• Risk Education sessions will be conducted for IDPs and host community members to protect themselves against the hazards, pre and post-test will be conducted.
• Provide GBV case management including in-kind assistance and CVA using CARE's SOP on Integrating CVA in GBV Case Management.
• Provide CP case management including in-kind assistance and CVA using the CP SC’s SOPs, to support children who are suffering from (sexual assault, physical assault, Child Early, Forced Marriage, psychological/emotional abuse, neglecting, child labor, children exposure to exploitation and sexual violence, and children who are out of school).
In Addition to providing GBV training to non-protection staff and supporting WGSS that annexed to Atma maternity hospital, Providing GBV services
• To ensure PSEA in targeted communities, HIHFAD will contribute by providing PSEA Focal Point training for humanitarian workers and PSEA awareness-raising sessions for targeted groups, conducting workshops for humanitarian workers to share experiences on PSEA mainstreaming and PSEA best practices in the field operations and recruitment.
• This project will work on the capacity building of the humanitarian workers by providing training on variety of topics (RE, Child Protection Minimum standards, GBV and Inclusion)
• Update and modify the existing service map, and coordinate with service providers for referrals.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Security and safety concerns of parents on girl's access to the structured PSS activities will reduce the number of girls participantsMitigation 1: Coordination with parents and local councils to provide this activity in a suitable place making sure that girls can accompany more than one girl from the neighbors and move in groups. Risk 2: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and negatively affecting their wellbeing. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and a lack of trust in services. Mitigation 2: Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor-centered services. Risk 3: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance provided by humanitarian organizations. Mitigation 3: Coordinate with development organizations and community networks to ensure the establishment of local initiatives. Risk 4: Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV servicesMitigation 4: field team ensures confidentiality principles are in line with global standards; data protection protocols are in place and physical space is designed to meet criteria. Risk 5: lack of acceptance from the community of CP and GBV activities.Mitigation 5: Coordinated with local councils and schools and conduct induction session.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA risks increase in emergencies settings due to the nature of power dynamics between humanitarian staff and beneficiaries which allow staff to perpetrate while beneficiaries are vulnerable to becoming victims. Aid workers always have more power over those we are there to serve.besides the strong PSEA policy that HiHFAD has which includes mandatory training and a PSEA COC to be signed by all the staff members, HiHFAD is implementing a set of PSEA activities through a strategic approach based on capacity building for the staff and raising awareness for the community using the following activities:-PSEA raising awareness sessions for the community.-Identify a PSEA field focal point.-Applying the PSEA field and organization risk and mitigation checklist.-Conducting PSEA training and refresher training as a part of the CB of all staff members.-Including the PSEA and PSEA COC in the mandatory induction training for any new staff.-Allocate specific safeguarding private numbers for beneficiaries has female and male well-trained respondents available to respond to any SEA complaint.-Distribute PSEA raising awareness materials in the project locations and in social media includes the safeguarding numbers.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- COVID19 Spread in NW of Syria during the project implementationAssumption : No major spread or lockdown in targeted communities and the project continues normally. Mitigation:Canceling group activities, Using online platforms, Using network members' contact lists to share messages with community members through social media. 2- Security incidents impacting border-crossings, terror attacks, cross-border shelling, or clashes.Assumption:Cross-border operations and humanitarian space are sufficiently preserved to allow implementation, monitoring, and evaluation.Mitigation: - Options for procuring the needed supplies from inside Syria. - Explore alternative official border crossings. - Maintain good relations with local communities - Secure stock in HIHFAD warehouses for emergency measures.3- The beneficiaries relocated, making follow-up impossible.Assumption: There is no huge displacement and no relocation of staffMitigation: Collect phone numbers of the beneficiaries or their relatives, provide remote counseling, referrals in their new locations, and train the team to provide teleservice especially for VA.4- Access of humanitarian actors to CP and GBV cases limited, reducing service availability access of humanitarian actors to be limitedAssumption: the community showed acceptance for these services and there is an active service plan.Mitigation: access through other activities or services.
