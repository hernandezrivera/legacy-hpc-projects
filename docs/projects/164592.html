Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb and Aleppo from the host community and IDPs which includes the most vulnerable groups (children under 12 years, IDPs children, children with disabilities) to provide equitable access for quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in the primary grade in both formal and non-formal settings. The implementation approach will be focusing on delivering quality education in the existing formal and non-formal learning spaces in the targeted locations that have been either complete cessation or substantial reduction in non-earmarked funding support to the education based on the result of the education need assessment conducted by Violet's team in those locations. The project design complies with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles for children to ensure that children have access to inclusive and equitable quality education that provides opportunities for lifelong learning.
Violet aims to achieve the overall change (the impact) we want to have is improved student resilience and learning outcomes – specifically, improved resilience for up to 6,800 children (50 percent girls) with at least 50 percent achieving improved basic learning outcomes through:
- The provision of incentives has a positive impact on teacher attendance and retention
- The provision of formal education with complementary basic literacy and numeracy intervention for primary-age children through testing their abilities in reading and math through ASER assessment tools
- The Ongoing teacher professional development and support which effectively equip teachers with the skills to provide protective and quality education in the supported learning spaces.
- Enhanced participation by children, parents, and communities in supporting children’s education and protection
- Conduct inclusion activities by screening children for functioning problems, training field officers and teachers on alternative education plans and scaling up its inclusion activities by developing AEPs for all children with functioning problems and resources to provide support for children with more significant functioning problems, ensure to implement the RECU design in the education facilities (Reach, Enter, Circulate, Use) to fully mainstream disability in education
- Conduct safeguarding activities and scale up a suite of activities designed to help keep children safe including emergency preparedness and response activities.

4 - Needs

For children and educators in fragile or conflict-affected contexts, the education system is constantly changing, mostly in a negative way and in some areas is not even existing. As a consequence of the years-long crisis in Syria, the number of people in need and vulnerable communities has reached 11.7 million people in need. Needs are broad across the sectors, leaving host communities and displaced people independence for assistance. 5 million of those are children; 12.537 have experienced grave violence between 2013 and 2019 e.g. through killing, injuring, child labor or abuse. Education and school attendance is one of the most important ways of providing protective systems to children, despite their situation at home. Especially in displacements without regular homes or safety networks, children are in dire need to attend school and participate in education. Additionally, school dropouts are significant for the future of the children and will adversely affect their education and livelihood opportunities. In Syria, right now almost one in three school-age children (aged 5-17 years) are out of school, 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out, and more than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed or used as a shelter. It is not only necessary to provide classes and education, but also to uphold and maintain the quality needed to build knowledge and support the future generations with platforms for social justice, social cohesion, peace, and economic empowerment. The source of information is UN High Commissioner for Human Rights In the past months, Violet has led an initiative to assess the work done in education and to outline the challenges faced by the NGOs working in the volatile areas in Syria. During those meetings, discussions for political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, legal and environmental factors were considered and are updated by Violet’s teams on a regular basis.

5 - Implementation of activities

Recruitment of school staff and involving the education directorates in Idleb and Aleppo in the recruitment process and provide incentives for them on a monthly basis. Train teachers and other education personnel in the Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) Primary School Training Package, deliver training on literacy and numeracy materials. Assess learning levels and enroll students in both formal and non-formal learning spaces accordingly (needs-based) and grade level/basic literacy and numeracy level by conducting ASER learning assessment for all children. Support monthly running costs of learning spaces. Distribute teaching and learning materials for all enrolled students and classroom teachers. Conduct child functioning Screening for all children to assess capacities and mobility. Identify and support pathways from non-formal classes into formal school. Run structured recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in learning spaces. Conduct awareness-raising sessions with children and parents/caregivers on protection and child rights. Conduct back to school campaigns in targeted communities to increase children's attendance and enrollment in schools. Procuring and provision of safety equipment to schools (first aid kits, fire extinguishers). Service mapping and referral pathways, refer children and support using appropriate child protection mechanisms.
