Project Summary

Geographically, Upper Nile State lies in the North East of South Sudan, bordering Sudan to the North, and Ethiopia to the South West. Upper Nile State region is classified a Complex Humanitarian Emergency, with lingering devastating consequences of both natural and man made emergencies overwhelming national response. These multifaceted emergencies are attributed to prolonged conflict, flooding, cattle rustling, and the influx of returnees fleeing the Sudan civil war which broke out in April 2023.
In October 2022, MSF South Sudan reported that conflict in Greater Upper Nile was impeding assistance to people already devastated by flooding. https://www.msf.org/conflict-greater-upper-nile-impeding-humanitarian-aid-thousands-people-already-devastated-flooding,
By December 2022, the UNHCR also reported that 'Fleeing civilians to upper Nile state were visibly traumatized and reported killings, injuries, gender-based violence, abductions, extortion, looting and burning of properties, many children have lost their homes and been separated from their families and at least 3,000 displaced persons has fled to neighboring Countries.“ U.N. agencies Reuters December 7, 2022 Violence in South Sudan's Upper Nile displaces 9,000 ".
The situation escalated rapidly after onset of the civil war in Sudan erupted in April 2023, displacing hundreds of thousands especially women and children who had previously fled Upper Nile for safety in Sudan, according to OCHA, as of 16 October 2023, 317,993 people had been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18 years. South Sudanese returnees accounted for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals, while new arrivals continue to report harassment, intimidation, torture and extortion of money during their journey to South Sudan, including by security personnel along the route. OCHA South Sudan: Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16, 20 October 2023
GCAF intends to respond with a budget of 300,000 USD, targeting 3,000 beneficiaries in the areas of Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile State, where the HPC intersectoral prioritization 2023/2024 has classified Nasir/Luakpiny a Priority level 1 and Ulang and Maiwut Priority response level 2, with possible escalations. GCAF plans to target 1500 vulnerable Children, Boys 735, Girls 765, for critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, the project will also target 1500 men and women caregivers, Men 735, Women 765 women, and of the over all 3000 beneficiaries, special consideration will be accorded to Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) and 15% Boys of the overall target population will be PWD. Targeted benficiaries may be members of Host community 20%, Returness 35% or IDP 45% vulnerable and at risk of abuse, PSN of diverse age groups will also be consider for AGD considerations. The project will work towards prevention of boys and girls from risks associated with complex emergencies of this magnitude, including SGBV, recruitment and abduction (CAFAAG), Mental Health breakdown, Diseases, lack of access to services, malnutrition, Early and Forced Child marriage due to power exploitation and overwhelmed protection monitoring system. Caregivers will be reached with MHPSS services, counselling, mentorship programs among others.
GCAF intends to respond with child protection programming in line with the HRP Strategic objectives of, dignified access to life saving assistance, mitigation of protection risks, and increased resilience in the community.

1. Needs

As of 14 September 2023, 30,000 Sudanese returnees have been recorded crossing the border into South Sudan from refugee camps in Ethiopia into Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang Counties in Upper Nile state, and Akobo County in Jonglei. Returnees are driven by cuts in humanitarian aid in camps and escalated conflict in the area, UNICEF South Sudan Humanitarian Situation Report No. 9 - September 2023.pdfChild Protection Needs in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut, are largely cases of Unaccompanied and Separated children (UASC) composed of, returnees including women men and PWD leaving TC Renk, to their final destinations, cases of internally displaced children but also women and men and PWD who are not yet in their final destinations, thirdly children, women, men and PWD in the host community of Nasir who have lost their homes, livelihood and parental care, and are at risk of exploitation and abuse, exposure to the risk of SGBV and GBV in the forms of Child Marriages, Child exploitation, including child labor and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), recruitment and abduction into armed groups and armed forces (CAFAAG), Mental Health breakdown, Diseases, lack of access to services, and high risks of acute malnutrition.A joint rapid Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) risk assessment, conducted from 15-19 June in Renk, highlighted that risks of SEA and other misconduct by humanitarian staff in Renk and Juba boarder, the assessment identified adolescent girls, single women, and single female‑headed households as the most vulnerable to SEA, most of these survivors have not yet reached their final destinations, and will continue to relive their ordeal if not reached with counselling and access to recovery services, and PSS and MHPSS, therefore the project intends to access survivors and respond to cases of SEA at their final destinations, secondly the project will also raise awareness on SEA Prevention and reporting systems to mitigate the occurance by addressing the stigma associated.The numbers of IDP returnees requiring onward transportation assistance remains a key priority. This calls for need of Cash Based Intervention to address transport needs and to plan for service provision at destination areas south of Renk. South Sudan Response Scale-Up Situation Report | 3.Food security outcomes are likely to deteriorate, and humanitarian needs to rise, including for host communities in the already acutely food-insecure areas. households are likely to resort to negative coping mechanisms, Response to the Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 15The influx will exacerbate protection risks for children looking for resources, exposing boys and girls to sexual exploitation and abuse, Child GBV/SGBV, increased trauma, increased barriers to protection services, recruitment and abductions. Refugees who fled to Sudan in 2022 witnessed gruesome murders and atrocities, once again they have been exposed to high scale conflict in Sudan and there is need for MHPSS intervention.Given the larger numbers of girls and young adolescent mothers coming in, there is priority need for distribution of Dignity kits to maintain hygiene of young girls and young mothers to deter chronic infections which can exacerbate the health risk in the community at large, proper waste disposal is also a major factor in this response.Tensions in some areas hampers people’s ability to move and access services mostly due to impassable roads, this is a big barrier to access of humanitarian services, however there is need to mitigate these risks through outreach programming, GCAF seeks to work in three counties of Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut, with regular field visit to the bomas and parishes responding in the HRP Strategic Objectives areas of Creating and facilitating meaningful Access, Protection risk mitigation through partners centrality of protection actions with communities, Capacity building to strengthen child protection structures at the higher and lower levels of implementation.

2. Project activities

The Key response priorities/activities are aligned to the project Strategic objectives derived from the three HRP Strategic Objectives as maintained by the Protection Cluster, ensuring that there is dignified access to life saving humanitarian assistance, that crises-affected population protection risks are mitigated through centrality of protection, and finally, increasing protection structures capacity. Project Strategic Objectives; 1. Provide vulnerable girls and boys with critical Child Protection assistance and services, including specialized response with key consideration to AGD approach, and Disability inclusion, in Nasir/Luakpiny, Ulang, and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile South Sudan2. Support and strengthen community protection structures to prevent, advocate, and respond to the problem of early Child marriage and 6 Grave violations against children in Nasir, Ulang, and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile State, through Dialogue, awareness raising, radio talkshows, and school Child Rights Clubs (CRCs) programs3. Strengthen capacity, support and coordinate community based protection structures to advocate, prevent and respond to CP human rights violations, through training of men and women leaders in the community in Child Protection in emergencies.To achieve the above strategic project objectives, GCAF will implement activities that will directy respond to the key objectives and indicators, as agreed upon by Protection Cluster.The priority response are;Provide case management services (also through cash-based initiatives).Identify Unaccompanied and Separated children and facilitate FTR, for those who have been referred, FTR services must be provided and follow up made.Update Child Protection referral pathwaysSupport children, adolescents and caregivers to access community-based mental health and psychosocial support, this will be achieved at the CFS facility once on groundConduct awareness sessions with children and caregivers (CFS) and outreach Train women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff) on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut counties in Upper Nile State
