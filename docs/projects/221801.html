Project Summary

The project aims to address critical health needs and improve resilience among vulnerable populations in nine counties across Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Upper Nile states. It will be aligns with Health Cluster objective 2025 to ensure continuity of health care services to most vulnerable population for the next 12 months that will create a sustained resilience among communities to continue support their own initiatives among themselves after the project phased out by the end of 2025. The project will improve access to emergency healthcare services through static mobiles and outreach intervention by detecting and managing common morbidities and mortalities among affected population. 200,000 beneficiaries men, women, boys and girls in the nine(9) counties (Fangak,Ayod,Canal/Pigi,Aweil East, Guit, Leer, Malakal, Panyikang and Gogrial East) of the mention states will be targeted to benefit from this project due to their vulnerability caused by the Sudan crisis and the prolong flooding that will continue to lead people into acute food insecurity, poverty and poor access to primary health care, basic Nutrition needs and protection services by 2025. This will be achieved through health cluster intervention package to deliver timely and critical, life-saving frontline health services interventions. The project will provide healthcare services interventions to reach 145,00 beneficiaries (39,150 boys, girls, 47,850 men and 21,750 and women 36,250) with host populations 50,750, returnees 36,250, IDPs 43,500 and refugees 14,500) with 10,800 PWDs to achieve its purpose. The healthcare component will focus on provision of routine technical support for appropriate response by the CHD-MoH and other health partners in the targeted counties. HFO will provide integrated package of health services that include outpatient consultations (OPD), Antenatal Care (ANC), Post-natal care (PNC), referral of critical condition to counties/states hospital , support routine and mass immunisation campaign services through CHD cold chain system. HFO will also support the existing health teams in the current locations and recruit and train teams in the new locations to continue delivering services (clinical officers, midwives, nurses, and county health department teams). The teams will provide BEmONC services in 5 PHCCs and 6 PHCUs and 7 Mobile clinics across the targeted locations, Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) to injuries victims, and support Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) survivors with the clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support, and referral for further management. HFO will also provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non-specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) while ensuring inclusion of persons living with disabilities (PLWDs). Provide sexual and reproductive health services when treating common diseases in the mobile clinics and reach beneficiaries in hard-to-reach areas through outreaches and mobile response. The project will provide complementarity to existing health services by filling in the critical service gaps and bridge critical emergency health needs that are created by Sudan crisis and floods that led to the destruction of properties and health services and shortage of supplies . The secondary healthcare facilities in the counties will be used for referral of cases that cannot be handled at the PHCCs/PHCUs and mobile clinics and outreach posts. The Mobile Team (MT) will ensure continuity and equal coverage to all beneficiaries through weekly outreaches in the mapped hard-to-reach areas in the selected counties.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, with persistent challenges from ongoing conflict, climate shocks, and economic instability. In 2024, approximately 9 million people, over 70% of the population, are expected to require humanitarian assistance. The crisis has been exacerbated by flooding, displacement from internal conflict, and the influx of returnees fleeing the Sudan conflict. An estimated 7.1 million people are projected to experience acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) during the 2024 lean season. This includes over 2.5 million children and women at risk of acute malnutrition. Catastrophic conditions (IPC Phase 5) persist in several regions, including parts in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Northen Bhar El Ghazal and Upper Nile states, due to conflict, high food prices, and displacement. The healthcare system is overwhelmed by disease outbreaks such as measles, malaria, and cholera, particularly affecting internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services are critically inadequate, with 62% of the population lacking access to clean water, exacerbating diarrheal diseases and other health risks. Severe flooding has affected over 1.4 million people in 44 counties, displacing 375,000 and damaging infrastructure and agricultural livelihoods. This worsens access to food, clean water, and healthcare. South Sudan hosts over 2.2 million IDPs and has absorbed 300,000 returnees from Sudan since April 2023. Overcrowded transit centers lack adequate food, water, and sanitation, increasing vulnerability to disease and malnutrition. Women and children face heightened protection risks, including gender-based violence and exploitation, especially during displacement. Strengthened funding, supporting health care services is vital in improving access to living condition and basic health services by taking the services directly to the most vulnerable community through outreach intervention and setting up mobile Clinics will support delivery of services and address the crisis and preventing further deterioration of living conditions among host community, IDPs and retunes.In 2024, several states in South Sudan, including Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Upper Nile, have been severely affected by flooding, leading to increased vulnerability to disease outbreaks. As of mid-September 2024, over 735,000 people have been impacted by flooding across 38 counties, including these states, with significant displacement and damage to homes, crops, and infrastructure. Flooding in these areas has led to a rise in disease risks, such as waterborne diseases, snakebites, and outbreaks linked to disruptions in health services. For example, Unity State has seen rising snakebite incidents, while communities in Upper Nile and Warrap have been struggling with access to health services. The displacement of thousands of people has further strained already limited healthcare and sanitation resources, increasing the risk of disease transmission. Overall, the combination of flooding, displacement, and lack of access to basic services has put many people in these states at heightened risk of health crises. These states have been affected by conflict, displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to healthcare services. Malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), and acute respiratory infections (ARI) are among the leading causes of morbidity. In the Week 34 of 2024, 98,537(49.4%) reported cases of malaria, along with 30 suspected deaths 29.8% and approximately 2.6 million suspected malaria cases reported in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, NBelG and Warrap States recording the highest morbidity rates UNICEF 2014, with some counties surpassing their historical averages for the disease. Additionally, AWD, including cholera outbreaks, and ARI also contribute significantly to the disease burden with Very high risk of malnutrition, cholera, measles, yellow fever, meningitis, dengue, mpox

Malaria cases management: Treatment with Antimalaria (ACT) and provide awareness on the prevention due to stagnant water, inadequate preventive measures, and lack of access to antimalarial drugs.Diarrheal diseases management: Treating with ORS and zinc and provide awareness on Water and sanitation issues contribute to outbreaks of diseases like cholera and dysentery. Rapid access to oral rehydration therapy (ORT) and other treatments.Acute Respiratory Infection management: Treating with anti biotics such as amoxicillin Tuberculosis and HIV care: Crisis-affected populations often experience increased vulnerability to tuberculosis and HIV, requiring diagnosis, treatment, and prevention services. Will screen and provide basic services and refer for secondary services. • Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)Psychological first aid: With high levels of trauma, displacement, and insecurity, mental health services are urgently needed to support individuals and communities affected by violence, loss, and displacement. Restoring functional health centres is critical for delivering these services.• Health Surveillance and Response SystemsEpidemic preparedness and response: Rapid response to outbreaks such as cholera, measles, and other communicable diseases is vital through establishment of rapid respond team in each location.Early warning systems: Establishing systems in coordination with CHD/WHO for detecting and responding to emerging health threats in a timely manner.
