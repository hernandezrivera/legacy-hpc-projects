Project Summary

The effects of conflict in South Sudan continues to affect the humanitarian response as the effects of widespread violence and the deteriorating economic situation and will be aligned to Nutrition Cluster specific objectives, namely 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition-specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women; 2 To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls & boys under five years of age and PLW & IDP'affected by acute malnutrition; 3 To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information; 4 To increase equitable access to nutrition-sensitive interventions from WASH, FSL and Centrality of Protection through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups. The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the nutrition care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups for what concerns MAM/SAM treatment but also prevention (as per MIYCN principles) and timely detection of acute malnutrition. Nutrition services and related activities will be informed by the most recently updated guidelines, taking into account COVID19 prevention and containment measures. Different services delivery modalities will be considered, from static to mobile/community based, as a proactive and articulated strategy to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current nutrition care coverage and to reach both hosts and displaced population. The proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Nutrition services will be provided at the Health Facility level or through mobile team and community-based initiatives in their catchment area and under their supervision and guide, resulting in highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the inter-related complications. In this way, the project will contribute to enhancing the institutionalization of management of acute malnutrition into the health system as suggested by the Cluster. The cornerstone of the proposed nutrition intervention will be its integration with other sectors as well. WASH & FSL will be given high attention, with the result of the applied strategy to be wide enough to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, as poor hygiene and access to clean water, limited food availability, low knowledge. Expected outcome, through early detection and case management, expected caseloads are disaggregated as SAM U5’s: 2,324; MAM U5’s: 5,093; MAM PLW: 5,640; MIYCN: 15,056; VAS: 38,110 and Deworming 34,299 in YE county with estimated cost of $ 390,460. Help will adopt a combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as the establishment of static services, mobile OTP and TSFP site, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening and assessment of immunization status using child health care, distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap for handwashing, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL (vegetable gardening, cooking demonstration, and ), Education (establish ECD kits) and protection for complementarity and synergy and preposition of supplies for desired treatment outcome. Key stakeholders shall be engaged in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including the establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback and the complementarity of the project will contribute towards realization SDG's 1, 2, 13, 15 & 16 to end hunger & poverty.

1 - Needs

Help-Hilfe Zur Selbsthilfe has been present in Yirol East since 2016 following vulnerability and needs trends and constantly monitoring WaSH & Nutrition Data of the population. This monitoring has been coupled with a nutrition pre-harvest SMART survey in June 2018 in Yirol East and the result showed increased food insecurity at the household level and high rates of acute malnutrition with of GAM rate of 22.6% and SAM rate of 6.5% among children 6-59 months. Acute malnutrition prevalence among PLWs measured by MUAC (<23 cm), was 25.7%. The latest 2021 National Nutrition cluster proxy GAM rate projects Yirol East 13.6%. Help proposes Multi-dimensional Nutrition sensitive programming integration of WASH, In the first IPC projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County in Pibor Administrative Area. During this period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. A total of 21 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 46 are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 11 are classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. Help's approach will be involving nutrition stakeholders and will implement numerous interventions and activities to respond to needs. The planned activities are: - Train health and nutrition workers on malnutrition prevention at national, state, county and community levels. - Provide targeted individual and group counselling on MIYCN to caregivers of children aged below 24 months, including men. - Support the establishment of demonstration kitchen gardens at facilities and in communities. - Promote dietary diversity, food demonstration with locally available food, and cooking methods through cooking demonstrations. - Support growth monitoring and promotion at facility and community levels. - Provide micronutrient supplements, specifically vitamin A, and deworming through integration with National Immunisation Days (NID), as a standalone activity or with acute malnutrition screening.- Conduct malaria testing and treatment at nutrition sites (OTP). - Distribute long-lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs), WASH non-food items, and conduct hygiene promotion at OTP sites through the WASH cluster. - Train health and nutrition staff on how to provide psychosocial support to families and distribute ECD kits at nutrition sites (OTP). - Train health and nutrition workers on community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) to improve care for children aged 6-59 months with malnutrition.- Conduct community mobilization activities and active screening for early case detection and referral of children with SAM.- Provide integrated treatment to children with SAM, both at static facilities and through community outreach. - Provide integrated care for children with SAM in OTP/Stabilization Centres in health facilities, including WASH services and ECD at nutrition sites. - Create awareness and provide gender-based violence referral services at OTP sites, focusing on women’s empowerment in community meetings. - Conduct timely emergency preparedness and response. - Monitor the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites. - Provide integrated on-the-job coaching and mentorship on SAM treatment, supplies management and MIYCN. - Routinely manage nutrition programme data and information. - Participate in SMART and Coverage surveys and will mobilize resources to participate in the bi-annual FSNMS.- Prevention, detection and care of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) amongst children under 5 years old and of malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women, through 12 OTP's and 3 Outreaches.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will ensure protection mainstreaming through the implementation of its activities across all sectors. Special attention will be provided on the identification and referral of the most vulnerable and at risk. Some parts of the population (Persons with disabilities, older persons, women and children) face additional challenges to access humanitarian support and are “invisible”, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE teams will be trained in order to identify and prevent potential risks while responding to the specific needs of the most vulnerable. o All HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE staff in South Sudan are required to sign the code of conduct (See Annex E) and its additional policies pertaining to 1) Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), 2) Prevention of Fraud and Corruption and 3) Child Protection. In addition, briefings are provided ensuring a good understanding of core humanitarian values and beneficiary protection. As a whole, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE has made a particular effort to ensure a balanced recruitment process in terms of gender and ethnicity, particularly for health and protection teams in order to ensure accessibility of services. o Furthermore, all members of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will received protection mainstreaming training including a strong component of Psychological First Aid (PFA) allowing them to provide good listening, identify potential risks and either internally or externally refer. HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is aware of the importance of including gender equality to increase the efficiency of the humanitarian assistance and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. In NE South Sudan, Displaced Women and Girls have been particularly exposed to violence, for this a particular referral pathway ensuring confidentiality and protection of the beneficiary will be put in place and explain to relevant staff. o With strong protection and outreach community component, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will foster the protection environment by enhancing awareness, information and identifying individuals at risks requiring an immediate response. Protection Outreach teams will be specifically trained to assess basic and specific needs (with a health approach) and to analyze, with a holistic vision, the vulnerability of the individual or Household and to refer to the relevant HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE department in order to receive an adequate response. For instance, in link with the food security department of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE (financed by USAID-FFP), particularly vulnerable households identified as facing protection risks because of the lack of sufficient food will be referred to FSL teams for a sectorial assessment to establish the need and the responseo In parallel, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is ensuring that complaint mechanisms are in place. In Yirol East a hotline has been available for beneficiaries since the beginning of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFEs intervention. Through this project, teams will enhance the diffusion of the hotline number to ensure beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries have access to it. In Yirol East, with low telephone coverage, a complaint box will be available for beneficiaries to communicate with HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE and receive treatment to their complaints.
