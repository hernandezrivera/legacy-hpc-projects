Project Summary

Medair’s proposed action will provide up to 455 PLWMI, including survivors of explosive hazard incidents, with access to quality assistive devices and community based training for caregivers. Beneficiaries will be referred to specialist rehabilitation services and/or MHPSS services if needs are identified. Additional GBV and MHPSS activities are fully integrated into Medair’s multisectoral programming, particularly Medair’s primary health care rehabilitation and community health programmes.
The project builds on the expertise and relationships from previous project cycles to reach the most vulnerable and in need. Newly accessible, hard to reach and high need locations will be targeted, with Medair already active in areas that have seen high levels of hostilities including Deir ez Zor, Dara’a, Aleppo and southern Idleb.
To address MHPSS needs, Medair will engage a CBO/NNGO to conduct community-based peer support group sessions, piloting this activity in at least 1 location. CBOs with capacity to work in DEZ will be prioritised. At supported health centres, coordinated scale up of accessible and sustainable mental health services will also be provided through PFA and mhGAP HIG consultation provision in the PHCCs (this is described in Medair’s health submission, and is aligned with the MOH and WHO plans for roll out of mhGAP HIG). CHWs will be trained as PFA providers and be able to refer those in need of further MHPSS support to PHC facilities.
Increased identification and referral of GBV survivors or those at risk of GBV will be carried out as part of household level health awareness sessions by trained CHWs and clinic consultations by trained clinic staff. The GBV training for health workers includes 4 sessions, covering GBV concepts and root causes, early identification/stigma, communication skills with survivors and a survivor centred approach. The training includes case studies and interactive skills training. Due to MOH restrictions, the training does not include clinical management of rape guidelines or discussion of mandatory reporting. Clinic staff will provide initial case management (within MOH restrictions) and referrals to specialist providers. Referral mapping will be carried out in each supported area. Where Medair staff or surveyors are able to refer GBV survivors, the GBV subsector referral pathway will be used.
The activities extend Medair’s multisectoral programming in Aleppo, Homs, Dara’a, Quneitra, Deir Ez Zor (accessed from 2018), southern Idleb (accessed from 2019). . Given the high needs and likely reduction in the presence of other actors, Medair is also actively exploring expanding programming into Hasakeh or Raqqa.

4 - Needs

War related injuries and explosive hazard accidents are contributing factors to the increase in disability prevalence in Syria to an estimated 27% (HNAP 2019). 1/3 of communities are thought to be contaminated with explosive hazards (HNO 2020). In NWS, half of mobility impairment cases are a direct result of conﬂict (TRDWG, 2018). 57% of explosive hazard accident survivors sustain lifelong impairments, including amputations (Mine Action Database 2019).Over half of all households have at least one member with a disability (HNAP 2019). Medair’s data suggests 19% of PLWMI are children, and 44% female. Mobility impairments restrict access to livelihoods, social inclusion and services. PWD report access to healthcare and assistive devices as some of their primary challenges (TRDWG, 2018). Simple interventions are effective: 98% of assistive device beneﬁciaries reported increased independence since receiving the device(s) (Medair, 2019). A recent survey suggests high levels of anxiety/depression in NWS, with respondents reporting a family member experiencing these symptoms at rates of 14-21% (TRDWG, 2018). Due to a severe lack of psychiatrists, specialist treatment is hard to ﬁnd, making appropriate management of MHPSS in primary care a critical need. Service gaps are acute in NES: MHPSS WG mapping in 2019 identiﬁed 80 MHPSS services nationwide, but none in DEZ or RAQ. Women in DEZ described increased responsibility, sexual harassment, intimate partner violence and raised anxiety as consequences of the crisis. Informal social support mechanisms have been affected: women meet less in homes than pre-crisis as they can’t afford tea/sugar (Medair FGD, 2019). ). GBV cases are likely to be grossly under reported due to stigma and mandatory reporting. CHWs/clinic staff report intimate partner violence is common. Child marriage is widespread. Clinics are poorly equipped to support survivors due to restrictions on GBV training and treatment.

5 - Implementation of activities

PLWMI are identiﬁed using Washington screening by clinic staff, CHWs, or the NFI team during HH assessments in supported communities. A trained physiotherapist then uses a standardized tool to assess individual needs at home. Community selection criteria include HNO severity, absence of other service providers and distance to nearest PHCC. Newly accessible/HTR locations are prioritised. All identified PLWMI are eligible for assistance. Assistive devices including wheelchairs/crutches/rollators will be distributed to 455 PLWMI depending on identified needs. Special hygiene kits (incontinence pads/barrier cream) are distributed if needed. Beneficiaries/caregivers are trained on safe device use and maximizing inclusion. Assessment staff are PFA providers and will refer beneficiaries to the PHCC/other providers if MHPSS needs are identified. PDMs monitor beneﬁciary satisfaction. Medair will support up to 62 referrals to specialized physiotherapy if selection criteria are met. This includes the cost of physiotherapy and transport to appointments. Medair will pilot community-based peer support groups to respond to MHPSS needs through a CBO (to be identiﬁed). A curriculum implemented by Medair in Jordan will be adapted for use in Syria. Up to 6 groups of 10 people will be held in the catchment area of a Medair supported PHCC, or an area of other Medair activities. Participants will be identiﬁed by clinic staff/CHWs/NFI staff/community leaders. The groups address grief, stress and depression using a participatory approach to encourage peer support.Clinic staff/CHWs will be trained on GBV and supervised by Medair/DOH. Identification, initial management (including PFA but not CMR due to restrictions) and referral of GBV cases will be by clinic staff. CHWs also identify/refer GBV cases during HH visits for health awareness messaging. Service mapping will take place in targeted communities. All HHs within the catchment area are eligible for services/CHW visits.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Confidentiality of beneficiary data is breached, risking stigmatisation and harm to individuals/HHs: Training all staff on data protection principles.• Discrimination or unfair selection of beneficiaries for household-level interventions: Clear community sensitization on intervention details;Medair to assess all potential beneficiaries ; sign boards during implementation, including contact info for feedback; PDM/PPM conducted by Medair staff.• Head of house reluctant to accept HH interventions:: Medair teams (including CHWs, surveyors, physiotherapists) are predominantly female (85%) to achieve acceptance for HH visits.• PSEA by Medair staff /partner: All Medair and implementing partner staff are trained on PSEA. Reporting systems are in place internally, both in the Medair Syria structure and a confidential email reporting hotline allowing all staff to report concerns directly to GSO (Global Support Office).Complaints box provided at PSS groups and AAP contact details during distributions. PDM surveys include asking about PSEA.Disability• Further stigmatization of families with disabled family members because of prevalent cultural perception: Include variety of community stakeholders and the wider community in inclusion and advocacy training/awareness raising activities• Distribution of assistive devices without adequate assessment and beneficiary/carer education may inadvertently cause harm: Assessments done by trained CHWs and reviewed by a physiotherapist; an external disability consultant will provide training and on the job supervision to ensure quality service delivery; Post distribution monitoring; referral pathways in each location mapped, shared and utilised. • Poor quality implementation of PSS groups causes psychological harm: PSS groups will be led by trained facilitators. Feedback on facilitation quality obtained by direct observation and from beneficiaries. Initial training and technical supervision will be provided by skilled Medair staff.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS- No significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria and the GoS continue to hold control over recently-retaken governorates- No excessive fluctuations affecting value of the Syrian pound and any inflation/deflation will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD)- Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions- A suitable implementing partner is identified and able to meet Medair’s partnership due diligence requirements- Referral facilities with appropriately trained staff and adequate equipment are accessible from the targeted communitiesRISKS- Temporary suspension of programme if insecurity status is too high, delaying project delivery- Medair cannot secure approvals from ministries within the timeline outlined, delaying project delivery- Implementing partner has political or other affiliations that damage Medair’s reputation- Either trainers or participants are unable to obtain timely visas for PSS group training- Medair staff or partners submit fraudulent expenses/do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines leading to loss of financial resource.MITIGATING MEASURES:- Robust, regular context analysis as part of security management- Keep security planning up to date and train a crisis management team for each location - Daily, careful monitoring of security situation (INSO, UNDSS, National Staff etc.) including close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues - Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders- Identify potential implementing partners early and begin partnership approval process promptly- Complete training for all new staff on procurement guidelines, systematic internal and external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines, spot checks for any fraudulent activity.
