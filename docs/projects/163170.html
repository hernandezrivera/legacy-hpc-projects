Project Summary

This project aims to provide basic shelter assistance to hard-to-reach communities identified by Reach Out in Fako and Meme Divisions.
These communities are found between Munyenge and Bai Panya. These are critically affected areas where the current Anglophone crisis started and is most intense, but that have barely received humanitarian assistance and have so far received no shelter kits.
Due to the extreme sensitivity of the conflict, the shelter kits do not include construction tools that could be rejected by either parties of the conflict (such incidents have been reported from both the Non-State Armed Groups of Fako, and the Senior Divisional Officer of Manyu, as well as other stakeholders) as they could be used as weapons. Distributing such items would put beneficiaries at risk. Moreover, the project responds to direct community feedback that has consistently been the need for tarpaulins to improve on the temporary shelter structures currently developed in self-settled communities. Communities are not requesting for full shelter kits, but consistently requesting for tarpaulin and mosquito nets. This, alongside regular accompaniment by Reach Out engineers will ensure the construction of 2,000 temporary shelters protecting at least 10,000 persons.
Activities:
1) Access mediation
Introductory meetings will take place at community level to ensure acceptance for Reach Out’s activities and respect for neutrality and safety during distribution.
2) Community mapping of shelter needs
Before distribution, a series of village assemblies will be programmed in each community. There, the project will be outlined, and a sample of the shelter materials presented to the community. Then, men, women, boys, girls, and the elderly, will specify their needs in terms of shelter, and earmark critical households in need of shelter support. In these gender-segregated spaces, specific resolutions to serve each group effectively and with dignity will also be adopted.
A draft beneficiary list will be collected by Reach Out, who will then program distribution accordingly.
The community will be allowed to suggest the use of shelter kits, specifically Plastic Sheeting for community projects such as latrines.
3) Validation of beneficiaries
• Validation is carried out by Reach Out alongside designated community members. A sample of prospective beneficiaries are visited and their current shelter conditions evaluated to confirm the need for the kits.
• The results of the validation process are presented in front of the community.
4) Shelter Kit Distribution (2,000 kits, targeting 10,000 persons at least)
1) Market Assessments
2) Call for tenders
3) Review Panel and selection of service providers
4) Purchase of commodities
5) Quality control
6) Packaging and labeling
7) Transportation to Buea
8) Warehousing
9) Secondary transportation to Distribution sites.
Each family will receive 2 Branded plastic Sheeting (4m x 5m) with 4 aluminum reinforced holes, 20 meters of rope and 2 insecticide-impregnated mosquito nets.
5) Monitoring and evaluation.
Embedded in all activities, independent calls, verification rounds and focus group discussions are also organized.
After distribution, 02 community meetings are organized to ensure proper use of shelter kits among beneficiaries and to evaluate the project.
Integration with other sectors:
1) To ensure cases of abuse are reported and attended, distribution staff and Monitoring Committees will be trained on Child Protection, GBV guidelines, Psychosocial First Aid and the local referral pathways.

Besoins / Needs

A civil war began in December 2017 when insurgency groups fighting for the independence of the two Anglophone regions attacked military posts in Meme, Manyu, and Ndian. They were immediately answered with indiscriminate village raids conducted by the state military. By May 2018, all Divisions of the North-West and South-West were the scenario of an active guerrilla war, and hundreds of thousands of people were displaced. The International Office for Migration (IOM) estimated in August 2019 that 203,000 persons in the South-West and North-West are returnees, in addition to the 485,784 currently displaced from the crisis. Adding the numbers, 688,784 persons have been displaced at some point of the past year. Displaced persons don´t feel safe in areas controlled by the state, they have been victims of military attacks and they are now afraid of random shooting and arrest. Many prefer to stay in remote areas with blocked roads where the army cannot access. In these communities, there´s limited or no access to basic services and humanitarian assistance. In these camps, they have built tents with bamboo and palm leaves, or populated farming structures such as cocoa ovens. Many shelters are not sufficiently protective against the weather, insects, and pests. The most precious construction material in this communities is a tarpaulin plastic sheeting to protect households and community spaces against rain. While many communities follow a pendulum movement, where displaced persons move between their original village and the forest communities, and re-settle in the village after hostilities resume, there are critical communities such as Munyenge that are completely displaced in the forest. This is because hostilities are going on constantly in the original village since 2018, and the military and NSAG have both set-up permanent operations in the village. This project will target such communities with shelter assistance, to ensure each kit used creates the most impact. Reach Out began responding to the needs of displaced persons in early 2018. A consistent piece of beneficiary feedback that has never been satisfied in Meme, Ndian, and Fako has been the need of Shelter Kits, tarpaulin, tents, and mosquito nets. Serve as an example the community of Munyenge. Reach Out received 33 complaints and pieces during food distribution on the month of October. Of these, 45% were not actually complaints about food distribution, but requests for tarpaulins and tents. Women in rural communities of the South-West do not equally participate in community decisions. This tendency has been even reinforced by the crisis, as those who speak up in community gatherings could be misinterpreted and attacked by parties of the conflict, and women are afraid to participate. There will be need to use gender-segregated spaces with equal decision power in each community gathering, to ensure their voices are heard. In the current conflict, families separate, leaving the woman and the children in a community, while the man looks for opportunities somewhere else. Because of this, women-headed households are more vulnerable, require increased attention, and will be prioritized by the project. This also includes young girls, who have been exposed to early sexual relationships and pregnancy, and have now compounded vulnerabilities. Having access to shelter is capital for them to begin an empowerment process. The current conflict has exposed the elderly, as many of them have been unable to relocate in urban areas, and they have been forced to the extremely challenging conditions of the forest. They will need to be prioritized during community targeting. Appropriate community mechanisms will have to be in place to ensure that the stronger and faster do not capitalize on the assistance, leaving out the elderly.
