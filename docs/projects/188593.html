Project Summary

The proposed project’s WASH interventions seek to improve the humanitarian situation for the war-affected population by restoring water and sanitation systems that will be able to respond to the increasing demand in the overburdened communities because of the continuous arrival of tens of thousands of forcefully displaced people because of warfare. The action will be combined with emergency response WASH activities to the affected population in Idleb and Aleppo.
Directly 71877 people will benefit. The coordination with local councils, camp management, and other actors is the first step for all outputs. Through the proposed project SIMRO will produce the following outputs:
• Undertake the rehabilitation for two water systems with operation and maintenance for three months with gradual activation for cost recovery system: the rehabilitation includes provision and installation of submersible and horizontal pump set pump sets, generator sets, chlorine dosing pumps, with all valves and other accessories. Also, the rehabilitation includes the Construction of the water station building that included (generator room, pumping room, worker room, and groundwater tank and repairing the water network.
- Rehabilitation of one sewage systems: the rehabilitation includes excavation, extension concrete pipes, reinforced concrete for manholes, and cleaning 35 manholes. 21450 BNFs will be served by this activity.
- Water trucking and solid waste collections for IDPs in 8 informal settlements in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. 105 m3 of safe drinking and chlorinated water will be delivered to 3000 BNFs for 6 months in the average of 25l/day/BNF will provide to most vulnerable IDPs by trucks, including daily water quality control in the water source, trucks, and household level.
- Daily removing 10 trips of 4m3 of solid waste for 6 months to serve 3000 BNFs.
- Hygiene kits to most vulnerable IDPs. 1500 hygiene kits will be distributed to most vulnerable IDPs in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. the selection criteria include women or child-headed households, disables, more than 5 individuals per family, who did not receive hygiene kit recently. verification will be done, and people who meet the criteria will be listed in the final distribution list. Within the distribution, two separate queues for men and women in distribution points and there will be chairs for elderly people. PDM report will be conducted after distribution to measure the level of satisfaction

4 - Needs

Access to the WASH service is a major barrier, among many others, for newly displaced and most vulnerable people. Moreover, the lack of clean water, storage containers, and hygiene items potentially increase risks of possible outbreaks of water-borne diseases. An initial desk study conducted by SIMRO of available resources found the proposed areas of intervention to be in critical need of WASH interventions. Currently, water systems are inoperable due to improper operation, absence of maintenance, looting of equipment and facilities, and lack of management systems (water utility) to ensure the sustainable operation of the system. Deterioration of the infrastructure and equipment in addition to the overall effects of the crisis and sanctions also feeds into the deterioration of the infrastructure. According to SIMRO’s recent infrastructure study, targeted locations (Harim, Idleb, Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Jebel Saman) require 43% of its infrastructure to be restored. Based on HNO 2021, more than 162,176 people are in need of WASH assistance in the targeted communities (Harim, Idleb, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Jebel Saman, Albab and Az’az), which is about 65% of the total population (700,000 people), with 60% being children. Furthermore, all the targeted communities are ranking 4 to 6 out of 6 in terms of the water-need severity scale.

5 - Implementation of activities

This Project has been developed in coordination with Cluster leads/ WASH and is in line with the priorities of a focus on reducing WBD by improving access to water in targeted locations through Restoring water stations and distribution networks (Repair/replace/extend water distribution network and ensure wide connection); Provision of supplies and materials (fuels, water disinfectant (chlorine), oil and spare parts) necessary for systems operation; and capacity development of water utility unit through staff training on operation and maintenance and the establishment of cost recovery system to ensure self-operating. High prevalence of water-borne diseases (WBD) such as acute diarrhea, acute bloody diarrhea. * Water and Sewage system rehabilitation: - Confirm the BOQs and drawings through a field visit to achieve the last technical assessment - Starting the procurement and contracting phase for excavation, provision, and installations of the pipelines, and backfilling the pipes, in addition to the installation of equipment (Genest, Pumps, electrical panels..) - Installation phase for signed contracts. * Water trucking and solid waste collections for IDPs. - Contracting phase with water tankers and solid waste collection trucks. - Providing water for IDPs in the daily average of 35l/day/BNF, in the total of 105 m3/daily for 6 months. - Daily removing solid waste for 6 months. * Hygiene kits to most vulnerable IDPs. - Beneficiary selection and verification. - Procurement phase.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

SIMRO is committed to mainstreaming protection into all the projects and does so through the use of several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff in relation to protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. The collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries and in the presence of MOUs with partner organizations. Furthermore, SIMRO documentation policies, including photography and videography, strongly consider protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity. Participants and beneficiaries will be protected by hired staff. the principle of “Do No Harm” is of utmost importance in the implementation of the project, and staff will take care not to put others in any unnecessary risk, taking into account the existing security situation at a given time and adapting to changing contexts as needed to further ensure safety as a priority. The concept will be added when providing safe and clean water to the household, in this way, women and children will no longer be forced to face the dangers that they may encounter waiting in long lines in order to receive water from water trucks.
