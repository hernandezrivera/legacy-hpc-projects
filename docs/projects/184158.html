Project Summary

An estimated 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden) face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 likely to be severely malnourished. Urgent nutrition and health support is required to address their needs. Urgent health and nutrition support is also required for areas with high prevalence of acute maternal malnutrition. • Coverage of health and nutrition services (both treatment and prevention), including vitamin A supplementation and measles vaccination should be expanded, especially in areas where coverage is currently low.
RRP will provide treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) through OTP (Outpatient Therapeutic) services in line with the Basic Nutrition Service Package (BNSP) at up to 2 fixed and 4 outreaches in Mahas and Bulo Burto district. RRP will train and support nutrition workers to identify, provide lifesaving treatment and follow up on cases of malnutrition. RRP will also integrate preventative services including providing Infant Youth and Child Feeding (IYCF) counselling, micronutrients supplementation at MCHs and routine immunization. Community level social mobilization will be done to promote healthy nutritional practices, including campaigns for micronutrient supplementation, deworming, and improved feeding practices. RRP will actively participate in both national and Hirshabelle regional cluster meetings. Participation of the community groups such as women, youth, and local leaders will be prioritized so as to enhance and get more feedback as an aspect of accountability to the affected population. RRP will do monthly, quarterly, midyear and end year reporting and will work towards reduction of the high prevalence of SAM and MAM in the two districts. Hence reducing mortality and morbidity rates related to malnutrition and improve, provide and integrated, nutrition related resilience interventions life-saving and quality nutrition services to acutely malnourished children and PLWs through community based nutritional service. RRP will also increase access and utilization of BNSP, MCH services to acutely malnourished boys & girls U5, and PLW and integrate WASH services.
Capacity building to the community health workers, volunteers, care givers and field staff and strengthen the existing community based nutrition service structures to provide comprehensive basic nutritional service packages (BNSP) to malnourished children U5 and PLW in the three districts. PSEA will be a part of the training that will be offered to RRP staff, they will further be tasked to offer the same training to the community for them to understand how to report cases as well as to equip them with knowledge on what their right is.

RRP is Commit to collective approaches to community engagement and accountability in order to ensure that RRP decision-making and strategic planning is informed by feedback data from affected communities, including, proactive efforts to include the voices of women and girls, as well as vulnerable and marginalized groups. Linkages to be sought between the donor Common Feedback Project coordinated Communication and Community Engagement Initiative closely collaborating with the Protection health, WASH and food security to handle some of the cases. RRP will focus of the humanitarian response and provision of protection services has been accessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance or in drought impacted communities in accessible, less conflict prone areas. Areas with active conflict, will be benefiting from all measure of the health humanitarian response, have seen minimal protection engagement through the efforts of some highly motivated individual at the community. RRP will engage further in conflict affected areas through various actions including but not limited to engaging the various gatekeepers and the local authorities and the IMAMs. Local authorities in Hiran region will be the key individuals to work with in order to ensure that the target women of child bearing age and the children under five are taken care of in times of crisis. The target population is at risk of displacement that may pose human rights violation. The Protection Risk Analysis highlights the extremely difficult situation in IDP settlements and collective centres across the country, in light of growing displacement, due to drought and conflict and continuing protracted and secondary/multiple displacement situations (including the particular challenges facing refugee returnees who get displaced again). The IDPs in these displacement locations frequently live in undignified and hazardous circumstances, where they face multiple protection risks/threats such as, unlawful evictions, overcrowded and unsanitary environments with limited access to basic services, exposure to explosive hazards, increased risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage and child labor, and tension with the host community. Children and young people (mainly male) in displaced situation, particularly those from Al-Shabaab controlled areas face risks of arbitrary arrest once out of those controlled areas, and therefore live in fear and sometimes isolation. Lastly, IDPs continue to struggle to end their displacement and pursue voluntary, informed, safe, and dignified durable solutions based on their individual and household needs.
