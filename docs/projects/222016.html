Project Summary

In 2025, REACH proposes to continue supporting the humanitarian response by being an active member of humanitarian coordination structures and providing information management and assessment to the humanitarian community.
Within a context of reducing humanitarian resources, REACH aims to continually inform and enable evidence-based decision-making within the humanitarian coordination structure and individual agencies for strategic prioritisation and programming. This will be facilitated through the provision of robust and reliable data on the most vulnerable populations and locations and provision of technical support to joint analysis, to ensure critical life-saving assistance is provided. In order to achieve this goal, REACH aims to conduct regular monitoring of humanitarian needs across the country, needs assessments and information management and support clusters and coordination structures to identify locations at risk of excess mortality to inform ongoing resource prioritisation and decision-making.

1. Needs

Since independence in 2011, populations in South Sudan continue to experience extremely severe and widespread needs across most sectors, manifesting most notably in prevalent acute food insecurity, acute malnutrition, and morbidity. Populations across South Sudan remain impacted by a range of long-term and worsening underlying vulnerabilities, including virtually non-existent public health infrastructure, widespread acute food insecurity, malnutrition, and disease. In 2024, 9 million people (excluding refugees) were reported to face critical needs. While the humanitarian data and evidence ecosystem has been transformed over recent years by numerous forces, research continues to demonstrate at best a limited link between humanitarian diagnostics, humanitarian decision making and resource allocation processes. In a context characterized by acute and recurring shocks further weaking structural vulnerabilities, the information management system needs to have steady and dedicated resources providing open-source monitoring flagging areas of concern for further in-depth assessments. In parallel, to avoid duplication of data collection efforts and foster a solid partnership between information management and operational stakeholders, coordination structures and joint analyis processess need to be upheld to support a joint understanding of needs and priorities.

2. Project activities

To supprt a well-coordinated and evidence-based humanitarian response, REACH activities will focus on two main objectives : Monitoring stress and shocks and Supporting Humanitarian Prioritisation. First, REACH will provide data to humanitarian stakeholders on multi-sectoral stress and shocks and explore their drivers and impact on humanitarian needs. This will be implemented through REACH key research cycles induding: 1) Quarterly multi-sectoral data collection accross the country through the Area of Knowledge (AoK) methodology to provide regular update on needs and their trends; 2) Pilot of the Acute Needs Framework (ANF) aiming to identify areas with risks of excess mortality to support evidence-based advocacy and prompt emergency action; 3) Inform Public Health needs in specific locations through SMART Surveys focusing on the prevalence of wasting, stunting, and underweight among vulnerable populations and Public Health Assessments to identify immediate priorities; 4) Conduct ad-hoc rapid need assessments (multi-sectoral or sector specific based on needs) to identify the most pressing needs following a shock such as natural hazard or displacement; 5) Provide regular updates on market trends through its Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) implemented in coordination with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and ad-hoc market assessments. Second, REACH will contribute to the design of an evidence-based humanitarian response strategy through technical contribution and joint analysis with key stakeholders of the response through 1) its support to OCHA that is coordinating priorization in areas of concern through REACH co-chairing of the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG); 2) support the design of the 2026 HNRP by contributing to the 2025 Inter Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) alongside OCHA and IOM; 3) support to joint analysis such as the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) and any other joint analysis implemented by coordination bodies such as the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster, CWG, Communication and Community Engagement Working Group (CCEWG), NGO Forum, etc; 4) Mainstream and conduct dedicated Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) research to assess community perspectives on humanitarian assistance coupled with system-wide recommendations. All research activities led by REACH will be coordinated with key stakeholders of the response to avoid dupplication of data collection efforts and promote a high uptake of REACH assessment' findings. Throughout its work, REACH also ensures that its assessments and technical contributions enable affected populations to communicate on their most severe needs and priorities to support an evidence-based response centred on the experiences of those affected by crisis.
