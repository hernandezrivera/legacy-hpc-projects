Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in North East Nigeria. UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by identifying and minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed and by expanding the breadth and quality of protection response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa through:
1. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring, including through community-based mechanisms, and protection by presence;
2. Quality of registration and profiling of Persons of concerns
3. Enhanced access to legal assistance and legal remedies;
4. Enhanced access to Civil registration, including civil status documentation;
5. Advocacy and capacity building in support of the development of Law and policy, including the domestication of the KC;
6. Promote Self-reliance through access to protection safety-net activities
7. Support the discussions and the processes for durable solutions

1 - Needs

The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States has been characterized by mass human rights violations, brutal attacks, abduction, rampant sexual and gender-based violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, family separation, disappearances, and forced displacement. Widespread abuses, coupled with the break down in civil administration structures, law and order throughout the conflict, has seen the erosion of the protective environment for those in most affected areas. The most vulnerable civilians face heightened protection risks, including female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, adolescent boys and girls, the elderly, chronically ill, people with physical and mental disabilities, pregnant and lactating women within IDP camps, host communities and return areas. Recovery efforts continue to be hampered in many areas by lack of basic infrastructures, conflict-driven insecurity resulting in fresh displacements, limited access and availability of basic services and absence of relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations. These exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable segment of the population. Threats are often compounded by the high level of congestion among displaced families, lack of basic resources for survival and by the location of vulnerable individuals in isolated, insecure or inhospitable places. UNHCR has conducted multiple rounds of vulnerability screening for the displaced population, as well as protection monitoring and assessments which has captured the severity of protection needs for those most affected by conflict. Survivors of sexual and gender-based violence face rejection and stigmatization by their families and community members. Additionally, the conflict has disrupted the social protection structures in the community, separated children from their families and resulted in serious child protection critical concerns. Due to the protracted insurgency, a new phenomenon has emerged with hundreds of thousands of Unaccompanied and Separated Children, UASC; thousands more have been orphaned by their death of their parents, leading to many child-headed households. Given their young ages, many of these children are rendered destitute with many resorting to hawking and begging on the streets as means of survival. There has been a sharp increase in the instances of attacks, harassment, abduction and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV) when women and girls venture outside sites' security perimeter in order to search of firewood. The other challenges exist for boys in reintegration, especially those who had been forcibly recruited and or affiliated with Boko Haram militants. The absence of civil authorities in newly accessible areas and civil authorities’ functions has undermined the humanitarian and civilian character of IDP sites. These duties have been undertaken by military with a lot of concern on the respect of the persons of concern to UNHCR. In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded the precarious protection situation in the region. The need to continue to scale up efforts to mitigate, prevent, refer and respond to cases with identified protection risks, and effective protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration and coordination with all stakeholders on durable solutions is an imperative in the humanitarian response. In line with the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy, the protection of affected persons must remain central in humanitarian intervention, decision making and informing the strategic response. As per the Displacement Tracking Matrix, DTM, (Round 33, August 2020), confirms that there is a total of 1,714,682 displaced population comprising of IDPs and returnees in the BAY States. Out of which, 138,366 are refugee returnees from Chad, Niger and Cameroon who have returned to their places of origin.

2 - Activities

A wide range of activities will be implemented under 5 main axis of intervention namely favorable protection environment, fair protection processes and legal documentation, Security from violence and exploitation, durable solutions, Community Empowerment and Self Reliance and eight objectives. Favourable Protection Environment: • Implement the Tripartite Agreement between Nigeria and Cameroon and develop Tripartite agreements with Niger and Chad Republics • Coordination with relevant stakeholders to implement UNHCR protection strategy; Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring and protection by presence: UNHCR will do the following: • Strengthen its protection monitoring system and enhance presence in the field; • Continue and strengthen detention centers and facilities monitoring as well as efforts to seek access to military screening and detention centers; • Analyze information on protection risks and track incident trends Quality of registration and profiling: • Profile and track vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees through the continuous vulnerability screening processes to allow for delivery of assistance tailored to specific needs, such as referrals to specialized services including, child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical responses by UNHCR and Protection Sector partners. • In collaboration with the competent national institutions, continue the registration of spontaneous refugee returnees to ensure the planning of comprehensive reintegration programs. Civil registration and civil status documentation: • scale up its support to national institutions for the issuance of civil status documentation namely birth certificates and letters of indigene. Law and policy developed: • Advocacy efforts for the domestication of the Kampala Convention on IDPs through the adoption of a national law for IDP protection in Nigeria. • Carry out capacity building activities for relevant national institutions, humanitarian actors and the affected population; as well as provide support and advocacy to all stakeholders to ensure that assistance is provided in adherence to international standards and respect for core protection principles to by service providers. Community Empowerment and Self-reliance UNHCR will actively support: • Strengthening the support to livelihoods and response to stimulate recovery especially for women and groups with specific needs; • Providing protection-based material assistance. construction of shelters and provision of core-relief items.

Protection mainstreaming will be an important element of this project from the designed, implementation, monitoring ang reporting faces. This will be achieved through the following modalities; 1. The safety and dignity of our population of concern who are the right owners will be prioritized. All faces of the project must be analysed to prioritize and respect safety and dignity of persons of concern. 2. Also the project will provide ample access the rights owners through the project cycle. 3. Furthermore, there will be continues engagement with the right owners and setup of information centre at UNHCR protection desks to provide a channel for feedback to inform reassessment of needs to ensure the real needs of the people are addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will leverage its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity AGD, mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express complaints freely and in a confidential manner; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and ensure they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these mechanisms, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention, in a timely manner. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of protection-based materials. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, PSEA. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality; and respecting the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by ensuring that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to the provision of assistance. For this reason, UNHCR will create a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) process, maintain open and continuous channels of communications with various stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities and beneficiaries. This will be done through Participatory Approach, which is critical to guide activities and to monitor the evolution of the protracted insurgency and its aftermath on the affected populations.
