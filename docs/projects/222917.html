Project Summary

Toward 2025, BRAC's Child Protection programme aims to continue strengthening the child protection system and services across the Rohingya and host communities for the well-being of the children and adolescents. The objective of the BRAC Child Protection programme aligns with the objective and target of the CPSS. BRAC has proposed full package of child protection intervention through 11 multi-purpose centre, 67 child friendly space/ humanitarian play lab including home-base support in 11 camps and 3 unions of the host community. The programme will target 51,041 (57% female) individual children, adolescents, community members, and stakeholders.
This programme will ensure the continuation of existing services, e.g. unstructured Psychosocial Support (PSS), structured PSS, case management services including UASC, adolescent engagement activities, e.g. life skills-based sessions, positive parenting sessions, community-based child protection mechanism, e.g., community-based child protection committee (CBCPC), outreach activities, early childhood development (ECD) activities, social cohesion activities, and maintain sectoral coordination with a priority focus on integrated services for children with disabilities. Children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) will be supported through case management, focusing on the identification, referral, and prevention programme. The programme will also work to monitor the situation of child rights violations. It will support the Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS) in doing FGD & KII, analysing the child protection situation, and monitoring reports. The gender-diversified children groups and children with disabilities will be supported through this programme’s activities. The referral pathway system and support for the targeted children from all gender, cultures, ethnicities, and other backgrounds will be ensured to enhance effective and efficient programme support. The intervention will engage and encourage the adolescents in various activities, e.g., skills development and dialogue within the community, to promote the empowerment of children, adolescent girls, and boys in the targeted communities. Therefore, the programme will adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18-year-old children and adolescents, and above 18 years (female and male in different camps and host communities) will be reached with the CP services. To provide the above-mentioned child protection services, a holistic approach will be applied.
BRAC will consider the most vulnerable population in this programme and prioritise safety, dignity, and meaningful access and participation of children, adolescents, and community to ensure the integrity of the programme. To promote protection of children, cross-cutting areas, e.g., accountability to affected population (AAP), climate change adaptation, emergency preparedness and response, and youth engagement will also be strategically engaged.
BRAC will maintain strong coordination with the CPSS and CMTWG by attending the different meetings and workshops and providing technical support where required. This CP programme will also coordinate with the Protection Working Group (PWG), Youth Working Group, and ECD Working Group as well as with the government, e.g., Department of Social Service, through active participation and contribution to ensure effective programme exposure and intervention. BRAC will make sectoral inputs to the situation report, 5W report, and need-based report, including the child protection situation monitoring and child rights report.
BRAC will apply its safeguarding policy, CP Code of Conduct, to ensure the safeguarding and Protection From Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) related cases of the staff, volunteers and programme participants in alignment with the ISCG guidelines. The Minimum Standard for Child Protection (CPMS) in humanitarian action guidelines will be followed while implementing the interventions to ensure “Do no Harm”.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Among 1,003,394 Rohingya people living in Cox’s Bazar, 52% are children who are mostly dependent on humanitarian support (UNHCR-Govt. Population factsheet September 2024). Children living in Cox’s Bazar (camps and host communities) are still facing various and complex protection risks i.e., neglect, psychological distress, physical abuse, child marriage, child labour, etc., and challenges that reflect their different vulnerabilities, needs, strengths, and capacities depending on a number of intersecting factors, such as age, gender, disability, and other relevant diversity.According to the trend analysis of child protection concerns (CPIMS+, CPSS, January-September 2024), it showed that 31.86% children are neglected, 11.57% children affected with psychological distress, 10.48% children experienced physical abuse, 7.84% children affected by emotional or psychological abuse, 7.58% engaged in child labor, 5.49% children have physical disability or impairment, 5.18% children are identified as unaccompanied and separated, 4.53% adolescents have experienced or at risk of child marriage, 1.36℅ children experienced at risk of trafficking, and 1.13% are sexual abuse or sexual exploitation (or at risk of). The Rohingya influx has passed seven years and entered eight years with no significant progress on the repatriation as well as insufficient livelihood opportunities, education, etc., in the camp. According to the Inter-Sector Joint Needs Assessment (ISNA, 2024), 10.35% of children have a sign of psychological distress that include anxiety, depression, etc. While conducting household survey under the J-MSNA 2024, the respondents ‘against the question on the reason of why children left the house’ said that 43.4% (out of total respondents) of Rohingya refugee children aged under 18 years reported being married who left the house, and on the other side, the percentage (out of total respondents) being married in host community is 4.5% which is less than the camp. According to the Joint Protection Monitoring Report for the 2nd quarter of 2024, reported, child recruitment raised in almost all refugee camps. Children aged 14 to 18 were coerced into joining armed groups. These groups primarily targeted young boys with prior training in Myanmar. Dusk has been identified as the most dangerous time for abductions for recruitment in the camps. Children were either coerced into joining armed groups or do so voluntarily, often driven by financial incentive.Based on the Child Protection Situation Analysis (CPSS, October 2024), physical and emotional maltreatment has also been observed as significant. According to the KII as a part of child protection situation monitoring in 2024, the respondent (69.15% of adult respondent) reported the incident of child neglect while 60.57% of respondent indicated physical violence against children. The reason of the neglect and physical violence agains children include lack of awareness (20.4%), domestic violence (19.5%), lack of livelihood for the caregiver (18.0%), lack of resource (15.3%) etc. The REACH report showed that 12% has at least one disability. While the UNHCR-GoB factsheet (as of September 2024) identified persons with disabilities is 3.02%. The prevalence rate of disabilities was found to be increasing, ranging from 2% among 2 to 4 years old and to 51% among older persons (Source: REACH, May 2021, “Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment”). The scenario was almost the same for the host communities. The unstable situation of the camps, such as internal conflict and gunfights, generates anxiety, aggression, depression, and various sorts of psychosocial stress, which need to be addressed and treated properly through comprehensive and structured Mental Health, and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). Moreover, it is observed that there is a lack of social cohesion between the host and Rohingya communities, which results in tension among them.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Child Protection programme will closely work with the Education, general protection, and Gender-based Violence (GBV) programmes to ensure relevant services for children, adolescents, women, Children with Disabilities, etc. This programme will provide integrated services along with general protection and GBV. As part of this, BRAC will also consider protection mainstreaming through ensuring meaningful access, safety and dignity, awareness-raising, and community engagement activities, where all protection messages will be disseminated to the target children, adolescents, and community members to enhance the knowledge of the protection mainstreaming mechanism. Especially, BRAC will make reasonable accommodations and need-based tailored for the programme activities to ensure the meaningful participation, accessibility, safety and dignity of children with disabilities and other vulnerable groups in the project activities. In addition, BRAC will modify the existing content, which will be more inclusive for the targeted children with disability and community members. Heighten protection concerns cases will be referred to the relevant sectors and ensure they get better support from the programme personnel. GBV-related cases received under the Child Protection programme where survivors under 18 years old will be referred to the respective sectors (e.g., MHPSS, GBV, and legal). This programme will also orient the staff, volunteers, Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC), and caregivers on GBV, protection, CFRM, and service mapping. BRAC will also adopt and follow the protection principle during the implementation of programme. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended adverse effects of any programme.

2.3 - Age & Disability

BRAC will remain actively engaged in ensuring equal opportunity for the participation and fair distribution of humanitarian assistance without discrimination, abuse, neglect or exploitation of any population group. This project will ensure gender equality and disability inclusion through awareness sessions, parent meetings, community consultation, Community Based Child Protection Committee, etc. Besides, BRAC will emphasize Gender and sexual orientation diversity in its programme design by integrating and involving people from diverse backgrounds through different intervention activities. As part of this, the elderly will be reached through awareness-raising activities, engaging Community-Based Child Protection Committees, parent engagement, and community consultations. BRAC will be imperative to ensure that protective measures are not only culturally sensitive but also provide equal access to essential services and opportunities for all members of the Rohingya and host communities. BRAC has a commitment to creating a more just and equitable environment for all Rohingya individuals, where the vulnerabilities of age and disability are addressed comprehensively, ensuring that no one within the community is left behind in their pursuit of safety, dignity, and well-being. This holistic approach prioritizes preventing harm, promoting resilience, and providing support regardless of age and disability. To make bridging through multi-level child protection activities and minimize the gap among the target community, particularly for the elderly and Persons with Disabilities. Reasonable accommodation, need-based tailoring, and person-specific project activities will be built and incorporated into the project design and service points. BRAC will design holistic session content for all backgrounds of children in the sessions. This year, at least 3% of the targeted participants will be from the Persons with Disabilities in BRAC's Child Protection programme.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) is an active commitment to be responsible by considering giving account to and being held accountable by the people humanitarian organizations seek to assist. BRAC will provide scope to the project participants from diverse communities with a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory arrangements. Besides, BRAC will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently.The designated programme is accountable to the affected population, e.g., boys, girls, youth, and diversified population. Under the pillars of AAP, BRAC will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities and special needs through the programme activities, which include the participation of the persons with disabilities and taking opinions during the designing, planning, and implementing of any activities in the programme and ensuring the transparency of the programme. However, in all stages of the interventions, the programme will ensure the effective and meaningful participation of the community following CPSS and ISCG AAP guidelines. The project will continue to receive feedback through established feedback help desks in all facilities to address opinions and suggestions of the community to strengthen the protection services following BRAC's procedures. Besides, project volunteers and staff will raise outreach awareness/ session on the community feedback mechanism ensuring the feedback from the persons with disabilities. Following the BRAC's guidelines (with technical support from CPSS), feedback and complaints will be addressed. On the other hand, to make the programme sustain, CBCPC, youth group, adolescent group, and community volunteers will be oriented and guided on how to take responsibility.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC is a member of PSEA network and has policies, procedures, and mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse and other Safeguarding related issues. BRAC has been driving utmost effort in ensuring the safety and well-being of programme participants, staff, and the community at large. BRAC maintains a safeguarding umbrella policy and 5 subset policies including PSHEA policy, Child and adolescent protection policy, Whistle blowing policy, Prevention of Workplace Bullying and Violence Policy and Adult with special needs policy aimed at preventing PSEA and child abuse across all stakeholder interactions. To ensure BRAC’s AAP, a feedback mechanism such as complaint boxes in service points, 24-hour helpline numbers of PSEA focal, sensitisation on right-based assistance in existing information provisions, orient community volunteers, vendors, and consultants on PSEA matters to ensure BRAC’s commitment on ‘do no harm’ will be in place.BRAC IEC materials are in line with PSEA network materials and will utilise those materials to raise awareness among Rohingya and host communities. BRAC will follow the Inter-Agency SoPs for SEA complaint referral in Rohingya Response and acknowledge that reports of sexual exploitation and abuse are mandatory, investigation, follow-up, and potential disciplinary action against SEA offenders will be based on the BRAC’s internal procedures. In 2025, the programme will ensure that target children, both communities, can easily raise their voices through the CFRM box, BRAC's helpline, and community consultation meetings. An autonomous body that is built to maintain the CP programme and BRAC team will be operational in addressing issues by following and adopting all protection principles and privacy and ensuring the required support. BRAC will closely coordinate with the national network and conduct reference checks about new recruitments to ensure that SEA offenders are not employed or re-hired.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. BRAC has been exploring methods for climate change response, adaptation, and mitigation through the program. In the Child Protection programme, BRAC has prioritized participant engagement to improve flexibility and resilience via community consultations, involvement with the Community Based Child Protection Committee, and engagement with parents and caregivers. BRAC Child Protection has incorporated climate change information, content, awareness messaging, and mitigation techniques into the session modules, emphasizing the crucial role of community members in alleviating the adverse effects of climate change. BRAC would commence planting within their facility premises and inside the centre, fostering a greater empathy towards nature and the environment. BRAC will adopt the sectoral guidance to incorporate the programme for designing and implementing more climate-friendly activities for the targeted community. BRAC Child Protection programme will empower and encourage children, adolescents, youth, and community people to protect the environment through their small steps towards nature, which might be implemented through awareness sessions, structure sessions, and meetings with the community. BRAC initiated a reduction in the use of plastic play materials for children rather than wooden play materials from the existing programme. BRAC Child Protection will coordinate, collaborate and communicate with SCCCM to ensure the lighting for nighttime protection for children, adolescents, children with disabilities and more vulnerable communities. This project will engage the stakeholders, including the humanitarian actors and community, to ensure the lighting system for vulnerable children.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. BRAC HCMP has already adopted an Emergency Operation Centre (EOC), Emergency Response Team (ERT), and Business Contingency Plan (BCP) structure with representatives from all programmes to respond in an integrated way in emergencies. During any emergency, all programmes work jointly in the affected areas by conducting primary assessments and analysing the situations. This project will work in a holistic approach, considering the disaster risk reduction plan to ensure all the facilities are kept safe from disaster and make sure as a temporary shelter. It will provide essential disaster risk management and emergency response training to the programme staff and volunteers, including community volunteers, to respond effectively during an emergency. BRAC follows the guidelines of the RRRC and ISCG during the disaster period to avoid the duplicity and effectiveness of the response strategies and would utilize CP facilities as a temporary shelter upon the coordination with SCCCM and CiC. The BRAC's DRR and CP programme team will collaborate closely with Site Management, Camp in Charge (CiC), Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), and the Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO) to organise awareness sessions on hazards. It will help in preparing this intervention to mitigate the risks associated with potential landslides, cyclones, and floods. An ERT will be established to respond to during any emergency. Evacuation of identified Persons with Disabilities will be ensured through the ERT in case of any issues raised during the disaster. In case of any child or adolescent’s needs, special care during an emergency or crisis, one-to-one counselling at these centres will be available. Regular assessment-based precautions will be taken to address the DRR issues. BRAC EOC will adhere to the guiding principles of RRRC and ISCG to ensure a better response from the participants. This project allocates the contingency fund for covering the targeted participants.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The BRAC Child Protection programme has been designed to engage multidimensional participants, including the youth. According to the Children Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS) and Youth Working Group (YWG), BRAC substantially engages youth groups through the child protection integrated programme. BRAC representation in YWG has been noticeable and would be sustaining in future for assuring the child safety in the community. BRAC child protection programme will directly cover adolescents 15-18 years old through Case Management, life skills, and mental health support. In addition, BRAC engages the youth groups 18 to 24 years old as part of community engagement in the BRAC Community-Based Children Protection Committee, where youth representation has been noticeable. Along with these engagements, BRAC also ensures the involvement of the youth through community consultation and strengthening the child protection and development mechanism. This project will engage the youth group to sustain the child protection mechanism in both the Rohingya community as well as the host community for ensuring child protection. Youth group members and representatives of CBCPC will be the first responders to provide and enhance the community-based child protection mechanism to sustain and strengthen the child protection programme.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes. BRAC will initiate the Humanitarian Development Nexus through the programme; Child Protection is not exceptional here. The Child Protection programme will provide a scope for the adolescents in the Rohingya community to self-resilience through providing vocational skills training, connecting them with BRAC existing skills development programme which can support them to make enable in income generating in the community. BRAC will also provide life skill session and mental health support to children and adolescent as well as ensure the referral to other appropriate programme. Hence, the integrated project activities like life skills session, PSS/ counselling, vocational skills training etc., for the Rohingya and host community will support increasing self-reliance and resilience.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes. BRAC child protection programme has been considering the host community for increasing the resilience among the children and their caregivers through taking the steps of provision of life skills session, mental health, and social emotional learning programme. BRAC has child protection intervention in host community that include life skill session for adolescent, MHPSS, vocational skills training, and social cohesion and resilience. Social cohesion and resilience support both Rohingya and host community to promote co-existence. This programme will align with the BRAC run other development programmes in the host community. The project aligns with the Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan (CDDGP) through improving economic opportunities and protection, building capacity of government official on child protection, and connecting with SDG and localization. Hence, BRAC envisions creating a meaningful programme for the programme participants that support to the CDDGP.
