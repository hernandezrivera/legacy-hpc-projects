Project Summary

This intervention aims to link to the strategic objectives of the HRP 2021 by providing a life-saving humanitarian intervention through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH service for displaces population, their host communities, returnees and other vulnerable groups in West Darfur, West Kordofan, South Kordofan, Kassalla and Gadarif States. The project will focuses to address the protracted health, nutrition and WASH needs of the vulnerable target population and enhance basic health and nutrition service through static and mobile outreach service in the targeted localities along with improving the institutional WASH activities.
The intervention will work with the existing health, nutrition and WASH systems in the targeted locations, and will improve the available resources (human capacity, infrastructure) to be able to deliver min. standard for the most in need. Strengthen pharmaceutical supply chain to ensure availabilities of the essential drugs and medical supplies at the facilities level is another key project focus. Moreover, the project will improve the early warning capacities of the health and nutrition and support the prevention measure of the current COVID- 19 pandemic through providing infection prevention material and awareness creation activities.
The project will be implemented in 21 selected localities in five states 3 in West Darfur, 5 in South Kordofan, 9 in West Kordofan additional scale up in 2 localities in Gadarefi and 2 localities Kassala state to provide primary health care such as curative consultation, ANC service skilled delivery, management of acute malnutrition in OTP/TSFP or SC. Affected people will receive hygiene promotion messages, including handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage.

1 - Needs

The 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) identified 12.8 million people (28 per cent of the population) including 7.4 million women and girls in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021.Nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and undernutrition. Based on data available (S3M II 2018) the national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was recorded at 14.1 per cent. Similarly, according to HNO 2021 draft report, health service providing complete health packages are lacking in Sudan. Currently only 33% of the health facilities offer the complete basic PHC package including maternal and reproductive health care services, nutrition services, immunization, and free medicines. Only 51% of the women received the minimum 4 antenatal care visit during pregnancy across Sudan, lowest in West Kordofan state (41%). Vaccination coverage remains below standard, EPI is approx. 60%. Measles outbreaks and cholera outbreak indicated the clear gap of EWRS in place, with adequate health staff to provide support. The continuing economic crisis, exacerbated by COVID-19, high inflation and erosion of many families’ purchasing power has resulted in high levels of food insecurity, and severely curtailed livelihoods. A decline in the economy is disrupting health services, including the availability of medicines with only 15 per cent of essential drugs available in the country. The ability of households to meet and access basic needs and services including purchase of food has been severely affected. Concern would like to focus on the following geographic locations because GAM rate is high: - West Darfur state (GAM rate 8.4) critical locations Jabel Moon (10.5), Kulbus (12.6), and El Geneina (9.2) localities. Target: 11 HFs/OTP in the three locations (Jabel Moon, Kulbus and El Geneina hospital and catchment HFs/OTPs. - West Kordofan (GAM rate 12.7) in Abyei-Muglad locality Al Sunut, El Nehoud , El Odaya, El Salam,Abu zabad Ghubaysh Keilak, Lagawa Target: 12 HF/OTPs in eight localities. South Kordofan (GAM rate 9.5) Abu Jubaiha, Leri, Tadamon,Gedr and Talodi. Target: 10 HF/OTPs in 5 localities. Gadarefi in Western Galabat locality (GAM 19.78) and Gadarif Town (GAM 16.64) and targeted 4 HF/OPT- Kassala in rula Aroma Loclity (GAM 18.36 ) and North Delta(GAM12.51) and targeting 4 HF/OTP.Micro-nutrient deficiency among children and their mothers is common, including iron-deficiency, anemia. Stunting is high 36.5% in Sudan with an additional poor hygiene practices, lack of potable water contributes infections that contributes to malnutrition. There is a very weak nutrition monitoring system in, Gadaref, Kassal, West Darfur, West and South Kordofan that contributes to high malnutrition case, so it is important to strengthen the mobile nutrition services linking with existing nutrition and stabilization centers. Linking nutrition services with other sectors e.g. health, WASH and food security is critical to eliminate the root causes of malnutrition that links with poor water and hygiene practices, lack of food diversity and basic health care. In Concern's operation areas, institution WASH is one of the most critical needs besides health and nutrition support. The health and nutrition facilities don't have basic, potable water therefore the Life-saving service delivery is not functioning: old, non-functional pipe system, unclean facilities as a lack of water is an undermining problem of quality service deliveries. Hygiene promotion knowledge and practices, even among the health/nutrition staff in the facilities are weak, required strengthening with a sustainable institutional WASH system improvement. Many of the rural health/nutrition facilities lack adequate sanitation services:, non-functional latrines, non-segregation is a critical problem. Community/health/nutrition facility staff education is important about hygiene promotion to improve the situation.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Health: The project will provided essential package of health services include the treatment of non- communicable diseases, ensuring the availability of essential medicines, provision and expansion of MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health; and strengthening the expanded program of immunization EPI. The COVID-19 response and mitigation measures will be integrated within the planned activities with emphasis on strengthening surveillance and reporting systems, infection prevention and control, case management, and risk communication and community engagement. Outreach services and community-based interventions will follow the in-place guidelines to minimize risks of transmitting the infection and promote preventive measures within targeted communities. Nutrition: The intervention will support the provision of nutrition-specific services through existing structures including Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Stabilization Center Services through an integrated CMAM approach. Support for an integrated IYCF services through routine counselling at nutrition centres and through mother support groups at community level. Minor renovation of nutrition distribution centres and provision of outreach nutrition services for rural communities and isolated locations. Training of nutrition workforce including nutrition staff and volunteers on CMAM guidelines and community mobilization Provide promotion of breastfeeding through counselling services and peer education through Mother Support Groups Support. WASH: The project will support maintenance and selective rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities and provided hygiene promotion messages, including handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage. The project will distributed soap and some hygiene kits for the target beneficiary and rehabilitates latrine in the selected facilities Health/hygiene education both at the facility and community level for population and health/nutrition staff. Provision of IEC materials for client education on health, hygiene, nutrition and facility sanitation services. • Moreover. the project support health system strengthening through supervision and quarterly joint monitoring of integrated health, nutrition and institution WASH services. Strengthening referral services for medical and reproductive patents that need secondary health care Training and on job coaching of health facility staff, Community Health Extension Workers and Community Volunteers. Home visits through the Community Health Extension Workers and Community Volunteers. Support the SMOH to strengthen EWAR System

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups( IDPs,refugees) within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; andreducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. - The affected population, who live very far from the health centers will be in focus. Mothers who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. - Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers in the localities plays an important role in health, with only 43% of the affected population seeks medical treatment at facility level when their children got sick. While 11% visited traditional healers, exposing their children to harmful practices, such as children's removal of uvula using unhygienic materials, a very common practice in the targeted localities and South Kordofan in general. These issues will be addressed via behaviour change activities outlined in this project. - Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be identified and they will be responsible for dealing with sensitive issues e.g. GBV cases and related protection issues. - Mobile services (nutrition/health) will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly female) for dealing with sensitive issues - Children will be in high focus during the field activities by the field staff and they will also report any protection related case through the child protection reporting mechanisms, and available referrals - Other issues e.g. youth (children) and refugee youth migrating from the targeted localities to other locations due to livelihoods opportunities will be a central focus for the IGA and related activities to encourage them to stay in the region. - Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). - In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

This project will contribute to the HRP 2021 health/nutrition WASH project with a special focus on IDPs, returnees, pastoralist groups and host communities, therefore, the Accountability Mechanisms will be developed as a joint exercise with the beneficiaries. The intervention will build on MoH strategic partnership in South/West Kordofan, Gadarife, Ksassla and W/Darfur, to ensure that Concern meets PHC minimum standards. Concern is a member of HAP, hence, the principles and values of humanitarian accountability will be applied over the course of the project. Given the project is developed to fill the gap in the services provided previous interventions, and it will extensively focus on the refugees needs. This will be clearly communicated to all segments of the targeted population. The selection of the beneficiaries for particular activities will be carried out by the community management committees representing the different segments of the targeted communities based on set criteria that will bementored by CWW and its national partner NGO. Applying the "'Do No Harm"" concept,services between the different kind of groups e.g., IDPs, returnees and host communities, will be carefully distributed as per their population proportion in the community and level of vulnerability. Concern has an updated Community Response Mechanism (CRM) guidelines which has been developed in consultation with community members. There will be various CRM tools applied for the intervention e.g. feedback boxes, focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions/meetings and record on feedback will be documented and responded. Concern, the affected populations, MoH, the national partner NGO, MSGs and community health and MSG, will select two community members, including one female, to represent their community and to be part of CRM committee to participate in the opening of the CRMboxes, complaint analysis and giving feedback to the community for the relevantcomplaints. During the project implementation, focus group discussion, informal/formal meetings and discussion will be held with various groups with a special focus on refugee groups, and their host communities, MSGs, youth group to have their feedback about the intervention. As much as possible Concern field team will use the feedback to build on the next step in the project implementation, andwhere it is not possible, it will be clearly communicated to the local community.
