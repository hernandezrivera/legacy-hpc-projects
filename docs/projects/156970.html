Project Summary

The IRC and it is partners will support primary health service points and will integrate referral pathways between different levels of care. Clinics will provide standardized packages of primary and reproductive health services aligned with WHO EHSP for Syria using WHO protocols and guidelines, including Syria specific communicable disease and preparedness planning guidance. Clinical packages will specifically address child health needs with integrated management of childhood illnesses as well as reproductive healthcare (RH) services. RH services will include ante and post-natal care, and family planning counseling. This strong link between the community and the clinic allows for better follow up of cases, case management, and referrals. In addition, the IRC will procure and supply medicine, medical equipment and supplies, laboratory equipment, supplies in addition to nutrition supplies and necessary furniture and other supplies such as toiletries, stationeries, etc.. to all supported facilities. Furthermore, clinics will be supplies with fuel to ensure power supply and ambulance services.
The IRC and its partner will work to strengthen referral systems, coordinate with other implementing health actors and link IRC’s services to specialized care including referrals of complicated deliveries and pregnancies to other higher level of care.
The IRC will complete minor repairs of facilities that are necessary to deliver the full range of services outlined in the EHSP package and ensure compliance with Health and WASH Cluster standards. This may include equipping selected facilities with waste management infrastructure to ensure IPC, such as incinerators, sharps pits, and biological pits for placenta disposal . Ensuring that all facilities meet minimal standards for infection prevention and control is a key priority. The IRC and it is partners have technical expertise in providing infection prevention and control trainings for health care workers. The IRC will support partners to make sure that all health care workers have sufficient knowledge and skills to protection themselves and their patients, developing SOPs and trainings for staff as needed. All training materials are available in Arabic and English with WHO, MSF references and IPC is part of supervision checklists.
The IRC will provide technical trainings and assistance to effectively respond to emerging health needs and improve general health seeking behaviors. Technical trainings will cover medical topics such specific disease management and adherence to treatment protocols, standard training packages related to health in humanitarian settings and minimum standards of services in crises, as well as psychological and traditional first aid. Additionally, training efforts will target health program staff to strengthen skills in project management, specifically related to management health information systems, emergency preparedness and response, reporting into and monitoring early warning systems, and best practices in pharmaceutical management.

Needs

Despite an increase in the number of available health facilities, which include public healthcare services, large proportions of households still struggle with accessing required treatment. Healthcare suffer from shortage including sufficient personnel, a lack of specialized services such as dialysis treatment, and lack of vital medical supplies such as prosthetics. Main healthcare needs reportedly include emergency care, provision of assistive devices and limb reconstruction surgery, and treatment for chronic diseases. There is an increased risk of communicable diseases. For instance 4,264 cases of typhoid were identified between 23 June and 31 August 2018 due to the consumption of unsafe water, which is also thought to be causing the ongoing acute bloody diarrhea outbreak in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate according to EWARNS. According to a June 2018 assessment, access to basic services across Deir-ez-Zor Governorate remains limited. In 52 of the 112 locations assessed, primary health services, including first aid and treatment for conditions, such as diarrhea, were identified as key needs.Abu Khoseb, an informal site in northern Deir ez Zor governorate, was established in 2017 to host IDPs from the western and eastern suburbs of Deir ez-Zor City. As of January 2019, there are more than 4,900 IDPs and refugees on site, almost doubling the size of the population in less than two months. There is no access to Protection services on site, and camp administration highlighted the serious gap posed by this absence. During IRC assessments of Abu Khoshab site, the threat of violence in relation to women and children was reported by the authorities as a high risk. The need for a private space for women was spoken of very freely, and the needs of women and children spoken of as the highest priority need for the camp. There are several cases of unaccompanied minors and no access to education programming reported by camp administration as accessible to children in the community.Since November 2018, there have been more than 12,000 new arrivals in Al Hol camp, with needs facing children, women and girls and other vulnerable individuals so high, there is a waiting list for case management in the site. New arrivals to Al Hole Camp are predominately women and children. For example on December 24th, 778 arrived to the camp, out of which 50% were children, 38% women and 12% men. 90 families out of the 200 families were female headed households (FHH). The number of health facilities in Raqqa City has increased over the first half of the year with at least six hospitals operational, including five private hospitals where services are provided for a fee and one primary healthcare facility. This primary healthcare facility opened at the beginning of June and, supported by an NGO, has a capacity to receive 100 patients per day. Despite these improvements, as of July 2018 the Health sector continued to highlight major gaps in primary and secondary health care, nutrition services as well as comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care (CEmONC). The constant increase in the number of victims of explosive hazards also contributes to increase the needs of the health sector, especially for emergency trauma care, physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support.

Implementation of activities

At camps setting, the IRC coordinates referral of sever cases with KRC, the IRC will conduct initial medical assessment and treatment the refer to KRC who has ambulance services at the camp. In addition, KRC is the only authorized health actor to refer emergency cases to outside the camp. Feedback of referral cases and follow up provided by KRC during weekly coordination meetings. In addition, outside of the camp, the IRC coordinates with other actors in the catchment areas using the referral pathway and health service mapping.

Protection Risk Analysis

According to all data sources collected by protection actors across the Whole of Syria, women and girls are disproportionally affected by gender-based violence, such as early marriage, sexual violence and domestic violence . Adolescent girls remain particularly vulnerable to sexual violence and early marriage, families fearing for their daughters to be kidnapped and raped therefore preventing them to attend school. Depending on humanitarian assistance to provide for their families and as counting as the majority of IDPs, women and girls are at high risk of sexual exploitation and abuse as food and NFI distributions is irregular and lack clear criteria for selection as well as the overcrowding of distribution points . As reported during a protection assessment conducted by the IRC , women and girls do not communicate their needs if there is no communal area or women only space. The same is understood from our field teams to be true of other excluded community members, such as persons with disabilities, the elderly, children and persons with mental and physical health concerns, Without access to Child Protection services or PSS, the most vulnerable do not have the space necessary to safely seek support for protection concerns or experiences of violence or harm. Pregnant women, recently delivered women, and women of child-bearing age currently have insufficient access to specialized and emergency obstetric services, for them and for their newborns. Many public reproductive health (RH) services are no longer operating or partially functioning, due to understaffing, insecurity and access constraints, the LHAs’ inability to procure drugs, supplies and equipment in sufficient quantities, etc. In addition, in some parts of Syria, women’s access to RH services is also hindered to a certain extent by cultural barriers, which prevent them from visiting a male gynecologist without their husband’s consent for instance. In post-natal care, neglect of the female child can occur as the male child’s nutritional needs are prioritized, particularly in cases of multiple births. If the mother does not produce enough milk for both babies, the breast milk will be given to the male child and formula to the female child. People with chronic illness, disabilities and injuries have specific needs which cut across sectors and may face difficulties in accessing needed assistance. The elderly face specific needs with regards to security, shelter, access to services, health, NFIs and WASH facilities. IDPs with no other options go to camps, informal settlements and transit centers and collective centers. Field based evidence and feedback from Protection monitors is that these individuals are particularly vulnerable, and as such require specialized support and attention. Given these realities, it is essential that information is shared rapidly, efforts are coordinated amongst actors, and advocacy is ongoing. Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA)1 during the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents). The attacks resulted in 97 fatalities (of which 17 health care providers and 11 patients), and 169 injuries (of which 44 health care providers and 41 patients). The majority of the aforementioned attacks took place in contested areas and areas that witnessed changes in control, namely in Rural Damascus (46 attacks), Idlib (47 attacks), Dara (14 attacks), and Hama (10 attacks). Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security: While the operating environment in Northeast Syria is relatively stable, the programming during the award period is likely to face a higher level of instability due to the December 19, 2018 announcement of the U.S. intention to withdrawal from Syria and ongoing statements from the Government of Turkey on an intended military incursion. Additionally, concerns remain over the presence of IEDs in Ar Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorate. Therefore, there must be a close working relationships with other actors in Northeast to monitor the situation and coordinate on operational and programmatic responses. Additionally, IRC will work closely with the Mine Action Group and other actors to ensure safe programming and to prevent pull factors towards areas of potential harm.Access: IRC endeavors to have unhindered access in all of the communities. However, in preparation for severe security deterioration or changes in the control in the areas where IRC manages programs, has developed Syria-specific remote management guidelines, in-line with IRC’s Global Remote Management Guidelines. Based on level of access and capacity of staff, IRC in NES is able to provide remote control over programs, where highly skilled national staff implement with decisions from international managers located apart from programs, or remote delegation, where there is partial or temporary delegation of authority to national staff at project sites while other staff are in a separate location. In case expatriate staff have to relocate to Iraq during a full military operation and manage remotely, our national staff are capable of implementing emergency programs in NES and will be supported with technical guidance from the expatriate staff working out of Iraq. Additionally, key positions such as of Field Coordinator (FC) have been nationalized and the FC will be the focal point for the emergency response.While the IRC has actively taken steps to mitigate the risks posed by operating in a conflict zone, instances of aid diversion, theft, or loss of equipment by armed groups or community members can occur. When theft or aid diversion occurs, it’s IRC’s organizational policy to immediately report the loss accordingly to relevant stakeholders, and then investigate the circumstances and financial extent of the loss. IRC monitors programs closely to ensure the sound justification of all financial transactions, reviewed in real time by the finance team and one a monthly bases by management during grant review meetings. IRC also uses a anonymous reporting mechanism for any issues to be raised.
