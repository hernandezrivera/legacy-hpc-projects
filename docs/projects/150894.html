Project Summary

The project aims at providing tailored assistance to communities living in hard-to-reach settlements, and whose rights to access services are constantly violated. Traditional humanitarian-type assistance and emergency response sometimes fail to adequately address the needs of these communities, due to the numerous restrictions of access (military control, red zones, etc.) and insecurity, and some extremely vulnerable categories can be excluded from traditional approaches as it is hard to reach out to them in such conditions. The project will target two vulnerable, hard-to-reach settlements among the list of proposed locations, and based on assessments and the following criteria:
- being located at the contact line;
- being deprived of access to essential services or experiencing problems in accessing them;
- having a social centre (a school or a Feldsher and Midwives Point - FMAP) available.
The project will establish a community mechanism through the recruitment, capacity building and support of community mobilizers (CMs). They will be responsible for organizing social/PSS/recreational activities, monitoring and referring protection or MHPSS cases, and act in general as interlocutors for community members to receive information and support in accessing services. This community mechanism will allow for important engagement with community members and make sure that their feedbacks are incorporated into activity designs and implementation. Preparedness activities will also be included into this mechanism by pre-positioning emergency kits to be used and dispatched by the trained CMs in case of conflict fare-ups or serious degradation. This community mechanism will also look at durable solutions by providing small business grants to vulnerable HHs, support to community projects and rehabilitation of social institutions. The work of the CMs will be monitored by the PUI project team in order to follow-up on their activities and provide them with tailored support and advice to carry out their tasks.
The project is a pilot intervention that will be implemented in a limited number of settlements (2) in 2019 in order to assess the feasibility and challenges of such an intervention. Depending on context evolution and needs, this intervention could be scaled-up in 2020, with adjustments to the intervention modality based on the community feedbacks collected through the community mobilizers. Accountability towards affected population will be at the core of the project since most of the activities will be implemented through a community mobilization mechanism, through which feedbacks will be collected constantly to adjust the activities based on the comments made by the different categories of affected populations.
Though the project aims at serving the entire populations of the targeted settlements, particular attention will be put so that activities benefit to the most vulnerable age and gender categories:
- PSS activities will take into consideration the different ways women and men are affected by and react to distress (see needs assessment part)
- Community activities will specifically target CU5, single mothers (18-59 y.) and older people (60+). Older people’s needs are often not enough covered in traditional humanitarian assistances in Eastern Ukraine, and can be ‘excluded’ from assistance because of bias in selection criteria. Social activities are also important for this age category as they are more prone to social isolation.

Needs

According to the Protection Cluster, in August 2018, nearly 30,000 People reside in villages in GCA where there are restrictions of movement. Access to basic services is impeded for people living near the LoC and the security situation severely restricts humanitarian access and assistance in these areas. Militarized checkpoints installed at the entrances to these settlements impacts the establishment of civil administrations. These areas are abandoned in terms of material, financial, social and administrative support. Individual living in these ‘hard-to-reach settlements’ do not benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to essential services and enjoyment of their rights. living in these areas is already considered as a factor of extreme vulnerability but particular attention has to be paid to the most vulnerable categories: older people, PLWDs, children and HHs headed by single mothers. In October 2017, PUI conducted an assessment of 15 hard-to-reach settlements. Among 514 interviewed 56% were female and 44% male. Percentage of people over 50 y.o was the highest (45%), percentage of of under 5 y.o. was 7%, 5-17 y.o. - 12%, 18-49 y.o.- 37%. The most of HH were headed by female over 50yo (34%), followed by male over 50y.o (31%), then by female 18-49 y.o. (21%) and male 18-49 y.o. (14%).. Insufficient income and unemployment were reported to be the reason of the majority of vulnerabilities as it has a direct impact on the capacity to access essential services. First aid kits were requested as a must by a significant number of interviewees due to the fact that they have no immediate access to pharmacy while having to cope with minor injuries. MHPSS issues were a major concern, especially for children who experience significant stress when going to their schools (located directly at the LoC). When HHs reported that one of the CU5 member had encountered health issues in the two weeks prior to the assessment, this was due to psychological trauma in 23% of the cases. In the MHPSS assessment conducted by PUI-IMC in the Donetska Oblast in early 2018, safety (70%), income or livelihoods (63%), separation from family members (35%) were cited among the main personal problems. When it comes the reasons for psychological distress among communities, separation from family/property/work and disruption of access to basic services were cited as the two main reasons. More than 70% of the interviewees living in the 1-5km zone experienced physiological signs of distress. Common signs of distress and coping skills were differently expressed by men and women. When asked their current coping strategies compared to pre-conflict (which was ‘outdoor activities’), men answered “nothing” as the most common response, suggesting that men’s prior engagement in stress reducing activities is largely not seen as available to them now. Women identified most common pre-conflict coping strategies as related to recreational activities like “singing” and community/village activities and events. While family and friends was overwhelming indicated as a means of help-seeking, social support systems were articulated as either individual (family and friends) or community. Though the MHPSS assessment did not focus specifically on hard-to-reach areas, it can be assumed that this applies even more acutely in these areas, where social isolation can be a major issue. Another important lessons learned from PUI’s hard-to-reach assessment was that 100% of respondents pointed out inequality of the humanitarian assistance distribution process: according to their feedback, most INGOs prefer to work through local intermediaries causing bias and choose one and the same vulnerability selection criteria, leaving aside the category of people between 35-60 y. old plus elderly pensioners receiving a bit more than 1300 UAH. This points towards the necessity to develop mechanisms more inclusive of community feedback in the design of assistance and selection of recipients of aid.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1 Protection and resilience mechanisms are strengthened at community-level Activity 1.1 Identification and capacity-building of community mobilizers (CM). In each settlement, 2 CMs will be identified jointly with community members and local authorities. Gender balance will be ensured in the selection of the 2 CM. These volunteers will be selected based on several criteria including motivation, capacity to carry out to role of CM, understanding of gender/age needs), capacity to interact with community members and stakeholders, and acceptation by the community members. More detailed criteria will be agreed upon between PUI project team and the community. CMs will receive capacity-building on their role and community mobilization (formal and on-the-job training). The capacity-building will cover various fields but a strong emphasis will be put on themes such as protection mainstreaming, basic PSS, community-based health education, GBV. Taking into consideration gender and age needs in all aspects of project implementation will be at the core of the training provided. Activity 1.2 Community activities and projects: CMs will identify, along with community members, activities/projects aiming at strengthening social cohesion and avoid isolation of vulnerable categories (older people, HHs headed by single mothers, children under 5). Projects will be selected jointly with community members and CMs based on their integration of gender and age needs and vulnerable categories such as older people, PLWDs. Community activities will also include recreational activities, and awareness-raising adjusted to different gender/age groups. The community activities will particularly target CU5, single mothers and older people. Activity 1.3 Feedback and accountability mechanism- Throughout the project, CMs will constantly take feedbacks from community members (direct feedback, questionnaires/complaint boxes, FGDs organized by age and gender groups, etc.). This will enable to influence the activities and assistance constantly based on the community preferences and vulnerabilities of different categories, such as older people. Activity 1.4 Support to and referral of individual protection cases Comm mob will also act as interlocutors of individuals in need of protection and/or psychosocial support. Thanks to the training they will receive, the CMs will be able to identify such cases, provide them with basic advice and/or refer them to other stakeholders or mechanisms with the support of PUI based on the identified needs.Outcome 2 Preparedness capacities are developed at community-levelActivity 2.1 Pre-positioning of emergency kits at community-level (social institutions) such as: shelter kits, first aid kit, energy kits, security equipment (anti-blast films, protective boards). The kits will be under the responsibility of the CMs, who will be sensitized in taking into consideration gender and age needs in using them. This will develop internal emergency response capacities case of prolonged restriction of access to these areas, to support small repairs, provision of basic NFIs or first aid, on a case-by-case basis.Outcome 3 – Durable solutions are identified and supported Activity 3.1 – Provision of small grants. This activity aims at supporting the development of small economic activities hold by individuals. The individuals supported will be selected with the help of the CMs, based on criteria such as sustainability, , capacity of the project holders, vulnerability (with the view to empower, vulnerable cases such as PLWDs, single-mothers heads of HH, IDPs). Each project will be monitored by the CMs and PUI. Activity 3.2 – Renovation works of spaces in social institutions (school or PHC) to improve the services and condition provided. Needs will be identified jointly with CMs/community members and PUI. PUI will engage with community to make sure that access constraints (for PLWDs or older people) are addressed through the works.
