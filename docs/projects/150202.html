Project Summary

The project addresses the country strategic objectives SO2 and SO3 in terms of providing the essential services, and responding to protracted stress. The project will focus on ensuring access and building the resilience of education staff, children and their families, which is directly linked to education cluster specific objective 1. Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and objective 2. Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school based psychosocial services. The majority of the schools are included among the lists identified by MoEHE, cluster and WV assessment.
Needs were aggregated from the recent assessment conducted by WV field team in the targeted zone, data was also collected through WV assessment and MoEHE’s Monitoring and Responding Mechanism. The objectives will provide 1964 vulnerable students, teachers and PTA with safe access to education and maintain the resilience of vulnerable students’ communities. It will ensure vulnerable students (boys and girls), school staff and community ( parents and community members) affected by the conflict and insecurity have equitable access to safe education through activities that will provide them with: 1.
physical resources which aim at improving their access to safe and inclusive education services through providing temporary learning spaces ( TLS) and tools. 2. It will provide students, staff and community members (PTA) with means to cope with protracted stress through PSS/PFA capacity building. 3. WV will conduct PSS/PFA related activities at schools to help children cope with stress.
The project will establish strong monitoring and learning mechanisms to assess and track progress against project objectives. The monitoring will be conducted in accordance with existing WV monitoring systems under the local responsibility of the field team in close liaison with the country office (Jerusalem).

Needs

World Vision’s assessment in August, 2018 for 84 Area C schools, focusing on 44 communities within its capacity has filtered the communities on the basis of high potential risk of violations due to their location near segregation wall, bypass roads, proximity to settlements and checkpoints. Supplementary information has been obtained through general observations by WV field staff; Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with community members, parents and children. Secondary data was collected from sources including EiE cluster and SCI’s school violation reports, OCHA and MoEHE's (MRM) reports. Data from 84 assessed schools show that 45% of schools are subjected to education related violations from IF, settlers’ attacks and checkpoints. 35 schools reported experiencing more than one violation within the past year. Education plays an important role in providing psychological, emotional and social support to assist in developing cognitive competencies that foster positive coping and adaptation when confronted by adversity. As per WV assessment, 49% (42 out of 84) of assessed schools don’t have counselors to support children in need for psycho-social support. Teachers likewise, lack the capacity to provide children with psychological services revealing a high need for building capacity of school staff on PSS/PFA. Both girls and boys are affected by IHL violations and often display different symptoms, PSS/PFA training will address those differences. Girls are more susceptible to GBV in marginalized areas, providing PSS/PFA support to teachers and parents (not just students) may reduce negative or destructive coping behaviors that increase the likelihood of violence in general and in particular gender-based violence. Findings also reveal inadequate and insufficient learning spaces in 25 schools. The provision of extra classrooms will contribute to children safety and reduction of school drop-out, which is a particular concern with female students.Needs are pertinent and WV cannot secure the funds to address the needs of 84 schools through its technical program. Accordingly, WV will intentionally target 11 schools; 9 co-educational, one girls’ school and one boy’s school which are assessed as generally worse off, in terms of violations, infrastructure and safety. The 9 schools selected from Bethlehem, South Hebron and Nablus are identified by the MoEHE as most vulnerable .Two extra schools are selected due to # of dropout at boys school and the risk girls face while walking around 3km to school. All selected schools are subjected to human right violations facing more than 164 violations within the past year. 8 out of 11 schools do not have counselors to support children in need of PSS. The project will target all PTA committees; it will establish committees with community’s support for schools with no PTAs. Recreational activities related to PSS/PFA such as play, sports and drama can help reduce symptoms of stress. Activities will be gender-sensitive, allowing male and female learners – together or separately - to do activities that are familiar, reassuring and fun. In reference to MoEHE Joint Protection and Advocacy Strategy, there is an urgent need to provide children in Area C with PFA/PSS. Installing extra temporary learning spaces (TLS) in three schools will address the issues of safety and providing adequate learning environment. Students ( boys and girls) can finish high school at their school, as walking extra miles to reach other schools, will expose them to high risks. There is also a high need for enhancing security in 2 schools through rehabilitating perimeter walls and fencing to protect against settlers’ and IF attacks.

Activities

The 11 schools are included among the list identified by the MoEHE and EiE cluster as the most vulnerable to attacks. The needs are aggregated from WV recent assessment. All Activities will be gender-sensitive, allowing male and female learners – together or separately - to do activities that are familiar, reassuring and fun. Activities will also attend to the needs of CWD’s.Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for children. IndicatorActivity 1.1- Provision of 6 caravans to 3 targeted schools. It will be implemented while ensuring high community participation. The schools were selected based on needs as identified by MoEHE, school principals and community. The additional temporary classrooms (TLS) will help the schools add two grade levels to help accommodate students who have to walk distances to other areas to continue their education. It will also help provide safe and adequate learning spaces to replace old dire ones. 1.2 Provision of material support and teaching/learning supplies to 11 schools.The 11 schools will be provided with material support and teaching and learning supplies to enable students and teachers execute activities. WV will assess the needs of tools and supplies teachers and students need to implement school based activities. All relevant tools and rehabilitation will be carried out through WV procurement department following donor’s requirements.1.3: Provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that schools environments are safe for students. As per the assessment conducted 4 schools have reported the need to rehabilitate their school fences to protect students, ensure safety while within school perimeters, and promote school attendance.Objective 2: Most Vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school based psychosocial services.Activity 2.1: Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education related violations. This aims to improve teachers and students coping mechanisms with emergency situations, and release stress.WV assessment revealed that the schools are in high need due to location, number of violations and lack of counselors, as only 4 schools reported to have school counselors. There is a need to build capacity of school staff (teachers, counselors and principals). WV has produced evidence of the effectiveness of its PSS /PFA support, of which curriculum was developed by WHO. Results published in Disaster Health Journal, October 2015, show positive benefits to beneficiaries confirming that PSS/PFA contributed to safety and reduced distress.2.1.1 WV staff in collaboration with MoEHE staff who received PFA/PSS training will revise and update available content (WV developed curriculum by WHO).2.1.2 A teacher training will be conducted by WV in collaboration with MoEHE.2.1.3 To ensure the activities related PFA/PSS training is well implemented and enriched to reach out to all targeted children and help reinforce their academic school work, in collaboration with Palestinian Child’s Home Club a training of teachers and counselors on establishing relevant PSS activities to help children release stress 2.1.3 Awareness sessions to parents on PSS/PFA techniques will be provided by WV in collaboration with MoEHE supervisors and school trained counselors.
