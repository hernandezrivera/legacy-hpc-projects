Project Summary

In support to the UN-led coordinated humanitarian response in Borno State, INTERSOS is proposing a 12-months project that will address the basic housing, NFIs and registration needs of the most vulnerable groups of IDPs in Monguno and Ngala Local Governments Areas (LGAs), Borno State, Nigeria.
The project has been designed to meet the affected population’s most urgent needs in a sustainable way that empowers local communities and service providers by working through existing structures where available, adhering to Nigerian guidelines and protocols, and empowering community stakeholders and other project’s beneficiaries in direct service delivery. Focusing on the particular needs of women and children, elderly and disabled people, INTERSOS will target communities where service providers are currently unavailable or unable to cope with the overwhelming needs of returnees, IDPs and host community.
In the project focus areas, INTERSOS will scale up its interventions with the provision of 750 emergency shelters kits and will strengthen the reception center management in Monguno and Ngala. INTERSOS will strive to fulfill the basic humanitarian needs of the ultra-vulnerable communities through the provision of adequate temporary housing solutions and reception center facilities. With the assistance of other services providers, the project will enhance the different referral pathways and the referral mechanism.

Needs

Through the OCHA-led HRP 2019 planning process, humanitarian actors have assessed the most urgent needs across Borno State and assigned responsibility for sector interventions by LGA. With regard to this, INTERSOS, alongside other NGOs, has taken the role in providing emergency housing and reception services to Monguno and Ngala LGAs. According to the OCHA-led Humanitarian Situation Update, as of July 2018, only 44% of the response was covered, and more than 30,000 households remains without an emergency shelter solution. Recurrent security incidences in LGAs’ such as Ngala, Bama and Monguno has also led to the hike in the trend of displacement and movement, thereby rapidly increasing the shelter need across board. As of September 2018, according to International Organization for Migration Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM), the town of Monguno is estimated to host 124,000 IDPs. According to the Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT IOM), in May Ngala witnessed 3,574 new arrivals, with a peak of 2,016 during the third week (ETT n. 67). As far as Monguno is concerned, the ETT IOM reports that 834 IDPs arrived just during the first week of September 2018, from Guzamala LGA and surrounding villages in Monguno, further contributing to the already high humanitarian needs in town. Nevertheless, expected Military operation of Nigerian security forces against AOGs in the outskirts of Monguno will cause more than 10,000 IDPs. This number is in addition to the over 1,000 household IDPs who do not have shelters and over 6,000 Household IDPs, whose shelters urgently need repair since they are makeshift and made of grass material. The security situation in both locations Is quite uncertain and volatile, especially increase in the most recent AOG’s attacks in the outskirts of Monguno and Ngala caused a huge increment in the IDPs influx.as a result people fled from their places and seeking shelters in Monguno and Ngala. As INTERSOS exist in both locations and managing reception centers, receiving bunch of IDPs on daily basis, there are more than thousand families did not receive any assistance from the service providers and passing through form worst circumstances. Like other services, shelter services are also overstretched: people are living in a congested environment in camps and some in host communities, which create public health risk in terms of disease outbreak. According to INTERSOS CCCM team working in Monguno and Ngala, there are still more than hundreds of families residing in open sky, many are using vacant buildings which are itself a potential threat to their lives as these are crumbly and friable buildings. In addition, both locations are receiving daily new arrivals from surrounding villages including Rann and Dikwa, Gudumbali, Guzamala, Minati and Katana and with the military operation at play, Ngala has also been tipped to expect 13,500 new arrivals as a result. Following the Shelter, NFIs and CCCM sector priorities and the dire needs of shelter & NFIs and CCCM services, INTERSOS will target Monguno and Ngala. This allocation will allow INTERSOS to provide lifesaving and humanitarian assistance and to address the basic needs of the 24,000 individuals still in need in Monguno and Ngala. INTERSOS will extend its reception center services in both project proposed locations Monguno and Ngala. Each reception center has the capacity to host and accommodate 500 individuals at once, in which they are receiving basic safe and secure living services and nonfood items.
