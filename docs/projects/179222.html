Project Summary

The project aims to increase safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years) and improve the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5-17 years) within a protective environment. The project will be implemented through ADRA Syria’s education team, in close coordination with MoE and related DoEs, ensuring the protection of all beneficiaries through a ‘do not harm’ approach. Through this project, ADRA Syria will improve access to formal and non-formal education and learning opportunities for girls and boys by creating safe and protective environments for 14,644 children who have been affected by the crisis. These education interventions will be implemented in the governorates of Rural Damascus, Quneitra, Homs, Aleppo, Dara’a and Deir Ez Zour (all targeted sub-districts are in the areas of Education severity levels 3-5). ADRA Syria will enhance the formal and non–formal education level of the students - boys and girls - in the schools rehabilitated by ADRA. This will be accomplished through a holistic program with psycho-social, familial, and educational support, encouraging the engagement of mothers and fathers in educational activities. The average cost per beneficiary is estimated at $75. The total number of targeted beneficiates is 14,644 individuals, of which 5% (732 individuals) are people with disabilities.
ADRA will coordinate with key stakeholders to ensure the most vulnerable boys and girls are identified in the target locations. Further, ADRA will conduct comprehensive needs assessments in the areas identified to locate out-of-school children (OOSC) and students with priority educational needs.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has had a devastating impact on education nationwide. According to the HNO 2021, an estimated 6.9 million people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance, 94% of whom live in high severity areas. The number of OOSC increased by 16% from 2020 and is currently approximately 2.45 million, including an estimated 1.5 million boys who are engaged in child labor and other negative coping mechanisms. In addition, there are approximately 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out, an increase of 23% from 2020.As Syrian schools were intermittently closed in 2020, access to online education was limited due to multiple challenges, including lack of electronic devices and connectivity, movement and difficulty of guardians to support or monitor home-learning. Based on KIIs in January 2021, children remain OOS across Syria due to lack of rehabilitated schools, the lack of parental support for child education and poverty causing child labor. Children in formal education have low classroom engagement and retention due to protection concerns, life stresses, poverty, child labor and lack of educational support at home.The infrastructural damage in conflict-affected areas results in inadequate and overcrowded education facilities with the average number of students per classroom reaching up to 80, and children sitting on the floor in classrooms that are without doors or windows. In other areas students walk 2-4 kms per day along dangerous roads to attend alternative school facilities if they attend at all. Schools are often ill-equipped to accommodate students and teachers with disabilities. ADRA field staff estimate that 90% of children attending school require additional PSS and educational support.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project activities have been designed based on the identified needs Children and Teachers. Rehabilitation of damaged schools will ensure equal access to classrooms including gender segregated WASH facilities. Provision of NFE programs (with incorporated PSS), school kits, learning materials will be provided to at risk children (female and males), and children with disabilities. Capacity building for male and female teachers on topics including active learning and PSS. ADRA Syria will be responsible for the implementation of project activities in close partnership with MoE and DoEs. The ADRA MEAL team will develop a comprehensive MEAL plan including tracking tools to ensure project performance is on track as well as the survey tools for data collection. ADRA will receive feedback from potential and actual project beneficiaries including boys, girls, adolescent boys, and girls as well as men and women and people with disabilities. ADRA will solicit feedback through including direct communication with project beneficiaries, constant follow-up with key stakeholders and anonymous feedback via the established complaints feedback mechanism. This feedback is used to plan project activities, undertake adjustments to ongoing activities, as well as help measure the impact of the activities. ADRA has established offices in Damascus (covering Rural Damascus, Quneitra and Deraa), Homs, Deir Ez ZouR and Aleppo. ADRA Syria is an active member of all relevant sectors in Syria involved in the implementation of the project including WASH, Education, Protection. ADRA coordinates on a bi-lateral basis with other humanitarian partners working in the same area to ensure complementary approaches and that the most urgent needs are met.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Distribution of learning material and school kits create tension between host community and IDPs. Mitigation: ADRA will create certain criteria for distribution, depending on the vulnerability level and need of the beneficiaries which will include both IDPs and host community beneficiaries. (Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: Children are exposed to the risk of experiencing harm or sexual abuse by adults such as teachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students. Mitigation: Teachers and other education personnel will be regularly trained on positive discipline and child safeguarding and the implementation of the gain training will be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. All staff will sign a code of conduct and a zero-tolerance policy will be applied (Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Moderate; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: Learning facilities or are unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements such as, rain, damaged elements…etc) Mitigation: ADRA will maintain schools and learning spaces to ensure that buildings are safe and protected. This will include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electricity, water…etc) (Likelihood: Low; Impact: High; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: children’s exposure to protection concerns either on the way to and from schools. Mitigation: ADRA will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the children when attending school. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes). ADRA has an established a complaints feedback mechanism and a monitoring and evaluation mechanism that incorporates the feedback of beneficiaries, at all stages of the project.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Project staff or beneficiaries are a subject of Sexual Harassments, Exportation and Abuse (SHEA). Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigation: ADRA developed Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)policy. A focal point has been assigned to ensure and monitor the application of this policy in the project activities and in the offices. The policy covers standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. In addition, the ADRA Syria Code of Conduct was updated in 2020, including PSHEA, which each employee signs at the beginning of their contract of the new year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: changing security situation. Mitigation: ADRA will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments and implement in safe areas. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities. Mitigation; ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the MoE and DoEs (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: lack of or difficulty securing supplies and materials. Mitigation: ADRA will ensure a wide advertising to attract potential contractors. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes). Risk: the procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest. Mitigation: ADRA has developed and follows a clear procurement policy which includes a clear segregation of duties, levels of authorization, and conflict of interest declaration to help prevent fraud, corruption, and conflict of interest. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes) Risk: Local or global epidemics/pandemics such as COVID-19. Mitigation: Minimize interaction among staff, decrease working hours, expand awareness among staff; provide personal protection equipment. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes) Risk: Fluctuations in exchange rates. Mitigation: Closely monitor exchange rates, use open and competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding to complete the works (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes)
