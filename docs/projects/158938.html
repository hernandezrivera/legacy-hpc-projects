Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objective 3 and the FSS cluster strategic objective 1. It will do so by providing livelihood support to farmers in 6 villages of Qalqilya and Salfit governorates who face specific challenges to cultivate their lands due to settlement-related access restrictions (coordination periods), settler attacks/vandalism and other restrictions linked to the coercive environment.
The project is based on a needs assessment conducted by PUI and UAWC in September 2019 that identified the most pressing needs of vulnerable farmers working in areas that require prior coordination with the Israeli authorities to access during the harvest period, and other lands adjacent to these areas. In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and cluster objectives, PUI and its partner will provide two types of assistance:
First, the project will target 40 vulnerable olive farmers men and women with and without disabilities whose livelihoods are threatened by settlement-related access restrictions; they will receive urgent support for olive harvesting during access coordination period. The support will include the provision of agricultural tools and cash to hire daily workers to support the harvesting during the very short coordination period.
Second, the project will support 240 vulnerable farmers, men and women with and without disabilities working in lands adjacent to the coordination areas and whose livelihoods are threatened by settlement expansion. Agricultural roads will be rehabilitated (8 km), which will increase a safe access to lands and contribute to preventing land confiscations. Out of these 240 farmers and based on identified needs/vulnerabilities, 30 of them will receive land rehabilitation support to increase their production capacity. This support will also include the provision of cisterns (8) to enhance the supplementary irrigation to olive trees, and thus the production capacity.
In total, 280 farmers' households (1288 individuals) will benefit directly from the Action, and 200 daily workers' households will benefit indirectly through cash for work opportunities.
The project is integrating protection by aiming to mitigate the exposure of farmers to the coercive environment with a specific focus on settler violence. The design of the project is based on a risk analysis conducted jointly with the communities to ensure their safety and dignity and also enhance their capacities of self-protection. Furthermore, the extensive experience of the partners and knowledge of the communities will ensure accountability to the affected community and their needs.
The project will be implemented in partnership with UAWC and each organisation will implement the project’s activities in their areas of operations (PUI in Qalqilya from Nablus office and UAWC in Salfit from their Ramallah office), building on their experience implementing similar projects and knowledge of the local context. Both organizations will bring their expertise to the partnership: UAWC supporting agriculture from a development perspective and PUI rehabilitating lands and assets, easing access and providing support to farmers with a protection lens through emergency and protective responses to settler-related incidents. Furthermore, the project will complement the Action of the West Bank Protection Consortium by covering a need gap in the provision of assistance to vulnerable farmers in coordination areas and their adjacent lands.
Finally, in September 2019, PUI coordinated with FSS, MoA Central, MoA of Qalqilya and Salfit, and the Chamber of Commerce of Qalqilya in order to identify and prioritize FSS needs and localities in these 2 governorates. FSS needs have been identified in 12 communities in the two governorates. 6 communities will be targeted by a HPF project under the 2nd allocation 2019 while this HRP project will target the other 6 locations not supported by HPF 2019.

1 - Needs

In 2018 PUI conducted an impact survey on 5 years of support to farmers during the olive harvest collecting quantitative data through questionnaires with 241 farmers in North and Middle of the WB. Villages targeted in Qalqilya and Salfit are facing a protracted humanitarian crisis due to Israeli restrictions and settlement expansions which is affecting respectively around 41000 and 52450 dunums of land. These conditions are worsened by the full Israeli civil and security control over as much as 90% of the land of these villages. Lack of construction and maintenance of agricultural assets including agricultural roads, water cisterns, retaining walls and the planting of new seedlings impedes the utilization of these lands. Villages’ proximity to settlements impede access to their land, but also make them highly vulnerable to settler violence, further deterring farmers from going to their lands. The Action is designed to continuously analyse and secure equity between age and gender groups. In relation to agricultural work gender roles are traditionally expressed by women often contributing directly to the livelihood activities of the household, but with limited access to and/or control over productive assets. Female farmers are considered among the most vulnerable to poverty and discrimination due to their reduced level of income, social exclusion, their lack of professional skills and their limited access to control over productive assets. On the other hand in terms of settler violence the most vulnerable will be farmers working in few numbers/alone, since they are more likely to be attacked by settlers as persons with disabilities (PwDs), elderly people and children are especially vulnerable.In August/September 2019 PUI/UAWC conducted a rapid needs assessment in six vulnerable communities in the governorates of Salfit and Qalqilya. The findings of this assessment are considered valid for communities facing similar challenges in the same governorates. The survey shows that 53% of the targeted communities depend on agriculture as the first source of income. 26% of the population surveyed is earning less than 1450 NIS a month, and 39.7% is earning between 1450-2999 NIS, which is considered close to below the poverty level. The main obstacles faced by the farmers in the assessed areas are pests and diseases (69%), lack of rehabilitated or constructed agricultural roads (49%), lack of water resources (44%), settlers’ attacks and fires (38%), and frost (11%). The main obstacles faced by the farmers in prior coordination areas and the adjacent lands are the attacks and damages to the trees (63%), lack of water resources (49%), difficulties in accessing the lands safely and easily (48%) and the stealing of crops by settlers during the harvesting season (39%). The rapid needs assessment led to the below recommendations: For prior coordination areas: To support the farmers during the harvesting season through cash to hire daily workers in order to harvest as quickly as possible in the very short coordination periods, as well as the provision of olive harvest machines. For the lands in area C: • To rehabilitate the agricultural roads and construct new agricultural roads in areas under risk of confiscation to facilitate access and provide a safe exit for the farmers in cases of settlers attacks. The estimated number of the existing agricultural roads in the 6 communities are 81 roads, total length of about 107.7 km. Among the 81 roads, 51 are in need of rehabilitation with a total length of 65 km. This rehabilitation would target 1910 farmers in the six communities and facilitate the access to 30,600 dunums. • The need for around 70 new agricultural roads with an estimated length of 76.5 km. These roads would benefit 1150 farmers and facilitate the access to 16,200 dunums. • The scarcity of water was very clear and few farmers depend on agricultural water wells. Cisterns are an urgent need to ensure the sustainability of their work.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Land rehabilitationLand rehabilitation will be carried out for 150 dunums (75 in Salfit and 75 in Qalqilya), including building of retaining walls, ploughing, fencing, cleaning and planting seedlings and seeds for intercropping agriculture. 30 vulnerable farmers will benefit from rehabilitation of their lands. All of them will also benefit from the rehabilitation of agricultural roads (activity 2). Rehabilitation works will be implemented directly by the farmers, with whom agreements will be signed.The Action will encourage the participation of men and women with and without disabilities of different ages, and promote equal access to productive resources and joint decision-making by engaging a diverse representation and the methodology of working with entire households instead of only household representatives. Additionally women and PwDs will actively be called upon to apply for land rehabilitation activities and thereby including their related needs. They will be specifically prioritized through the scoring system used for beneficiary selection.The land rehabilitation will also include the construction of cisterns and benefit the same 30 vulnerable farmers. The objective of this sub-activity is to enhance the supplementary irrigation to olive trees, which would increase the production by 50% according to MoA. Support will be given to farmers whose lands are adjacent to the access restricted areas and in areas exposed to settler attacks/vandalism. The support will be provided through the construction of 8 cisterns with a capacity of 70 cubic meter each. The need for the construction of cisterns was highlighted by the MoA Directorates in Qalqilya and Salfit in August 2019 (total need of 100 cisterns across the West Bank). The targeted villages identified in this HRP project are among the villages that MoA considered as the most affected by settlements expansions and settler violence. Activity 2: Rehabilitation of agricultural roadsThis activity includes the rehabilitation of 8 km of agricultural roads (4 km in Salfit governorate and 4 km in Qalqilya governorate). 240 households will benefit from increased safe access to their agricultural lands, which will accelerate pruning and/or support land use – which in turn will contribute to preventing land confiscations. The rehabilitation of agricultural roads will increase the number of persons accessing the land, resulting in increased protection of farmers; furthermore it will secure the access of ambulances/fire fighters in case of protection incidents occurring. Materials will be provided by PUI/UAWC and the local councils/municipalities will carry out the work by providing machinery and workers. Agricultural roads will be selected through a consultative process involving local councils/municipalities, MoA representatives, and UAWC agricultural committees, giving priority to areas exposed to threats of settler violence and settlement expansion. The selection will be based on a scoring system that will take into account protection concerns and the number of farmers who can benefit from the rehabilitated road. Activity 3: Support during olive harvest seasonThis activity will benefit 40 olive farmers through the provision of agricultural tools (olive harvest machines) and cash to hire 200 daily workers to support the harvesting during the very short coordination period. Support will be given to farmers whose lands are located in areas that require prior coordination with the Israeli authorities to access, and who without the assistance would have limited possibility to complete their harvest due to the few days of the permit. Priorities will be given to the most vulnerable among the farmers as per the criteria mentioned above. PUI and UAWC will coordinate with PDCO to ensure security and information on coordination dates.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The Action’s primary concern is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase, and through consultations with communities. Hence, community members and potential targeted beneficiaries are involved in all the phases of the action including identifying vulnerabilities and related needs. As soon as the action starts, to ensure transparency PUI will meet with all selected beneficiaries and community representative to inform them about the final accepted project and explain all different steps where beneficiaries will be asked to actively participate for the success of the Action. During those meetings, PUI / UAWC staff members will ensure to handout information cards on the feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM) and explain the mechanism. They will pay attention to highlighting that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with in respect to the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. In each encounter with community members they will be informed about the FCM and will be invited to use boxes and templates located in the premises of UAWC for that purpose as well. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally in the field, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), in addition to all other available media in the organization. Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / UAWC interventions. The FCM will directly contribute to the improvement of the project implementation. It will assist also in understanding beneficiaries’ perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in the early detection of problems such as in regard to targeting.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PUI/UAWC is fully committed to the do no harm principle, and promoting safety and dignity. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with the targeted groups as well as through the FCM. The targeted communities and beneficiaries will be actively involved in all phases of the Action and all feedback will be taken into account in best possible manners. The community-based protection approach involving CBOs and DPOs and participants of the Action is designed to ensure privacy, safety, dignity and accessibility for men and women, with or without disability and to contribute to the enhancement of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights. Lastly all involved staff is trained in PSEA policies and the action continuously engage in GBV mainstreaming by as an example having staff trained in identifying and referring GBV survivors as non-specialized actors.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the participatory means, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI/UAWC will analyse possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the community, CBOs, PDOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs through a scoring system prioritizing the persons/households most at need specifically focusing on PwDs and female farmers. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making of income use. Female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved beneficiaries on challenges and possible solutions.
