Project Summary

Education needs are the priority concerns address by this project, owing to the protracted conflict which erupted back in 2011. Both Libyan and non-Libyan children are disproportionately affected by the consequences of the active hostilities. Namely, they are at risk of domestic violence, recruitment by armed groups, SGBV, abduction, unlawful detention, ill-treatment, killing and maiming due to the presence of UXOs. In particular, children on the move face heightened risks of human trafficking, arbitrary detention, forced labor, physical abuse and sexual exploitation. In order to effectively reduce the vulnerabilities INTERSOS has established in 2018 a Center of Excellence, called Baity center, in Tripoli and implemented the same model in Sabha in 2020. Bayti center is a multi-sectoral programme involving education and child protection. The majority of the participants are children aged 6-18 and youth up to 24 years old, both Libyans (host community and IDPs) and non-Libyans (refugees, migrants). Baity aims at creating a safe and supportive environment and providing a range of education services that encourage children and youth’s self-development and well-being. Indeed, the center is a safe space which provides non-formal education services, life skills through learning support services as well as awareness raising sessions to children and families. In fact, both children’s families and the local community are involved in the project through outreach and awareness-raising activities.

1 - Needs

Since the start of the armed conflict in 2011, Libya continues to experience armed clashes, insecurity, political crisis, increased displacement and a collapsing economy. In the first nine months of 2020 a sharp increase in the number of armed conflict-related events was reported in Libya. Continued armed conflict and the use of air strikes and artillery shelling in the civilian residential areas and the basic service centers continued to negatively impact the safety and lives of the civilian population. This insecurity has resulted in significant displacements. IOM reports indicate that all the displacements observed in Libya are reported due to the armed conflict which has directly or indirectly increased the insecurity. The overall humanitarian situation in Libya remained volatile throughout the last 12 months as conflict escalated and lives of civilians were at risk. The start of the 2019-2020 academic school year was delayed by more than one month as the teachers were on strike demanding the payment of their salaries. From January to September 2020, there were 16 reported attacks on schools affecting the education of more than 16,000 students. In an effort to prevent the spread of COVID-19, in mid-March the Ministry of Education closed all 5,000 schools with indefinite plans for reopening. The protracted crisis has severely affected children’s access to education. Displacement of families and school aged children, attacks on school infrastructure, the closure of schools due to COVID-19 and conflict, occupation by IDPs, has severely affected children’s access to education in safe and protective environment. 9% of Libyan households’ report facing issues in sending their children to school. These issues include: Lack of teaching and/or learning material, Lack of transportation, overcrowding in functional schools, lack of adequate WASH facilities in schools, security risk commuting to and from school, and inadequate WASH facilities in schools and learning centers. Similarly, 42% of the non-Libyan population has reported that at least one child in their household is not enrolled in school. 21 per cent of the non-Libyan population report that they are facing problem of school fees, transportation, education materials and increased food expenses. In the context of COVID-19, all the schools have been closed as precautionary measures. Students’ access to distance learning is very limited. Only 19 per cent of children have accessed the distance learning. For many children, the schools serve as the entry point for other lifesaving services. The closure of schools to them means the denial of these services including psychosocial and recreational services, supplementary school feeding, access to life saving messages.
