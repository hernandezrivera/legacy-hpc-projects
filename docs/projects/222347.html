Project Summary

The project aimed to contribute to the FSL cluster CO2 – Emergency Livelihood support and CO3 – Resilience-building activities in Aweil East, Aweil South counties of Northern Bahr el Ghazal state Rubkona, Koch, and Panyijiar Counties of Unity State. The IRC will respond to the most vulnerable and food-insecure households classified in IPC4 or, worse, acute food insecurity phases. The project focuses on distributing emergency agricultural inputs (cereal and vegetable seeds, tools, and fishing kits) to support food security and improve nutrition, particularly for pregnant women and caregivers of malnourished children under five. It includes providing nutrient-dense vegetable seeds, establishing demonstration gardens near nutrition centers, and offering basic vegetable production training. Women and youth will receive training in climate-smart agriculture and timely weather information to build resilience against climate shocks. The project will also promote agroforestry technologies to enhance food security, green jobs, and environmental conservation while diversifying livelihoods through fish value chains, groundnut production, fuel-efficient cooking stoves, and improving access to income and assets. The project will mainstream gender, protection conflict sensitivity, disaster risk reduction and inclusion of persons with disability to enhance climate and conflict-affected populations' economic well-being and resilience. In pursuit of the project objective(s), the following activities will be implemented:
(a) The IRC and the complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM/AAP) committees will mobilize and engage community members to inform the needs of the most vulnerable households and selection criteria for registration together with FSL, Nutrition, and protection partners.
(b) (Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (Cereal and vegetable seeds, and tools) and fishing kits. The IRC anticipates FAO will provide complimentary in-kind input to support the project.
(c) To improve nutritional practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under 5 years with MAM and SAM. Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds, demonstration gardens at nutrition facilities close to water points, and basic training on vegetable production techniques will be provided. The IRC Nutrition team will conduct nutrition awareness and food cooking demonstrations showing how to enrich households’ meals with locally available foods
(d) Provide training to women and youth on climate-smart agriculture practices and timely dissemination of climate and weather information to individuals affected by climate-related shocks.
(e) Build farmers' resilience on climate-smart agroforestry technologies to improve food security, green jobs, and environmental protection.
(f) Women and youth diversified livelihood opportunities (fish value chain, groundnut, fuel-efficient cooking stove) and access to financial services through training and in-kind supports
(g) Flood affacted communities capacity are strengthen to response to flood through rehabilitation/construction of community assets through cash for work
(h) Overall, the project will reach 6% (218,000 people) (i.e 18,420 people) in emergency or severe (IPC 4 for 2025) in Rubkona, Koch, and Panyijiar counties in Unity state and Aweil East, Aweil South counties of Northern Bahr el Ghazal state. In Particular, Koch (929 people), Aweil South (2175 people), Panyijiar (1,512 people), Rubkona (8,462 people) and Aweil East (5,342 people).

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple shocks and stressors, including insecurity, intra-communal violence, and conflict, low agricultural productivity, poor macroeconomic conditions compounded by recurrent climatic shocks (flooding and prolonged dry spells leading to low agricultural productivity), and the ongoing conflict in Sudan. The continued influx of returnees and refugees has driven high humanitarian needs, putting an additional burden on host communities who are sharing scarce resources with new arrivals. According to UNHCR/IOM Sudan Crisis Displacement tracker, As of November 27, 2024, South Sudan received 878,719 returnees and refugees from Sudan, of which 57,644 people are in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 29,338 people in Unity states. Food insecurity is likely to worsen in 2025 in South Sudan. According to the Integrated Food Security Classification Phase Classification (IPC, 2025), food and nutrition insecurity in South Sudan is expected to worsen at the peak of the lean season (May – July, 2024). An estimated 218,000 people will experience emergency or Catastrophe food insecurity (IPC 4) in. Koch (15,000), Aweil South (37,000), Panyijiar (25,000) , Rubkona (141,000), Aweil East (89,000) counties. With huge food gaps among returnees and refugees. This is likely to increase severe and harmful coping mechanisms like begging and borrowing, leading to the accumulation of debts; forced migration in search of jobs; the sale of productive assets, reducing the frequency of meals; and eating wild fruits, among others. The lack of food among refugees and returnees and host communities is likely to increase the strain on host communities in counties as they turn to their hosts for support. It is also likely to increase tensions between the refugees, returnees, and host communities, with the risk of exacerbating conflict over available food and natural resources. According to the OCHA flood situation report, as of November 2024, about 1.4 million people are affected by flooding in 43 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area, with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising more than 51 percent of the affected population. The Recurrent climatic shocks continue to destroy farmlands, reducing annual crop yields. Other sources of livelihood, like fishing, experience loss and damage of fishing equipment, thereby reducing fish trade, which is one of the key income sources. Pastoralist livestock continues to be affected by pests and diseases, significant damage to pasture/grazing land, and decreased milk production. Further, communities and local authorities lack the capacity, knowledge, and skills to respond to climate shocks and new emerging disasters. Thus, there is a need to provide emergency cash, food, and livelihood response to the vulnerable affected people's needs.

2. Project activities

(a) At the inception phase, the IRC will establish and train the CFM/AAP committee on their roles and responsibilities, using WFP’s/FAO standard operating procedures. The APP will include local authorities, CAD and RRC, women, and youth leaders. (b) The CFM/AAP will validate beneficiary selection criteria and participate in the beneficiary identification, selection, and registration process to ensure equal representation of vulnerable groups, including beneficiaries from the nutrition and protection sectors. The targets are vulnerable households, including IDPs, returnees (from Sudan), and host communities, including pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), caregivers with children under five with SAM and MAN, and women GBV survivors. (c) Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (cereal and vegetable seeds and tools) and fishing kits. The IRC anticipates FAO will provide complimentary in-kind input to support the project. The response Koch (Priority 3) is for 6 months and (Priority 1) locations will be within the critical lean season. (d) Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds will be provided to caregivers, and demonstration gardens will be established at nutrition facilities close to water points for training in vegetable production. This is aimed at enhancing access to nutritional food to improve nutrition and caregiving practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under five with MAM and SAM. (d) The IRC will conduct nutrition-sensitive awareness and cooking demonstrations on preparing nutritious meals, particularly for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5, using vegetables from gardens and other locally available foods. Nutrition-sensitive messages will be shared on hygiene promotion to caregivers.(e) Farmers groups will be provided with short-maturing grafted fruit trees (guava, mangoes, jackfruit, avocado, papaya, etc.) to establish gardens. AEAs will conduct customized training for women and youth farmers on climate-smart nursery bed establishment and management, fruit tree grafting and management, seedling multiplication of short-maturing fruit trees, and vegetable production using the FAO training manual. (f) The project will engage women and youth in diversified livelihoods (fish and groundnut value chains) to meet the nutritional needs of food-insecure HHs and to generate income. Also, train beneficiaries on agribusiness, provide in-kind or cash grants, business mentorship, formation of agribusiness cooperative society, and access to financial services via VSLA, and offer training on GBV mitigation. (g) IRC will engage flood-affected communities to rehabilitate/construct community assets (dikes, drainage channels, etc) through Cash for Work (CfW) targeting 3,070 people. The IRC anticipates carrying out two cycles of CfW activities with beneficiaries to receive conditional cash assistance to be informed by the unskilled daily wage based on the CCCM rate. (h) The IRC will deploy a team of technical staff to implement and monitor the project activities in the field directly. The project will engage beneficiaries and local authorities for participatory joint monitoring every quarter. The project M&E plan will include periodic data collection and tracking through standard FOA WFP and IRC. The IRC will engage relevant stakeholders to document lessons learned that the IRC and partners can use in designing future projects.
