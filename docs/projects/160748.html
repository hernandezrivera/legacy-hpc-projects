Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to reduce food insecurity of targeted 6,100 household (36,600) people IDPs and host communities affected by impacts of complex crisis including effects of the recent civil war, ethnic conflicts over resources, worsening economic situation across the country, poor farming skills, loss of productive assets during the conflict, climate variability induced droughts and flooding and in Yirol East of Lakes State, in South Sudan. The project seeks to re-establish the self-reliance capacity of communities in own food production systems by improving the agricultural productive capacity of farmers who hitherto were able to produce their own food before the upheavals caused the recent crises referred to above.
This will be achieved through improving farming skills of household farmers who will be organized into farmer support groups. The farmers will be trained in various areas including promoting sustainable agricultural practices, climate resilient agricultural practices, farmer extension services and promotion of kitchen gardens.
During lean hunger periods from July to August the project will support distressed households with immediate life saving assistance through voucher and cash based interventions.

The project will benefit 6,100 households (36,600 people) who are Internally Displaced Peoples (IDPs) who will constitute about 75% of the beneficiaries and vulnerable host communities’ households. Targeting will be based on humanitarian principles of humanity and impartiality to save lives and alleviate suffering. A participatory and consultative approach will be used to agree on targeting criteria that ensures the most vulnerable individuals/households are identified and benefited from this support. The selection criteria based on CTP’s SADI (Safety, Access, Dignity and Inclusivity) Framework will be used to ensure inclusion of groups such as poor female headed households, households with malnourished children, Pregnant and Lactating Mothers, households with persons living with disability, those with low income, and the most food insecure households. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will make, at least, 10 percent of the beneficiaries’ households will be targeted according to membership of person living with disabilities.
The project will collaborate and work with other agencies to achieve synergy. It will collaborate with WFP or its partners such as CRS involved in food distributions in the area. The project will work closely with the agricultural ministry whose staff together with CTP and partner staff will play a key role in farmer training and extension services. The project will also collaborate with health and nutrition actors to target and support households with malnutrition cases with vegetable gardening.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is faced with a complex and protracted humanitarian crisis reflecting the devastating effects of the protracted years of war. The cumulative impact of the war including recent post-independence conflicts, resulted in deeply impoverished communities which are highly vulnerable to external shocks. During the conflict and displacement most IDPs lost or left their productive assets as they fled, making it next to impossible to pursue viable livelihoods activities. The host communities some of whom have accommodated the displaced households also experience poverty and their coping capacity has been stretched to the limit. According to August 2019 JIMM, undertaken by Cash Working Group, has identified Yirol East as the second most expensive places in South Sudan in terms of meeting minimum survival food basket.The population of Yirol East is dependent on rain-fed agriculture. Climate variability has resulted in increasing frequency and intensity of droughts and at times floods. The ever-increasing dry spell and ongoing crises have destroyed traditional positive coping mechanisms, and this has resulted in increasing numbers of people to depend on external assistances and relief interventions by the UN agencies and NGOs. Yirol East is in perpetual Emergency Phase (IPC Phase 3 or 4) and with high malnutrition rates. According to IPC population estimations for August 2019, about 57.9% of the population, circa 660000 people, in Lakes State under which Yirol East falls, are in IPC phase 3 and above with high likelihood to decline further. In August 2019 about 10,000 people in Yirol East were declared to be experiencing IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) conditions of food insecurity. According to (CUAAM and HELP) the malnutrition caseloads in the month of August 2019 reported on rise with GAM rate at 22.6 % in Yirol East, beyond the WHO 15% emergency threshold. Malnutrition cases continue to be on the rise due to the food insecurity situation coupled with communicable diseases. The delay in the onset of rains and eventually late planting in 2019 has further worsened the food security situation. Often employed coping strategies include wild fruit foraging, skipping meals, reducing food portion, and prioritizing family members, and splitting family, and charcoal makings among others. In some cases young girls are forced into early marriages in order to receive bride price to sustain the family. Though it is not common and deteriorating further through time, in addition to aforementioned strategy, inter-clan and inter-family charity and loaning schemes is also being used. This project will seek to break this cycle of food insecurity by supporting communities to re-establish their food production capabilities and self-reliance. In poor households women are expected to provide food for their families and husbands even in cases where the husband has not provided for the family. Supporting household food production will help contribute to reducing cases of SGBV. Cattle raiding is rampant in Yirol East. It is a principal cause for inter communal conflict and displacement, loss of lives and livelihoods. Recently, in Yirol East, the intercommunal conflict along the border of Lake State of Dinka communities and communities from Unity state i.e. Nuer resulted in displacement of hundreds of people and this has also significantly impacted access of the community to alternative food sources such as wild fruits and roots, fishing grounds and access to pasture and water for their livestock.

2 - Activities

The project will assist crisis affected communities’, households, and individuals to produce enough crops, legumes and vegetables through agricultural support intervention. The key activities will include- Farmer training and promoting sustainable agricultural practices including improved agronomic practices on seeding, weeding, pest management/control and disease management, and minimization of the post-harvest and storage losses.- Climate resilient agriculture practices such as soil and water conservation mulching, tie and ridge farming and use of appropriate seed varieties..- “train the trainer” methodology - Toward this, the project will second and deploy agricultural extension staff and assistants from CAFOD, local partner and collaborate with state and country agriculture department to educate and train farmers targeted under the programme. Promising lead with potential will be trained to offer peer support to fellow farmers.- Promotion of nutrition/kitchen gardens through women’s groups.- Farmers will be supported to access productive assets such as farm implements and tools, inputs and support for farm labor assistance. Opportunities will be explored for collaboration with WFP through the voucher or cash for assets to support poor resourced farmers to plough, plant and weed farmlands. For this, WFP wage rate will be used to support this aspect of the project.- Farmers will be supported with extension services through CAFOD and partners staff, agriculture ministry staff and peer farmers who demonstrate good potential.CAFOD will provide locally adaptable and drought-tolerate seeds for traditional staple crops grown in the area like sorghum and millet and ground nuts which is a major source of income and contributes immensely to food security. This intervention will enable the targeted households to be self sufficient in own food production and obtain income from marketing the surplus production. The intervention will be implemented in ways that support local markets through seed fairs based on seed market assessment results which will be done in collaboration with UNFAO. Households affected by malnutrition will be prioritised for support with vegetable seeds and assorted hand tools to enhance food dietary diversity of that particular household. This intervention will enable the targeted households to produce their own food and obtain income from marketing the surplus production.In addition, in order to avert perennial food insecurity and starvation experienced in the project area during the lean period, short term immediate lifesaving assistance will be provided through voucher/cash based interventions. The modality will be informed by findings of comprehensive market assessments which will be conducted by CAFOD humanitarian staffs drawn from both the region and in country. During the lean period household food security deteriorates with high levels of malnutrition especially amongst the under five year olds. This approach will be used sparingly. Another important intervention included under this project is provision of comprehensive fishing support. This is because of the fact that, the area where this project will be implemented has high potential for fishing activities. As a result, the project will provide training on fishing activities, along with this, will also provide recommended fishing kits. Among the training package, to reduce post-harvest loss and advance fish production, the programme will promote fish preservation technologies piloted by UNFAO South Sudan. So, this will be implemented in collaboration with UNFAO South Sudan programme. The project will work to develop capacity of asset poor female headed households and families affected by malnutrition through activities like distribution of locally adoptable chickens. Conflict sensitive programming and the promotion of peaceful co-existence will be mainstreamed in the project. Gender equality and women empowerment will be mainstreamed.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

CAFOD has a robust SADI (Safe, Access, Dignified and Inclusive) framework which recognizes the rights of disaster affected individuals and groups to have access to safe and adequate basic services in a dignified manner that offers protection and goes some way to ensure inclusivity of marginalized groups. CAFOD has included a strong component on safe and responsive feedback and complaints handling and response in its SADI Framework which is rolled out across all its programme. The main emphasis of this is on ensuring that systems are safe, accessible, dignified and inclusive for every group in a community, particularly the most marginalized, and that there is consultation in the project design, the feedback loop is closed, and that the effectiveness of the mechanism is monitored. This allows room for flexibility in programme to ensure they have a system that is appropriate and adapted for their context. Requirements to have a feedback and complaints mechanism in place have also been incorporated in every partners operation, with requirements for any sensitive / serious complaint to be shared with CAFOD within a set time limit.The beneficiaries CAFOD works for are citizen of South Sudanese and they are also legally recognized (both IDPs and Host communities).

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project design puts IDPs at the heart of this intervention. The main beneficiaries of this intervention include both IDPs and host communities. In proportion, the beneficiaries will be 75% IDPs and 25% host communities in order to ensure equity and cohesion between the IDPs and the hosts. It is also in recognition of the fact that a lot of host communities play host to IDPs in their areas and homes which has overstretched their meagre resources. So this project will make efforts to address the needs of both Host and IDPs. The project focuses on meeting basic needs of the most vulnerable including IDPs, female headed households, families with persons living with disability to access basic services offered by the project such as capacity building for own food production, protection and referrals of survivors of SGBV, support for PSS and Cash/Voucher for food insecure households. The project design has taken into consideration the needs and the expectations of different community groups such as women, men, boys, girls, and children through participatory approaches that have been used to determine the community’s needs.The Displacement Affected Communities will have safe and dignified access to this support and to materialize this women groups containing 5 members will be established as beneficiary selection committees (2 from host, 3 from IDP HHs) in various project locations. These committee will identify most vulnerable IDP and Host communities as per the set and agreed criteria with validation done at public meetings.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will work with and through the relevant government line offices and bureaus at various levels such as State, County and Payam Level across all phases of the project (planning, implementation, and monitoring). There is collaboration with Government technical departments in design of project. The government staffs in the ministry of agriculture, trained and equipped well on agricultural extension works will facilitate and provide technical advices and support to the beneficiaries. Moreover, the project will also work closely with Relief Rehabilitation Commissions. The project will also train local beneficiary farmers with potential to offer peer to peer extension services. This approach will ensure the long-term sustainability of the project in terms of smooth implementation the project activities and government departments rendering services to the community beyond the project period.Project committees made up of beneficiaries and local leaders will be established to mobilise community members for active participation in different project activities. To avoid duplication CAFOD and its partners are active participants in various coordination fora at State and national level. CAFOD and its partners also attend various government meetings at county level and has good working relationships with different government departments at the field Level.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

CAFOD is implementing an integrated and multi sectoral program which supports saving lives and at the same offering durable solutions that build the resilience of communities to future vulnerabilities. The interventions include hygiene and sanitation promotion, gender based violence, Psychosocial Support (PSS), various livelihood support activities including food security, promoting peaceful coexistence and improving access to safe water. This would enable most vulnerable households to recover from crises by building resilience capacity to acute and chronic shocks through well designed and integrated programming to support positive coping capacity and promoting livelihood productivities. As a result of ongoing peace negotiations on the revitalised peace agreement, CAFOD hopes and expects that there might be increased caseloads of returnees and efforts to support these group is vital to promote resilience capacity of the communities, households, and individuals. The project will promote peace building initiatives which is a national priority. The project will make efforts to address the root causes of conflict and drivers of displacement, which can provide the basis for further returns and stability. This intervention will be implemented in conjunction with ongoing interventions being undertaken in collaboration with South Sudan Council of Churches (SSCC). This is being undertaken through other funding sources and contributes toward peaceful coexistence through conflict prevention and mitigation measures which is a prerequisite for development programs to take place. The intervention CAFOD implementing through and with its implementing partners benefits IDPs, returnees, and host communities, that have been affected by protracted crises.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is stable but requires more effort to improve the situation and challenge deep rooted cultural practices. Understanding and capacities on protection and gender are low, both among government, service providers and communities. Communities especially women and girls face a wide range of protection issues. These issues include sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), forced and early marriages and poor access to basic services such as case management of rape (CMR). Often people struggle to find out where and how they can get help.About 80% of displaced households are headed by females who assume even greater family responsibilities. Displaced female headed households generally experience higher levels of vulnerability and poverty than male headed households. The conflict has worsened pre-existing gender inequalities and poverty among women. From a study by CAFOD and TOCH in 2019 on gender based violence in the project area it was found out that there are high levels of SGBV especially rape in Yirol East. There are widespread forced and early marriages of girls driven by poverty, commodification of women and girls as a source of wealth through bride price and deep-rooted cultural practices which make such practices acceptable in the community. Due to customary patriarchal practices, gender-based power relations are skewed against women and girls. Women and girls are restricted and suppressed in decision making on various issues and at all levels- at community and household level. Women and girls lack equal access to education, livelihood assets. economic opportunities and decision making compared to men and boys. To address this, this intervention will complement with other CTP on ongoing multiyear and multi sectoral response where capacity building for psychosocial support and GBV service providers, and community at large are provided along with working with others to establish safe referral pathways and better service provision.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CAFOD seeks to provide both life saving support and initiatives that help communities in transitioning to self-reliance and build resilience to reduce future vulnerability in Yirol East. The project will adopt an integrated multisectoral approach that will focus on the humanitarian development nexus that seeks to provide appropriate lifesaving relief support and at the same time build durable solutions to vulnerability through facilitating and strengthening communities’ livelihoods opportunities. CAFOD Humanitarian program logic focuses on the linking Relief Recovery and Development (LRRD) approach. In this approach agriculture is an important and strategic opportunity for livelihoods and economic recovery for IDPs and host communities alike because the vast majority of them have been engaged in agriculture for generations. Agriculture provides a pathway to self-reliance in food production. Community participation at all stages of the design and implementation process is important for sustainability of the project. CAFOD works with local leaders and project committees which create avenues for community engagement and involvement in decision making on management of the project. The project will be purposely implemented in collaboration with key government departments such as the agricultural ministry to ensure complementarity and continued support to communities beyond the project period. The project will utilize government staffs especially for agricultural extension training and these have perpetual existence in the project areas. The local seed fair which will be undertaken will also support farmers to have access to market. By coordinating with other actors, by engaging and participating community across all project management cycle, using existing government capacity and shifting to resilience approach, the project will ensure sustainability of its impact after the intervention period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats both external and internal. All intervention responses will be guided by Do-No-Harm and conflict-sensitivity approaches. CAFOD uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion or lack of consultation or participation of all marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of particular groups of people. From a research undertaken by CAFOD and TOCH in 2019 in the project area women and girls are not included in decision making in civic matters of the community and at family level as a result of the patriarchal nature of the Yirol East community. In order to mitigate this CAFOD has through this project and other interventions designed specific project to address gender inequality and women empowerment through various activities including women capacity building for leadership, confidence building, access to economic opportunities. The project will enhance protection especially of women and girls against SGBV. Activities include offering PSS, strengthening capacity of organisations to improve service delivery in areas such as case management of rape and others and strengthen referral pathways to other service providers. To minimize interference of elites and interest groups the project will undertake fair and impartial targeting mechanisms using its past experiences. Use of the SADI framework results in an objective identification of deserving cases.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CTP ongoing response in Yirol East has established a robust complaint and response handling mechanism and referral pathway in place and this project will build on the existing mechanism. Currently in place, exist help desk/focal person, toll free hotline, and community monitoring evaluation accountability and learning (CMEAL) committee as channels for receiving complaints and providing feedback. CTP implementing partner have dedicated staff as focal persons for complaints handling. CTP will ensure all relevant information pertaining the feedback mechanism is shared with the community in every meeting. The different category of people will be consulted in separate focus group discussions to ensure they feel comfortable to express their views and their feedback incorporated to programme on real time. CTP has a rigorous safeguarding policy in place and committed to systematic implementation and monitoring of SADI framework.
