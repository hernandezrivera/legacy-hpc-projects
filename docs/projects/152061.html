Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in North East Nigeria. UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed and by expanding the breadth and quality of protection response. Specifically, the project will improve the protection situation of the most vulnerable in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa through:
1. Identification of vulnerable individuals through community-based protection monitoring, vulnerability screening and refugee returnee registration, referrals and comprehensive protection response;
2. Enhancing access to legal protection and access to documentation;
3. Provision of protection-based material items to reduce specific vulnerabilities;
4. Strengthening stakeholder capacity on protection principles; and
5. Supporting durable solutions for returning refugees and IDPs.

Needs

The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States has been marked by mass human rights violations against the population, brutal attacks, abduction, rampant sexual violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, family separation, disappearances, and forced displacement. Widespread abuses, coupled with the break down in social structures, law and order throughout the conflict, has seen the erosion of the protective environment for those in most affected areas. 823,000 civilians in "hard-to-reach" areas are severely at risk, requiring urgent support. The most vulnerable civilians face heightened protection risks, including female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys and girls, the elderly, the chronically sick, people with physical and mental disabilities, and pregnant and lactating women within IDP camps, host communities and return areas. Recovery efforts continue to be hampered in many areas by lack of civilian infrastructure, conflict-driven insecurity resulting in new displacements, access constraints, limited availability of basic services for survival and limited presence of the relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations, all of which exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable populations. Threats are often compounded by the high level of congestion among displaced families, lack of basic resources for survival and by the location of vulnerable individuals in isolated, insecure or inhospitable places. Multiple rounds of vulnerability screening, ongoing protection monitoring and protection assessments show the severity of protection needs for those most affected by conflict and displacement. Insecurity and desperation has resulted in additional challenges for female headed households and widows, increasing their vulnerability to sexual violence and forced/early marriage, leading then to risk of rejection and stigmatization by their families and community members. High numbers of unaccompanied and separated children, orphans and child-headed households have critical protection needs and many children have had to resort to hawking/begging as means of survival. There has been an increase of instances of attacks, harassment, abduction and GBV when women and girls venture outside sites' security perimeter in order to search for firewood. The challenges for reintegration facing boys who were recruited and used by Boko Haram are acute. The absence of civil authorities in newly accessible areas and role of military has undermined the humanitarian and civilian character of IDP sites. There is a critical need to continue to scale up efforts to prevent, refer and response to cases with identified protection risks with fulsome protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration and coordination with all stakeholders on durable solutions is required. In line with the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy, the protection of affected persons must be at the forefront of decision making, informing strategic response. 1,580,093 formally displaced individuals have returned to their places of origin as of August 2018. This includes 35,701 refugees who returned to Nigeria from neighbouring countries from January to August 2018, many of which have fallen short of international standards on voluntariness, safety and dignity, including refoulement. IDP returnee and refugee returnees are staying in difficult-to-access areas which lack basic services, infrastructure and livelihood opportunities, and both groups also face challenges with regards to housing and repossessing their original properties. UNHCR, and the Governments of Cameroon and the Federal Republic of Nigeria signed a Tripartite Agreement for the voluntary repatriation of Nigerian refugees living in Cameroon in March 2017, setting out modalities for the voluntary and facilitated return of Nigerian refugees in safety and dignity to Nigeria.

Activities

o Identification of vulnerable individuals through community-based protection monitoring, vulnerability screening and refugee returnee registration, referrals and comprehensive protection response: UNHCR will work with community-based protection monitoring systems (Protection Action Groups) to support affected populations to identify protection risks/incidents facing their community members and capacitate them to be able to refer/respond to issues. UNHCR will profile and track vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees through the rolling vulnerability screening process to allow for delivery of assistance tailored to specific needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, GBV, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response by UNHCR and Protection Sector partners. UNHCR will analyze information on protection trends as a basis for evidence-based advocacy in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming within other sectoral interventions. o Enhance access to legal protection and access to documentation: Access to quality legal aid and services, including legal advice, counselling and legal representation will be provided on civil and criminal matters. This includes legal assistance on housing, land and property issues and for survivors of SGBV and support for mobile courts. Detention monitoring in police stations and immigration facilities will continue, alongside efforts to seek access to military screening centers/detention. UNCHR will scale up its project on legal documentation, providing national identity cards, birth certificates and other documentation. UNHCR will continue to conduct targeted advocacy for the adoption of legal frameworks for IDP protection in Nigeria. o Provision of protection-based material items: UNHCR will ensure that protection-based materials are provided to those identified with specific protection needs, including the provision of solar lanterns, energy-efficient cooking sets and hygiene kits to ensure that vulnerabilities are addressed. o Capacity building: Enhancing the capacity of stakeholders to the humanitarian response remains will ensure that assistance is provided in line with international standards and that core protection principles are adhered to by service providers. o Durable solutions for returning refugees and IDPs: UNCHR will engage in advocacy and efforts to establish the conditions for the pursuit of durable solutions to internal displacement and for refugee returnees. UNHCR will provide support to both spontaneous refugee returnees, as well as engage on facilitated voluntary repatriation for refugees in surrounding countries who elect to return. Registration of refugee returnees will continue to ensure the planning of comprehensive reintegration programs. UNHCR will provide reception and transit facilities for refugee returnees, equipping the way stations and reception facilities with basic accommodation, food and health facilities. Sustainable return in final destination areas will be ensured through provision of a full return package of non-food items, food items, shelter materials and cash assistance. Peacebuilding interventions will reinforce communities' capacities to manage disputes and reduce the impact of violent conflict on the most vulnerable, including women, youth and marginalized groups with communities. Livelihood programs addressing specific protection needs will continue through referral of beneficiaries to UNHCR Livelihood programs to receive relevant skills for self-reliance thereby reducing negative coping mechanisms and empowering the most vulnerable persons.
