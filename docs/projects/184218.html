Project Summary

Project Summary
To improve the wellbeing, protection and quality of life, WV Somalia will target 15 districts across 8 regions in 4 states of Somalia. The targeted districts were ranked 3 to 5 on the severity index by the WASH cluster (Somalia: WASH overall severity score, 20th October, 2020).
The program aims to achieve three outcomes: 1) poor and vulnerable communities have increased access to sustainable and safe water supply in households, communities, school and in health facilities; 2) poor and vulnerable communities have increased access to quality and sustainable sanitation facilities; 3) targeted communities have improved awareness on and able to practice optimal hygiene practice
WV has a commitment to supporting capacity building efforts for local people and government line ministries. World Vision currently works and had MoUs with the 4 states governments in South West State, Puntland and Somaliland. This deliberate effort at partnering provides a platform for capacity building for the Ministry personnel, and at the same time strengthens collaboration and ensures World Vision strategy is in sync with the mandate and strategy of the local government.
WV is mindful of local custom and culture. We are committed to participatory and sustainable development and to provide assistance impartially. WV acknowledge their accountabilities to those whom they assist and those who provide support to their work and also committed to ensuring that it does not expose intended beneficiaries, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation. To ensure sustainable delivery, WV seeks to encourage participation in decision making processes and skills training so as to ensure that the beneficiaries will act as an active component in the implementation of the program.
The WASH program has been deliberate in ensuring its supported facilities are inclusive and can be used by people with physical disabilities, thus reducing environmental and social barriers. One of the examples will be constructing latrines which are accessible to wheelchair-bound persons by incorporating ramps as standard at doorways and having wide fully opening doors. For public sanitation facilities, ramps and rails have also been installed as standard for the sanitation facilities.
The WASH program will work closely with the Education and Protection and Health and Nutrition sectors to foster sector integration. The WASH programme in Health Facilities will focus on ensuring all consultations and admissions, including children under the age of 5, have access to clean water and sanitation within the target locations. At each of the health facilities, World Vision will provide an elaborate water and sanitation management programme, which will include incinerators, autoclaves, clean water, and institutional latrines for proper infection prevention and control practices. The schools will be connected to the main water supply and receive occasional water trucking at times when the main water supply is erratic. Children will also be provided with WASH NFIs, including MHM kits that will be given to adolescent girls at the schools to help reduce on the number of girls who drop out of school during their menstrual cycle. Ideally, the 3-star approach will be adopted and guide operations in schools. DRR will include developing capacity of local government to understand, own and trigger demand for WASH through advocacy to take policy decisions to develop a political will that enable a WASH resilience building environment. In WASH programming mainstreaming issues such as DRR, protection and gender will be incorporated at all levels.

1. - Projects activities

- Supply and Install submersible pump, motors pump, chlorine pucks and inline chlorinator system with by-pass valve to 5 boreholes to provide sustainable water for both IDPs, rural communities and schools. - Five hand dug well rehabilitated: The hand dug wells will be deepened to a minimum depth of 10m, and then well rings were installed to protect the sides of the wells from collapse. This will be followed by grouting around the shallow wells to a depth of 3m and construction of minimum 0.5m high headwall and 1m wide concrete apron to prevent contamination of the hand-dug wells by surface runoff- Establishing 5 water supply system using pipe connection from exiting boreholes to households and institutions (schools and clinics) together with elevated reservoir and tap stands.- Upgrading and rehabilitating 5 diesel run water systems to solar-powered system.- Installing one pilot solar-powered reverse osmosis desalinization system in Puntland.- Rehabilitate and upgrade 2 earth dams to an improved (HDPE lined, solar powered pumping system and water filtration system) high capacity haffir dams.- Household water treatment and safe storage (HWTSS) - Installation of 20 remote ground water monitoring sensors on boreholes and establish an online data repository. - Supply Water Quality Testing kits - Construction of 200stalls upgradable family shared VIP latrines in IDPs. An upgradable latrine consists of a lined latrine pit designed to last a minimum of 5 years and much longer if desludging facilities are available. The superstructure is made of cheaper materials, but with emphasis on providing privacy, and satisfying the GBV checklist. The latrine design will be as per latrine design standards set out by the Somalia WASH Cluster. Institutional facilities will be supported with a 4-door inclusive latrine, with doors and lockable doors - Construct gender segregated twin latrines with hand washing facilities for 10 schools - Conduct Hygiene promotion activities and Clean up campaigns both at community level and in Schools within the Target areas. Hygiene promotion-messaging will focus on AWD awareness, emphasizing on simple but effective hygiene practices such as washing hands with soap and clean water at the critical times, use of safe and clean drinking water at all times and safe excreta disposal using an improved sanitation facility as some of the ways of breaking the fecal-oral disease transmission route. - -Water trucking to 52000 people 30-60 days after a disaster- Distribution of NFIs such as jerry cans, dignity kits, and buckets (with lids), bars of soap, aqua tabs, P and G sachets and sanitation tool kits, MHM kits to 55,000 people.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

WV will prevent risk of exclusion by ensuring project and beneficiary selections are need-based and designed with close consultation with communities. User rations and quality of WASH infrastructure will be considered in target selection. WV will work closely with the communities to identified the disadvantaged groups and include them in project design, monitoring and evaluation process. This means to ensure their specific needs and barriers are identified as well as their concerns can be heard. WV will promote active and representative participation of different groups in communities to influence decisions that affect their lives.WV will ensure water source and hygiene facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. Consider the prevalent gender inequality and gender-based violence, WV will take specific focus on ensuring women and girls are protected and are served in a gender-sensitive way in WASH intervention. Gender-specific protection needs will be mainstreamed by providing latrines with locks both inside and outside. Separated latrines will be constructed and labelled to ensure separation use of latrine use for males and females. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to women and girls to address their menstruation need. Both men and women will be encouraged to take part in hygiene promotion, water committees and any other forum in related to this project. WV will endeavor to reach a 50% representation of women whenever possible and appropriate. Competing over access to essential commodities and services such as water source are often one of the significant causes of community conflict. To reduce conflict, the project will engage and consult with different community groups throughout the project cycle including site and beneficiary selection. WV will also ensure transparency by sensitizing communities of the infrastructure and services available, their rights and responsibility. By fostering community engagement throughout the project cycle, the project can reduce the risk of conflict caused by unfairness and/or misunderstanding from the communities. The project will take into consideration Inter-Agency Standing Committee commitment to affected populations. A complaints and feedback mechanisms (toll free number, community meetings, and feedback boxes) are in place. A toll-free hotline with Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system proved to be an effective tool to receive feedback from different stakeholder. WV’s quarterly accountability report (April – June 2021) reported 86% of the feedback was channelled through the hotline. It also observes to be a more suitable tool for women and girls to provide their feedback as more than half (64%) of the feedback were received from female who may find more difficult to voice their opinion in community meeting in Somalia context. The project will leverage on existing WV Community Response Map platform that facilitates online recording, tracking, compilation and visual mapping and feedback received from community members dailyWV will work with inter-cluster agencies to ensure that further WASH infrastructure development is undertaken where the displaced have been permitted to settle. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during the program. Assessment will be conducted to make sure the project will not expose beneficiaries to security risks. Throughout the project duration, WV will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advise and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.
