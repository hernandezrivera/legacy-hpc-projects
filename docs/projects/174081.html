Project Summary

The war waged by the extremist group has affected the entire communities, but among those hit the hardest were women, girls and children who now comprise a great percentage of IDPs in camps and host communities. The humanitarian crises created by Boko Haram conflict have severely affecting socio-economic, physical and emotional wellbeing of the people. Given the protracted nature of the crisis coupled with limited access to basic services, women remain economically vulnerable due to the shift in their traditional responsibilities from wife to head of households, this have exposed them to domestic violence, including physical and sexual assault as revealed by the UN- Interagency Humanitarian needs assessment 2020. More so, a Gender dynamics survey conducted by the British Council in Yobe State reveals other prevalent forms of GBV include early and force marriage, abductions, sexual violence, sexual harassment, domestic violence, denial of resources and witchcraft accusations.
Sexual exploitation of women and girls are also linked to closure of IDP camps as women and girls have no option but to have sex with men who provide them with shelter, food or money. Other social norms has however impacted prevention and response to GBV, SEA are less likely to be reported as access to justice in most cases are frustrated by delay and status variance, while Cultural believes and practices seem to favor the perpetrators more than the survivors as SAE cases are mostly shrouded in secrecy as its considered more of a shameful act for women and girls than to the men and boys. These continue to increase the vulnerability of women and girls thereby perpetually exposing them to social and emotional/psychological hardships, including rejection by families. Despite the alarming GBV cases in Yobe state, less than 1% of people targeted were reached in 2020 according to (humanitarian situation analysis of June 2020 published by UNOCHA). While these crimes and other harmful practices continue to cause deep emotional and psychological trauma to women and young girls, community support system remain weak, thereby creating heighten vulnerability in conflict affected areas.
As a serious human rights problem further intensify by the insurgency, it is imperative to note that GBV requires all actors to work in concert to strengthening prevention and response initiatives. It is therefore imperative to augment resilience of women and girls through adoption of broader strategy that seek to reduce vulnerability of persons at heightened risk especially women and girls affected by conflict so as to allow them fully enjoy their rights to safe, dignity and nondiscrimination
The overall objective of ‘Strengthening Gender based violence prevention and response through effective engagement of local actors in Damaturu, Gujba, Potiskum in Yobe state’’ could be achieved through integrated community-based approach in addressing gaps identified in prevention and response to Gender Based Violence in conflict affected communities of Yobe. The project will also support the achievement of Northeast GBV strategic objectives for 2020, especially in Yobe state where response is reported to have been less than 1% during the year. In addition, it presents an opportunity to reach more vulnerable women and girls in hard to reach communities with access to life-saving and specialized GBV services through enhanced community response.

2 - Activities

Specifically, AFRYDEV will conduct awareness creation to community persons at heightened risks, including women and girls, men and boy and other at risks groups. Capacity building to selected community stakeholders (women and girls, men & boys, persons with disability, traditional, religious, SARC and Social welfare staff, Police and NSCDC gender desk officer on GBV prevention and mitigation. Support and Establish GBV Community response Committee to strengthen community effort on documentation, reporting and referrals for quick response and access to comprehensive services for survivors.Provide at least 1000 women and girls and persons with disability with GBV specialized service includes; psycho-social support (PSS), medical-legal services and economic support to reduce their vulnerability and associated risk.Conduct State level policy dialogue with 100 key stakeholders includes; Min of Justice, MOWA, MORA,FIDA, GBV sector CSOs, SARC, NPF, NSCD, Min of Youths, Women &Girls leaders, Youths, Traditional and Religious leaders to identify gaps in GBV and strengthen prevention and response efforts through policy implementation. Goal: The overarching goal of this activity is to strengthen prevention and response to GBV through awareness creation, reporting and effective linkages for access to comprehensive services.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Fundamental to the operation of African Youths for Peace Development and Empowerment Foundation is respect for the dignity and basic human rights of people within Nigeria and throughout the world. AFRYDEV has a PSEA policy it ensure strict adherence. A robust communication strategy has been pur in place ensuring every person who represents AFRYDEV reflect the organization principles and values in their professional conduct, regardless of whom they are dealing with, or where they are working. AFRYDEV PSEA policy aims to provide a safe and trusted environment that safeguards everyone from SEA including beneficiary communities, workers, program participants and partner organizations. AFRYDEV is committed to safeguarding the people it helps and who it works alongside. We recognize that the nature of AFRYDEV’s work places our workers and program participants in positions of authority and trust in relation to the communities we work with, especially vulnerable adults and children. AFRYDEV workers and program participants have an obligation to uphold high standards of personal and professional conduct at all times and must not abuse this position in order to exploit or abuse another person. AFRYDEV will actively prevent and respond to SEA and maintain an organizational culture that prioritizes safeguarding against SEA so that it is safe for those affected to come forward and report incidents and concerns with the assurance they will be handled sensitively and appropriately.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AFRYDEV has a robust M&E framework that enables us track activity progress and measure result at every step of implementation. AFRYDEV will develop M&E plan that involves learning from the feedbacks received from beneficiaries on real time, also explore the use of technology in data collection aligned to the standards and procedures of the GBVIMS. Participatory community based monitoring of project activities and outputs will be done through field projects visits, interviews and observation. On spot and back-checks monitoring visits to monitor indicators set in the M&E plan, this will guide and inform the process. Data collection and analysis will be done in a participatory manner and reports produced which will be for reference and decision making. Supportive supervision and data validation and will be carried out under each milestone. . Activity progress report will be compiled weekly and Project reports on monthly basis including the 5W and shared with the sector. The most significant changes/impact and success stories in the project will be captured through monitoring and evaluation and shared as evidence of expected positive changes among women, girls and boys and men.
