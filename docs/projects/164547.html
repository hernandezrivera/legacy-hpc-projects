Project Summary

The project aims to deliver WaSH services in Ar Raqqa governorate in 9 communities (Bader C5759, Jdidet Khabur C5760, Jdidet Kahit C5757, Nasra C5762, Ojet Btir C5765, Samera - Oweija Hamad Elassaf C5771, Modar C5776, Masaada (Karama) C6379, Qadessiyeh (Karama) C6381
This intervention aims to deliver humanitarian development aid to a total of 31,000 affected individuals in Bader and Jdidet Khabur communities and surrounding areas in Ar Raqqa governorate – Ar Raqqa district. Through this project, a total of 4428 households across nine rural communities will have access to essential and stabilization WaSH services. The project will aim to increase the resilience and stability of the target communities after the latest military operations between the Syria Democratic Forces SDF and the Islamic State IS militias.
Based on an assessment of the need that has been made in the aforementioned areas, it was found that most families do not have water storage tanks and depend on the water available through the network if any and in the event that there are tanks, they need to be replaced because of their age and inability to preserve water. Accordingly, 1000 tanks will be distributed to the neediest and most vulnerable families.
Domestic waste from the mentioned communities will be collected on a daily basis by renting trucks and employing sanitation workers. Waste will be collected from the containers that will be distributed to the neighborhoods. Two types of containers will be distributed, one for solid waste and the other for organic garbage. After the collection process, the waste that will be carried out at the neighborhood level will be transferred to a designated place, according to the directions of the local authorities.
The beneficiaries will be counted and divided according to age, gender, and residential status. The priority in terms of access and support will be given to the most vulnerable groups, which are:
- HHs without water tanks.
- HHs with a history of a high incidence rate of communicable diseases.
- Pregnant and Lactating Women PLW.
- Under-five children.
- Elderly persons who are suffering from non-communicable diseases.
- Women-headed households.
- Malnourished children and women.
- Reproductive-aged women
The catchment population of the nine target communities will be considered as indirect beneficiaries. Because the access of these communities to health and WaSH stabilized services will be increased, the overall socio-economic status of the population will be enhanced. As a result, the contribution of these communities to the economic condition and the sources of income will be increased.
Local councils will be involved since the beginning of the project and consulted and involved during its implementation in order to coordinate and reach a broader target.
WHOSD has drafted an exit plan to be implemented by the end of this project, and the local authority is a part of this plan. WHOSD will improve and build the capacity of the field team to be able to administrate and function them after the end of the project. WHOSD will sign an MoU with the local authorities by which the equipment and assets procured through this project will be handed over to them.

4 - Needs

The WaSH needs were related to the provision of safe drinking water, especially that most of the containers were destroyed due to the military operations. Access to drinking water is a challenge to the people of Ar Raqqa, especially those living in temporary shelters and rural areas. Additionally, there is a need for waste management and disposal. In general, the level of awareness in the target areas was low even before the crisis in Syria. Thus, people are in need of raising awareness campaigns.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Obtain the required permits. 2- Coordination with the stakeholders, including the WaSH working group and NGO forum. 3- Procurement. 4- Recruitment. 5- Identify a place to be a dump for solid waste disposal. 6- Waste management and disposal activities. 7- Identify the most vulnerable 1000 households to provide them with water tanks of a capacity of 500 liters. 8- Set up the 1000 water tanks. 9- Training and capacity building. The solid waste management and water tank installation will be carried out and completed through a service contract with local suppliers who will be vetted before the final decision.The White Hats will focus on a daily monitoring of activities. Therefore, one monitoring and evaluation (M&E) field officer will be assigned to produce daily field reports to the M&E officer at the HQ. This procedure is to ensure the quality of implementation.The program officer will be responsible for the reporting, while the M&E officer will be responsible for monitoring and evaluation in the field as well as the systematic mechanism of reports submissions from the communities to the HQ. All surveys and collected data will be used as a baseline to measure the impact of the intervention on population and communities. Throughout the project period, M&E officer will be responsible for the monthly reports that shall include the implemented activities, challenges, and achievements, as well as the planned activities for the following month. Moreover, other tools will be used to monitor the project which may include narrative issues, challenges faced, and how to resolve them during the implementation's timescale. All these aspects shall be part of the lessons learned reports issued at the end of each phase. The M&E officer at the HQ will be in charge of the monthly reports. On the other hand, monthly narrative reports shall summarize the implemented activities, risks, and planned activities for the following month.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The area is considered to be relatively safe and secure comparing to other regions in Syria. The staff will receive awareness sessions regarding mine-risk education as the area is subjected to explosive remanents. WHOSD, for any project, conducts a risk analysis matrix, in which the staff is part of. GBV and PSEA are cross-cutting themes for all the humanitarian projects of WHOSD. Therefore, the staff of any project must receive training and raising awareness regarding the two issues. WHOSD is part of the PSEA network in NES. WHOSD integrates the PSEA and GBV into the staff contracts, and link it to clear M&E and accountability policy. WHOSD will develop an evacuation plan to respond to the scenarios of GoS or GoT advance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Recruitment activities: lack of skilled staff. The likelihood of risk (1-5): 3, impact (1-5): 4, level: 12. Mitigation: include transportation costs to the budget so we can hire staff from Ar Raqqa city and transport them on a daily basis to the site of implementation. - Procurement activities: the unstable exchange rate of USD to SYP. The likelihood of risk (1-5): 5, impact (1-5): 2. Mitigation: perform the financial procedures and contracts in the USD. - Procurement activities: Lack of expert logistics companies or suppliers who will implement the logistics preparations related to the project. The likelihood of the risk (1-5): 3, impact (1-5): 5. Mitigation: contract with suppliers in Erbil - Iraq if the cross-border was open.
