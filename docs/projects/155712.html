Project Summary

The project focuses on the urgent needs of women and girls affected by Boko Haram crisis in Far North region,particularly in the areas of protection and livelihood. Overall goal of the initiative is to ensure that women and girls lead, participate in, and are empowered by and benefit from humanitarian response and recovery efforts by meeting specific needs of women and girls as well as mainstreaming gender in humanitarian actions. It also contributes to the prevention and mitigation of violent extremism, addressing some of the key drivers of radicalization such as limited economic opportunities. It builds on the success of UN Women’s humanitarian work since 2014 in response to the refugee crisis from Central African Republic (CAR) in the East and the Boko Haram crisis in the Far North, much of which was supported by the Government of Japan (GoJ). These consecutive initiatives have improved the lives of thousands of women and girls by providing an integrated service package at women cohesion spaces created in the refugee camps.

This project consists of four outcome areas:
• Outcome 1: Women and girls in affected areas have increased protection against gender-based violence (GBV)
• Outcome 2: Women and girls have new sources of income and economic opportunities
• Outcome 3: Women’s leadership and engagement is increased in community mobilization, social cohesion and decision making
• Outcome 4: Gender analysis and assessments are reflected in multi-sectoral humanitarian crisis response planning and programming
The project is in line with the Cameroon Humanitarian Response Plan 2019

Needs

Cameroon has been plagued by triple crises in the recent years: 1) continuous attacks caused by the Boko Haram terrorist group in Far North region, 2) political instability in CAR resulting in refugee crisis in the East regions, and 3) recurrent environmental shocks such as flooding and drought that exacerbate food insecurity. The number of people requiring humanitarian aid in the country reaches 3.3 million, including 331,000 refugees and 398,099 internally displaced persons (IDPs). Women make up about 53% of this population. Moreover, 2018 witnesses the socio-political unrest in Northwest and Southwest regions (Anglophone regions) developed into an increasingly bloody separatist conflict which has forced 200,000 people to flee their homes, in addition to at least 21,000 who fled into Nigeria. These crises represent serious humanitarian crisis situations in rural areas with a multitude of existing developmental challenges. In response to the crises, humanitarian actions have been carried out by various actors. However, significant gaps remain in terms of gender equality and women’s empowerment in humanitarian response. - Increased needs for protection against gender-based violence (GBV) of various forms, including early marriage and sexual exploitation -Livelihood opportunities as critical life-saving needs for women -Women’s role in conflict management and prevention of violent extremism -Need to strengthen gender-responsive programming in the humanitarian response
