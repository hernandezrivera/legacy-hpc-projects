Project Summary

The project aims to target the most vulnerable IDPs in the camps and informal settlements with minimum WASH services within Afrin district. The project will provide the following activities in nine sites:
- Water supply: supplying water to the IDPs through water trucking with an average amount of 9072 m3/month
- Solid waste management: This will include the collection of solid waste from the nine sites on daily basis and send it away to the proper dumpsites.
- Emergency sanitation (desludging): desludging the septic tanks and Cesspits on regular basis
- HKs distribution: Distribution four rounds of HKs, (2,271 kits per round)

4 - Needs

According to OCHA situation reports; Despite the WASH Cluster’s efforts to phase out costly and short-term activities such as water trucking, 70 percent of IDPs in camps in northwest Syria continue to rely on WASH partners to provide trucked water on a daily basis. The Cluster continues to advocate for funding to support the connection of camps to existing piped water systems, which would dramatically improve the quantity and quality of lifesaving water services while substantially reducing costs.Also, as per HNO 2021, Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a real challenge for 1.75 million people living in IDP sites that are highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance (77% of interviewed residents of IDP sites received some kind of WASH humanitarian assistance, with 11% not being satisfied with it, mainly due to insufficient quantity or low quality of assistance). 69% of interviewed HHs living in IDP sites exclusively depend on water trucking services and often reported water insufficiency (20%) to meet daily household needs, 45% couldn’t access one or multiple hygiene items, 54% faced barriers to effective handwashing - key preventive measure against COVID-19 virus transmission, 26% of HHs reported issues with toilets functionality or wastewater disposal, while lack of access to bathing facilities was reported by 75% of IDP sites’ female headed HHs. According to the assessment conduct by Bahar for nine camps, it shows that there are needs to support these camps with water, SWM, sanitation and HKs. This project will benefit around 2271 HHs targeting 12096 IDPs taking in consideration the people with disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will benefit around 12,096 IDPs through improving the living condition and providing the life saving activities, the activities as follow: - Provision of safe water through trucking:Bahar provides water trucking to ensures that there are accountability and transparency in carrying out water trucking operations and to determine focal points which will be responsible for the operation. It is important to point out that water trucking is a short-term solution, in order to meet the needs, and is normally applicable to camps, informal settlements and collective centers. The water trucking modality is normally used as lifesaving intervention covering all the people in the camp. The water will be supplied to the targeted beneficiaries on daily basis with ratio 35 l/p/d increasing to 40 l/p/d in summer- SWM in IDPs sites:Bahar implementing solid waste activity on regular basis in several locations (generally in Camps).These activities are very important to maintain hygiene and prevent diseases and disasters that can increase among people when neglecting waste removal activity. The implementation will be through renting solid waste trucks with workers to collect the garbage and send it away to the landfill. - Desludging:Bahar provides the desludging service to ensures that there are accountability and transparency in carrying out desludging processes and to determine focal points which will be responsible for the operations. It is important to point out that the desludging is a short term solution, in order to meet the needs, and is normally applicable to camp management only. Note that it is used normally for life-saving interventions only.- Procurement and distribution of 2271 consumables HKs in 4 rounds:

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Emergency water supply: Movements of water trucks in congested, camp like settings , mitigation: Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators;-Emergency sanitation: Movements of desludging trucks in congested, camp like settings;Reliability of services., mitigation: Consult/engage community regarding desludging services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Establish clear routes and schedule for desludging trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training (include operational safety/PPE) with truck drivers/operators;-SWM: Movements of garbage trucks in congested, camp like settings; Unreliability of services. mitigation: Consult / engage community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from schoolHKs distribution: Sale or exchange of NFI kits at lesser value. mitigation: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Bahar will ensure the field team attend PSEA\safeguarding trainings and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal points responsibility is to ensure training are delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complains pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and that its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas Mitigation: ● In case of emergency situations, the plan is to be re-adjusted based on an assessment of the situation and accessibility. ● Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation. 2. Insufficient quantity and increase of fuel prices Mitigation: Regularly assesses the increase of fuel price in the market in the targeted areas and will accommodate the increase if its more than 15% than agreed to the contractors. 3. Sexual exploitation and abuse. Mitigation: ● PSEA principles developed and shared with all staff ● staff and personnel having direct contact with affected population are aware of PSEA requirement and in all interventions, consideration will be made to prevent PSEA. 4. Fraud, aid diversion and mishandling of supplies Mitigation: ● Beneficiaries complaint mechanism in place (Hotline, Complain boxes, staff members, field office, etc) ● MEAL team acts as focal point ● Beneficiaries are provided with communication methods through brochures, community sessions, etc.
