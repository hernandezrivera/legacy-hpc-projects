Project Summary

This 12-month project addresses critical gaps in hygiene and sanitation in the Rohingya refugee camps and host community through hygiene awareness campaigns with the distribution of post-delivery hygiene kits, breast pad, reusable katha's for babies, top inner wear, aprons, cleaning kits and for few identified gaps MHM kits. The project aims to foster community ownership by promoting good hygiene practices, as before distributing the items it's needed to have a sensitization and awareness session on why to use these items and how using these items will be beneficial. Women and men will actively participate in pre distribution hygiene awareness campaigns to ensure healthy new born baby and mother.
By empowering the community to take responsibility for sanitation and hygiene, the project promotes long-term behavioral change. The project will provide general cleaning kits and distribution of post delivery hygiene kits, breast pad, reusable katha's for babies, top inner wear, aprons, and cleaning kits to facilitate proper hygiene upkeep within households, contributing to overall health improvements in the camps.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Sanitation and hygiene management gaps remain significant in the camps, where poor hygiene practices and limited access to maternity hygiene materials continue to impact the well-being of new born baby and mother at camps and host community. Mothers and girls are disproportionately affected, noticed that there are some identified gaps to menstrual hygiene products, which impedes their mobility and participation in daily activities. Additionally, community engagement in maintaining hygiene is minimal, as cultural and logistical barriers often prevent new mother for a healthy baby. This project addresses these gaps by engaging individuals, where they will take an active role in participating hygiene awareness campaigns and hygiene promotion. Cash-for-work opportunities to produce all the distributing materials will further empower women to earn an income while contributing to public hygiene efforts, enhancing their economic resilience. Gender-sensitive approaches will ensure that post delivery hygiene kits, breast pad, reusable katha's for babies, top inner wear, aprons, and cleaning kits and training are tailored to the needs of women, girls, and PwD, ensuring inclusive access to essential resources and participation in sanitation effort

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project includes protection mainstreaming into activities, ensuring that both men and women are safe, supported, and empowered. Women's engagement is a cornerstone of the project, with women being actively recruited and trained as CfW. These volunteers, comprising at least 100% women, will play an essential role in hygiene awareness campaigns, and hygiene sensitization. This not only promotes gender equality but also ensures that women, often the most affected by poor sanitation after delivery, are at the forefront of addressing these challenges in their communities. Community participation is central to the project’s waste collection approach. Community-led hygiene campaigns, where a significant number of residents will be directly involved in post delivery hygiene kits using. To create a safe and supportive environment, all project activities will be designed to mitigate risks of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). For instance, female volunteers will receive GBV awareness training, and the project will implement strict codes of conduct for all staff and volunteers. All participants will have access to ACTED’s Accountability and Feedback Mechanism (AFM) to report any incidents of harassment or exploitation, ensuring that any concerns are addressed quickly and transparently.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project is committed to inclusion of people with disabilities (PwD) and individuals of 12 above age groups in both hygiene awareness and post delivery hygiene kits distribution activities. Post delivery hygiene kits distribution production will include at least 5% PwD participants, ensuring their active role and efforts. All awareness and sensitization materials will be adapted to cater to the needs of PwD and various age groups, including the elderly and children. The distribution points and hygiene awareness sessions will be made accessible to PwD, and additional support will be provided for vulnerable individuals, including elderly participants and pregnant women, to ensure equal participation.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project integrates a robust Accountability and Feedback Mechanism (AFM) that ensures direct input from camp residents, including marginalized groups, on project activities. This system enables affected populations to provide feedback, raise concerns, and report issues in a confidential and secure manner. AFM mechanisms, including community feedback desks, will be established at distribution points and during waste management activities, ensuring transparency in all processes. Additionally, camp residents will be regularly consulted on their satisfaction with the hygiene services provided, enabling continuous improvement based on community needs.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

All staff, volunteers, and beneficiaries engaged in the project, particularly in the hygiene awareness campaigns, will undergo PSEA training to ensure awareness and understanding of their responsibilities in preventing sexual exploitation and abuse. 4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by engaging them in cash-for-work programs through Waste Management Brigades. These opportunities provide income generation for vulnerable camp residents, allowing them to support their families and contribute to the long-term sustainability of sanitation and hygiene efforts in the camps. Training in waste collection and management builds skills that can be transferred to other livelihood activities beyond the camps.
