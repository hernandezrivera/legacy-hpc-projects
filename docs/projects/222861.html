Project Summary

HI is building on over 14 years of extensive support for the most vulnerable people among refugees and host community population living in Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts by addressing their urgent acute and chronic health needs. The main objective of this JRP project 2025 is to ensure quality and timely access of people with disabilities and impairments and elderly people to comprehensive functional rehabilitation, MHPSS and health care services etc. This JRP project will be directly implemented by HI and by its two operational partners Center for Disability in Development (CDD) and Social Assistance and Rehabilitation for the Physically Vulnerable (SARPV) as well by funding support from BPRM, DFAT, GFFO, FCDO and ECHO. Disability identification, Specialized services provision, referral support, community awareness and capacity building of relevant actors are the major broader activities will be undertaken under this JRP project in 2025.
As per the nature of HI interventions, four activity groups are selected for 2025 under health objectives 1 & 3 to frame this project. The selected activity group-wise descriptions are given below-
Rehabilitation, Physiotherapy Center
There are three major interventions designed under this activity group- 1) disability identification and individual need assessment through door-to-door survey; 2) disability specialized one stop service provision and 3) reasonable accommodation and accessibility work. The disability identification and need assessment will be conducted at 33 camps in Teknaf and Ukhiya. This includes provision of eye and hearing and P&O (prosthesis and orthosis) services through organizing mobile campaigns in all camps. In addition, the proposed specialized services (such as rehab, MHPSS and SRH) provision will be continued in the same camps as done under JRP 2024 with including one more camp (camp 19) as the necessary services do not exist there. To provide the service for the host community people, HI supports 4 health facilities in Teknaf and Ukhiya that include Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila health complex, KTP MSF-OCA-331 and IOM Leda-200 with physiotherapists providing rehabilitation services. HI will also provide stimulation therapy with collaboration with Nutrition sector.
To meet the diverse needs of the beneficiaries, the project employs various service delivery methods that include centre-based services at static points and health clinics, and home/community-based services through mobile vans. HI deploys multisectoral qualified technical teams consisting of Physiotherapists, Occupational Therapists, MHPSS officers, skilled nurses, midwives and social workers to provide the necessary services in timely and quality manner.
Referral services
HI team is working closely with the relevant actors to ensure existing services at field which support are not available in the project. For that, HI team refers to the existing services with ensuring that the services are received by the beneficiaries.
AAP mainstreaming
CFRM (compliant and feedback response mechanism) is in place by which Beneficiaries' complaints and feedback received and take necessary actions for solution
In addition, PSEA focal are assigned at each site offices who ensure PSEA principles are mainstreamed through all activities through extensive training of all staff and volunteers and ensure different IEC materials at field.
Community Health Education, mobilization, Behaviour Change
Under this activity group, community awareness raising activities are organized at field on different important topics under rehab and MHPSS. Capacity building of Health staffs are also included in the group on ensure disability identification and referral of persons with disabilities and impairment.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to recent ISNA-2024, 12% population are living wit disabilities in Rohingya refugee camps and 79.21% Persons with Disabilities experience challenges in accessing humanitarian assistance. Among them, 4.58% have visual impairments, 7.19% have physical disabilities, and 8.39% have mental and intellectual disabilities. As per REACH age & disability assessment study (May 2021) and, the prevalence of persons with disabilities was 12% among refugees and ranged from 6% to 19% based on the camp and the presence of mobility barriers. In shelters and around camps, 52% and 76% of persons with disabilities reported struggling with mobility issues and 51% of people had unmet needs in terms of assistive products. There are 89-98% of individuals who have functional limitations in different domains that still have unmet needs. In the October 2023 update, the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) revealed that 8% of individuals within host communities and 15% of households among refugees included at least one member with a disability. According to the National Survey on Persons with Disabilities (NSPD) conducted in 2021, approximately 7.14% of the population in Bangladesh faces functional difficulties across various domains. This percentage demonstrates a slightly higher prevalence among males (7.27%) than among females (7.00%). As of the GoB-UNHCR Factsheet from September 30, 2024, there were a total of 1,003,394 individuals recorded, with 52% being children, 44% adults, and 4% older persons, while women and men made up 51% and 49% of the refugee population, respectively.In Bangladesh, Rohingya refugees have no legal status, denying them the rights associated with refugee status and income generation needed to fulfil their basic needs. Thus, Rohingya refugees remain one of Bangladesh's most vulnerable groups, and rely heavily on humanitarian aid. Women in Rohingya communities typically face a variety of protection concerns and barriers to services, which make them particularly vulnerable to human rights violations and negative coping mechanisms.Limited access to quality SRH services during crises heightens risks of morbidity and mortality for women and adolescent girls, necessitating urgent focus on service design and delivery. Rohingya refugees face severe mental health issues, including depression and psychosocial distress due to persecution, violence, and forced displacement from Myanmar, worsened by poor living conditions and joblessness. Older adults and persons with disabilities are especially vulnerable to violence and neglect, hindered by stigma, communication, and environmental barriers, which increases their protection risks and limits their ability to report abuse.The absence of specialized services such as rehabilitation, assistive devices, and medication increases the risk of exclusion (as per REACH study of 2021, 56% expressed the desire for these devices but lacked support). In addition, the influx of humanitarian support has concentrated essential services like health and protection services in camp areas the camp fencing creates additional barriers for services to reach the host community having to walk an hour or more to receive health services. According to HI internal assessment, 12% of the population exhibits functional limitations. Among them, 57% are adults (18-59), and 16% are elderly (60+). In the host community alarmingly, 79.2% of persons with disabilities lack access to rehabilitation services. These findings underscore a substantial and urgent need for increased availability of rehabilitation services. In regards to the above-mentioned needs, HI has begun to expand services into host communities and in the 28 refugee camps to provide dynamic and multi-sector services at a household and center based to the most vulnerable households there.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The program incorporates protection principles throughout all the phases including design and inception phases. In doing so, the program systematically takes into consideration protection risks and potential violations. Starting from the design phase, HI has carried out protection needs assessment through community consultations, analysis of data from ongoing projects, and analysis of specific feedback received through accountability mechanisms. Analysis has given special attention to age, gender and disability in order to analyze protection risks specific to different community members. Consultation and dialogue with communities, including women and girls with and without disabilities have been ongoing and will continue throughout continued implementation to promote active participation of affected populations.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The organization has Gender, Disability and Age (DGA) Institutional Policy. HI’s “Age, Gender, and Disability Policy”, whose guiding principles are participation, equality and non-discrimination, accessibility and protection, inform all aspects of project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. The policy supports the development and implementation of transformative gender-sensitive projects, where age and disability are fully considered. HI has in-built human resources and technical tools dedicated to identifying the specific needs and capacities of people from different groups. HI strongly follow the DGA Policy Guidelines and DGA Marker in every project.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Prior to service delivery, HI will conduct individual assessments, protecting each beneficiaries’ rights to confidentiality, in consultation with beneficiaries and caregivers. Informed consent will be obtained before service delivery or referral, and beneficiaries may refuse HI services at any time. CFRMs enable teams to respond effectively to program participants' complaints and feedback in a timely manner by establishing two-way communication channels. The two-way communication mechanism is designed specifically to gather and respond to feedback from program participants and other community stakeholders. This mechanism includes accessible complaint boxes in static points, a hotline, an email address, and contact of Country PSEA focal points with phone number. Information related to the CFRM is shared during individual sessions and community awareness sessions and posters are displayed in static points and distributed at household level including local language.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Recognizing the lack of consideration of persons with disabilities in existing disaster risk reduction (DRR) service planning and implementation, the program will deliver specific activities to improve DRR stakeholders’ understanding of barriers limiting access to DRR information as well as facilitators for ensuring persons with disabilities are included in all disaster response programs. Technical support will be provided to Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) working group members. Accessibility works will include removal of environmental barriers currently present in evacuation routes and safety shelters. Awareness sessions will be delivered to persons with disabilities and their family members to ensure they are empowered with knowledge of local emergency response plans, evacuation routes, and safety shelters.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

HI formed and are mobilizing the EPR team at base level to provide emergency support to beneficiaries in emergencies. HI maintains contingency stock to support in emergencies. HI also provide training and on the job support to CPP and PERU volunteers to support person with disabilities and elderly persons in emergencies. HI’s static points are also used as safe shelter during cyclone emergencies. Psychosocial first aid, assistive devices, hygiene kits, and contingency stocks of dry food and safe water are planned to provide if required.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, HI engages youth as community volunteers recruited from the same locality who are responsible to organize and faciliate awareness sessions, assist HI staff to provide services and its follow up. They sometimes organize inclusive sports and recreational events for children with and without disabilities at community level. Before the engagement of these volunteers, they are well trained on the topics by the technical staff of HI.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, HI’s project promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugees through several initiatives. Caregivers receive hands on training to better support their child and person with disabilities, while local volunteers are engaged to foster community involvement. We also trained community volunteers on different health topics for their effective engagement with the project. This knowledge will remain in the community as they are from the same locality. We ensure accessibility by making reasonable accommodations and locally producing assistive devices with available materials through deploying trained local artisan which skills will also remain in the community to continue support. Additionally, we also provide technical support and guidance of local actors, Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs), disability inclusion promotion groups (DIPG) and local partners to sustain and localize these efforts.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, HI’s project activities in the host community align with Cox’s Bazar district development priorities by supporting inclusive, sustainable growth. We focus on capacity building of local actors and provide technical support to health sub-sectors, advocating at Cox’s Bazar level on disability inclusion. We promote the inclusion of disability data in the UNHCR progress data sheet, provide rehabilitation services at district and upazila hospitals, and develop partnerships with local and national actors to enhance localization, inclusion, and accessibility.
