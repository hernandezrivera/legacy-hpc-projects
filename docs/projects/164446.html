Project Summary

The intervention contributes to the reduction of morbidity and mortality, and improve overall health services for conflict-affected Syrians and IDPs. It will also focus mainly on the provision and improvement of primary health care services, secondary health care, specialized and immunization services.
The scope of QC 2020 is to support the core health services represented by the primary health care services (the plan is to support 22 PHCs so far in line with the essential health package, these centers are distributed between (Aleppo, Idlib, Tell Abiad, and Ras Al Ain). Currently supported centers with Response ID are: 22879, 22892, 22907, 22910 and 22915.
Immunization centers will cover many areas in (Aleppo, Idlib, Tell Abiad, and Ras Al Ain). There will be four specialized hospitals (two surgical and two gynecological and pediatric) operating as follows: 2 surgical and 1 women's hospital in Aleppo, 1 women's hospital in Idleb.
All projects are planned through self-implementation by QC field teams in targeted areas; partners may be involved only in exceptional circumstances and will be decided how and when required.
So far $2M USD is secured from different sources. This fund of $2M will be utilized for the procurement of drugs and consumables AND for fully supporting 4 PHCs (salaries, operations, drugs, and consumables) for one year. The project target is 940,000 populations over 12 months.

4 - Needs

The scale, severity, and complexity of the humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive due to continued hostilities in localized areas and new and protracted displacement. Since 1 December 2019, nearly 390,000 people have fled from their homes, mostly escaping hostilities by moving to urban centers and IDP camps in northwest Idleb; with an estimated 400,000 people displaced in northwest Syria between May and August 2019 and nearly 390,000 people displaced in northern Syria since December 2019, (OCHA)Accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for persons with disabilities among beneficiaries who received assistance. However, 86%percent of surveyed households who sought health services in the previous three months reported having to pay for care most often for medication. The availability of medicines and treatment costs are also leading determinants of child mortality in Syria(HNO).QC intervention and because of its comprehensive nature will target the whole community groups including (Women, girls, men, and boys) with different ages. The assessment conducted by QC through contacts with local health directorate, communities, and other stakeholders indicated the need for continuity of the support to health facilities by staffing, provision of medical supplies and equipment and improvement of the facilities' WASH systems and covering the running costs. Need assessment conducted in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates for QC health projects indicated that drugs is a priority in 68.8% of total facilities followed by medical equipment with around 18.8% while medical consumables come at least with around 12.5% of the total. The inadequacy of health services in Northern Syria exposes women to risks of morbidity and mortality due to lack of maternal health services, including the ANC, safe delivery and BEmOC services. The assessment targeted the whole community of different ages.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will focus on implementing the following activities:1- Increasing access to primary health care through the provision ofthe Essential Package of Primary Health Services, including reproductive health services and child health care ( boys and girls) 2- Rehabilitation and reinforcing health facilities, including physical structure, equipment/supplies to provide safe and secure environments for health service delivery for men, women, boys, and girls.3- Procurement and supply of medicines, consumables and medical equipment for trauma, obstetric care for women, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions and strengthen the medical supply chains, including emergency supply stocks at districts warehouses and health facility levels. 4- Increasing access to treatment for people who live with (NCD)5- Training of staff on key essential services such as management and primary and secondary prevention of non-communicable diseases, treatment of NCDs, basic surgical and obstetric care, task shifting of basic services to staff to provide care in their home communities when mobility restrictions or health facility are found. 6- Cover the cost of salaries of medical staff and provide incentives to attract and retain qualified medical staff from both sexes 7- Support Health information systems (HIS) at the health facility levels to track morbidity and mortality data 8- Provide running costs of the hospitals and health centers9- Monitoring and evaluation of the health program activities where QC has a MEAL system in Gaziantep and inside Syria to monitor and evaluate the planned activities. In addition to the health facility-based information that will be collected as part of the HIS, QC will organize regular submission of 4Ws reports to a cluster10- Monitoring and evaluation of project activities will take special consideration to seek feedback from the beneficiaries and other stakeholders. QC will report to the cluster at the Gaziantep level on the implementation

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The Whole of Syria (WoS) Health Cluster conducted a review to identify the most pressing health-related protection risks facing health actors and the affected population and suggested a set of mitigating measures. The document has been updated for the 2020 Humanitarian Program Cycle to guide donors, health agencies and operational partners during the process of program design and implementation.Risk: Attacks on health facilities and health care staff: Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA)1 during the period of January - October 2019 documented 73 attacks affecting 52 health facilities.The attacks resulted in 52 fatalities (of which 11 health care providers and 3 patients), and 96 injuries (of which 22 health care providers and 19 patients). Mitigating measures: While neither the health cluster nor humanitarian actors can guarantee protection from attacks on health care, health actors are encouraged to avail themselves of mechanisms for visibility, advocacy and accountability as follows:Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities.Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks.Risk of Gender Based Violence GBV. Mitigating measures: Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Specialized health staff will be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Risk of lack of Duty of care. Mitigating measures: implement staff careprograms which provide psycho-social support services and resources to staff. Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (floods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on the implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happens in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff . In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm/risk. *These can be mitigated by: putting in place task-shifting guidelines as part of care protocols - providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency - decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care. Risk of Increased morbidity and mortality rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. * Mitigating measure: - Hire a qualified medical staff - Distribute medical supplies and consumables. - Choose a safe hospital (underground, fortified). After Sushi treaty between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in north-west Syria will be stable for the whole of next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases. * Mitigating measure: - Expand and strengthen the available health services to meet the needs of new IDPs. Add mobile clinics to reach more people. More coordination with health actors on the ground to maximize use of the available resources to meet the increasing needs.
