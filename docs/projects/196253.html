Project Summary

OXFAM will contribute to WASH sectors strategic priorities for 2023-25 to improve and sustained inclusive & integrated WASH services to Refugees and host community (HC). This will be achieved through a) technical support to WASH sector on strategy and technical gaps, and b) by implementing sustainable O&M and quality WASH service systems and supporting localization & capacity building.
Thematic focus for JRP 2023, Oxfam will work on sustainability and quality of solid waste management (SWM) systems, faecal sludge management (FSM) and sanitation service chain, contribute to coordinated efforts to mitigate water insecurity in Teknaf host community.
Through its implementing partner (IP) DSK & NGOF, Oxfam coordinated with relevant AFAs (UNICEF & UNHCR) and WASH sector systems proposing to cover ;
1.sanitation, bathing, SWM and hygiene needs in Block D camp 22 and adjacent host community with efforts improving sustainability, quality and inclusions with strong & empowered community engagement.
2.Covering FSM gaps for C26, 27, NYPRC and adjacent host community. UNHCR have leased a non-forest land near camp 26 (Eastern side of UKH-TEK Highway).in 2022, Oxfam supported the design of 2 modules 60m3/each=120m3/day capacity FSTP3 to cover Teknaf camps and host community. OXFAM with its partner NGOF will construct the third centralized faecal sludge treatment plant (FSTP) with treatment capacity of 180m3 per day to serve camp 26,27 and Nayapara TC & nearby HC ( Nayapara , Jadimura) and whykong, baharachara union of Teknaf. This plant will support improving sanitary condition of onsite latrines and environment of the HC where faecal sludge management chain is restricted to only containment till date.
3.Improve and sustainable SWM solutions for Teknaf HC. Construction of MRF facility for Whykong and baharchara union , distribution of colour coded bins for HH and shops, strengthening climate/environment resilience concept through community engagement , engaging gender balanced SWM collector team ; all proposed activity will support creating a sustainable and resilient waste management system opting the value recovery from compost , recyclables & enabling market committees , WATSAN Committees in close coordination with UP chairman’s / Govt. authorities for activating monthly fee collection system.
4.Upgrading surface water sources and rain water harvesting (RWH) systems , surface water treatment PSF (pond sand filter) and established solarized mini piped water supply systems in Whykong and Hnila, setting up sustainable O&M.
5.Oxfam’s hygiene-behavior promotion strategy will focus on strengthening community-centered actions for sustained behavior change, optimal use and self-maintenance of WASH infrastructures ensuring women and girls, old age, people with incontinence and people with disabilities participate and influence decisions to the WASH services. The project will reinforce overarching community engagement, gender-responsive, inclusion, market and evidenced-based pilot studies for incontinence, eco-sustainable solutions, to actively reduce the harmful effects of gender norms, roles and relations and gender inequality in the WASH interventions. The key activities include rationalizing CBVs moving towards strengthening WASH user groups, using participatory behavior change based on communicable diseases and environmental risk-based solution-oriented actions, Community Perception Tracker, capacity building and distribution of critical hygiene supplies on soap, water containers, waste bins, menstrual hygiene management and incontinence items. In-depth studies will focus on building evidence for pilot interventions on women-produced ecological sanitary pads, incontinence response elements and community-preferred hygienic eco-sustained water containers.
Contingency budget for cholera outbreaks has been proposed for maintaining rapid mobilization capacity for intensive WASH interventions– supplies covers by AFA.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In addition to JMSNA- In Whykong , Hnila and Teknaf host community, water crisis is identified as pressing need. Pollution from solid waste and contamination from effluents & waste water put SWM & FSM in 2nd priority ranks. Quality, equality and inclusion of the services and its sustainable O&M across the response area. In Whykong , Hnila - water crisis and menstrual hygiene support for adolescent girls were identified as gaps. 47% of girls and women reported irritation and itching during their period; another research study found that 87% of women and girls reported using towels and pieces of worn out fabric or clothes or leaves to absorb menstrual blood during their period. Camp 26, 27 and Nayapara RC is covered by 9 small scale faecal sludge treatment plant (FSTP), quality of effluent releasing to environment doesn’t meet the Department of Environment (DoE) standard as per DPHE lab test round 1 and 2, also same evidential from OXFAM–UNHCR and ICDDRB waste water monitoring surveillance & WASH sector monitoring overview. The Faecal sludge collection and treatment systems are absent in Whykong, Baharchara and adjacent host community of Teknaf camps, area heavily dependents on surface water sources, which has higher exposure to faecal contamination compared to ground water-based water supply. The proposed centralized faecal sludge treatment plant will support community for ensuring safe sanitation and reduce pollution & contamination risks to natural water bodies and environment. Despite significant gains in handwashing practice at the peak of covid 19 pandemic & fear, the functions, use, and practices decline or couldn’t sustained after covid situation trending down stabilization. Findings from Oxfam Community Perception Tracker and monitoring in camps show that households living in hilly areas have limited access to water; barriers to use of latrines at night because of fear due to damage and lack of solar lighting; no locks in latrines and bathhouses; preference of women and girls to use an aluminium pitcher for collection and storage of water as it is handy, easy to clean, lasting and eco-friendly; and for menstrual hygiene pads – the need for easy-to-wash/dry, less use of water, eco-friendly and anti-bacterial sanitary pads are expressed by adolescent girls and women. Communities also expressed the huge need for mosquito nets due to the discomfort of mosquito bites at night, especially during the monsoon. UNDP withdrawn from teknaf host community SWM project Jun’22 created gaps in continuation to a transition to sustainable operating model integrating with Union council SWM system. the potential of producing and marketing compost products from organic waste is still absent in Whykong and Hnila union. In camp 22, one MRF facility is operational but capacity is not enough to support HC waste stream . Considering the 0.13 kg / cap / day waste generation rate and improving collection efficiency from current 40% in camp will require additional MRF's for camp and HC for achieving efficient collection and treatment of solid waste.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Oxfam WASH strategy for Rohingya Response is in built with transition, localisation, capacity building and crisis resilience. Oxfam is committed to support WASH sector JRP 2023 and onward sector strategy that include rationalisation. The propose JRP project has been consulted with AFA, for Teknaf host community UNICEF and Oxfam is proposing one IP NGOF, for Camp 22- DSK is the Oxfam & UNICEF IP. Therefore, integrated approach adapted in both camp and HC proposition. As the current humanitarian context transition towards sustainable solutions and cost-effective operations in the midst of a protracted crisis, the WASH project will reinforce community engagement strategies that are beyond ‘business-as-usual’ approaches and focus on changing targeted high-risk behaviours and perceptions through participatory approaches and capacity building particularly on safe water usage, solid waste containment reducing waste entering the surrounding environment, use ecological hygiene items, and water conservation. Through increased cross-sector linkages, the rationalization of Community-health volunteers, hygiene items with support to the market system, finding ecological solutions and pilot studies for evidenced based interventions will be applied in the project. In host community, priority will be given to sensitize WATSAN committee, market committee, chairman/ members to enable fee collection system for waste management, producing and selling compost to make the waste management sustainable. Camp level coordination with CIC, CFP, SMSD and others sectors will be strengthened and for host community attending regular WASH sector meetings, updating UNO , DPHE SAE / Ex. En about the progress and other coordination mechanism will be reinforced. Oxfam has been transitioning with capacity and knowledge share. It will continue enabling national capacity design and construction of sustainable WASH infrastructure and sustainable O&M in collaboration with Sector.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

By ensuring people’s safety, dignity and supporting affected populations to access and enjoy their rights, beneficiaries will safely access WASH services, without causing harm, have meaningful access, participation and accountability. The project will reinforce regular Protection monitoring, Community Perception Trackers, establishing mechanisms such as Listening groups, consultations, referrals, feedback gathering, and training staff to strengthen participation and empowerment.

3.2 - Gender

In the Rohingya camps in Bangladesh, women of reproductive age represent around the 31% of the whole refugee population. Using 50% SWM waste collector .. what other gender facts do we have .... The WASH project is designed to involve and reach gender-responsive demography such as male, female, people with disabilities, old age, persons with incontinence and other female-related service deprivation as a result of discriminating norms and service policies. This will be informed by a gender analysis to identify their specific needs and involvement in decisions that impact their rights to access WASH and other services. Inclusive consultations in gender and age-disaggregated groups will address issues around accessibility, appropriateness, and safety, encourage maximum participation, respect cultural norms, and change the discriminatory norms and service policies that deprive their rights.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project ensures access to appropriate WASH services integrating child protection (persons who are under 18 years of age) by responding to their specific needs and prevent risks they face against all forms of discrimination, abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation. Staff and workers are trained to be aware, report and refer identified children at risk to existing community-level child protection systems for services.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will contribute to diversity and inclusion within the refugees and host communities. The approach is informed by a stakeholder analysis to understand the community stakeholders and leaders with diverse backgrounds, profiles, power dynamics, and their experiences in prioritizing decisions and actions pertaining to WASH services delivery. The project will engage with a diverse range of people around the leadership, and effective decision-making would require that everyone is able to speak and have their views heard. Reinforcing gender equality and equal opportunities in leadership with a specific focus on women leadership in the entire project will be ensured.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Through assesments, continued consultations, feedback mechanism, Listening Groups and community perception tracking, the views, self-identified needs and priorities of Rohingya refugees and host community members will be heard and addressed in the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Oxfam employs the Social Architecture approach to integrate the DRR/DRM elements through the community playing the architect role in designing resilient WASH infrastructures informed by their experience from the recurring disasters. The community empowerment is fueled by the active participation of women in households being involved in decision making under this approach. The community's responsibility is embedded in building resilient WASH infrastructures, including the development of preparedness plans and mitigation measures against the impacts of monsoon season, cyclone, drought and AWD season. The infrastructures are sustained by using locally available materials and the community being responsible on its long-term repair maintenance including addressing the post disaster impacts.
