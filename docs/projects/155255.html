Project Summary

The ISCG identified GBV support services as a critical need, highlighting safety and security concerns for women and girls, as the sites are overcrowded without proper planning, lighting, privacy, availability of dignity and hygiene materials for basic dignity, safety and mobility. According to the November 2017 ACAPS report, an estimated 448,000 people are in need of GBV support with widespread reports of the use of sexual violence against women and girls while in transit. Women and girls are at risk of trafficking, domestic violence, assault, or other abuse. As of 12 November, over 2,000 GBV cases have been reported. While the exact number of women experiencing sexual violence is not known, it is believed to be high as women and girls have indicated being limited in their movement due to a lack of culturally appropriate clothing, a fear of harassment, and a fear of getting lost. Given the widespread reports on the use of sexual violence in the conflict, ongoing risk of GBV in the target sites and the lack of available services, there is a critical need for GBV prevention and response services for survivors. Rohingyas have conservative social and cultural norms, creating additional barriers on women’s empowerment. Women experience barriers to freedom and access to, and control over resources, with girls’ access and mobility restricted once they reach puberty. Women’s mobility is also restricted by the observance of purdah - the covering of themselves and removing themselves from public space, is limiting their ability to access aid or GBV services - a problem compounded by the stigma faced by GBV survivors and the limited information to which women have access. Lack of sufficient lighting at night in the camps exposes women to risks of attack, and limited consideration of gender sensitive services (e.g. gender segregated WASH facilities; shelters with lockable doors etc.) put women at further risk. Seventy seven percent of women and girls located in the 28 identified settlement sites across Cox’s Bazar District reported feeling unsafe. The lack of space for community structures also limits the ability of humanitarian actors to provide protection services, including community centres, child-friendly spaces (CFS) and safe spaces for women and girls.

Activities or outputs

"Essential GBV services will be provided to women and adolescent girls in the safe spaces, encompassing both group and individual engagement, founded on women, girl and survivor centered care practices, based on international standards on GBV prevention and response. The safe spaces will be staffed by women to promote independent spaces for women and adolescent girls, ensuring comfort in accessing life-saving medical and PSS and risk reduction services. The safe spaces will also allow community spaces where women and adolescent girls can comfortably interact, build support networks and seek services. With integration of basic SRH services, the safe spaces, called Integrated Women’s Centers (IWCs), will offer the following services through (5) Five IWCs (Prioritizing camp 6,17,19, 20,22) encompassing both group and individual engagement, founded on women-, girl- and survivor-centreed care practices, based on international standards on GBV prevention and response. These services will include GBV case management (Including case managment for child surviviors and adolescent girls; Crisis counselling, psychosocial support and mental health care; Risk reduction, safety planning and information provision, emotional support groups and adolescent girls gender transformative and life skills cirricliums; Community outreach and engagement. As part of the emergency repsonse and preparedness, IRC does plan to maintain a small stock of dginity kits to be provided to women and adolescent girls of reproductive age. These kits have been based off of the GBV SS Guidelines on Dignity kits, and have been modified slightly based on consultations with women and girls. Additionally IRC is exploring the most appropraite approach to gather compliants and feedback from women and girls accessing IRC services. Recognizing constraints around literacy, new appropached will be piloted to ensure timely collection of complaints and feedback are taken into account with clear feedback from IRC back to the client are established. IRC will also look at client responsiveness models, incuding client satisfaction on case management services. Moreover, IRC will seek to avoid harm with all interventions, and therefore will strive to enure all activities are designed in consultation with commuities and that they are regularly seeking approachess. To ensure maximum reach, IRC s coordinating internally with Child Protection, Protection, health and SRH teams, as well as other sectors, such as Sie Management or other reevant sectors especially when protection risks are identifying through risk mitigation work with women and girls around WASH, Shelter and site management. Finally IRC is a member of the GBV SS steering committee and actively particpates at cooridnation on the national level and has representaion at the field level coordination with protection/GBV focal points, CIC and Protection meetings in the relavant camps. IRC will build off of referral pathwys and seek opportunities to strengthen referral pathways through identification and referrals, as well as rolling out GBV Core Concepts trainings at various levels to improve quality. "
