Project Summary

In rural areas of Donetsk and Luhansk region (GCA), particularly in the Buffer Zone close to the contact line, a large number of internally displaced people and conflict-affected local residents experience food insecurity. Due to loss of livelihoods, higher unemployment rates, rising prices, and disruption of access to markets, food security is a year-round challenge.
The aim of the project is emergency strengthening the food security and resilience of most vulnerable IDPs and other conflict-affected households from Donbas through the provision of agricultural grants and reviving the essential agricultural assets.
To improve food security and livelihood to population of concern in rural area Caritas Ukraine will provide multi-components support included:
- cash grants to vulnerable households (HH) to start or expand small-scale agricultural activities, including fruit and vegetable cultivation and animal husbandry
- technical information, counselling, trainings as an ongoing support for grant beneficiaries and other stakeholders. Acquiring knowledge allow beneficiaries to effectively utilize resources and maximize production.

Needs

Up to 3.5 million people have been affected by the conflict . Of these, up to 1.1 million people (including IDPs outside of Donbas) are food insecure and in need of food security interventions, agriculture inputs and Livelihoods support. Particularly the needs for agricultural type of assistance in rural villages along the line of conflict are high. Around 21,000 rural households residing within 20 km from the contact line in GCA that rely on self-production of their food are in need of agricultural inputs. In areas where fighting has been reduced, support of small-scale agriculture has been shown to be effective at renewing livelihoods for vulnerable households, particularly women who are unable to access other forms of employment due to lack of mobility or caretaking responsibilities. Results of past interventions have shown that household agricultural production both reduces expenses related to food consumption and provides an additional source of income from selling surplus production, which can be used to help meet other household needs. For households in rural areas, subsistence agriculture is an important activity to support food security. Despite rural households experiencing lower income levels, and having greater difficulties reaching markets, they have considerably higher levels of access to arable land, and therefore greater potential capacity to mitigate food security shocks through agriculture. Especially food safety through agro activities is extremely necessary for - Older people who have not yet reached pension age and may not be entitled to humanitarian assistance as they do not fall under traditional vulnerability criteria; - Unemployed adults with one child, who may also not fall into a traditional vulnerability criteria The main difficulties faced by subsistence people are lacking money for required inputs (seeds, fertilizers, fodder for animals), insecurity and mines, damaged infrastructure (greenhouses, irrigation) and cost of accessing markets (checkpoints, road conditions, costs of transport, distance)An additional challenge faced by people is the lack of technical assistance and extension services. As a result of the war, the region’s agricultural academy was relocated to Kharkiv, resulting in the Luhansk Province Agricultural Extension Service becoming inactive. Based on the experiences of supporting rural livelihoods, improved extension services and training for beneficiaries have been identified as a need by organizations including FAO, Mercy Corps, and PIN, particularly for urban IDPs that have relocated to rural areas and have less experience engaging in agricultural activities. The lack of extension services could provide a challenge for IDPs who have relocated from urban to rural areas, as they may be less experienced with household gardening of livestock rearing.Over the agro project servey 37% of beneficiaries who received agro grants from Caritas participated in on agriculture and life-skills trainings, 97% of all beneficiaries who participated in the training noticed they had gained new knowledge. Which indicates a lack of knowledge among the population.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Emergency strengthening the food security and resilience of most vulnerable IDPs and other conflict-affected households from Donbas through the provision of agricultural grants and reviving the essential agricultural assets.IR 1: Vulnerable IDP and other conflict-affected households use cash grants to recover and to strengthen small-scale agricultural livelihoods activities. Output 1.1: Vulnerable households receive cash grants to resume or expand small-scale agricultural livelihoods activities. • Households will apply for agriculture livelihoods grants. CUA staff will be available in-person and over telephone to provide clarification of what information to include in each section of the application. Implementation: Jan – April 2019• Selection criteria is advertised and publicized.Implementation: Jan – March 2019• The selection process will include:- Evaluation of written applications (available on paper and digitally). Implementation: Feb-April 2019- HH visits to confirm vulnerability and evaluate the potential for success of the applicant. Implementation: March-June 2019• Average grant value is 550 USD. The value of grants will be based on the type and scale of proposed activities, number of family members, children, elder people and will range from 300-1000 USD. * Grant modality is cash transferred directly to beneficiary bank accounts, which are common for all target households and the means by which HHs receive government social benefits. Banks are locally available in, or close to, target locations.IR 2: Vulnerable IDP and other conflict-affected households utilize new knowledge and techniques in their small-scale agricultural livelihoods activities. Output 2.1: Vulnerable households receive written technical information on agricultural techniques, technologies and strategies. • CUA agriculture experts or outside consultants will develop written materials with best practices for the most common types of household agriculture activities.Implementation: June-Sept 2019• Printed materials will be distributed to grant recipients, training participants, as well as to local government offices and agriculture officials. Technical materials will also be available online for beneficiaries to read and/or download. - Manuals on Raising broiler chickens, and Cultivating vegetables, have already been produced.Implementation: March-November 2019Output 2.2: Vulnerable households receive individual consultations from Caritas agriculture experts • Two project staff members have agricultural experience and expertise. These staff members will:- Advise beneficiaries during the application process, and provide input on beneficiary selection.- Be available to beneficiaries via phone for remote consultations throughout the period of the project.- Make HH visits when available to provide necessary assistance to beneficiaries.Implemenation: Feb-November 2019Output 2.3: Vulnerable households participate in group trainings conducted to gain new life-skills, knowledge and techniques for fortify small-scale agricultural livelihoods activities. • Throughout the project period, CUA and external agriculture experts will provide 20 group trainings in target communities to provide information about best practices and new agriculture techniquesImplemenation: July-November 2019• Topics will be selected and presented according to the agriculture calendar, to prepare grant beneficiaries and other conflict-affected people for strategic moments in the agriculture cycle• Training topics will be selected to target the most common agriculture activities in each region.• CUA project staff will take into account beneficiary requests and challenges observed by agriculture experts during monitoring visits. CUA will seek to coordinate with other implementing organization to combine beneficiaries and maximize the efficiency of any trainings provided. Implemenation: Jan-November2019
