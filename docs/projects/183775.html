Project Summary

Under the HRP 2022, ZOA is planning a multi-sectoral response (WASH response) in 13 localities in North Darfur, South Darfur, East Darfur and Gedaref states to respond to critical WASH, gaps. The action targets roughly 643,946 beneficiairies of IDP, host and returnee communities, approximately 26% of total populations in targeted areas.
Localities selected are: Mellit, Kuma, Dar el Salam and rural Al Fasher (ND); Damso and Bileil (SD); Bahr el Arab, Assalaya, Adila and Abu KArinka (ED); Al Fashaga, Al Mafaza and Galabat Ash Shargi (GED). Localities selected are mostly (with the exception of 3) in IPC Phase 3, and have high WASH severity ranking related to open defecation and poor access to drinking water, resulting in potentially catastrophic health outcomes.

1 - Needs

The FSL sector estimates a total PiN of 9.8 million, with a target of 8.4 million (5.7 M reached with food assistance, and 3 M reached with livelihoods/recovery). The highest percentage of populations in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse is found in North Darfur (25%). In South Darfur, 17% of the population are in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3). The target localities host many IDPs, returnees and refugees with host communities, stressing access to resources and limiting access to livelihood opportunities. Many, many of whom have been impacted by historic flooding in 2020 that coincided with the peak of the COVID-19 outbreak, which has limited livelihood recovery abilities of vulnerable households. Small scale farmers targeted in these localities face high costs of production and limited access to inputs, improved techniques while facing high post-harvest losses estimated at approx. 40%. Ongoing conflict and displacement continues to impact smallholder farmers, with repeated attacks by nomads against farmers. In Dar el Salam in North Darfur, repeated attacks by nomads against farmers and security breaches have resulted in the state government declaring a State of Emergency to protect the harvest season. Nearly all targeted localities in this project are classified as IPC Phase 3 (with 3 out of 13 being in IPC Phase 2). Most localities show that 30% or more of the total populations in targeted localities are in Phase 3 or higher. In the target localities, water availability and latrine coverage are below standard, with high rates of open defecation reported. These poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, combined with the high concentration of people living in camps, increases exposure to diarrheal diseases. The targeted localities for WASH show high sector specific severity ratings of 4 and 5, with only one rated 3 (Ged- Al Mafaza, El Fashaga; ND – Dar es Salam, Melit; South Darfur – Bileil; East Darfur – Abu Karinka and Bahr el Arab. The 2022 WASH HNO shows high percentages of populations drinking from surface water and high rates of open defecation, signalling potentially catastrophic health outcomes. Most of the targeted localities show poor sanitation and access to water sources. Bahr el Arab, Mellit, Bileil and East Galabat show particularly poor WASH outcomes. In Bahr El Arab (ED), approximately 20% of the population are disposing of human faeces in open spaces. In Mellit locality (ND), this reaches 56%; in Bileil (SD), 44% and in East Galabat (GED), 19%. In Bahr el Arab (ED), approx. 16% are drinking from surface water, Mellit - 25% and East Galabat – 20%. According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan, some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition. In Sudan, the Global Acute Malnutrition and Severe Acute Malnutrition rates are 14.1% and 4.5%. Vitamin A supplementation coverage is estimated at 39.87%. The age-appropriate dietary diversity is only 24.11% indicating that malnutrition is not just caused by lack of food. The health systems in Darfur remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Health promotion, disease surveillance and referral remain poor due to low coverage of community outreach activities. Health facility delivery rate is also low (11.5%) which requires an increased effort on identification and referral of pregnant mothers to give birth at facility level. There are outbreaks of AWD, malaria and measles that occurred frequently.

The multi-sectoral response will target critical WASH needs in all locations. 6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ZOA and partners adopt a protection mainsreaming approach and will ensure the following during the project’s design and implementation: Gender sensitive latrines, with separate stalls for men/women, with locks on the inside for safety. Gender sensitive latrines will also be constructed in schools while in public social places, separate latrines will be constructed for men and women. Basic facilities including lighting will be installed in public places to ensure safety to women and children when using them even at night. In the water infrastructure rehabilitation and development activities, the project will ensure to put in place the following protection measures: Ensure that the planning of newly constructed water points will be jointly determined iwth the community to avoid any inter-communal tensions; the water points are at a central and safe, well-lit location easily accessed by most users; and the timing for access; if regulated, is convenient for majority of users. Further, water pumps will be well positioned so that even children, the elderly, and pregnant women can easily use; separate watering points for animals.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of accountability, ZOA has a complaints reporting mechanism in place in all its programming that is adopted by all implementing partners. The reporting mechanism is part of a broader three-pronged organizational integrity framework (IF) made up of i) code of conduct (coc)signed by all staff and consultants; ii) complaints reporting mechanism for staff, partners and beneficiaries and iii) Addressing complaints. Under the CoC are the child protection policy, PSEAH and fraud & corruption, among others. Each new ZOA project develops a beneficiary reporting mechanism that is shared with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project through sensitization so that they know their rights on where and whom to report to incase they have complaints.
