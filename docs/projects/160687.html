Project Summary

Protection monitoring in a context of internal displacement generally involves collecting, verifying and analyzing information to identify violations of rights and protection risks encountered by internally displaced persons (IDPs) and other affected populations to inform effective responses. The immediate objective of protection monitoring is to assist and guide evidence-based action by relevant national and international actors to ensure the full respect of the rights of IDPs and other affected populations. These actions might include speciﬁc interventions on behalf of individuals and groups, the planning and implementation of humanitarian assistance operations or protection interventions and advocacy or other activities by international actors and national and international civil society. GISCOR will undertake the protection monitoring through a series of steps that usually include: the collection of information on protection problems and violations of rights; analysis of the information to produce regular or a d h o c reports, including for instance statistics to identify trends; and preparation of the information for deciding protection responses. In all cases, it is of the essence that protection monitoring agencies have the necessary capacity, skills and willingness to comply with adequate standards for the collection and management of protection information. Internal displacement is often a result of violations of human rights or international humanitarian law and may itself constitute a violation of rights. At the same time, internal displacement always bears important consequences in terms of the enjoyment of rights by the IDPs. Internal displacement is, therefore, in itself a protection concern that protection monitoring systems need to measure. In some cases, displacement is a one-off phenomenon and rapid needs assessment mechanisms will be able to ascertain its magnitude and characteristics. This is, however, rarely the case. In most contexts, displacement follows complex patterns that are extended in time and space, and it is nearly impossible to foresee with any accuracy of its occurrence. Therefore, GISCOR shall strive to measure the dynamics of displacement over time, including the numbers of persons being displaced (taking into account the age and gender breakdown), the causes and patterns of displacement and the places of origin and destination. The movement that needs to be measured may also include secondary displacement or spontaneous return or settlement elsewhere in the country.

GISCOR will conduct protection monitoring under different modalities. The choice will be determined by contextual and operational factors, such as the type of setting, the size of the population being monitored, the speciﬁc operational purpose of the monitoring activity, and the resources (both human and ﬁnancial) available for monitoring. Monitoring can take place within camps or collective centers, in rural areas or urban settings. While always based on the same international standards, monitoring can focus on certain aspects of any given situation. For example, within camps, monitoring can examine distribution systems, the physical locations, and layout of camps, health facilities, the situation of vulnerable groups, physical protection, including sexual and gender-based violence and abuse, and issues related to psychological assistance and social welfare. When IDPs are in host communities, it is important to look at the impact they may have on the protection situation of these communities themselves.

1 - Needs

The conﬂict and the escalation of insurgency and counter-insurgency in North-East Nigeria have resulted in an unprecedented protection crisis with the displacement of Nigerians within the country and the ﬂee of thousands to Niger, Chad, and Cameroon. While great progress has been made on the military front, the conﬂict continues to inﬂict devastation and undue hardship, especially on displaced persons. With military success, many Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are leaving the camps and returning to their homes however, many of these homes have been destroyed. It is currently estimated that over 900,000 IDPs have returned to their areas of origin in North East (NE) Nigeria with the majority returnees in Adamawa state followed by Borno state. However, the IDP returns are still hampered by insecurity, lack of protection for women and children, e.g NFIs and adequate shelter in the areas of return. On a similar note, there is a continuous return of Nigerian refugees from neighboring countries of asylum, with many returns inconsistent with international law. Refugee returnees often end up in situations of secondary displacement in need of protection and reintegration assistance as well as access to basic needs and essential services. In the second half of 2019 increasing number of persons of concern in need of protection were recorded (4,836,459 individuals) by the experts and given the operational context, including reports of forced returns, it is highly likely that these trends will continue into 2020. As per the report from UNHCR and partners Vulnerability Assessment, Human Rights Watch, internally displaced women and children are disproportionately vulnerable to SGBV. Unaccompanied girls and adolescents, single heads of households, child mothers, child spouses, and women and girls living with disabilities are most at risk. This reality relates to several factors including separation from families, limited access to support and economic opportunities, and overcrowded living conditions that offer minimal privacy, movement restrictions, and security. The worsening economic situation of most IDPs, particularly women and young girls, exposes them to vulnerable environments making them susceptible to abuse, violence, and exploitation. Given the continuing emergency situation, the overall objective for humanitarians remains to provide immediate life-saving protection and humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and returning refugees. GISCOR will ensure that the centrality of protection, as well as protection mainstreaming, underpin the humanitarian response, particularly for women and children who face a severe risk of exploitation and abuse. In light of all these issues, GISCOR intends to support the improvement of protection and provision of basic assistance to the general population in IDP Camps, host communities and communities of returnees in Northeast Nigeria. GISCOR will support to sensitize andbuild the capacity of the target population (persons of concern) to enable them to protect themselves, meet their most essential needs using existing referral pathways, regain livelihoods and dignity. Vulnerable individuals will be reached with material protection based assistance including core relief items and shelter interventions. Equally, GISCOR will assist in the identiﬁcation of SGBV cases which will increase prevention and mitigation of SGBV with speciﬁc objectives to; create a safer environment for women, girls, boys and men in the community, ensure multi-sector quality response services that are equally accessible for all SGBV survivors.

2 - Activities

In general, this project will seek to address the protection and basic social needs of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and refugee returnees in Camps and local communities located in Northeastern part of Nigeria particularly Borno State, through community sensitization and community based Protection Action Groups (PAGs), GISCOR will conduct protection monitoring, identify and collecting information on SGBV for referrals, distribution of Non Food Items (NFIs) and vulnerability screening. This project also seeks to enhance the protection of vulnerable and conﬂict-affected individuals in general, in the location mentioned in the preceding paragraph. It will empower displaced persons and communities so that they become self-reliant and ﬁnd ways to meet their own basic needs. It will also create awareness among the people of concern thereby supporting the prevention of protection issues. With regards to protection monitoring, GISCOR will work with Protection Monitors (PMs) and Protection Action Groups (PAGs) to ensure that the persons of concern are reached, identiﬁed and supported with all required assistance. For the proﬁling and screening of returnee components, GISCOR will work with the relevant stakeholders to identify and screen households and individuals who are the most vulnerable among the people of concern in all the communities engaged within the domain of GISCOR which is Borno state. For material based support, GISCOR will work closely with IDPs, returnees and other relevant stakeholders to distribute NFIs and identify beneﬁciaries that require adequate shelter. GISCOR will support the prevention and reduce the risk of SGBV through identiﬁcation and management of minor cases, refer onward action and follow up through existing support structure and referral pathways.
