Project Summary

The project aims to support the Refugees and vulnerable host communities in East Darfur to improve food security and livelihood, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), Nutrition and protection assistance. The project aims to assist 25,000 beneficiaries. The target beneficiaries include 20,000 South Sudan Refugees (SSRs) settling in camps and refugees’ settlements, and 5000 vulnerable households. The project will work in close coordination with UNHCR and COR and other sector leads to ensure collaborative and complementary coverage of the needs and avoid overlap or duplication.
The project activities incorporate humanitarian response by provision of assistance to save lives and alleviate suffering, and building the target communities’ resilience through livelihood restoration and develop productive and managerial skills to enable the target beneficiaries sustain the provided assistance.

Needs

Oxfam conducted needs assessments in East Darfur during October and November 2018 to identify needs for the Refugees and host communities. Oxfam participated in inter-agency assessment led by COR and UNHCR and covered six locations hosting SSRs and host communities (El Nimir, Kario, El Ferdous, Abu Karinka, Jadalseed & Alriyad). The influx of SSRs created additional pressure on the host community WASH resources. This situation impacts access to essential services by the affected community, especially women and children. There is lack of safe water facilities for refugges and host community (HC) and in schools and clinics need to be addressed. Sanitation facilities in and outside camps are not enough; and there is poor hygine knwledge among the refugees (especially in the refugees‘ communities outside the camps) and host communties. This requires educating the refugees on hygiene and sanitation to adopt improved practices such as hand washing and hyginical preparation of food. Regarding food security the camps need assistance to access food during the lean season (Jan – Jun), and there is need to address protection concerns to cross cut with other activities. Some of the most vulnerable hot cmmunities also suffer from poor livelihood conditions, especially with the current increase in prices. So there is need to assist them with refugees with food secvurity assistance.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The complex crisis in Darfur has strong links to environmental and natural resource deterioration. Competition over land and water between sedentary ago-pastorals and nomadic tribes has long been a part of Darfur’s history. The conflict and displacement and refugees influx has exacerbated environmental degradation and desertification in Darfur. Other factors that compound environmental issues include poverty and underdevelopment in the region. IDPs and refugee populations contribute to deforestation and soil erosion. Oxfam recognizes that environmental considerations must be integrated into the planning and operation of projects to ensure any activities implemented are environmentally and socially acceptable. While carrying out the planned activities, Oxfam will take into account a variety of environmental considerations such as the management of wastewater, environmentally sound solid and liquid waste management (e.g. proper draining of water from tap stands so that it will not create pools of stagnant water that could contribute to the spread of disease.) Similarly, individual and communal latrines will be properly drained once they become full and effluent will be safely treated. All WASH related infrastructure design and construction will take into account environment issues such as the distance of shallow and deep wells from latrines and residential blocks, materials used for the construction of the hardware facilities, existence of proper drainage systems form sanitation facilities, etc. The project will make maximum effort to use environmentally friendly materials and options in WASH and livelihoods activities and will advocate with the UN and local government on how better to protect the environment in the midst of the crisis. Oxfam will also try to innovate the water supply with the application of solar power (green technology) which is friendly to the environment and at same time has very insignificant running cost. In Sanitation we started using mud bricks in latrines fencing in Kalma camp and by this we avoid using plastic sheeting, palm leaf mats and wood for fencing, which are more environmentally sound as well as long lasting compared to plastic sheeting and local tree materials. When drilling boreholes Oxfam will take care to avoid depletion of ground water. After the drilling of the well, a pump test will be conducted to check the yield and the water draw down, this testing helps to see the ground water recharging capacity to avoid any future risk of ground water depletion. Distance from any household and public latrines and waste sites will be considered, so that potential underground contamination is prevented.
