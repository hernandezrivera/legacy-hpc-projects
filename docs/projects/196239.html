Project Summary

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicates that ‘’approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to be impacted by the destruction of housing and loss of essential goods caused by ongoing flooding and conflict. In addition, nearly 329,000 refugees and asylum seekers also require shelter and non-food item (NFI) support. At the end of 2021, nearly 1.9 million men, women, boys, girls, and older people live in inadequate shelter, while a total of 2.3 million require NFIs. The number of people in need increased in 2022 due to the increased displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021 and the returns of nearly 270,000 from neighboring countries. The top three shelter/NFI-related needs are access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing, land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure’’. With the budget of $700,000, this project will enable World Vision International South Sudan to serve 55,000 (17,500 men, 17,500 women; 10,000 boys, 10,000 girls) IDPs, returnees and host communities with in-kind emergency shelter and non-food items in the counties of Tambura, Nagero and Yambio in Western Equatoria State, Tonj North, Tonj East, Tonj South, Gogrial East, Gogriel West and Aweil South counties in Warrap State, Laukpiny/Nasir, Ulang Melut and Baliet counties of Upper Nile State. In areas with existing functional markets, 18 % of the targeted beneficiaries will be supported through unconditional but restricted modality of cash-based interventions. Beneficiaries for both in-kind SNFI and cash interventions will be identified during needs assessments, where methodologies like Focused Group Discussions (FGD), Key informants (KI), Observation will be deployed to collect data for decision making and before beneficiary registration and distribution missions. Post distribution missions would also be effected to get feedback from affected population. In the course of the project, WVISS team will continuously conduct community awareness meetings and workshops on issues like climate change, AAP, Covid-19 etc. The project would contribute to the two Cluster objectives; (1). Percentage of the targeted population are able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition, (2). Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills. World Vision International South Sudan will implement the project in adherence with the SNFI cluster standards where the cluster coordination team is closely consulted on operations at national and sub-national levels. The organization will also collaborate with men, women, boys, girls, persons with special needs, government authorities and partners like UNHAS, UNOCHA, IOM/pipeline, local and international organizations to realize durable solutions for emergency shelter and non-food items. The affected populations will be encouraged to actively participate in the project through the mainstreaming of AAP (accountability to affected populations) approach into project activities. Existing local community structures would be strengthened to support the project and where they are non-existent, WVISS will facilitate the formation to enhance full participation of affected populations in area. The CFM, (Complaint Feedback Mechanism) will be introduced in each project site and community members would be sensitized on how to use it throughout the project period. The awareness creation sessions will be tailored to complement efforts for conflict mitigation, long-term solutions for floods, Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Housing, Land & Property, and COVID-19 pandemic. WVISS will continue to respond through the mobile response mechanism by deploying teams in hard to access locations or where there is urgent need of intervention across the country based on SNFI request.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022) estimates approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan (SS) affected by destruction of housing and loss of essential goods due to continuous flooding and conflict. Nearly 329,000 refugees and asylum seekers also require SNFI support. At the end of 2021, nearly 1.9 million men, women, boys, girls, and older people live in inadequate shelter, while a total of 2.3 million require NFIs. The number of people in need increased in 2022 due to displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021 and the returnees of nearly 270,000 from neighboring countries. The top three SNFI-related needs are access to emergency shelter; NFIs and housing; land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure. People who will need emergency shelter and NFI support in 2022 include displaced people, returnees and 5% of host communities. More than half of the affected people in 23 counties require emergency shelter support (Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states),have the highest concentration of displaced people. According to UNOCHA-South Sudan continues to receive returnees from neighboring countries. There was a stagnation of returnee numbers in 2021 before a sharp rise in October (more than 60,000) returnees were registered. Often arriving with minimal resources returnees will need shelter and NFI support. Inter-sectoral analysis showed that Shelter provides people with protection, identity, peace, stability and security, with a lack thereof having a negative impact on the wellbeing, dignity and health, and mental health included, further affecting education, livelihoods and food security leaving women and children exposed to protection risks. Shelter is the foundation and basis for engagement in livelihoods and economic activities while higher-quality shelter is linked to improved educational outcomes. Vulnerable individuals may be forced into negative coping mechanisms, such as exchanging sex for accommodation or forced marriage. The health of pregnant and lactating mothers, children under age five, older persons and persons with disability is compromised when living conditions are poor. Additionally security of tenure of the shelter ensures prevents forced eviction and loss of assets accrued. Women face considerable challenges (despite constitutional provisions for women and land rights) in asserting their HLP rights. Cultural norms, customary land tenure law and manipulations present barriers to women benefiting from these rights. The continued instability in neighboring Ethiopia and Sudan, will see a continuous rise the number of returnees. Returnees will require shelter and NFI support, as well as HLP information to establish a durable return in South Sudan. WVISS will assist at least 55,000 IDPs, returnees and host communities with in-kind S&NFIs in WEQ; Warrap; Unity and Upper Nile States. Where functional markets exist, 18% of targeted beneficiaries will receive unconditional but restricted modality of cash-based interventions. The project will contribute to Cluster objectives, (1). % of the targeted population are able to access SNFI material in situations of emergency and transition, (2). Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills. WVISS will implement activities with guidance from SNFI cluster standards on operations at national and sub-national levels. The organization will also collaborate with government authorities and partners like UNHAS, UNOCHA, IOM/pipeline, local and international organizations to realize durable solutions for emergency shelter and non-food items. The AAP (accountability to affected populations) approach will be incorporated into project activities, together with the Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM). Existing local community structures would be strengthened, where they are non-existent, WVISS will facilitate formation at each project site and community members.

2 - Project activities

1. Needs assessment. WVISS will use both the direct and indirect inter-agency rapid needs assessment (IRNA) missions to ascertain community need for In-kind or cash interventions and only prioritize severely affected people. Beforehand, desk reviews will be done to gauge the community situation ahead of deploying staff in the field. Affected beneficiaries will take part in the project in meetings and awareness workshops to discuss the project. WVISS response team will gather information through Focused Group discussions (FGD), Key Informants (KI), Observation of affected populations in order to take concrete decisions. SEA, HLP and Covid-19 issue would be mainstreamed into project activities. Additionally, CFM (Complaint Feedback Mechanism) will be introduced at this phase of project cycle to enhance participation of affected population from launch of the project to closure. 2. Beneficiary identification, verification and registration. WVI will identify and register beneficiaries with extreme vulnerability. This will follow the shelter and non-food items cluster selection criteria. Volunteer, local leaders and beneficiary representatives will take part in the verification and registration to enhance community participation in the project. 3. ES/NFI Distribution. Distribution missions will address critical SNFI needs of the affected population. WVISS will establish CFM (complaint feedback mechanism) where trained local personnel are assigned to manage. And as the covid-19 is still posing challenges, strict adherences shall be ensured for beneficiaries to mitigate spread of the virus. Educational messaging would be carried through distribution sessions to highlight the dangers and prevention measures for the pandemic virus. Based on the needs assessment findings, the distribution will include shelter, non-food items and covid-19 materials. 4. Deployment of response teams. The mobile team will also engage and respond to the emergency needs of affected populations in project sites and hard-to-access areas where the cluster has recommended. Deployment will be both bi-sectoral and multi-sectoral. 5. Post distribution monitoring. This activity will inform the project and WVISS in particular on the level of satisfaction or dissatisfaction of the affected population who received in kind NFIs and cash transfers. However, WVISS will ensure continuous monitoring for the cash interventions to understand market dynamics throughout the project lifespan. 6. Meeting/workshop, campaigns. Robust community meetings and workshops would be carried out in the course of the project to discuss and sensitize affected populations on critical matters like Climate Change, HLP, GBV, Covid-19 pandemic, community resilience and coping mechanisms. These awareness meetings will empower local leaders and institutions for the wider betterment of the community.7. Accountability to affected populations activities including information sharing, community feedback mechanisms, complaints redress will be implemented throughout the response cycle of this project.

Materials like plasticsheets would be used to construct new huts for the case of returnees, Displacedpersons can also rebuild their homes with it. Kitchen sets are used to prepare meals andimprove hygiene of household members. Blankets and mosquitoes’ nets are to be used atnight to protect beneficiaries from cold weather and protect people from mosquito bitesrespectively. Community members of the project will also be empowered invarious ways by conducting meetings and awareness on coping mechanisms andcommunity resilience. This is to be done from the start of the project by exploring andstrengthening local community structures and using them to disseminate information.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

World Vision will only use the approach in locations that have functional and stable markets, for example, Yambio, Tambura and Tonj South. Detailed market assessments will be carried out prior to launching the project in these locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The cash transfer modality would help stimulate recovery of communities from economic hardships by creating short-term income flows and increase the purchasing powers among beneficiaries. The approach builds upon linkages, capacities, incentives and relationships to encourage market effective recovery. It also enables beneficiaries to access assistance in a more dignified manner. This approach will be feasible for communities that have vibrant markets.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The E/NFI project will involve persons with special needs including the impaired and individuals who are not literate. The tools used during needs assessment, distribution and PDM will specifically target these group of people, in order to gain their inputs. Data that is gathered will also be dis-segregated including during reporting. Community mobilization for activities will also be done in a way that encourage them to actively participate in the project. Various local workers including interpreters will be recruited to disseminate information in local languages.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision will ensure that all staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.)Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, WVI will maintain its commitment to engage with affected communities at all phases of the project cycle, through focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls on issues concerning their education, WASH, and food security concerns. Establishing/strengthening local structures such as Project Management Committee is one example of how WVI engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine and support appropriate need-based responses. WVI's Quality Assurance framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with community needs and in line with other humanitarian frameworks.
