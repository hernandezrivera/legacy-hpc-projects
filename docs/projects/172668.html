Project Summary

The project will assist 69500 people with unconditional food or cash assistance, agricultural inputs, resilience building trainings ,cash for work ,rehabilitation of productive assets to meet their minimum food needs ,restore food and income security and build resilience to future shocks during severe seasonal shocks or other disruptions.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

People with disabilities (PWDs) making up 7% of the total population in Zimbabwe as well as the elderly are subjected to numerous obstacles to access food and livelihoods assistance. People with disabilities are vulnerable as they are unable to travel long distances to registration sites. They are labour constrained and cannot participate in farming and public works under cash for work projects. People with visual and hearing impairments tend to lack information about food security projects during emergencies. With this background, Oxfam will deliberately prioritise people living with disability during food/cash targeting. At least 5% of the cash for work beneficiaries will include people with disabilities and special considerations will be given when allocating tasks at the project sites. The project will work with local authorities for information on the disabled in the communities to reduce exclusion errors for the disabled. The disabled will also be included on the selection criteria to promote participation of the disabled in the project.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Oxfam will mainstream and implement environmental considerations across projects, as is currently done for gender and disability to holistically address the environment across our work. In practice this means mainstreaming environmental considerations across policy and programming. The three implementing partners, ORAP, AQZ and Help Age are not environmental specialist Oxfam will therefore consider bringing in Zimbabwe Environmental Law Association (ZELA) a technical and specialists environmental NGO that Oxfam is already collaborating with on an extractive and governance project to the technical consultant on mainstreaming environmental considerations. As part of the mainstreaming ZELA will develop methods to incorporate local voices and build the capacity of the three partners’ field staff to have expertise in programming and humanitarian advocacy. The capacity building will focus on the impact and importance of local knowledge in relation to preventing / mitigating environmental damage pre- and post- disaster to be adequately understood and/or utilised. The project will rehabilitate productive assists in the target communities for improved food and income security. Oxfam will also work closely with the local Environmental Management Agency (EMA) for feasibility assets for new constructions and mitigation measures to protect the environment. Conservation works for all the created assets will be included in the project design. The project will make a deliberate attempt to train communities on conservation works, protecting wetlands and environmental considerations for their communities
