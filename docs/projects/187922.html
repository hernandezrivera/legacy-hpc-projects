Project Summary

The project seeks to provide services in the Child protection sub-cluster to achieve the HRP objectives (SO 5) " Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action "in Dana, Harim in Idleb
governorate. All project activities will in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards
in Humanitarian Action(CPMS) and other standards and SOPs such as UNICEF alternative care SOP, CP Case management SOP, UNICEF Guidance for Inclusion of Children with Disabilities in Child Protection Interventions (UNICEF 2018) " to physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, and lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of worst forms of child labor, child recruitment and child marriage.
MASRRAT insure (600) vocational training (female) during MASRRAT WGSS centers.
Finally, the project team will take into consideration the data protection and confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols, and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries.
This intervention will support 20215 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis (800men,1000women, 9100 boys and 9315 girls, 200 PWD) for two years in Idleb /Harim / Dana /Tal Elkaramej .
Also, the project team will ensure data protection, confidentiality at all stages, protocols of information sharing and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries. The project activities will be in line with Protection Cluster Recommendations, SOPs, Inclusive COVID-19 response - NWS Disability - TWG on Inclusion Guidance Note April 2020 and GBV SC guidance note on GBV service provision in the time of COVID-19 such as Hand sanitizing when conducting the sessions/ Gathering of five beneficiaries per session and social distance/Measure the temperature for each beneficiary as a precaution/ Plan has been put for online sessions/ Put awareness posters about Corona disease during sessions.
MASRRAT has been in the protection sector for seven years. MASRRAT is a focal point to provide GBV services in Jebel Seman district.
This project will complement the ongoing UNFPA project through covering of more area of intervention in Jebel Samaan ,where the two teams will work together under the same management and technical support, also the project will support the centers by vocational activity (sewing).
Each outreach team will be accompanied by a case manager who identifies the cases and coordinates with the GBV technical supervisor on UNFPA project to receive the cases in WGSS centers.
MASRRAT will commit to send the 4w report on the specific time monthly .

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, Multiple and complex child protection risks will continue to shape the lives of girls and boys. Grave violations against children remain a significant concern including the risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual domestic violence. In addition, Psychosocial distress, family separation, and child labor are among the most CP concerns mentioned. Insecurity, economic hardship, and Covid-19 exacerbate both CP concerns and GBV risks.The need assessment survey was conducted for three days in May 2021 by MASRRAT field staff and it included two tools (The Child Protection needs assessment and Protection Need Assessments). During April 2021 the enumerators (6 enumerators 2 male and 4 female) were trained on the tools and on the aims of the assessment by the ISHA team and during May they were trained on collecting data.All the interviewed respondents said that the mobile networks are available and working and 73% of them said that the communities preferred the community volunteers to receive information, in addition to mosques (68%), SMS text messages (63%), online (like Facebook (53%) and via banners/posters. Furthermore, most members in the communities preferred to share information and provide feedback through face-to-face discussion

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is in line with 2021 Syria Humanitarian Response Plan Objective 5: (SO 5) " Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action.By addressing these objectives, MASRRAT will respond to the strategic needs of the targeted beneficiaries, especially UASC, child laborers, CAAFAG, children deprived of their liberty, early marriage, survivors of violence, sexual violence, including children with disabilities (CwD). These most vulnerable children will be provided quality child protection services through case management and referrals. MASRRAT will provide life-saving specialized child protection services, per CPMS standards, through case management and individual protection assistance to address the individual protection needs of the child and their caregivers. In addition, the intervention will establish child protection community awareness . MASRRAT will implement through a static center in addition to community outreach activities. Identification of cases will be conducted through the centers with existing caseloads, group activities, visitations to collective centers and camps, and referrals.in the time of COVID-19 such as Hand sanitizing when conducting the sessions/ Gathering of five beneficiaries per session and social distance/Measure the temperature.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.Protection risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MASRRAT will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. MASRRAT understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about MASRRAT position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. MASRRAT mobile teams, will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and will follow up the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and involved in the discussions in the community of intervention.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk (1): Changing the security situation that interfere with implementation of the project. Likelihood of Risk Occurring: Medium. Effect on Expected Results: high. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a ﬂexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results. and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA . Stopping the activities for short period. moving the center to another place. Risk (2): Security issues being sometimes unstable in the project’s areas such as faction armed ﬁghting. Likelihood of Risk Occurring: Very high. Effect on Expected Results: Very High. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: According to lessons learned from previous projects, it was found that while conﬂict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Risk (3): There is no coordination between organizations, especially inside Syria, where most of organization opening the same activities in the same time: Likelihood of Risk Occurring: medium. Effect on expected Results: Very High. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: MASRRAT would be conducting more meetings and coordinate with those organizations.
