Project Summary

The project aims to provide emergency life-saving assistance to detained or stranded migrants as well as the most vulnerable Libyan IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of essential non-food items (NFIs) and rehabilitation of damaged houses. The total requested amount of USD 4,700,000 split between the two above-mentioned activities will cover the needs of a total of 70,637 beneficiaries including 19,443 migrants, 15,304 IDPs, 27,411 returnees and 8,479 non-displaced population in the East, West and South of Libya.

1 - Needs

Despite the prospects for a durable peace following the signing of the ceasefire in November 2020, the situation in Libya remains complex and volatile, as a result of years of protracted conflict and political and economic instability. Moreover, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the preventive measures and curfews have further impacted the precarious socio-economic situation, especially for the most vulnerable. Reduced livelihoods and higher prices have affected the availability and affordability of primary needs goods and shelter. For weeks, people have protested in the streets for the poor living conditions linked to the difficulty to access basic services and a deterioration in infrastructures. According to the 2020 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), over 376,000 people in Libya are presently in need of shelter and NFI (SNFI) support, including 58,000 displaced people, 82,000 returnees, 107,000 non-displaced Libyans, 82,000 migrants and 46,000 refugees. This represents an almost 10 per cent increase when compared to figures in 2019.High displacement rates, damage of property, particularly in urban areas, and lack of income are among the main factors linked to an increase in the need for safe accommodations and NFIs. Aljfara, Alkufra, Almarj, Benghazi, Misrata and Sirte, Tobruk and Tripoli were identified as the locations were the needs are most severe. Around 15 per cent of all displaced people and 30 per cent of affected returnees require shelter support according to HNO 2021 while IOM DTM Round 32 identified NFIs as a priority need for 11 per cent of IDPs and 18 pr cent of returnees. . From the affected non-displaced Libyans, about 9 per cent are estimated to be in need support for shelter and non-food items, with the majority in Tripoli. In addition to this, around 15 per cent of all migrants and all refugees are in need of shelter assistance or support with non-food items, with the highest number in Tripoli for both groups. Migrants and refugees have also been more affected, compared to other groups, from lost livelihoods due to the temporary or daily nature of their work making it more difficult to afford essential non-food items and have disproportionally affected by movement restrictions further impacting their access to markets and livelihoods. Beyond rental assistance, many people report living in sub-standard accommodation and therefore support for improving shelter conditions is required, with 54 per cent of displaced, 68 of returnees and 75 of migrants and refugees reporting at least one issue with their accommodation . Conditions for the 6 per cent of displaced and 9 per cent of migrants and refugees living in informal settlements or collective shelters also require investment to ensure that they reach minimum humanitarian standards. Many vulnerable households also highlighted needs for different non-food items. These included items such as generators, cooking fuel, blankets or heating systems, computers, mattresses, cooking stove and water storage containers, although different population groups identified some differences in their needs. For example, migrants and refugees were more likely to highlight needs related to communications, such as phones, than Libyan population groups. Major concerns have also been raised by households in needs related to hygiene items which have been particularly affected by price increases. Lack of appropriate shelter and NFIs including clothing, bedding, and hygiene items (particularly affected by price increases) negatively affects all population groups (Libyan and non-Libyan) regardless of their age (children, young adults, vulnerable adults and elderly) and gender (men, women, boys and girls) worsening their wellbeing, with negative impacts on health, protection and access to basic services. Households are also in need of generators, cooking fuel, blankets or heating systems, computers, cooking stove and water storage containers.

2 - Activities

This project will include need-based provision of emergency shelter and non-food items to most vulnerable Libyan and non-Libyan population including IDPs, returnees, host communities and migrants in detention centers and urban setting. IOM will collaborate with government, S/NFI sector members as well as partners under the Rapid Response Mechanisms (RRM) in the provision of life-saving assistance. This project aims to support a total of 68,837 people: 18,843 migrants, 15,304 IDPs, 26,211 returnees and 8,479 non-displaced population urgently requiring lifesaving NFI supplies. NFIs will be distributed also through the joint inter-agency RRM targeting up to 6,000 IDP households out of the overall IDP project target. IOM will also distribute NFIs in hard-to-reach locations where beneficiaries have less access to services through mobile outreach teams. The content of NFI kits is harmonized with the S/NFI sector and are gender sensitive to ensure the needs of vulnerable groups are accounted for. Moreover, the project aims at supporting 1,200 IDP returnees and 600 migrants in urban setting living in unfinished or damaged shelters with rehabilitation of damaged houses. The project will identify vulnerable IDPs returning to their places of origin and require small scale shelter repair support such as roofing, windows, doors etc through provision of shelter repair kits and labor support. Female- children- and elderly- headed households will be prioritized in the response to ensure increased protection to those vulnerable groups. The total requested amount to cover the needs of all 70,637 beneficiaries split between two activities is USD 4,700,000.

Moreover, IOM continues its engagement to advocate on alternative to detention. 4.8: IOM’s activities will contribute to addressing some of the key critical and vulnerabilities identified in the IASC Framework on Durable Solutions for IDPs, which notes that IDPs who have achieved a durable solution will enjoy without discrimination an adequate standard of living, including at a minimum access to adequate food, water, housing, health care and basic education. In this context, through S/NFIs support to IDPs and migrants, IOM's project will temporarily fill in gaps in service provision and provide humanitarian assistance and protection for those vulnerable groups. As for returnees, the provision of shelter/NFIs will contribute to the resumption of normalcy and to achieve durable solutions.4.10: Verification of requests through DTM, supervisions of distributions, engagement of communities, application of S/NFI vulnerability criteria, consultation with Protection sector. 4.13: Based on consultations with the population, IOM will ensure accurate selection of distribution points, and considerations about the timing of distributions (to ensure it beneficiaries’ daily activities and responsibilities, especially women's household and care responsibilities). 4.14: Training of staff on PSEA as well as awareness raising for the population.4.17: IOM will ensure preliminary assessment to ensure property of the land and houses in collaboration with municipalities.4.20: IOM will conduct awareness raising on the one hand and collaborate with relevant partners including Area Sector Mine Action to verify clearance of ERW. 4.22: IOM will train its distribution staff to ensure they know how to safely and ethically support and refer survivors to the services they might need/wish to access. 6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

On one hand, accountability is ensured through the utilization of relevant inter-agency mechanism endorsed by the HCT, such as the Common Feedback Mechanism. On the other hand, IOM will use internal AAP mechanisms such as follows. Prior to planning any distribution, IOM will consult community representatives and local partners to identify the critical needs and needs of vulnerable groups. DTM will carry out needs assessment and verify priorities in accordance with S/NFI Vulnerability criteria guidelines and overall Protection Vulnerability criteria. Consultations with communities and local authorities will be conducted to collectively agree on places and time of distribution to ensure safety, security and accessibility. Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be put in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation. IOM will also conduct post distribution monitoring to gauge beneficiary satisfaction, appropriateness and usefulness of supplies provided.
