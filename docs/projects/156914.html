Project Summary

This project aims to continue sustaining reproductive health services and expand protection services in northwest Syria. This includes improving women and young and adolescent girls access to obstetric care to reduce the maternal and new-born morbidities and mortalities in conflict-affected areas in northwest Syria. Emphasis will also be on family planning activities and awareness raising strategies aiming at reducing unwanted pregnancies.
SAMS will accomplish these targets by sustaining the provision of services through its four facilities (Maternity Specialized Hospital CEmONC and Kafr Diryan BEmONC in Idleb district - Tarmala Hospital CEmONC in Al Ma'ra District in rural Idleb and Southern Rural Aleppo Mobil Clinic in Jebel Sama’an district in rural Aleppo) by supporting it with operational costs and ensuring all needed medications, medical consumables and lab supplies, are provided and available for the treatment of patients inside Syria, also SAMS will continue its efforts in building the capacities of the medical personnel on reproductive health protocols and family planning in coordination with UNFPA, it’s worth mentioning that SAMS supported facilities need for 612 RH Kits per year . The focus on medical supervision and quality of care will also be considered.
SAMS will continue providing monthly incentives to the medical and non-medical personnel according to the health directorate salary scale.
SAMS also will continue its efforts in raising awareness of the population in the targeted areas on SRH issues and supporting decentralized RH basic services and referrals, these sessions will be conducted by well-trained community health workers (two CHWs in each facility), they will refer the cases to the facilities.
This project aims to structure the medical education process inside NWS by meeting the different and increasing needs for Midwives. This medical educational program like this was established and
supported by SAMS through the past year and are crucial to addressing the gap in SR Health Care Workers in Syria. This program objective is to provide and secure Syrian civilians with quality health care staff, enhance the livelihoods for medical workers, and work to develop health services and a future workforce for Syria through supporting Midwifery Education in NWS, by implementing two modules of the Midwifery
Training Program due to the strategy which was developed by the SRH-TWG
Moreover, and through this project, SAMS will continue to support Midwifery Education in two locations in Idleb – Al Mara’a located in southern Idleb and Termanin in the north, by implementing a two-year midwifery training program. In Al Mara’a, specialized courses will be held at the institutional arm of the Al Salam hospital and in Termanin, the courses will be held at Atmah Hospital, Al Ferdous Hospital in Darat Ezah and Aleman hospital in Orem. Because the training will take place in hospitals, the program will adhere to standards as defined by the Ministry of Health in addition to the international standards
of midwifery training strategy produced by the reproductive health technical working group in the Health Cluster.
Finally, the project will aim to launch a nursing program that will extend for 2 years
categorized as follow:
- First and Second Academic Year:
- 2 semesters extending for 14 weeks each, followed by 2 weeks of examination periods
- Summer semester extending for a month and comprised of a clinical training

Needs

Based on REACH need assessment in NWS, the households in 92% of assessed communities in Idleb gov and its surrounding areas reportedly faced barriers to accessing healthcare in the month prior to data collection. Such barriers include a lack of health facilities, available medicines, female doctors, also the safety and security were the most commonly reported top priority need for households in assessed communities. In addition to growing concerns of a sharp escalation of conflict in the region, both resident and IDP populations were reported to have faced a range of different protection risks in the month prior to data collection, including threats from forced and early marriages, movement restrictions, and kidnapping, also most commonly reported child protection issues in the assessed communities was: Child labour, psychological trauma, risks from general security situation and violence against children. Pursuant to HNO and as of Dec 2018 numbers for health sector needs, 382,352 (PiN) in Dana, 144,125 in Idleb, 86,442 in Kafr Nobol, and 77,297 in Zarbah sub-districts with 4 out of 6 ranking (High) on the severity ranking. 160,129 PiN in Ma'arrat Al Nu'man and 105,484 PiN in Madiq Castle, with 3 out of 6 severity ranking (moderate). Based on the needs assessment conducted in (Northern Hama- Al Madiq Castle area and Idleb- Harim, al dana, Atmah camps.), health care provision, including reproductive health, were the least provided services to girls especially adolescent and young ones, with only 6% of the services available safely for them in the collective centers, Indicating the absence of health-care services in general and reproductive health as well as the failure to take into consideration the health needs of girls, especially those relating to reproductive health, in the planning and implementation of the services during the crises. RH is known to affect and get affected by the social, economic and cultural situation of society. RH was also affected by the status of women in society. Females face discrimination in matters related to the distribution of family resources, access to health care, especially in times of instability and war, and young and adolescent girls have often been silent about their reproductive health needs either because of their belief it’s insignificant compared to the overall situation. Data collected showed that health care was only 13%, safely available which is a very small percentage that reflects how women and adolescent girls needs in this regard are marginalized, especially mothers and pregnant women who need more general health care and reproductive health, especially those with chronic diseases. A severe shortage of skilled birth attendants and obstetricians remain present in the targeted location, meaning that pregnant women still lack access to critical childbirth services. According to HeRAMS data compiled by the Turkey-based health cluster in mid-2018, 28 percent of the overall functioning health facilities are operating without having a professionally trained midwife. Those who are currently working as nurses and midwives in Syria often do not have the formal required professional training and certification, thus frequent mistakes are reported. As outlined in a 2019 report by SAMS on disrupted reproductive health care in Syria, mistakes reported include misuse of equipment, misunderstanding of particular symptoms, or mismanagement of the patient. Such cases can be attributed to the fact that a number of midwives have been only relying on their practical experience, and have never undergone any professional training. According to an assessment carried out by SAMS (relying on the HIS and nurses/midwives certificate records) about 39 percent of nurses and midwives working in HF in NWS are not certified. Most have accrued experience and skills through difficult work in the midst of war, yet they still lack the education needed to safely provide care.

Implementation of activities

The project implementation will focus on thre components:1. Provision of quality and essential SRH services to women, young and adolescent girls. These services include ANC, PNC, deliveries, post-abortion care, STIs management, CMR and family planning through 2 CEmONCs, 1 BEmONC, and 1 Mobile Clinic in Aleppo and Idleb by supporting staff salaries, running cost and other needed cost such as travel, medical supplies, equipment, and others.2. Capacity Building: Enhancing the quality of services through capacity building initiatives for SRH. The targeted trainees will be physicians, midwives, and other staff. The planned training will include SRH clinical protocol, family planning, and PSEA in addition to the guidance and supervision sessions that will be provided to the clinical staff in facilities by SAMS Health and SRH officer.3. For supporting the Midwifery program: There will be 2 programs that will take place in 2 locations (Termanin Nursing and Midwifery Institute in Harim District / Al Ma’ra Midwifery school in Al Ma’ra District) and will include:A 3 years’ program targeting 30 secondary school graduates and an 18 months’ program targeting 43 nurses who will be trained to become midwives. Each program will consist of theoretical lectures, hands-on workshops (demonstration on mannequins) and clinical rotations/practical training in the hospital and laboratories. The program will be structured as follows: 1.Theoretical Training: Through lectures and demonstrations, the curriculum will cover care during normal pregnancy, identification of high-risk cases torefer, managing vaginal deliveries, initial new-born evaluation, and care, follow up mother recovery and support, interpretation of laboratories results and safe pain management, with a total of 30 hours of instruction per week during the first year. The lectures will also cover GBV case management, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Psychological First Aid (PFA), IYCF and the nutrition of PLW 2.Clinical Training: Students will begin the clinical training within the last month of the first year of the program, with between 30 clinical hours per week. Clinical hours will double in the second year of training. Experience in thedelivery room will advance the students through the following stages: Observation > Secondary Assistant > Primary Assistant > Independent Midwife. Ultimately each student will take part in a total of 200 deliveries. In case of disruption of services at Al Salam hospital, other SAMS-supported facilities in the area will be selected to serve as back-up facilities to ensure continuity of the program. 3.Certification: To ensure unification of the curriculum with nursing and other standard medical practices in Syria, SAMS will ensurethe use of curriculum approved by the Aleppo and Idleb universities prior to the crises and the health directorates in each province.
