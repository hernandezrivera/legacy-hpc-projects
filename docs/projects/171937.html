Project Summary

The HALO Trust will conduct risk education, survey and clearance operations in eastern Ukraine, to
respond to the needs of mine-affected communities across Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts,
focusing on high-priority areas within the 15km buffer-zone from the line of contact. HALO
will also provide training and capacity building sessions for the key mine action
stakeholders including the newly established mine action authorities in order to contribute
to an effective and efficient mine action sector.
Risk education sessions are a preventive measure that will inform people about the threat related to
mines and other explosive items in order to decrease the probability of accidents. Survey work
will establish the extent and nature of mine contamination in the region, while the marking of minefields further prevents accidents from happening. Clearance will permanently remove these threats by making land safe, which improves the physical security of people in eastern Ukraine. Clearance of land also improves livelihoods by releasing land that can be used for productive purposes. HALO has thus far released seven million square metres of land previously contaminated with explosive hazards and aims to clear another two million square metres in 2021.

1 - Needs

Six years after the start of the conflict in eastern Ukraine, the humanitarian needs of the civilian population on both sides of the contact line continue to be serious and wide-ranging. In the first half of 2020, 36% of all civilian casualties of the conflict were attributed to landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) and Ukraine still ranked fifth in the world for highest casualty rate of mines and ERW. Landmines and ERW have taken a heavy human toll in eastern Ukraine, causing more than 2,243 casualties since the start of the conflict in 2014. In 2020 only, there were 115 reported casualties caused of mines and ERW, which was higher than the year before. More than 130 children have been victims of mine or ERW accidents during the same period, killed or injured while playing, walking in the fields or travelling with their families. Civilians do not just face the threat of injury but often also lose access to their land; depriving them of subsistence and income-generating activities. Although most civilian casualties were men (57%), whole communities and households are affected by the contamination of valuable land that can no longer be used for farming, foraging, firewood collection, or the development of land for infrastructure, industry, housing or commercial purposes. Around 70% of mine contamination is located on agricultural land, which severely impacts livelihoods. Heavily contaminated areas around roads and checkpoints present a further risk civilians as well as to humanitarian organisations providing support to the conflict-affected communities, limiting access to crucial aid for those in need.Rapid assessments conducted in government controlled areas of Donetsk and Luhansk regions since 2016 have noted 214 settlements in which land is thought or confirmed to be contaminated by landmines, cluster munitions or explosive ordnance. Non-technical survey (NTS) in these settlements has so far identified almost 23 million square meters of contaminated land. Although two-thirds of contaminated land is situated within the 0-15km zone from the contact line, a third of surveyed land and of total civilian accidents happen outside this area. Survey is still ongoing and the area requiring systematic clearance will probably increase even further. At the political level, a Mine Action Law came into effect in 2019 and was further amended in 2020. It is a positive development for the mine action sector in Ukraine as the law establishes a legal framework in line with international standards. The National Mine Action Authority and the National Mine Action Centre have not yet been established, however, it is probable that they will draw from the institutional capacity already present in the sector. HALO regularly contributes to the capacity development within the sector by providing technical knowledge, experience and guidance to key mine action stakeholders.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

HALO began mine clearance in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in April 2016 and has since grown to the largest humanitarian demining organisation working in Ukraine. Since the start of operations, HALO has cleared 7 million square meters of land, with almost 104,000 people benefitting so far from completed or ongoing clearance. Current capacity comprises more than 400 staff in 25 manual clearance teams, five mechanical teams, three technical survey teams, three NTS teams and three mine risk education (MRE) teams. In 2021, HALO aims to continue risk education, survey and clearance operations in government controlled areas of eastern Ukraine including along the contact line, and is exploring opportunities to start work in non-government controlled territory. HALO is looking to increase its capacity to over 500 staff next year, and to this end, plans to train five new manual clearance teams and two mechanical teams. HALO will ensure that female staff, who currently make up 16% of operational staff and 56% of support staff, are recruited as part of these new teams, by targeting job advertisements to encourage women to apply and by ensuring an inclusive work environment for all. This will not only contribute to further gender mainstreaming, but will also ensure that all voices are heard during community outreach. Natural curiosity often leads both children and adults to touch hazardous items, collect them, or even try to destroy them, which can have tragic consequences. In order to prevent such accidents, HALO visits local government buildings, schools, factories, or households in settlements impacted by the threat of mines or other ERW. Throughout 2019, HALO provided risk education for over 80,000 people during direct sessions. The MRE teams will use various methods, depending on what is appropriate for the audience, in order to teach safe behaviour and increase awareness of the dangers of mines and other ERW. HALO already uses innovative methods to target a larger and a more diverse range of people through the production and distribution of various MRE materials which provide long-term messaging and visibility. NTS teams will continue to identify, map and mark hazardous areas across both regions in the GCA. This will continue to improve HALO’s understanding of the mine threat especially in the buffer zone. NTS teams will also facilitate explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) through the State Emergency Services (SES), in instances where civilians report the presence of hazardous items in their settlements. All clearance and technical survey teams will be deployed onto areas identified as high humanitarian priority during NTS. A large proportion of this clearance will take place in the buffer zone, provided security conditions allow demining to take place safely. Mechanical capacity has expanded after the successful trials and deployment of various mechanical machines throughout 2020, allowing for higher clearance rates. Mechanical teams will clear abandoned military positions and trenches along with critical infrastructure sites, all of which are difficult and slow to clear manually. This will allow land to be released back to the communities more quickly than through manual clearance alone, and ensure that items of infrastructure, such as water pipes and electricity pylons, can be safely maintained. As the new Mine Action Law became effective in 2019 and underwent the necessary amendments in 2020, a new National Mine Action Authority (NMAA) is expected to be fully established in 2021. HALO believes it is crucial that the NMAA and its members will be equipped with knowledge, skills and procedures in line with international mine action standards. Additional specialised training for the key mine action stakeholders and government representatives will support an effective mine action sector with national authority that is capable of coordinating activities across eastern Ukraine and respond to the humanitarian needs of civilians.

All activities will be monitored by senior management staff as well as HQ. In line with Ukraine Accountability Framework, HALO has developed a robust system of procedures to ensure accountability to affected people and communities. All relevant HALO policies are publicly available as well as all contact details for feedback or complaints. HALO’s global model of employing staff from mine-affected communities is being successfully implemented in Ukraine. This approach aids HALO’s relations with local communities and ultimately increases the confidence in the end product: land free from the suspicion of mines. Those living and working in mine-affected settlements have a chance to witness first-hand the processes that take place during survey and clearance. Additionally, in order to receive feedback from the beneficiaries, HALO conducts regular needs and impact assessments throughout its area of operations.
