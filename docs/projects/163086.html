Project Summary

The project seeks to directly work with girls, boys, women, men and the traditional leadership to strengthen protection mechanisms during the emergency. Activities will be aimed at building resilience through collectively identifying protection risks during the emergency and risk mitigation. The most vulnerable groups of children that have been identified include the girl child, Child Headed Households, Children with Disabilities, children from marginalised communities (the San Community) and out of school children. A Rapid Assessment on the situation of children (RA) conducted by Plan International Zimbabwe in September 2019 involving children from different age groups, adults and key stakeholders indicated likely protection issues to arise out of this emergency situation include neglect, negative coping strategies such as risky sexual behaviour for economic gains, teen pregnancies, child marriages, sexually transmitted infections. Furthermore, the RA indicated a high likelihood of children (in particular boys) engaging in high risk economic activities further compromising their protection and security. These risks include but are not limited to illegal migration to neighbouring countries, gold panning and moving to “emlageni”. Emlageni involves seasonal to provide in grazing areas that are far from usual homes. The RA also indicated that as a result of food insecurity, there has been an increase in protection issues affecting children within the district. It is thus assumed that by addressing food insecurity at household level and building resilience of children and their families this should translate to strengthened protection of children. Both children and adults indicated that as a coping strategy, there is high likelihood of more children dropping out of school hence further jeopardizing their protection.
