Project Summary

DRC will build the resilience of 14,000 conflict affected and displacement-affected people in the Borno state. This will enable them to regain their economic well-being thus from meeting their basic needs in emergency contexts to recovering and / or strengthening sustainable and resilient livelihoods.
The concept of graduation underpins DRC’s approach to economic recovery. Across all activities, DRC aims at graduating people of concern from assistance to self-reliance and resilience or, in other words, to being able to cover their needs independently of external assistance and in a dignified and sustainable manner.
Using small business cash grants, DRC will enable 2,800 households to re-establish or expand their income generating activities (IGAs). For those without pre-existing skills, DRC will facilitate them with training and also provide them with cash grants to establish IGAs or get employment. Households that have access to land for cultivation will be supported to engage in farming while for those who can practice livestock rearing, they will also be supported to do so. To promote social cohesion, beneficiaries will be form savings and loans groups (SLG), will be trained on the Sharia-compliant SLG methodology.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan, as a result of the protracted conflict which restricted communities from accessing their livelihoods, markets and income-generating opportunities, 8.4 million people require assistance across Borno, Yobe and Adamawa (BAY) States conflict . DRC’s most recent needs assessment found that the major livelihoods for households in Borno is petty trade (40.3%) and food production and sales (53.3%) and this has dropped from 67.4% and 64% respectively pre-conflict . However, 98% and 95% of the population in Borno and Adamawa States respectively still earn below the monthly minimum wage. Indeed, in Borno, 27.2% of the households have less than 5000 NGN on a monthly basis while this proportion is 18.1% in Adamawa. In addition, 16.3% of the surveyed households in Borno don’t have any income compared to 4.3% in Adamawa and this is mainly attributed to insecurity. The recent Cadre Harmonise findings show that between October – December 2022, 3,043,001 people in BAY states are food insecure thus between Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 3 and 5 and this figure is expected to raise to 4,352,431 between June to August 2023 . In addition to the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic (iMMAP/DFS ), since October 2020, inflation rates have continued to increase and saw an especially sharp jump in early 2021 and then again in March 2022 and it is expected to further increase in light of the Ukraine crisis . Food consumption is expected to deteriorate slightly due to reduced markets stocks. Its resultant hike in prices of staple foods will limit households’ capacity to acquire stocks and sustain access to quality and nutritious foods. The continuous and planned camp closures in and around Maiduguri only exacerbates the situation as displaced persons move to locations with already stretched resources and little to no livelihood’s opportunities, increasing intercommunal tensions. Additionally, floods have greatly impacted over 210,859 individuals in the BAY States (Borno - 50,986, Adamawa - 135,552, Yobe - 24,321) leading to the loss of lives, injuries, property and infrastructural damage and left people in need of urgent assistance including food, shelter, NFIs and health. According to IOM, as of the 24th October 2022, 116 people in the BAY states were reportedly dead (Borno – 39, Adamawa – 18 and Yobe – 59). The farmlands partially destroyed were 4,933 ha (Borno – 2,463 ha, Adamawa - 1,105 ha and Yobe – 1,365 ha) while those totally damaged were 26,024 ha (Borno – 11,872, Adamawa - 10,159 ha and Yobe – 3,993 ha).

2 - Activities

DRC's donor for the selected activities is USAID Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA). Activities are planned to be implemented in Borno state. Activities for the project will include; Activity 1 – Small business management training and distribution of Business Grants for Small Scale Business Start-up: A total of 3,820 households (representing 14,100 individuals) will be supported with small business management trainings and business grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer livelihood options for income opportunities and future recovery. Beneficiaries will engage in the viable small businesses identified by DRC Livelihood Market Assessments for Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. DRC will also provide business advisory and monitoring for women and youth assisted with income generation activities.Activity 2 - Vocational Skills Trainings for Youth: DRC will train 750 youth representing 3,750 individuals in new marketable vocational skills identified by labor market assessments. This activity will strengthen capacity of youth and create off-farm income generating activities in Borno state. Activity 3 – Support formation of Village Savings and Lending Associations (VSLA) groups to enhance access to financial services that include credit/loans, savings and fund growth for 1,000 women and men (representing 5,000 individuals) in Borno state.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project uses a graduated approach. It has livelihood restoration interventions that are aiming at enabling communities restart or expand their original livelihoods. It also aims at equipping people with new skills such as vocational skills. There is an aspect of financial inclusion through village saving and lending associations. The project also aims to build connections between beneficiaries, LGA officials, and State officials for greater co-management of sustainability of the interventions.The project sequenced and integrated with other DRC emergency programs in the same or nearby LGAs. It is also implemented in coordination with other partners in the ERL.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

DRC in all proposed locations works with Protection staff that will mainstream protection issues before and during project implementation. DRC has Protection Mainstream strategy for all sectors to ensure protection issues are well mainstreamed. Assessments and community consultations will consider all age, gender and marginalized/minority groups during programme design and planning to ensure that they influence the decisionmaking process for the project. DRC will ensure implementing a do no harm approach to its programming to minimize disrupting social cohesion and peaceful existence. DRC also has a complaints and response mechanism in place to facilitate the report of sexual exploitation and abuse also complemented by regular Protection Monitoring and Screening conducted by Protection staff.
