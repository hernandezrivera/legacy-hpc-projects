Project Summary

The main goal of this project is to improve access to and utilization of essential lifesaving emergency healthcare services by all people in need and more so the vulnerable populations in Nyirol and Fangak Counties. This is in line with the Health Sector Response plan 2021 for South Sudan which emphasizes three objectives i.e. reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, improving access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems and increasing access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.
The design of this project is emphatic on provision of basic essential health services in line with the established protocols and guided by the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable groups like women, children and person with disabilities is paramount in the delivery of this project. Therefore, the emergency that this project will address is in respect of the high rate of mortality from the highly prevalent epidemic diseases. In the volatile context of Jonglei, the project will target both IDPs recently displaced and newly displaced by conflict and flooding, and host communities in areas with limited access to health services. The 2021 Health cluster data shows people in need are 67,124 and 41,633 in Fangak and Nyirol respectively. In the two counties, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) has persisted due to seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning. This points to an anticipated period of food insecurity which has the potentiality to midwife MAM and SAM with their concomitant health implications.
In order to achieve the expected results, the project has prioritized PHCC, PHCU and mobile team clinical packages. The strategies include: Recruitment and placement of adequate staff in key cadres namely midwives and clinical officers to provide key services, Provision of outpatient consultations, Provision of BeMONC and CeMONC in key health facilities, Initiating and sustaining Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in health facilities, Establishment of mobile response units and deployment of medical teams, Renovation and equipping of health facilities, Conducting monthly supportive supervision of health facilities jointly with the CHD, Provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities, Provision of rehabilitation services for disabled, Capacity building of health personnel for providing CMR and services following the standard treatment protocols, Referral of the survivors to safe houses and lastly, establishment and maintenance of feedback mechanism at health facilities.
It is envisaged that a successful implementation of these activities would significantly result in the overall decrease in morbidity and mortality arising from the epidemic prone diseases and improvement of access and scaling-up of integrated quality essential health services. It will further increase access to services for SGBV survivors, disabled individuals and people with mental health disorders. Besides improving resilience among vulnerable populations, the activities are further expected to benefit from an integrated response involving key sectors such as health, WASH, nutrition, FSL and protection. In order to ensure Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), a mechanism will be provided for affected populations to receive the information needed to make informed decisions on accessing health services, provide feedback on the interventions, share concerns and submit complaints by involving all stakeholders.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has continued to suffer some of the worst health indicators globally with <5 mortality rate of 96 deaths per 1,000 live births and with 75% of child deaths due to the preventable diseases of diarrhea, malaria, and pneumonia. In addition, the maternal mortality rate is 789 per 100,000 live births with less than 8% of deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants. The health care situation is worse in states such as Jonglei which have borne the brunt of conflicts and other natural crises. In Fangak and Nyirol Counties, for instance, poor access to healthcare services has been attributed to multiple factors including, conflicts, perennial flooding and displacement. Ultimately these conflicts have caused continued breakdown of health facilities and resulted in interruption of essential services and remains one of the main drivers of morbidity and mortality in the county. The two counties are currently experiencing rising rates of mortality and morbidity due to limited access to PHC services (with majority living further than 1-hour walking distance from a health facility), weak capacity to deliver essential clinical and emergency lifesaving health services. Additional stress on the PHC system is due to malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Similarly, seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and measles, and the continued endemicity of malaria pose additional challenges. These disease outbreaks tend to last longer hence weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks. On the other hand, Flooding has exposed people to Malaria, Diarrhea and other waterborne diseases. Unfortunately, flood-response activities have been considerably constrained by infrastructure damage and reduced physical accessibility and insecurity (OCHA, Dec 2020). Health issues are further being compounded by malnutrition as conflict continues to disrupt agricultural production and the cereal markets. In relation to acute food insecurity, the 18 Dec 2020 IPC report indicates both Fangak and Nyirol counties are currently classified as Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) acute malnutrition (IPC South Sudan Key Messages Report Oct 2020 – July 2021, 18 Dec 2020, pg. 8-10). The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. A critical analysis of gaps in the health sector in Fangak and Nyirol, therefore, points to the dire need for the provision of essential lifesaving basic health services at the health facilities including community-based healthcare. Besides the need to put in place adequate prevention measures there is urgent need for disease outbreak detection and timely response. Similarly, there is need to enhance the referral pathways for MPSS, SGBV survivors as well as maternal and child health emergencies. With recorded cases in Nyirol, there is need to mainstream COVID-19 prevention and response activities until such a time that it will no longer be a threat to communities. These factors indicate a sharp deterioration which requires intervention. The rise in SGBV has heightened the need for comprehensive care of victims of SGBV. Women, adolescent girls and children are most at risk. This reality coupled with conflict, insecurity and flooding causes severe restrictions to access health services. Special medical outreaches and community-based protection measures are needed to ensure PLW and children have access to services. Indeed, an agile mix of service modalities is needed in order to expand equitable access to services, including static services (health facilities), outreaches and community health services in the emergency contexts of Nyirol and Fangak counties.

2 - Project activities

The activities proposed in this project are aimed at improving access to and utilization of essential health care services by all the targeted people in need, in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality in Nyirol and Fangak counties. In these activities, the project seeks to facilitate the achievement of both strategic and specific as well as sectoral objectives. Key amongst the strategic objectives would be the need to reduce morbidity and mortality, protection threats and incidents for the most vulnerable populations and also the need to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5. It is envisaged that scaling up of the proposed activities will ensure that disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and supported by adequate surveillance systems. Further this project has an inherent plan to provide health services adapting to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. In alignment with the HRP and health cluster objectives, the project has been planned around PHCC, PHCU and mobile team clinical packages. Therefore, 1. To reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, CMA plans to carry out the following activities 1.1 Emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases, 1.2 Case management for selected diseases (Malaria, ARI, AWD, ABD, Measles); 1.3 Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, 1.4 Disease surveillance including outbreak investigations and response, 1.5 Epidemic disease prevention and control measures, 1.6 procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities. 2.To improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems, the project plans to focus on the following activities: 2.1 Recruitment and placement of adequate staff in key cadres namely midwives and clinical officers to provide key services, 2.2 Capacity building for health care workers, 2.3 Provision of outpatient consultations, 2.4 Provision of BeMONC and CeMONC in key health facilities, 2.5 Initiate and sustain Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in health facilities, 2.6 Support targeted number of static integrated facilities, 2.7 Establishment of mobile response units and deployment of medical teams, 2.8 Renovation and equipping of health facilities 2.9 Conduct monthly supportive supervision of health facilities jointly with the CHD. 3.To Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly, the following activities will be undertaken in the project, 3.1 Provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities, 3.2 Provision of rehabilitation services for disabled, 3.3 Capacity building of health personnel for providing CMR services following the standard treatment protocols, 3.4 Referral of the survivors to safe houses, 3.5 Capacity building of health personnel to provide MHPSS services, 3.6 Establishment and maintenance of feedback mechanism at health facilities.

3 - Project indicators

Measurement is crucial in knowing what makes up success versus failure in a project. There are various components of an effective performance measurement system namely: 1) Cascading performance measures that can be used to measure how well program objectives and by extension the goals are being met; 2) Established baselines from which progress toward the attainment of goals can be measured; 3) Accurate, repeatable, and verifiable data; and 4) Feedback systems to support continuous improvement of an organization’s processes, practices, and results. Therefore, in this project attempts will be made to establish if the project requirements have been delivered and success criteria set met as promised in terms of service delivery. The important factor here will be to measure if indeed this project did deliver on the set objectives. This will be measured by focusing on the review of planned activities and the operational and performance indicators. Some of the key indicators of success include: 1) Malaria incident rate 2) % of children vaccinated against measles 3) % of alerts, rumors and outbreaks investigated within 48 hours of notification 4) % of deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants 5) # of consultations per person yearly 6) # of persons treated for mental conditions & provided with MHPSS services 7) # of health facilities providing SGBV health and MHPSS services, Coverage of PENTA3 in <1-year-old by administrative unit 8) # of PHCCs with BEmONC)/500,000 population 9) % of health facilities assessed using IPC scorecard 10) % of facilities with community feedback mechanism 11) % of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision # of CHW per 1,000 people, # of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services, # of PWD receiving rehabilitation services. Further, from the health PIN and targets, the two parameters will provide a basis for the measurement of the progress attained. In terms of accurate, repeatable, and verifiable data, this project will endeavor to collect and store the same for purposes of not only measuring success, but also as aiding in decision making regarding adjustments or modifications in implementation and delivery. The baseline data for this sector has already been established in the DHIS, and program monthly and quarterly reports of CMA’s ongoing health programming. CMA will use the following tools to monitor project activities: (1) FGDs to monitor protection, impacts of awareness outreaches and access to health facilities; (2) Regular consultations with affected populations of all cadres to ensure participation in planning and monitoring the program, access to services, implementation of a complaints mechanism and raise awareness on its process, and ensure a system of representation of affected populations is in place; (3) Monthly HMIS, weekly IDSR reports and immunization campaign reports from health facilities; (4) Monthly activity reports from health facilities providing data not included in the HMIS reports; (5) Quarterly project reports to donors; (6) Quarterly field monitoring and evaluation reports Another strategy that will be used to measure success will focus on feedback from the key stakeholders and/or beneficiaries. Here, CMA will gather data to ascertain their overall outlook and satisfaction with the delivery. Instruments shall be administered to selected respondents to rate various success factors so that their answers are tangible. Subsequently, the data availed will provide evidence for strengths and weaknesses and hence help pinpoint what can be improved upon. Similarly, based on the contractual agreements between CMA and the donors, guidelines on reporting will be strictly adhered to as stipulated i.e. either per mid-term or end of project submission. Where necessary, joint participatory evaluation exercises will be conducted by CMA, health cluster team and the County Health Department (CHD) and donor representatives.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMA has completed the rollout of its revised PSEA policy with training of all management at Juba and County levels and the Facility lead officers at the facility level. The County level officers and the Facility lead officers are now skilled to implement the PSEA training at the facility level which includes training of facility-based, mobile team and outreach workers on: (1) gender, age and disability appropriate methods and content for disseminating information about PSEA; (2) available channels for reporting abuse; (3) confidentiality rules in reporting abuse cases; and (4) the mechanisms and locations available for victims of abuse to access support and treatment. CMA has already also developed a whistleblower policy which has been shared with senior staff waiting to be shared with all staff at various levels including the field to facilitate timely reporting of PSEA related cases. Similarly, adequate communication materials have been developed including posters and banners which would be used to sensitize and create awareness among the beneficiaries regarding their role in Prevention and/or Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). In this endeavor, the overall strategy will include use of these materials for awareness-raising campaigns to sensitize targeted communities on sexual exploitation and abuse; reporting mechanisms and procedures for ensuring confidentiality and the rights of survivors; and mainstreaming of sexual exploitation and abuse in any workshop and training conducted by CMA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CMA’s on-ground personnel will undertake focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs) as part of the planning and monitoring processes to ensure ongoing effective feedback. CMA’s Facility lead officers will ensure that the most vulnerable disabled, illiterate, women and children are included. At all sites, the affected population groups that CMA consults in the planning and feedback processes are as follows: (1) local women and men leaders, focus groups and key informant women and men; (2) Mother to Mother Support Groups, other social groups representing the most vulnerable including individuals living with disabilities and (3) discharged patients women, men, girls and boys including individuals living with disabilities. In all the deliberations held with these diverse groups, their concerns and complaints will be recorded and analyzed further by the project management team. Ultimately, this team will be tasked with the responsibility of coming up with strategies which are friendly and therefore able to address the concerns of the most vulnerable members of the targeted beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Needs assessment on entry to new locations and on an ongoing basis thereafter ensures that project activities are tailored to community needs. Information is disaggregated in respect of gender, age and the needs of different population groups to improve suitability of planned interventions. In locations already served, CMA has established mechanisms to ensure accountability to affected populations. In new locations, CMA will apply its established strategy and implementation model to ensure accountability to affected populations. The structures and mechanisms established enable affected populations to participate actively in planning services, receive information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, provide feedback on interventions, voice and submit complaints and participate in program decision-making. CMA’s programs are implemented in collaboration with local authorities and leaders of host communities and IDPs. At all locations, monthly meetings with leaders, Boma Health Committees, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and other key social groups are conducted to report on project output and obtain community feedback. The Facility lead officers or supervisors, (typically a Clinical Officer or Team Leader) conduct discharge interviews with a sample of the clients/patients to obtain feedback from those who receive services. These consultations provide the most vulnerable of affected populations full awareness and opportunity to utilize the complaints mechanism, the opportunity to actively participate in planning and monitoring the project, learn their entitlements for accessing project services and render complaints. CMA’s on-ground teams will ensure that affected populations are aware and able to utilize the complaints mechanism by practicing the principles of transparency, communication and provision of information linked to the complaints mechanism, and engaging the target populations to actively seek out their feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Facility lead officer will be responsible for coordinating the engagement with communities and actively seeking their feedback. The consultation activities will be recorded and reported monthly to the County Officers and the project management team, highlighting what new actions (if any) need to be taken. These measures will ensure that an effective system of representation of affected populations is in place for the purpose of improving project results. Further, the Project Manager will visit and supervise the project (once per quarter) to conduct monitoring and evaluation so that regular assessment of beneficiary knowledge on project progress and their satisfaction with project performance in conducted, and new measures put in place in response to beneficiary feedback. Further, the Project Manager will conduct meetings with local leaders of host and IDP communities and local authorities to ensure effective implementation and robust monitoring of the complaints mechanism is in place so as to achieve effective accountability to the populations being served

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• 8.1: High Risk: Mitigation Measures: To address the reality that many beneficiaries face exposure to violence while accessing facility-based services because of long distances to locations where project services will be centered, and to mitigate this risk, CMA will employ mobile and outreach approaches to go to the locations where at-risk populations live in order to reach beneficiaries in a safe way. Further, the project will promote community-based strategies and practices among affected populations to provide protection for the most vulnerable community members (children, PLW, especially IDPs and disabled). The project will engage men and women leaders of affected populations to take responsibility for mobilizing protection for disadvantaged and vulnerable individuals, so that they have unimpeded access to nutrition services. To adhere to the principles of “Do-No-Harm”, the project will strive to deliver services in a balanced manner so that IDP and host community populations, regardless of ethnicity or population group, will have equal access to project services. To achieve this balance, CMA will implement a strong awareness campaign to engage both IDP and host community leaders. • 8.2:High Risk :Mitigation Measures: To address the reality that women, girls and boy with and without disabilities feel unsafe and are at risk of sexual violence and abuse, CMA will implement full engagement of the leaders of affected populations and host communities in community-based assessments for delivery of static, mobile and outreaches services, and apply the “do-no-harm” approach to reduce the risk sexual of exploitation and abuse. The project will support the implementation of safety audits at all static, mobile and outreach sites, and implement all recommended safety audit measures to improve safe access to nutrition services. The safety audits will be led by CMA Safety Audit Focal Person, and implemented by female staff members in respect of commonly practiced protection protocols. These measures will ensure access and minimize exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to project assistance. • 8.4:High Risk: Mitigation Measures: To address the need for available and effective protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents, all of CMA’s project sites will be linked to health services where CRM support will be provided for victims of SGBV and psychological support and treatment for the trauma experienced by child survivors of abuse and conflict and the survivors of SGBV. Through public promotion and community awareness sessions both those receiving treatment and the wider community will be made aware of the appropriate referral pathways for the services that survivors need. Further, local authorities and community leaders will be engaged on issues around child protection and protection of the most vulnerable elderly and disabled and made aware of available protection assistance and the GBV referral pathway for CMR and psychological support for survivors of SGBV. • 8.5:High Risk :Mitigation Measures: Exclusion of marginalized community’s/minority groups from consultations and decision making on project approaches and interventions will be mitigated by adopting strategies and mechanisms through which affected populations can receive (1) the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing project services, (2) provide feedback on the project interventions, share concerns and submit complaints, and (3) by working directly with leaders of affected beneficiaries and through local authorities and community leaders. As women, girls, boys, the disabled and elderly are primary targets of this project, and to ensure they are all included in consultations and decision-making, CMA will organize specific forums (Key Informants, Focus Group Discussions) and meetings to engage these vulnerable groups.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The health project will be implemented along with nutrition services in both Fangak and Nyirol counties to ensure timely and inclusive medical care, mental health and psychosocial support are available to target populations especially at risk women and GBV survivors. Where Protection partners are co-located within the county and operating in the vicinity of this project’s catchment area, CMA will seek the assistance of these partners to support women at risk and GBV survivors with legal matters. Where Protection partners are not available to assist in a direct way, CMA will refer women who need protection services to capable service providers and assist these vulnerable women and adolescent girls to access required legal, mental health and psychosocial services.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Fangak and Nyirol counties are classified in IPC 4 Emergency for food insecurity. Further, UNOCH estimates that 18.2% of the population of Fangak and 19.0% of population in Nyirol will face IPC 5 Catastrophic by April 2021 (South Sudan HNO 2021, pg 34). The same strategy of an integrated approach of implementing health alongside nutrition will be adopted in order to mitigate the effects of food security which is likely to lead to IPC 3,4 and 5.Toward decreasing the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5, the project will serve an estimated population of 29,652 with specialized nutritious foods (67% of caseload in targeted counties), 23,795 with SAM and MAM treatments of children under age 5 (59% of county caseloads) and 9,735 pregnant and lactating women, including women with disabilities, with MAM treatments (46 % of county caseloads). These population targets include those facing IPC 5 Catastrophic conditions. On the other hand, through the proposed inter-sectoral coordination and linkage, CMA through this project will closely monitor the situation and advocate for immediate scale-up of humanitarian food assistance which will be needed to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the affected payams particularly those with populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4).

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This health project will be implemented along with Nutrition and WASH sector programs in an integrated approach providing the core set of basic services and with a focus on reaching the most vulnerable (women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons) through the combination of static service, outreach from static PHCC based service and the mobile service approach where required. CMA will adopt an inter-sectoral coordination mechanism at all levels which is envisaged to work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other sectors including nutrition, food security and livelihoods, WASH, and protection implementing partners. Further, the project will link with FSL and Protection partners where they are co-located with CMA. Even where FSL and Protection partners are not co-located within the vicinity of this project’s catchment area, CMA will support food security with kitchen gardens, support protection with safety audits, other basic protection services and community-based protection approaches to enable access to nutrition services. Through these measures, CMA will ensure that the complete spectrum essential cross-sectoral basic services reach the populations targeted in this project.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Fangak and Nyirol counties are classified as severity level 4 with and in IPC 4 Critical for malnutrition. It is a known fact that food production is dependent on a healthy population which is able to participate effectively in agricultural production. To ensure that production is not affected, this project will endeavour to reduce morbidity and mortality from many communicable diseases which make most people lethargic and unproductive. Similarly, in order to protect both patient and highly needed health staff, this project will encourage and support full implementation of IPC/WASH measures including COVID-19 prevention and management activities as COVID-19 is expected to have a long-term impact on, not only health service provision, but also food security. Furthermore, CMA will adopt an inter-sectoral coordination mechanism at all levels which is envisaged to work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other sectors including nutrition, food security and livelihoods, WASH, and protection implementing partners. Indeed, Nutrition Cluster data (16 Feb 21) shows the proxy GAM rate is 20.27% in Fangak, and 23.12% in Nyirol. In the proposed integration of health and nutrition, the latter sector will be at the forefront of addressing this problem. Therefore, towards decreasing the global acute malnutrition among children under age 5, CMA proposed nutrition project estimates show that in Fangak, it will reach 4,270 children under age 5 with SAM treatments and 6,566 with MAM treatment (59% of county case load). In Nyirol, the project will reach 4,210 children under age 5 with SAM treatments and 8,749 with MAM treatment (81% of county case load). Further, the project will support the delivery of specialized nutritious foods to under-five girls and boys provided reaching an estimated population of 29,652 (67% of caseload in targeted counties).
