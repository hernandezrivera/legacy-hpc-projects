Project Summary

WV directly and with IPs will offer a multifaced project in continuation of existing and expanding in NWS to support the most vulnerable in response to needs, including CwD in HC and camps between the ages of 6-17, by providing equitable access to quality and protective learning prospects to girls and boys in both FE and in NFE through ALP, Remedial, Catch-up, BLN classes and SLP. Additionally, nutrition, CVA, protection and WaSH will be integrated. 7 areas with the highest severity rates (4&5) as per HNO 2023 will be targeted: Maaret Tamsrin, Dana, Bennsh, Armanaz, Salqin, Harim, Atareb, covering 50 (13 new locations) as this is where populations & needs are highest.
In IDP camps, NFE provided to 1445 females and 1446 males including 40 CwDs. In 25 formal schools, we’ll support 3000 female and 2550 male students including 100 CwDs. 981 teachers, 29,441 14441 male & 15000 female students will benefit from SPHERE aligning and CWD-considerate rehab, construction and heating for schools in 120 schools that were identified through a comprehensive WV needs assessment.
We’ll continue to work with and recruit local communities to ensure apt education services are due at a level that meets the cluster and authorities’ standards, which include local outreach, a quality education in formal and non-formal settings with lifelong learning chances with a special focus on CwD. 13100 of parents (F 8100 M 5000) targeted in awareness sessions on the importance of education, nutrition, health, hygiene and CP. In compliance with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles, achieving improved learning outcomes through conducting BTL campaigns to encourage children to return and be retained in education while parents prioritise education. The sessions will be complemented by distributing learning supplies. We’ll also provide training to teachers and facilitators on F2F and distant ed, programmatic support throughout the project cycle. OOSC will be bridged back to formal education based on the term’s scores. Holistically, structured and semi-structured PSS sessions using WV’s models and expertise will be offered by training education staff (TiCC- PSS- SEL- EiE- CSG) in the established/rehabilitated CFSs at formal and non-formal settings in all of the targeted locations, to enhance the resilience, coping mechanism and life skills of children, in addition to mine risk awareness sessions, hygiene promotion education, case identification and referrals for most vulnerable cases in need of services through the protection team and external pathways.
Ensuring the continuity of learning, adapting plan B for distance learning due to COVID or escalation in the security situation, where we’ll be contingent on sufficient pre-existing equipment (independent contribution) from previous education projects (tablets, cameras, laptops, internet equipment, teaching tools-valued at $80,000). The project will provide heating materials (gas heaters in tents, fuel and heaters for block building schools). All projects will provide drinking and washing water, support to operational costs, cleaning materials, sanitisers and solar power systems as an electricity source in the rehabilitation works. Security equipment for education settings (first aid kits and fire extinguishers) given after training staff on first aid and emergency preparedness.
PTAs and SMC established to consult, inform of updates along with teachers' support circles as experience sharing and care. Project will offer incentives to teachers, facilitators, guards, cleaners and admin staff as an important factor in securing stable education process and retention of teachers while securing jobs and income for about 348 families. WV and IPs will facilitate experience exchange workshops with Aleppo and Idlib Education Directorate education personnel (30 Aleppo/70 Idlib) to improve the level of coordination and help build cooperation and partnership with the local councils and camp management.

3 - Needs

HNO 2023 confirms great needs for WV’s interventions, leaving 7000 where 87% of PiN in camps in NWS. 470 confirmed attacks on education and 60 verified instances of military use of schools until Aug 2022(WoS: Attacks on Edu). Lowest region (21%) of HH members working. Existing services are unable to physically accommodate or meet the different learning needs of adolescents, IDPs, returnees and children with specific needs (2022 MSNA). With an estimated 18% of school age children not attending any form of learning, mostly boys, OOSC remain one of the most affected groups. Over 3/4 of 12-17year-olds who were not in school had dropped out. 20% of schools in NWS are destroyed, the largest proportion of non-functional schools were in Idlib constituting 13% of all schools assessed (Schools-in-Syria_IMU- Aug. 2022). Safety and security concerns remain a gendered issue as women and girls continue disproportionately being affected by various forms of violence. Children will bear the greatest responsibility for reconstruction and growth when the conflict ceases. More than half of households with children in school don’t feel their children are learning sufficiently. NWS MSNA in Oct. 2022 by WV aimed to assess education needs and priorities and identify the key barriers to accessing education revealed %78.5 did not receive education support through last year, education costs reported as the major challenge faced by respondents in securing their children’s education.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will be executed by WV directly and through IPs to meet the global shift toward ER. The project will focus on establishing SMCs which will effectively link the school leadership, learners, and parents, ensuring community level ownership. Schools will be supported in full package (CWD, rehab, heating, safety & security, salaries, etc.). Education staff will gain skills through ongoing capacity building to lead education programming innovatively. Structural barriers to accessing education will be addressed such as operational and HH-level costs (CfE). BTL campaigns will be conducted by the IP and WV through F2F and virtual awareness messages, provision of learning supplies (school bags, stationery, textbooks, replenishment stationery each three months), in addition to administering teaching tools, kits, resources and guidelines. WV will be providing FE at a much higher reach than NFE compared to 2022 as the students have transitioned into FE now. Facilitators will receive recreational kits for conducting PSS activities at formal and non-formal settings. WV will coordinate with the Idlib & Aleppo EDs to facilitate transferring students to FE, termly. We will facilitate experience exchange workshops with both EDs in order to improve the level of coordination between the Directorate and local humanitarian actors while strengthening the IP’s position with the directorates.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

*Members of the community or local authorities may try to over-influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of support provided to education services or who will benefit from the support provided. To mitigate, the project is designed to be context and conflict sensitive, the community is consulted through the project's life and the relevant authorities are aware of the selection and procurement procedures.*Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, where WV has active policies in place to prevent this and staff and communities have access to safe reporting systems. *Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organisation staff or related entities, where WV has CSG policies and systems in place to maintain a safe learning environment, ensure regular PSS is provided, teachers and staff are regularly trained on positive discipline. Policies cover all people interacting with children.*Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements. WV will provide sufficient safe & clean bathroom facilities that are sex and age segregated and accessible to CWD.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Our absolute priority is protecting children, vulnerable communities and our own staff. Zero tolerance of incidents of violence or abuse against children and other people in the communities we serve, either committed by our staff or others connected to our work. WV abhors any misuse of power, status, or trusted position for any exploitative purposes. Continuous improvement of safeguarding efforts, which emphasises PSEA and other forms of violence or harm, is at the top of our agenda. We have mechanisms to enable communities, beneficiaries, and staff to report potential cases of abuse. Frontline staff are trained on how to recognise and respond to issues of SEA. We provide a confidential Whistleblower hotline. We continually monitor and update our policies to be accountable and improved. We believe we have a moral obligation to maintain the highest ethical standards in our work. The Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy expand upon WV’s standards, to cover all vulnerable people in one integrated policy. Community consultations used in the design of the project ensured adequate representation of WGBM. Female staff and enumerators are ensured for facilitation. We ensure local IPs follow measures and maintain gender representation amongst staff. Activities have been developed sensitively & consider Do No Harm (DNH) principles. To ensure a DNH Approach, protection is mainstreamed in all response areas & all staff have been trained in protection mainstreaming and the WV policies.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Learning facilities could be attacked in the ongoing unstable armed conflict in NWS. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, fire, theft, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. The project will be located within centralised learning facilities to decrease travel and consider daylight hours. Additionally, some facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including cholera. Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WaSH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages. Unsafe learning spaces and insufficient/non-existent WaSH facilities contribute to children not attending learning facilities. Sufficient water and sanitation facilities will be provided and improved structures to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected.WV has also factored in programmatic contingencies in Project design (ability to relocate projects and/or re-programme activities).At the wider institutional level, members of the community or local authorities may try to influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of provided support and the project may face pressure on selection, hiring or procurement processes. Delay or non-approvals of planned activities or planned processes.
