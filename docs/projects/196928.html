Project Summary

The project aims at supporting the decision makings of stakeholders operating in NES through the provision of evidence-based and data-driven HMA analysis. Firstly, iMMAP will strengthen the HMA coordination mechanism by leading a formal Mine Action sub working group under the NGO forum umbrella. It will coordinate with the inter sector working group to mainstream HMA into the rehabilitation and reconstruction response. In parallel, this action will aim at consolidating, processing and disseminating data collected from the HMA actors in NES to support decision makings and optimize the activities' impacts. At last, iMMAP will lead the coordination of a NES wide NTS in order to assess the scale and impact of the contamination along with providing an estimation of resources needed to eradicate the blockages caused by EO in NES.

3 - Needs

Explosive Ordnance (IEDs, landmines, ERWs) in Northeast Syria contamination continues to pose a great danger to population and hinder economic and social development. Based in the 2022 MSNA, one in three communities is reported to be contaminated by Explosive Ordnance. iMMAP reported that 22 million m² are confirmed or suspected to be contaminated in addition to 800 single hazards (source IMSMA data base managed by iMMAP since 2018). These figures are believed to be underestimated as up to now no large scale contamination survey (NTS) has been undertaken in the region. Coordinated efforts and Information Management are key to optimize and prioritize Humanitarian Mine Action activities. Through this action, iMMAP will lead the HMA coordination under the NGO forum umbrella, to avoid duplication of efforts and gaps, link implementing partners with the authorities, manage the HMA information management system and coordinate with the NGO forum to integrate the HMA component in the wider response. In addition, it will support in improving available knowledge on the full extent of the contamination in NES and its impact at community level. This will enable stakeholders to develop operational plans and set up a prioritization system for clearance efforts, along with informing community leaders about the current contamination status of their administration.

4 - Implementation of activities

To reach the expected outcome, the action will implement the sector MASWG developed strategy along with implementing (via a consortium with 3 HMA partners) a large scale contamination survey (NTS) in NES. As such, iMMAP will lead the Mine Action sub working group by coordinating meetings and ensuring its integration within the NGO forum planning and strategy. It will also support HMA partners with IM and GIS trainings along with managing directly the HMA Information Management for NES. Additionally, the action seeks to contribute to sustainable solutions by enhancing synergic cooperation Referral mechanism will be developed with the FSL sector for pre and post clearance along with Health sector for EO victims assistance and Education sector to mainstream EO Risk Education in schools. iMMAP will additionally implement with a consortium, a NES wide NTS to better understand the scale of the contamination at community level and its impact to inform a yearly response plan. To do so, gender balanced NTS teams will be recruited and trained to implement the survey in 972 communities across the region. MAG will in addition support the action by surveying 571 communities. One HMA partners and non HMA partners survey will be developed and disseminated to measure the project performance (baseline realized in September 2022 for HMA partners). In addition, iMMAP has developed an online (power bi) platform tracking the MASWG indicators performance and NTS outputs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project does not count direct beneficiaries amongst the population. The main beneficiaries are HMA organizations and other stakeholders that are using iMMAPs data driven analysis to take informed decisions. iMMAPs employees work from offices in Amman (Jordan), Amuda (NES) and Erbil (KRI). Some Field visits would be undertaken to meet with partners. Movements are covered by iMMAPs security guidelines.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

One of the main risk identified by iMMAP during the project design, lays in potential harassment caused by other iMMAP members, HMA's partners or by the authorities (NESMAO). To minimize this risk, iMMAP is enforcing its internal Global PSEA policy dating from December 2020. All iMMAP staff members are trained on this policy while signing their employment contract. A complaint mechanism is set up in all iMMAP missions, that staff or partners can raise to alert on a misconduct.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation remains highly volatile, hampering the access to efficiently survey and clear all hazardous areas in the region. The recrudescence of the conflict between different actors would result in additional Explosive Ordnance contamination. Already faced with funding shortages to tackle the current contamination, HMA partners would struggle to extend their operations in case of additional needs. The general NTS planned with this action will aim at developing a prioritized and optimized clearance plan for the next 3 to 5 years in NES. This plan will detail the resources required on a yearly basis and will be disseminated to donors (advocating for additional funding).
