Project Summary

The project focuses on:
- building capacities of community leadership and rehabilitation of local infrastructure in conflict-affected communities in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts to address social and economic needs through tailored Community Based Initiatives; and
- provision of comprehensive support to young people from underprivileged backgrounds who attend technical colleges in remote conflict-affected areas of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts; aimed at strengthening their resilience and self-confidence on their path to adulthood.

Needs

The conflict in Ukraine erupted in April 2014 and is still highly volatile. According to the latest OHCHR Report from August 2018 on the human rights situation in Ukraine, the active conflict in the east continues disrupting civilian life and causing deaths, injuries and destruction. The periodic spikes and lulls in civilian deaths and injuries reflect the volatile situation and sustained atmosphere of insecurity and anxiety that characterises civilian life on both sides of the contact line. An estimated 4.4 million people are affected by the Ukraine crisis today, and of these, approximately 3.4 million are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. The lack of jobs and livelihoods means that people are being forced to stretch already limited resources. Many are faced with impossible choices as to whether to prioritise shelter, food, medicine or heating. Limited access to stable incomes, psychosocial and other forms of support, leads conflict-affected populations to negative coping mechanisms, such as removing children from school, displacement, crime, and survival sex. From a community prospective, this leads to weakened social bonds and cohesion which in turn may negatively affect the process of recovery from the consequences of the conflict. Critical civilian infrastructure located in proximity to the contact line continue to come under shelling, light weapons and small arms fire. This includes private homes, but also water facilities, schools, and other public infrastructure. To address the complexity of the issues above, community-based initiatives have proven to have strong positive effects on the population on their path to self-recovery by putting the capacities, rights and dignity at the center of programming and using a community-based approach to address their specific protection concerns. Community-based initiatives support community protection and cohesion in different directions prioritized by communities which often contributes to social and economic development. Further from the ‘contact line’, hundreds of thousands of students and educators require support to cope with the impact of the conflict. An estimated 703,000 students (approx. 49% are female) and teachers (most of them are female) in more than 3,500 education facilities are also suffering from the cumulative psychological impacts. The conflict continues to endanger the physical and psychological wellbeing of children which may lead to long-term psychosocial problems among children and adolescents. According to the results of internal/small scale assessment conducted by DRC in April 2018 with adolescents of 14-17 years old studying in urban and rural areas of Northern Donetsk oblasts, young people continue to face a number of risks outside home and schools in their everyday life. DRC Protection team supported by national consultant (child protection expert) consulted youth in interactive format on the risks they may face outside the school during the ongoing conflict and in its aftermath. The findings showed that young people see danger in many abandoned, unfinished buildings especially those served as former military base, stating that many young people are attracted to those. Young people expressed their concerns in regards to mines, ERW, and arms, as well as people in the state of substance abuse. Adolescents identify the danger as an external factor and have little knowledge on how to behave in risky situations: young people are not well informed about and how to be protected from sexual abuse and trafficking, do not know safety rules when using Internet, do not know how to behave when facing people with arms, how to behave in EECP or check points. It is interesting that majority of participants stated that in case of risky situations it is worth to risk if you benefit from it. To respond to the existing need, DRC developed Safe living manual as a new resource materials for teachers and students, covering a broader range of risks.

Activities & outcomes

Activity 1: Support to eight community-based initiatives from conflict-affected Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts will be provided through funds and guidance on identification of their protection needs, development of response plan and implementation. Activity 2: Activity 1: DRC Mobile Youth Centre teams and partner organisation in in Donetsk oblast and Luhansk oblast will organise for adolescents training and information sessions on:o Life-skills focused psychosocial support (PSS) (based on the tested DRC module which received official licensing from the Ministry of Education for authorised use in all educational establishments). All materials and events will be adapted to ensure accessibility for young people with disabilities, as appropriate.o Safe living - to promote safety and security of adolescents. The module was developed by DRC-DDG in response to the requests from young people on the risks faced by them in conflict-affected areas.o Legal awareness and rights information sessions, focusing on legal issues and rights relevant to technical college students – based on consultations with them and a currently implemented series of sessions designed by DRC Legal Assistance Unit in a youth-friendly format).o Job preparation skills training (including writing CVs; searching and applying for a job; preparing for job interviews; and financial literacy, including managing utilities and personal bills) provided by DRC and partner organisation in cooperation with DRC’s Livelihood Unit.o Basic business skills trainings (including developing a business plan, writing a business proposal, basics of small-business management). DRC Protection and Livelihoods Units will also work together to link students with DRC Livelihoods vocational training (VAT) grants and small-business grants. Activity 3: Project development skills training for youth-led initiatives will be provided for young people – to enable students to continue promoting youth initiatives beyond the duration of this project. Activity 4: Youth-led social initiatives will be developed and implemented by students at targeted colleges with support and guidance and resources provided by DRC and implementing partner. Through this activity young people will be able to make meaningful positive changes for their peers and community. The modality of youth-led initiatives implies provision by DRC and partner organisation of required equipment and goods (according to the estimates) for the young people to successfully implement project ideas. Activity 5: Training for teachers from targeted colleges in working with young people in conflict-affected areas, using a developed and tested curriculum aimed at preventing emotional burnout, supporting teachers to understand adolescents’ behaviour and reduction of conflicts between youth and teachers. The trainings for teachers will be conducted by partner organisation operating in ten selected technical colleges. Activity 6: Training of trainers (ToT) for youth workers from Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts youth centres (representatives of other oblasts will be welcome to participate for experience sharing) in DRC Safe living and/or Lifeskills/PSS curriculum to enable them to use it in their work with young people. The ToTs will be provided by DRC Protection Unit. Activity 7: Sustainable youth friendly spaces will be established on the basis of technical colleges to create opportunities for young people in accessing safe space for meaningful leisure and development.

Indicators & targets

DRC Protection team will at all means ensure accountability to affected people and communities at all stages of the project cycle. Guided by the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) which goes in line with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability, DRC will use specific mechanisms as part of the six elements to ensure accountability to affected population. Leadership/Governance: DRC has a code of conduct that specifically addresses sexual exploitation and abuse. All DRC staff is well informed and educated regarding DRC’s commitment to preventing the sexual exploitation and abuse of the people it seeks to assist by anyone associated with the organisation or its partners. Transparency/Information Sharing: DRC collaborates with other stakeholders, such as peer agencies, clusters, partners and local authorities, to fulfil this commitment. DRC will ensure that communities always know who they are dealing with, their role, and for which organisation they work. Feedback and Complaints: DRC has a policy and guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback mechanisms. The guidance defines and outlines the purpose and limitations of the mechanism, the steps involved in processing and responding to complaints, what to do with sensitive complaints, and addresses confidentiality and non-retaliation issues. Local communities are/were consulted as to how they would best like to submit complaints. There are clear procedures in place to address allegations of abuse and the expertise to handle and investigate allegations of abuse, including sexual abuse and corruption, exists in DRC. Participation: DRC ensures that all interest groups have a voice, including women, children, the aged, minority cultural groups and people living with disabilities. DRC also ensures that community participation takes place at all stages of project cycle. The ‘Do No Harm’ framework is employed with the participation of stakeholders, to ensure that the DRC’s interventions will not exacerbate conflict. Design, Monitoring and Evaluation: Programme design is based on an analysis of the specific needs and risks faced by different groups of people. There is policy on monitoring and evaluation and integration of learning within DRC. DRC collaborates and shares information and knowledge with other organisations and partners. Working with Partners and Other Stakeholders: DRC works effectively in coordination and collaboration with others, including both UN and non-UN actors. DRC has a clear partner selection process that reflects its commitments to accountability. The quality and effectiveness of partnerships are regularly reviewed in collaboration with the partners. A total of 2600 individuals will benefit from the proposed interventions in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (GCA). Project indicators are presented below as follows:o Eight community based initiatives implemented benefiting approximately 250 persons per community (value: up to 5,000 USD) in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts;o 300 adolescent girls and boys trained in psychosocial support focused life skills;o 50 teachers at targeted technical colleges trained in PSS (disaggregated by sex);o 250 adolescent girls and boys benefiting from ten youth-led community-based initiatives implemented in technical colleges and/or local communities;o 10 technical college communities equipped with sustainable facilities (youth friendly spaces) for constructive recreational activities for at-risk youth;o 80% of targeted adolescents report improved ability to deal with challenges and adversity as a result of DRC interventions (disaggregated by sex) o Post activity monitoring will be implemented for up to 10% of the beneficiaries to measure the impact of the activities on the situation of the beneficiaries and better inform future intervention.
