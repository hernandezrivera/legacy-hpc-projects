Project Summary

The Employability and Apprenticeship Livelihood Support for Urban Youths in Northeast Nigeria is an IRC project targeted at providing support on 2 key livelihoods pillars: Employability Support and vocational apprenticeships. On the first pillar, youth will be supported with tailored employability training which will focus on workplace preparedness, personal and professional development skills, as well as CV/Resume writing and interviewing skills training. Under the second pillar, participants without adequate educational training to seek formal employment received vocational training and were linked to local vocational trade artisans to understudy various vocational trades. This group of youths will be further supported with start-up kits to commence their trades.
The program will target youths between the ages of 18-35 years in urban centes where IDP’s have been integrated into host community settlement with minimal livelihood opportunities.
The project will focus on five LGA’s cutting across the BAY states namely: Jere and MMC in Borno, Yola North and Yola South LGA’s in Adamawa and Damaturu LGA in Yobe State.
The project will leverage on key Government, private sector local partners to deliver both theoretical training as well as practical apprenticeships.
A total of 400 youths will be targeted will be targeted in these areas to receive livelihood support under this intervention. 150 will be targeted under the Employability track and 250 youths will be targeted for the vocational training and apprenticeship support.

1 - Needs

The project will address the needs associated with safe and sustainable livelihood opportunities in the urban centres particularly with the influx of IDPs from rural areas. The project will also speak to the difficulty faced by displaced youths in living among host community members who are not in formal employment and do not own or are to able to own businesses by providing them opportunity be be equipped for vocational trade through training and apprenticeships. In addition, the gap in formal vocational training is another issue to be addressed by the project.

2 - Activities

The overall project objective is that vulnerable youth refugees, IDPs, and host community members impacted by displacement crises in urban contexts have access to livelihoods opportunities that safely and sustainably meet their needs and contribute positively to their communities. The project will deliver activities around two expected results in order to achieve the project objective: 1. Youth participants are prepared for alternative labor market opportunities.For some youth starting and running a microenterprise is not a viable option for safe and sustainable income generation. The project will focus on supporting a groups of unemployed youth to engage the labor market in alternative ways, including:- Job counselling – One common barrier for youth is understanding their suitability and preparedness for labor market opportunities. The project will provide youths with SWOT analysis basis on their individual profiles and guide them on career path options where they would thrive. - Under this program, youth will also have access to employment support services such as, job search skills, CV skills, interview skills, and one-on-one job counselling to help break down the information barrier. 2. Vocational Training and Apprenticeships – On-the-job training is an essential component to preparing youth for employment in a hands-on, interactive, and concrete way. Selected youth participants will receive structured vocational training and be matched with apprenticeship opportunities with local microenterprises in order to gain valuable learning experience while on the job, and paid a modest market-rate stipend to cover the opportunity costs of participation. Upon successful completion of the apprenticeship, they would be provided with start-up kits to commence their various vocational trades. Additionally, IRC will emphasize community engagement sessions in order to inform affected communities (both displaced and host residents) of IRC’s presence in their neighborhoods, sharing information, and promoting social cohesion and accountability.
