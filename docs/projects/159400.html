Project Summary

The availability of basic health services in health facilities are limited, a situation exacerbated by low governance and scarce resources. Many areas do not even have any access to health services. OCHA’s 2019 Humanitarian Need Overview reported Somalia has less than 40% of its target of two health facilities per 10,000 population and has only 19% of its target skilled health workforce. Furthermore, communities may not be aware of the availability of, and/or have confidence in the health services; and even if they do, they usually lack awareness to identify health danger signs and seek appropriate care in good time. Limited knowledge of appropriate health practices at household level also increases the health risks of preventable causes. OCHA estimated 2 out of 5 people are in need of urgent life-saving health service throughout 2019 (Nov 2018). Except for limited access to health care, the quality of services appeared poor.
Somalia is reported to have the world’s highest child mortality rate and faces the sixth highest lifetime maternal death risk in the world despite most of those are preventable (World Bank, 2017). One out of every 22 Somalia women is likely to die due to pregnancy or childbirth-related causes during her life course; the under 5 mortality rate (U5MR) of 137 per 1,000 live births is presently the third worst in the world. According to the OCHA 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview – Somalia (Nov 2018), approximately 45% of death among children under five takes place in the neonatal period. Many diseases that contribute to high morbidity and mortality are vaccine preventable yet nearly 54% of health facilities do not provide an “expanded programme on immunization services”, driving coverage below 50% (OCHA, Nov 2018). DPT3 coverage is well under 50% in 2014. Measles-containing vaccine first dose coverage in recent year is estimated only at 46% across Somalia.
Communicable diseases such as AWD/ cholera and measles continues to be a common and recurring health threat to Somalia. From January 2019 to August 2019, a cumulative total of 169 725 cases of AWD/ Cholera were reported from health facilities to the early warning, alerted network (EWARN). Also, a total of 3402 suspected measles cases were reported, of which 502 were during August 2019 in Banadir region. The need for health service provision will also become high in the coming months. REACH’s assessment established 84% of the interviewed households were reported having child or adult with specific health problem such as malaria, stomach pain, AWD and fever 30 days prior to the assessment; sadly, 27% of them reported limited capacity to access health facility to respond to the issue.
The principle of Do-No-Harm approach is used during the entire project cycle. Community (men, women and youth) will be involved and will be fully participate in all project cycle. HIJRA is planning to reach and and provide basic health care services to women (90168), Men (86632), under five children (Girls 53199 and Boys 59113) and through the provision of primary health care services, strengthening of the referral system and increasing community awareness/collaboration by static health facilities and mobile clinic in target areas. HIJRA will coordinate other actors in the area to avoid overlapping and will also be used referral for cases so as to be complementary each other and to maximize impact.
HIJRA in collaboration with MoH plans to implement the project in Banadir, lower Shabelle and Bakool through already existing community health and community structures while leveraging of the already existing programs to ensure complementary and avoid the duplication of effort and resources. The project objective is increase access to emergency lifesaving primary services particularly IDPs and host communities with emphasis on disease prevention, treatment and health system strengthen in Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Bay and Bakool Regions.
