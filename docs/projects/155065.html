Project Summary

The projects seeks to offer basic education activities to adolescent girls and young women living in the makeshift settlements in Cox's Bazar. Women and girls will be reached through activities in a Women and Girls Safe Space as well as through home-based learning. Additionally, Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities parallel to the education sessions will ensure that beneficiaries can concentrate on their own learning.
Parenting sessions with children's caretakers will complete the approach.
Community involvement as well as capacity building of facilitators (national and refugee) are an integral part of the project.
A total of 1900 people will benefit form the proposed project, among them 1740 directly through education activities (Sector Objective 1).

Needs

In total, Bangladesh hosts over 900.000 Rohingya refugees .The scale, severity and complexity of needs across the hazardous conditions in the camps remain overwhelming and services, including education for adolescent and youth continue to be insufficient. The refugees are highly vulnerable with many being traumatized and facing continuous exposure to a multitude of protection risks. Specifically women and girls have been exposed to sexual- and gender-based violence and protection risks are further compounded by the lack of appropriate shelters, lack of female-only latrines and bathing spaces and damaging coping strategies such as child marriage have been applied. Combined with movement restrictions for female refugees and cultural practices this greatly impacts access to education services – if available. The need for specific programming for adolescent girls and young women was identified by the Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA). Results and recommendation show that mixed learning groups (boys and girls), lack of female staff and fear of harassment and gender-based violence (GBV) on the way to facilities are barriers for girls. However, as there are no activities offered yet, this feedback has to be considered as perceptions of the community and possible future barriers. Programming for adolescent girls and young women needs to address the mentioned challenges.According to UNHCR’s Multi-sectoral needs assessment 54% of the refugee population is younger than 18 years and youth (15-24) comprise 17% or the population. At the same time only 2% of 15 to 17 year old girls attend learning centres. Data from sector partners show, that only 190 18-24 year olds access education and clearly shows the great need for meaningful education programming for 15-24 year olds across camps.

Activities or outputs

Brief overview of activities targeting adolescent girls and female youth:• Lessons covering basic literacy and numeracy, life skills and skills training – including psycho-social Support (PSS).• Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities• Parenting sessions and awareness raising on the right to education• Capacity building of national facilitators and refugee volunteer teachers • Set up and regular meetings of community committees.Adolescent girls and young women will receive basic literacy and numeracy and life skills sessions (including PSS). Additionally small scale skills training will be conducted. The project will be delivered in Women and Girls Safe Spaces alongside gender-based violence interventions. Given the limited space in the camps and considering the barriers to access facilities for female refugees, home-based learning sessions will be conducted in female-headed households. This also increases the possibility for girls with disability to participate in education activities. Activities are of course aligned with the INEE Minimum Standards, IASC guidelines and Sector Standards. As many beneficiaries are mothers or big sisters themselves, ECD activities will be implemented in parallel. Beneficiaries have reported to worry about their children while they are attending class. Hence, the ECD activities will will not only benefit the children but also their caretakers as it will allow them to fully concentrate on their own.The project also plans to start sessions with caretakers on positive parenting and will work closely with the community to ensure needs are met. Additionally, DCA aims to raise awareness on girls’ education and its protective benefits. As staff will work closely with Gender-based Violence (GBV) colleagues, training will include topics such as Psychological First Aid (PFA) and PSS as well as referral pathways to ensure identified women and girls have access to protection services. Furthermore, contextualized teacher training will be delivered and benefit staff and volunteers. A mentoring approach will ensure that refugee volunteer teachers can slowly take on the lead on implementation. This also ensures community ownership and utilized contextualized knowledge of the community. To inform the project design, Focus Group Discussions will be conducted with adolescent girls and youth as well as volunteer teachers. Based on the results collected, the proposed project will be designed. Additionally feedback from participants of a current project will be collected to ensure that lessons learned are transferred.
