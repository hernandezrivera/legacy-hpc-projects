Project Summary

UNHCR will continue to explore possible shelter interventions to improve the physical living conditions for the refugees on the household and on the communal level taking into account age, gender and diversity and the specific needs of individuals across all camps under UNHCR's AoR.
We will continue to provide basic household items for new arrivals and replenish for families who received them more than a year ago. Distribution of LPG and adequate cooking equipment will continue.
In addition UNHCR will ensure prepositioning of emergency shelter materials and NFI's as contingency for disasters, including monsoon and high wind.

Needs

The varous reports and assessments done in 2019 are quite clear with regards to the lack of sustainability in the materials and construction techniques used for the majority of the shelters in the camps. 71% of Rohingya households (MSNA 2019) report roofs leaking. 46% report rotten or damaged bamboo. Solutions are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to insect attack. The topography and soil composition also demand interventions on a household level to prevent slope failures and improve access. Despite the widespread distribution of portable solar lights, 40% of households (MSNA 2019) say they have no functioning solar light. Only 20% of households possess two or more portable lights. These data highlight a need for high quality, durable portable lighting. The Rohingya refugee’s main safety concerns are a) unstable structure, b) shelter deterioration, and c) no adequate internal lighting (NPM R15). Insufficient supply of all the required NFIs has also been observed. 88% of households (MSNA 2019) state they exclusively cook with LPG. The need now is to reach 100% and ensure continuous refills. Precarious tenure arrangements and land dispute continue to impede the provision of shelter assistance, protection response and social cohesion between the refugees and host community.

Activities or outputs

UNHCR's AGD policy seeks to ensure that refugees enjoy their rights. It is linked to UNHCR's rights-based and community-based approach. Accountability to affected population (AAP) is at the centre of UNHCR's work, as set out in the AGD policy, and implemented through community-based, participatory approaches that are well-established. Home visits for those unable to access registration and protection will ensure outreach to all individuals in need. The AGD policy requires actors to adopt methodologies for engagement with individuals and different groups which aim at empowering individuals and communities in decisions that affect their lives. UNHCR’s shelter interventions will aim at improving the durability and safety of refugees’ shelters. We will advocate for more durable shelters through camp upgrades and shift to a resilient approach. The households who have not received a mid-term shelter will receive needs-based repair and maintenance assistance. Under the “50% Minimum standards”-indicator we are assuming 90% of the households will need materials worth 50 USD. Both the percentage and the unit cost will be scaled up or down depending on the identified need. UNHCR will develop further the shift from equal distribution of materials to targeted and needs-based distribution to the individual household.To counter the lack of space for shelter and prospects for additional land, UNHCR will continue to seek solutions to address congestion while improving living standards in collaboration with the refugees and taking into consideration the specific and perhaps special needs of boys, girls, women and men at the household level. Mid-term shelter development will include a plot development, and where possible, two-story shelters. We will also explore alternative interventions that may improve living conditions at the individual household level, the neighbourhood level and the community level. UNHCR has established channels of communication with affected populations, and they will be used to inform about procedures, structures and processes that affect them with regards to shelter interventions, so that the refugees are able to make informed decisions and choices. Monsoon and cyclone preparedness remains a priority. UNHCR will assess shelters and distribute shelter reinforcement material such as tie-down kits based on needs. UNHCR will also preposition emergency shelter/NFI assistance ready to be distributed in case of disaster. In 2020, UNHCR’s NFI interventions will aim at improving refugee’s safety and living conditions, and mitigate the impact on the environment and the host community. Based on assessments, the most vulnerable households will be prioritized for relevant assistance, including multi-purpose vouchers and seasonal assistance. UNHCR will provide new arrivals with essential household items such as kitchen set, buckets, blankets, sleeping mats, and a portable solar lamp. Refugee families who received these items more than a year ago will receive vouchers to replace items as needed. The collection, purchasing and selling of firewood is generating environmental and health risks, and protection risks especially for women and girls.Indoor cooking gives smoke in the main accommodation, which is a contributor to high rates of upper respiratory tract infections, especially for women and girls who are often confined indoors.In 2019 UNHCR worked closely with IOM in a commitment to reduce deforestation and lower HH emissions using LPG as a cleaner fuel. Both agencies are also piloting energy efficient measures using pressure cookers. An LPG assessment/ biomass survey is being conducted to highlight the effectiveness of LPG as a fuel. UNHCR will also provide refugee families with additional portable solar lighting to increase refugees’ security when moving at night. Adequate quantities of domestic fuel will be distributed for cooking needs, including to a number of vulnerable families in the host community.
