Project Summary

The context in Borno and Yobe states of large number of displaced persons particularly in camps on a background of weakened health systems increases the risk of spread of communicable diseases. For instance, in 2017, there was a large outbreak of cholera which affected more than 5,000 persons with 61 associated deaths mostly among internally displaced persons. Furthermore, as new areas are recaptured from non-state armed groups (NSAGs), many persons who were trapped in such areas return to many high risk areas for disease spread with low immunity to vaccine-preventable diseases as they have not received any vaccination in years. These, among other factors, predispose a large number of vulnerable persons to easily contract diseases in epidemic proportions. In order to ensure that mortality and morbidity from outbreaks are minimized, early warning systems are required to be set up to effectively detect and respond to any alerts of epidemic-prone diseases. The project, ‘Bridging Essential Health Service Gaps Among conflict-affected populations in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe States of North-east Nigeria is three-year project aimed at improving access to basic life-saving integrated health services in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe States. The project will work through Hard-to-Reach mobile medical teams, Community-oriented resource persons, and mental health focal points to reach 585,000 people in IDP camps and host communities in the project states. These include men, women, girls, and boys. Overall, the project will ensure continued provision of essential health services to conflict-affected people through deployment of H2R MHTs and implementation of iCCM while maintaining the specialized mental health care at the PHCs of Borno state.

Needs

According to figures from OCHA 2018 HNO, there are 5,100,000 people requiring humanitarian health assistance in Borno state. An inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessment which was conducted in July 2018 revealed that 1,600,000 people are displaced from the affected LGAs of the State. The 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) report of Borno assessed a total of 765 health facilities in the state and found 62% to be either partially or completely destroyed. The same report showed only 38% of the health facilities are fully functional, underlining the gap and the need for innovative approaches such as mobile health services to ensure access to basic health services. The conflict-affected population is burdened with easily preventable diseases such as malaria and diarrhea. According to the Integrated Disease surveillance and response reports of the rst half of 2018, the leading causes of morbidity in Borno were: Malaria, Diarrhea, along with severe acute malnutrition (SAM). This again underscores the apparent need for provision a package of basic health services to the affected population. The con ictaffected population particularly the most vulnerable groups such as children, are predisposed to illness and death because of malnutrition and poor environmental hygiene. Even though the nutrition situation in the insurgency-ravaged states (Borno, Yobe and Adamawa) showed some improvement, most of the domains still have the nutrition status in the 'poor to crisis' range. Results from Round 5 NFSS (April 2018) showed signi cant increase in prevalence of acute malnutrition by WHZ in some domains of Borno. Findings indicate that Borno State has a GAM rate of 9.1% and a SAM rate of 0.8%. Borno reported increased GAM from 11.6% in Nov 2017 to 14.1% in April (National Bureau of Statistics, UNICEF Nutrition and Food security surveillance, Round 5 April 2018) Humanitarian access to new areas is increasing, revealing signi cant humanitarian needs. Also, with the heightened military operations, the trend of new arrivals coming to accessible locations across the states has increased signi cantly, and at an alarming rate that might overwhelm humanitarian capacity. Recent SMART survey undertaken in August 2018 in Bama (one of the towns experiencing high influx of new arrivals) indicated higher GAM prevalence amongst new arrivals 37.5% (reception site) v. 7.8% (rest of camp) by WHZ. Similar situation is observed in other reception sites in Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kalabalge, Kukawa, Mobbar and Ngala, through the screening of new arrivals. Assessment conducted at the onset of the current cholera outbreak in Borno state documented, shortage of water and sanitary facilities to have contributed to the progressive increase in number of reported cases. Furthermore, there was a general lack of hygiene facilities and materials among IDP population. In conclusion, there is adequate evidence showing high prevalence of easily preventable diseases against a backdrop of high malnutrition rate and poor environmental hygiene. On the other hand, the existing capacity of the health delivery system and the actors in the states are limited.
