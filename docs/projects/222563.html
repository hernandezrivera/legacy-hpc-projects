Project Summary

The project focuses on improving access to and the quality of education in conflict-affected areas of Cabo Delgado Province, targeting Ancuabe, Chiure, Pemba, Metuge, Mocimboa da Praia, Ibo, Quissanga, and Palma. Benefiting 34,162 people in need, the initiative will employ a strategic approach to address educational challenges through two primary objectives: enhancing access and improving quality.
Objective 1: Improve Access
Efforts to enhance access to education will prioritize both children out of school and those facing learning challenges. Accelerated Education and Catch-up Programs will be implemented to support out-of-school children, while Remedial Programs will address gaps for in-school learners. To foster inclusivity, learning materials will be provided to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities, alongside essential supplies for teachers and school directors. Infrastructure will be supported through the construction of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS), and hygiene needs will be met by providing menstrual and general hygiene supplies. Additionally, Alternative Education Programs will equip adolescent girls and boys with life skills, vocational training, and basic literacy and numeracy, ensuring holistic development opportunities.
Objective 2: Improve Quality
The initiative seeks to strengthen the education system by training teachers in catch-up methodologies and effective pedagogy. This includes reinforcing catch-up and remedial courses and providing specialized training to enhance protection mechanisms within schools. Teachers, school councils, and parents will receive capacity building in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), life skills, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and addressing Violence Against Children (VAC) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Training on Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TICC), Inclusive Education, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) will further enhance educational resilience. Vulnerable children will also gain access to MHPSS services in schools, ensuring their well-being. Coordination with national and provincial Education Clusters will guarantee effective implementation and alignment with broader educational goals.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will integrate a protection mainstreaming approach to ensure the application of the Do No Harm principle, promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in all activities. To uphold these principles, all staff members will undergo comprehensive training, and community members will be sensitized to the relevant principles and procedures. They will also be required to sign key reference documents, including the Internal Code of Conduct, the Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy, the AVSI Child Safeguarding Policy, and the AVSI protection mainstreaming checklists.To mitigate potential conflicts between internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities, the criteria for selecting children will be defined collaboratively with community members. This participatory approach ensures that the vulnerability criteria reflect local realities and that diverse groups are represented in the consultations. Educational services will be designed to be inclusive, aligning with the principles of impartiality and neutrality. There will be no discrimination based on nationality, race, religion, social status, or political affiliation, and no sides will be taken in any disputes. Priority in the selection process will be given to children with disabilities, emphasizing equitable access to education.AVSI advocates for the comprehensive integration of Child Protection within the Education sector, advancing a strategy centered on multisectoral service delivery in both prevention and response programming. The core interventions are designed to address the needs of children aged 6 to 17, ensuring access to inclusive and quality learning opportunities alongside protective services. Additionally, the intervention offers adolescents and caregivers a diverse array of activities aimed at fostering individual development through human and technical skill-building, strengthening family bonds, and enhancing community support networks, all while providing essential psychosocial support.Child protection considerations will be incorporated across key activities, including teacher training on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and Violence Against Children (VAC), community sensitization campaigns, and the referral of child protection cases. Additionally, through ongoing projects, protection monitoring will be conducted as a routine activity. This will involve regular assessments of access to services for women, girls, boys, and men through spot checks and community discussions, with obstacles to equitable access promptly addressed.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

A participatory approach will be ensured throughout all phases of the project, including:• Needs assessment• Project implementation• Project monitoring• Project evaluationThe project will adhere to the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) outlined in the AVSI Foundation Accountability Policy, specifically regarding the ‘Feedback and Accountability Mechanism.’ Both newly recruited and existing staff will be systematically trained and reoriented to emphasize the importance of seeking community input during program implementation.Beneficiaries, including children, teachers, and the wider community, will be informed about the available mechanisms for providing feedback, suggestions, and complaints. They will also be actively engaged in implementing project activities, such as sensitization campaigns and community mobilization efforts. This initiative will adopt a community-based and participatory approach to foster meaningful involvement and shared ownership of project outcomes.The selection criteria for children and schools to be supported will be co-developed in consultation with community members and educational authorities to ensure transparency, inclusivity, and alignment with local priorities.To further enhance responsiveness, AVSI will conduct qualitative protection assessments, incorporating tools such as KOBO surveys to gather feedback from children and teachers. These assessments will evaluate satisfaction with the program and provide actionable insights to refine and adapt activities to better meet the needs of the target populations.AVSI’s experience demonstrates that this participatory approach not only ensures that the concerns and priorities of communities are addressed but also strengthens trust, ownership, and the overall effectiveness of interventions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Disability will be a key priority and criterion in the selection of beneficiaries for this project. All activities will be adapted to meet the specific needs of individuals with disabilities, ensuring their meaningful participation and inclusion.The project will incorporate comprehensive evaluation activities that prioritize the perspectives, suggestions, and feedback of people with disabilities. Screening, monitoring, and active participation of individuals with disabilities have been integrated as core components and indicators of the proposed initiative. During implementation, the identification of persons and children with disabilities will be documented, with data disaggregated by gender and age to provide a nuanced understanding of their needs and experiences.Building on this foundation, AVSI will, wherever possible, organize targeted studies on the inclusion and conditions of people with disabilities. These studies will inform the development of inclusive strategies, which will be discussed collaboratively in roundtables involving people with disabilities (PwD) and local communities.To foster broader awareness and support, the AVSI Foundation will conduct sensitization sessions throughout the project. These sessions aim to strengthen community support for individuals with disabilities and ensure their needs are prioritized in coordination and planning activities with local SDSMAS (District Services for Health, Women, and Social Action) and other partners.By integrating these measures, the project seeks to create an inclusive environment where individuals with disabilities are not only supported but actively empowered within their communities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

AAVSI will fully adhere to the regulations and guidelines outlined in its Environmental Policy, ensuring that all project activities align with principles of environmental sustainability.Given that the majority of the project involves person-to-person interventions, the environmental impact will be minimal. To further reduce waste, the project will prioritize the procurement of reusable supplies and minimize the use of consumables, particularly single-use plastics, throughout all activities.Additionally, to limit carbon emissions and reduce the ecological footprint of the project, the majority of staff and volunteers will be recruited locally from the communities and areas in close proximity to the intervention sites. This approach will significantly decrease the frequency of vehicle use in both IDP settlement centers and host communities, aligning with AVSI’s commitment to environmental responsibility.
