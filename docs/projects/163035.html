Project Summary

DCA proposes to implement short-term peak hunger food assistance to save lives of food insecure and at-risk households affected by both drought and economic shocks in Zimbabwe. ZIMVAC anticipates that nearly 8 million people will be in IPC 3 between January and March 2020. Over 47% of the population in Zimbabwe is food insecure and needing food assistance. The proposed intervention, therefore, seeks to cushion households from food insecurity by providing them with basic food and nutrition requirements during the peak hunger period. The proposed rural interventions are targeted at Manicaland (Mutasa and Mutare districts) and Matebeleland North and South (Lupane, Matobo and Insiza districts). In addition, the WFP IPC Compatible Analysis report shows that the proposed districts will be in IPC Level 3 between January and March 2020.
The proposed project will implement food assistance interventions in urban and peri-urban settlements of Mbare, Chitungwiza and Mabvuku-Tafara districts. Urban and peri-urban areas are targeted based on the results of the 2019 ZIMVAC Urban Livelihoods Assessment results which states that food insecurity is driven by economic shocks. DCA has a strong network of partners in all the proposed districts. In Mutare, Murehwa and Marondera, DCA is working with FACHIG Trust while in Shurugwi, Chirumhanzu and Mutasa DCA is working with Fambidzanayi. In Matebeleland North and South, DCA is implementing agriculture, livelihoods and food assistance programmes.
In undertaking the proposed interventions, targeting will generally be focused on vulnerable groups which are female and child-headed households, households with disability or chronic illness burden, at-risk households engaging in negative coping mechanisms and households with no or low incomes and or poor harvests. In rural districts, beneficiary targeting will use a combined model of community and vulnerability household baseline survey. Beneficiary lists will be derived from this process for each village and will feed into ward level distribution lists. Targeting in urban and peri-urban areas will entail conducting a baseline survey in targeted settlements with special focus on hotspots identified by all stakeholders. Collected data will be cleaned and subjected through a beneficiary targeting process using a targeting matrix that is made up of weighted food security indicators such as Food Consumption Score, Dietary Diversity Score, household characteristics, type of shelter and WASH-related parameters. Beneficiary registrations will be done using a scientific methodology that is beneficiary database system based.
The transfer modalities will be informed by market conditions and situations of the targeted operational areas. In rural areas the project proposes to use e-vouchers through the Red Rose Voucher System where local traders will be linked to wholesalers to guarantee the supply of basic food commodities. In all proposed rural districts, there is a strong network of local vendors/traders who can supply basic food items. Where local traders are unable to supply certain commodities, DCA will facilitate access through its network of wholesalers. This modality is working well in Epworth district. In urban and peri-urban areas, cash transfer will be used since there are functional markets and agreements with financial institutions that have guaranteed an efficient service have been made. This model is being implemented in Epworth and proving efficient. The food basket for both rural and urban areas will include, cereal, pulses, oil and corn-soy blend and in case of excess, non-food items such as toiletries can be bought by beneficiaries. The CBT value for urban sites is USD9/per/month and USD12/per/month in rural to cover for logistical costs. Nexus approach will be applied to complement cash transfer activities through DCA co-financing.

1 - Activities

a) Project activities will include the following: -Comprehensive local stakeholder and community sensitisation exercise will be conducted.b) Conduct beneficiary targeting and registration processes in selected districts- beneficiary targeting in rural areas will use a combination of community-based targeting approach and vulnerability assessment surveys co-developed and adapted after stakeholder consultations. A vulnerability starting matrix will be collaboratively developed with all stakeholders for the purposes of vulnerability aggregate scoring and ranking. The matrix will have the following key vulnerability indicators; household demographic characteristics, Food Consumption Score Index, Dietary Diversity Score, Coping Mechanism Index, type of shelter and WASH related parameters. Village-based beneficiary verification process will be conducted. For urban and peri-urban areas, stakeholder geographical mapping and hotspots identification will be conducted. A full sweep household vulnerability assessment survey will be carried out in the identified hotspots. Hotspots will be jointly identified with stakeholders. Special attention will be given to special vulnerability categories such as child and female headed households, disabled and chronically ill persons and the elderly.c) Conduct beneficiary verifications with stakeholders- beneficiary verification exercises will be conducted after beneficiary registrations. d) Conduct pre-disbursement and pre-distribution meetings-beneficiary mobilisation is either through local leadership structures in rural areas or through mobile bulk messaging in urban areas. During these meetings information on beneficiary entitlements, donor/s, implementation modality, beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism will be shared with the beneficiaries. e) Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established, and communities and local stakeholders will be sensitised. The mechanisms will include help desk, suggestion boxes, cooperating partner staff members, toll free lines and email addresses. f) Design a value-e-voucher system and an alternative CBT mechanism for the vulnerable householdsg) Monthly loading of the e-vouchers and disbursements of CBT to beneficiariesh) Provide monthly food rations through either vouchers and CBT to food insecure and at-risk households in rural areas of Zimbabwe. Distribution point are going to be set at places agreed with beneficiaries which do not pose a risk to special groups like children, pregnant women, elderly and the disabled. i) Conduct post-distribution monitoring-two weeks after every distribution to collect information on utilisation of the voucher and CBT, changes in DDS, coping mechanisms, food consumption patterns, collect and document human transformation stories j) Conduct weekly markets monitoring to track price fluctuation for basic commodities and availability. k) Attend stakeholder and donor humanitarian coordination meetings l) Facilitate awareness rising on health and nutrition, WASH, sexual and gender-based violence and child protection with the support of line ministries. m) Initiate complementary livelihood activitiesn) Production of weekly, monthly and quarterly reports o) Host donor missions in the districts of operation

2 - Indicators

a) Number of beneficiaries receiving support towards ensuring household food security through e-voucher and CBT on a monthly basis, disaggregated by gender and age b) Number of beneficiaries implementing complimentary livelihood activities disaggregated by gender and age c) Number of beneficiaries receiving information on gender-based violence, health, and nutrition, WASH and child protection disaggregated by genderd) Proportion of households with improved dietary diversity scores e) Proportion of households with improved food consumption score
