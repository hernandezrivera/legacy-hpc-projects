Project Summary

The project aims to address the many gaps in livelihoods and income generation; Ihsan is proposing to support beneficiaries (of both men and women over than 18 years) at individual level through Small Business Grants program to enable them to open business (especially those closely correlated with food security and income generation). The provided grants will support the target beneficiaries through purchasing the initial equipment, materials and supplies to get new business owners off and running their small projects. In addition, Ihsan will raise the abilities of target beneficiaries in creating and designing small projects through training sessions.
The general aim of these training sessions is to support the BNFs to plan and develop their ideas and give them the opportunity to increase income generation and to confirm sustainability impact for their projects in the future. On the other hand, the training sessions will support the BNFs to take the opportunity to apply for fund. After training session, the candidates will start designing their own projects, followed by the small business grant proposals from all beneficiaries.

Needs

Focus group discussions conducted by IhsanRD field team in cooperation with the local councils in the proposed communities indicated that the majority of assistance in the area focuses on emergency response activities-rather than activities that provide the community with income generating opportunities. On the other hand, results of focus group discussions indicated that approximately 80-85 % of the population in the target location was dependent on income generation activities-whether it be agricultural activities or different income-generating activities such as small shop for food sale, food processing, stitching, shaving restaurants, sweets production, repairing of electric and electronic equipment, marketing and storage of agricultural products.HNO report 2019 mentioned that after eight years of crisis, the resilience capacity of people in the most affected communities in Syria has been severely eroded. An estimated 83 percent of Syrians live below the upper (moderate) poverty. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 per cent of a public service employee’s monthly salary, demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. People in Syrian are adopting reduced coping strategies due to the loss or lack of sustained livelihoods, as well as being increasingly vulnerable to displacement. Protracted displacement, the depletion of productive assets and savings, limited economic opportunities and the widespread destruction and contamination of agriculture-related infrastructures have had a profound socio-economic impact on the population. In some cases, this has led to chronic levels of deprivation, contributing to people’s adoption of harmful coping strategies, such as reduced food consumption, deferment or delay in seeking necessary medical care; reduced hygiene practices, increasing public health risk; the spending of savings and the accumulation of debt. Such coping strategies are not only damaging and unsustainable but, once exhausted, may increase exposure to more harmful practices such as child labor, including in its worst forms, child recruitment, early marriage and other exploitative practices. As hostilities have reduced in many areas, particularly those that have shifted control, and as self-organized return increases, needs relating to access to basic services and livelihood opportunities have grown. Consultations with communities have indicated that access to livelihood opportunities and basic services are among their primary concerns as affected people seek to rebuild their lives.

Implementation of activities

1: Increasing the capacity of beneficiaries on planning small business projects through training sessions. Beneficiaries will be selected through a clear community mobilization mechanism, where the project is advertised locally, and interested households apply to take part in the various elements of the program. Both eligibility for the project and prioritization process will take place for beneficiaries in all activities. Once the initial application is gathered and families are prioritized depending on their vulnerability, verification of the selected households is conducted by Ihsan MEAL team, ensuring that the selected households do in fact meet the criteria established. After identification of BNFs list, the candidates will be trained on: 1- Business planning and designing and strategies of project development for the future2- Budgeting and Economic study for the project3- Marketing 4- Safety and security procedures 5- Respecting the Environmental Consideration through production systemThe general aim of these training sessions is to support the BNFs to plan and develop their ideas and give them the opportunity to increase income generation and to confirm sustainability impact for their projects in the future. On the other hand, the training sessions will support the BNFs to take the opportunity2: distribution of in-kind equipment for the selected beneficiaries.Ihsan will provide 1000 beneficiaries (500 from the host community, and 500 from IDPs) with small grants (1250 USD for each BNFs as an in kind). Ihsan team will make sure of buying the needed inputs for all BNFs. Distribution process of small businesses equipment and materials will be overseen by local councils and through advance coordination with Ihsan field team. Ihsan MEAL team will follow up the progress of the projects for each beneficiary through physical field visits and PDM survey. On the other hand, Ihsan field team will conduct continuous field visits to BNFs to provide them with the technical support and required instructions to maximize income generation of their own projects. Selection criteria will pay high attention on supporting women groups with special needs and the IDPs who own specific livelihood skills. Eligibility criteria will be essential for selection the beneficiaries followed by vulnerability and community needs. Indirect beneficiaries of this project include men, women, boys and girls for both IDP and host communities.

Protection Risk Analysis

This projects aims to preserve and restore the livelihoods of affected communities while stimulating economic recovery through supporting the target BNFs with small business grants. It worth to mention that Poverty, food and livelihoods insecurity, economic inequalities and conflicts over the control over resources exacerbate the risk and vulnerability environment for GBV. Livelihoods and protection can be linked in practice, both at the level of analysis and action. The main risks that may be occurred during the implementation of this project are gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. One of the key activities in mitigation of GBV is creating awareness sessions about the issues surrounding it. Gender training, information and communication campaigns are crucial factors to raise awareness of gender-specific issues in humanitarian interventions. Interventions in support of information and gender awareness raising may include: information dissemination and basic training amongst community and opinion leaders about issues of gender, power and GBV to raise their understanding of and responsiveness to the different needs of women, men, girls and boys; and training in roles and responsibilities amongst NGOs, their partners, refugees, IDPs, and host communities to promote the respect of accountability principles.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: 1- Availability of small projects equipment and raw materials 2- BNFs are not encouraged to take part in training sessions3- Bad security situation Mitigation: 1-Conduct in-depth market assessment prior to accepting small business projects Change the raw material to other available one.2-Ensuring the strong integration of the local council in their support for BNFs to take apart in the programs Raising awareness session to promote the importance of this projects in the communities’ level3- Avoiding gathering of BNFs during bad security situation and conduct training sessions in more safety space such as Basement.
