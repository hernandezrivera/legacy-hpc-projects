Project Summary

INTERSOS aim to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for conflict affected (IDPs/host community and returnees) communities, with an integrated CHAST approach in schools and community where INTERSOS is already working as education partner.
INTERSOS will provide temporary and sustainable WASH services to pupils in schools and Temporary learning spaces in Ezo, Yambio (Western Equatoria State) Mayom and Mayendit (Unity State) counties.
INTERSOS will target larger and most vulnerable schools, that experienced in 2018 the higher influx of IDPs and returnees or that lack basic WASH facilities.
INTERSOS will provide gender segregated sanitation facilities as per cluster and sphere standards, after consultation with relevant stakeholders (school PTA, teachers, local authorities and community leaders).
INTERSOS staff will conduct hygiene promotion activities in the schools and in the communities, with the set up of WASH and Nutrition club in the schools. Targeted schools will be provided with hand washing facilities and soap.
Where feasible INTERSOS will construct latrines to provide safe, dignified access to sanitation facilities to girls and boys in schools. According to the context (for example in TLS) INTERSOS may construct emergency latrine also with community mobilization providing digging kit and latrine slab through the WASH pipeline.
INTERSOS aim to provide access to safe water to schools by rehabilitation and repair of boreholes by training hand pump mechanics and water user committees responsible of the operation and maintenance of the water points. Spare parts and tools will be procured by WASH common pipeline.
Hygiene promotion activities in the schools will be accompanied by mass hygiene campaign and community mobilization in the villages to promote hand washing and latrine construction.
INTERSOS will target girls in menstrual age by providing MHM kits and training.
INTERSOS female staff will conduct GBV risks analysis throuth specific sessions and FGD with girls and women from schools and communities before implementing sanitation and water point rehabilitations.

Needs

South Sudan remains a country of increasing needs, with 7 million in need of humanitarian assistance. Increasing severe food and nutrition insecurity, diseases (cholera, malaria, typhoid, HEV, etc.), and economic crisis compound this situation.Coping abilities have been further eroded by prolonged food insecurity, economic collapse, malnutrition and disease outbreaks. The widespread lack of safe drinking water, underdeveloped sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable WASH related diseases. The economic decline, increasing food insecurity and renewed conflict led to continuous influxes to settlements in 2018, including the Protection of Civilian sites (PoCs). Some 90 per cent of the population do not have access to improved sanitation. Half of the population do not have access to improved water sources, with about 35 per cent of the existing boreholes estimated to be non-functional. Open defecation is persistent in 61 per cent of settlements in the country. Only 28 per cent of the population have access to a borehole within 30 minutes’ walk.For 2019 the WASH cluster has identified 1.9 million of IDPs and 5.7 million people in need of WASH assistance in South Sudan. Schools and learning spaces across the country has very limited access to WASH facilities and water, the influx of IDPs and returnees in 2018 has exacerbated the situation in many areas with pupils lacking access to latrines and clean water. INTERSOS is currently implementing education activities across the country in more than 80 schools. In the counties of Ezo and Yambio (Western Equatoria) INTERSOS is supporting 26 schools and TLS. In February 2018 less than half of the settlements in Ezo and Yambio counties reported to have access to improved water sources in both dry and wet season and most of the schools lacks adequate sanitation and water facilities. Unity State have experienced in 2018 widespread insecurity and violence, and saw a notable increase in reports of new arrivals of IDPs, especially in Southern Unity; a May joint humanitarian assessment estimated that about 8,000 persons had been displaced to Leer town, Mer, Koch town, Mayendit town and Nyal. The population of the Temporary Protection Area in Leer increased from 575 in early April to 1,995 by mid-May. In the same period, more than 3,000 newly displaced persons arrived at the Bentiu PoC site (REACH, Situation Overview: Unity State).INTERSOS is supporting 21 schools in Mayom and Mayendit counties (Unity state). In August 2018 REACH found that 38% of assessed settlements in Mayom county reports cholera or diarrhoea as their main cause of death. Hygiene promotion and latrine construction in schools is essential to respond to the dire WASH needs of those populations.

Activities

INTERSOS will address vital needs of conflict affected boys, girls and youth in schools in Western Equatoria (Ezo and Yambio counties) and Unity State (Mayom and Mayendit counties) INTERSOS will support formation and training of School WASH clubs and in target schools and provide technical WASH guidance and awareness sessions on hygiene and sanitation best practices. INTERSOS aim to provide access to improved sanitation facilities in schools: latrines and hand washing points with sufficient quantities of clean water. The installation of sanitation facilities will be discussed with education stakeholders including school PTA, local authorities, etc. - Capacity building for teachers, PTA, and educational person in WASH will be conducted with training in water on maintenance of water point and sanitation facilities. Community based hygiene promoter will be trained on CHAST and they will work for Hygiene promotion in schools and in the most vulnerable communities. Integrated education and WASH activities will address cross-cutting protection and GBV issue. Menstrual Hygiene Managements kits provided by the WASH pipeline will be distributed in the schools to girls in menstrual age. Community mobilizations and mass hygiene promotion will promote construction of households latrines in host community and IDPs settlements.INTERSOS will adapt design and interventions based on community’s needs: addressing most urgent needs in the most appropriates ways based on the feasibility and cost effectiveness of different design and solutions. INTERSOS will install latrines in schools with high number of children and will train hand pump mechanics and water user committees to repair and maintain water point serving both community and schools. INTERSOS will also focus on hygiene promotion in the schools and in set up and training of WASH committees that will includes teachers, students and communities member to promote the community-led construction of emergency latrines and hand washing point (where feasible INTERSOS will support providing pipeline materials like digging kit and latrines slab). In case of outbreaks INTERSOS will provide targeted schools with chlorinated water and soap to be distributed in the community through the schools. INTERSOS staff will collaborate with Health partners to target WASH-related disease vulnerable population with access to safe drinking/chlorinated water and soap
