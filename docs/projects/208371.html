Project Summary

The ongoing socio-political crisis in the Anglophone regions of Cameroon, has increased poverty levels, damaged livelihoods and resulted in gross human rights violations, including Gender-Based Violence. As this conflict continues, many cases of physical, sexual violence, harassment and child marriages accompanied by high rate of unplanned early pregnancies, are being reported. The high prevalence of GBV in the conflict affected Regions of Cameroon, particularly in Meme, Fako Subdivision, Tiko Sub-Division, Mezam Division and Boyo Division is worrying. In these areas 2 in every 5 teenagers get pregnant between the ages of 12-17 and this raises public health concerns (TeenAlive 2020). Determinants of teenage pregnancies include: lack of life and social survival skills, lack of knowledge on how to avoid pregnancy, low acceptance/use of contraceptives, neglect by parents, sexual abuse, pressure to contribute to family welfare through sexual transaction and child marriage, peer pressure and rape (TeenAlive 2021). These pregnant teenagers and mothers risk poor maternal and child health, being isolated, attempting unsafe abortions, failure to continue with school and poverty.
The sustainable Development Goals and the Global strategy for Women’s, Children’s and Adolescents Health (2016-2030) call upon all stakeholders to work toward a world in which every woman, child and adolescent in every setting realizes their rights to physical and mental health and well-being, has social and economic opportunities and is able to participate fully in shaping prosperous and sustainable societies. TeenAlive is committed towards realizing these Goals. This will go a long way to prevent maternal death amongst teenage mothers and create GBV-Free communities. It is against this background that there is the need to reduce teenage pregnancy and the adverse effect of teenage pregnancy in Fako Division, Mezam Division and Boyo Division and TeenAlive association seeks to do this through the following proposed interventions/activities:
1. Provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support to 550 Conflict-Affected Teenage Mothers in Fako (Buea and Tiko Sub-Divisions), Mezam (Bamenda I, Bamenda II and Tubah Subdivisions) and Boyo (Njinikom Sub-Division).
2. Provide Comprehensive sex education to 2,000 young children in primary and secondary schools in Fako (Buea and Tiko Sub-Divisions), Mezam (Bamenda I, Bamenda II and Tubah Subdivisions) and Boyo (Njinikom Sub-Division) aim at preventing teenage pregnancies.
3. Raise Awareness and sensitize 3000 community members against GBV/Child Abuse in Fako (Buea and Tiko Sub-Divisions), Mezam (Bamenda I, Bamenda II and Tubah Subdivisions) and Boyo (Njinikom Sub-Division) of Cameroon.
4. Train 300 school dropout teenagers within the ages of 13-17 on vocational skills (beading and embroidery) for income for self-reliance/improved welfare and reduce vulnerability and exposure to transactional sex in Fako (Buea and Tiko Sub-Divisions), Mezam (Bamenda I, Bamenda II and Tubah Subdivisions) and Boyo (Njinikom Sub-Division).

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Cameroonian society is patriarchal and characterized by institutional gender inequality, discrimination, and social exclusion, which uphold and compound GBV. The ongoing humanitarian crisis affecting the North-West and South-West is exacerbating the situation of women and girls, threatening their safety while also limiting their access to basic services, opportunities, and resources and compromising their resilience capacity and empowerment. As this conflict continues, many cases of sexual violence, harassment and child marriages accompanied by high rate of unwanted early pregnancies, are being reported. The high prevalence of teenage pregnancies and teenage mothers in the conflict affected Regions of Cameroon, particularly in Buea Subdivision, Tiko Sub-Division, Mezam Division and Boyo Division is worrying. In these areas 2 in every 5 teenagers get pregnant between the ages of 12-17 and this raises public health concerns (TeenAlive 2020). Determinants of teenage pregnancies include: lack of life and social survival skills, lack of knowledge on how to avoid pregnancy, low acceptance/use of contraceptives, neglect by parents, sexual abuse, pressure to contribute to family welfare through sexual transaction and child marriage, peer pressure and rape (TeenAlive 2021). These pregnant teenagers and mothers risk poor maternal and child health, being isolated, attempting unsafe abortions, failure to continue with school and poverty.Gender-Based Violence remains one of the main humanitarian concerns in Cameroon. According to the findings of “Voices from Cameroon” (2022), 149 100 per cent of respondents reported cases of domestic violence, 98 per cent the denial of resources and 95 per cent psychological violence in their communities. In addition, 90 per cent confirmed limited access to services for both women and girls. An estimated 987,000 people are in need of GBV prevention services and response across the North West, South-West. 150 GBV affects mostly women and girls subjected to increased intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual violence, denial of resources, emotional violence and forced marriage. Even if women are the most exposed group, among them there are some categories that are particularly vulnerable, including women living with disabilities, adolescent girls, and widows. Tailored GBV preventive services and responses are required for the protection of at-risk groups, with a focus on individual special needs. TeenAlive is committed towards fighting against GBV in our communities. This will go a long way to prevent maternal death amongst teenage mothers and create GBV-Free communities. It is against this background that there is the need to reduce teenage pregnancy and the adverse effect of teenage pregnancy in Fako Division, Mezam Division and Boyo Division.
