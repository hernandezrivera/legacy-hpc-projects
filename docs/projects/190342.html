Project Summary

The situation in northern Ethiopia continues to be tense and volatile. The hostilities continue to affect civilian lives, their safety and restricting their access to livelihoods, increasing the humanitarian needs notably with a continuing ad growing number of displaced people and hindering humanitarian access and delivery of aid to affected areas.
The humanitarian situation in the Amhara and Afar region remains very severe and the service provision across all sectors remains suboptimal. The target woredas have been selected based on the actual needs on the ground. Furthermore, due to the recent dynamics of displacement and the districts being inaccessible for several months before June, providing any assistance in these areas was not possible earlier, leaving them with severe unaddressed needs.
According to different needs assessments conducted in the regions have indicated lack of access to basic services and, needs and gaps in service provision which exacerbates protection risks of women and girls in the IDP sites. IDPs in the project sites are residing in sub-standard shelters, sharing sleeping space and in an unlockable shelter exposing them to various protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGVB) risks. Settlements are unsafe for women and girls because of mixed sex sleeping arrangements and overcrowding. The shelters are further lacking windows, doors and fencing. These factors contribute to the increasing rate of SGBV in the IDP sites. Provision of shelter and ensuring a safe living environment is the prioritized need of IDPs, especially of women and girls.
The intervention will therefore consider the nutrition needs of men, women, boys and girls ensuring their privacy and safety. The provision of life saving nutrition will contribute to creating safe living environments ensuring privacy and safety of IDPs, mainly women and girls.
This project will provide emergency lifesaving activities for 2,000 Households will have access to emergency nutrition response (1,000 HH at Awra zone, Afar, and 1,000 at Woldya town, Amhara).
DPO will actively involve target communities and local government from the initial introductory meeting in order to gain the full support of the local community and concerned local authorities. The project will also promote ownership through the joint participation of the hosting local community and IDP representatives in implementation and monitoring of the response activities. Before project commencement, DPO will organize meeting with beneficiaries to discuss on the planned response including the planned activities, type and number of targets and the duration of the support.

1. - Projects activities

1. Conduct the routine screening for under 5 children, PLW in both woreda. The screening will be at the community and facility level through routine and mass screening. Children who are 6–59 months of age and assessed as a mid-upper arm circumference <115 mm, or a weight-for-height/length <–3 Z-scores of the WHO Child Growth Standards median, or have bilateral pitting oedema, should be referred for a full assessment at a treatment center for the management of severe acute malnutrition at Health Post or Health Center. During follow-up, an appetite test will be performed; the anthropometric status and medical condition of children will be assessed. Those children who fulfilled the OTP discharge criteria will be lined to respective nutrition programs to prevent relapse. DPO will support the link to the program for children <5 identified as Severe Acute Malnutrition with complication (SC) and Severe Acute Malnutrition without complication (OTP) to nearest health facilities. DPO will provide refreshment training for Health Extension Workers (HEW) in community settings/ Health post and Health Care Worker (HCW) in Primary Health-Care Unit level to ensure measure the mid-upper arm circumference or the weight-for-height/weight-for-length status children who are 6–59 months of age and PLW. In both settings, children will be examined for bilateral pitting edema. 2. Support access to services for the treatment of children SAM with medical complications in Stabilization Centers (SCs) and children at Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) for the Management of SAM without Medical Complications among children aged 6-59 months. ASDEPODPO will support SC/OTP services through strengthening the targeted woreda health service system by providing capacity-building training of woreda health office nutrition focal, PHCU OPD, and MCH focal on updated CMAM/IMAM in collaboration with the woreda health office. DPO will support the referral linkage to nearest health facilities of under-five children identified as severe acute malnutrition during community and facility level screening. Medical items and routine drugs, such as Amoxicillin, Albendazole/Mebendazole, folic acid, vitamin A, anti-malaria and measles vaccine and ready to use therapeutic food will be supported by counter government, UNICEF and WHO. DPO will facilitate and transport the SC and OTP commodities from regional/zonal/woreda warehouse of government and donors to health centers and Health Posts. DPO will develop and print the service registration book and beneficiaries follow cards for SC/OTP Services.3. Support the provision of TSFP service for children 6-59 months and PLW and chronically ill and disabled people with moderate acute malnutrition. DPO will strengthen in screening and referral linkage of PLW and children in all selected woredas that aim to treat patients with MAM, and prevent deterioration of the condition to SAM. DPO nutrition team work with woreda health office, PHCU to promote early identification of malnutrition among the target groups with MAM in specific vulnerable groups such as children aged 6-59 months and malnourished pregnant women and lactating women with infants less than 6 months of age.4. Community Outreach Activities. It increases access and coverage. Community outreach activities ensure early identification and referral of children with SAM before the onset of serious complications, defaulter tracing and follow-up home visits for problem cases. DPO will work hand in hand with the Woreda community to identify, manage and prevent acute malnutrition.DPO plans to motivate and acknowledge Health development armies (HDAs) through involving them in the community forum and orient Community health leaders to debrief on how to increase awareness of the community on the availability of CMAM program, screening

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

DPO will ensure that information sharing and coordination for the project activities implementation is maintained at all levels. At national level, DPO will continue attending and sharing information in the Shelter and NFIs cluster's meetings as well as submitting timely 5Ws/updating for the cluster's coordinator. DPO will ensure proper engagement and participation in the Nutrition clusters at subnational and zonal levels, in order to avoid duplication of efforts, maximizing use of resources and supporting an integrated emergency response. DPO will establish clear, easily accessible mechanisms that allow partner staff and community members to provide feedback on the project and how it is implemented. These mechanisms will be included in the MEAL plan and accountability framework developed for the project and include locally preferred and most effective means, such as surveys, direct feedback, face-to-face meetings, focal persons and suggestion boxes. DPO will also deploy Safeguarding Monitors to distribution/activity locations to observe distributions and converse with participants, providing another direct avenue for sharing feedback and any safeguarding concerns. Timely responses will be provided for programmatic issues reported by staff and community members.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will actively participate in all levels of the project cycle, including in the rapid needs assessments in the design and planning, implementation as well as in monitoring and evaluation. DPO will establish information sharing mechanisms to enhance participation and ensure accountability to beneficiaries.DPO will maintain a commitment to engaging with affected communities through focus group discussions with women, men, and youth on issues regarding their concerns and feedback about the project. Regular meetings with community leaders in the areas of operation will generate feedback for the calibration of activities.Using the different complaints and feedback mechanisms, beneficiaries can report compliances in person, to DPO staff or anonymously by using the reporting tool in the area.DPO will pursue a people-centered and rights-based approach that will promote empowerment and protection of sexual and gender minorities, women, and girls. Additionally, regular oversight will be provided, and monitoring visits conducted to observe service delivery. Feedback collection mechanisms will be instituted, and data will be collected and segregated by gender and age.The projects will work with all segments of society equally. It will ensure participation of men, women, boys, girls, people with disability, people with chronic illness and the like starting from the inception of the project. The project intends to actively engage and provide opportunities to communities in targeted areas to participate in all aspects of the project. The project will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all activities of the project by using a protection mainstreaming checklist. In beneficiary identification, priority will be given for vulnerable groups such as FHH, PWDs, pregnant and lactating mothers. Distribution of nutrition will be made in a time and places that is safe for women and girls and PWDs to avoid any safety risks. Trainings will also be provided in a time and place that is convenient for women and people with special needs to increase their participation. Training on gender-based violence and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse will be provided for selected beneficiaries, and representatives of government and other partner organizations to raise their awareness and to allow beneficiaries to report if cases of sexual exploitation and abuse occurs. At time of the training COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures will be adapted.
