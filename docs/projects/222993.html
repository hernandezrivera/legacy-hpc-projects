Project Summary

This 6-month project plans to complement 2024-2026 FSLC HRP inclusive objectives; CO1: Food assistance through Cash transfer, CO2: Emergency Livelihood food production; and CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs in Eastern Equatoria (IPC 3 & 4) target Counties, in a way that prevents drought outbreak spread. The project is designed to support 7,503 vulnerable HHs (45,018 individuals) composed of IDPs, Host communities and Returnees, PWDs, and minorities through life-saving food assistance, own food production, livelihood support, and livestock health services. 13,955 men, 22,058 women, 4,052 boys and 4,953 girls are targeted. Implementation for food assistance (CO1) will be through general food distribution in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties targeting 1906 and2185 households respectively. The implementation of CO2 to enhance food production will be through in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits to44,124 households (Kapoeta East 21,553, and Kapoeta North 22,492),livestock support; treatment and vaccination campaigns to Kapoeta East (2,100) and Kapoeta North(1,850) counties targeting a total of 163,000 livestock heads through 105 trained Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (35 per county), and livestock restocking program in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North for 500households in each of the three counties. In light of resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids (CO3), this will be realized through training of farmers (750), fisher folks (360), 20 VSLA groups (20member each), and through local procurement of seeds from through seed fairs. The trainings will cover good and climate-smart agronomic practices, countering post-harvest losses, sustainable income generation activities. Seed Fairs (SFs) for Crop Seed procurement will be done from 1,500 seed producer groups/traders in Kapoeta North County where seeds will be procured locally from these farmers and distributed to the community farming purposes. Beneficiary targeting will be through the AAP committee with mainstreaming of gender, persons with disabilities, minority and marginalized groups. This project will benefit the most vulnerable; Child-headed, elderly and Female-headed households under IPC3& 4, and 5 suffering from food insecurities and malnutrition. Protection will be mainstreamed in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender-Based Violence and ensure the Do No Harm principle is followed during beneficiary targeting and provision of support. There will always be timely, routine and well-established feedback mechanism channels from the beneficiaries throughout the project cycle. Sustainability plan of the project will be achieved through community engagement in the design and implementation of the project in collaboration with County Agricultural Department, ROSS office, and other local authorities. Awareness and risk mitigation will be raised through training and engaging community members on specific techniques for contextualized approaches. Project staff will support assessments initiated by the FSL cluster and Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries and will monitor crop disease, pest outbreaks, Fall Armyworm (FAW), and desert locust and make referrals to FSL cluster and MAF. Complementary programming will be advanced in the counties, working closely with nutrition and health partners. The project will receive pipeline supplies from FAO whereas the requested funding will be used to provide front-line services.

1. Needs

October – November 2024 IPC and projection for December 2023- March 2025 and April – July 2025 findings estimate populations of 6.6 million people with Acute Food insecurity in South Sudan or over half of the population (54%) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity and classified in crisis (IPC 3) Emergency food needs and facing Crisis (IPC phases 4) in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North Counties (Eastern Equatoria state), respectively; one of the worst in South Sudan. High SAM and GAM rates in the counties have been attributed to deteriorating food security, high morbidity, and extremely poor diets along with underlying issues of suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM), and PLW’s cases. In Kapoeta North, the food security and nutrition monitoring system (FSNMS R25) assessment reported a GAM rate of 29.4%, MAM rate of 22.3%, and SAM of 7.1% for children under 5 years while pregnant and lactating women rate of malnutrition was 39.9%, all above WHO emergency thresholds. Kapoeta East, for instance, has GAM rate of 16.2%; SAM rate of 3.6%, MAM rate 12.6%, and Prevalence of malnutrition PLW is 34.2%. Kapoeta North County was assessed to have severe levels of malnutrition. (FSNMS R25). The situation has worsened in the era of drought, COVID 19 pandemic and floods there by pushing more people to IPC 3+ than the previous years (HNO 2023). CFSAM and FAO/WFP (2023) noted significant decrease of most trade routes activities and safety. Livelihoods disruptions have been recurrently driven by protracted conflict and displacement, missed planting seasons, lack of quality seeds and inputs, destruction and coverage of agricultural field by floodwater, livestock and crop diseases, lack of veterinary and agricultural extension services, post-harvest losses and lack of markets to sell their produce. There has been a public health concern on zoonosis particularly on Rift Valley fever due to the droughts and desert locust. Settlements within the target counties are using extreme negative coping strategies such as child marriages, child labor, intensification of cattle rustling and the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity. Through this project, PDP proposes to protect and safeguard lives in crisis phases by carrying outgeneral food distribution in the proposed target areas where food stores and reserves have been depleted due to natural disasters and conflicts. The provision of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will not only promote food production but also diversify the food varieties and nutrients within the diet. Provision of livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns will help minimize the conflicts, reduction of meals taken per day, only children eating with devastating effects on pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of five years. Eastern Equatoria State (84percent) has the largest proportion of female-headed households, with just 16 percent of households headed by males. Female-headed households also have a higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male-headed households), a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. Significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor passed from 20% to 30% to merely 6% to 8% in Eastern Equatoria States. 99.6 percent off a male-headed households were involved in decision-making about food and other resources in the household. If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the start of 2021, and in decimation of livestock due to disease outbreaks intensified by floods and inaccessible veterinary services thereby countering erosion of their livelihood sources. Provision of livestock through restocking program will help in the recovery phase of communities who have lost their livestock.
