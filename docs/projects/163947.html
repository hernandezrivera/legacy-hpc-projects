Project Summary

As the situation starts to be perceived as more stable in certain locations of Syria, people are returning to their locations of origin. Rural Damascus and Quneitra are experiencing big influx of returnees (85% are returnees). Before the conflict, these communities had strongly relied on livestock as their main income generating activity (IGA). However, during the peak of the crisis, these areas faced high levels of conflict and displacement, provoking thus that thousands of families lost their main source of income: livestock. And as of last year, only 9% received assistance through livelihood-based activities. To alleviate the crisis impact on animal source food supply and population itself, field assessments deemed a rapid restoration of the most essential livestock needs for the people in Kesweh and Quneitra as crucial. The project is targeting returnees in response to the SO 2&3, Support Income Generating Activities including vocational training, and Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal fodder distribution), recovery of economic programmes, to provide asset replacement including livestock and fodder to improve livelihoods opportunities and improving community capacities. The project aims to support restoration of livelihoods for the most vulnerable population in two areas; Kisweh and Quneitra, because the residents are farmers. The project will assist 100 households based on strict selection criteria based on the community and partner agencies feedback as well as sector and sub-sector chapters. Focus is to provide one cow per family, in addition to one water tank of 1000 Liter capacity. Beneficiary selection is based on; previous herders, Female led Households FHH, farmers under dire need for support, elderly, people with special needs, families with children at age of schooling to ensure their return to study. The project will procure 100 cows of the Holstein-Friesian local breed, in excellent health condition with veterinary license. Each at 4 years of age and will produce between 25-35 Kilos of milk per day based on its food and care. Training refresher will be provided on Animal husbandry, milk processing, feed production, para vet training and basic veterinary tools use and provision. The financial investment for IGA is justified by the fact that at the end of the project the Households (HHs) should be able to have a sustainable source of income.
The project is built with a community-based approach in order to minimize the conflict's impacts on poverty. It is natural resource management as well as social development approaches such as community driven development and community-based development. The beneficiaries shall have previous experience in animal husbandry and will be vulnerable groups as identified by the HRP. Females beneficiaries are targeted because they are traditionally responsible for livestock in rural Syria who now make up more than 60 percent of the country’s agricultural workforce, “This is often their only source of income to feed their families,”. The distribution of the cattle cows will be conducted in a systematic way, with clear beneficiary selection criteria emphasizing competence and access to feed the animals. The beneficiary should agree to submit a regular data collection and follow-up of the offspring.
Due to the amount of humanitarian support arriving in the area, the current food security situation is not likely to further deteriorate. Nevertheless, the levels of assistance are yet beyond meeting other living needs such as livelihood interventions, where as of last year, only 9% received assistance through livelihood-based activities. The basic main source of income is agricultural activities (crop production and livestock) followed by skilled and unskilled labour, in addition to humanitarian assistance.

4 - Needs

Rescate field visits to Kisweh and Quneitra showed the deteriorated standards of living. There is dire need for support to limit the dependency of the population on assistance. Livestock prices are likely to increase in winter. Coping strategies are likely to further deteriorate with the expected seasonal increase of food commodity prices, linked to increased cost of fuel and transportation. The project will enhance women empowerment for directly address their need for livestock since they are the main animal breeders in the rural areas. The project will help FHH to meet their families’ needs and support their children to go back to schools. To ensure capacity to care for the cows, three-month fodder will be supplied with each cow to each household after which they can depend on their own income to feed the cow. Communities were identified based on their vulnerability in coordination with the Syria Trust. Based on community meetings, farmers were identified at the respective pilot sites, A work plan with timeline and budget was developed which outlined the various activities to be implemented. The populations in the selected areas based on the life modality as being strictly farmers covering their living needs from farming, livestock breeding and market purchases and exchanges. The project will target 100 households covering ~1000 indirect beneficiaries who are in dire need as mentioned above,like; Small and marginal owner-farmers: - returnees who a) live in a rural area at or below subsistence level; b)- are dedicated full- or part-time to agriculture, the families' main source of income is livestock breeding, c) Livestock Producers in the past and small farmers who lost their livestock to the events d) landless laborers; e) are over-dependent for work and livelihood on others. FHHs, unemployed breadwinners; Farmers who demonstrated capacity to produce with the objective of generating reasonable income and utilize the good farming practices in a sustainable manner.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project objective is increasing beneficiaries’ food security via increasing their resilience in response to any future breakdown. Activities are as follows: - Coordination with Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform MAAR to approved work with the Farmers Units - Set the criteria for the targeted communities/ beneficiaries in Rural Damascus and Quneitra in the communities -SO.2: Support rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities. - Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution)- Veterinary products (ecto and endoparasiticides, plus vaccines, as the livestock will receive vaccination as required during cattle purchase)- Simple tools for home-made dairy products, cheese and yogurt (dairy small scale) manufacturing equipment - Training refresher courses will be provided by veterinarian to groups of 25 breeders each lasts for 3 days providing refreshment information on animal husbandry, milk processing, feed production, para vet training and basic veterinary tools use and provision. - Training and Equipment for Para Vets including on surveillance and early detection of infectious diseases

Since Rescate is well aware that resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception and risk of discrimination or perception of discrimination if a service is provided to the specific groups or individuals, thereby creating or exacerbating tension. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Rescate had 1. Ensured consultation with communities related to location of the services to understand relevant community perspectives; and 3. The mechanisms will be communicated with the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the stakeholders, local authorities and communities concerned. 5. Rescate will be keeping the stakeholders, local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress to efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ 6. Rescate will ensure that the process of receiving feedback and of responses to it is put in place by establishing a claim mechanism at the Project Management level reachable to all beneficiaries. 7. Rescate will set up and have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities), in consultation with concerned communities. Rescate will ensure that Do No Harm is established and abide to by all staff, partners, and contractors.
