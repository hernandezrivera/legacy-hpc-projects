Project Summary

DRC will continue its effort in improved accountability to the affected population (AAP) and enhancing community engagement, ensuring participation and representation of diverse Rohingya and host communities to build resilience and promote ownership of the programming. Building on 2019, 2020 will continue to focus on internal and external protection mainstreaming and capacity-building for field-based staff, contributing to an overall improved protection response across sectors in Camp 2E, 2W, 5,6,7,10,11,12,13,14. Critical individual protection issues will be addressed through case management, including referral and individual protection assistance (IPA), ensuring a tailored customised support. Protection monitoring will enable adaptability and a community-based approach on identified trends, concerns and needs and complement to evidence-based advocacy. Through community-based programming with existing and trusted community groups community-led programming will continue in its efforts to build community resilience and community-led advocacy. 2020 will further focus on social-cohesion within and between communities to foster social-cohesion and community-led conflict resolution and peacebuilding mechanisms.DRC is directly involved and a participant in the PSEA network and ensured a PSEA focal is in place. DRC works very closely with government counterpart Camp in Charge (CiC) in community level action planning and implementation. For protection, the protection team engages with the CiC offices and CiC's themselves to discuss and update on the protection environment, concerns and the protection community-led projects. At host community level, all DRC activities are informed and coordinated with the UNO offices in Rajapalong and Palongkhali. At Cox Bazaar level, DRC SMT regularly interacts with the District Commissioner, RRRC and other government agencies to ensure that DRC projects are supported and coordinated. Furthermore, as part of its sustainability strategy, DRC is currently conducting a comprehensive local partner mapping and selection exercise as part of its partnership strategy to expand its programming with national and local partners for the camp.

Needs

The systematic collection of data and analysis of protection trends in camps 2W, 2E, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 as well as Raja Palong and Palong Kahli host communities over the past year, provides DRC with the evidenced-based information required to ensure tailored protection programming based on communities needs and concerns. Protection Monitoring (PM) findings highlight considerable protection concerns specifically in accessing basic services (2493 total needs identified in camps from August to October; male 1647, female 846), security and safety (2433 total needs identified in camps from August to October; male 1614, female 819) in addition to a rise in inter and intra communal conflict/violence (over 70 concerns reported in August and September) specifically in camp 2W, 11, 12, 13 and 14 (DRC PM data collection). Based on the above outlined numbers the need for the continuation of DRCs individual-centred case management programme is evident, to ensure an effective protection response. Coordination and information sharing remain a challenge across camps with local, national and international field-based staff (incl. volunteers) not being trained in basic protection mainstreaming principals and disaster rapid response (DRR) mechanisms, having a direct impact on the overall humanitarian response during emergency as well as non-emergency. Hence, a need to build field staff and partners capacity in protection mainstreaming and DRR is paramount to improve the protective environment of beneficiaries. Further, based on PM findings, specialised services and assistance for vulnerable groups (individuals or groups who are unable to anticipate, cope with, resist and/or recover from emergency and hence in greater need of assistance e.g. women, children, elderly and persons with disability among others), remains a challenge inside the camps and in host communities. Service provision in host communities is particularly poor as the majority of services remain centred in the camps. This has led to an increase in tension between both communities. Violence, exhaustion, theft, economic and employment opportunities as well as access to basic services for host communities and/or refugees who arrived pre 2017 influx feel overlooked and demand compensation and equal support (PM team findings). Whereas the need for increased intervention to tackle social-cohesion has been identified. The rising tensions within Rohingya communities in the camps has widely been overlooked. This underscores the need to start strengthening social cohesion and conflict resolution activities within Rohingya communities through community-led protection efforts, fostering community’s self-reliance, dignity and self-determination for peaceful co-existence as well as quality humanitarian assistance in the camps and in the host community affected by the influx.

Activities or outputs

1. Systematic and regular collection, verification and analysis of information and data (through community-based data collection Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and direct observation) over an extended period of time by trained protection monitoring staff. This will permit to identify protection trends, risks and concerns the refugee and the host communities face, for the purpose of informing effective responses, coordination and evidence-based local, national and internal advocacy efforts (in accordance to Protection Information Monitoring (PIM) guidelines). 2. Identify and reduce the protection risks and/or specific protection needs of PoC through direct GP case management and provision of individual assistance, while conducting direct systematic referrals to specialised services (internal and external). Increased caseload in case of an emergency will be accounted for at the time of events. At each step of the case management process, which is the entry point for identification of IPA cases, care is taken to ensure that no harm comes to client or their families as a result of caseworker conduct, decisions made, or actions taken on behalf of the client or family, following the no harm principal. 3. Expand on DRCs current efforts and geographical training coverage of camp level staff of humanitarian actors of local, national and INGOs across different sectors in implementation zones in protection mainstreaming to support them in improving the access to, quality of and protection-sensitivity of their response. 4. Identify and strengthen community-based protection (CBP) mechanisms and resilience, through targeted community-based group sensitization, capacity-building and community-led activities in the Rohingya refugee camps and in the Host Communities to foster self-determination, self-reliance and social cohesion among communities through community-based programming. DRC CBP will tailor sensitization and capacity-building activities based on identified protection concerns by Community Protection Groups (CPG), providing a knowledge base enabling community-led projects and sensitization activities by CPG to the wider community contributing positively towards the overall protection environment within area of intervention. This will include targeted capacity-building training for SMS volunteers on Disaster Rapid Response by the CBP team. Further, DRC is an active partner of the protection sector Rapid Protection Assessment Team under the protection sector and partnership with other protection agencies. 5. Identify and foster peacebuilding and conflict-resolution initiatives by establishing peacebuilding groups (PBG), specifically women and youth. Linked to activity 4 outlined above, CBP teams will work with women and youth form Rohingya and Host communities by providing tailored trainings/workshop activities on conflict resolution/mediation, peacebuilding and community/based protection mechanisms. Gained knowledge will provide PBG with a firm base enabling a community driven approach through awareness initiatives aiming to foster social cohesion among their own and across communities. 6. DRC will continue to coordinate with the protection sector through three main avenues; a) regular attendance and active participation with the Protection Working Group (PWG) b) an active member of the PWG-Task Team (PWG-TT), c) information sharing of camp and host communities’ concerns through the dissemination of DRC monthly monitoring reports with the PWG and other relevant protection actors.
