Project Summary

The project aims to ensure life-saving, comprehensive, and equitable primary healthcare access to all women, girls, men, boys, and oppressed minorities (transgender, disabled, and elderly) from the HP at FDMN Camp 1W and NCD-focused primary care at Camp 09. In addition, services will be offered from the Path Lab and USG corner adjacent to the HP to strengthen focused medical care for FDMNs. The project will focus on delivering services from the HP located at Camp 09 on NCD chronic management, compromising screening, and chronic treatment for CVD risk factors, including HTN, DM, asthma, COPD, anemia, GDM, pre-eclamptic HTN, and malnutrition, using EMR, that will maintain record of patient findings and are crucial for longitudinal tracking and proper CVD risk-based chronic management along with referrals to complicated cases. The laboratory tests will provide blood lipid profile, serum electrolytes, and HbA1c in addition to USG services for monitoring the fetal conditions of GDM & GHTN patients to enhance NCD-focused medical care services with necessary referrals. Also, services, including mental health counseling, first aid for injuries, burns, and drowning, will be provided, along with referrals. To prevent and manage NCDs, beneficiaries will receive counseling on dietary advice, lifestyle modification, smoking cessation, and excessive salt intake. The services compromising primary NCD care along with essential screening, treatment, and referral for communicable disease outbreaks, including dengue, malaria, AWD, hepatitis, typhoid, skin infections, and, commonly, UTI from HP at Camp 1W, along with pathological tests will be ensured. Additionally, TB will be passively screened in the facility, and suspected cases will undergo sputum testing through AFB Microscope for case confirmation and diagnosis. The SRH services from the HP will be ensured, such as gonorrhea, syphilis, herpes, candidiasis, trichomoniasis, chlamydia, and more, prioritizing proper diagnosis and treatment, with equitable access to transgender individuals with confidentiality. The maternity care including ANC/PNC services with major pregnancy complications will be monitored and followed up with support of USG. In addition, abdominal, renal, breast, and pelvic ultrasound will be managed, treated, and referred. Preventive cancer crucial to women's reproductive health, such cervix cancer, will be introduced with single-visit screening and treatment. Family planning services, such as counseling and low-barrier methods, with menstrual hygiene, will be prioritized. Both HPs' will preserve age, gender, and disability-responsive infrastructure and service delivery orientation. The project has a digital EMR, which will aid in health information management and reporting viability. This EMR will help to expand access to healthcare and improve treatment by providing individualized screening, ICD10-based diagnosis, and treatment support, with follow-up, monitoring of referrals, and traceability for various conditions. The primary essential healthcare services will also be continued to the host community, similar to HP services, with screening, laboratory tests, and follow-up treatment across all age, gender, and disability groups. The necessary medicines following the ESP provision shall be continuously assured. Major emphasis will be given to community health education, awareness, and behavior modification through CHWs, counseling, and campaigning. The door-to-door model via CHW will ensure health and nutrition awareness, knowledge about disease prevention, and first aid skills required to respond. The HPs will continue to be decorated with posters. The project will work to the mainstream AAP standards by meaningfully engaging communities. All gender and age groups with oppressed minorities will have equal access to the project activities with decision-making privileges. Additionally, the complaint mechanism and exit interview will be continued to improve HP services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Meeting the healthcare needs of the FDMN population represents a pivotal imperative, particularly in ensuring the respectful and adequate provision of essential services within health post facilities. This diverse community faces a multitude of health challenges, including malnutrition, illiteracy, chronic illnesses, mental health disorders, and infectious disease outbreaks. Access to healthcare services is further hindered for individuals with disabilities, women, children, and transgender persons due to the lack of age, gender, and disability-responsive infrastructure and service delivery orientation in health post facilities. These inadequacies more often include the absence of gender-balanced staff, separate patient waiting areas, separate consultation and examination rooms, gender-separate toilets, common toilets, toilets equipped for disabled individuals, and prompt care initiatives in triage corners for the elderly, as well as features like ramps, child-play spaces, adolescent corners, and breastfeeding areas. The services also needs to extend to sexual and reproductive health, family planning, maternity care, and menstrual hygiene management. Furthermore, the prevalence of non-communicable diseases such as hypertension and diabetes mellitus, as well as common communicable diseases like tuberculosis and cancers related to reproductive health, poses a substantial health burden for many Rohingya refugees. Data from the HAEFA's digitized health record system in the Rohingya camps underscores the high prevalence of HTN, DM, TB, and reproductive cancer, with DM and HTN affecting one in three adults. CDs like TB and vaccine-preventable diseases, including CaCx, are sources of concern but have been insufficiently addressed in healthcare interventions for Rohingya refugees. This prevailing uncertainty and stress contribute to widespread mental health issues, emphasizing the urgent need for essential healthcare services, community surveillance, and referral systems to address these issues and reduce stigma. These concerns about healthcare services can be effectively mitigate by providing the necessary resources for comprehensive screening, diagnosis, and treatment, supported by an electronic medical record system to monitor community health outcomes. The proposed intervention acknowledges that in resource-constrained settings, increasing awareness, implementing screening, and intervening through a primary healthcare strategy can significantly reduce the need for more expensive and complex interventions. The project's goal is to utilize both primary and secondary prevention, as well as promotive and curative approaches, to alleviate the healthcare burden within this population. Demographically, the influx of FDMNs has significantly strained the healthcare system in Ukhiya Upazila, making essential healthcare services nearly unattainable for the large resident population. Remarkably similar healthcare needs are observed within the host community, especially in the areas of NCD and CD management, reproductive healthcare, and maternity care. Host community members face substantial barriers to healthcare access, with minimal reporting easy and safe access. Additionally, cultural taboos and a lack of knowledge have dissuaded host community members from seeking medical assistance for sexual health issues, thereby exacerbating stigmatization and fear. In Bangladesh, CDs account for 27% of mortality (WHO, 2021), while NCDs contribute to 67% of mortality, including cardiovascular diseases (30%), cancers (12%), chronic respiratory diseases (10%), and diabetes (3%) (who.int), and reproductive cancer accounts for 11.5% of mortality, as indicated by from Mabeya et al. (2012). In response to the pressing needs, the proposed project aims to provide equitable and essential healthcare services, extending its reach to the various unions of Ukhiya, to address the neglected requirements of this diverse population comprehensively and compassionately.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will conform to the rationalisation principle of "adapting an integrated approach" to a considerable extent. To prevent duplication, the initiative will assure that services are provided in the form of primary healthcare interventions from the two of Health Posts situated at Balukhali Camp 09 and Kutupalong Camp 1W with Pathological Laboratory and USG Corners, with activities linked with the services of the other secondary healthcare partners, creating an effective referral pathway for the secondary healthcare services and proper management of primary care. Additionally, under the suggested approach, services would be consolidated and the same for all health posts, ensuring a decrease in overhead expenses. The project will continue to participate in camp-level coordination platforms actively; additionally, meetings and seminars with local leaders (Majhis) and beneficiaries will also continue to be held for the effective engagement of the community and government authorities in the project activities and decision-making. The project will also adhere to the “Ensure predictability and continuity” principle of rationalisation, whereas yearly funding will be attained likewise in the previous years it attained yearly for both health posts. Consequently, stability in refugee operations, particularly in essential primary healthcare intervention for the refugee humanitarian response, will be addressed, as will major requirements in the host community. Since the influx of Rohingya FDMN in Bangladesh in October 2017, Health and Education for All (HAEFA) has been undertaking similar projects from the two health posts with coordination from DGHS, MOHFW, NGOAB, RRRC, DC, CiC, UNO, and Health Sector, and also plans to provide the primary vital healthcare services in future ventures in the same sector. The project will continue to coordinate with beneficiaries, local leaders (Majhis), and government representatives, and UN organizations on a constant basis.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is firmly grounded in the core principles of prevention, response, and remedy. The intervention is driven by a commitment to ensure that all gender and age groups have equitable and meaningful access to comprehensive quality healthcare services provided by healthcare professionals. The project will place a particular emphasis on addressing the needs of oppressed minorities, including children, women, elderly and transgender individuals along with those with disabilities.The Health Post infrastructure and service delivery are thoughtfully designed to maintain an unwavering focus on equality and inclusivity in all aspects. It incorporates features that enhance accessibility, effectively removing barriers for oppressed minorities while safeguarding their dignity and privacy.Community engagement is a cornerstone of the project approach. The initiative actively involves the community in project activities and the decision-making process through meetings and seminars. Recommendations are tailored to individual requirements, ensuring that the community's voice is heard and respected.Furthermore, the intervention seeks to empower and educate the community. Prioritizing health and nutrition awareness, imparting knowledge about disease prevention and management, and equipping individuals with essential emergency response and first aid skills. This empowers the community to take charge of their health and safety.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project is committed to ensuring equitable and comprehensive access to healthcare services for all age groups and gender groups, with a particular focus on children, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities.To achieve this, the project will implement activities that adhere to child safeguarding standards. Health Post services will prioritize assessing BMI (height and weight), detecting child malnutrition (MAM/SAM) using MUAC, and providing adolescent care, counseling, education on behavior modification, and guidance on accepting secondary sexual traits and managing menstrual hygiene. Child play areas in both Health Posts will be ensured to reduce accidents and lost cases while children wait for care.Efforts will be made to facilitate easier access for individuals with disabilities and the elderly without compromising dignity. A priority card system will be facilitated in the triage area to ensure prompt care for the elderly, along with the provision of wheelchairs, and crutches.Both Health Posts will maintain infrastructure and service delivery that is responsive to age, gender, and disability. This includes having gender-balanced staff, separate waiting areas for patients, separate consultation and examination rooms, gender-separate toilets, and toilets equipped for disabled individuals. Ramps and other accessibility features will be ensured at all healthcare facilities to improve access for people with disabilities, and existing approaches will be modified to reduce barriers.The introduction of common toilets will ensure easier access for transgender individuals while maintaining confidentiality for all users, including healthcare workers.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is dedicated to upholding the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) standards by actively involving the community. This commitment extends to all segments of the population, encompassing women, girls, men, boys, and marginalized groups, including transgender individuals, those with disabilities, and the elderly, having equal and comprehensive access to the project activities with decision-making privileges. The Health Points (HPs) will maintain anonymous and confidential feedback and complaint mechanisms, enabling enhancements in post-service healthcare. To address the needs of affected populations, the project will conduct meetings and seminars involving local leaders and beneficiaries. Recommendations arising from these interactions will be tailored to address individual requirements.Furthermore, the medical team at the HPs will hold monthly QA sessions with beneficiaries to explore new inclusions and improvements, all of which will be inspired by beneficiaries' prior input. The HP care support can also be easily accessed via phone and email, with contact information prominently displayed at the HP's entrance and on signboards. This comprehensive approach will actively promote AAP standards.Prioritizing the continual improvement of Health Post (HP) services and the identification and resolution of any gaps, patient exit interviews will be conducted.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

To mainstream PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), the project will conform to all PSEA Principles and Standards.Both the implementing partner and the appealing partner in this project are active members of the PSEA Network. This ensures that all project stakeholders are committed to PSEA principles and actively engage in PSEA networks and efforts.The project will also ensure that PSEA is integrated into every stage of the project cycle, from planning and design to implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. Also, incorporate PSEA measures into project documentation, risk assessments, and activities. All staff, including volunteers, have received and will continue to receive mandatory PSEA training. The training covers the basics of PSEA principles, guidelines, and reporting procedures. Training will be ongoing and updated regularly.PSEA awareness materials such as posters, flyers, and videos, which include key PSEA messages and information about Complaint Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs), will be visible to beneficiaries at the HPs, which will help in educating and empowering beneficiaries to understand their rights and how to report any incidents.The project will also continue its efforts to maintain Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms that are confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate. The mechanisms will encourage individuals to report incidents without fear of retaliation or stigma, ensuring that multiple channels for reporting, including both formal and informal options.The project commits to taking necessary actions following receiving a complaint in line with established procedures. This involves investigations, providing support to survivors, and taking appropriate disciplinary measures against wrongdoers.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In alignment with DRR/DRM component and environmental concerns, the project places significant emphasis on community health education, disaster risk management, and emergency health response initiatives by deploying community healthcare workers, offering counseling services, and conducting awareness campaigns. The house-to-house model, implemented by community healthcare workers, will be instrumental in ensuring that communities are well-informed about health and nutrition, disaster risk management, emergency preparedness, first aid skills, and the essential knowledge needed to prevent and respond to disasters, thereby safeguarding the well-being of individuals and minimizing the future damage and loss of life . This initiative will empower and educate the community to take proactive measures in response to health emergencies and disasters as they occur. To address the critical need for primary education in emergency health response and disaster management, monthly meetings and seminars will be organized involving camp leaders, local authorities, and government officials.In concern to environmental issues, the Health Posts, and Pathological Laboratory disposes of biomedical waste, which includes wastewater, treated through the Health Post's Waste Treatment Plant (WTP), furthermore, waste entailing pharmaceutical waste, sharp waste, and infectious solid waste, are managed through the use of facility incinerators, are essential for the preservation of the environment and the enhancement of overall community life quality.
