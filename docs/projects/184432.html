Project Summary

As per HNO 2022, it is estimated that 3 million school-aged children – 45 per cent of which are girls – need sustained equitable access to quality education in a protective learning environment. Out of these children, 2,419,059 are from non-displaced families and 667,891 are from displaced families. Despite the low caseload of IDPs, children living in IDP camps are particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to limited provision of services and poor living conditions. A recent detailed site assessment (DSA) 2021 confirmed low education service provision in IDP camps in some Banedir districts such as KAXDA and Heliwa. The increase of children in need of education assistance in 2022 is also attributed to the increased population, COVID-19 pandemic, economic deterioration, ongoing environmental shocks and conflict. In addition, Children with disabilities are significantly affected and face challenges to access an inclusive learning environment. Recent reports identified prevalent negative attitudes towards children with disabilities by teachers and parents, and structural challenges that hinder these children to access an inclusive learning environment.
The negative impact of covid 19 situation has also had grave implications on children's education as some have dropped out of the schools and indulge themselves in child labour in the local markets. SOS CV Somalia has documented a significant increase of children on the streets and local markets following the closures of the schools. As a result, many of these boys and girls will need concreted efforts to bring them back to schools using multiple approaches, including parents and community mobilisation and awareness interventions.
These Emergencies have had a significant impact upon children’s education over the years in Somalia. The education sector analysis 2021 highlighted the impact of emergencies on education as the enrolment gains made in recent years saw a setback. To retain children in school, SOS Children propose mitigative activities to provide access to education in emergencies for displaced and vulnerable children by establishing protective learning environment.
the impact of COVID-19 on household resources has also led to a reduction of children’s participation and retention in school, as demonstrated by the increased percentage of families who identified worsening financial constraints in 2021 as the most significant barrier to supporting their children’s schooling. It is assumed that schools will continue to struggle in the current academic year with meeting requirements for a COVID-19-preventive environment.
The crisis in Somalia has also significantly contributed to social norms and values that have reinforced gender inequality and greatly contributed to the vulnerability of children, particularly girls in Somalia. Girls consistently face greater challenges to accessing education . These are attributed cultural practices such early marriage, school drops out when most girls reach puberty age and girls become increasing vulnerable and require appropriate facilities for proper menstrual hygiene management. Most of schools have no sanitary kits in schools. More so, school teachers and management don’t have PSS and GBV skills which further contribute to girls school dropout.
Child Protection also in schools remains weak. Most of the schools have unprotective learning environment in IDPS and informal settlement in urban areas. These includes lack of disability friendly learning environments locking down children with disabilities from accessing education facilities.
This proposed intervention will target 6000 (3000 girls and 3000 boys) vulnerable IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Heliwa, Balacad, jowhar district including CWD. To ensure inclusivity, the project will ensure 600 (10%) of the target group with be children with disabilities will be provided access through an inclusive education approach and improve access to schools for CWD.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed project will ensure that the most vulnerable crisis affected children have access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities through provision of a combination of safety, socialization, cognitive and skills development to prepare children to be active and resilient members of their community and society. SOS CV will design this project to ensure that services and aid delivered will protect all segments of the target population and will not put some at risk, especially girls. As a strategy, gender will be significantly entrenched in this project, to ensure protective environments for girls and boys. The project will also integrate psychosocial support activities into the schools using CFS approaches. These include Set-up 60 temporary learning spaces and renovate/rehabilitate existing public and community schools’ spaces in target schools. Each learning space will also be equipped with furniture. Provide basic teaching and learning supplies and recreational materials for 6000 (50% girls) vulnerable girls and boy (including learning resources for learners with disabilities, dignity kits) in 40 targeted public/IDPs and community schools. Identify and support a total of 120 volunteer teachers (30% female) with monthly emergency incentives. Provide in service training for 200 teachers (30% women) with a focus on improving equity and sustainability in the education profession and integrate life-saving messages on key issues on education in emergencies (such as risk reduction, gender-based violence and peace education) and on child centred, inclusive teaching, and fundamental basics of pedagogy, child protection and psycho social support, in an emergency setting in Hilwa, Jowhar and Balcad, districts. Construction/rehabilitation of 40 temporary gender sensitive latrines and hand washing facilities (in consultation with WASH cluster) in 20 target public and private schools in the target districts. The project will also improve school learning environment for all children to access it. These include improving school physical environment, provision of dignity kits to girls The project will provide community-based PSS and resilience activities aimed towards coping and strengthening protective environment for children and their parents. The project will establish 10 Child right groups at school level and provide psychosocial support (PSS) workshops (MHPSS) services to key targeted beneficiaries in view of increasing child resilience in IDP and surrounding communities. The project will facilitate the organization of events in coordination with respective Schools management and CECS and local authorities. Provision of sports facilities will also be prioritized to heighten students’ motivation to go to school and because playing enhances learning ability as well as it empowers both girls and boyTrain school children on Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) and Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) and conduct interactive hygiene and sanitation campaigns in 20 schools, to include messages on COVID 19, hand washing, drinking safe water and environmental hygiene within the school and its surrounding, among other important sanitation & hygiene messages. The messages will be formulated using the WASH cluster guidelines. provide awareness creation on COVID 19 adopting UNICEF and WHO IEC and communication material with local languages. Establish CFS in target school
