Project Summary

The draft 2020 Humanitarian needs overview (HNO) estimates that more than 20% of Somalis (2.7 million people), including one third of the IDPs (0.8M) in the country, require support to access basic WASH services in 2020. Inadequate access to WASH services is a leading cause of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) and Malnutrition, especially where health services are too few and/or too distant (23% of non-displaced and 35% of IDPs do not have access to a health care facility). It is also a cause of relapse when treated patients and children return to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health programs. Lack of access to water and adequate sanitation in schools is also a factor contributing to poor school attendance.
IMC proposes emergency water, sanitation and hygiene intervention to respond to the needs of vulnerable and conflict affected IDPs and host communities in Bay (Baidoa), Banadir (Mogadishu/Kahda), Middle Shabelle (Jowhar) and Mudug (Galkacyo South) regions of South Central Somalia to ensure WASH related gaps contributing to mortality and morbidity are mitigated. These are regions where IMC has been providing integrated health, nutrition, protection and WASH interventions and has noted critical WASH gaps. Findings from various assessments done in all 4 regions evidenced that IDP camps are suffering from water scarcity linked to lack of access to water sources. In chronically drought-affected districts like Galkacyo South, lack of access to water for humans and for livestock remains one of the leading causes of displacement, conflict and diseases. This has already negatively impacted women and children who are walking long distances to fetch water and face higher risk of violence or other protection related risks.
In Kahda district, a baseline survey conducted by IMC in September 2019 revealed that out of 1985 households surveyed, only 72% wash their hands after defecation, 23% before handling food, 5% after eating and 1% before feeding the child. 43% of the respondents treat water before drinking whereas 57% consume untreated water. There is a limited understanding of the risks surrounding drinking contaminated water and gaps in promoting handwashing before feeding children who are most susceptible to diarrhea and other respiratory infections.
Kaxda region has the highest number of households with no access to latrines (64%) of all 4 proposed regions. IMC WASH team observed children are often urinating and defecating in open areas in Kaxda. The team also noted high protection risks for women and girls using makeshift structures as washrooms and trekking long distances to fetch or buy water.
Indiscriminate disposal of solid waste has become an increasing health concern especially at the IDP camps. Solid Waste Management sites are very poor or limited. The accumulated wastes act as breeding ground for vectors and vermin, which are harmful to the health of the IDPs and by extension, host communities. Solid waste emanating from IDP households has accumulated at the targeted IDP camps due to lack of proper management of the solid waste. Through this proposed intervention, IMC will aim to reduce the risks associated with the poor management of the waste, including environmental pollution, outbreak of diseases and breeding of disease vectors/vermin.There has been limited space for excavation of refuse pits as IDPs live in overcrowded and privately owned plots particularly in Kaxda (Mogadishu) and Harhar villages (Galkacyo South).
It is on the basis of these identified needs that IMC is proposing interventions that are feasible and appropriate. IMC underscores the importance of ensuring WASH related gaps contributing to mortality and morbidity are mitigated, therefore the proposed interventions are suited for the needs and the context to ensure an effective and flexible emergency response that avoids duplication of services.

Project Activities

1. Rehabilitation of 10 shallow wells with installation of hand pumps and solar power (specifically to 6 high yielding shallow wells: > 5m3/hour). For the solar powered wells, elevated 10m3 water reservoirs will deliver water through 6 tap stands strategically located around the water source. Dedicated WASH committees (3 men and 2 women) will be selected at each site to manage rehabilitated water facilities and ensure safety and security of solar panels. 2. Short term Emergency water trucking of safe, potable water. IMC proposes serialized water vouchers stating the safe water entitlements per day (based on family size), in 5 Baidoa IDP settlements which do not have boreholes or shallow wells. In case of deterioration in drought situation in other locations, IMC will extend water trucking to Galkacyo South and Jowhar districts. IMC will provide 2,240, 20-liter containers/Jerry cans (2 per household lacking storage containers). Where central distribution stations/facilities do not exist, IMC will procure 10m3 liters fabricated tank in each of the identified IDP camp strategically located in areas accessible to the most vulnerable, including women and girls.3. Minor rehabilitation of boreholes in hard-to-reach rural nomadic villages in Galkacyo South district. This will include replacement of generator and borehole spare parts and rehabilitation of water storage facilities and animal troughs.4. Improving water quality at all levels of the water supply chain, including the rehabilitated water sources and point of use (household level). IMC will adopt a mixed approach to provide safe water to the beneficiaries through use of bulk water treatment (batch chlorination) and bucket water treatment. To complement routine water quality monitoring, IMC will occasionally undertake bacteriological tests. In the event of an AWD/Cholera outbreak in areas of operation, IMC Community hygiene promoters (CHPs) who are part of emergency rapid response team (ERRTs), will initiate bucket chlorination at water sources.5. Construction of 80 gender segregated emergency VIP latrines with hand washing facilities and bathing units in IDP camps. Design and location of latrines, bathing/ washing slabs will comply with SPHERE standards and will consider Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities and sanitation materials/equipment.6. Hygiene promotion. IMC proposes to carry out routine and continuous hygiene promotion activities at four levels: communities, schools, water points, and health facilities, including use of Wash’em tools.7. Procurement and distribution of 4,500 hygiene kits. Hygiene kit distribution will target households with severely malnourished children, PLW, child or woman-headed households and families affected by AWD/Cholera.8. Capacity Building of community hygiene promoters and 50 WASH Committees. IMC will conduct refresher training for CHPs using the Somalia WASH Cluster endorsed hygiene promotion training curriculum.9. Install portable covered solid waste collection bins/containers at strategic locations and establish/maintain excavated solid waste disposal pits at targeted schools (9) and IDP camps in Baidoa and Mogadishu- Kaxda. The activity aims to reduce risks associated with poor management of waste including: environmental pollution, outbreak of diseases and breeding of disease vectors/vermin. IMC proposes inclusion of a school WASH program (school hygiene/sanitation clubs) to enhance awareness and coverage of solid waste risk mitigation at all levels of IDP and host communities. IMC will work closely with teachers and 9 school administrations to come up with mechanisms of improving solid waste management.10. Engage 80 solid waste management volunteers to increase coverage in IDP camps and lead monthly clean-up campaigns in each IDP camp. IMC will provide volunteers with personal protective equipment (PPE), waste collection/storage equipment and a stipend for drinking water and food during clean-up exercises.

Mitigation Measures

1b. • IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkage of all the WASH activities with Health/Nutrition/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. 2a. • Beneficiary selection will be systematic to ensure vulnerable persons, such as older people, marginalized minorities and PWD are not excluded. • Outreach messaging will be respectful inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from different sociodemographic, economic and cultural groups know about WASH services, distributions times/sites and how to use the items. • WASH staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, socioeconomic group, disability). 2b.• Women, men, girls, boys and persons with specific needs (persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised persons) will be consulted at all stages of the WASH intervention, to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences. Shallow well/Borehole pump design will also consider their physical capacity of children and other groups in the community. • IMC will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Hygiene promotion activities will involve women and men and will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. Hygiene promotion activities will also consider child-friendly formats (ie. Cartoons) for the community contexts and the 9 selected schools. • WASH committees and teams will be gender balanced to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. 3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of IMC Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • IMC will engage with local IDP and community leaders, the community and WASH committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. 3b. • All activities will ensure that protection concerns voiced by women related to risk of GBV especially in IDP settlements are taken into consideration. • All IMC WASH staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • All IMC WASH staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) 3c. • IMC will locate water sources and water trucking points in visible, central locations and ensure these are accessible by vulnerable population groups and women and girls. • Hygiene kit distributions to take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. In some cases IMC may consider door-to-door distribution of hygiene kits. • With the support of IMC Protection teams, IMC will consider introducing risk mitigation activities such as provision of solar lamps / torches for each VIP latrine, accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls. • Sanitation infrastructure will be sex segregated and clearly marked (including pictorials), and will be equipped with locks on the inside at varying heights, to enhance privacy and provide safety including for children. WASH facilities will include Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities, catering for dignity and private disposal of personal hygiene items. 4b. • IMC maintains a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
