Project Summary

The overall of the project activities is to support disaster affected populations in Hiran and Bari regions to protect their livelihoods, support local food production and prevent adoption of crisis strategies. In order to reach the objective of the project, AADSOM will carryout the following outputs: AADSOM will increase access to food and improve nutrition through food voucher to 1000 IDPs and host communities HHs in Bari and Hiran regions, benefiting around 6,000 women, men, boys and girls; AADSOM will Strength the livelihoods of 5,100 affected households representing 30,600 women, men, boys and girls in protecting and restoring their livelihood assets through rehabilitation of productive assets in CFW; supporting 2000 farmers with land preparation and input distribution; supporting 600 fisheries with boats and fishery gears; and distributing 10,000 goats to 2000pastoralists who have lost their animals during the disasters. To keep the sustainability of the project and increase the production of the beneficiaries, AADSOM will train selected beneficiaries from the different livelihoods on the best technologies and methodologies to increase their livelihoods production. During the project implementation, women, men, children, disables and IDPs will be heard their voices and consulted.

Needs

Despite improvements in the FS and large-scale of humanitarian assistance there are still, acute food insecurity situation, malnutrition and livelihood needs. 2 in 5 Somalis remain acutely food insecure, more than 2.6M people IDPs and the seasonal assessment results indicated severe levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition among IDPs. In Hiran, continued rise of the river in March and April was supposed to enhance the opportunities for crop cultivation, but conditions were deteriorated after the flooding occurred, where the cultivated crop and properties inundated and pastoral communities have lost their animals during the three consecutive droughts. In Bari, heavy rainfall, cyclone and dangerous flash floods that hit coastal areas, resulted in the loss of lives, crops and livestock as well as the destruction of property and infrastructure, including the fishing boats and gears. The cyclone has also worsened the humanitarian situation in Puntland, which have experienced protracted drought dating back to 2015, leaving them particularly prone to flash flooding in the direct aftermath of massive downpours. Over 211,000 people are facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in Hiran (41,000) and Bari (170,000) regions and need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support. 282,000 people who are stressed (IPC Phase 2) also require livelihood support. 32,000 and 11,000 people in Bari and Hiran respectively are IPC 4, while 1000 in Bossaso IDP and 2000 in Belet Weyne IDPs & urban are in IPC 5 and need urgent food security and livelihood support (FSNAU report Sep. 2018). The residual impact from the drought, combined with floods, cyclone, continue to undermine opportunities for food security improvements especially for the most vulnerable people. The forecasted El Nino and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected to contribute an increase in production while at the same time risks of shocks, including loss of production are real.

Activities

CO1: Improve 1000 households’ immediate access to food through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoods.Act.1: Provide Food Vouchers to improve the nutrition and food security to 1,000 IDP & Host Community HHs in Bari (500) and Hiran (500) Regions for 3 months, benefitting around 6000 men, women, girls and boys. CO2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support to 4100 households in rural and peri-urban settings.Act.1: Distribute farm inputs and tools to 2000HHs (1200women & 800men) and prepare their farmland (2000ha) with tractor in Hiran & L/Sh regions; each hector with 3 hours’ time for plough, benefiting 12000men, women, boys and girls. The farm input composed farm tools, fertilizers and seeds.Act.2: Distribute restocking 10,000 goats to 2000 pastoral HHs (5 goats/HH), representing 12000 men, women, boys and girls, who lost their animals.Act.3: Distribute 10 boats and fishing gear to 100 fisher folks in Bari region, who have lost their fishing assets during the cyclone. Benefiting around 600 men, women, girls and boys. Each 10-fisher folks will be regrouped and will be given fishing gear and one boat. Act.4: Train 300 farmers (100men & 200women), 300 pastorals (100men & 200women) and 200 fishermen for the best farming methodologies & technologies, animal husbandry and modern fishing technologies respectively. CO3: Support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets to 2000HHs in Hiran and L/Shabelle regions, enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness.Act. 1: Rehabilitate 5 primary canals &1 15km water catchments in Hiran & L/Sh. regions respectively through CFW benefiting 2,000 workers, which represent 12,000 men, women, girls and children.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Preventing displacement and IDPs’ protection within their country is mostly an obligation of the state but in circumstances where the national authorities need support or where state protection is not guaranteed, this role cascades to the international community. But protection gaps not only results from the reluctance of states to guarantee the displaced persons’ rights but also the insufficiency of state organ’s capacity to provide the necessary protection. And as such, protection gaps identification is a necessary for prioritization and development of measures to support protection capability. Protection can be seen as an objective which requires full respect for the right of all without discrimination and limitation to survival and physical security but covers the full range of rights, including civil and political rights, like freedom of movement and political participation, and economic, social and cultural rights, such as the rights to health, food security, shelter, WASH and education. Thus, in this project, IDPs will be part of the project beneficiaries, heard their voices and will be part of the established implementing committees to restore their dignity.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project is aimed to improve the livelihoods and food security of the IDPs and host communities. During the selection of the beneficiaries female headed households, families with disables, family with malnourished children and women, and the most vulnerable households whether is headed by men and women will be given priority. During the project implementation men, women and people in disabilities will be consulted and they will be part of the implementing committees.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Somalia in general is fragile state in terms of security. Conflicts between clans, and insurgents and government/AMISOM are common. Constant fighting that hampers efforts to reach the needy women, men, girls and boys is another challenge. Bari: This region is stable comparing to other parts of Somalia. AADSOM will work closely with the state government line minister and community prominent leaders, elders, women groups and IDP leaders. If any security issue arise, those groups will be engaged to sort out before things go out of hand. In general, the security situation in Puntland has shown signs of improvement as police and community representatives, including elders, elites and religious leaders, have combined efforts to improve security. Hiran: The security of Hiran is relatively stable as the government with the support of AMISOM controls the region, although the rural areas is not safe, as the insurgents controls most of the rural areas and have blockage to the roads that connects the towns. AADSOM has good relation with the clan elders in the region since it has been working the region through out its establishment. AADSOM also has good understanding of the security context in the area. A security checklist is provided on monthly basis by the field security focal point at AADSOM’s office in Belet Weyne. AADSOM anticipates that the project can be implemented smoothly without any security threats and challenges. In the event the insecurity prevents staff movements, it is anticipated that it would be for short periods only and coordination can continue by phone until the situation calm down and travel can be resumed. All other assumptions can be addressed by negotiations and discussion with local authorities that may cause delays, but are normally successful in dealing with challenges that arise.
