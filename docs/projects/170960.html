Project Summary

HI in partnership with The Culture and Free Thought Association(CFTA) and in cooperation with 19 local organizations members of the emergency committee, designed a 10-months action aiming to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable people with/without disability to minimize the impact of acute crisis through improving their substandard shelters and enhancing access to the physical stockpiling of emergency NFIs. The proposed intervention is fully aligned with the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview. It also support the achievement of 2020 HRP Shelter & NFI Strategic Objectives, specifically Objective 2 “the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”, as well as Objective 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”.
The project is needs-based designed and respond to the findings and recommendation of different recent assessment conducted by HI, NRC and EducAid, and to the requests of the Disability Working Group. It responds to the main gaps faced by people with disability (PWDs) from the Gaza Strip during the COVID crisis: lack of access to healthy, safe and accessible environment in the individual shelter; and, due to the ongoing economic crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic, no accessibility to dignity and infection protection and control (IPC) kits, and need of NFIs that meets their basic needs.
The project aims at upgrading substandard shelters through the rehabilitation and adaptation of 100 shelters of persons with disability (50% women, 50% men) highly impacted by the deteriorated economic situation and the COVID-19 crisis .
A set of criteria, including Shelter Cluster criteria, will be applied in the identification of the most vulnerable households and reviewed by HI, partners and Organizations of Persons with Disability (OPD) member of Disability Representative Bodies Network (DRBN). The intervention follows the HI international accessibility standards and the minimum standards of the Shelter Cluster taking in consideration the full participation of the targeted beneficiaries in the rehabilitation process. The accessibility focal points, trained by HI during a previous project, and DRBN will monitor that the accommodations and rehabilitation performed by HI are responsive to the needs of targeted beneficiaries and committed to the gender and accessibility perspective.
On its second outcome the project will enable the current members of the emergency committee to replenish their physical stockpiling with 2440 emergency NFIs to be stored in the existing 5 partners’ contingency stores located one in each governorate. An inventory system was well-established and allowes to have clear indication on the stock available, expiring date and distribution of items. The emergency stocks will respond to the needs of most vulnerable groups among persons with/without disability in case of emergency crisis, HI already activated its stock during the Great March of Return (GMR) and the ongoing COVID-19 crisis. In addition, the project will support the local emergency actors’ members of the emergency committee to regularly update their emergency preparedness and response plans with full involvement of service users’ representative to ensure their needs are taken care of 150 emergency volunteers (75 women, 75 men) trained by a previous project will be mobilized, and regular capacity building will be conducted through various online and face to face tools to ensure their capabilities to respond to the needs of most vulnerable groups like persons with disability, elderly, women and children during any foreseen emergency.

1 - Needs

In “Disability considerations during the COVID-19 outbreak” WHO highlighted how persons with disability may be impacted more significantly by COVID-19, because of barriers in implementing basic hygiene measures and difficulty in enacting social distancing. Person with disabilities are also affected by the environmental, behavioural and institutional barriers, and ensuring non-discrimination and leaving no one behind principles will be possible only identifying and responding to their needs and rights.According to Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, in 2017 the persons with disabilities in oPt are 255,228, among them 127,96 live in Gaza Strip (6.3% men, 5.4% women- 6.8% of the total population). To tackle the needs of children with disabilities, people with disability and caregivers in the context of the sanitary crisis, HI conducted a rapid needs assessment in September 2020 identify the gaps faced in accessing non-food items, safe and accessible environment in Gaza Strip. The assessment targeted 287 persons (67% are persons with disability) from whom 43% women 57% men though interviews, and focus groups were conducted including decision-makers and field workers in active UN agencies, international and national organizations. According to the results 78% of targeted persons reported that their homes are not healthy, safe nor consider accessible environment. In EducAid Covid-19 Assessment (Sept 2020), 42% of people with disabilities result dependent and rely on family members to eat, dress, using toilet and searching on internet this expose them to a higher risk to contract the virus. The need of independency to be autonomous increases due to the protective measure and in case of quarantine of them or the caregivers if they get tested positive. The need of shelter rehabilitation, improvement and adaptation was previously highlighted in December 2019 by Ministry of Public Works and Housing Assessment, that identified 16,985 shelters in needs of repair or rehabilitation, were 29% of targeted households include either a PWD or a person with chronic disease and 14.57% of substandard shelters are headed by women.The technical needs for adaptation and rehabilitation of shelters for PWDs proposed by HI will take into consideration the specific needs of increase safety and security ensuring the protection and safety condition of the substandard shelter through the installation of usable and clear evacuation route, repairing ceiling insolation, plumping works, fixing windows, install outside lights and intercom systems. To respond to the identify need of enhance mobility inside and around the house stairlift or banister on the stairs can be installed, as also stair rails, ramps, widening doorways. These actions will guarantee higher autonomy and reducing the need of support and contact, and consequently reducing risk of Covid-19 transmission, with caregivers or others. The ability to perform self-care, leisure and productive activities as independent as possible, including ability to perform hygiene self-care (i.e. washing hands) will be increase with the installation of grab rails in the bathroom, lowering worktops in the kitchen and improving the accessibility. The occupational therapy assessment should identify the main obstacles and barriers that limit the users’ participation in family and community activities and to identify the user’s priorities of intervention.Another finding of HI’ rapid assessment is related to the no accessibility to dignity and infection protection and control (IPC) kits, perceived as important for their resilience (89% of the targeted people highlighted the gap). Interviewees also stated the lack of attention on specific needs of people with disabilities, especially women and children, in the selection of items included in those kits. Moreover, NRC’Covid-19 rapid assessment (April 2020) identifies an increase of price for food and hygiene items and the incapacity to afford hygiene items such as soap or detergent by the 91% of the interviewees in Gaza Strip, and for this reason 63% of the HHs report an urgent need for hygiene items.The current measures countering the health, exacerbate the ongoing protracted crisis in Gaza Strip bringing the unemployment rate to 46% during the first quarter of 2020 (PNGO,2020) and families reported not having enough money to stockpile the specific food and non-food (COVID-19 Response Plan for oPt, OCHA 2020). The access to food and NFI to meet the basic needs is not only affected by the economic power but can also limited by political reason. During the month of August 2020, Karem Shalom crossing point had been closed as response to the escalation of violence blocking the import of items. The 24th of August the first positive case had been detected at the community level and the availability of supporting items in the 5 HI Emergency stocks, located one in each Governorate, allowed the distribution of NFIs to the most vulnerable households including person with disability and children with disability overcome the limitation of movement between Governorates and the reinforcement of the blockade.

2 - Activities

The project targets 100 vulnerable households, especially with person with disability lacking shelter basic needs highly impacted by the deteriorated economic situation and COVID-19 by improving 100 substandard shelters of PWD (50% women, 50% men) in Gaza Strip. The shelter rehabilitation will enhance the accessibility and consequently the autonomy of PWDs and the health and hygiene standards reducing the health risk and removing barriers to access wash and hygiene facilities within individuals’ shelters. This will be achieved through a sequence of activities: 1.1 Identifying 100 substandard shelters of persons with disability (50% women, 50% men) in urgent needs for rehabilitation interventions; 1.2 Elaboration and signature of agreements with targeted HHs concerning the rehabilitation construction interventions; 1.3 Conducting the rehabilitation intervention in of the identified 100 substandard shelters.HI will closely coordinate the action with the Shelter Cluster. The identification and prioritization of the vulnerable households will be in line with the shelter criteria and the HI criteria including: socioeconomic aspect; family’s size; PWD living in the house; effect of barriers on participation of beneficiary in family activities and in the community; applicability and feasibility of intervention, and received home adaptation works.Through the database of HI and its partners as well as the referral network, will identify 150 homes of persons with disabilities in urgent needs home-adaptation. Specific needs of each beneficiary, with clear understating of the general situation of the family, priorities of the targeted beneficiaries, and physical barriers will be identified and an accessibility audit assessment will be conducte. Moreover, surveys, field visits and interview to the beneficiaries and their families will be realized with the participation of an Accessibility focal point (PWDs trained in previous HI action). Ideas and drawings for construction and adaptation works based on the identified needs, accessibility audits, interviews and available budget will include the recommendations of the targeted beneficiaries, technical team and occupational therapist, in respect of the ISO international standards for accessibility. Coping with COVID-19 restriction, HI and his contractors will work according to the Ministry of Health and Ministry of Interior recommendations and HI guidelines on construction activities during the current health crisis All health protection measures including sanitization of the place of work before and after each working day will be applied and requested as contract condition to the contractor.The second project’s outcome aims at enhancing the capacity of local partners’ members in the established emergency committee to respond to the needs of most vulnerable people with/without disabilities during local natural or manmade hazards in a timely efficient and effective manner. Though a BMZ-funded project, HI established 5 contingency stocks, one per each Governorate, where a list of NFIs including hygiene, infection protection and control kit (COVID 19 family Hygiene and cleaning kit ), kitchen and wound dressing kits, Assistive Devices (AD), battery for hearing aid device, selected based on the Shelter standards and HI previous experience, are stockpiled and ready to purchase in case of emergency assistance. The emergency stocks were activated in response of GMR and also in the last months within the COVID-19 pandemic ; expected funding will enable HI to replenishing the established physical stockpiling of emergency NFIs. These actions will respond to gaps in accessing dignity and infection protection and control (IPC) kits, especially for women and children, underlined in the HI rapid assessment, access to hygiene items, needs resulted from NRC Covid assessment. Moreover, it will respond to raised gaps by Humanitarian Country Team who highlighted that many persons with disabilities depend on services that have been suspended and families reported not having enough money to stockpile the specific food and non-food items. This will be achieved through a sequence of activities: 2.1 Mainstream gender in the Emergency Preparedness and Response Plans to support IDPs and affected people with/without disability during and post emergencies situation; 2.2 Online and face-to-face training of emergency volunteers on emergency preparedness to respond to the needs of persons with/without disability in acute crisis; 2.3 Replenish physical stockpiling of emergency NFIs in 5 emergency stocks (one per each Governorate) to respond to immediate needs of persons with/without disability in acute crisis; 2.4 Raising the awareness for the targeted communities about gender equity and equal opportunism and COVID-19. The strategic partnership with Culture Free Thoughts Association (CFTA) will also improve the gender mainstreaming of the action, ensuring gender equity and equal opportunities within the different interventions of the proposed activities.CFTA’s programs are designed for children, adolescents, youth, and women with a special focus on marginalized and underprivileged groups in communities across the middle and southern Gaza Strip. CFTA coordinates closely with the shelter cluster especially for activities to distribute NFIs as blanket, mattresses, and wintering kits and gathering/sharing information on gender mainstreaming and protection concerns of vulnerable groups that supports CFTA reach most vulnerable individuals and communities. Currently, CFTA is leading gender mainstreaming activities mainly at the southern governorates of the Gaza Strip. CFTA contribution will enhance the gender mainstreaming in all the proposed action, ensuring the consideration of different gender-age needs in both sector shelter rehabilitation and emergency preparedness.

3 - Indicators

HI will ensure that the project is monitored and evaluated based on HI’s Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (PME) policy. In line with the policy, the following system will be established: - Planning: set up of project steering committees, project steering tools, and quarterly risk assessments. - Monitoring: project quarterly reviews, monthly data collection, beneficiary database, administrative and financial tools, and regular self-assessments. - Evaluation: satisfaction monitoring visits, and final participatory evaluations. Project managers and technical teams will be responsible for the implementation and monitoring of the project. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

In 2018 HI has launched the implementation of safeguarding procedure that include an internal and external complain mechanism. A well-defined, clear, safe, confidential and transparent processes had been put in place to allow individuals to report breaches or suspicions of breach of HI safeguarding policies or/and Code of conduct. The feedback and complaints mechanisms for beneficiaries and the community is put in place to guarantee the accountability to local population and are accessible for beneficiaries, community members, external stakeholders, victims or witness of an abuse or suspicion of abuse from one of HI members or HI partners. Different channel are ensure to submit the complaint: direct contact to HI staff members (all HI staff members – as any humanitarian workers – which received a complaint have the duty to report it immediately to his/her line manager, for investigation); email address or reporting system available on hi.org. The alert will be treated as soon as possible, an investigation will be conducted and if the contact had been communicate to the HI complain mechanism, HI staff will get in contact to investigate or to give feedback. As a result of the investigation a decision will be taken and a final report produced. A Whistle-blowing mechanism is put in place also for HI partners allowing them to submit feedback about the intervention and report any failure of a HI employee to comply with the protection polices and code of conduct. As a signatory to HI’s protection policies and code of conduct, each partner must establish whistle-blowing mechanisms at their level, with the support of HI and based on an assessment of their capacities. Risk analysis and mitigation procedure are developed and put in place for each action, as raising awarness session that target the beneficiaries and capacity building training to enhance the partners safeguarding policies and complaining mechanism.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In line with its strategy of intervention, HI will implement the activities of this proposed project in partnership with local organizations representing the local communities, both civil society organizations and mainstream and special schools. In a partnership relation defined into a signed agreement, HI will be responsible for the implementation of specific activities and for the support to partners, while local partners (CSOs and mainstream and special schools) will implement other activities such as services provision to girls and boys with disabilities and their parents. The beneficiaries will be active participants and contributors to the implementation and monitoring of project activities. At the level of monitoring and evaluation of the project, each project in HI has a steering committee, which includes parents’ representatives, partner’s representatives and project teams.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all beneficiaries considering theirs needs and gender, through physical access to services and through consideration of their specificity. Additionally, all project and technical tools/materials will be tailored to suit the specificity of the Gaza Strip context.
