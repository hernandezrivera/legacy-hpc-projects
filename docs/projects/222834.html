Project Summary

Proposed activities will enable targeted 60,000 beneficiaries(10000 men, 10000 women,20000 girls and 20000 boys (includes those with disabilities) in Greater Upper Nile region, who will be supported through actions that are in line with FSL Cluster 2024-2026 strategy through an integrated package of agricultural support and resilience-building initiatives. Through inter-cluster agriculture initiatives to improve social cohesion and engaging young people to support their involvement in agriculture and strengthening community resilience along agricultural value chains. Vegetable, crop and fishing livelihood kits provision will be enhanced with resilience building through training to support production including establishment of backyard gardens with linkages to markets; in addition to livestock support via treatment, vaccination and disease surveillance.
SPOCI will also link with health and nutrition facilities and provide hands on agronomic training that will include nutrition sensitive feeding and cooking practices. These initiatives will support recovery and improvement and equitable provision of diversified and sustainable access to food, incomes and improved livelihoods in addition to strengthening community capacities to coping with threats. Targeted beneficiaries will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability to Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches and do no harm approaches. Activities will ensure delivering a package of integrated activities through linkages with other sectors through coordination. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse. AAP will be mainstreamed through two way feedback including options for program adaption where feasible to incorporate key beneficiary feedback.

1. Needs

High levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by consecutive years of flooding (in 2024, at least 1.4 million people have been affected – OCHA 2024 Updates), insecurity, population displacements, poor crop and vegetable production, crop pests and livestock diseases, deteriorating economic crisis, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods (IPC Nov 2024;WFP Market Price Bulletins 2024). Sudan conflict implications have further led to influx of refugees and returnees; further exacerbating food insecurity. These will put much further strain of host communities, who are still reeling from severe flooding impacts. At the height of the lean season in 2025, at least 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (IPC November 2025). High food insecurity levels have been compounded inflation with majority being unable to afford food in addition to limited livelihood opportunities particularly among youth including people with disabilities. This has been exacerbated by bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints and increased cases of GBV along major roads (MAG Updates, 2024; NGO Forum and REACH2024). SPOCI has recently completed RRF, FAO and Korea ChildFund funded projects and just jusr started an FCDO funded project in Greater Upper Nile in collaboration with other agencies and aims to scale up actions in 2024. By delivering an integrated package of agricultural support and resilience-building initiatives, promotion of social cohesion and engaging young people to support their involvement in agriculture and strengthening community resilience along Vegetable, crop and fishing and livestock value chains.

2. Project activities

Through private sector and seed fairs support vegetable, crop and fishing including resilience training, post harvest handling and preservation and linkages to markets. Train Household Head beneficiaries on appropriate sustainable fishing practices, preservation and post-harvest handling, and marketing. Train Household Head beneficiaries on appropriate vegetable agronomic production and post-harvest handling- and preservation and marketing Establishment of kitchen gardens in collaboration with nutrition partners including trainings on cooking and nutrition Animal health service provision through training of community animal health workers to support treatment and vaccination of livestock including disease surveillance.
