Project Summary

The LWF Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) project is targeting natural disaster/flood and conflict (compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding) affected most vulnerable IDPs, and Returnees and host communities who have been affected over the past one year. LWF plans to reach 2150 HHs (having about 12900 individuals) and additional 220 individuals from IDPs, and Returnees communities in Duk, Bor South and Twic East counties through supporting emergency food production and building capacities of people and households level to build resilience to better respond to shocks. This project will contribute to objective 2 and 3 of food security and livelihoods cluster and of the South Sudan and Humanitarian Needs response plan. The project intends to reduce the food insecurity and malnutrition through enhanced emergency food production via distribution of seeds, agricultural tools, fishing kits and livestock for the most vulnerable but also to build resilience slowly reducing the dependency on food and agricultural inputs through training & capacity building on-farm production, with demonstration plots and kitchen garden created also in proximity of schools and health facilities to be of example for the community; awareness on nutritious food consumption and food waste management, capacity building on saving and income generation.
The project will ensure the sustainability of its outcome through working together with customary institution, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from beginning of the project.
The project activities will be implemented in consideration of preventive measure to prevent the spread and transmission of COVID-19.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA Flood Snapshot released on 21st of October 2020, Jonglei state have been impacted by flood with an estimated 369,891people(61,545HHS) being affected. The communities affected have fled to higher grounds to save their life’s.Close to 400,000 people have been displaced, with women, children, elderly and people with disabilities being the most affectedIn Duk County there has been torrential rain from July to October 2020. As a result of the flood, significant number of house/Tukuls were submerged and collapsed, people affected by flood faced displacement and loss of livelihood and livestock., 50% of planted cereal crops was damaged at early growth stage in June and at maturity stage in September. Significant number of domestic animals were died. Pit latrines submerged in water collapsed. Due to flood and inter clan conflicts in Duk over 50,000 individuals verse I in sever condition (RRC Inter Agency Rapid Assessment October 2020). From 20th of July 2020 to December 2020, Twic East county had been under flood due to torrential rains across the upper Nile basin that increased the water in the river. The primary dyke got broken, and the water reached Pakeer, Ajuong, Nyuak, Lith and Kongor payams of the county. This primary dyke was constructed in early 2003/2004 by heavy machineries’ (tractors). It had been separating Jonglei from direct flooding from the swamps. With the flood, the county got completely taken by water, with few areas being still protected by the secondary dyke, for example Panyagor town has been surrounded by water becoming a port for medium and small boats. There are few still few other spots in other five payams not covered by water. Except these few spots, all areas are under water. Of the 120,000 inhabitants of the counties, majority were displaced and moved to Central Equatorial states and Lakes state. In addition to the displacement, the flood caused, almost 85% of houses/Tukuls to be under water and many collapsed,100% of planted cereal crops were damaged at early growth stage in June and at maturity stage in September, over 3000 goats died due to flood, schools, health centers, latrines had been under water, boreholes have been flooded. After the displacement at end of November’20, there were about 20,000 people remaining in Twic East county. In February 2021 reports indicate that some returnees are coming back to the county, with hope that the water level is reducingSimilar to Twic East county, Jalle community of Bor south county were displaced by flood in August 2020, LWF conducted a rapid assessment on 23rd and 24th of October, to assess the conditions of the affected population. The displaced community resettled around Bor town in places called Kondai, Wutchuei and Malual Aglobar. Kondai is about 20 km from Bor town in the north east; Wutchei is 25 km also in the north east, and Malual Aglobar is 30 km to the south east. Communities from Akuac Deng settled in Kondai; Kolmerek community settled in Wutchuei and Kuei/Jalle community settled in Malual Aglobar. Akuac Deng community counts 1,430HHs / 5,000 people displaced and settled as IDPs in Kondai. 2,114 HHs / 22,000 people from the Kolmerek community were displaced and settled as IDPs in Wutchuei. Kuei/Jalle community counts 2,810 HHs /18,265 people displaced and resettled as IDPs in Malual Aglobar. Similar to other counties there is high need for emergency food production to improve living condition, support resilience of the most vulnerable households in the county. This project intends to respond with emergency food production and building the capacities of the most vulnerable community members building their resilience while ensuring adoption of measure to prevent the spread of COVID-. 40% of the target of the project will be in Duk county based on the severity, 20% from Bor South and 40% will be in Twic East county.

2 - Project activities

Specific Objective 1: Support emergency food production through crops, vegetable and fishing production while considering COVID prevention measures • Provide 850 most vulnerable HHs with agriculture kits (cereal crops and vegetable kits) for emergency food production • Provide 950 most vulnerable Fishing folks with fishing kits to catch fish for HH consumption • Distribution of goats for 100 most vulnerable HHs (4 goats each)- goats fair• Distribution of poultry birds for 120 most vulnerable Women headed (3 hens and one cock each) • Provision of early warning and food security information at payam level Specific Objective 2: Enhance capacity of people and families for resilience to absorb shock while considering COVID -19 prevention measures • Establish 10 FFS (Farmers Field School) , each 25 members (250members)• Train 10 FFS facilitators on FFS approach and facilitation and support to facilitators with incentive• Establish 10 demonstration sites,• Support FFS groups with crop and vegetable kits - 250 member and 10FFS groups• Training on farm and off farm (proper tillage, raw planting, weeding , pest management , harvesting , post-harvest management)• Training of farming households on nutritious food consumption and food waste management • Organize and support (in training) most vulnerable women into 5 Village Saving and Credit Associations (VSLAs)-each 20 members (100 women)• Provide small grant (seed capital ) to five VSLAs

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will mainstream protection in the implementation to ensure that the right holders are protected and sexual exploitation and abuse is controlled with zero tolerance. LWF will inform beneficiaries through awareness on PSEAS using meeting , and pamphlets. In addition, it will inform beneficiaries on its own PSEA policy.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most of people in targeted counties are illiterate, and among the most vulnerable selected beneficiaries several will be people living with disables, elderly and females whom are physically and culturally less prone in providing feedbacks. For this reason LWF intends to work in close collaboration with the community committees who can closely work with the most in need providing immediate support and orientation throughout the project phases.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Planning and implementation will be done with the full involvement of the right holder. The project will have complained response committees at each Payam and county level, so that the complains are addressed immediately

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will consider the feedback from beneficiaries through reorienting or revising the operational plan and revising where possible the key project delivery mechanisms. Feedback will be gathered through the community’s committees and the project staff on the ground.
