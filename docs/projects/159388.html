Project Summary

According to the last FSNAU report on September 2, 2019, – Up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2019. Moreover, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. An estimated 2.6 million people across Somalia remain internally displaced which will worsen humanitarian needs. Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions and compacted by continued displacements and conflict. In August 2019, 27,000 new internal displacements were reported year-to-date by the UNHCR-led Protection and Returns Monitoring Network (PRMN); drivers include conflict and insecurity (141,000), drought-related (112,000), floods (6,000) and other reasons (11,000).
As the results from last Nutritional surveys conducted by FSNAU in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu).
Access to basic and lifesaving health care services remains a critical challenge that needs to be prioritized within Somalia. Health service coverage is critically low. Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD)/cholera, measles, malaria, and other communicable diseases outbreaks are constantly reported in many areas including in IDP settlements and areas where Concern and its partners have health facilities.
Maternal and child health indicators are among the worst in the world in Somalia, with maternal mortality rates at 732 per 100,000 live births, under-five mortality rate at 137 per 1,000 live births and skilled delivery rates of only 29.7% (UNICEF Somalia Annual Report 2017). Furthermore, Somalia continues to have the worst IYCF and micronutrients indicators in the world with health facility utilization rates critically below the minimum recommended by Sphere Standards. Concern has directly run Health and Nutrition programmes in multiple regions Banadir (Mogadishu) and Afgoye communities. Lower Shabelle and Gedo since 2008 and developed long-standing, trusting relationships with key actors including communities served, MoH authorities, peer Non-Government Organizations and UN. In 2018 Concern undertook direct service provision in 10 integrated health and nutrition facilities in Banadir (6) and Lower Shabelle regions (4), including over 100,000 Out Patient Department (OPD) consultations for children and adults, serving a total catchment population of 400,000. However in 2019 with loss of ECHO multisector (incl H&) funding 4 of these facilities had to be closed – we remain ready and have the capacity to reopen them should funding be available. Furthermore, since late 2017 Concern has managed the stabilization centre at the National Referral hospital in Mogadishu, which provides treatment for children with life-threatening complications of severe acute malnutrition. The project will provide lifesaving integrated health and nutrition services to affected communities using the approaches of supporting static (2 functioning and 2 non-functioning health facilities) and mobile clinics (1 mobile clinic) in hard to reach communities at five sites in Banadhir, Lower Shabele and Gedo regions with a focus on maternal and child health.

Project Activities

Concern is proposing to support and increase access to health and nutrition services, particularly maternal & child health services at five sites in Banadhir, Lower Shabele, and Gedo regions. The basic service provision will support communities through the prevention and treatment of malnutrition (SAM and MAM), immunizations, ANC, PNC, and outpatient treatment for diseases as per the IMNCI approach for < 5 children and vaccinations as per the Somalia EPI schedule. OPD consultation services will be offered to all members of the community in addition to PLWs. This will be Primary Health Care services as per the EPHS.The project will support the work of Community Health Workers in sensitizing and mobilizing communities in accessing health and nutrition services by focusing on IYCF messaging at both community and facility levels for better detection of malnutrition and other illnesses and thus shock responsive early referral to relevant services at community and facility levels.Concern and partner’s health worker staff will be trained on the provision of EPI vaccines as well as IMNCI.

Mitigation Measures

2a) Project implementation staff consider the IOM displacement registers for each camp to ensure they are aware of potential new arrivals that may not be aware of the services; have CHW conduct sanitization at field level to ensure maximum awareness; clearly demarcate the facility (were security permits) with both graphics and signage to increase awareness in communities; work with community groups for inputs and ensure they have a diverse demographic of the community.3a) Work with DAC forums and other community groups to ensure the displaced person are being included to decision making processes and meetings (men and women); accurate assessments, both individual and joint, are conducted throughout the implementation period to determine caseloads and levels of need taking into consideration extremely vulnerable persons; project activities are designed with the specific and additional needs faced by displaced communities in consideration; registrations at health facilities are aware and sensitive to communities dynamics: clans, host vs displaced etc. so as not to increase possible conflict triggers; 3c) Facilities are, as much as possible, located centrally to affected populations so as not to cause undue pressure on patients trying to attend; clinic hours take into consideration the daily responsibilities of patients, particularly women who culturally have responsibility for domestic duties; Code of Conduct and patients right information is displayed clearly within facilities, patients and attendees of the clinic are made aware of CRM mechanisms.
