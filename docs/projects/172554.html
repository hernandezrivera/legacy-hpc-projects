Project Summary

Gender Based Violence remains a major threat for women, adolescent girls and other at risk groups. Over the past year an increase in GBV has been noted in the wake of drought and COVID-19. A 38.5% overall increase in GBV cases was reported during the first few months of COVID 19 . CARE drought RGA noted an increase in early child marriage, and transactional sex . According to the SAFE study, IPV slightly increased, from 68.8% to 71.1 % during the period March to April 2020. Riding on CARE’s experience on Gender and GBV programming, the project will leverage on existing CARE projects, strengthening GBV risk mitigation and prevention mechanisms. The project will enhance key stakeholders, partner staff and communities capacities to address and handle GBV cases, and facilitating improved access to GBV essential services through referral system. CARE in all the three districts will partner with Musasa, a Women Rights NGO that provides GBV specialized services, as well as other local NGOs. Women’s economic empowerment and male engagement will be critical components towards realizing project outcomes. The project will be implemented in Mberengwa, Zvishavane and Mwenezi, CARE operational wards reaching a total of 33 000 females and males.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

CARE is committed to meeting international standards of equality and accountability as guided by its accountability framework and the CARE Gender Equality Policy and CARE International Safeguarding policy among others. In February and April 2020, CARE conducted two (2) Rapid Gender Analysis with a view of understanding the impact of drought and later on COVID 19 on women, men, boys and girls. GBV and protection was one of the area of inquiry. Key issues that emerged was an increase in GBV, transactional sex and early child marriage. The communities also highlighted GBV service provision gaps and needs. In order to protect affected communities from any form of abuse by our employees and related personnel. CARE has also put in place open communication channels that allow for people to come forward with sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse claims using multiple reporting channels that include suggestion box, help desks (manned by both community represenattives and care staff), walk-ins, Deloitte hotline and community meetings. CARE also has a single global CARE Line for receiving reports with support in over 100 languages, online or phone options, and anonymous reporting options. CARE will hold quarterly review meeting with the communities, to assess project outcomes, and community satisfaction with services provided. Equitable access to opportunities and protection services will be supported by the project.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is not intending to carry out any sub-projects that will or adversely affect the environment, as such no environmental assessments have been planned. However, The ongoing climate change, has forced women, men, young and old, to engage in illegal gold panning (CARE RGA-2020) and increasingly heightened insecurities for women and adolescent girls. CARE will leverage their existing resilience and climate adaptation projects in the target districts, to integrate GBV prevention, mitigation and response, including holding sessions around equitable access and Natural Resource Management (NRM), to curb potential conflict that may arise as a result of resource scarcity.
