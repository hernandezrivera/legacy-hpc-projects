Project Summary

Support 200,000 conflict affected individuals (33,334 households) with lifesaving food and livelihood assistance across the governorates of Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Idleb, Al-Hasakeh. Response will be prioritized based on the severity of the needs in the target location. Mainly, Mercy Corps will provide:
1. Multi-purpose cash Assistance (MPCA): whenever cash is feasible, Mercy Corps will prioritize provision of one-off MPCA to newly IDPs and severely food insecure population. MPCA will be based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates of each location, which includes food assistance. In-kind distribution will be considered only when cash and voucher deemed to be infeasible.
2. Multi-round food assistance: Eight rounds of food basket assistance will be provided to IDPs and food insecure population covering 70% of the individual monthly dietary needs. Cash and vouchers (CVA) will be prioritized when CVA for food assistance. Electronic transfer will be utilized whenever possible. In-kind food will only be used when CVA deemed to be infeasible.
3. Inputs for food production: Mercy Corps will support vulnerable farmers to purchase quality summer and winter crops inputs from local markets. Each farmers will receive USD300 or equivalent amount in the form of voucher. In-kind distribution of inputs will only be considered when CVA proved inappropriate.
4. Livestock: Mercy Corps will provide subsidies through vouchers for fodder and animal health consultation services to vulnerable livestock herders in the targeted locations.
5. Kitchen Garden: Mercy Corps will provide vulnerable households with in-kind kitchen garden kits for improved vegetable and summer crop production.
Target households will be selected based on vulnerability criteria and food security indicators. Additional eligibility criteria will be considered to target farmers and livestock herders.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Syrian HNO, at least 12.4 million people are facing acute and chronic food insecurity, demonstrating a sharp increase of 4.5 million from 2020 - a 60% increase in people facing acute food insecurity in 2021:NWS: Following the March 2020 ceasefire, NWS has witnessed a shift in humanitarian needs from mass displacement to a fragile and protracted displacement in camps and host communities across Idlib, Western and Northern Aleppo. As of February 2021, the total population in need in NWS reached 4,407,755. Conflict continues to affect humanitarian dynamics across NWS, with the nine months following the March 6 ceasefire in 2020 witnessing an average of 32,000 individuals displaced per month. As displacement becomes embedded following the Idlib Dawn offensive, IDPs have limited access to food and livelihood leaving them exposed to the harshness of the seasons, and reduced coping strategies in the face of a deteriorating local economy. In a December 2020 RNA Mercy Corps found that 44% of the households assessed reported that they limit the meal portion for all of the household members while 50% reported that they limit the meal portion for half of the household members NES: According to Syria 2021 HNO, there are 266,874 people in need in the North-east Syria. The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) carried out by Mercy Corps in 2020 identified four key intervention areas for agricultural support in North-East Syria (NES). These support areas are food assistance, agricultural extension, farmer inputs and improvements to agricultural technology. Based on the findings of the MSNA, all the major markets in NES are generally active and accessible.

5 - Implementation of activities

Multi-purpose cash Assistance (MPCA): Where money transfer agents are available and functional markets are accessible, one-off MPCA will be provided to acutely vulnerable households. The MPCA transfer amount will be delivered based on the local survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates, which is aligned with the Food Security Cluster recommendations, and harmonized with other cash actors. The SMEB is calculated using ongoing price monitoring data. Clear communication channels will be maintained through Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting and Monitoring (CARM) system. Baseline and endline data will be collected prior to and after the transfer.Multi-round food assistance: Mercy Corps’ food response activities have been designed to adhere to the FSL sector recommendations for regular food assistance. Households will be identified through a community-based targeting methodology. Targeted locations will firstly be selected through inter-agency and NGO forum coordination mechanisms. Post Distribution Monitoring will be administered 15 days after each distribution.Inputs for food production (farming and livestock): Farmers and herders will be selected based on vulnerability and eligibility criteria. During and post distribution monitoring will take place and post-harvest assessment will be conducted at the end of the season to assess the impact. Kitchen Gardens: These will be delivered via an in-kind modality, with the distribution of physical kits procured by Mercy Corps through reputable suppliers to ensure quality of the packages that the farmers will receive. Each kit consists of a 500 liter tank, lateral pipes and drip lines that can support vegetable production.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The following protection risk and mitigations measures have been identified:Large population movements in the target areaRegular monitoring of conflict dynamics in the areas of interventions in order to identify when conflict may result in population movement and how this may impact the program.Local authorities restrict activities due to lack of support for protection interventionsWhere programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. Deterioration of relations between IDPs and host communityAgreed vulnerability criteria will result in assistance that is directed towards vulnerable community members and households regardless of whether they are returnees, IDPs or host community members. The identification of beneficiaries is done in partnership with local communities including IDPs and host communities. Communities and local stakeholders are sensitized on the selection criteria to be used.Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partnersMercy Corps has existing codes of conduct and safeguarding practices for partners and staff. Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Partners and staff are engaged in PSS and case management exercise in areas where there are limited/no services for onward referral. Mercy Corps will ensure that both program teams and partners are engaged in the appropriate cluster service mapping exercises. Partners receive resources to conduct service mapping (and feedback to the cluster).

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero-tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labour and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

MC has identified the following potential risks during the design phase of this project, and planned for mitigation measures:Deterioration of the security situation precludes access to target areasThe activities outlined above will only be implemented if access conditions inside Syria remain permissive. Regular monitoring of conflict dynamics in the areas of interventions in order to identify when a loss of access is becoming increasingly likely and transition programming to national NGO partners to the extent possible. Negative impact of livelihoods interventions on community and household dynamicsMC is aware that cash and asset transfers, and activities that aim at promoting equitable access of vulnerable groups to resources can provoke a backlash at the community level and/or changes in household dynamics that increase protection risks. To mitigate this risk:● Activity design is based on gender and disability sensitive livelihoods assessments as well as do-no-harm analysis;● Field teams will engage with local stakeholders in discussions on necessary actions for creating an enabling environmentIncreased inflationMC will conduct market assessments in order to monitor for price increases. MC will coordinate with UN clusters/peer agencies and follow standard modalities agreed-upon by clusters.Key local stakeholders do not have the interest and/or capacity to fully engage in the projectMC will conduct a stakeholder mapping at the beginning to identify local stakeholders, who have the capacity and willingness to support interventions designed to promote resilience.
