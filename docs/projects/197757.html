Project Summary

Emergency WASH response to the IDP crisis in Mozambique with a strong focus on responding to the needs of children and the most vulnerable. The response will target both IDP and host communities in the area of Metuge and Montepuez with improved sanitation, water systems and innovative hygiene promotion activies.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Many IDP families in Cabo Delgado live in overcrowded conditions and have access only to emergency latrines and inadequate water supply systems, most of which are not designed for the specific needs of children. The needs are greatest in transit camps, where only communal latrines are available. Specifically targeting children will introduce positive hygiene practices into their socialisation and have a positive impact on the health of the whole community.In order for the programme to have a holistic effect and to eradicate open defecation 100%, the programme focuses on entire geographical units, including the sites discovered by the actors present, as well as schools and health centres in the geographical area. As a result of the relaxation of COVID standards, many IDP children also attend schools in the host communities where they live, which in many cases doubles the number of pupils returning to school without access to the WASH facilities they need.The project specifically targets children to have the necessary WASH facilities in their communities and schools and to learn positive hygiene practices and act as actors of change in their communities. With this holistic approach, the project aims to establish 100% open defecation free communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Programme Objective: Improved health conditions of targeted populations through better access to WASH for children and their families. Outcome 1: Improved conditions and access of conflict displaced populations and host communities to safe and dignified hygiene, water and sanitation facilities in schools.Output 1: Children and parents of 3 sites, and 3 villages have learnt good WASH practices: -Development of a theatre programme for information dissemination to children;-HP messaging for parents of new-borns and children;-HP in schools (WASH clubs/committees, murals, playground equipment);-Donation of IPC materials to schools and health centres;-Training of mother in ante-natal care on safe WASH practices.Output 2: Construction of 3 adapted and accessible sanitation blocks 3 different sites and communities to improve the access of children and girls to safe and dignified sanitation facilities at schools. Output 3: Construction of 3 Boreholes and installation of afridev hand pump in 3 schools in 3 different communities. - Design and rehabilitation/construction of sustainable water supply (rainwater or solar system), in one school and one health centre; Outcome 2: Improved hygiene, water and sanitation conditions in IDP centres and host communities Output 1: Prevention of open-defecation through improved access to sanitation:- Vulnerability assessment. - HP activities in 4 different communities- Distribution a total of 1800concrete slabs in 6 different communities- Technical support for latrine construction- Post Distribution Monitoring Output 2: Reduction of incidence of communicable diseases through improved access to sufficient and safe water : - Construction of 4 waterpoints ;- Rehabilitation and improvement of 10 water points, renovating slabs and provisions to be more inclusive; Output 3: Distribution of 1500 Higyene kits for IDPs families displaced due to the conflict.- Vulnerability assessment- Distribution of Hygen kits to 1500 families- Post distribution monitoring

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection mainstreaming is an integral part of AeA’s design strategy, this alongside the participatory methods that AeA use to collect information help greatly to identify the risks posed to each demographic. Staff working in the field are aware of the do no harm, a principle which assists in making the most suitable decisions for the populations in question. We prioritise preserving safety and dignity in the design of any installation, infrastructure or the planning of any event. Ensuring that there is equitable access for all and encouraging participation in order to empower those we are helping.A multi-sector risk analysis for each component of the project will be made to ensure that all risks are considered and mitigated. Some examples of this occurring in the project are ensuring communal facilities such as handpumps or communal latrines are in visible and lit locations, working alongside CCCM to locate street lights where necessary. Where wash facilities do not have inclusions for children or the disabled; specific focus groups will be held to present options for improvement and allow the focus group to suggest their own changes.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AeA designs projects through participatory methods, engaging affected communities, listening and recording their needs. AeA ensures that those in the most vulnerable groups are also included in the discussion, always taking into account the diversity of the population to allow the most at-risk to be equally considered. These considerations help AeA to design programmes tailored to the specific needs of the population. AeA ensures staff on the ground are capable of effectively transmiting important information to communiites, including sharing the organizational structures or processes that may affect the population; and methods of making complaints or finding information, such as Linha Verde or CCCM’s complaint channel, in Cabo Delgado.This project will continue to ensure that these practices are continued, most specifically through keeping strong contact with CCCM’s complaint channel, setting up complaints desks at distributions and ensuring field staff involved with community leaders encourage an open line for contact via telephone for complaints which are to always be shared.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This project as stated previously will ensure that the ideas and views of those with disabilities are solicited throughout the project cycle. They will be involved in the design of WASH infrastructure such as latrines and water sources to ensure that their suggestions can lead to improvements to access. Some physical installations can include but are not limited to handrails, seats or ramps. AeA will also ensure that their issues will be disseminated to the community in a meaningful manner to help improve the understanding of their needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

An analysis of the risks to the environment for each installation will be made, and mitigating actions will be undertaken to minimize any negative impact or eradicate it completely. For example the design of boreholes will be done to the highest standards, ensuring aquifers are protected from contamination from above. This will include lining these boreholes as deep as possible and building sanitary seals of good quality. Information on these holes will be provided to the local govt. structures SDPI, who will be responsible for the sources in the long-term. With water such a precious resource in the region, rain water storage will also be targeted in order to reduce the pressure on the precious groundwater sources that are used in Cabo Delgado.
