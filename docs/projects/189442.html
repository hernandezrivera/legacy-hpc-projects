Project Summary

The project seeks to address the immediate lifesaving food needs and livelihoods challenges faced by the IDPs, returnees, and host community members in Koch, Panyijiar and mayendit counties of Unity state. It will enable vulnerable households affected by the humanitarian crisis to withstand the effects of the food security and livelihood shocks as well as put them back on the road to recovery. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and host community members will be targeted to complement UNIDOR’s relief and recovery activities in Koch, Panyijiar and mayendit counties of Unity state. UNIDOR will undertake multiple activities using vulnerability and eligibility criteria factoring gender, disability, poverty, nutrition status, and other social inclusion factors. Activities will include distribution of agricultural and fishing inputs, multi-purpose cash, VSLA groups, and income-generation activities. UNIDOR will also be supporting communities to transition off humanitarian assistance through sustainable and long-term systemic interventions. UNIDOR has identified that flood displaced and other conflict-affected households in Koch, Panyijiar and Mayendit are facing multiple shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic that has severely limited livelihoods, which has come on top of economic erosion, failed harvests that are stretching the lean season flooding, communal violence, and displacement.
UNIDOR has presence and ongoing interventions in Panyijiar, Koch and Mayendit, in FSL, Nutrition, Health, Wash and Education.

1 - Needs

People’s humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to be mainly driven by years of conflict, increasing underlying vulnerability and which has been further compounded by the impacts of climate change and the COVID-19 pandemic. The already critical humanitarian situation has been compounded by the third consecutive year of atypically severe flooding in 2021, with 835,000 people reported as affected between May - December 2021. Floods have destroyed critical infrastructure and have limited humanitarian access to heavily affected populations, while simultaneously limiting the ability of people to migrate to access services. Climate change is aggravating the risk as well as cascading and compounding the effects of hydro-meteorological hazards. As their severity, frequency and complexity increases, the level of disruption to socioeconomic systems and of destruction to humans, livelihoods and infrastructure rises. The 2021 Global Climate Risk Index, which rates the impacts of weather-related loss events ranks South Sudan as the 8th most affected country. (FAO) assessment findings indicated that 65,107 Ha of land planted with cereals had been damaged, with an estimated loss of 37,624 tonnes of grain in the flood-affected areas. The effects of floods and climate change call for urgent actions, measures and approaches that will help farmers sustainably increase their agricultural productivity and incomes. High levels of food insecurity drive humanitarian need in most parts of the country. An estimated 8.3 million people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022, an increase of 7 per cent from 2021. High inflation is persistent with high basic commodities prices despite the fiscal reforms. The South Sudan JMMI for Jan 2022, recorded that the cost of a food basket has risen to 31,996($73.55). People’s food security situation is expected to further deteriorate as a result of continued violence that disrupt people’s livelihood activities and trade, the impacts of flooding, ongoing economic crisis and seasonal effects. Localized dry spells at the critical time of crop growth pose the risk of low agricultural production and further deepen the food gaps. Food insecurity negatively impacts people’s health and nutritional wellbeing, and their ability to carry out livelihood activities. Those identified as most affected are children, people with disabilities, older persons and women-headed households. These people are the most vulnerable to be affected by increases in food insecurity. The FNSMS+ identified Food security and livelihoods as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55 per cent of assessed households; similarly, food and livelihoods were recognized as the primary priority need for women, in 52 per cent of assessed households and in the case of children, food and livelihoods was the second most frequently identified pressing need, as reported in 29 per cent of assessed households, following education in 48 per cent. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs overview projects that Koch, Panyijiar and Mayendit will have significant populations that have slipped back to IPC 4 and 5, with Koch and Mayendit recording a 24% and 9% increase of People in need in comparison to 2021. A recent SMART survey conducted Panyijiar by IMC recorded that Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition for children between 6 and 59 months, is at 15.7% which is very low as per the WHO recommended rate. The plight calls for timely life-saving and life -sustaining Food security support through conditional and un-conditional safety nets interventions as well as restoring already weakened and fragile livelihoods and further strengthen resilience of the most vulnerable households to cope with the current and future shocks. UNIDOR will respond to needs by delivering agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through community and resilience-based agricultural interventions.

2 - Project activities

CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)- Distribution of Unconditional cash transfer to the most vulnerable households - Distribution of fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);- Provision of 3,000 Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits and vegetable kits to help them meet their food needs from own production and improve livelihood condition - Conducting extension trainings for most vulnerable farmers on modern agricultural techniques- Livelihood support to returnee households through provision of multipurpose cash - Establish kitchen gardens near nutrition sites- Conducting awareness for most vulnerable populations both female and male on environment protection, gender involvement and equality.- CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19- Training and supporting agricultural extension workers to provide sessions and technical support to agricultural beneficiaries - Establish and support VSLA groups for vulnerable households to improve savings- Establishment of Farmers Field Schools (FFSs).- Training the beneficiaries in postharvest handling techniques - Establishment and training of project management and AAP committees made up of 60% women and 40% men. - Sharing COVID-19 prevention measures messages

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher modalities will be preferred where possible. This includes preference from beneficiaries and availability of items in local markets as well as capacity of the market to absorb such transfers as in areas directly affected by disaster, functioning markets are either not available or slow to recover from the shock with limited availability of goods due to hampered access or damaged infrastructure. UNIDOR will implement unconditional (emergency needs for the most vulnerable households). The unconditional modality will facilitate access to a wider range of items and empower the beneficiaries to choose the specific items that their household needs, in locations where the markets are functional.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market analysis is a crucial part of UNIDOR’s cash and voucher activities and are incorporated into the response. Before undertaking any cash or voucher activities, UNIDOR will conduct a detailed market analysis and routinely monitor prices to ensure the market is functional and has the sufficient capacity to meet the needs.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will have a database for feedback. UNIDOR also conducts regular review of feedback received and consider any changes to the project. All feedback will be responded to individually or in groups, as appropriate. UNIDOR usually responds to feedbacks at least 2 weeks from when it was received, and that feedback is analyzed to inform project decisions. Feedback will be managed by dedicated gender and protection staff in collaboration with the M&E team to ensure communities are aware of how to submit feedback and expectations regarding responses.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Among feedback and complaints channels, UNIDOR will ensure that there is at least one channel that gives community members an opportunity to share sensitive complaints related to safeguarding and fraud issues in a confidential manner. UNIDOR projects also actively seeks feedback from beneficiaries through informal discussions with all the projects beneficiaries who include the most vulnerable members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNIDOR has a PSEA and safeguarding policy. This project is tailored to comply with this policy. The requires all UNIDOR staff and volunteer undergo a sensitization session. UNIDOR will also conduct pre-distribution and pre-training meetings to inform the beneficiaries of their right to receive the form of assistance provided by this project without discriminations or exploitation

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

UNIDOR will conduct Community based participatory planning meetings. The objective of these meetings will be to share information of the project and to also collect suggestion, complaints and feedbacks from the community on this project. Through CBPP, UNIDOR consults beneficiaries on their preferred feedback channels and engages key stakeholders at different levels at the appropriate targeting of individuals. UNIDOR disseminates knowledge on services provided through IEC, mass messaging, and community meetings. UNIDOR Projects also conduct education during pre-registration and distribution, which highlights the existence of a response and feedback mechanism and how the mechanism can be accessed.Throughout the project cycle, UNIDOR will create awareness on the project’s feedback and complaint channels (two channels in each target community). These may include channels such as suggestion boxes, focus group discussions, and in-person reporting to trusted focal points. UNIDOR will also setup gender disaggregated help desks manned by male and female staff respectively.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNIDOR has a robust internal mechanism to safeguard, combat and prevent child Abuse and PSEA. All UNIDOR staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes UNIDOR’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. UNIDOR will not operate in locations of active political violence over boundary setting and will re-allocate project resources to more stable payams. Projects will explain this policy as part of its engagement with communities and stakeholders. All sensitive feedback (feedback regarding suspicions of fraud, protection violations, or exploitation, abuse, and harassment) will be handled by the Country Representatives of UNIDOR or the protection staff assigned to the project. Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by UNIDOR. Throughout the project cycle, UNIDOR will create awareness on the project’s feedback and complaint channels (two channels in each target community). These may include channels such as suggestion boxes, focus group discussions, and in-person reporting to trusted focal points. UNIDOR will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood. Before engaging farmers in demo plot trainings and crop production, UNIDOR will ensure the necessary land agreements with the local authorities are in place.
