Project Summary

In Somalia conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Conflict-induced shocks exacerbate the humanitarian situation of both IDP and host communities, with increased numbers of children married earlier as a coping strategy. Somalia remains on the frontline of climate change, which continues to induce crises resulting in widespread displacement, rapid urbanization, food insecurity, and increased poverty.
Critically, climate change is also increasingly understood as a major driver of conflict in Somalia as the struggle for dwindling resources intensifies clan divisions and inter-clan conflict.
To address these humanitarian crises, CARE will implement an emergency health services working with the Ministry of Health, Local partners targeting 30 Health Centres and 5 mobile clinics in drought and conflicted affected regions of Sool, Sanaag, Bari, Nugal, Galgadud, Banadir and Lower Jubba regions. The proposed intervention will provide services to 120,000 vulnerable persons affected by disasters, including 58,000 women, 61,200 Men including 11,760 Girls and 12,240 Boys. These will include approximately 20,000 internally displaced populations,13500 individuals with different forms of disability and 200 survivors sexual and gender based violence.
The health interventions will be aligned to the Health cluster objective of reducing excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict among 4.7 million IDPs and non-displaced, contributing to improvement in physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement among 4.7 million IDPs and non-displaced; including 705,000 people with disabilities, Improving equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected population to 1.2 million IDPs and 3.5 million non-displaced population and improving case management and referral services for 235,000 Survivors of sexual or gender-based violence.
CARE emergency health services will be implemented through static Health Facilities and mobile outreach teams that will work closely with the District hospitals in for referral for safe delivery services, medical cases requiring specialized services. CARE will provide outpatient consultations for both common communicable and non communicable diseases.
CARE-supported health facilities will provide Antenatal care, Post natal care and free BEmONC services. Pregnant women requiring comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care will be referred to District hospitals providing CEMONC services (including cesarean sections and blood transfusions) to ensure access to timely life-saving services for mothers and neonatal babies.
Community engagement to create awareness and behavior change will be key in the project. SGBV (Sexual and Gender-based violence) survivors will be offered confidential CMR (clinical management of rape) services in line with WHO guidelines. CARE will assign specific staff to support person with disability and special needs. Facilities to aid patients' movement will be provided too. In light of the COVID-19 pandemic, CARE health staff will use standard IPC as outlined by WHO. In all health centers and mobile clinics , health workers will be encouraged to exercise a high level of clinical suspicion. Equipped triage stations will be at the entrance to each facility and COVID 19 preventive measures put in place. CARE will train all health workers on diseases surveillance, EPI, IMCI, MHPSS, EWARN, Sexual and reproductive health, Clinical management of rape and health information management system. CARE will use and build capacity with MOH Staff at field and regional level while working closely with other Partners for coordination and complementarity.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of basic treatment communicable, non communicable and referrals for complicated cases to 120,000 disaster affected beneficiaries. The service will be offered through 30 static and 5 mobile clinics. CARE will ensure persons with disability and special needs will be given dues consideration. 2. Training of 120 Health care workers( 70 Female, 50 male) health workers on; 1. Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) which will cover how to diagnose and treat common child hood illnesses. 2. Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) covering Immunization schedule, safe administration of vaccines, vaccination strategy, common side effects among others. 3. Health information management system(HMIS) which focus on reporting, data collection at the health facility and reporting to DHIS2 and EWAR. 4. Integrate disease surveillance3. Provision of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services (including for SGBV) including CMR and mental psych social support and counselling and referral for comprehensive care. CARE will also provide antenatal, Delivery and postnatal services to women and Girls of reproductive age.4. Support to routine and supplemental immunization services for Children under 5yrs and Pregnant lactating women and Girls. Vaccination will be provided at both fixed health centers and mobile teams. Mobile teams will use cold boxes and return the remaining vaccines to the MCH and report the number of vaccinated children and pregnant women to MCH using EPI registers at the end of each day. CARE will liaise with UNICEF and MOH for vaccine supply.5. Capacity building for 70 Female community health workers on community engagement, Community mobilization, community disease surveillance and referrals.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Should the security situation worsen, CARE's robust safety and security protocols shall be activated. A Security Management Plan is in place, identifying threats/risks, implications, and mitigation measures. Due to its long-term presence in the project area, CARE has development cordial relationship with communities and the local authorizes, thus enjoys excellent acceptance. CARE will actively consult and engage community members in beneficiary selection and targeting to see to it that minority and other vulnerable groups are not left out. In addition, over 95% of its staff are national with the required expertise and experience with good understanding of the context. This staff are able work across all the villages in the target regions.
