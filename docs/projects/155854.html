Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates number of crisis-affected population in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria at 11.7 million people (49.8% Male and 50.2% female) out of which 4.7 million and 5 million live in areas of major severity of needs (inter-sector severity 3) and acute of need (inter-sector severity 4-6). The above include 6.2 million protracted Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs); 1.4 million returnees; 324,000 IDPs in hard to reach areas, 1.6 million-population movement and the rest overburdened host communities. In addition, 480,000 Palestine refugees are also considered part of the people in need. 67% of the 11.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria according to Syria Response Access Dynamic report by OCHA (December, 2018). Overall, 9 million people are estimated to be food insecure and at risk of food insecurity by Food Security Sector. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 170,000 vulnerable crisis affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 1,020,000 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve access to staple food for vulnerable farmers through increased cereals and pulses production. The expected outputs of the project are:- Output 1: Access and availability of agricultural inputs of 170,000 households to facilitate production of cereals and pulses increased. The agricultural input packages will be in line with sector recommendations for provision of 200 kg of wheat/barley seeds (adequate to plant 1 Ha), 50 kg NPK 10:10:10 (adequate for 1 ha). This is will be supplemented with requisite skill enhancement through training. 40/50 kg of various legume seeds (adequate to plant approximately 0.25 0.4 ha) or 200 kg per dunum for Potatoes per households.
Output 2. Production skills for cereal and pulses of 170,000 targeted producers enhanced
The output aims at building capacity of 170,000 households that will benefit from cereals and pulses agricultural inputs on skills related to the inputs. The training programme will be built on the following FAO concepts: “Save & Grow”, “Climate Smart Agriculture” and “Conservation agriculture”.
Output 3. Enhanced production and preservation of high quality wheat seed for 6,000 beneficiaries.
It is expected that the introduction of agriculture based livelihood protection support in current protracted nearly eight years of crisis will be able to increase local availability of food, self-reliance and improve food and nutrition security of the Syrian people, especially for the most vulnerable ones.

Needs

Cereals and pulses production is vital to ensuring food security for Syrians. It increases food availability at household level and provides income for farmers (different gender/age) enabling them to increase their food purchases in local markets. Wheat production at 1.2 million tons in 2018 was the lowest in 30 years, lower than the previous year (1.8 million tons) and only 30 percent of the pre-crisis average of 4.1 million tons. The implication of lowest recorded production is that the availability of wheat, a major staple is further constrained and there is a significant shortfall to meet national requirements even after considering potential government, commercial and food assistance imports.Agricultural production have been hampered by drought and insecurity, which has affected large number of farmers. The major constraints to production being dependency on rain-fed farming, decreasing availability of quality farming inputs coupled with increased prices, severe disruption of input and output markets, destruction of infrastructure and services (irrigation, storage facilities, veterinary and roads), contamination and land degradation. In addition, availability of quality inputs have been affected by sanctions.The Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) 2018 report indicate that there is huge shortage of quality seed in Syria. GOSM, agency responsible for the production and distribution of seed of improved cereal varieties has not managed to meet seed needs of producers in Syria and only 12% of pre-crisis average of 300,000 tons of seeds will be available for current planting season. With high shortage of certified seeds, most farmers will not have adequate seeds for next planting season and few that may through their own means will face quality concerns. Farmers have and will continue to face particular challenges accessing high quality and locally adapted seeds. As a result, quality of seeds being used have degenerated affecting production and productivity. Improving access to high quality and locally adapted seeds delivers immediate productivity benefits and helps restore the quality of seeds that farmers use in subsequent seasons. Support provided to crop production will help alleviate scarcity of bread, the basic food for poor people, and reduce dependency on food imports and lessen the demand for food assistance. FAO experience over the years has demonstrated that by supporting farmers to cultivate one hectare of wheat, 2–2.5 tons of grains will be produced adequate to feed 11-14 people for one year. In addition. crop residues are used as supplementary feeds to sustain remaining livestock population therefore the whole crop is put to the best use.This project will support farmers through provision of agricultural inputs and assistance to produce and save high quality and locally adapted seeds through community based seed multiplication. This is expected to contribute to poverty alleviation by generating decent formal and informal employment providing income to households and the catchment. Training will be provided to local seed producers and farmers interested in small scale and rapid seed multiplication to increase the availability of better quality locally adapted seeds. Close cooperation with other UN agencies(UNICEF, UNDP, WFP) and other stakeholders focusing on agricultural production in Syria, will further improve the positive impact on beneficiaries creating synergies between the different assistance packages and making good use of the specialized expertise that each partners can provide. Further coordination amongst agencies are encouraged to protect youth and bolster capacities of families and communities to create an effective resource for a wide range of activities. The proposed intervention will be implemented under a Whole of Syria approach with direct supervision of FAO in close collaboration with MAAR, National and International NGOs.

Implementation of activities

Currently FAO is operating in thirteen governorates – Aleppo, Al Hassakeh, Ar Raqqa, Dar’a, Deir e Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, As Sweida, Quneitra, Rural Damascus, Tartous and Lattakia. In governorates where UN staff presence is not permitted by UN security regulations, FAO operates through government seconded officers and NGO service providers who are directly supporting implementation of activities and report to FAO.FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the activities, backstopped by FAO headquarters with technical support of FAO’s multidisciplinary team in the Regional Office for the Near East and North Africa. FAO is working closely working with MAAR to ensure that the project goals are achieved through proper technical expertise and monitoring capacity. The implementation will be further supported by:• A central coordination committee that includes representatives from FAO, relevant MAAR directorates, community-based organizations and implementing partners. The committee’s role is to ensure facilitation of the project implementation and application of the criteria for beneficiaries' selection• Local team(s), at village/district level, led by FAO and composed of local community representatives (usually the traditional leader or “Moukhtar”), extension workers and implementing partners respectively will be in charge of selecting project beneficiaries in line with FAO established set of criteria that take into account gender and age sensitive considerations.• FAO Resilience Officers in the targeted governorates will operationally and technically supervise the inputs distribution, training and monitoring of the project.In locations inaccessible from with Syria, FAO Syria will liaise with FAO country office with access to reach out to planned beneficiaries through either service providers or NGOs partners. To ensure integration between food assistance and livelihood, 15% of the planned project beneficiaries will be targeted from the food assistance beneficiaries. The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity which can be scaled up at short notice whenever required. Below are activities that will be implemented per output:- Output 1: Access and availability of agricultural inputs for 170,000 households to facilitate production of cereals and pulses increased. Activities 1) Identification and selection of beneficiaries taking into consideration the gender and age requirements of the most vulnerable individuals and ability to utilize to full potential the provided inputs.2) Procurement and distribution of agricultural inputs (certified cereal and pulse seeds, fertilizers, herbicides)3) Undertake post distribution monitoring and impact evaluation and collection of disaggregated data by gender and age. Output 2. Production skills for cereal and of 170,000 targeted producers enhanced Activities:1. Design training programme depending on FAO materials2. Contract implementing partners to conduct the training3. Conduct a TOT programme for technical extension workers taking into considerations gender and age requirements. 4. Delivery of training to targeted households using proven methodologies including Farmer Field Schools while taking into consideration both gender and age of group leaderships as well as facilitators. Output 3. Enhanced production and preservation of high quality wheat seed 6,000 beneficiaries. Activities:1) Identification of beneficiaries including producers, seed certification staff and processors 2) Design training programme on seed selection, multiplication, quality control, processing and marketing3) Conduct a TOT programme for technical extension workers 4) Delivery of training to 6,000 targeted beneficiaries5) Procurement and distribution of breeders seed, production inputs and equipment to 6,000 beneficiaries NB: All the activities under each outputs will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.

Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population examples IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth base their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors; Mitigation measures: establishing a local beneficiaries selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiaries selection criteria; ensuring an effective communication in local language on guidance.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance: Mitigation measures: establish a community based beneficiaries selection and community based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do not harm principles. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of the being selected for the project. Inputs and training will take account of the differential requirements of women and men. Returnees will also be targeted so that their re-settlement will be aided. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible frictions between the two. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanisms will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways express their concerns and grievance. This will be used to make adjustments and improvement on the implementation of activities. Special attention will be provided to the returnees to help them in their settlement and resuming their production activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and assumptions beyond project control and their mitigation measures:1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation; Probability: Likely; Impact: Severe; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; Advocate with RC/HC and other instances to regain access. Develop contingency plans in coordination with Service Providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements/return of IDPs/Refugees that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities; Probability: High; Impact: high; Mitigation measures: Continue with advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities. Advocacy with other organizations to include new arrival in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross border movements and operations; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Ensure that Service Providers (SP) have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: major; Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate implementation of project activities. 5. Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the project area; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Insignificant; Mitigation measures: Assistance is balanced and not directed only to one specific group. Identification of beneficiaries is done in partnership with local communities including IDPs and host communities. Selected implementing partners that are accepted by local communities and aware of conflict sensitivity.6. Risks: Increased control of land and water by parties to crisis; Probability: Likely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from a range of sources. Accurate choice of target areas ensuring that farming is possible and beneficiaries will undertake the activity. Advocacy with local authorities by service providers. If necessary, move to alternative locations previously identified7. Risks: Possible climatic shocks during the implementation period (drought and floods in particular). Probability: Possible; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Select distribution areas in different agro ecological zones and in irrigated areas to reduce risks where possible. Distribute adapted varieties which possess specific desirable traits, including drought tolerance.
