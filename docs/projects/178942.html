Project Summary

Economic livelihoods across Syria have been severely impacted by the crisis. This impact is particularly apparent in the agriculture and livestock sector. Increases in input and production costs and disruptions in value chains, coupled with reduced availability of power and water resources are all factors leading to decreases in production levels, poor availability of locally produced foods, rising sector-related unemployment, and placing added strain on already weak social structures and communities in general.
Taking into consideration the above trends, the project aims to improve the livelihoods and food security of 1,700 unique beneficiaries including vulnerable female and male producers within Hama, Tartous and Rural Damascus. Building upon the strong linkages between the rehabilitation of the local economy and household livelihoods and food security, R2R project aims to enable vulnerable households to protect, sustain and re-establish livelihoods within a longer-term resilience-building goal. As such, this action is developed to:
1. Bridge the gaps within local value chains, namely knowledge, information and human resources by establishing Farmer Field Schools (FFSs) in the targeted villages in collaboration with public agriculture extension workers.
2. Improve access to agricultural and livestock productive assets and inputs, particularly for females and youth. Under this target the following activities will be implemented:
2.1. Provide basic agricultural, livestock and food production inputs for rural households
in the target villages.
2.2. Established and equip community-owned agricultural and livestock productive assets groups in the target villages of rural Hama, Damascus and Tartous.
Target villages will be selected carefully to satisfy a specific set of criteria that AKF has established for communities where the resilience model could be employed with minimal risk:
• A mix of vulnerability segments and hosting IDPs and returnees with relatively a large number of vulnerable female-headed households.
• A history of social activity and implementing community-based projects
• Agriculture and livestock are the predominant livelihoods activities
• Potential for diversified agricultural and livestock activities
• Accessible and safe (e.g. no mine risk).

4 - Needs

Depreciation of the Syrian currency in 2021 caused volatility in the exchange market and agricultural inputs prices to rise, making it remote for vulnerable farmers. Notable price increases were seen for mostly top dressing and phosphate fertilizers, herbicides, insecticides, poultry feed and diesel fuel (FAO_Agriculture_Input_Commodity_Bulletin_Feb21).There is a need to address the economic hardships and limited access to agricultural inputs by Syrian families in a holistic and multipronged approach.14.2 m people (68.6% of the population) need some form of food and agriculture assistance. At least 12.4 m people are food insecure, of which 1.3 m people are held severely food insecure (HNO 2021). The estimated sector (People in Need) PiN for 2021 –of food insecure or at risk of food insecurity– increased from 9.8 m to 14.2 m. That highlights an alarming decline due to currency depreciation, loss of purchasing power, hikes in food prices, large population displacements, lack of livelihood and income-generating options, losses in agricultural production and assets, all joined with ongoing conflicts and the COVID-19 pandemic.In Salamieh District, expenditure on food needs on average is SYP 54,000/US$ 20.0. Payments of loans/debts and instalments of rotated saving are SYP 45,000/US$ 16.7. Health expenditure is SYP 32,000/US$ 11.8. Education expenditure is SYP 23,000/US$ 8.5. Expenditure on shelter is SYP 20,000/US$ 7.4. The expenditure on social occasions is SYP 17,000/ US$ 6.3 all at the market exchange rate (AKF Syria 2020).While there is a lack of in-depth information on Rural Damascus and Tartous, the 2021 HNO statistics indicate that negative, coping strategies are a major problem across the country.

5 - Implementation of activities

10 Farmer Field Schools (FFSs) in 10 villages will be established and invested in as platforms for learning among local communities. Through a partnership with the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reforms (MAAR), agriculture and flock management best practices will be introduced to farmers who largely rely on traditional methods of farming. FFSs will reach a total of 200 farmers (30% females) to improve their technical knowledge and skills in agriculture. The project will improve agricultural basic inputs by providing 500 farmers with inputs such as winter and summer seeds kit + drip irrigation system (nets, plastic mulch ...etc.) To improve livestock health, the project will support 800 rural livestock farmers (20 % female) with veterinary kits and 400 tons of barley feed which meets the energy and protein requirements for sheep and dairy cows. The project will also establish and equip 20 community-based organizations (CBOs) that are representative of the ethnic, social and gender composition of 10 targeted villages. Under AKF’s supervision, CBOs will establish and equip 20 community productive assets groups to facilitate community access to critical production equipment, thereby increasing food production and enhancing farming households’ resilience against economic shocks. 200 households will benefit from community assets like olive harvesters, seeds, shredders for olive pruning, refrigerators and electrical shearer machines and chicks hatchery.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Increased risk of inter-community tension where beneficiaries may be exposed to discrimination from other farmers who are not targeted under this project.Likelihood: Low; Impact: Low.With the help of CBOs, AKF-Syr will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria and aim to strengthen knowledge sharing of new agricultural methods and communal benefits within their community. B: Women who own land assets or businesses may hand day-to-day control over to the male relatives, as a result of social pressure or because they do not feel confident enough to manage assets themselves. Women may also face some difficulties in using the new agricultural equipment and seek help from other male farmers which may expose them to exploitation.Likelihood: Low, Impact: Medium.Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against them. Strengthening women decision making through increase their access to technical knowledge and expanding their participation in CBOs. C: Gender-related cultural and traditional norms are more appreciative of men knowledge and work than women. As such, female farmers participation and work could be underestimated and may be exposed to verbal abuse by other male farmers while working together.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium.AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution along with an active complaint reporting system and facilitate gender equality awareness-raising or orientation sessions to targeted farmers to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting the movement of staff and beneficiaries.Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Mitigation: AKF-Syr regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. If the mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation can be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk 2: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in successfully procuring required items for delivery to Salamieh and other program areas. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation: AKDN has extensive experience in rural livelihoods programming in extremely difficult environments. AKF will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement tendering offers to within the Governorates.Risk 3: Inflation and exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level.Likelihood: High, Impact: low.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project and revise as needed.
