Project Summary

Bangladesh is currently hosting more than one million stateless Rohingya refugees accommodated in overcrowded refugee camps in Cox's Bazar District (CBD) and highly vulnerable to landslides, tropical cyclones, flash-flooding, and communicable disease outbreak. The monsoon season continues to impact the Rohingya refugees, with heavy rains and strong winds severely diminishing quality of life.
Next Year AMAN has the initiatives to enable the Rohingya refugees and vulnerable host Communities in Cox's Bazar through providing Emergency shelter assistance, Provision of HH level solar lights, Provision of NFI (Floor Mat, Mosquito Net, Bucket, Mug, Plate, Bowl, Glass, Blankets, Sweater, Shawl, Socks) and above all involving women, older people and other forms of gender diverse population reaching to 17,016 individuals (Whole Camp 17). To reach these targets, AMAN’s estimated budget is $888,248.
AMAN has experience in assisting refugees with shelter and NFI support since 2017. Next year AMAN is planning to build 40 two storey MTS based on the approved design and based on the site plan coordinated with UNHCR, camp 17. AMAN always emphasizes on capacity building trainings and its concerned staff has already attended in different training by the initiatives of Shelter /NFI, other sector focal and stakeholders that is the strength of AMAN to achieve the targeted objectives to ensure project sustainability. Moreover, AMAN’s field staff will be trained in PSEA to ensure proper protection mainstreaming.
Systematic process of program Monitoring using participatory methods is what AMAN will do next year to deliver the project and to ensure beneficiaries inclusion in the project design and implementation. Before intervention any projects, we will conduct baseline survey, FGD, KII, etc. Collect and analyses and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring. Overall, AMAN will coordinate with UNHCR Shelter Focal point in camp 17 for the emergency assistance and other shelter activities for proper implementation, monitoring and supervision.

1 - Identified Needs

The Shelter Sector’s Rapid Damage Verification recorded over 100% increase in damages in 2020 comparing to the damages reported in 2019, with a total of 26,739 verified damaged shelters recorded between May and August 2020, compared to 10,073 during the same period in 2019. This can be explained by several factors: a reduction of shelter activities due to COVID-19 containment measures (thus shelters were not repaired/upgraded for a year), heavy rainfall and winds during monsoon season and Cyclone AMPHAN, and the ongoing use of lightweight and temporary materials such as untreated bamboo and tarpaulin. Shelter materials is top priority need of Refugees (63% of households) identified Needs assessment by J-MSNA Sectoral preliminary findings, 8 October 2020 as follows- Shelter issues: Lack of sufficient shelter assistance to make repairs was a common concern. Some shelter reported to have been completely destroyed (collapsed/flooded) – in particular, shelter located on hillside and in low-lying areas reported to be at risk. Households lack money to repair shelter themselves – some save on food or borrow to afford shelter repairs. Moreover, considering the increasing of population and limited land area, the construction of 2 storey shelter (durable materials such as treated bamboo, steel shelters, RC post) based on the approved design and irrespective of the site planning is a need based strategy. As part of the MTS strategy, UNHCR is advocating for the construction of ‘transitional shelters with innovative shelter alternatives to address spatial constraints and congestion in the settlements. NFI issues: Most commonly issues with lights, cooking and sleeping items reported. 58% Households reporting not having enough light. Light does not work/is broken 8%; Light does not last long enough 19%; Not enough light outside shelter 25%; Not enough light inside shelter 29%. Due to the lack of electricity and malfunctioning of some solar panels, poor lighting in the camps creates an increased protection risk for women at night time. Other most needed NFIs are kitchen sets (42%), and blankets (39%) (JMSNA, Host Community, 2019). The Rohingya frequently requested fans, blankets, and mosquito nets. After the brunt of the monsoon, found an immediate need with mosquito nets, mats, and solar lamps prioritized instead and requests for fans increasing by 14% (NPM 08/2020). Gender resilience: The normative framework that governs the lives of Rohingya refugees unequally affects women and girls by limiting their mobility, their ability to make decisions about their lives and access lifesaving services, and deprioritizes their needs and demands compared to those of men. Women are excluded from decision-making systems in the community, and their lack of access to reliable information, consultations and feedback mechanisms limits their ability to influence both prevention and response plans. The inclusion of women leaders, networks and volunteers in the planning, implementation and monitoring of response plans will have a strong impact on making these right-based strategies, inclusive and effective. Aman will ensure the most vulnerable households, including female-headed households, still have access to materials and support to make shelter improvements and to prepare for the cyclone season. Through the proposed project, Aman will provide culturally and gender appropriate NFIs, including for those in isolation and quarantine facilities. As per gender age group adolescent, women, elderly will be provided training on GBV issues, Covid-19 prevention, personal hygiene and above all social cohesion thus they will be aware of their rights and their roles in the Communities in regards of achieving Shelters and NFI objectives.
