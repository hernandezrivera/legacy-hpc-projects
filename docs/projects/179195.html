Project Summary

It is the 10th year of the Syrian crisis that resulted in millions of refugees and IDPs, devastating destruction of civilian buildings, infrastructures, and facilities. With the political solution far from close shelling and airstrikes on opposition-controlled Northwest Syria continues to cause losses and human casualties and hinder the area's development.
NW Syria (Aleppo western and northern countryside, and the governorate of Idleb) is an area that is estimated to hold around 1.5 million internally displaced persons came in different influxes since the regime lost control gradually in 2012, now the crowded area shapes the highest density per acre compared to all governorates of Syria struggles a spread of destruction, the spread of camps and loss of infrastructures and government support to all sectors, and relying on humanitarian interventions support and international aid.
The objective of this project is to improve strategic crops value chain through the establishment, training, and developing governance to a Strategic crops Quality Control and Research Facility in Northwest Syria and working through coordination to obtain community recognition of the testing facility and prepare the base for the certification system of olive oil in Euphrates Shield Area.
This project aims to apply interventions in the value chains of local strategic crops of Northwest Syria by utilizing evidence-based practices towards a sustainable effect on the value chains.
The main intervention would be the establishment of an agricultural product community based strategic crop quality control facility; this will be based on Orange’s experience in developing agricultural laboratories in the previous years of interventions in NW Syria; the establishment process will include training and capacity development of technical and administrative staff, procurement, setting up and operation of equipment and testing devices and, the development if governance methods and cost recovery plan for the facility to run sustainably. This facility will be established in close coordination then handed over to the ministry of agriculture of the Syrian interim government, and Orange will be working closely with the latest and their Turkish counterparts to obtain national or international accreditation for the facility.
After the facility is established, Orange will disseminate information for the local community members and producers about the benefit of the services of the facility, and its team will reach out to local farmers and collect periodical samples for testing. Those tests will be used for research purposes by universities in Turkey, pass on helpful advice, and promotion of good practices in agriculture in addition to forming the basis for the distribution of integrated pest management kits for farmers in need.

4 - Needs

UN estimates the number of people, including IDPs, is more than 4 million, most of which living very hard security, shelter, and economic conditions; knowing that the nature of the areas is rural Mediterranean heavily dependent on agriculture with the shy presence of light industries and services, it becomes quite obvious that the absence of governmental support.It is estimated that 9.6 million olive trees (71%) of Syria’s trees are concentrated in NW Syria, making Syria a major producer of olives and olive oil. The international olive oil council estimated Syria’s contribution to the olive oil production to 35,000 Metric tons in 2011, but this production is believed to have plummeted during the war years for multiple reasons listed in Orange’s Olive Production Quality Assessment in Azaz and Afrin ; the assessment concluded that the lack of quality control is the main theme of the market in NW Syria which poses the main obstacle against export.Around 80% of the survey participants listed the main difficulties facing the marketing of olive oil to either lack of quality control, lack of certification. The current olive oil market in NW Syria consists of farmers who produce olives, mills that produce olive oil, whole sailors that buy a large amount of either olive or oil and distribute them to retailers. The assessment, while shows a moderate availability of wholesales, reveals that there Is a very small number of companies that trade olive and olive oil, and the majority of the available ones either sell to retailers or limited amounts gets smuggled outside NW most of the time unfair trades and value due to the loss of standard methods of quality check.

5 - Implementation of activities

This Project aims to contribute to the economic development in North West Syria through enhancements in strategic crops value chain with a focus on olives through the establishment of credited, technically equipped, well-governed, community-based quality control and technical testing facility. The main adjective is improving strategic crops value chain through the establishment, training and developing governance to a Strategic crops Quality Control and Research Facility in Northwest Syria and working through coordination to obtain community recognition of the testing facility and prepare the base for the certification system of olive oil in Euphrates Shield Area. Through a dedicated and experienced team of agronomists and technicians, Orange will be working to identify all the required tools and equipment required for carrying out the recognized quality control tests. The identification will utilize previous implementation experiences of Orange in addition to multiple consultation meetings with relevant project stakeholders. The identification will be conducted in consultation with the agriculture department, the agriculture directorate, and the ministry of agriculture of the Syrian Interim Government (SIG). Moreover, Orange team will coordinate with the relevant academic entities in the local faculties of agronomy in Idleb and Jarablus, as well as the faculty of agronomy at Gaziantep University.
