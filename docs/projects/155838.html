Project Summary

The project design is based on the following:
Increasing the food security and enhancing the livelihoods of IDPs and Host Communities (25% IDPs, 75 % host communities) in Idleb Governorate (Al Ma'ra District) as following:
1. (350 HHs) in (Khan Shaykun - Hbit- Qasabiyeh - Abdin- Kafr ain) will receive vouchers of (wheat seeds, wheat fertilizers, wheat pesticides , fuel for irrigation , and costs of harvest). To enable them to cultivate 3500 dunum wheat as total.
2. (200 HHs) in (Bennsh- Toum) will receive vouchers of (wheat seeds, fertilizers, pesticides , fuel for irrigation , and costs of harvest). To enable them to cultivate 2000 dunum wheat as total.
WSA MEAL department conducted need assessments (please refer to attached documents): which have identified significant need for both IDPs and host families who have lost productive crop agriculture due to ongoing conflict and displacement, this led to reduced purchasing power and income generation , which resulted finally in hindering food security. It is critical to support these households to begin to restore the lost agriculture crops , as main sources of livelihoods, and enhancing their resilience by enabling them to have more food availability through personal production of the products (as well as income generation from selling the more extra food products).
Key interventions will include:
1- Provision vouchers to buy agricultural inputs, accompanied by related extension services (Irrigation + Harvest).
2- Capacity Building targeting women farmers and local community members.
3- Follow-up the project during the production cycle of the crops.
WSA is planning to implement the project through providing (wheat seeds vouchers, 2 types of fertilizer vouchers, 4 types of pesticide vouchers, fuel vouchers, Harvest vouchers), to:
1. 350 HHs in Khan Shaykun and surrounding villages (1135 female and 615 male), approximately (130 men, 430 women, 705 girls, and 485 boys) (the women and men farmers and their children).
2. 200 HHs in Bennsh- Toum (625 female and 375 male), approximately (80 men, 225 women, 400 girls, and 295 boys). (the women and men farmers and their children).
Beneficiaries HHs will receive 9 types of vouchers and redeem their vouchers against agricultural inputs and extension services, at the local markets contracted by WSA.
WSA will hold awareness sessions with the traders and all beneficiaries to explain the voucher redeeming mechanisms and to sensitize them to the list of authorized agricultural inputs.
Throughout the duration of the project a market monitoring system will be put in place in all targeted areas. To ensure that the project does not disrupt or affect the markets systems and balance.
WSA monitors will collect information for the market monitoring during spot checks monitoring visits at the local shops involved in the project. Traders will be warned ahead of the project start that spot checks can be conducted by WSA so as to discourage any forms of corruption.
WSA will also establish a complaint mechanism (hotline), and a participatory monitoring system through working with local authorities, beneficiary representatives. Depending upon the good relationship that WSA has established through the WGSS (*) in Khan Shaykun and Bennsh, All these measures to mitigate the risks of fraud and to assure the gender sensitivity.
WSA will identify, verify and profile target beneficiaries through community leader, Volunteer and local council (where the long list will be prepared). Then the short list will be identified by the criteria of selecting beneficiaries, considering vulnerable people, including; IDPs, hosting families, and female headed HHs as priority. (**)
Note:The planned date for the project and distribution of agriculture inputs vouchers will be in November 2019 The project will end at the June 2020 .

Needs

Drawn from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations ( HNO 2019).The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnes of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes. Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation are affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies HRP 2019A rapid needs assessment in the project area shows that the target areas of the project are villages lacking agricultural supportIn addition, these areas are difficult to reach and include a number of IDPs from the rural Damascus and Homs governorates, as well as directly dependent on the cultivation of the wheat crop.Through the focus group Discussion with the host community (men> 19 - women Age > 19-girls <19- youth<19 ) and IDPs (men> 19 - women Age > 19-girls <19- youth<19 ) in the project areas the results showed that project areas are considered ideal areas for planting wheat, large agricultural areas are available, irrigation sources are available in the area.However, the continuation of the Syrian crisis and the security threats in the region, in addition to the high prices of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, fuel) have led to the reluctance of farmers to cultivate their lands and abandonment, which led to the spread of many agricultural problems such as the spread of vole for exampleThe needs assessment revealed that the IDPs suffer from many social and economic difficulties and problems related to integration with the community in addition to lack of employment opportunities.A rapid needs assessment in the project area shows the wheat production in Bennsh reduced during the past years due to the difficulty of access of farmers to agricultural land as a result of clashes between the militants of Kafria and Al-Fu'a and FSA in Bennsh .Wheat production in Khan Shaykun and its villages has also reduced as a result of continues security threats in the region, leading to the reluctance of most organizations to operate in the region .The needs assessment also revealed (Women>19 )and (girls <19 )within Al-Ma'ra district( Khan Shaykun) and Bennsh are shouldering much of the economic burden and striving to ensure livelihoods for themselves and their families .During the survey was conducted by the M & E staff in the region and focus group discussion with farmers and local councils, it was found that the project areas include large holdings of farmland for farmers that lack agricultural support. It is also feasible to support at least 10 dunums to encourage farmers to planting and not to sell inputs And achieve a good income (third of the production will be stored for next season to achieve sustainability) Based on the survey and lessons learned from previous projects, the project was designed on the basis of agricultural support for at least 10 dunums,by considering the most vulnerable groups of beneficiaries.

Implementation of activities

This project will be directly implemented by WSA in coordination and cooperation with the local councils of the selected target districts.Line of command and operations: The project will be led by an experienced project manager who will be assisted by community mobilizers.The community mobilizers will be in charge of beneficiary selection from both IDPs and host community, based on the following criteria: most vulnerable households, households headed by women, household with elderly, disabled, households with sick care takers (these groups will be defined as non-working benefices and families suffering from a lack of employment), child mothers, youths, and child headed households. They will be strongly supported by the M & E team, which will provide information on market prices, beneficiary satisfaction, etc., and a finance department (supporting the team for budget management and follow up). One key aspect of the project financial management will be the verification of vouchers and the payment of traders. WSA collaborating with local councils will implement full market assessment and identify traders and evaluate their capacity and reputation, to sign the MOU with selected traders. many awareness sessions will implement with traders and beneficiaries to identify vouchers and how benefices redeem it. Voucher will provide with many information (beneficiary name, ID, code, signed of field manager, .... etc.) to avoid fraud.The beneficiaries will be verified and their ownership of agricultural lands or rent agreements will be verified and registered in local councils to avoid fraudThe project will integrate IDPs with the host community through the establishment of clear mechanisms in cooperation with the local councils to rent agricultural land from their owners during the project period by paid money or part of the production at the end of the project and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and supervision by coordination with local authorities.WSA will select days for beneficies to receive vouchers and will select days to redeem it, according to the agricultural process. Under this project with WSA team in operational area verifying all voucher received by the traders at the end of each process, and transferring money to them. The composition of Female will be recommended to be at least 60%. The project will be managed by a qualified FSL technical adviser and oversee the local staff (Agronomist, Project Manager) by providing technical advice and guidanceThe project will have four M & E staff (2male and 2female). The female monitoring staff will remain in tandem with the women beneficaries of the project to avoid any cases of sexual harassment and immediate reporting in coordination with ISWA GBV centers.WSA will integrate gender-based violence prevention activities into project activities through the development of an action plan that will incorporate the principles of protection during project implementation stages.Throughout the WGSS : Women and Girls Safe Spaces which WSA is managing as sustainable community centers in Khan Shaykun and Bennsh , they are called SALAM center 1 in Khan Shykun and SALAM center 3 in Bennsh. (SALAM center 2 is in Madiq Castle).Those center are providing GBV ( Prevention and response ) services for Women and girls. The project is funded by THF 7012 now and then by THF 9297 afterwords. This new livelihood proposal is aiming to complement the protection one, provide livelihood opportunities to the women farmers , and vise versa , provide protection awareness and gender sensitivity Capacity building for the women and men farmers beneficiaries in the new proposal.Total project beneficiaries in this sector will be 64% female and 36 % men is due to the fact that majority of Agriculture related work is done by women and men. in addition of WSA is specialized in supporting the resilience of Syrian women and all interventions will be in line with “Do no harm” approach.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1:Some people and dominant groups in the work area do not accept the idea that there are particular centers for women and girls especially that our our center is the ﬁrst one of its kind in the area. Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure: External awareness sessions to introduce the center's activities. Risk 2: Distinguishing between residents and displaced citizens Risk Level: Average /Medial Mitigation Measure : Full survey of the percentage of the displaced people in the region and implementing external activities for them and inviting them to the center. Risk 3: Distinguishing between residents of the city and the residents of the neighbourhood areas Risk Level: High Mitigation Measure: Increase the external activities in the neighbourhood villages more and more.Risk 4: threats of kidnapping the workers in organizations that have been widely spread in the Syrian inside Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure : district of safety and security rules in terms of mobility and not to declare the dates of mobility and not to paste any banners of the association logo on the cars.Risk 5: The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. In terms we are the lonely center that is specialized in the service GBV Risk Level : Average / Medial Mitigation Measure : Prioritizing the women according to their needs and provide them the services they need. Risk 6: Fear of the inability to keep the conﬁdential and secret documents safe due to the emergency security incidents. Risk Level: High. Mitigation Measure: Archiving all the documents on confidential electronic system and destroying the paper documents periodically as soon as they are completed and done.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conﬂict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneﬁciaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities and multiple locations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensiﬁed shelling 5- Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activities. B- Unforeseen Changes in the Conﬂict / This risk including that staff of organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Continue to gather information from range of sources. 2- Develop contingency and evacuation plan to work in alternative location. 3- Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 4- Training to the staff about contingency and evacuation plan in addition to Information Security 5- Duty of staff C- IEDs/VBIEDs/PBIEDs/ Can be direct or indirect. It is one of the major threats in Syria. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose centers far from military locations 2- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activates. 3- Commitment to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality to avoid Targeting. 4- Continuing coordination with the Security and Safety department during staff movement D- The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, especially extremist groups, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Solution/ Mitigation measures:1- The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. 2- WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. 3- Coordination with civilian local authoritiesE- Flocculation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation:1- WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up.2- Sending money to Syria by PTT (Turkish government postal and telegraph organization)
