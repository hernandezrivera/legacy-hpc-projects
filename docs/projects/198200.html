1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will support the treatment of acute malnutrition in children under five and pregnant and lactating women and the elderly to reduce mortality and related morbidity, including those suffering from HIV and other vulnerable groups (adolescent girls and people with disabilities). The project will cover 6000 beneficiaries.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will carry out the following activities to be carried out in the two districts: Conduct social mobilization intervention at community / resettlement level for adherence to integrated health and nutrition service delivery  Support EPAs / activities / volunteers as required for nutrition screening, nutrition counseling and provision of nutrition care and referral of at risk groups (children under 5 years. PLW, PLW with HIV) at community level Support EPAs / activities / volunteers as needed for home visits to follow up nutrition cases at community leve Provide support, as required, in the distribution of supplies and goods from province level to district level and from district level to facilities  Monitoring stock and ensuring stock-outs  Ensuring the quality of data collected through community actors such as EPAs, activities, mothers groups  Reproduction/printing of log book for data collection and MoH guidelines  Ensure timely submission of data from health and nutrition activities at community level  Cooking demonstrations and awareness of IYCF good practices (Cooking toolkit, Ingredients for cooking demonstrations in the health community) Training for nutrition activists, teachers and community leaders  Conduct training of activists/volunteers on screening for acute malnutrition in children under 5 years  Support PEAs in capturing nutritional data for UPSCALE  Conduct baseline study / lessons learned on the use of Family MUAC  Monthly nutritional assessments for IDPs in communities and resettlement centres Support in logistics and supervision of nutrition sites (NIP) Reproduction/printing of cookbooks based on locally produced foods  Conduct training for model mothers' groups to Promote good cooking practices and use of Family MUAC Conduct training for community activists and district supervisors  Conduct community awareness session (public announcement) in places of high population concentration (markets, schools) for adoption of healthy, sanitary and child protection behaviours  Promote radio spots in the community with key WASH messages (6 spots per day for 6 months) translated into one or two local mian languages  In coordination with local radio stations, facilitate radio debates on PC, WASH with the participation of DDS, DDEDH, DDGCAS technicians (1 debate per radio per week for 6 months)

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The risks will be identified at the field level and the potential risks to be identified and mitigated (places of provision of the project services; gender balance in the project; The type of product or service to be provided. )- Free and easy access to people: areas or places accessible to all beneficiaries, including people with disabilities, will be selected and conditioned to guarantee non-exclusion without exclusion. Gender balance in the project: this component guarantees that the project has complete data, for example: it is easier for a lady to talk to another woman to report something that is bothering her than with a man, and this prevents cases in the community. Service to provide: identify based on community needs, and avoid giving a non-food product without explaining how to use it. Promote lectures in the community to explain the project and all the possible services to be made available, this avoids a bad perception and creates risks during implementation

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Consultations will be carried out with individual people, including specific reflection with women, older men, children and people with disabilities who are most related, to understand the level of perception about nutrition in general and from that point, the team will promote lectures of sensitization in the communities with themes related to nutrition and chronic malnutrition, and a balanced and maintained diet of an unbalanced diet, and in the same way the community will be made aware of how to make meals rich in animal and vegetable proteins from the foods available locally, reducing the risks of malnutrition in the community, supplements and a package of advice will also be allocated to each identified family, on breastfeeding, water and sanitation, and others.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

As it is considered an inclusive project, people in need will be the focus of Caritas, including the elderly to identify specific needs for infrastructure adaptation. The infrastructure will be adapted to these needs. Persons with disabilities will be one of the priorities during distributions. Distribution points will be serviced, ensuring ease of access for community members, especially people with disabilities and women. If there are any people with disabilities and if they have some leadership character and ability to do some work, such as registering, organising beneficiaries, writing reports, they can be included in the field team

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The focus of the project is nutrition, so field agents will be trained to serve the target local communities, how to make healthy food for their families, keep the environment clean, avoid throwing garbage in inappropriate places, promoting the use of latrines to avoid water contamination, promoting advice on breastfeeding, among other practices, to ensure the nutritional well-being of communities.
