Project Summary

The food security situation in Syria continues to deteriorate. An estimated 15.5 million people are in need of food or livelihoods support. At least 12.9 million people are in need of food assistance, and a further 2.6 million are at risk of food insecurity. Food needs are widespread across Syria due to many factors contribute to food security deterioration in Syria such as weather anomalies and climate change, water scarcity, limited energy supply, food and agriculture commodities prices increase, very high prices of all the agriculture production inputs, and poor quality of some of them, lack of access to pastures and veterinary services, high prices of feed and veterinary treatments, inflation, lack of purchasing power due to limited livelihood and lack of income sources. ACF in 2024 and 2025 will maintain minimum food consumption for the most vulnerable severely and moderately food insecure households by providing food assistance through the appropriate modality. ACF will provide food assistance covering 8 months and will adopt the recommended Transfer Value by the FSA Sector. ACF will also support self-reliance and sustain households’ livelihoods by providing agriculture-based livelihoods interventions, including the distribution of agricultural inputs such as seeds and fertilisers; distribution of seed drills and grinders; improving the vegetative cover of pastures by planting drought- and saline resistant forage plants; support the adaptation of the Conservation Agriculture approach through inputs distribution and capacity building; providing capacity building on Climate Smart Agriculture related concepts such as Climate Smart Crop Production and Climate Smart Livestock Production; conducting capacity building on aspects related to composting, sustainable water management, hydroponically sprouted barley, and holistic pasture management; establishing Farmers/Livestock Keepers Field Schools to provide the needed knowledge to produce silage and animal feed blocks from crop residues, including the widespread plant Arundo donax, which blocks agricultural drainage canals; the installation of rainwater harvesting units to improve water availability for the livestock keepers; the installation of biogas units to improve access to renewable source of energy; conducting light rehabilitation of key economic infrastructure such as irrigation canals, and agricultural drainage canals for the purpose of improving the availability of water for irrigation, and mitigate any harm of increasing the water table in the gypsum lands by rehabilitating agricultural drainage canals; conducting light rehabilitation of bakeries; creating income generation opportunities for female headed households by providing them with vocational training on food processing and preservation and by providing them with grants for establishing their own businesses. ACF will consider that all proposed interventions are aligned with the FSA Sector's SO1 and SO2 and SO3 recommendations and the FSA Strategy for 2024 in terms of targeting, prioritisation, and supporting the multisectoral graduation approach. ACF will ensure Accountability to the Affected Population by i including disabled persons - by mitigating any potential barriers they might face as much as possible - in all phases of the project cycle, consulting them about the intervention, and sharing information.
Gender sensitive approaches and safeguarding of beneficiaries are integral to ACF programming, with dedicated staff upholding gender minimum standards and mainstreaming safeguarding processes. ACF recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ACF’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF provides compulsory training to each new staff member and refresher sessions to existing staff on organizational ethical policies. Internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistleblowing/reporting mechanisms are followed with employees receiving instruction on these internal policies. ACF employees and any third parties contracted by ACF sign a Code of Conduct and are inducted on its contents. There are various gender-balanced focal points against harassment at both capital and base levels in Syria, as well as a Safeguarding lead for ACF Syria based in Damascus. ACF staff are trained in Gender and Protection mainstreaming, to ensure any assistance is by ACF in a safe and dignified manner. Protection standards are followed to ensure safe access to Food Security and Agriculture services. Child Protection charters are included in contracts and HR procedures, with zero tolerance for child labour and abuse. ACF upholds the IASC principles on PSEA and has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA. ACF protect people’s dignity and safety by implementing protection measures in all implementation modalities, whether in-kind or cash, and maintaining Protection and Gender minimum standards for all activities, including at on-site in rehabilitation projects of basic service facilities, interventions in collective shelters, or any other contact points with beneficiaries throughout ACF programming such as during assessment, consultation or feedback mechanisms. All employees are trained in safeguarding and have access to reporting systems. ACF also upholds a multi-modal and accessible Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism, through which people can freely provide feedback to ACF, which is handled according to a stringent internal protocol. Assumptions are related to securing timely approval for proposed projects, including by the concerned Line Ministries. An unstable security situation, restricted access to targeted locations, and lengthy administrative procedures may cause a high impact. Mitigation strategies include advocacy, ongoing context analysis and monitoring, and coordination and access negotiation with local authorities. Additionally, there is a risk of currency devaluation, fraud, lack of data access, and ethical concerns. Mitigation strategies for these risks include close and direct monitoring, training staff members on organisational policies, transparency, and trust-building.
