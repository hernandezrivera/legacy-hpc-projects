Project Summary

Management, coordination and monitoring of activities, and deployment of professional staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver timely and effective humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination with relevant stakeholders and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine refugees.
While the vast majority of posts needed to deliver the mandate of the Agency in Syria will continue to be filled by Palestine refugees and Syrian nationals, UNRWA will maintain some international capacity, particularly specialist staff in the fields of humanitarian response, monitoring and evaluation, and grants management.
UNRWA will continue to conduct safety and security trainings for its staff across Syria, including on COVID-19 safety measures. UNRWA has made significant investments in the safety and security of its staff, assets and facilities despite financial challenges. In 2021, the Agency will continue to focus on security personnel staffing costs, the procurement of communication devices such as VHF radios, as well as the procurement of PPE for security staff in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. These investments will enable UNRWA to facilitate and provide security support for over 600 field missions in 2021 in a medium to high risk security environment, and to conduct security risk assessments of its installations.
In order to ensure that UNRWA facilities are compliant with minimum operating security standards, the Agency will conduct security risk assessments on targeted facilities and will carry out necessary physical enhancement works to ensure a safe and secure environment for its staff and beneficiaries accessing assistance and services. In addition, UNRWA plans to install a closed circuit television (CCTV) room and Internet Protocol (IP) surveillance cameras at its offices in Damascus and Central areas. UNRWA will continue to train its staff in Safe and Security Approaches to Field Environment (SSAFE) and will also provide fire safety trainings. Finally, through its commitment to ensuring gender considerations in security management, and recognizing that women are more exposed to risks of GBV, UNRWA in Syria will maintain a Women’s Security Awareness Training (WSAT) programme for UNRWA personnel.
Finally, under this project, UNRWA will also sustain the operational needs of support departments, including HR, Administration, Procurement and Logistics. UNRWA has also included a budget for the minor repair and maintenance of UNRWA facilities to ensure safe and functional delivery of services for both beneficiaries and staff.

4 - Needs

In Syria, the protracted conflict has left 91 per cent of the 438,000 Palestine refugees estimated to remainin the country in absolute poverty and 40 per cent displaced. Following nearly a decade of extreme hardship, in 2020 living conditions deteriorated further as a result of an economic crisis that has seen a sharp fall in the value of the Syrian Pound against the US Dollar, leading to an increase in prices including of the most basic commodities. The vulnerability of Palestine refugees in Syria has been further excerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. In July 2020 UNRWA assessed the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 on Palestine refugees in Syria, and found that close to 80 per cent had reduced the number of meals or quantity of food consumed, and over 90 per cent were consuming food that was cheaper and/or less nutritious, since March 2020. It is imperative for UNRWA to continue provide essential service to the Palestine refugee community in Syria and to ensure operations are adequately supported. Support for UNRWA operations continues to be key. Staffing, emergency repair and maintenance of UNRWA’s facilities, as well as investments in the safety of personnel and assets are a prerequisite for UNRWA to continue operating in Syria, and to deliver vital assistance to Palestinian refugees affected by the crisis. UNRWA has been assisting Palestine refugees within a volatile security environment over the past decade. Investment in experts, security and safety of staff, assets and facilities, as well as in its security procedures, is critical for reducing the risk faced by staff and beneficiaries while maintaining operational integrity and neutrality.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities include 1. Recruitment of local as well as international staff; 2.Procurement of security and safety equipment items; 3. Security enhancement of UNRWA’s facilities; 4. Building the capacity of staff on safety and security including SSAFE training; 5. Maintenance of UNRWA programme support and operational premises ; 6. Covering the operational costs of support departments. The project aims at ensuring that UNRWA support departments are well equipped to provide support to the UNRWA implementing programmes, to ensure the efficient provision of service to PR and a secure and safe environment for UNRWA staff and beneficiaries. Maintained UNRWA installations will ensure the Agency’s ability to continue operate and provide assistance to Palestine Refugees. Management, coordination and technical staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine refugees. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programmes. UNRWA will continue to build staff capacity regarding neutrality, to comply with the UNRWA regulatory framework in relation to AVAC and the prevention of SEA, and to participate in humanitarian coordination mechanisms at country level, including the humanitarian sectors and the HCT.

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of SEA for beneficiaries of UNRWA services and assistance • Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management processes 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor interventions through results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
