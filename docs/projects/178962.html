Project Summary

CP:
Shafak organization will respond to the increased child protection concerns of the crises affected children including CWDs through center-based activities in five Child-Friendly Spaces and 19 community outreach teams deployed in Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Ariha and Idleb districts in Idleb and Jabal Saman, Azaz, Jarablus, and Ghandorah in Aleppo.
Shafak will provide CP case management and referral in the targeted locations targeting children with CP concerns. CM services will be complemented with provision of emergency case fund and CVA to respond to the immediate and urgent needs of children including CWDs. The following CP vulnerability criteria and concerns will be considered for CM and IPA support:
1- UASC
2- Child labor including its worst forms
3- CAAFAG
4- Early Marriage
5- Child headed household
6- Domestic violence
7- Severe Psychological distresses
Moreover, age and gender appropriate structured and non-structured PSS activities for children and adolescents will be organized in and out the CFSs. Different curriculums will be used such as; IRC PSS manual, I DEAL and Child Resilience.
Positive parenting skills sessions will be provided to caregivers to reduce the physical and humiliating punishment of children in the home, and improve the capacity of parents and caregivers to practice positive parenting.
In addition, Shafak will target the communities with awareness-raising activities on PSEA, child rights, and CP issues. Thus, messages will be tailored to gender and age needs in target locations to ensure gender sensitivity and also that messaging is relevant to children at specific age levels.
Finally, Shafak will strengthen the capacity of local communities about the child concerns by establishing CPNs covering all age and gender groups in the targeted communities to prevent and respond to CP needs at the community level. And secondly, train key persons in those communities and the workers in care centers, schools, hospitals who are in direct touch with children about the CPMS, PFA, and Safe Identification and referral.
Shafak will adapt the CFSs and the static facilities to be accessible and friendly for CWDs through establishing ramps and toilets for CWDs.
Overall: the project will target 58450 (5550M, 10300W, 19300B and 19300G) with the CP activities
Mine Action:
Shafak will deliver a comprehensive approach to Mine Action involving Risk Education, and Victim Assistance. This project will be a continuation of Shafak work in Explosive Remnants of War Risk Education ERW - RE programme in Idleb and Aleppo.
Under the ERW-RE part of the project, 4 RE teams who have already been trained by Shafak, will target IDPs and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo providing school-age children with RE activities, and materials to inform them about dangers of ERW. They will also provide RE material to adults, putting up posters about the ERW risks in the public streets and communities to increase the awareness of ERW threats.
Moreover, the RE (ToT) Trainers will provide RE training to Community Focal Points (CFPs) in affected communities, and RE teams and other workers from NGOs across all humanitarian sectors, to improve the relevance, integration and reach of RE messaging across NW Syria.
Furthermore, the project will also enhance the resilience of people living with injuries and disabilities (PLWDs), focusing on ERW survivors, through targeting those victims with vocational trainings and referrals to specialized services to enhance their access to livelihood opportunities and re-integrate them with their communities. Overall, the project will target 25500 individuals (3000M, 5500W, 8500B and 8500G).
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity to maximum 8 people and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, and the use of PPE.

4 - Needs

Multiple and complex child protection risks continue to shape the lives of girls and boys throughout Syria. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. Repeated and prolonged displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty, disintegration of social support structures, and the persistent challenges in access and availability to services continue to take a huge toll on children.Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, sexually abused, attacked while at school or at the hospital and denied humanitarian access by parties to the conflict Explosive ordnance contamination continues to be a major protection issue in Syria, with approximately one in two people estimated to be exposed to the risks posed by explosive contamination, and around a third of populated communities estimated to be potentially contaminated. Communities reporting potential contamination generally correspond to areas having witnessed high levels of hostilities over the past years, in particular in Aleppo and Idleb. (Draft HNO 2021)As per CPSM Report Dec, 79% of KIs reported the need for PSS during COVID 19. 19. 83% of the KIs reported that children are engaged in various types of work preventing them from going to school, this high percentage and index are related to the high displacement and the economic situation as well as due to COVID 19 breakout. 40% of the KIs reported that children separated from their usual caregivers.

5 - Implementation of activities

CP activities will be delivered through five CFSs, that will be rehabilitated and equipped with ramps and toilets for CWDs, and 19 CP mobile teams and will include:-PSS: Age and gender appropriate structured-PSS activities for children will be delivered, each group will receive 6 to 8 session, 1 to 2 sessions per week. Adolescents boys and girls will be also targeted with the PSS activities. Target:12000 (6000B,6000G)-Parenting Skills: 2600 (600M,2000 W) parents/caregivers will be targeted with this activity divided into groups 6 to 10 participant, 6 to 8 sessions for each.-Case Management: Shafak will provide CP CM, referrals, and IPA in the targeted locations, these services will target children with CP concerns (e.g. PSS distress, UASC, CAAFAG, child labor). IPA will be provided through in-kind and Cash modalities to 2600 (1300B, 1300G)-Awareness raising: Sessions will be conducted on the impact of key CP issues on children’s life and PSEA to 41000(5500 M, 9500W, 12500B, 12500G)-CP Networks: establish and support 18 CPNs. -Training of 250 CP staff, CPNs and humanitarian workers on CP topics: CPMS, CPIE.MA: -Deliver RE sessions to 25100 individuals by the RE teams and public service providers-Train 150 people on RE to deliver RE messages.-Support 250 victims through victim assistance and referrals to specialized services to receive adequate support. MEAL Plan: MEAL activities will be conducted to measure the impact of the delivered activities through:1-Sessions feedback for AWR activities2-Pre/post-test for training and PSS activities.3- CM will be monitored and evaluated by trained CM team ensuring confidentiality principle through satisfaction survey tool and checking the CM files by the CM supervisors

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

CPRisk1: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized CP services. Likelihood: HighMitigation:-Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context-Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.-Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.-Establish consultation with communitiesRisk 2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff member’s/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: Medium.Mitigation: -Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. -Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.MARisk1: Familiarization may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Likelihood: Medium Mitigation: -Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education. -Conduct regular KAPB surveys to monitor changes in risk behavior, as well as qualitative evaluations to understand the issues underpinning risk behavior.Risk 2: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance and medical capacity provided by humanitarian organizations. Likelihood: LowMitigation:Coordinate with development organizations to ensure the establishment of local medical capacities.Develop projects that can be sustained on a long-term basis without NGO support

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security related SOPs in place.1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to pre-empt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly.Risk 2: Loss and/or damages to program equipment and supplies and/or staff injured or killed due to airstrikes or other security-related incidents; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:2.1 Ensure adequate security/surveillance at Shafak warehouses, facilities, and offices.2.2 Security related SOPs in place.2.3 Minimizing value/quantity of items at any given site while ensuring balance with programmatic needs and contingency.Risk 3: Road cutting due to internal clashes and IED detonations; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:3.1 providing clearance for the field team before conducting any missions, identifying safe roads from/ to the both centers and informing staff to use it.3.2 Avoiding areas with a history of explosion/bombardment incidents.Risk 4: Aid diversion, corruption, fraud; Likelihood: low; Impact: High.Mitigation measures:4.1 Appropriate mitigation measures within the procurement process (including mandatory reviews from Compliance and other relevant departments).4.2 Anti-fraud, anti-corruption, code of conduct, humanitarian principles and other relevant trainings provided to all staff.
