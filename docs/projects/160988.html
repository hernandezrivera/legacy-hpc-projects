Project Summary

ADRA has been implementing protection services in Upper Nile in the counties of Nasir, Maiwut, Pagak, Ulang and Longechuk since 2014 and has become versed with the protection concerns of the communities. During monitoring activities in 2019, communities living in these locations experienced both man made and natural disasters which increased vulnerabilities and risks of the affected population.According to IRNA findings conducted in Nasir, Maiwuit, and Ulang in the first half of 2019, the communities living in these counties experienced a fluid security situation throughout the dry season with conflicts reported amongst different clans and sub clans resulting to massive displacements, loss of property, loss of lives, sexual assault and exploitation,inability to access services including life saving services,compromised protective environment and increased vulnerabilities especially for women , children, marginalized groups and persons living with disability.There were reports of cattle raiding and abduction of children by Murle, revenge killings and conflicts between individuals which affected safety.During flee, at least 40% of children were separated from their families.For instance in Maiwuit county,during the inter clan fighting, at least 40% of children were separated from their parents exposing them to risks of exploitation and recruitment as child soldiers.During the crisis, women and girls bore the brunt of the conflict as some of them were raped.
Further, conflict between Gajaak and Gajiok clans in refugee camps in Ethiopia in December 2018 and January 2019 led to an increased number of returnees from Ethiopia to Upper Nile State increasing struggle for the existing resources which were by and large stretched leading to conflict. Land dispute was reported in Longechuk, Nasir and Maiwuit where individuals who had arrived earlier had settled in land that previously belonged to other people who had fled the county during the crisis.In Nasir, Maiwuit and Longechuk for instance, ADRA protection staff worked closely with the local authority to advocate for resolution of land related disputes between returning population and members of the host community.
Flooding which was experienced in these locations affected about 80% of the population living in Nasir, Ulang, Logechuk and Maiwuit from June 2019 in some cases displacing entire villages.The affected population lost NFIs and shelters and were forced to seek shelter in collective infrastructure such as schools interrupting learning.In all locations targeted by the inter agency assessments, more than 3 families comprising of 10-15 individuals were observed living in spaces meant for 4 to 5 persons. This not only compromised privacy but exposed women to risks of abuse.
The proposed project will therefore endeavor to continuously monitor by static presence the protection risks and threats that IDPs, Returnees and Host community are exposed to in the targeted locations with the aim of providing assistance to the most vulnerable individuals.The findings will be used to advocate for assistance through different stakeholders at community and cluster level.To ensure access to services, the project will utilize referral mechanisms and service mapping to link for services.The project staff will conduct awareness raising campaigns to sensitize the affected population on the existing services. The project will also seek to increase resilience of the affected population by facilitating community dialogues and youth conferences to build on the peaceful coexistence activities.The project will train Faith Based organisations, community leaders including women and traditional conflict resolution committees on peace building and conflict resolution.The proposed project will target 20,600 (Male=9,888 and Female=10,712) individuals by proportion of 50% IDPs, 30% Returnees and 20% host community. ADRA will use updated service mapping to avoid duplication of services.

1 - Needs

Safety and security. There was continued instability reported in Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang and Longechuk in 2019. During outreach activities, IDPs were found to be reluctant to return to their places of origin due to lack of a guarantee that the security incidents that forced them to seek safety and refuge elsewhere in the first place will not occur again.Occasional inter-clan fighting, revenge killings and cattle raiding that displaced communities in these counties compound the existing fears.The cantonment sites being established in various locations posed threats to civilians. Two women were reportedly raped in Turu, Maiwuit county by the soldiers in the cantonment site while one minor in Nasir was impregnated and abandoned by a solder in the cantonment in Nyetot. The culture of revenge continue to undermine safety in Upper Nile. Civilians who lose their family members during any attacks seek to revenge. The absence of organized governance structures, lack of personnel capacity occupying leadership structures and void of functional legal structures to enforce laws that create a conducive environment for the right holders to claim their rights have worsened the situation for IDPs, Returnees and host communities alike in Upper Nile. The proposed project will ensure the needs of persons of concerns and civilian community who are threatened regardless of their gender and age are prioritized and supported either with direct assistance or referrals to other service providers. IDPs, Returnees both from locations in South Sudan and those returning from neighboring countries and Host community members will be reached through protection monitoring visits and other various outreach activities.Meaningful Access: ADRA staff will identify and address gaps in basic needs of PSNs. Due to distance from established humanitarian facilities such as health facilities, schools, boreholes and distribution sites, some of the affected population especially the PSNs do not access services. Populations living in hard to reach locations are also excluded from humanitarian assistance. Other reason for lack of access is lack of information especially for the returnees. During implementation of protection motioning activities in 2019, ADRA found out that IDPs and returnees were being discriminated and excluded from accessing services like trainings and NFI distribution because they were considered outsiders or labelled ‘cowards’ who hadn’t stuck around and defended their community during the fighting.Protective environment: Apart from human rights violations and abuses such as physical violence witnessed during inter clan fighting, revenge killings, cattle raiding rape, loss of property, there are a number of factors that undermine the protective environment. Cultural norms and beliefs hamper human rights particularly victimizing women and girls. In different project locations, women and girls are brought up in a culture of silence and compliance. They are also unable to access justice due to the culture that does not allow them to speak freely. Rules are made by men who also hold the positions of influence within the community and make decisions even in the event of human rights abuses. This limits the opportunities of protection and redress.Presence of PSNs such as elderly, persons living with disability, women at risk, orphans and unaccompanied children with majority living in child headed families. A good number of young people in Nasir, Maiwuit Ulang and Longechuk are out of school and idle without meaningful economic activities. These youths are reported to turn to drugs as a way of coping with life threatening traumas and frustrations. Most of them result to theft of other people’s property, join armed groups and fighting threatening peaceful coexistence in the community. The proposed project will seek to engage the youth and the community in peace dialogues and campaigns to enhance better conflict resolution approaches

2 - Activities

Conduct weekly protection monitoring in consultation with affected population to monitor violations, discrimination, exclusion, coping mechanisms, and access to services. ADRA protection team will monitor the protection situation in the target areas in consultation with the affected populations and local structures through a field-tested methodology consisting of spot observations, key informant interviews, FGDs, and individual interviews with IDPs, returnees and members of host community in locations of implementation. Protection monitoring will focus on key protection concerns faced by the target population, including human rights violations and targeted violence, discrimination and problems related to access to services. Periodic monitoring reports will be produced to inform the program approach and strategies, and disseminated at specific forums without compromising confidentiality and the safety and security of target population. These reports will analyse barriers to accessing services, and will serve as tools for advocacy in improving the meaningful access to and the quality of service provision.Protection Case management and referrals for persons with specific needs (PSNs). Through the protection monitoring missions, regular home visits by the protection team will be conducted. ADRA will identify, document, and refer PSNs for appropriate services, both internally and externally, and/or will provide direct assistance. Protection reports containing case-specific (identifiable) data will only (and exclusively) be shared with other agencies providing direct services for a specific case and only when informed consent is received. All protection monitoring forms, including potentially identifiable data, will be stored in a safe manner and be accessible only by the protection monitoring team. ADRA will conduct protection mainstreaming training for different service providers to improve meaningful access to services to the most vulnerable and to ensure that these services are being provided in a way that protects the safety and dignity of service users. Protection officers will set up feedback and complaint mechanisms. Core relief items: ADRA staff in collaboration with community leaders, partners and the community members will identify the most vulnerable persons and target them for support with core relief items using the most appropriate mode of support. Regular market analysis will be conducted to inform the modality of assistance. Cash transfer modality will be most preferred especially for survivors of gender-based violence. ADRA will work in collaboration with other partners to refer PSNs for assistance while linking them to livelihood opportunities. Strengthening of community-based structures-ADRA will conduct training and capacity building activities for the community leaders on human rights law, community-based protection as well as conflict resolution to enable the community to mitigate protection risks and advocate for vulnerable individuals. Leaders will also develop community action plans and implement to mitigate protection concerns. The community action plans (e.g. dissemination information, community mobilization, advocacy initiatives) will detail how they seek to contribute to their community with support of the relevant service providers. Mass campaigns and sensitization: Information campaigns on human rights, access to services particularly the importance of timely access to health services for survivors of sexual assault will be conducted to addresses the critical gaps in information and knowledge on GBV responses. Activities will include community theater in which the staff will mount public system speakers on boats or vehicles; training for community members, partner staff, and mobilizing and sensitizing members on key knowledge gaps identified during monitoring visits.Peaceful coexistence campaigns, conferences and dialogues will be conducted through out the implementation period.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The rights of beneficiaries will be addressed using the acceptable Universal Declaration ofHuman Rights laws which South Sudan is signatory to and has enshrined in the constitution.The communities will be sensitized on their rights through mass awareness campaigns, celebrations of international days like Human rights day .During the celebrations of 16 Days of Activism,the affected population will be educated on their rights and where they can report violations accordingly.Any violations of beneficiary rights will be defended through using the laid down reporting procedures.Signing Code of conduct and protection of sexual exploitation and abuse by ADRA staff will be a commitment that the project staff will recognize the beneficiary rights and seek to preserve them. ADRA will also work in close collaboration with traditional justice systems and build their capacity to adjudicate through fair trial human rights violations that may be brought be reported to them

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The proposed project will employ local staff from the affected communities who have knowledge on the local context to ensure that during displacement, affected communities can be reached and supported.The project will also endeavor to work with the local authorities to ensure access to affected communities is enabled during displacement.The project will utilize early warning systems to inform possible displacements so that planning and mobilization of resources can be done to reach the displaced persons.Monitoring activities will include mapping locations where populations are displaced into to enable planning.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The proposed project is linked to government and community leadership systems. ADRA will work in close collaboration with the local leaders and community leaders like chiefs to ensure the community members are reached with services without discrimination. Through bilateral meetings, ADRA will engage the local leaders and community leaders to advocate for support of access to services and land for the IDPs and returnees.The community leaders will be trained on Human rights and conflict resolution to increase their capacity to respond to the legal needs of the affected population.Additionally, the project staff will involve the local and community leaders in joint activities such as mass awareness campaigns and during distribution to provide security for the beneficiaries as well share relevant information. Peaceful coexistence activities such as conferences dialogues and foot ball matches organised to promote peaceful coexistence will be organised and facilitated in collaboration with government and community systems in respective project locations. This project will seek to contribute to the government development goals. ADRA works closely with all stakeholders and actors relevant to its actions and operations in each targeted County. These include government ministries; where Government structures are functional, at National, State and County level, local authorities and other agencies. In addition, ADRA works with children, women and men including community groups such as IDPs/returnee groups, youth groups and women groups.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project is aligned to the International human rights laws(IHRL ) and international humanitarian laws.The implementation will also refer to the IDP guiding principles though not ratified but they are aligned to IHRL. The project will promote Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by creating and maintaining environment free from sexual exploitation and abuse and promote code of conduct. The Management of organization will ensure support and strengthening of the internal complaint, reporting and investigation mechanisms concerning misconduct of the staff to ensure zero tolerance to sexual exploitation of abuse of persons of concern.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

ADRA and other partners have been implementing a number of projects in Upper Nile since 2014 to date by static presence with bases in Ulang, Nasir and Maiwuit. ADRA also had previous base in Pagak and Longechuk and already in verge of re-establishing its base again by early 2020. Currently ADRA is accessing Pagak through Maiwut, and longechuk through sharing base with UNKEA. The protection projects has been working in collaboration with local authority to promote peaceful coexistence by involving communities in peace dialogues and conferences which has seen a reduction in cases of revenge killings in 2019.The leaders have taken initiatives to engage rival groups and communities to amicably resolve conflict and improve the protective environment.For instance after the fighting between the Luo Nuer and Luo Jikany in Ulang, ADRA facilitated training on peaceful coexistence for leaders who in turn held meetings with the leaders from Luo Nuer to advocate for peaceful coexistence.ADRA also trained faith Based organisations on conflict resolution and human rights.In turn they conducted campaigns to preach peace in different locations of program implementationThe ongoing peace talks and efforts to form a transition government and absence of fighting between government soldiers and the SPLA/IO has created perceived protective environment and encouraged return of refugees from countries of asylum.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The proposed project is about access to services and realization of human rights.From the inception, the project takes into consideration the participation and inclusion of community through involvement in assessments for them to be able to identify their needs.During implementation, the community leaders will be strengthened through capacity building training on human rights and will be empowered to claim rights and services from the stakeholders on behalf of their communities.Most of the activities of the project are geared towards building the capacities of the communities to realize their rights.The acquired skills will help the community when the program comes to an end.The project will work with community based protection networks who will be trained on vulnerability criteria to identify the most vulnerable persons and refer them for services.The networks will remain in the community and can continue to identify and refer the PSNs as well as care for the most vulnerable members of the community.Working closely with Faith based organisation is another sustainability approach the project intends to utilize. The churches will always be there and they can continue to offer training on Human rights, SGBV concepts and peace building to members of the community beyond the project life.
