Project Summary

The IRC will support vulnerable host communities improve their livelihoods status through integrated livelihoods support. The project will address the needs of the affected community by providing access to income generating opportunities in Hassakah, Der ez-Zor, Raqqa governorate. 1,270 livelihood households are protected and/or restored income generating through appropriate market skills/inputs/services including vocational training, apprenticeship, SME grants, and business start-up kits, as well 1,020 households with appropriate agriculture inputs (seeds, fertilizer, tractor ploughing hours) and farming tools needed to increase production both for domestic consumption and for commercial to increase household income.
The IRC will conduct a market assessment and engage with farmers to understand inputs needed, availability and market prices to best design appropriate input distribution modalities. In addition, livestock breeders will receive cash assistance for fodder to protect productive assets and improve food consumption at household and community levels.
In addition, 430 households will be engaged in gainful temporary employment through cash for work involving rehabilitation of prioritized community infrastructure. Cash for work activities are intended to provide households with much needed cash injections to meet their basic needs while rehabilitating community assets and infrastructure that are vital to sustaining and restoring the livelihoods of communities.
The IRC will support the agriculture community through skill-building via improving agriculture and veterinary service activities which play a crucial role in boosting agricultural productivity, increasing food security, improving rural livelihoods, and ensuring that 8,000 farmers and livestock breeders have access to improved technologies. Activities will include assistance in the creation of production groups and deliver information on new technologies and innovations available to agriculture manufacturers. Agriculture extension and veterinary services programs will cover a broad area including improved crop varieties, livestock management, improved water management, modern irrigation methods, and control of weeds, pests, and diseases through mobile veterinary team, training, visuals posters, and brochures, electronic media (WhatsApp app groups), learning by experimentation, field trips and exchanges, and FFS.
The IRC will mainstream gender equity and promote gender inclusion, allowing the most vulnerable and disadvantaged women, girls, boys, and men to have equal participation in the program and to realize benefits for their own wellbeing. A participatory beneficiary identification and selection including agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 50% female beneficiaries.
To facilitate women’s accessibility, and promote their participation in livelihoods activities, the IRC provides safe places at livelihoods centers managed by childminders, for children whose mothers attend the livelihoods activities.

4 - Needs

The water crisis in NES and associated climate shocks led to a 20% decrease in yield in the 2020-2021 harvest, as compared with 2019-2020. 90% of rainfed crops were not harvested, which led to a loss in income, assets, and main livelihoods for many households in NES. Additional stressors include the devaluation of the Syrian Pound (SYP) resulting high prices of agriculture inputs, low availability of high-quality seeds in NES, and the COVID-19 pandemic which accelerated the economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in a context where 50 percent of the work-age population is now. Findings of the market assessment conducted by the IRC in Hassakah and Al Raqaa in September 2021 depicted a myriad of shortages. These include a shortage of skilled workers, including women, in local markets due to a lack of available training opportunities, lack of experience in modern agriculture methods and techniques, a lack of capital to launch food processing businesses, and a lack of sufficient capital to start-up and continuation of enterprise.

5 - Implementation of activities

The IRC will employ its bundled services approach, integrating provision of capital and inputs with market-relevant and climate-smart vocational skills training.Cash transfers designated for livestock and agricultural inputs and agro-vocational training in climate-smart agricultural and livestock production. Training will provide information about input quality and will focus on breeding and fodder production for livestock, seed saving, quality testing, and bio-fertilizer production for agricultural value chains. Beneficiaries will access vocational training, SME grants, and business start-up kits, gaining practical and transferable skills to increase their resilience to shocks. With training and skill-building, farmers will gain access to improved technologies, experience in boosting agricultural productivity, increasing food security, and thus will be better prepared for the next growing season even given unpredictable weather and shifting conflict-driven context. CfW opportunities will provide beneficiaries with short-term opportunities to meet basic needs, on projects based on community needs such as community infrastructure rehabilitation, irrigation canal maintenance, road maintenance, and building climate resilience.The IRC will provide safe access to capital and employment by establishing VSLA groups to provide savings, loan facilities and a form of self-insurance to members in a community that do not have access to formal financial services.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Project staff and volunteers including the cash and livelihoods team, with support from the protection mainstreaming officer, will sensitize on the "Do no harm" principles and PSEA. By working with community leaders, community representatives, and elders, IRC undertakes through sensitization and awareness-raising of stakeholders on the rights and responsibilities of all project parties. Beneficiaries are made aware of their entitlements, freedom of choice, and how support is provided. Stakeholders and project staff are sensitized to exercise and abide by the core humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, humanity, and independence while providing and accessing support.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The IRC will exercise program integration with the protection sector to provide protection assistance to survivors and ensure referral pathways including during beneficiary selection through referrals, data triangulation and sharing, reporting and follow-up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support. The project will be inclusive in targeting the most vulnerable groups. During project launch, IRC will ensure the representation of marginalized groups in reviewing the selection criteria. At distribution points, IRC will designate lines and provide chairs especially for PwD.The IRC will conduct FGDs to increase beneficiaries’ awareness of IRC Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms. Embedded will be gender-sensitive feedback and complaint committees. During sessions and throughout implementation, beneficiaries will be provided with the platform to provide feedback on services.IRC will use ONA mobile application or CommCare/Kobo for registration. Beneficiaries’ data will be stored and saved electronically in a centralized database by the IRC M&E unit. IRC will ensure non-disclosure and protection of beneficiaries’ personal data. During the project launch, the IRC will engage the authorities and key local stakeholders to explain project services and expectations of community leaders and beneficiaries. Also, beneficiary selection criteria will be shared and agreed upon registration, mitigating the risk of impartiality or inequality during selection.
