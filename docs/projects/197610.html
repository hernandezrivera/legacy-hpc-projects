Project Summary

The general nutrition situation in Northwest Syria deteriorated severely in 2021, as stated in the needs section. Poverty, low socioeconomic situation, unsafe or unhealthy living conditions, inadequate maternity, infant, and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition have all contributed to this deterioration. A significant continuous contributing factor is a mass internal displacement that occurred at the end of 2019 and early 2020. A further deterioration of the nutritional situation is expected as a result of the economic slump and depreciation of the Syrian Pounds, as well as the impact of COVID-19 in NWS.
BAHAR will execute a bundle of activities in response to the identified needs, that will assist 35,000 individuals.
1. Screening for malnutrition, identifying and referral if any through rapid response teams 11 RRTs of 2 CHW & 1 nutritionist
2. Provide one-on-one counseling on WHO-recommended newborn and young child feeding and maternal nutrition practices in facilities and communities, either through the outreach team (RRTs) or the PHCs, Hospital, BEmONC, or CEmOnc based CHWs/Nutritionists
3. Providing children between the ages of 6 to 59 months with specialized nutritious food (LNS).
4. Early detection and referral of cases of Acute Malnutrition
5. Providing medical/nutritional treatment to all cases of severe acute malnutrition with complications (identified and/or referred) in BAHAR's stabilization center in Afrin city.
The stabilization center has a total capacity of 35 beds (30+5ICU)in addition to incubators,& Phototherapy
the proposed project will target the flowing subdistricts Afrin, bulbul, Jandairis, Raju, Sharan, and with special focus on M'abatli and Sheikh Alhadid this is going to be achieved through the deployment of RRT teams that will conduct a general screening to detect malnutrition cases of children U-5 and PLW/Gs within targeted communities, referring the identified cares either to OTPs or BAHAR SC for further investigation and management the case management will include medical supervision and treatment for SAM cases with medical complications In addition to that RRTs will conduct regular follow up visits to the previously identified, referred ,and treated cases up to 12 months with special and focused intervention

3 - Needs

According to the SMARTs survey reports published in October 2022;The acute malnutrition situation between the WRA and PLW showed some increases compared to 2021 results, the MAM PLW rate increased from 12.4% in 2021 to 12.9% in 2022, and for the anemia, the prevalence of anemia among the women had decreased from 53.85 in 2021 to 52.63 in 2022. The increasing SAM rate either by WHZ or by MUAC can be attributed to multifactorial elements, including high and recurrent episodes of diarrhea, disrupted health systems, and a need to tackle the underlying factors of undernutrition while further scaling MAM prevention and treatment services. The overall nutrition situation continued to be the same as the last survey conducted in 2021, but compared with previous surveys, we could see a clear increase in malnutrition rates. Nutrition-specific and sensitive interventions geared towards preventing and treating all forms of malnutrition among women of reproductive age should be scaled up in Northwest Syria. The anemia level between children showed some improvements 36.18% compared to last year's 46%, but still high and considered a serious problem in NWS. According to the SMARTs survey reports published in October 2022; The GAM prevalence did not show an improvement from previously conducted surveys, while the SAM rate increased. The stunting rate is considerably high among children -22.3% which is considered as serious in the global nutrition severity scale.

4 - Implementation of activities

BAHAR will provide the LNS product, nutributter, to children 6-59 months, in highly vulnerable and IDP families living in informal settlements and IDP camps in North Aleppo. Alongside these supplements women of reproductive age and caregivers of children, 6-59 months will be trained on the family MUAC approach and provided with a MUAC tape to take home as well as with information on the nutrition treatment and prevention services available in their local area, if they need to access them. This aims to increase early self-identification of acute malnutrition within the households and increase health-seeking behaviors. Nutrition counseling will be provided to the same populations on the prevention and treatment of malnutrition and maternal-infant and young child nutrition. Coordination and referrals will be carried out between all health & nutrition actors in the area through the Nutrition Cluster and bilaterally. Coordination and referrals will also take place between the food security actors in the area to ensure that the people most vulnerable to malnutrition receive both food assistance and nutrition services.Moreover, BAHAR will provide a full treatment package including admission, 24/7 monitoring, treatment of all identified cases of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications within Bahar's stabilization center in Afrin city.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Beneficiaries' exploitation of violence or abuse during distribution rounds.Mitigation: PSEA orientation will be conducted to team leaders and they will train their teams. Also, the accountability field team will monitor the services provided. Risk: GBV and CP’ discovered cases during the screening.Mitigation: Detection and referral orientation from the field coordinator to the team leader to observe the visual signs and refer the victims to the case management teams according to service mapping in the region. Risk: Gender imbalance among project staff and higher risk of GBVMitigation: the nutritionists, rapid response members, and other project team members will be both male and female, however, Bahar will hire a higher proportion of female staff given that the target population is PLW and children under 5.Risk: Access restrictions to the area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines that prevent the humanitarian actors from providing timely emergency relief to the affected population. Mitigation: Constantly monitoring the security situation on the ground and developing contingency planning, as well as exploring different implementation modalities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying the related procedures. Bahar ensures that the field team attends PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal point's responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of the online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complains pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor and report if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence. Bahar is committed to making sure that every child and adult survivor of SEA and SH receives quality, survivor-centered assistance, and support tailored to their specific needs, such as medical care, psychosocial support, legal assistance, and reintegration assistance, as well as to providing comprehensive support and assistance to employees who have been subjected to sexual harassment, including confidential ethical advice and guidance, as well as protection from from retaliation.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow BAHAR to implement. Risk: Continued military offensives present the risk of death or injury to Bahar staff, damage to or destruction of Bahar premises or assets; reduced access to program areas, and border closure. Mitigation: Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and SOPs are in place and an experienced security team. Assumption: Syria Cross-Border Security Council Resolution will not be renewedRisk: Failure to renew the cross-border agreement would exacerbate an already dire humanitarian situation in northwest Syria. Border closure disrupts the supply chain.Mitigation: Bahar keeps stores stocks sufficient to meet requirements for two months and continues to explore the possibility of local procurement, and other cross-border available mechanism Assumption: Cholera/COVID-19 will continue to be a risk for 2023. Risk: Urge or resurge of Cholera/COVID-19 in northwest SyriaMitigation: Regarding Cholera Bahar will continue following a multisectoral approach to participate in minimizing cholera impact thru, outreach team training, and incorporating cholera awareness messages into nutrition key messaging for HH, caregivers, and communities. However, Bahar will aim to deliver assistance, at the HH levels with increased cholera mitigation measures being utilized to protect staff and beneficiaries.
