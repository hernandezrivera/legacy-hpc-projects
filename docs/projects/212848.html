Project Summary

In 2024, The organization will continue its dedicated efforts to control leishmaniasis amongst vulnerable populations in Northeast Syria through a refined strategy and targeted interventions. Embracing a new supervisory role, the organization will oversee the technical aspects of case management in the health sector, ensuring the quality assurance of partner organizations' implementation activities.
Furthermore, through Information, Education, and Communication/Behavioral Change Campaign (IEC/BCC) activities the organization improves the communities’ awareness about leishmaniasis detection, control, prevention, and health seeking behavior.
Our field Medical Officers (MOs) will play a pivotal role by visiting a total of 88 Health Facilities (HFs) in the region, this encompasses 84 PHCs, 4 Hospitals. Under this strategy, the health staff from our partner organizations will be responsible for the direct case management of Leishmania. The organization will not only provide the essential medications and medical consumables to these HFs but will also continue to spearhead the collection and analysis of leishmaniasis data using a unified Kobo-Link system. This data will be instrumental in updating the Health Technical Working Group in NES about the trends and developments in leishmaniasis cases. This strategic shift in 2024 reflects our commitment to enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of leishmaniasis control in Northern Syria, adapting to the evolving needs of the region and ensuring the highest standards of care and support for vulnerable populations in addition to enhance localisation.
Large-scale IEC/BCC campaigns ensure that communities have improved knowledge about leishmaniasis and disseminate key messages about leishmaniasis detection, control and prevention. The organization applies a comprehensive IEC/BCC approach through social media and various printed materials, such as leaflets and brochures, that are displayed at supported HFs and main areas of targeted communities. As of late, street signs are also being erected, warning about leishmaniasis. The organization also approaches Key Community Representatives as well as Community Health Workers (CHWs) employed by partner organizations and trains them in delivering leishmaniasis messages in order to improve the dissemination of leishmaniasis key messages within their respective communities. Both IEC/BCC staff and CHWs refer suspected leishmaniasis cases to supported HFs.
