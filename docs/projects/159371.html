Project Summary

Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. Many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced. The Gu cereal harvest failed in most regions, leaving most poor agropastoral and riverine households unable to meet their minimum food needs given significantly below-average food stocks and income from seasonal agricultural employment. In pastoral areas, the impact of the drought on livestock led to lack of access to milk, and many poor households have accumulated large debts while struggling to feed their families and rescue their remaining livestock. Current levels of humanitarian assistance must be sustained in order to prevent anticipated food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2 or higher). Thus, AADSOM would like to support the crisis affected community households and IDPs in Bari, Hiran and Bakool regions of Somalia to reduce people’s needs, risks and vulnerabilities and increase in their resilience, to be achieved over a period of 3-5 years. AADSOM will strengthen coherence and complementarity between humanitarian and development efforts, and ultimately aim to reduce needs and vulnerabilities to a point where humanitarian action is no longer required. In order to achieve that objective, AADSOM will carry out the following activities:
(1) AADSOM will increase immediate access to food and improve nutrition through distribution of food voucher to 500 crisis affected IDPs and host communities’ HHs in Bari, Hiran and Bakool regions, benefiting around 3,000 women, men, boys and girls for a period of three months.
As an exit strategy, AADSOM will:
(1) Support 500 crisis affected farmers with land preparation and farming input; benefiting around 3,000 men, women, children including people with disabilities and minorities.
(2) Distribute 5 boats and fishing gear to 50 fisher folks in Bari region (each folk consist 10 fishermen), which are representing 500 HHs who have lost their fishing assets during the cyclone. Benefiting around 3,000 men, women, girls and boys. Each 10-fisher folks will be regrouped and will be given fishing gear and one boat.;
(3) Distributing 2,500 goats to 500 pastoral HHs who have lost their animals during the recurrent crisis (5 heads of goat/HH0; benefiting around 3,000 men, women and children.
(4) Introducing and supplying the basic and advanced necessary beekeeping equipment to 200 low-income female-headed HHs, who have lost their livelihood assets and depend on low income casual works, which sometimes they are at risk of GBV and abuse including deny of their wages, to give a chance to improve their living standards by producing more hygienically clean honey.
To keep the sustainability of the project and increase the production of the beneficiaries, AADSOM will train selected beneficiaries from the different livelihoods on the best technologies and methodologies to increase their livelihoods’ production. During the project implementation, women, men, children, disables and IDPs will be heard their voices and consulted.

The community will also Identify the type and quantity of each food item they prefer but should enable HHs to meet their daily caloric needs in accordance to FS and Nutrition clusters. Act. 2: Provide Food Vouchers to improve the nutrition and food security to 500 IDP & Host Community HHs in Bari, Hiran and Bakool regions for 3 months, benefiting around 3,000 men, women, girls and boys. The farm input composed farm tools, fertilizers and assorted seeds. Act.2: Distribute restocking 2,500 goats to 500 pastoral HHs who lost their livelihood assets during the recurrent crisis (Each household will receive 5 goats); representing 3,000 men, women, boys and girls, who lost their animals. Act.3: Distribute 5 boats and fishing gear to 50 fisher folks in Bari region (each folk consist 10 fishermen), which are representing 500 HHs who have lost their fishing assets during the cyclone. Benefiting around 3,000 men, women, girls and boys. Each 10-fisher folks will be regrouped and will be given fishing gear and one boat.Act. 4: Introduce and supply the basic and advanced necessary beekeeping equipment to 300 low-income women in the target locations, representing 200 HHs (composing 1,200 men, women, boys and girls) who have lost their livelihood assets and depend on low income casual works, who are at risk of GBV and abuse including deny of their wages, to give a chance to improve their living standard by producing more hygienically clean honey.Act.5: Train 50 farmers (25 men & 25 women), 50 pastorals (25 men & 25 women), 100 fishermen and 200 beekeeping women for the best methodologies & technologies to their respected livelihood activities. During the project implementation, AADSOM will ensure: to minimize the distance of walking and to avoid women/girls to fall risk of GBV, intimidation or extortion while taking their rations home; the distribution of the food voucher will take in the daylight time giving beneficiaries sufficient time to go home before night; no queuing during food voucher distribution. In worst scenario, if queuing occurs, men and women will be separated. Always female staff will be present at food vendor place so that women/girls can access without any risk; to distribute the agricultural input in very safe place, away from military camp; and not left vulnerable persons such as IDPs, minorities, women and people in disabilities; men and women to participate equally in decision making and in the selection of the beneficiaries and finally AADSOM will ensure that women, disabilities and minorities' voices should be heard.
