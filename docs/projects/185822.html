Project Summary

The project will contribute to the improvement of the public health situation in the Rohingya camps and host community by addressing gaps in the access to quality service and the awareness on non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and gender-based violence (GBV). The intervention will entail an extensive health education and awareness programme targeting women and men at risk of NCDs, ensuring that appropriate care and support is provided by their families and clinics. It will enhance community support mechanisms, linking health, protection or social services. Selected existing community health posts and clinics will be supported to assist populations at risk to adopt conducive behaviours for primary and secondary prevention of NCDs, including Covid-19.
Furthermore, the project will acknowledge the role of primary health care facilities for reaching GBV survivors. The intervention will raise awareness and address shortcomings at selected health posts in the provision of professional, survivor-centered GBV services, to ensure enforcement of their mandate on the Minimum Package of Essential Services (MISP) as per binding policies of the Health sector and GBV sub sector. The project will also strengthen professional medical skills, basic MHPSS and communication to ensure standardized service quality.
MdM will establish 4 community resource centers and provide technical support to the IPs who oversee operations. The CrCs, close to heath facilities, will enhance community-based support mechanisms through GBV outreach and critical health issues including NCDs. The CrCs will increase GBV prevention and holistic assistance to survivors, and reinforce comprehensive referral pathways.
Training on Gender, GBV principles and referrals for all staff and outreach including what is conducted by partner organizations will ensure engagement of staffing and communities in support of GBV survivors. The network and referral coordination mechanism will reinforce a comprehensive GBV response by linking community-based resources, outreach and sensitization, and existing community centres with the available medical facilities of proximity in camps and host communities.

1 - Identified Needs

As in the global trend, NCDs have been becoming the major disease burden with high numbers of morbidity and mortality. In Bangladesh, NCDs are estimated to account for 67% of all deaths in 2016 (WHO). Although there is no comprehensive relevant research or data in the Rohingya camps, it is assumed that a high number of people are at risk or suffering from NCDs, though not being well identified/cared or treated. Recognition and knowledge of NCDs among people is low: they do not know about co-relation between their health condition and which NCD(s) the condition is from or that between their diet/lifestyle and NCDs. Even for people diagnosed and being treated, proper medication on prescriptions is not followed. On the other hand, caretakers do not know appropriate ways to support their family members at risk/suffering from NCDs.Meanwhile, in this situation of high vulnerability, Rohingya refugee women and girls in Cox’s Bazar face several risks of GBV, including rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced and child marriage, human trafficking, denial of access to resources, opportunities and services, and psychological or emotional abuse. Women and girls represent 97% of the survivors of reported GBV incidents today in the camps, with 81% of incidents perpetrated by intimate partners. It must be noted that GBV remain widely under-reported: cases recorded by the Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS), an inter-agency initiative put in place in 2018 only represent the tip of the iceberg. Data on non-female GBV survivors in the camps and host communities is scarce. The GBVIMS recorded in 2019 3% of male GBV survivors. Adolescent boys and young men, boys with disabilities (especially intellectual disabilities), persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, or sex characteristics (SOGIESC) are exposed and those involved in informal work and child labour are among at-risk groups of GBV (Women’s Refugee Commission, 2018).
