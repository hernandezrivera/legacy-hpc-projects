Project Summary

NRC ICLA will be implementing activities across 16 camps and 8 unions ensuring protection and gender mainstreaming. ICLA will work with the Camp-in Charge’s, UNHCR and other actors in camps to secure identity and documentation of Rohingya refugees through existing registration and documentation guidelines. NRC will also work with Ukhiya and Teknaf government authorities e.g. UNO office, UP office to host communities. The project designed to positively increase access to the documents required for accessing essential services and to the records of vital events, which can also be used as a proof of facts (e.g. age and identity) in the case of return or resettlement. The project also aims at mitigating and addressing tensions between the Rohingya and local communities generated by the HLP disputes.
To fulfil the Accountability to the Affected population (AAP) principles, targeted objectives will be achieved through the provision of information, counselling and assistance based on individual needs, to enable target beneficiaries to overcome legal, procedural, and documentation challenges, including the referral to specialized case management actors as it may be required. Improvement of the relationships between refugees and host communities, and within the Rohingya community itself, will be mainly achieved through the building of capacity of actors within the recognized dispute resolution structures to identify and address (or refer to the NRC) the HLP disputes. Key staff, volunteers, and other stakeholders, including target communities whenever possible, will be involved to identify existing and emerging threats to safety, dignity and rights faced by different sex and age groups among the refugee and the host populations. Special attention will be paid to threats and vulnerabilities generated or exacerbated by COVID-19 and the associated containment measures. This information will be analyzed and used for evidence-based advocacy and will also be shared externally with other protection actors and stakeholders. ICLA field teams will continue the deployment of ‘mobile legal clinics’ – portable canopy tents, equipped with folding furniture and some basic equipment in the targeted 16 camps. NRC will also maintain “help desks” at the camp 2E, 8W, 10, 17, 21 and 22 CiC offices, as well as ICLA hubs at the camps 4, 21, 25 and 26, based in the premises rented from local community residents (meeting the due diligence requirements), accessible by both refugees and host communities.
Four Protection Mainstreaming principles – a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment will be promoted throughout the project implementation; while the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (hotline for complaints and feedback) will be constantly upgraded in line with the NRC CFM Policy and the NRC CFM Handbook. NRC has also engaged the PSEA Network to train camp volunteers during inductions, and NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff. NRC ICLA will be an active participant within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone responses and in addressing the challenges brought on by COVID-19. ICLA team will coordinate with PERU teams in camps during any emergency to support on the registration, documentation and other related services. NRC staff will continue working closely with the CiC office, site management, and protection focal points so that services are be tailored to reflect camp needs, in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms.

1 - Identified Needs

The proposed response is informed by an analysis of needs and gaps identified in the J-MSNA, 2022 Guidance Note (PWG, ATWG, ADWG, GDPWG, HLP TF), 2021 JRP, ACAPS Thematic Reports, CDR needs assessment conducted by the NRC Bangladesh in June-July 2021, as well as NRC`s ICLA Programme`s field experience. Overall findings on identity documentation and HLP in the Refugee camps and host communities: both refugee and host communities, women in particular, are not aware of the application procedures for identity documents and for the registration of vital events, and the potential benefits of these documents; vital events registration procedure is inconsistent, varies from camp to camp, is burdensome, not free from abuse of authority, and requires Bangla language speaking and writing skills; lack of literacy and access to the internet remain barriers for many people in host community to registering births, especially in rural areas and amongst adolescent mothers; roughly one in ten refugee households is engaged in tenancy relationships with the host community landlords and face rent extortion and threats of forced eviction; there is an increase in requests for relocation due to safety security concerns in and around the camps and within the camp consolidation initiatives; there is a lack of specific information about how dispute resolution affects women’s HLP rights; there is a lack of collaborative dispute resolution skills and information that can help resolve the ongoing HLP disputes, especially for disputes that affect refugees; there is a lack of coordination between the different dispute resolution structures that have emerged in host communities and refugee camps. Slowdown in registration activities during the lockdown created a backlog of cases to update. Continuous registration process is important because it is ensuring that the refugees can record and document their status and have access to assistance and services. Major gaps remain in the civil registration and documentation of births and deaths in the camps. Refugees’ unrecognized legal status renders them unable to access civil registration and hinders their access to justice, putting them at risk of unlawful detention and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation and abuse. COVID-19 situation exacerbated specific risks for refugee women, girls, and persons with specific needs (PSN) along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information. Meaningful participation of women and female youth volunteers was challenging given their limited access to phones, and the corresponding shift to remote modalities of work. To address the Legal Identity and HLP needs of the affected communities according to the sex and age disaggregation, gender and disability marker, ICLA targeted the individuals with wide range of criteria. These includes the individuals of Adult Females (18-59 years), Adult Males (18-59 years), and Adult Females (above 59 years), Adult Males (above 59 years), Unknown Sex and Age and Differently abled persons.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1 : Information sessions: awareness raising for refugees and host community explaining the importance of registering vital events (birth, death, marriage, divorce), and respective application procedures (for refugees, this will also include the procedures of obtaining / relacing “Smart”, Family Attestation and WFP cards); COVID-19-related health messages (approved by WHO, the health and other relevant sector working groups); available health, protection, DRR and other essential services; legal consequences of child marriage; antitrafficking messages developed by the ATWG; ways of resolving disagreements on rent issues, women HLP rights (inheritance, dower, maintenance). Should there be any mass returns to Myanmar, "Know Before You Go" information materials will be developed jointly by Bangladesh and Myanmar ICLA and Protection teams and distributed at the refugee camps. The materials will include the information on services available at the places of return, protection messages, NRC and other relevant hotlines numbers, amounts of required payments (both official and unofficial), other potentially relevant information. Activity 2 : Provision of counselling: one-to-one advice to a beneficiary or beneficiary group (individual beneficiaries who are in a similar situation and share a specific issue) on what is the best possible course of action to resolve an issues related to all aspects of identity documentation, documents required for accessing such essential services as food, health, shelter, education, registration of vital events, and HLP rights including tenancy rights. NRC counselling sessions are designed to enables beneficiaries in both the refugee and host communities to claim and exercise their rights by themselves, and to pass obtained knowledge onto family and community members. Counselling is a step beyond the provision of general information on rights because it entails a degree of analysis of the problems faced by the specific beneficiary or beneficiary group. It may require more than one interaction with the beneficiary or beneficiary group, and it may entail making contact with institutions or third parties. Counselling services will be available both in-person (during field visits and in ICLA hubs) and through the hotline. Activity 3 : Provision of legal assistance: assistance with reviewing or filling out applications for birth, death, marriage, divorce, etc., accompaniment to administrative bodies like the Camp administration, or UNHCR and WFP registration sites, assistance with the registration for COVID-19 vaccination (host communities), HLP case management, including the drafting and facilitation of signing of informal lease agreements and verification of the ownership and other rights over the land or property. NRC will be conducting an HLP due diligence for the large/important infrastructure projects, and/or capacity building in the HLP due diligence for actors implementing smaller projects or projects requiring frequent HLP due diligence activities. Activity 4 : Trainings in Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) techniques for local authorities, humanitarian staff and community representatives: NRC will scale up the provision of trainings in the CDR (basic and ToT), each of which has a module on women`s HLP rights. Trainings will be mainly provided to the camps administration staff, mahjis, imams, local community leaders, local authorities mandated to address HLP issues, as well NGOs involved in the mediation of disputes at the camps (recognized dispute resolution structures). NRC will be doing the capacity building in the HLP due diligence for actors implementing infrastructure projects, especially projects requiring frequent HLP due diligence activities (HLP due diligence for the one-time and large/important infrastructure projects can be conducted by the NRC staff upon request).
