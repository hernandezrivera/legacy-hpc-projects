Project Summary

Ending violence against women and girls, children and child marriage is a priority for Street Child in Mozambique and its local partners. There is a pressing need to raise awareness in communities about the child marriage and sexual, physical, and mental violence associated with it. Through the proposed project, child protection systems at the community and regional level will be supported to provide an effective response. A main area of support is the multi-sectoral collaboration between government and communities to prevent and address violence against children, particularly child marriage.
The targets are as following:
Children: 8054 (80% female) in the target districts receive i) case management services, with 2900 girls and 600 boys, ii) awareness raising and VAC workshops services through games and sport events at schools with 2700 girls and 800 boys, iii) referral follow up for 1000 children (800 girls and 200 boys) whose case being managed by the government institutions, and iv) through capacity building for CBCPC children members for 54 children (40 girls and 14 boys)
Government officials: 930 (75% female), in the target districts i) attend professional workshops on VAC and CM (with 720 officials, out of them 540 women and 180 men), ii) 60 officials (45 women and 15 men) advocacy /awareness raising meetings and capacity building for SDSMAS for case management, and iii) 150 officials (112 women and 38 men) will be supported on case management and get on the job training by the project case workers,
Adults: 3810 (75% female) receive i) VAC and CM prevention training through the project engagement with CBCPCs for 270 CBCM members, ii) 540 teacher and school staff personnel, and capacity building for government instructions on case management, and iii) 3000 adults (2250 women and 750 men) through social awareness raising events in the communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

According to global UNFPA data, child marriage is often followed by pregnancy, even when a girl is not yet physically or mentally ready. Mozambique is no exception. According to the 2017 Population Census, with 56% of 17-year old married girls having children. In developing countries, complications from pregnancy and childbirth are the leading cause of death among adolescent girls aged 15 to 19. Furthermore, it is known that marrying before the age of 18 increases the chance of intimate partner violence by twenty-two per cent, put girls at the risk of sexual exploitation, and health complications, besides mental and physical difficulties. Several factors like the COVID-19 pandemic, armed conflicts in the north of the country and natural disasters hava sharpened pre-existing inequalities between men and women and exacerbated some of the main drivers of child marriage, such as limited access to education, early pregnancies and poverty. To restate the urgency of the matter for Mozambique, The Minister of Gender, Children and Social Action, has mentioned combating child marriage as one of the priorities of the government: “the emancipation of women is a priority of Mozambique’s Government, which prioritizes actions aimed at empowering women, with progress in the political, economic, social and cultural spheres, in the Human Development Pillar of its Five-Year Program 2020-2024. Therefore, in light of the Constitution of the Republic of Mozambique, the Gender Policy and its Implementation Strategy, the Family Laws, and the Law to Prevent and Combat Premature Unions, among other guiding instruments, we have been achieving remarkable progress in the promotion of rights and in the eradication of all forms of gender-based violence, specifically against women and girls.”In the context where many displacements and movements are a reality, GBV risks, including child marriage, are expected to increase. GBV risks exist at temporary accommodation centers and at household level given infrastructure-related gaps, which, coupled with women and girls’ pre-existing vulnerabilities such as lack of decision-making power, exposure to polygamy and violent behavior, and lack of access to birth registrations and livelihood opportunities carry the risk of further harming women and girls and negatively impacting their safety and wellbeing [GBVAoR, safety audit, 2022].

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The proposed project addresses the drivers of child marriage by adopting a gender-transformative approach to ensure that adolescent, marginalized girls become active decision-makers, while protecting them from further harm. To respond and influence the target communities and the government to act to reduce the risk of escalation of child marriage in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, address the increased risk and exposure of internally displaced women and girls to gender-based violence and sexual exploitation, and continue to ensure quality essential services to this vulnerable population, Muleide and Street Child propose the following sets of activities:•Identification and referral of CM and VAC cases, 3500 cases with 90% girls, to appropriate services (e.g. SDSMAS, Police, PGR) at community level in selected districts in Nampula and Zambezia, with a strong focus on existing community structures such as community leaders, CCPC, permanents,) INAS. All the 36 (80% female) caseworkers will be trained at the beginning of the project in GBV case management, protection mainstreaming, and MHPSS including PFA. CBCPC members, 135 members with 75% female and 40% children, will be trained on a monthly basis and will be supported throughout the project to identify and refer cases of CM and VAC. •Monitoring of identified cases (using existing structures, such as reference group, GTCOV, PGB etc.) and provide technical assistance to SDSMAS to develop and utilize action plans for each case, the mapping and review of available services and develop and maintain an updated registry/database. The project staff will support and supervise the case management process if each case from identification to closure. The project staff will also follow up on each case up to 3 months for 1000 after the case closure to ensure the safety and wellbeing of the individuals. •Technical assistance, including frequent training and professional events, to relevant government departments to strengthen the coordination of legal and social welfare system and management of identified cases until the case is closed (identified protection needs addressed). In total 720 government officials, 75% female and 25% male, will be attending the professional workshops. Additional, the project proposes to ensure an adequate implementation of the child marriage law and strategy, such as National Gender Policy and its Implementation Strategy, National Action Plan for Children (PNAC II now in revision for implementation of PNAC III), Family Law nr29/2009, Law against Premature Unions nr19/2019 and Succession Law nr23/2019, at community and statutory level. This activity includes consultation sessions with women's rights organizations (CSOs and CBOs), community women’s and girls of the more marginalized communities to identify strengths and gaps in laws, policies and strategies (Children's Law Regulations) on forced unions, sexual abuse in schools and other legislation related to VSBG and SDSR/PN. •Awareness raising, information dissemination and prevention of child marriage and violence against children, at the community, regional, and national levels. This activity includes i) distribution of leaflets containing a summary of national laws, policies and regulations regarding premature unions and VAC, designed in two versions of low-literacy environment and child friendly; ii) Broadcasting of radio programmes on the law of premature unions and protection against child abuse in the districts where the project is implemented; iii) and awareness raising through social events in all 9 districts, with a focus on care givers; iv) training of 540 teachers and organizing education events in 54 schools on CM and VAC with child friendly material and modality.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Street Child recognizes four key risks which include (i) natural disaster and further displacement; (ii) community disapproval of project activities; (iii) safeguarding (iv) attacks. Street Child operates an active natural disasters Crisis Contingencies Strategy for contained, controlled and severe scenarios in Nampula, Niassa and Cabo Delgado. This includes conducting contextualized natural disaster preparedness training for staff. As child marriage and sexual reproductive health are still very sensitive topics in many target communities in the provinces, the proposed activities could be contentious, with the project facing initial resistance. As mitigation, early dialogues with be carried out with community influencers, leaders as well as prospective direct and indirect beneficiaries to ensure project rationale and activities are introduced and explained in a context-appropriate manner. Sufficient opportunities for feedback at every stage of the project through effective feedback mechanisms will also be provided. Street Child and Muleide operate a safeguarding system that is authorised as 100% compliant by international humanitarian standards. Safeguarding policies and procedures are cascaded to our partners and all project personnel and partners are provided with comprehensive training on Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) and protocols related to prevention, survivor support and investigations. Our Safeguarding Committee is responsible for reporting to the donor within 24 hours and 100% survivor safety using the UNICEF Risk Assessment Matrix as per Survival Support Standard Operating Procedure.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Street Child and Muleide will leverage extensive understanding and experience in addressing root causes for early, forced marriage amongst marginalized communities in Nampula, Cabo Delgado and Niassa and successfully mitigate against them. Street Child is a child protection and localization expert, bringing in years of experience in both. Street Child implements CP and CP integrated projects for UNICEF in many countries such as Nigeria, Cameroon, Afghanistan, Uganda, Somalia, etc. In its localization pillar, Street Child focuses on building the capacity of local NGOs to be able to absorb more funds, improve donor management and communication strategy competencies, and implement activities with high quality, among other areas. Muleide is a nationwide organization whose impact is also felt internationally. Its experience in human rights advocacy, children's rights and constant militancy against gender-based violence as well as child abuse, in the fight against practices harmful to society, makes this organization an emblem of harmony and protection and a symbol of change for which Mozambicans look and trust. Its reputation comes from more than 30 years of acting at the national level, in the relentless pursuit of better living conditions for its target audience, providing them with knowledge and skills for insertion and inclusion in society. Muleide is distinguished by the multiple and interdisciplinary principles that it advocates, focusing its actions not only on the target audience but also on training and sustainable practices in its projects, in the inclusion of different actors of change in search of the intended results and permanent behavioural changes, MULEIDE holds strong ties with the government as a recognition of implemented work along its institutions: Ministry of Gender, Woman and Social Action, Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Interior and Ministry of Health, drawn on 3 levels: national, provincial and district.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure participation of children and people with disabilities throughout and directly targeting between 5-15% of total beneficiaries. Capacity building and sensitization on disability inclusion and inclusive child safeguarding will be mandatory for all staff and focal points to ensure meaningful inclusion and participation. All data collected will be disaggregated by disability to monitor participation and access to services by children with disabilities. The project will collaborate with disability specific organizations and OPDs to support with identification of children with disabilities as well as for capacity development. The project will use both targeted and mainstreamed interventions to facilitate inclusion and participation of people with disabilities. In addition, communication, attitudinal, policy and physical accessibility barriers will be identified and addressed. All information produced and disseminated through the project will be in multiple accessible formats to ensure information reaches to people with different disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Sustainability of this intervention will come firstly from the capacity building of national actors, allowing them to form new partnerships and bringing them into the fore of the humanitarian response of Cabo Delgado. Frequent training on child rights and child protection for CBCPC members and local authorities, coupled with awareness raising and sensitization around MHPSS and grave violations against children, ensure increased community’s understanding of the CP issues and provide the space for them to be the main agent of change in the community.
