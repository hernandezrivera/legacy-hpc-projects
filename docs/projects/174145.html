Project Summary

The proposed response aims to provide cash transfers to support 5,000 individuals to purchase food and fuel from the functioning markets. This will not be conditional but “labelled” or in cash parlance, does not come with strict conditionalities that are expensive to monitor or unrealistic to achieve, but rather comes with activities such as nutrition session with lead mothers participating in the cash program on preparing nutritious food available in the local markets.
Humanitarian Cash Transfer; The transfer modality is unconditional cash which has proven to be effective and preferred in the target area. The value of the unconditional cash transfer is 3,770 NGN per individual for both food items and cooking fuel: 3,580 NGN is expected to be used for food items and 190 NGN to be used for cooking fuel. Those values are calculated using the food security sector and cash working group harmonized Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) valid for Jere LGA. The grant covers 70% of the MEB. It is expected that the remaining 30% of caloric need is either produced in-kind through small-scale gardening or earned through market driven income generating activities by beneficiaries.
Development activities; The development activities will be founded on community participatory activities such as transect walks and mapping to identify safe places for food production and eventual expansion to agricultural production as well as businesses which are needed by the market. Besides relevant training by LHI, the transferring start-up kits, seeds and tools will be distributed through participating vendors. ZOA takes advantage of the RedRose platform, a digital system using biometrics, which significantly reduces misappropriation and fraud and improves efficiency of the distribution process. Distributions will be spread over two weeks and at different times of the day to adhere to SEMA (State Emergency Management Agency) regulations on distributions and social distancing.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 and Food Security ChallengesNigeria as at 25th November 2020 reported 66,607 cases of COVID-19 from which 62,311 cases have been discharged and 1,169 deaths. Borno has reported 745 cases and 36 deaths. The Borno state government has adopted measures such as increased surveillance at airports, mandatory hand sanitizing stations at banks and ATMs and limited number of passengers in banks and public transportation to maximize social distancing. SEMA has issued guidelines on humanitarian distribution activities that mandate crowd management. Humanitarian work continues under these guidelines and ZOA Nigeria has adopted these guidelines in its operations. Emergency Food Security Assessment (EFSA) conducted by WFP together with government counterparts-National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), The National Program for Food Security (NPFS) under the Project Coordinating Unit (PCU) of the Federal Ministry for Agriculture and Rural Development (FMARD), the National Population Commission (NPoPC), and other Food Security Sector partners met in April 2019 to update the food security situation in BAY states. Jere LGA was among one of the LGAs visited during the assessment. The findings corroborated the results of the livelihoods assessment conducted by ZOA in 2020 Some of the reasons provided why households were food insecure include (i) Insecurity and communal conflict between farmers and herders; Insecurity continues to restrict livelihood activities including functionality of markets and optimal recovery of economic activities, which underscores pronounced levels and, in some cases, deterioration of food security in the most affected areas. (ii) Displacement of population from their home of origin; Displacement primarily limits access to basic livelihood opportunities such as land and skilled employment and this is often compounded by lack of skills and low level of literacy, which limits the capacity of IDPs to connect with local opportunities within areas of new habitation, consequently forcing them to engage in jobs that require lower level of skills such as land clearing and manual labor (iii) Dependency of IDPs on household communities; This continues to put pressure on limited and often times, overstretched resources in such communities as seen in the cases of Monguno, Maiduguri, and Jere where host community households were found to be more food insecure compared to IDP households in camps. (iv) Disability within households; The presence of members with long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments would potentially hamper economic productivity of such households, which explains the higher prevalence food insecurity (v) Lack of access to farmland; Households that do not have access to land to undertake agriculture are more food insecure compared to those with access, which implies that accessibility to land for farming remains a key determinant of food insecurity in the Northeast, particularly in LGAs like Jere where land is scarce. (vi) Shocks; A recent market price monitoring conducted by ZOA (September 2020), showed that some food items in the market have increased in prices such as vegetable oil, rice, beans and onions from the month of September to October with 3%, unlike in August when food items increased with 15%. This corroborates WFP Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket Monitoring (SMEB) done by ZOA. Between August and September(2020) the cost of the Food Basket, which is 70% of Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) for an average household size of 7, remained stable with no noticeable change between August and September (from 19,233 Naira to 19,245 Naira) in Maiduguri and Jere. Gender: The issues of women small holder-farmers cannot be separated from the border issues of gender equality and discrimination against women. Women are still voiceless in decision-making and policy issues. The project therefore intends to mobilize for greater participation of women.

2 - Activities

Humanitarian ActivitiesZOA proposes to stabilize and increase immediate food consumption of targeted host communities and IDP households and decrease negative coping mechanisms. The proposed response aims to provide cash transfers to support families to purchase food from the functioning markets. This will not be conditional but “labelled” or in cash parlance, does not come with strict conditionalities that are expensive to monitor or unrealistic to achieve, but rather comes with activities such as nutrition sessions with lead mothers participating in the cash program on preparing nutritious food available in the local markets.Early Recovery and Development Activities 1.) Sustainable food production within the safe confines of the community with the option to branch out to larger scale agriculture as safe zones expand.a) Participatory Rapid Appraisals. The communities have benefitted from ZOA’s cash programming and support from other humanitarian stakeholders. The shift to recovery programming means a shift in paradigms, mindsets, methodologies and ownership of the program. Involvement of the larger community beyond the traditional leadership shapes a recovery lens2.) Distribution of seeds and tools to 600 most vulnerable households.Households that have been determined to be able to practice micro-gardening securely within safe spaces such as open spaces within the confines of urban areas will be supported with seeds and tools for plots or appropriate methodology such as elevated plots. Due to high protection risks to which IDPs and host community members are exposed during farming activities outside the villages, the agricultural activities will be mainly conducted within the communities and on the edges of the villages/town. 3) Provision of extension services on 10 demo plots through extension volunteers.Community committees will be involved in identifying safe spaces for plots. The project will provide practical, tailor-made training on demo plots in all villages in best practice small-scale cultivation through community-based extension volunteers. 4.) Start-up non-agricultural businesses for women or women headed households through the provision of kits, enterprise training and linkages.a.) Selection and training of 300 women in small-scale non-agricultural businessesThe livelihoods assessment conducted by ZOA shows that female-headed households find it difficult to earn a sustainable income. Women are less involved in agricultural activities, but majority of the women established a form of small-scale business. Women are currently processing groundnuts (roasting, oil making), knitting fishing nets, producing local caps, are involved in tailoring/clothes production, or have started up petty trading activities to generate income. This activity of the project builds further on these initiativesb.) Distribution of 300 tailor-made start-up kits to women to kick-start non-agricultural income generation.All selected women will receive a demand driven start-up kit to jump-start or expand a small business. 5.) Setting up and support of Group Savings and Loans Association (GSLA) groupsTo ensure targeted households can financially sustain their agricultural and non-agricultural production, GSLA groups will be formed or existing ones strengthened and provided with essential inputs and training for financial inclusion. The GSLA is basically the much-regarded Village Savings and Loans Association (VSLA) labelled differently as the IDP context doesn’t allow to group strictly according to villages. Jere has over 90 similar groups organized by various actors including ZOA’s strategic partner LHI. Ninety one percent (91%) of the population has not received any formal education but 94% are GSLA members. The GSLA model is suitable within the local context because it is similar with the ”Adashe”, a form of indigenous women’s self-help and financial inclusion group.
