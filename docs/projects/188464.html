Project Summary

According to the draft HNO 2022 released by the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, 13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2021 - as needs are increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy.
Statistics indicate increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in the Northwest. Limited access to services and displacement, which led to the deterioration of the economy, the spread of poverty, the lack and loss of livelihoods, the destruction and loss of housing and property, the lack of natural resources, the continued spread of the epidemic, the exacerbation of protection needs and the increased dependence on negative coping mechanisms. Movement restrictions affect communities due to a variety of issues.
Older people, people with disabilities and chronic diseases face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, particularly in areas affected by conflict or multiple displacement. They face stigma, social isolation, and people face an increased risk of losing civil, land and property documents.
. The project aims to reduce the above-mentioned protection risks for the displaced and the host community in northwestern Syria in Jisr Al-Shughur in the Genoa region within the Al-Sham Center for Community Protection, through which the center will provide multiple protection services. .
1. Awareness campaigns through awareness teams to take into account general protection issues and work to mitigate them related to persons with disabilities, civil documents and property.
2- Implementation of C.M services that address the weaknesses of the elderly and people with disabilities. These services will be provided through referrals from the community protection committees and through the monitoring team, according to joint procedures between the authorities.
3. Provide Individual Protection Assistance (ECF) to cover protection gaps when the service is not available within the communities
5. Implementation of MHPSS activities within the AHF center for the elderly and persons with disabilities
6 - Legal attention to the beneficiaries by verifying the ownership of homes, land and civil documents to ensure the validity and credibility of the papers
7- Hold legal awareness sessions on CSD and HLP: Sessions will focus on CSD and HLPrights, the importance of civil documentation, steps and processes for obtaining civil documentation, recording life events and explaining the risks associated with obtaining civil documentation.
8 - Individual legal advice on issues related to the land and property sector. In addition to providing information about administrative procedures and associated risks, the legal team will obtain information about HLP rights in areas of return and provide legal advice on contracts and agreements.
For individual legal advice on legal issues and inquiries related to the financial and administrative services sector, land and property. The project will ensure the accountability of the affected population and stakeholders through coordination, participation, transparent accountability, complaints mechanism, feedback mechanism and worker competency monitoring.
The project is designed to enhance GQ to include women and girls as an integral part of the project

The project is designed to reduce protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, by signing the Code of Conduct for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and by creating complaints mechanisms.

4 - Needs

The needs assessment was conducted by Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation. The main objectives of the assessment are to provide baseline data for a comprehensive understanding of the existing land and property, civic documentation and the needs of persons with disabilities for the assessed communities, • identify the main existing reasons that prevent residents from accessing registration and issuance services for those documents, and to determine the impact of loss, damage to those Documents on the population. • The total population in 13 communities is 170,819 (29% of whom are displaced and 71% are host). • 2.2% of the population are individuals with disabilities (3,843). • 54% of the assessed communities have service centers that issue housing and land documents. • 46% of the communities assessed, residents do not have access to these service centers. • 92% of the population does not have access to their property. • 46.2% of children under 12 years old, it is very common that some do not have any documents. . • 54% of the assessed community have service centers for issuing housing, land, property and civil documents, while 46% of them are not issued. • Service centers are; Land registry office, notary public, civil registry, court and local council registry). • 54% of the assessed communities have access to these service centers while 46% do not.43% of local communities have persons with disabilities who cannot access the main required services

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risk of tension within or within the community due to service delivery sites or the types of services provided if one community is provided and no service is provided to a neighboring community. 1- Ensure comprehensive planning of services to mitigate risks and communicate with communities in a proactive and informed manner. 2. Ensure that neighboring communities are consulted regarding site services and remediation. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for people with visual/auditory impairments. 3. Linking the protection intervention to existing services within the target communities to reinforce the system's approach. 4. Forming community committees to enhance community protection mechanisms. Girls, boys, women, and men are at risk as a result of violence related to the civilian population/places targeted by armed groups; Failuretoconsider the safety and security of accessing/receiving services; Failure to take into account contextual information (such as aerial bombardment) and to target civilian infrastructure en route to, during, and directly after service provision. 1. Consider hostilities patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions to establish a fixed site or provide a mobile service. 2. Ensure that a safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (road and location) for people with mobility impairment (due to physical, visual/auditory defects, and psychological issues).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA.Mitigation:1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and societyAll project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on awareness activities, case management, : high Mitigation: - Maintaining social distance between beneficiaries and the project staff - Use methods of protection, including masks, gloves, and sterilizers -Avoiding meetings that contain many beneficiaries -Transfer project personnel in batches within transportation means to reduce congestion. Risk 2: CPactivities are suspended due to the conflict. probability: medium. Mitigation: in the event of attacks or invasion of the region, the service center will be moved to the JisrAl-Shughour area in coordination with the local authorities to ensure the safety of project personnel and beneficiaries and after acquiring the required approval Risk 3:Council local intervention and impeding the implementation of the project interference by armed groups to prevent the implementation of protection activities situation probability: Medium Mitigation: signing a memorandum of understanding with the local council to avoid obstacles, interference or external dictated by armed groups -Raising awareness of the community and primary caregivers to cooperate mitigating the risks of protection. - Establishing community protection networks that are made up of community leaders and influencers to support unhindered protection activities
