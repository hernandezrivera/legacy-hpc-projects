Project Summary

In Libya, the presence of explosive remnants of war (ERW) poses a significant protection concern to the civilian population. ERW continue to threaten physical security, restrict movement, impede livelihood opportunities, and prevent access to basic services. Following the escalation of violence in April 2019, active fighting largely concentrated on Tripoli means that the level of contamination continues to grow.
The HALO Trust has introduced mechanical clearance to Libya. Despite previous efforts by several humanitarian mine action organisations to remove unexploded ordnance and provide risk education to those living in areas impacted by ERW, the technical knowledge for large-scale clearance was missing. Drawing on its 30 years of experience in mechanical clearance, HALO has introduced the equipment and skills necessary to work systematically through potentially contaminated rubble and collapsed buildings. HALO is upgrading and armouring civil engineering machinery including excavators, front loaders, and dumper trucks. Operations currently focus on the coastal city of Sirte, the former Gaddafi stronghold and last bastion of ISIS in North Africa until their defeat in 2016. HALO’s first mechanical clearance teams in Sirte received full accreditation by the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC) in October 2019.
In addition to ERW contamination of urban areas, unsafe storage of weapons and ammunition in destroyed facilities remains a threat to civilians as well as to stabilisation efforts in Libya and the wider region. During the 2011 NATO campaign in support of the Arab uprisings, hundreds of Ammunition Storage Areas (ASAs) across Libya were targeted by NATO airstrikes. The airstrikes on ASAs threw huge stores of damaged munitions into urban areas in close proximity to critical infrastructure. Thrown out weapons and ammunitions are being used by both state and non-state actors to further their objectives throughout Libya and the region. The volatile security situation, continued presence of non-state armed groups, and a political power vacuum throughout most of Libya facilitate the movement of weapons and munitions throughout the region.
Building on HALO’s expertise in mechanical clearance in Sirte and ERW hazard mapping in Tripoli, HALO aims to expand its mechanical clearance capacity to launch a comprehensive clearance project in Misrata. While plans are underway to extend operations to several other locations, including Tripoli and Benghazi, HALO seeks to clear ASAs in Misrata first, as HALO already has an established presence there, including office facilities and good relations with the municipal authorities. The project does not only reduce the risk to the local communities living close to the storage facilities but also curbs the threat of illicit proliferation of weaponry in the region.
HALO is currently implementing an innovative ERW mapping and information management (IM) project in Tripoli. By using open source data collation and verification tools, HALO aims to create heat maps of conflict hotspots and to establish a live database logging information on ERW contamination. The goal is to provide IM capability to advise the LibMAC, UN and humanitarian organisations working in Libya on the extent and nature of ERW contamination, including information on types of ammunition and weapon systems and frequency of ERW accidents. This enables the LibMAC to coordinate and direct specialist clearance capacity to the most highly contaminated areas as soon as fighting ceases. Once fighting stops, HALO will be able to move into the zone to conduct on-the-ground survey and plan clearance operations. Teams that were trained in EOD and mechanical clearance in Misrata can then be redeployed to Tripoli.
HALO is also authorised to work in the East and seeks to commence operations in Benghazi in 2020.

1 - Needs

Libya has remained in a state of crisis since the escalation of violence on 4 April 2019, when Field Marshall Khalifa Haftar launched a military offensive on Tripoli. The need for humanitarian intervention remains high with record numbers of Libyans who are either displaced or have recently returned home. Latest IOM figures indicate that 301,407 people are displaced, in addition to almost 450,000 returnees. As a result of the current crisis, over 128,000 people have been displaced since April. Recent trends in IPD movements show that Libyans are increasingly moving back to places with high levels of ERW contamination, largely due to being forced out of Tripoli. For instance, among the 77,510 people who have returned to Sirte, a significant part is settling in damaged buildings in areas littered with potentially contaminated rubble. As returnees go wherever rent is the most affordable, this means that they might be forced to take clearance efforts into their own hands, thus elevating the risk of ERW accidents. According to HALO’s socio-economic baseline data gathered prior to the start of clearance in Sirte, 51 per cent of respondents feel unsafe living, walking and travelling around Sirte due to the presence of ERW. Moreover, 19 per cent of surveyed households have experienced an ERW accident, half of them resulting in the death of a family member. Humanitarian need is high in the eastern region of Cyrenaica as well. Benghazi remains largely uninhabitable and the level of ERW contamination is estimated to be ten times higher than in Sirte. The eastern town of Derna was under siege for two years and is strewn with unexploded ordnance. The large scale and systemmatic clearance of potentially contaminated rubble and damaged buildings allows affected people to return home and rebuild their lives.

2 - Activities

HALO deployed the first mechanical clearance teams to ever operate in Libya. Despite ongoing conflict, HALO drew upon its acceptance with the local community and authorities to recruit and train people from the city of Sirte. HALO procured heavy plant in country and armoured it in Misrata according to International Mine Action Standards (IMAS). Mechanical mine clearance teams consist of both mechanical operators, who are trained in safe and efficient handling of heavy plant, as well as manual clearance teams, who are trained in inspecting potentially contaminated spoil using specialised equipment including metal detectors and protective gear. The combination of people and machines allows the clearance of potentially contaminated land and its safe release back to the community according to IMAS. Capacity-building in Libya also involves supporting the LibMAC in coordinating and providing quality control over the new equipment and techniques in use. HALO’s first mechanical clearance teams in Sirte received full accreditation by the LibMAC in October 2019. In 2020, HALO seeks to expand its mechanical clearance capacity to Misrata, with the option to redeploy clearance teams to Tripoli upon cessation of hostilities. Moreover, HALO has recently been granted permission by authorities in the east to start operations there. Hence, the requested budget of $5,000,000 can be split up to cover five locations, i.e. Sirte, Misrata, Tripoli, Benghazi, and Derna, with a budget of $1,000,000 per location.

4 - Protection risk analysis

The main protection risk lies in local residents trespassing on the designated task sites for mechanical clearance/ERW removal and thus increasing the risk of ERW accidents.

5 - Mitigation measures

HALO is implementing an elaborate community liaison strategy that includes ad-hoc risk education sessions by community liaison officers in safe distance from the task site. The emphasis of these RE sessions is to explain the purpose of HALO's work and make sure beneficiaries are aware of safety distances requirements.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Recognising the need for robust community feedback mechanisms in order to gauge levels of community acceptance towards HALO’s work, we are implementing an elaborate community liaison strategy. This includes engagement prior and during work at a specific task site and a community hotline for community members to report suspicious items and deposit feedback.
