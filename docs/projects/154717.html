Project Summary

ACTED’s strategy for Child Protection activities in 2019 will continue to focus on the stabilization and recovery programming while addressing the most pressing needs of the conflict affected displaced at-risk girls and boys both in and out of camps throughout Ninewa, Dohuk and Kirkuk governorates. Planned interventions are designed in line with the CP Sub-cluster objectives for 2019.
ACTED as one of the main actors on child protection in Dohuk Governorate with an expertise in case management for complex cases as well as building capacities of local CP actors will continue utilizing a flexible approach in order to cover numerous camp and non-camp locations across 3 governorates, focusing namely on Zakho and Sumel in Dohuk governorate, Hawija in Kirkuk governorate as well as Mosul, Telafar and Sinjar in Ninewa governorate. ACTED will continue building on the capacities of the community to respond to child protection concerns through community-based initiatives and to reinforce social cohesion in particular in non-camp locations. As long-standing member of the Erbil and Dohuk Child Protection Working Group and an active member of the Mobile Team Task Force, Case Management Working Group and Best Interest Determination (BID) Panel, ACTED will capitalise on its extensive experience to deliver following activities:
a) Identifying and responding to the needs of the most vulnerable girls and boys including children affiliated or with perceived affiliation to extremist groups as well as unaccompanied and separated children through specialised case management services;
b) Providing structured and sustained PSS or resilience support to children and caregivers through community-based initiatives;
c) Engaging communities on preventive initiatives of child protection concerns through community outreach, awareness campaigns and community mobilization activities;
d) Strengthening the capacity of frontline workers, volunteers to respond to child protection issues as well as the national and local capacity to provide sustained child protection services;
e) Building the capacities of the non-CP actors through trainings on protection mainstreaming.
This intervention will target approximately 7,000 vulnerable at-risk individuals across 3 governorates where service provision by government and/or humanitarian support is currently insufficient.. This will include 5,166 highly vulnerable IDPs in and out of camp and non-displaced vulnerable men, women boys and girls, in areas of displacement, namely 1196 returnees and 638 members of host communities.

Needs

Although during 2018 Iraq entered into the phase and the recovery efforts are underway, it is estimated that in 2019 a total 6.5 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance. This includes displaced people in camps (500,000) and out-of-camp locations (1,500,000), communities hosting large numbers of displaced people (400,000) and returnees (4,000,000) (numbers as per HRP 2019 planning). Among these, 1,750,000 vulnerable population will be targeted for HRP 2019 living in locations with very high and high severity of conditions, namely in in Anbar, Baghdad, Diyala, Erbil, Kirkuk, Ninewa and Salah al-Din governorates. With regards to protection, the main needs and gaps are centred around protection monitoring, provision of legal assistance, psychosocial support of GBV and GBV case management, community approach case management services alongside integration of Child Protection in education – this will require critical assistance throughout 2019.As per the CP Sub-Cluster District Needs Severity Table as of November 2018, child protection needs are particularly significant in Ninewa governorate, including Mosul, Telafar and Hamdaniaya districts (with respectively 273,293, 128,917 and 103,309 children affected overall).Kirkuk governorate, notably Hawija also remain at the top of the priority list for child protection interventions with 80,309 children in need as per the Districts needs severity.Finally, child protection needs are also pressing in Dohuk governorate, both in camp and out of camp, with the most urgent needs identified in Sumel, both in and out of camps with 22,139 and 24,182 population affected. Similarly, for Zakho in and out of camps significant needs persist with more than 10,000 IPDs in need of the CP assistance in camp locations and around 16,000 IDPs out of camps.As the CP Sub-Cluster expects around 35,000 extremely vulnerable children in need of case management services in 2019, especially children with perceived affiliation to extremist groups, as well as children subject to exploitation or abuse it is crucial to continue provision of the specialised case management services which has been identified as a major bottleneck in terms of inadequate funding by the sub-cluster. Full case management services for the most vulnerable girls and boys represent the main gap and top priority among the CP Sub-Cluster objectives for 2019, especially regarding camps and out of camp areas. Other priorities refer to Child Protection integration with Education programs, including structured PSS programs in learning spaces, as well as training of teachers / social workers on CP issues, referral pathways, code of conduct (child safe guarding) and PSS activities.

Activities

Building on its existing experience and operational capacity in all of the governorates targeted, ACTED 2019 CP strategy will contribute to two of the objectives of the CP sub-cluster. Objective 1: Provide specialized protection assistance, and community-based support, to vulnerable conflict-affected individuals, thereby enabling them to secure their rights, access humanitarian assistance and public services, and overcome barriers to achievement of durable solutions.Under this objective, ACTED will: 1 Identify and respond to the needs of the most vulnerable girls and boys including children affiliated or with perceived affiliation to extremist groups as well as unaccompanied and separated children through specialised case management services via:- Case management through static and mobile Child Protection Units in and out-of-camps including specialised case management for GBV survivors;- Establishing Case Management Emergency Fund for medical and basic needs identified/referred. 2 Provide structured and sustained PSS or resilience support to children and caregivers through community-based initiatives via:- supporting in-camps community-based structures through training, mentorship and financial support to lead PSS activities and child protection community mobilisation initiatives. - establishing/supporting out-of-camps mobile teams to run PSS and child protection awareness raising activities in communal spaces.- establishing/supporting Child and Youth Friendly Spaces (CFS/YFS) in and out of camp locations for provision of counselling and life skills sessions with high focus on self-esteem, stress management, problem solving and positive coping mechanisms;- establishing/supporting parents’ groups with provision of parenting programs and parent-to parent peer support as structured trainings on increasing the knowledge, skills and confidence of parents; and fostering the use of positive discipline methods. 3. Engage communities on preventive initiatives of child protection concerns through community outreach, awareness campaigns and community mobilization activities:- In out-of-camp locations conducting awareness raising through mobile teams with outreach services, disseminating and advocating key child protection messages including content relating to resilience and safety;- Support/establish the community structures: Child and Youth Protection Committees (CPC/YPC) to enhance the ability of the community to identify, report and respond to child protection concerns;- Train CPC and YPC members on the identification and referral of at risk/vulnerable children and to finally become community focal points for identifying and referring child protection issues. 4. Strengthen the capacity of frontline workers, volunteers to respond to child protection issues as well as the national and local capacity to provide sustained child protection services:- Trainings for ACTED Child Protection staff and other non-governmental CP actors including but not limited to teachers, Gender-Based Violence actors, health providers, camp management staff and Community-Based Organizations, on risk identification, prevention strategies and referrals; Objective 2: Support and advocate with authorities, humanitarian and recovery actors to address protection issues in accordance with protection norms and applicableUnder this objective ACTED will build the capacities of the non-CP actors through trainings on protection mainstreaming depending on the gaps in knowledge identified. This can include: - Child Protection in Emergencies Trainings;- Psychological First Aid, - Mental Health and Psychosocial Support; - Referral pathways;- Identification of unaccompanied and separated children;- Gender Based Violence.

Protection Risk Analysis

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation 1: ACTED will maintain close coordination and communication with relevant security forces and community leaders (Mukhtars) in the project areas and will continuously monitor security situation.Mitigation 2: ACTED already has access permissions for all of the project areas and maintain coordination with relevant governmental offices (BRHA, Asayish) ensuring that necessary permissions are obtained. Shall the access permissions be withdrawn or refused, ACTED might consider distribution of assistance through relevant traditional community structures, taking into account feasibility.Mitigation 3: ACTED will ensure that all staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of ERW/UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe.Mitigation 4: Staff will be trained on Chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRNE) defence, provision of protective equipment to field staff will be considered.Prior to any humanitarian assistance delivery, ACTED will assess all risks mentioned above and adopt risk mitigation strategies for the beneficiaries and ACTED’s team when implementing the activities. ACTED will ensure that regular formal and/or informal risk analyses are performed and relevant colleagues are consulted (Country Coordinator, In-Country Security Manager, Area Coordinators and project staff). Communication with external forums for security updates (INSO) and humanitarian access will be regularly conducted by its ACTED’s Country Security Manager to inform project implementation and minimize risks. ACTED will ensure the security and safety of its operations through close liaison and collaboration with local authorities. ACTED’s security staff will also be tasked with continuously monitoring interactions between IDPs, returnees and host communities in order to determine the risk level of tensions and recommend relevant adjustments to the intervention. Moreover, ACTED’s Security Plan for Iraq currently in place will be activated at relevant stages of the project’s implementation. If the security situation deteriorates to the extent that ACTED’s team is not able to implement activities in proposed areas, activities will be reoriented to accessible areas.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Through a CP community-based approach, ACTED will promote the meaningful and inclusive participation of parents, teachers, community members and relevant stakeholders, building their capacity to respond to child protection needs in a sustainable manner. ACTED will ensure that beneficiaries are included in activity designs, notably through regular FGDs. Women, men, boys, girls, and persons with disabilities will be engaged to identify protection concerns and suggest solutions. ACTED also has a Complaint and response mechanism – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to register complaints and make suggestions. ACTED will ensure that its teams are gender-balanced with female and male staff, and are trained in gender-sensitive approaches. ACTED has identified that young girls, adolescent girls, and pregnant and lactating women have reduced access to protection services. ACTED will put an emphasis on enhancing their access to services and information about said-services with this project.Moreover, ACTED will pay careful attention to the differential and unique needs of children and adults within the target group. An emphasis will be put on girls and adolescent girls who are at greater risk of suffering from sexual and gender-based violence, including early marriage, and who are generally more difficult to target. Reaching and engaging adolescent girls in community-based CP and PSS services in both camp and non-camp settings remains challenging. To address this issue, and promote meaningful participation of adolescent girls ACTED, as a member of the Adolescent Girls Task Force and contributor to the development of the Tool Kit, will roll out the Adolescent Girls Tool-Kit developed by UNICEF and UNFPA. The project will also take into consideration the specific vulnerabilities and needs of persons with disabilities who will require further specialized support in adapted spaces. Additionally, ACTED will aim at reaching women and men equally through its activities targeted as parents, caregivers, community members and non-protection service providers.
