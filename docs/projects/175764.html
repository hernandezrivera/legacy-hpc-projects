Project Summary

This project aims to reach 49,579 beneficiaries (2.1% of the Health Cluster target) comprising of women, men, boys, girls, the elderly and those with disability in 7 Counties (Juba, Terekeka, Ikotos, Lafon, Magwi, Bor South and Panyikang) over the project period 2021-2022. Health Link intents to achieve about 14% of the cluster target across the selected 7 counties in Central & Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Upper Nile States. This project will complement the current development projects in most of the selected counties with focus on delivery of integrated essential health care services. Health Link will implement specific activities under the 3 Health Cluster Sectoral Objectives as outlined below;
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities
a. Conduct disease surveillance in 7 Counties. Health Link will support Weekly Reporting through IDSR and EWARS for all the supported health facilities including IDP Sites.
b. Establish 1 emergency team that will ensure Preparedness and Response to any outbreaks within the Country. Potential diseases of importance will include Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis and Hepatitis E among others
c. Conduct immunisation services targeting vaccine preventable diseases, both routine and campaigns to avert the effect of epidemic-prone disease outbreak.
d. Integrate Covid-19 response in emergency and routine health care delivery through delivery of critical response activities by pillars (Cases Management, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, Infection Prevention and Control (WASH/IPC), Continuous Training, Surveillance, RRT and Case Investigation, as well as operational support and logistics. Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care and EVD
2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems
a. Support 15 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 2 Hospitals (Al-Sabbah Children Hospital Stabilization Centre, Magwi CeMONC centre), 5 PHCCs and 8 PHCUs across the selected Counties.
b. Conduct facility based and mobile clinic consultations as well as community based consultations to diagnose and treat common disease including Malaria, RTIs and Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) and non-communicable diseases to reach estimated 49,579 beneficiaries.
c. Provide BeMONC and CeMONC services at 7 health facilities, 2 Hospitals and 5 PHCCs.
d. Support the management of SAM with medical complications at Al-Sabbah Stabilization Center and in Bor PoC, estimated to reach 250 cases of SAM with medical complications.
e. Training of health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. Health Link intends to train 40 health care workers in total, 25 clinicians and nurses in malaria and 15 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries.
f. Conduct monthly and quarterly WASH health facility assessment using standard tool and recommend for WASH/IPC improvement, Establish a network of Boma Health Members to provide community based diagnosis and treatment of common childhood illness including Malaria, RTIs and AWD and conducting health promotion activities and link them to health facilities, Conduct supportive supervision to the supported health care facilities and at the communities where BHWs are providing lifesaving services to ensure quality services are delivered across the project locations.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly
a. Establish and provide CMR and MHPSS services at 7 health facilities, 2 Hospitals and 5 PHCCs, and community based awareness.
b. Training of Health Care Workers in CMR and MHPSS.
c. Identify/registration of persons with disability and provision of assertive devices along-with other partners

1 - Needs

Estimated 56% of the population in South Sudan live at least 5km away from the nearest health facility and have much difficulties accessing essential health care services (SSHHS 2008, NHSSP 2017-2022). Critical associated factors compounding access to health care in the seven selected counties include inter-communal and civil conflicts, flooding and underdeveloped health care system. Worsening the current health situation is the common sub national conflicts especially in Lafon and Panyikang Counties, a second consecutive year of major flooding mostly in Bor South, Panyikang, Lafon and Ikotos Counties and the impact of Covid-19 across the selected Counties. In Mangala Payam (Juba County), there is an ongoing conflict between the Mundari and Bari communities affecting health care delivery. Overall, this is associated with poor health indicators, for example high mortality rate of under 5 children at 90.7/1,000 live births and maternal mortality rate of 789/100,000 live births (South Sudan Mortality Statistics, 2016) among others. Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. Immunization coverage over 2 years (2017 & 2018) for all vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained below 50%. This has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone and other infectious diseases including acute watery diarrhoea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by flooding and food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers, children under 5 years of age, people with disability and the elderly (60 years plus). Over stretching the health care system is the current ongoing Covid-19 pandemic with observed second waves far above 100 cases per day (MoH, Daily Updates, Feb 2021) and the looming threats of importation of viral heamorrhagic fever (Ebola Viral Disease) in the Country. With the ongoing revitalized peace process the anticipated returnees would even pose greater risk to Covid-19 infection, outbreak of other epidemic prone diseases (Measles, Hepatitis E, AWD/Cholera), Malaria and the importation of EVD in the Country. This necessitates for early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks while strengthening the current response to Covid-19 across all the response pillars . Cross cutting issues like people with disabilities, mental disorders, HIV/AIDS, TB and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. This has been related to contextual disruption of services, unpredictable health care funding in conflict affected locations with increasing IDP burden, and a lack of integration of services across the different clusters. Whereas the Ministry of Health Launched Boma Health Initiative Strategies (BHIs) with a focus on community based health care system to breach the gaps in access to integrated health care services, the implementation is currently limited to the counties with good access networks for service deliver. An expanded approach to reach out to the most vulnerable populations (men, women, boys, girls, elderly and disabled) through the community health care system is critical to improve access to care and also establish referral pathways from the community to the health facilities. Under this response plan, HLSS intends to fully integrate health care activities with nutrition, IPC/WASH, Protection and Food Security and Livelihood while ensuring both static and mobile services and community based interventions. Protection areas of focus will mainly include the clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV and MHPSS services.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Providing information about SEA to the beneficiaries is very critical to enhance community reporting of any form of SEA committed with the beneficiaries. As a bottom line, HLSS has PSEA policies and practices aim to prevent sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by its employees and it provides that any allegations of SEA are responded to in a timely and appropriate manner. All new staff in emergency context are oriented on this policy before deployment to deliver health care services to the beneficiaries. It is the principle of operations that HLSS ensures beneficiaries are oriented on their responsibilities of reporting SEA through a confidential protection desk that is always made available and linked to CMR services and legal support. This is always done during various community engagement process in the course of the project implementation. HLSS will also ensure beneficiaries education on SEA and reporting channel throughout the project period. The community-based feedback mechanisms to prevent and respond to SEA will be established. New staff at all service delivery points (static facility, mobile clinics and community based health care workers) will be trained in how to handle such cases with the level of confidentiality they deserve.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HLSS provides an environment where beneficiaries have the confident to provide feed-backs on health care projects being delivered to them. The most vulnerable are particularly given opportunity to express and share their views is the simplest way or in local language specific to the population group. This can be in form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions and community meetings. The information collected will be analyzed, and provided responses accordingly at different levels, and will also guide for inclusion of missing components or change of course whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries and the donors.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HLSS clearly recognizes the value of community and stakeholder engagement as an essential part of project planning, implementation, monitoring and collaborative decision making in regards to the project. Under this service delivery area, HLSS intends to employ three (3) approaches in engaging communities; (i) Conduct Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, (ii) conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community level health care services; (iii) conducting defaulter tracing, follow up and referrals.(i) Conduct Monthly Boma and Payam Health MeetingHLSS proposes to extend support to BHTs/Payam health administration for conducting monthly review meetings and activity planning to ensure effective implementation of the project. This forum will also provide opportunities for giving feedback to communities as a way of transparency and accountability to beneficiaries.(ii) Advocacy/Public Campaigns for Utilization of Community Level Health Care ServicesHLSS will work with the SMOH, CHD, Payam health officers, BHCs, and BHTs to conduct various meetings/campaigns at County, Payam, Boma and community levels. The meetings will target stakeholders and beneficiaries to understand the scope of this project including BHI components and strategies and to make sure the communities understand that BHI is part and parcel of the health care delivery system adopted to increase universal access to services and utilization. HLSS will also use mass media platform such as local FM radios and posters to advocate for the availability of the services offered under BHI at community levels. This is expected to increase understanding and utilization of the services by all the vulnerable populations in the project areas.(iii) Conducting Defaulter Tracing, Follow up and Referrals HLSS will support the BHWs to conduct defaulter tracing for EPI, TB and HIV services, children on treatment for MAM/SAM and follow up of ANC/lactating mothers, conduct early and prompt referrals of patients in critical condition and children under five years with danger signs using bicycles, motorcycles, or using stretchers from the Community to health facilities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project has been designed to benefit the host community, IDPs, returnees and other affected populations. The project intends to ensure that the services provided doesn't pose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted through carrying out joint activities and integrating some of the services within the population targeted. To prevent conflicts, this project intends to engage the local authorities in the proposed project locations for their full engagement during the project implementation to minimize some protection concerns that would arise such as Land disputes over project sites. The implementation of this projects will also ensure equal access to men, women, boys and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, Psychosocial Support with high level of privacy and confidentiality. Different approaches such as Conducting Monthly Boma and Payam health meetings, conducting advocacy/public campaigns for utilization of community level health care services, and conducting defaulter tracing, follow up and referrals will be adopted to enhance community participation that would contribute to risk reduction.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Recognizing the emergency humanitarian food assistance for the population in IPC 3,4 and 5 are never sustainable, HLSS intends to work with Livelihood partners to reach out to the vulnerable population and provide life-skills training. The network of BHWs/CNVs will be use to identify the most at risk groups for training. Such training will include alternative means of survival in crisis settings.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project was designed to significantly address access constraints to basic services including protection concerns of the most vulnerable section of the population especially women,children, older persons and persons with disability. Although the political environment remains fragile, the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement is beginning to take shape and there is currently relative calmness. This is anticipated to further improve access to remote areas, reduce risk of new displacement and subsequent stable system that will ensure protection of humanitarian workers and beneficiaries. The implementation of this projects will ensure equal access to men, women, boys and girls to basic and essential services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This project has been designed with inclusive consideration of integration of health and nutrition services at all the proposed project sites in line with the BPHNS. This approach will enhance early detection of children under 5 with malnutrition and earlier initiation of treatment with good cure rates. At the facility levels, the Stabilization Centers in Al-Sabbah and Bor PoC will be maintained and treatment of children with MAM/SAM with medical complications will be ensured. Coordination of activities with other partners implementing OTPs/TSFP services is critical to ensure timely and appropriate referrals of children with Malnutrition. At the community level, the Boma Health Workers (BHWs)/Community Nutrition Volunteers will be trained and deployed to continually conduct health and nutrition promotion activities including but not limited to household hygiene, personal hygiene, breast feeding, how to introduce other feeds for children 6 months and above, use of local available food materials to enhance diet, cooking demonstration among others. The BHWs/CNVs will conduct house to house screening of children using MUAC TAPES to identify those at risk and ensure earlier and appropriate referrals. The drivers of Malnutrition including common childhood diseases such as malaria, RTIs(Pneumonia) and Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) will be diagnosed and treated with ACTs, Amoxicillin, ORS and Zinc respectively. Strengthening community health care system through this strategy will ensure the barrier in access to health and nutrition services is bridged especially among the most vulnerable population in hard to reach locations.
