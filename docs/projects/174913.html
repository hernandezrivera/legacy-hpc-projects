Project Summary

FHI 360’s Integrated Humanitarian Assistance to Northeast Nigeria (IHANN III) project aims to contribute to reducing morbidity and mortality and improve the wellbeing of conflict-affected and displaced Nigerians through the provision of multi-sector humanitarian response in Borno State, Nigeria. IHANN III is a continuum of IHANN and IHANN II. The program has the following core objectives:
I. To improve access and quality of Primary Healthcare (PHC), reproductive health, and health outreach services in conflict-affected and displaced communities.
II. To improve the overall wellbeing of vulnerable individuals through increased access to integrated psychosocial support services, gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection services to mitigate the protection-related impact of the ongoing conflict.
III. To improve access to and utilization of safe WASH services including clean and safe water, improved sanitation facilities, and improved hygiene practices to prevent waterborne disease.
IV. To improve the nutritional status of the most vulnerable groups, including pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under five, in both the IDP and the host community populations through access to nutrition services.
The project targets 200,000 individuals affected in the target areas – Banki (Bama), Damasak (Mobbar), Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs. With support from USAID’s Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA), FHI 360 will sustain the implementation of the following activities designed to meet the most pressing needs of IDPs in the four field locations. Key activities will include:
• Support to five static health facilities and ten health posts in the form of laboratory testing, pharmaceuticals supply, and health staff for the management and prevention of communicable and non-communicable diseases, and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, among others;
• Training, provision of outreach materials/supplies, and supportive supervision to Community Health Workers (CHEWs) to enable them to conduct community outreach;
• Support to safe spaces with case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services for survivors of gender-based violence;
• Support to safe healing and learning spaces with case management and PSS services for children aged 3-17 and child protection community-based mechanisms to identify and respond to child protection concerns;
• Support to vulnerable individuals (women, adolescent girls, boys, and med) with PSS services to manage stress and other challenges presented by the ongoing conflict;
• Training for GBV and child protection caseworkers to maintain quality services;
• Rehabilitation/installation of essential WASH infrastructure, including latrines/bathing facilities, and water points;
• Distribution of hygiene kits coupled with hygiene promotion to improving knowledge, awareness and practice;
• Sanitation interventions, such as drainage interventions in flood-prone areas and clean-up campaigns, to reduce the environmental health risks;
• Support for optimal infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E) with integrated multi-sectoral interventions;
• Training of CHWs and health workers and support for them to provide appropriate services for the management of SAM.
FHI 360’s nutrition, health, protection, and WASH activities will continue to comprise a fully integrated package to promote comprehensive support to IDPs, returnees, and host communities thus reducing risks of morbidity and mortality and contributing to improved wellbeing of displaced and conflict-affected individuals in Borno State.

1 - Needs

The continued conflict in Northeast Nigeria has resulted in widespread displacement, international humanitarian and human rights law violations, protection risks and the deepening of an already protracted humanitarian crisis. The Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) shows that of 755 health facilities in Borno State, 292 (39%) are fully damaged and 205 (27%) are partially damaged; 50% (376) are nonfunctional. The conflict has led to an exodus of skilled health workers, leaving many people without basic health care. Across the Northeast states of Adamawa, Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, Taraba and Yobe, the estimated number of IDPs rose from 2,088,124 in June 2020 to 2,118,550 in August 2020. Borno State continues to host the highest number of IDPs at 1,566,011. Most displaced populations are sheltered in crowded formal and informal camps across the state and depend on humanitarian assistance for survival. Their number continues to grow with influxes of new arrivals, overburdening the already scarce resources, especially in border towns/local government areas (LGAs) like Banki, Damasak and Ngala. Since the onset of COVID-19 in Nigeria on the 27th of February 2020, the number of people in need of urgent assistance in Northeast Nigeria rose from 7.9 million to 10.6 million. Populations across the region remain at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases like cholera, measles, meningitis, hepatitis and viral hemorrhagic fevers. Malaria is endemic and among the top causes of illness among IDPs, along with acute respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhea, coupled with severe acute malnutrition. More than 40,000 children are at risk of dying due to the combined threat of severe acute malnutrition and medical complications within a context of high malaria prevalence and frequent population movement. Borno is far below the World Health Assembly’s 2025 targets for exclusive breastfeeding in the first six months (50%) and childhood wasting (less than 5%). According to the Nigeria Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey 2016-2017, the percentage of children exclusively breastfed was 29.5% in Borno. The state’s 16% prevalence of nutritional wasting was more than double the national prevalence of 6.8% (Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey [NDHS] 2018). NHDS 2018 also showed that one out of every two women of reproductive age in Borno has anemia (53.9%), while more than two out of every three children under 5 are anemic (71.2%), which is higher than the national average. Poor WASH conditions in camps and host communities remain a priority concern that contribute to illness and death. WASH infrastructure is under pressure from influxes of new arrivals from neighboring countries such as Cameroon, Chad and Niger, coupled with the relocation of IDPs from the state capital and other LGAs to their original homes. Women and girls are highly susceptible to the risk of rape, forced or early marriage, sexual exploitation because of the ongoing conflict and lack of services.

2 - Activities

FHI 360 with on-the- ground presence in Banki, Damasak, Dikwa, and Ngala will implements the following activities, delivering targeted interventions that are designed to meet the most pressing needs of IDPs across the project sites. The key activities include: • continued support to five clinics in the form of medical commodities and staff training to facilitate primary health care (PHC) and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, among others; • training, provision of outreach materials/supplies, and supportive supervision to Community Health Workers (CHEWs) to enable them to resume community outreach; • establishment of health outposts in each location to reduce the caseload on static health facilities • support to safe spaces with case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services for survivors of gender-based violence; • support to safe healing and learning spaces with case management and PSS services for children aged 3-17 and child protection community-based mechanisms to identify and respond to child protection concerns • support to vulnerable individuals (women, adolescent girls, boys, and med) with PSS services to manage stress and other challenges presented by the ongoing conflict • rehabilitation/installation of essential WASH infrastructure, including latrines/bathing facilities, and solar-powered boreholes; • distribution of hygiene kits coupled with hygiene promotion; • sanitation interventions, such as installation of waste points, drainage interventions in flood-prone areas and clean-up campaigns, to reduce the environmental health risks; • support for optimal infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E) with integrated multi-sectoral interventions • training of CHWs and health workers and support for them to provide appropriate services for the management of SAM . FHI 360’s nutrition, health, protection, and WASH activities will continue to comprise a fully integrated package to promote comprehensive support to IDPs, thus reducing risks of morbidity and mortality and contributing to improved wellbeing of displaced and conflict-affected individuals in Borno State.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

FHI 360 recognizes the impact of the ongoing conflict on women, girls, boys, and men on protection-related issues, psychology, and concerning access to humanitarian services, reproductive health care, nutrition, raising children, water, sanitation and hygiene, information, and opportunities. Also, cultural factors influence gender roles and can limit the ability of women to work or make household decisions. This project will build upon FHI 360’s extensive SRH, GBV, and child protection expertise and knowledge of gender-related and other vulnerabilities, particularly within the Nigerian IDP and host community context. There will be special attention given to safeguarding of program participants from harm, especially children, adolescents, and vulnerable adults during the delivery of our services. To ensure program participants are protected from harm, FHI 360 has developed three stand-alone safeguarding policies that protect program participants. These policies are Protecting Program Participants from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Safeguarding of Children and Combating Trafficking in Persons, and FHI 360 Code of Ethics and Conduct all-encompassing policy that includes key elements from the stand-alone safeguarding policies, as well as other prohibited conduct and expectations on how the organization expects staff to treat each and anyone who interacts with our programs. Equally, FHI 360 will continuously engage its community-based workers on PSEA messages during training, meetings, gathering, and outdoor activities.FHI 360 safeguarding focal person will continue to provide training every six months for all staff, CVs, and CHEWs on this code of conduct including e-learning opportunities while making sure that all offices have the written policies and reporting channel pasted and supporting reporting of sexual exploitation and abuses and action through its HQ Office of Compliance and Internal Audit (OCIA) Unit and community feedback mechanism (CFM) while ensuring accountability to the affected population. Consistently, FHI 360 works with its staff, stakeholders, local authorities, and other actors to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming into all activities by prioritizing consideration of the specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability, and protection among the beneficiary populations. FHI 360 will promote close community participation in the design, implementation, and evaluation (e.g. quality peer reviews) of all project activities to support inclusion based on gender, disability, and age. Persons affected by GBV and child protection concerns will be considered (e.g. special sanitation for women and girls). Disability considerations, especially for physical access to service delivery points (latrines, health facilities, water points) will be paramount when designing and selecting locations for such sites. Through each sector, FHI 360 will ensure protection mainstreaming is at the forefront. This includes the location of, access to, and safety while using WASH facilities. FHI 360 will continuously take into consideration the needs of female patients and children, by ensuring privacy during consultations, recruiting female health/community outreach personnel, and aiming for an equal ratio of female and male CHEWs. Health care staff will further be sensitized to identify and respond to the child involved in physical abuse and/or who have suffered sexual violence/other forms of GBV such as forced marriage.
