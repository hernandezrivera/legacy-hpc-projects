Project Summary

The proposed project aims to increase food and livelihood kit access for vulnerable populations in Fangak, Ayod, and Canal/Pigi counties. The project provides emergency kits and adaptive training and builds capacity for crop, vegetable, and fish production. CAO plans to reach 15,740 households in IPC phases 4 and 5. CAO intends to reach out to the 94,440 households population in the region through the distribution of agricultural kits, support to fisheries, emergency food and livelihood, livestock treatment, and vaccination. They plan to recruit and train 400 Community Animal Health Workers (CAWHs) for livestock support, and 15,740 households will be trained on modern agronomic practices. CAO has a physical and operational presence in Jonglei state, mainly in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod, where they have World Bank development health (BHI) activities, protection, FSL, WASH, and nutrition with support from their partners.

1. Needs

South Sudan is one of the countries most affected by food insecurity globally and in East Africa. Over 60% of the population is expected to experience acute food insecurity between June and September 2023 (FEWSNET, 2023), with Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes anticipated to be widespread during the peak of the lean season. According to the South Sudan FEWSNET report from June 2023 to January 2024, most households face stock depletion earlier than usual in many areas, income-generating opportunities remain limited, and staple food prices are atypically high. This is worsened due to disrupted trade flows and increased demand related to the conflict in Sudan and the influx of refugees and returnees. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes, associated with significant food consumption gaps and high acute malnutrition and mortality, are assessed in 40 counties, including mainly in Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Unity States. The FEWSNET food security outlook is consistent with the current IPC analysis report, which estimates that 7.8 million people (63% of the population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or, worse, acute food insecurity. During the lean season projection period from April to July 2023, 43,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, Canal/Pigi, and Fangak counties of Jonglei (SS IPC, Dec 2022). The situation is compounded by various covariates, including climatic shocks leading to severe floods and, at times, dry spells, sub-national conflicts, and economic shocks with continued high food prices and disruption of livelihoods owing to legacy conflicts and insecurity. Household-level stressors such as the death of breadwinners, GBV, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services are greatly amplified by the compounded effect of shocks. Women and children continue to suffer the most significant proportion of the impact.Recent reductions in funding against rising humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to compel humanitarian actors to freeze entitlements and prioritize the most vulnerable households. Overall, food insecurity continues to persist and is currently exacerbated by the effects of the high influx of refugees fleeing the Sudan/Khartoum crisis. The current food insecurity is expected to worsen during the peak of the lean season. Livestock contributes to food security in most rural areas in South Sudan; however, the lack of veterinary services, livestock disease outbreaks, conflicts, inter-communal cattle raiding, adverse weather conditions, availability of water, pasture, and abundance of diseases continue to threaten the lives of millions of livestock (FSNMS round 24, 2022). There is a high need to respond to these emergencies. Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod counties have suffered massive effects of Floods, conflicts and displacements, and disease outbreaks. According to the current IPC analysis, Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod are all in IPC phases 4 and 5 (IPC, 2023). According to FEWSNET, the food insecurity situation in Fangak, Ayod, and Canal/Pigi is expected to be severe among households whose food stocks will run out and rely on markets for food purchases (SS IPC, Oct 2023). To address the urgent needs of households in IPC phase 4 and 5, CAO intends to take the following steps:1) Provide emergency food production interventions by distributing emergency livelihood kits.2) Build the resilience of at-risk populations through support to animal health, a key component of food security, and establish VSLA groups.3) Establish climate-sensitive agriculture technology to increase food production and incomes at the household level in the proposed project locations in line with 2024 FSL sectoral HRP objectives. CAO will coordinate with WASH, Nutrition, Health, and other partners to integrate CAO's FSL emergency response activities within their activities to reach all IPC 4 & 5 HHs.
