Project Summary

Alliance For Action Aid, (AFAA), aims to provide emergency protection services to host communities, IDPS, returnees, and refugees targeting vulnerable women, girls, children, men, and persons with disabilities in Panyinjiar and Longechuk Counties through series of planned activities. These activities outcomes will be measured by # of women, men, girls , boys, and persons with disabilities with protection concerns or specific needs reached with protection awareness raising messaging. AFAA will achieved these by working with communities of affected population and other protection local partners/service providers, develop key protection awareness raising messages and information on availability of services. One of the key steps AFAA will do is community mobilization and sensitization, where AFAA will engage in dialogue with the local communities in the project areas. Through these interactions, the organization project team will raise awareness about the components of the project support, its implementation timeline, and its relevance to the communities. Additionally, AFAA will conduct information-sharing sessions to ensure that the communities have a comprehensive understanding of the project which includes carrying out activities such as project launches, conducting meetings and awareness raising sessions with key stakeholders (Boma chiefs, paramount chiefs, Payam Administrators, County and payam legal structures ,etc), At each project site, key stakeholders will be brought together to reflect on key protection concerns and proposed tailored made interventions, come up with criteria for selection of vulnerable households with serious protection issues, methods for maintaining protection mainstreaming in available services offered at the County and Payams level by other partners.
Mobilization of local leadership that influence local policies and key decision making, conduct bi-weekly deep field protection monitoring missions to identify protection concerns of hard to reach communities, conduct quarterly project review meetings which will be held and facilitated by the project team. The objective of these meeting will be to bring together key stakeholders with the aim of updating all on progress, challenges and together determining ways to overcome the identified protection concerns .
Over the last three years, AFAA’s work with communities and local authorities has focused on identifying and addressing protection concerns through monthly protection monitoring in both static and mobile response to deep field hard to reach locations. This experience will contribute to smooth implementation of this project in fulfilment of the protection cluster objectives. Under the cluster GP-2 and GP-3 approaches, the project will target 10,000 beneficiaries ( 3500 women, 2500 girls, 1750 boys, 1500 men, and 750 persons with disabilities).

1. Needs

South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Since the independence and the signing of revitalized peace agreement in 2018, the situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks and climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary flooding and localized drought. These in turn have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hamper access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, especially for women and girls. People affected by violence have limited access to formal justice as well as restriction in their movement and access to basic needs such as food.The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing that 5.83 million people (46.3 percentage of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State; and the Rubkona County (15,000) of Unity State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency).The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61.3 percent), Unity State (57.9 percent), Upper Nile State (56.1 percent) and Lakes State (52.8 percent).In the first projection period of December 2023 to March 2024 (harvest/ post-harvest season), the situation is projected to improve marginally as a result of seasonal availability of harvets. An estimated 5.79 million people (45.8 percent of the population) are projected to face IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), including 25,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) and 1.71 million people likely to be in Phase 4.In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3 percent of the population) will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency).The most food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance.Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.As for the severity of the situation, between July and September 2023, 46 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), 15 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious), 10 counties in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) and 9 in IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable). During the post-harvest period of October 2023 to March 2024, the AMN situation is expected to remain the same. Deterioration in 66 counties is expected during the lean season period of April to June 2024.

2. Project activities

PC1 – Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations. This can be achieve by 1) working with communities of affected population and protection partners, develop key protection awareness raising messages and information on availability of services, 2) working with protection partners, develop and implement static or mobile activities targeted at identification and referral of PSNs, especially in emergency situations. This will be measure through 1) number of women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs reached with protection awareness raising messaging, and 2) number of women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs, identified and referred through static or mobile means. Other key activities includes a) Community awareness raising on protection risks such as child marriages, abductions, revenge killings, child labor, domestic Intimate Partner violence, cattle raiding, land disputes and legal policies.b) Training and workshop on policies on community rights and proper execution of protection related cases in the community. The training targeting community leaders, existing local institutions, youth and women groups and local humanitarian actors. c) Identification of Persons with Specific Needs (PSN), Person with Disabilities and other vulnerable groups and support them through referral pathways. d) Conduct community dialogue to address identified protection concerns targeting community leaders, opinion leaders, women and youth groups.
