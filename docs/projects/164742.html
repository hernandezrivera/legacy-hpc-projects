Project Summary

This project supported the operation of Leen Center in Kherbet Eljoz. The center was established 3 years ago by Al Sham Humanitarian Foundation, as a response to health requirements within the region. The center offered primary healthcare services to IDPs who’ve fled to the area escaping shelling. The center offered medical consultations in 2 specialized clinics Internal Medicine Clinic and Pediatrics Clinic. The center also participated in epidemiological monitoring of the area which is reported to the WHO in order to respond to outbreaks rapidly and keep track of communicable disease cases. The center also offered general nursing services including but not limited to wound care, nebulization, giving injections and other services in addition to providing patients medicines they require. All the services offered by this center were mainly for the IDPs and host communities and were free of charge without any discrimination, giving priority to most vulnerable groups. The project aimed to fill the gap in PHC services for the most vulnerable groups among IDPs in their camps, to contribute in decreasing the morbidity and mortality rates in the area and participating in EWARN to eliminate any potential outbreaks early.
We are coordinating regularly with the IHD, Health cluster, submitting 4Ws and with the HFs and NGOs operating in the area.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. 4 - Needs

Most of the beneficiaries (92%) were the IDPs in the camps near Kherbt Aljouz in Idleb and the rest (8%) of the beneficiaries were from the host communities. Around 43% of the beneficiaries were children, and they visited the pediatric clinic and the rest of the beneficiaries were provided with consultations from the internal medicine clinic. Most of children patients (80%) were visited the center with upper and lower respiratory infections and Influenza-like illness, and about 10% of them came with non-bloody Diarrhea. And for adults about 50% of the patients were visiting the clinics with NCDs and the rest of the percentage were for various reasons. 239 PLWs and CU5 cases of malnutrition provided with treatment and follow up through the pediatric clinic.
