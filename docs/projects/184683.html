Project Summary

As per Humanitarian Needs Overview a total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. School age children are most likely to face difficulties in terms of schooling opportunities and protection services. Somalia Education Cluster Needs Analysis Note 2021 indicate that an estimated 1.4 million school aged children (45% girls) are in need of humanitarian assistance to retain children in schools or enroll in schools.
Children in Hiran Beletweyne Somalia have been suffering from multi-faceted disasters including repeated flooding, Covid-19 pandemic crisis for the last three years. Hiran Multi-cluster Joint Assessment for Shocks-affected People September 2020 reported that 39% of school age children don’t go to the schools due the different factors including: 39% of the children in the age of school don’t go because they can’t scholastic materials even if the school is free, 34% due to fees being unaffordable, 24% due to the long distance of the nearest school and 3% due to the security concerns. Furthermore, those emergencies left Somali children extremely vulnerable to violence, abuse, neglect, exploitation, soldiering and discrimination that relate to displacement, food insecurity and cultural norms. Last report of UN Security Council indicates that Somalia is among the countries that have recorded the highest number of grave violations against children, including recruitment and use of children. Between 1 September 2020 and 31 July 2021, 1,431 children, including 37 girls, were reportedly used or recruited by armed groups.
The proposed project aims to improve enrollment and retention of vulnerable school aged children through creating safe and protective learning environment in Beletweyne, Hiiran region of Somalia.
The target beneficiaries of the project are 3,000 crisis affected school children (1,500 girls and 1,500 boys) including those with disabilities. The project will focus on rehabilitation of 30 classrooms in 10 school, distribution of learning and teaching materials and recreational materials and hygiene promotion supplies for 3,000 children (50% girls), distribution of safe drinking water, construction of 10 gender responsive payment of teacher incentives for 60 teachers (30 female and 30 male) in 12 months, establishing referral systems in 10 emergency schools including formation of 150 CEC members (75 female and 75 male) and 10 school clubs with a total of 30 members (15 boys and 15 girls). Child Safeguarding and protection mapping will be the central in the project implementation activities.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

School children in Hiran regions, with a focus on girls, children with disabilities, poor children, working children, orphans, IDP children, children associated with militia, ethnic minority children, who are frequently neglected and suffer from social deprivation, are among the target groups and beneficiaries expected to be reached by the proposed program. So, FENPS will take into account the geographical conditions of the location of schools, as well as threats in the surrounding area, and plans will be developed accordingly. FENPS will create protective environment that natural disasters and associated hazards can be mitigated or prevented before they occur, and to handle any harm or minimize its effects, or, if possible, to be well prepared to reduce the risks of the disaster. FENPS will comprehensive child safeguarding and child protection guidelines, and Codes of Conduct in place so project staff and suppliers will exercise those guidelines.
