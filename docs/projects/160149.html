Project Summary

The project is to provide emergency life-saving emergency primary health services to 17,000 people drought affected direct beneficiaries in Badhaade, Afmadow and Kismayo district, through the provision of 6 mobile clinics and 3 MCH as well services. Emphasis will be on maternal, neonatal and child health including emergency immunization to the host communities and Internally Displaced People (IDPs) as well as treatment of severe acute malnutrition. This action will focus on provision of both preventive and curative health services at primary level integrated with Nutrition activities that are undertaken by SA. Building the capacity of health workers through training on integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI), community case management (CCM) as well as cholera and measles management training and basic obstetric care training that will be conducted to equip health workers with the required skills to deliver quality health care services to the affected .The proposed project took into consideration the impact of the drought/AWD/cholera affected women, and children in terms of access to Health and services.
SA with the technical guidance of Federal ministry of health, UNICEF and WHO as well as the health Cluster will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness in the program so that enough steps are taken in ensuring early response to the reported cases. Finally, SA will coordinate with Health and clusters to coordinate response activities for maximum impact to beneficiaries in the targeted project.SA shall also coordinates with other local actors in the target district to enable avoid any duplication of services as well work in a coordinated manner to enable increase access to life-saving emergency response in the areas.

Project Activities

1. Provide daily outpatient consultation services to 17,000 through the 6 static clinic and 6 mobile outreach services linking with other ongoing OTP and livelihood program in L.Juba regions target area. 2. Provide safe motherhood primary health care services in antenatal and postnatal care to 4,000 women returnees,IDPs and host communities in Lower Juba region. 3. Establish good referral services through linkage with other implementing partners between community health workers and health staff for critical medical cases for women, boys, girls and men in L.Juba region. 4. Provide access to cost effective life- saving and life sustaining primary health care through static and mobile clinic for IDPs, returnees and host communities’ boys, women, girls and men. 5. Capacity build 20 project staff (10 male and 10 female) on effective management of IMCI and BEMOC case management for boys and girls under five children. 6. Provide refresher training to CHWs on hygiene and health promotion and behavior change communication to boys, girls, men and women beneficiaries in the target area. 7. Conduct routine EPI services to children under five boys and girls through linking them with other available nutrition and WASH services and in coordination with other partners and Juba MOH in the target locations in Lower Juba regions 8. Maintain and operationalized 2 BEMOC health facility in Kismayo district.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement The project targets to intervene in Lower Juba more specifically Kismayo IDps,and Badhade district where there is large number of internally displaced person as well as returnees from the border Dadaab refugee in Kenya however, this different diversity in the target areas may create conflicts or community tension among the already weak IDPs, returnees and host communities on resources sharing, to mitigate this Southern aid will enable to provide equal and transparent project information and target beneficiaries hence reducing the community tension. Also, lack legalize land for constructing permanent health facilities is a big challenge and may create animosity between the various stakeholders, where such issues raise Southern aid will ensure closer coordination and information sharing, as well as government guidelines on the best way possible as well, engage the community for a durable solution.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and agePoor beneficiary data dis-aggregated on needs may challenge to enable reach the actual people in needs, to mitigate this Southern aid will strive to conduct pre- intervention assessment as well as provide mobile clinic to the far rural areas with limited access to enable save the life of the vulnerable groups. Location and distance of the health facility may be challenge for the boys,girls and pregnant and lactating women in emergency , SA shall be able to ensure the health facility is closer to the beneficiary to avoid any risk related such as sexual violence/abuse and explosion of war remnant. Unfriendly health facilities infrastructure will discourage the disable and physically challenge members of the community, to mitigate this SA will ensure all health facility are up to date and health cluster standard required ensuring further on gender sensitivity twin latrine hence friendly to all user including boys and girls.￼￼￼￼￼￼￼￼￼Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence￼Project staff capacity will built on prevention of sexual exploitation among the vulnerable women, girls and boys as well the staff will sign code of conducts, Southern aid will ensure to promote all inclusive consultation and feedback within the project staff as well as the community to avoid any gender base violence among the displaced and returnees. It will also ensure to prioritize the most vulnerable among the target population giving more consideration on women, boys and girls.
