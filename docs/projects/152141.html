Project Summary

Plan International requests funding of 4,000,000 USD for a 12 months’ period to implement Education in Emergencies (EiE) activities to reach 60000 children (45% boys and 55% girls). The requested fund will be used to create equitable access to education for conflict-affected children. The funding will also improve the quality of learning while providing a protective learning environment for children under the do no harm policy and focus on targeting new arrivals in the local government areas of Gwoza, Kaga, Pulkha, Dikwa, Konduga and Mongunu all in Borno state North East Nigeria.
To achieve the above project objectives, and based on needs identified, Plan international I will establish, and refurbishment temporary learning spaces, do awareness raising and community mobilization to create access while to achieve improved quality, the action foresees training of teachers, provision of teacher materials, in-service and mentorship sessions. The action will build on the exist School Based Management Committees, train learners on life skills, establish and clearly mark gender segregated latrines, incorporate safe schools approach and other interventions in a bid to provide a protective learning environment.
The action will further seek to use the TaRL like approach that Plan International is currently using in providing education services to the children in MMC and Jere LGAs that has proved effectiveness in improving learning outcomes in literacy and numeracy.
The proposed action will target children aged 3-6 both boys and girls to benefit from early childhood development (ECD) and children aged 6-17 to benefit from formal education and non-formal education(ALP). Both in and out of school children belonging to the host and IDP communities will be targeted through the project with an aim of increasing enrollment, retention as well as increasing school attendance.

Needs

Between 2017 to date 3 in depth education needs assessments have been carried in which Plan International either directly led the assessment of was a key member of the assessment team providing technical support during the development of the needs assessment tools as well as during the actually needs assessment. In April 2017, a post-displacement community needs assessment in Borno State was jointly conducted by Plan International and Action Aid, In July 2017 due to the urge to better understand the needs of the population and the changing dynamics, plan international carried out an in depth multi sectoral needs assessment in the sectors of Education, Child protection, Nutrition and livelihood. In September 2018, the education working group led an all education needs assessment in North Eastern Nigeria covering all the accessible LGAs including the 5 targeted LGAs under this project (Raan, Dikwa, Pulkha, Gwoza, Konduga) In September 2018 before the submission of this proposal, Plan International carried out a rapid needs assessment in 6 locations (Mongunu,, Gwoza, Konduga, Raan, Dikwa, Pulkha) to further make a follow up on the earlier identified needs and verify the existing camps, and additional need for the new arrivals in the target locations that has informed this proposal development and as well to select 3 locations out of the 7 initially assessed locations. All the assessments focused on collecting real time primary data from selected Local Government Areas in Borno State. Host Communities, formal and informal IDP camps were visited. The assessment tools used were Household questionnaires, Key Informant Interviews, Focus Group Discussions and Transect Walk with different groups such as the women, adolescent girls and the disabled to understand the different unique needs. Household Questionnaires administered to 1000 Heads of Households (MALE & FEMALE) KII to Camp Managers, Community leaders and Focus Group Discussion. Focus areas for the assessment included: opportunities for Skills Development, Acquisition and Support for Livelihood Activities for Youth and Women, Social Investment and Protection for the affected population, Community Based Cooperative Shared Access to Resources, Market, Education and Health, especially for Women and Children. Given the wealth of secondary data available, the assessment complimented the primary data with a desk review of relevant literature.In November and December 2016, Plan International conducted a participatory needs assessment focusing on Education, Child Protection in Emergency (CPiE) and Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) in Adamawa and Borno States.The primary data collection assessment tools included Key informant interviews, Direct observation and Community group discussions. Three (3) levels of interviews were carried out: state, local and community. A questionnaire was developed and used to capture KAP of the community. The state level assessment involved all key stakeholders such as Ministries, UN agencies and INGOs. The State level discussions were followed by Key Informant Interviews discussions involving LGA administrator, departmental heads and community leaders while the community level discussion will involve focus group discussions with women, men and youths (young men and women). At the LGA level, entry meetings were held with the Local Government Chairman and Key Informant Interviews were conducted. At total of 25 communities in 7 local governments have been assessed through the most recent rapid needs assessment One Community Questionnaire was administered to sectoral key informants, recorded and observations noted. A secondary data review of relevant sources based on the annual school censors board report and other existing reports from sister organizations like IRC Mercy Corps, ESSPIN was conducted.
