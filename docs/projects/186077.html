Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s north-eastern states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe is expected to persist unabated in 2021: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of people in 2021, subjecting them to displacement (new or continued), impoverishment and threat of violence. Some 1.92 million people are displaced internally, and 257,000 have sought refuge in neighbouring Cameroon, Chad and Niger. The majority (54%) of the internally displaced people (IDPs) have found refuge in host communities. Borno State has 81% of the IDPs, of whom slightly more than half (54%) stay in IDP camps. The COVID-19 pandemic both deepens humanitarian needs and complicates the response. The Nigerian economy has suffered from the fall in global oil prices and from restriction measures to curtail the spread of the virus, particularly intermittent border closures and the need to dedicate resources to respond to the pandemic. The consequent impairment of livelihoods cascades down to loss of income and buying power, with acute effects on the already-vulnerable and food insecure.
To strengthen the resilience of vulnerable conflict- and displacement-affected populations, through livelihood support and thereby addressing economic recovery of the affected people. In order to ensure that the GHIV Africa programme response is relevant and appropriate it will be required to understand and address the needs of the different primary groups who are displaced and otherwise conflict-affected: - IDPs in host communities and camps - Returnees (from internal displacement or voluntary/forced repatriation from camps) - Host Communities (hosting IDPs or receiving returnees). GHIV Africa strives to address tailored economic recovery assistance needs of people affected by conflict and displacement.

1 - Needs

. According to 2020 November Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis which projects that up to 5.1 million people risks being critically food insecure during the next lean season (June to August 2021). Food insecurity at such large scale had not been recorded since 2016-2017 when some worst-affected locations faced famine-like conditions. The outbreak of COVID-19 has only further worsened the food insecurity by impacting the livelihoods and income sources of already vulnerable households .The Borno state government had on May 29 officially started closing some of the IDP camps as part of Governor Babagana Zulum’s commitment to building the resilience of local communities against the scourge of organised armed groups. Hundreds of internally displaced persons (IDPs) who were recently moved out of displacement camps in Borno, Northeast Nigeria, to resettlement housing sites have expressed worries that they may not survive at the sites chosen for them by the state government. As many as loss their means of livelihood and going back to their communities meaning they have to start thinking of what they can do to sustain them.In line with the above, GHIV Africa will strengthen the resilience of this vulnerable groups and other vulnerable groups of Kaga and Konduga by Providing livelihood support and income generating activities to the vulnerable group and support social cohesion.

2 - Activities

GHIV Africa shall be conducting the following activities:1.Deliver vocational training and business skills development and provide support for small scale business establishment (start-up grants or in-kind grants)for 2000 individuals (Kaga-500, Konduga-1500) IDPs, Returness and hostcommunities 2.Improve community saving, increase access to credit through creation and capacity building of community cooperatives, Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs) to the 2000 beneficiaries 3.Provide emergency employment opportunities and Cash-for-Work activities for 100 (Kaga-30, Konduga-70) which will be targeting environmental hygiene in the communities. GHIV Africa will be carrying out the above activities through: 1. Consultation with community stakeholders across the project locations. 2. Mapping of returness, IDP and host communities to identify 2000 households hit by drastically reduced incomes, especially female headed households, persons living with disabilities with unstable incomes (daily workers) as well as the poorest households with little savings. 3. Conduct an assessment of business viability in the targeted communities in order to provide tailored training and strategic support to the selected beneficiaries in areas of basic business principles, finance management, bookkeeping, savings and loan schemes, and ethics 4.. Advocacy meetings with community stakeholders (community leaders, religious leaders, youth leaders, etc) on promoting gender equality and women’s participation and community engagements to strengthen social cohesion.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa will ensure that safety, dignity, accountability and do no harm procedures are implemented and respected during the entire period of implementation. GHIV Africa will constantly monitor the security situation of the area of intervention in order for the mobile teams to choose secured and authorized roads to access the vulnerable IDPs in need. The mobile teams will only organise their activities in places considered as safe, in areas agreed on with the local authorities to minimize the risks taken by beneficiaries of settling in areas of continuing conflict or areas that have not been cleared of landmines and unexploded ordnance.To support the recognition of protection concerns, GHIV Africa field staffs will receive an initial training on child protection and GBV mainstreaming before the implementation of the activities. There will also be an emphasis to ensure an equal number of project staff will be female to ease the communication and interaction with female beneficiaries. Mobile teams will allow a flexible access to various locations where IDPs will be established, even temporarily, protection services will thus be available during day time, ensure safety for children, and the teams are free to come back to any location if any urgent needs arise. In order to avoid the creation of tensions or added risk for the beneficiaries who will receive items through the distribution of NFIs and food distribution for in the project activities, GHIV Africa will ensure strong information sharing on selection criteria, the value of items and usage and distribution processes, and that beneficiaries’ specific needs and opinions are taken into account. GHIV Africa’s MEAL department will establish a feedback mechanism that beneficiaries can access.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. GHIV Africa will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, project activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to GHIV Africa’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. GHIV Africa will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. GHIV Africa’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable GHIV Africa to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. GHIV Africa will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, GHIV Africa's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
