Project Summary

Plan International's project in Cox’s Bazar seamlessly aligns with the Rationalization Principles, particularly emphasizing Ref. #3 - Adopt an integrated approach, and Ref. #4 - Ensure predictability and continuity. Through an integrated approach, the project comprehensively tackles multifaceted challenges faced by impoverished households in the host community and Rohingya camps.
To ensure predictability and continuity, Plan International has established sustainable frameworks. Temporary training centers and partnerships with existing facilities contribute to the project's flexibility and adaptability.
Aligning with the Bangladesh Technical Education Board's curriculum enhances the standardization and longevity of the training, ensuring that acquired skills remain relevant in the evolving job market. Connection with MFIs provide ongoing support for entrepreneurship, fostering economic sustainability. PIB's working structure in the host community facilitates vocational and technical training and networking with job providers to connect trained-up youth for suitable employment.
PIB will provide vocational training on different trades for 200 project participants such as Solar Installation maintenance and repairing (25 individuals), Sewing machine operation (75 individuals) Electronics (Building) (50 individuals), Plumber (25 individuals) and Small Engine mechanics (General Mechanics) (25 individuals). Among them 50 trained up project participances from the trades of Solar Installation maintenance and repairing, Electronics (Building) and Small Engine mechanics (General Mechanics) will be engaged with EEN through coordination with Energy & Environment Network (EEN). Rest of skilled project participants will be engaged with self-employment through providing in kinds support except the trade the participants from plumber and sewing machine operation.
PIB will also provide non-formal technical training on tailoring trades for 100 project participants embroidery for 50 project participants and handicrafts for 50 project participants. Then all 200 project participants will be self-employed through providing necessary in kind supports.
In the case of youth selection, the Washington Group questionnaire is utilized. Identified persons with disabilities will engage with suitable trades as per the selection criteria of project participants. The project will initially identify suitable beneficiaries requiring business start-up support for small-scale enterprises and job creation. PIB will continue business startup support to strengthen their assigned businesses as entrepreneurs and enhance their job performance.
Plan International Bangladesh has the tools for the protection of the target people from any harmful situation such as risks assessment before organizing any event as well as mitigation the identified risks. In addition, project staff will provide awareness session on Human rights, gender equality, SRHR, safeguarding, PSEA, GBV and child marriage for Rohingya people and people of Host community. Plan International always take immediate attention for prevention, mitigation and responding any child protection issues. PIB will Mobilize youth to fight harmful practices such as child marriage and Identify the barrier of GBV and find out the way forward for execution accordingly.
In support of LSDs interventions, PIB will initiative accelerate adult learning opportunities for the illiterate or semi-literate selected participants selected for LSDs interventions. This accelerate adult learning opportunities will specially focus on functional literacy and numeracy skills in Burmese language.
By embracing these Rationalization Principles, Plan International's project not only addresses immediate skill development needs but also establishes a foundation for long-term, predictable, and integrated solutions. This empowers individuals in both host communities and Rohingya camps, fostering sustainable development and resilience.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The job market assessment for Teknaf, referencing the Bangladesh Bureau of Educational Information and Statistics (BANBEIS), highlights a significant dropout of adolescent boys and girls. The absence of a Technical Training Center (TTC) in Teknaf and the emphasis on technical skills education in the 8th Five Year Plan (8 FYP) create a gap for youth employability. Many youths in Teknaf are inclined to go abroad due to this dearth of skills.The lack of a standard BMET facility in Teknaf results in youth joining low-paid informal work due to insufficient technical skills. Recognizing the demand for skilled workers locally, nationally, and abroad, the project focuses on providing market-relevant skills to girls, boys, and young women for dignified work. The Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) reveals that only 42% of households in the host community can access employment, and 29% can work on electricity, solar lamps, and battery maintenance.The MSNA highlights that 29% of host community households and 53% of refugee camp households lack nearby income and livelihood opportunities due to limited income-generating options in the area. Limited data on Rohingya youth engagement in education exposes them to exploitation, protection risks, and negative coping mechanisms. The Camps have made strides in recruiting female teachers, but a majority are Bangladeshi, posing challenges in delivering education services.To address these gaps, there's a need for NTVQF-level 1 training for the host community and non-formal training for Rohingya individuals, focusing on essential soft skills. The utilization of the Washington Group questionnaire is imperative for participant selection. This comprehensive approach seeks to bridge skill gaps, create employment opportunities, and empower both host communities and Rohingya individuals.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project targets people from 18-24 years age group and will follow inclusion & diversity principle to include marginalized and excluded people in collaboration with Department of Social Services, Camp in Charges and Site Management. It is expected that 8% of the targeted beneficiaries will be persons with disability will be included in the project. Besides, women headed households and households with persons with disability will get priority. During beneficiary selection, project staff will explore persons with disability through PRA session, HH visit and verification by criteria for persons with disabilities. The project will provide TVET training at persons with disability-friendly training venue or project Multi-Purpose Development Centers. Project will assign persons with disabilities friendly Income Generation Activities as small entrepreneurs or wage employment.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by Plan International Bangladesh. Plan International inform people in the camps and the host community about their rights and entitlements, and participation in decisions that affect them. The courtyard session is a platform in the project where the target people express their opinion which has been included in the design of the project. The income empowers the youth, especially girls and young women and creates space for control over resources. Awareness raising on disaster preparedness is part of the project. The project ensures services from the government offices for better resilience. In addition, PIB is maintaining a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) through Comment / Feedback box, Field visit,,Focus group discussions, Community meetings, Child Safeguarding reporting structure, Via SMS, Feedback Survey Question, Feedback Form, Via Phone Call etc.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the DRM and climate change issues are embedded in the project. The target people are oriented on the preparation and recovery mechanism from the courtyard sessions. As a risk mitigation plan, the target people are linked with the Micro Finance Institutes (MFIs) for savings and loans. so that they may use it as further capital after any disaster. The project encourages environment-friendly technologies for agricultural production. As a global humanitarian organization, Plan International has a well-equipped DRR response team who are ready to respond immediately after any disaster. There are 2 warehouses of Plan equipped with Non-food items. In Teknaf, Plan has responded to the people affected by Cyclone Sitrang in 2022 and Cyclone MOCHA in 2023. The project included plantation activities along with the kitchen garden as a long-term impact on climate change. Plan International Bangladesh will assess the Climate Resilience Assessment for both host communities and refugee camp before taking the interventions on climate smart agriculture practices in consultation with Department of Agriculture Extension and Project Implementation Office, and Disaster Management Committees at union and Upazila level.
