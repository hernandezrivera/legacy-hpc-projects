Project Summary

The project aims to address the widening gap between the number of people with specific needs and the deteriorating health care services in Libya after years of conflict and economic crisis. In order to avoid a likely decline in the physical and mental health state of the most vulnerable people, HI’s intervention seeks to respond to the most urgent needs identified at individual level, while at the same time improving the quality of and access to local-level services.
The project will target people with physical and functional limitations suffering from psychological distress due to injuries/ disabilities within the conflict affected population, IDP settings and areas of return. After years of conflict in Libya, it is expecting that conflict-affected and injured persons may have now developed severe physical and functional complications as consequences of a lack of physiotherapy at hospital level during the early stages of the recovery (often linked to the lack of trained medical staff on early rehabilitation but also due to the lack of access to rehabilitation services).

Needs

The WHO estimates the number of persons with disability globally as up to 15% of the population, which represents around 970,000 persons in Libya today according to population figures. According to HI data collected during previous projects, between October 2017 and June 2018 in Misrata and Tripoli, 41% of female and 49% of male beneficiaries reported the lack of availability of services as a barrier to access physical rehabilitation and 29% of female and 37% of male beneficiaries reported a lack of financial means. During the same period, 18% of HI beneficiaries had developed functional limitations due to cerebral vascular accidents and 9% were suffering from arthritis or arthrosis (which may be the result of infection or injury to the joints). In addition, 13% of the beneficiaries’ difficulties were caused by fractures and 14% by musculoskeletal conditions (deterioration of the muscles, bones and/or joints) that can also be linked to a physical trauma. When related to access to Psychosocial or Mental Health services, 65% female and 63% of male beneficiaries mentioned the lack of services available as the main barrier, followed by the lack of information according to 33% of female and 37% of male beneficiaries.The overall needs of persons with disabilities and injuries in Libya can be summarized as the following:- Lack of access to essential services: persons with disabilities or injuries are often not only physically unable to access to services; they also report significant barriers to access including fear for safety and financial constraints.- Lack of availability of essential services: In particular specialized services for persons with disabilities and injuries such as Prosthetics and Orthotics and physical and functional rehabilitation. Also increasingly unavailable are established psychological or psychiatric services for persons with severe mental illness or psychological distress as a direct result of the ongoing crisis. Public health services are suffering severe shortage of trained staff, whilst the cost of private services is in itself prohibitive. - Lack of information about available services for persons with disabilities and injuries: Even despite the limited services available, there is also lack of understanding and knowledge about the specific needs of persons with disabilities and how to access them. Caregivers and family members in particular struggle with the physical and emotional burden of caring for the individual, lacking the time, energy and ability to seek out information about what assistance is available to them. The project will deliver direct, comprehensive rehabilitation services to children and adults with disabilities and persons with injuries, in particular survivors of accidents from explosive hazards, in targeted areas where health and protection needs are high. Whilst providing direction protection services in the form of psychosocial support, HI will also assess the beneficiary's wider needs in terms of protection (GBV, child protection, safety, risk of discrimination and isolation) during individualized assessments and also taking advantage of the project's outreach approach where much of the service delivery is in the home. HI will ensure to contribute to the development of referral mechanisms with other protection and health actors to ensure identified needs are addressed outside of the scope of HI's services. At the same time, the project aims to reinforce the access and availability of qualitative health and protection services in Libya. The promotion of inclusive practices among service providers, and the support to reduce access-barriers linked with gender, age and/or disability, will increase the overall access to essential services for the most vulnerable population groups.

Activities

- Identification and assessment of the most vulnerable persons with functional limitations and/or psychological distress in affected communities;- Direct provision of comprehensive rehabilitation, composing physical and functional rehabilitation and psychosocial support services and guidance to people with functional limitations due to injury or disability through home visits or at a fixed point;- Direct provision of guidance, advice and psychosocial support services to the caregivers of persons with injury or disability to improve care and coping capacity;- Direct provision of assistive devices and guidance on their use to people with functional limitations due to injury or disability and their caregivers;- Provision of external referrals of people with functional limitations due to injury and disability and/or with psychological distress, to facilitate access to basic needs and other services and to ensure continuity in the quality of care following the intervention;- Provision of basic rehabilitation equipment to supported health facilities;- Provision of awareness to health care providers on early rehabilitation and inclusion of persons with functional limitations due to injury or disability;- Provision of awareness sessions for community focal points including IDP leaders, and local and international NGO staff on the identification, inclusion and referral of vulnerable people including those with a disability or psychosocial distress;- Provision of technical support to the humanitarian community (sector or agency level) on inclusion of adults and children with disabilities into the humanitarian response.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

HI ensures consultation with targeted adults and children throughout project implementation with 2019's project designed based on consultation with current beneficiaries. During implementation of the project beneficiaries are consulted on their specific needs and an action/treatment plan is designed with HI outreach workers and the beneficiary together, to agree on the individual goals for the beneficiary during the course of the service delivery. Goals can include an increase in mobility or better copying capacities, but can also include aims to increase social participation for example. The beneficiary's individualized action plan is based on a comprehensive assessment and follow-up tool that is already in place for the project and is conducted with each beneficiary to assess both physical and functional capacities as well as needs in terms of psychosocial support. The initial assessment defines the needs of the individual whilst also enabling to monitor other needs or protection risks faced by the beneficiary throughout the course of their engagement with HI. These include measuring the perception of safety, risk of discrimination and risk of eviction of beneficiaries, the identification of GBV or child protection concerns, and if the person has other specific vulnerabilities, such as risk of isolation or if they are a single head of household. A recently implemented household assessment tool also monitors social factors impacting the family and protection concerns. HI also assesses the level of access to services for its beneficiaries through the same methodology. The project aims to remove barriers to access health/rehabilitation and PSS services for those identified as unable to reach a specific service. Through implementing a referral mechanism the project also aims to match beneficiaries with specialised service providers depending on the identified need. By the beginning of 2019, all HI's staff, partners and suppliers will have been sensitized to the organisation's new Code of Conduct, of which the PSEA policy is a central component. A dedicated human resource within the program has been identified to act as the PSEA focal point who will (following training) be assigned specific PSEA responsibilities, in close link with the Head of Mission. HI will actively seek feedback from beneficiaries at all stage of the project cycle. Different avenues will be established depending on the type and nature of feedback, with the mechanisms established to do so including a telephone number published on communication materials to contact HI for feedback/complaints, social media accounts through with beneficiaries and the local population can contact the organisation, as well as through monitoring visits conducted by the Head of Mission, Project Coordinator and Project Manager, allowing dedicated time to direct feedback from the beneficiary or benefiting community.
