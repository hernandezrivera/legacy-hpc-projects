Project Summary

The project aim at providing integrated CCCM, shelter reinforcement kit, NFI, and shelter repairs to meet the need of the most vulnerable households in need of shelter support in Maiduguri, Jere and Rann.
The project will engage the POCs with carpentry training on shelter repairs and maintenance which is aimed at providing them with livelihood support through the cash for work strategy to enable them get cash to cover their needs.
This project will target 35,000 beneficiaries for the CCCM support, Emergency Shelter construction, shelter reinforcement/emergency shelter kit and NFI kits to fill gaps in IDP camps situated in Jere.
More so, their poor living conditions does not allow any privacy and consequently even the protection of the most vulnerable (mostly single women and children) is not guaranteed.
Furthermore, The CCCM project will improve the quality of management of the women, men, boys and girls IDPs within the camp settings, monitor and assist those out of camps and some vulnerable persons in host community. The project will support systematic follow up of the IDPs and returnees new arrivals particularly vulnerable women and girls. The project seeks to provide quality services during the registration process and will ensure that reception facilities are equally accessible to women, men, boys and girls newly arrived. Camp managers will ensure proper relocation of new arrivals from the reception centres to the shelters constructed taking into account the needs of the most vulnerable. Through this project, CCCM interventions will also provide monitoring and making referrals and report on all protection gaps and needs to be addressed by different sectors in order to improve the living conditions of women, men, girls and boys displaced. The CCCM project will promote the participation of the IDPs based on age, gender and diversity in the life cycle of camps particularly care and maintenance of infrastructures, small scale drainage, etc. In case of Rann, the Reception center will be reactivated providing the basic needs of new Arrivals.

1 - Needs

SHO conducted a needs assessment in July 2019 in Maiduguri, Jere, Kala Balge LGAs to identify gaps in CCCM assistance.This assessment confirmed the increased trend of new IDPs in Borno state, as highlighted in the DTM Nigeria Round 28 assessmentpublished in August 2019. The major increase between DTM Round 27 and Round 28 was reported in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council withan additional 16,657 new IDPs registered over that period. This indicates a percentage of 6% new arrivals (from 252,217 to 277,268individuals) to Maiduguri between May and August 2019. In the month of September 2019, the total number of displaced persons trackedwere 4,982 individuals (1,392 households). The largest movement (986 individuals) was recorded on 10 September 2019, including 835departures from Gajiganna Ward - 578 to Maiduguri Metropolitan Council, and 257 to Jere LGA.Approximately 182,202 households (836,520 individuals) have been identified living in a camp like setting across 273 sites in the BAY statesaccording to DTM site assessment Round 28, out of which 138 are located in MMC and Jere. Of the 273 sites, 139 sites, all of which areinformal spontaneous sites, neither have a Site Management Agency nor any CCCM support. The majority of them are within the LGAS ofJere and Maiduguri, where 50 and 40 sites respectively are lacking any CCCM support or activities. These gaps in CCCM assistance arecompounded by the regular new arrivals of IDPs, which compounds on congestion in these sites.In addition to this, many sites are lacking basic assistance, in particular in Maiduguri and Jere. This is confirmed by the shelter DMS/CCCMmultisector tracker report released in September, highlighting that Maiduguri and Jere witness critical level of shelter gaps, includingdamaged shelters or households sharing shelters (nearly 4,500 households in Maduguri and 2,900 in Jere). Maiduguri is also the secondLGA with the highest number of households in need of NFI (11,022), whereas Jere is the LGA with more number of sites (11) withoutfunctional and accessible health facility on site or nearby. Needs are also critical in the targeted areas regarding food security assistance,with large number of families not benefitting from food distributions (nearly 6,000 in Jere and 3,000 in Maiduguri).Hence, the NRC assessment and recent reports confirm that MMC and Jere LGAs are both the areas receiving major influxes and hostingthe higher number of IDPs. In the same time MMC and Jere sites are amongst the areas where gaps in service provision are more critical.These trends are aggravated by a lack of CCCM coverage in many of those sites, which leads to uncoordinated assistance, gaps in serviceprovision and increased protection risks. In addition, the Joint Shelter Needs Assessment that was carried out in July 2019 across all IDP sites with all shelter and CCCM partners,identified a total of 97,817 households requiring shelter assistance. Households were categorized according to different priorities with thefollowing breakdown:Priority 1: Households living outside and households living in makeshift shelters made up of blankets and mosquito nets.Priority 2: Households living in un-partitioned transit shades, households living in unpartitioned pre-existing structures, and households livingin makeshift shelters made from local materials.Priority 3: Households living in partitioned pre-existing structures and households sharing with another households.The LGAs having highest shelter needs are MMC, Jere, among other locations Finally, according to the latest Site Tracker published by the CCCM/Shelter/NFI sector, there are 61,644 households that are still in need ofbasic household non-food items (NFIs).

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SHO has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. SHO will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, on CCCM activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to SHO’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. SHO will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. SHO’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable SHO to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. SHO will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, SHO's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
