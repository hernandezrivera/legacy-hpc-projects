Project Summary

In this project, Voice of the Peace will directly support the Protection Cluster framework for the HRP 2020 – specifically the GBV Sub-Cluster by: providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs through comprehensive case management to GBV survivors, other vulnerable children, persons and survivors; enhancing prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience of women, girls, GBV survivors, youth through establishment of Women and girls friendly spaces, conducting psychosocial support services (PSS), Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services, women’s rights, establishment of women’s safety committees, strengthening participation of women groups in humanitarian meetings; enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations through peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence, Income Generating Activities (IGA) and livelihood for protective purposes for persons of concern including GBV survivors, other vulnerable persons and survivors, and youth using Cash-Based interventions (CBi).
The protection needs are severe and therefore require urgent humanitarian interventions including the GBV. The degree of protection risks and women vulnerability is high.
By the end of the project, a total of 400 most venerable women, and girls especially GBV survivors, other vulnerable children, persons and survivors are expected to benefit from comprehensive GBV case management services. 20,000 women and girls are expected to benefit from GBV preventive and resilience building services and messaging. 200 women with leadership and decision-making responsibilities in community-level peace and security initiatives and 300 GBV survivors, other vulnerable persons and survivors, and youth benefits from IGA through cash-based intervention (CBi).
Voice of peace is a Protection lead in Pibor and has implemented GBV activities using funding from IOM, UNICEF and IsraAID.

1 - Needs

Jonglei and Central Equatoria states like other former states affected by the decades of conﬂicts in South Sudan, Rape and sexual violence among other GBV types have been perpetrated on women and girls during crisis yet opportunities to access basic life-saving services have been limited. The need for multi-sectoral humanitarian response to address the multiple needs of the population and scaling up of existing services to meet the growing demand of most vulnerable and conﬂict affected population therefore becomes eminent. Pibor and Pochala of former (Jonglei) State have experienced multiple displacements and insecurity due to internal conﬂicts, political rivaling within the SPLM and other factions. Key drivers of conﬂict in Jonglei, speciﬁcally in the above-mentioned location were identiﬁed as food insecurity, cattle raiding/theft for commercial purposes and rising bride prices GBV Sub-cluster assessments presentation by VOP, 21 March and April 2019), and the proliferation of guns ﬁght in Boma (IRNA, April 2019). These main drivers of conﬂict are likely to continue next year (2020). The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020 indicated that 168,971 affected people will require urgent protection needs in Pibor 100,678 in 23,755 in Pochala (OCHA, 2018). The IPC projected for the ﬁrst quarter of 2020 (January – March 2020) indicates that 20,000 people in Pibor will experience famine. In Pochala, 31,000 people will require emergency humanitarian assistance, 99,000 in Pibor. Households most at risk are those; who have been displaced due to cattle raiding, who have continuously engaged in extreme coping strategies to cover food consumption gaps (presentation by FSL in Pibor). Women have been victims of GBV due to poor shelters where women and girls sleep outside, women and girls move to bushes to look for wild fruits, collect ﬁrewood and water from river exposing them to further GBV or protection risks. Children support their mothers to hunt wild animals, ﬁshing and animal grazing, children are subjected to child labour by their families for survival. These group of affected population are stressed and have lost hope in life. All these expose the women, girls, and boys to further protection risks (IRNA, OCHA 2019). In Pibor there are rampant cases of domestic violence due to lack of basic needs as they fail to manage stress (psychological problems), cases of rape against women and girls are common as well and the perpetrators are men. The above needs overview is severe and therefore require urgent humanitarian interventions including the GBV. The degree of protection risks and women vulnerability is high. With this project, Voice of the Peace (VOP) will directly support the Protection Cluster framework for the HRP 2020 – speciﬁcally the GBV Sub-Cluster by: providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs through comprehensive case management to GBV survivors, other vulnerable children, persons and survivors; enhancing prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience of women, girls, GBV survivors, youth through Maintaining existing WGFs and establishing new Women and girls friendly spaces, conducting psychosocial support services (PSS), Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services, women’s rights, establishment of women’s safety committees, strengthening participation of women group in humanitarian meetings; enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations through peace-building/ peaceful co-existence, Income Generating Activities (IGA) and livelihood for protective purposes for persons of concern including GBV survivors, other vulnerable persons and survivors, and youth using CBI. Stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear understanding of the context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Nutrition and Education responses plan based on needs, access, safety,

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. All VOP stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the VOP program activities. Complaints are collected through paper-based system at the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within VOP mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention. VOP tries to adjust the intervention accordingly. M&E team is monitoring the community engagement through questionnaire designed to monitor safety of beneficiaries during assistance delivery and AAP. A checklist is used for the improvement of community engagement activities

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, this includes logistic and security challenges. NGO forum and OCHA is focal point for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. In addition, VOP will prioritize needs and design all activities with the project interventions in line with the identified and verified needs. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analyzed, prioritized with clear recommendations captured from the beneficiaries and later utilized to influence the intervention modalities. VOP will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear understanding of the context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Nutrition and Education responses plan based on needs, access, safety, and gap, this will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, and host community. Awareness raising by use of IEC materials for protection specific messages. Distributions on HH level for people with disabilities, chronically ill. Supporting equality through recruitment of men and women on field level activities. The project measure access of beneficiaries to assistance provided through complaint mechanism, tailored focus group discussions with women groups, youth groups, people with disability, elderly and other community members. Protection specific data collection tools are used by the monitoring team to gather valid information for lessons learnt and future recommendations.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. Project activities will be sustainable by training and engaging the community-based protection network members from each community to support GBV prevention and response during project implementation. These groups will be encouraged to continue GBV prevention and response activities they are trained on after the project ends. VOP will ensure that all trainings cover ways to continue activities using locally available resources. VOP is a static partner in the project locations, with a continuous presence in Pibor County. VOP staff remaining in Pibor will also help monitor and provide guidance on the continuation of activities after the funding period is over. VOP will also link the activities to available health facilities/programs being managed by other static partners to help sustain the GBV prevention and response momentum. As VOP is a project- based organization, the Executive Director will continue to liaise with other donors for more funding to continue protection activities in Pibor County. We are committed to working with communities and the local leaders, we believe will have a long-term impact in VOP ability to communicate with the communities beyond the project phase and receive updates from the already established community-based protection network. Reach up to 20+ Payams within three location and engage the communities at a grassroots level with the potential of reaching out up to over 100,000 women with a message of GBV prevention and responds. The community will they own the projectIt will provide a stepping stone for further and future cohesion of different community to interact and initiate risk mitigation. VOP will at the end of project will inform GBV if there is still need to continue intervention for another one year.
