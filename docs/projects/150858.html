Project Summary

The project will focus on improving the physical protection and dignity of IDPs, refugee returnees and poor host communities across Somalia through the provision of locally purchased Non Food Items (NFIs) and Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) through cash/voucher/in-kind modalities, construction of Semi-permanent and Permanent Shelters through community driven or owner driven approaches, settlement (re)planning training to reduce congestion and overcrowding, conducting Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) exercises for Shelters and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercises for NFIs & ESKs and participation in infrastructure mapping exercises led by Shelter cluster or any other partner.
For the beneficiaries’ selection and registration, NRC will use an electronic mobile data collection tool called Kobo tool box, which provides an accurate and real time data. The data received through Kobo is easily manageable and transferable. 20% to 30% Poor host community members will also be selected to ensure 'DO NO HARM' principle ensuring other humanitarian principles including humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. For the provision of NFIs, each HH will be provided with a cash of $100 as per cluster agreed standard NFI kit while for the provision of ESKs, each HH will be provided with a cash of $145 (70% or $101 as first installment and remaining 30% or $44 as second installment) as per cluster approved ESK design. NRC will acquire the services of local telecom companies (viz. Hurmuud telecom in South and Central Somalia and Somtel & Golis in Puntland and Somaliland) for the cash transfers. NRC will provide a complete list of selected beneficiaries to respective telecom companies for their cross checking purposes. This list will include all details of selected beneficiaries including their telephone numbers and token numbers. After reviewing and cross checking, respective telecom company will transfer specified amount of money to selected beneficiaries through mobile transfer facility.
Beneficiaries will be selected through predetermined beneficiary selection criteria prioritizing the Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) including female and single headed HHs, orphans, chronically ill, aged and child headed households. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data. NRC will target total 130,000 individuals (50% females) consisting of 45,000 in South Central, 45,000 in Jubbaland, 30,000 in Puntland and 10,000 in Somaliland through different Shelter interventions throughout the year 2019.
NRC staff will ensure that the provided cash has been utilized for intended purpose by coordinating with the beneficiaries themselves and conduct follow ups with the support of the community leaders, local authorities and the relevant clusters. A Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercises for NFIs and ESKs and Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) exercises for semi-permanent and permanent Shelters will be conducted to analyse outcomes, the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction and draw lessons learnt for future programming.

Needs

Displacement in Somalia due is not a new phenomenon. In January 2016, more than 1.1 million people were already living in protracted displacement, including those who have been affected by drought crisis in 2011 and have not return home. Since then, Somalia has seen additional displacements of an estimated 1.5 million people who have left their homes primarily because of drought but also insecurity. To date, the country has an estimated 2.6 million people displaced. Armed groups still control much of the countryside. Years of conflict have weakened government authority, and the federal and state governments have not always been able to guarantee people’s security and rights. The settlements that people move to, are largely spontaneous (79%), rather than formally managed. Many are overcrowded and, in several cases, insecure. IDPs are vulnerable to protection threats, and sexual violence is very common, making women and girls most vulnerable. In newly displaced setting, most IDPs, who are often running away from violence, it is observed that they are not able to carry their household possessions/assets. As a result, they will continue to be in need of emergency assistance, including basic household assets, and emergency shelter that can lead to quick settlement and coping in the newly settled environment. In protracted IDPs situations, settlements are poor and suffer from overcrowding and congestion, lack basic services and expose most residents to the risk of fires especially during the dry and windy seasons. Besides, most of the shelters are made of poor materials- combustible recycled cartons, plastic sheets and cloth, making them further vulnerable to fire and burglary. Returnees are in need of secure and durable shelters with sufficient safeguards against forced eviction. Vulnerable Host community are equally destitute and in need of the same basic services and support. Besides the influx of IDPs often adds pressure to existing services.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In Somalia, the local authorities have shown willingness to donate land for refugee returnees and IDPs willing to locally integrate. This has been achieved in many areas of Somalia such as Kismayo, Garowe, Galkayo, Bossaso, Baidoa, Dhusamareeb and Burao. This land is accompanied with permanent title deeds for full ownership in household level. This has created space and opportunity for the humanitarian actors to participate in the implementation of durable solutions in Somalia. Therefore, NRC contributing to this objective, will construct permanent shelters and infrastructures in these locations through proper settlement planning. Furthermore, through construction of semi-permanent (transitional) Shelters and rehabilitation of Caarish Shelters, shelter projects will focus on enabling communities to withstand at least 2 subsequent weak rainy seasons without major external intervention or wide-scale displacement, while building towards the achievement of durable solutions for displacement affected population. Similarly, NRC's Shelter projects will be integrated with NRC's other sectors (WASH, LFS, CCCM, Education and protection) and will adopt area based integrated programming to have maximum impact and have more sustainability, resilience and durability. NRC will also advocate through Shelter cluster, other available coordination mechanisms and local authorities to mobilize different organizations to work in the same area to achieve the goal of integration, resilience and durable solutions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The principle to be adopted for this project will address critical protection concerns with increasing displacement towards IDPs sites and collective-centers, including heightened protection risks/threats that have emerged in the failure to end displacement through appropriate solutions (local integration, return, and settlement elsewhere). The targeting criteria will include areas that experience high levels of migration due to recurrent droughts as per PRMN displacement data. This will be used to determine district vulnerability aligned with the objective to target rural locations to increase resilience and prevent future displacement. This will be balanced out with criteria relating to good programming opportunities (potential for return, relatively continuous access)

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’s empowerment and eradicating violence against women, recognizing that without institutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilize and develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear an unequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-based culture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbated by cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. Women with disabilities are often marginalized on account of their disability and their gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion, and often have limited social, political and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination, for example in education and family life. This project will ensure engagement of people, especially women with disabilities, and the elderly at the co-management level and will ensure that programs interventions do not generate negative side-effects. These populations will be involved in project management at community level to ensure that their resilience is built in ways adapted to their specific needs and capacities and that their access to basic services is prioritizedWomen will be give special preference in site/settlement planning training, NFIs and ESKs distribution processes to enhance their skills, where needed, referral mechanism will be used at different forums and at cluster level so that principles of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) are ensured.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In a rural context, communities or groups are either forced into temporary displacement because of climatic shocks or conflict, or might join urban centers in search of better access to humanitarian aid (pull factor), access to services and economic opportunities. During slow onset and long-tail crisis such as the recurrent drought and conflict, the same communities may partially displace to neighboring towns. Since all the Shelter interventions will be channeled through cluster approach and involvement of relevant local authorities, thus the impact of armed conflict and violence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy at national and international level, media and referral mechanisms.
