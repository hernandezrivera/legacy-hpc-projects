Project Summary

SI’s presence in Aleppo Governorate’s territory since 2012 allowed the organization to build a robust knowledge and credibility within the local communities to be able to analyze and respond to the evolution of needs in the area. SI long term presence and assistance to the population of the area built its knowledge of the context and the needs; taking into account the constraints of a critical moment where humanitarian needs are high in the region but access difficulties make humanitarian interventions more and more challenging to operate, SI intervention targets IDPs with adequate access to a comprehensive package of WASH activities by ensuring the continuation of its lifesaving services in 7 IDP settlements in A’zaz district (Karaj Sijjo, Ehtemlat, Dabiq, Yazibagh Al Jabal, Al Mokawama, Al Resala and Al Nour).
SI’s package will include: supply of safe water to target camps in adequate quantity through water trucking; water quality insurance through treatment at water truck and monitoring at water point, truck and household level to ensure water quality, according to standards; maintenance of gender appropriate distribution points for timely and protective water collection; provision of waste removal and sanitation facilities cleaning and maintenance services, along with HP awareness for the entire population of the target camps.
While it is imperative to continue emergency support to IDPs with WASH essential services, SI is also aiming to transition its assistance modality to conflict-affected populations with durable water supply solutions. Having established a cost-efficiency analysis for each camp, SI will provide sustainable WASH infrastructure allowing for an exit of the emergency-oriented strategy for WASH needs in the camps where assessed relevant (Karaj Sijjo, Ehtemlat, Yazibagh Al Jabal and Al Mokawama). Infrastructure will focus on water supply and sanitation through networks, sanitation facilities, and the creation and training of community WASH committees (CWC) to insure continuity of operation and maintenance as well as hygiene promotion. A continuity of services in the camps where the implementation of such an exit strategy is not cost-efficient (Al Resala, Dabiq and Al Nour) will also be provided. SI will monitor all along the project the capacity and functionality CWCs. This will be complemented by an external evaluation enabling to state on the capacity of the CWC to be autonomous to inform on and develop the need of additional if need be.
SI proposed intervention will enable addressing an estimated total population of 40,000 individuals.

Needs

The needs analysis is based on various Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) surveys in IDPs settlements in Azaz district. These surveys are complemented through triangulation with secondary sources (CCCM displacement tracking) and existing knowledge and experience in the area (direct observations, KIIs with local authorities and interviews with IDPs families). In all the IDP settlements supported by SI, needs remain high. Potable water supply, food, primary health care, shelter and other daily essential items are scarce or inaccessible forcing an already deprived population to remain vulnerable and dependent on humanitarian assistance in the long term. The HNO 2019, reports 990,494 people in acute need of WASH assistance in the governorate of Aleppo, 354,724 for the districts of Azaz and Afrin together. REACH’s assessment of the area showed that in A’zaz district the majority of respondents reported having insufficient amount of water to cover household needs. These communities reported primarily coping with the lack of water by modifying hygiene practices and reducing drinking water consumption. Access to water is constrained due to the destruction or dysfunction of key water plants and their supply through water networks, resulting in increased demand and cost for water trucking. Moreover, waves of IDPs fleeing from cities to informal settlements in Aleppo countryside, found conditions that were lacking social-economic development in terms of access to water networks. Thus, populations relying on water trucking for water supply increased drastically. Alternate water supply services and WASH supplies are available but they are relatively expensive, pushing vulnerable families to adopt unsafe coping strategies. These sources are also often found unsafe in terms of water quality. According to the SI KAP survey, hygiene and sanitation practices, even if well-known and operative among 90% of the population before the conflict, are now challenged by lack of means. Soap is available in just over half of households (55%) and a dependence on hygiene kit distributions for primary hygiene items has been identified. While the majority of the population stated to wash their hands on a daily basis, knowledge of the critical handwashing moments resulted relatively low, with episodes of diarrhea among children and infants. Concerning sanitation practices, communities reported to regularly use dustbins for their garbage, but without a public system of solid waste management disposal in place (except for SI activities), the majority of targeted people still rely on negative coping strategies such as burning garbage. Furthermore, relatively high levels of open defecation (34%) and the presence of stagnant water were observed in some areas. Currently, SI water trucking is the main source of water (drinking and non-drinking water) for the target settlements, as well as sanitation and cleaning activities are the only services available in the area. Nevertheless, continuous needs are arising according to seasonal constraints and new arrivals that are expected due to the context scenarios related to Tal Refaat and Idleb Governorate illustrated above.

Implementation of activities

SI will aim to contribute to WASH Cluster Sector Objectives 1 and 2, transitioning progressively from emergency-oriented assistance to support to community-based sustainable WASH services.SI will continue ensuring daily water provision in targeted camps through locally sub-contracted water trucking (activity 2.2.), while it will remain responsible for water quality monitoring and ensuring sector standards are met. Water is collected from local sources, treated and distributed at household or community storage facilities. SI monitors quality at truck, distribution and household levels. SI will carry out waste removal and waste management activities as well as desludging and maintenance of the sanitation facilities – latrines and hand washing stations as required (activity 2.2). These will be progressively transited to community-based initiatives accompanied by a range of hygiene promotion activities (activity 2.1). The involvement of the beneficiaries will be ensured through community hygiene promoters from each camp (both men and women will be recruited), who will implement hygiene promotion activities with the support of SI staff. Hygiene promotion will include separate sessions for men, women, and children under 10. Additional efforts will be made to arrange hygiene promotion sessions at times suitable for men to attend, as they often work outside the camps during the day. In parallel, SI will support the rehabilitation and extension of water networks, so as to reduce the dependence on water trucking in A'zaz District, and at the same time improve the facilities and infrastructure damaged by the conflict. This will also facilitate the provision of water closer to users, reducing the needs of water storage and protection-related risks. Building water networks in established camps will improve the cost effectiveness of the intervention while supporting the community and reducing the needs of transportation of water from the water points to the dwellings. This will reduce the burden of water collection on women and girls, who are usually have responsibility for such activities. Different groups within the community, especially men, women, elderly and people with disabilities will be consulted on the design of the network.The camps will undergo the construction of a sewage network, connected to the main sewage network of the nearest village for the villages, where the communal network is connected to a treatment station. The camp sewage network will be branched by SI to collect waste water from all the tents/caravans/houses. To ensure programme sustainability and in the line of SI’s exit strategy, the Operation and Maintenance (O&M) of the WaSH facilities is compulsory. Therefore, SI designed a strategy to combine the "contractor approach" and "community mobilization." Major construction and repairs requiring heavy work will be performed by contractors under the close supervision of SI teams. With the Community WaSH Committees (CWC) approach, responsibility for maintenance and small repairs will be transferred to the community, increasing ownership and autonomy.

Protection Risk Analysis

All activities will take into consideration protection risk mitigation throughout the project by ensuring that SPHERE standards are respected. SI always makes sure to include protection mainstreaming throughout the implementation of its projects by strictly respecting Do No Harm principles. Prior to the implementation of projects, SI staff are trained in protection, disability awareness and inclusion as well as gender equity which will enable and support the inclusion of all gender and vulnerability groups throughout the project.Protection risk: The risk of GBV can be compounded when sanitation facilities are located far from the dwellings, when there is no proper segregation between male and female facilities, or when there is no lights which restrain the access to the facilities at night. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1) Consultation and inclusion of women, adolescent girls and children when designing, implementing, monitoring and evaluating the projects; 2) Monitoring of the facilities to make sure of the respect of the gender separation and the functioning of lights; 3) Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up.Protection risk: If the sanitation facilities constructed are not culturally or socially appropriate, or do not meet the needs of people with disabilities, the risk is that some members of the communities will not use the facilities, developing then negative coping mechanisms such as open defecation which represents a risk.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1) Consultation and inclusion of each group from the community when designing, implementing and monitoring the projects to understand the cultural and social norms, as well as the special needs of the population; 2) Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up.Protection risk: The risk of inappropriate content of hygiene kits which do not consider the gender of beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1) To consult each group of the community to make sure to understand the needs of everyone, especially women and girls 2) To harmonize and standardize gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use.Protection risk: Distributions unfairly exclude some members of the community, or community members are put at risk by the organisation of the distributions. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1) Consultation and inclusion of each group from the community to ensure that distributions are scheduled for times and locations that does not unfairly exclude certain groups. 2) SI will distribute to no more than 250 households per day in order to minimize crowds of people in one location. 3) Throughout the distribution process SI’s MEAL team will be present to ensure equitable distribution and prevent risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries.Protection risk: When rehabilitating or constructing WASH infrastructures, there is a risk of theft of goods such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by groups and used in conflict activities.Likelihood: Low Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1) Information sessions on the purpose and benefits for the community of the rehabilitations/constructions in order to promote community ownership; 2) MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Administrative:Risks:Since 2017, a majority of NGOs face administrative restrictions in Turkey with a delay of registration renewals. This, added to restrictions for international staff visas, had negative impact on turkey based NGO staff access to the “Euphrates shield” area and pushed several major agencies to leave Turkey and scale down their operations in North Aleppo. In parallel, GoT through AFAD has been increasing its control over Humanitarian assistance in the area. This could become a major threat to humanitarian access and the humanitarian principled assistance even if so far SI has been able to operate remotely from Jordan. Mitigation measures:Remote management, establishment of alternative solutions for supplyActive Acceptance measures: Compliance with administrative regulations, Promotion of Humanitarian principles, Coordinated AdvocacyPassive acceptance: Ensure quality of interventions Military/conflictRisks:Tensions between armed groups of the free Syrian Army may trigger armed clashes (ground, aerial) which would be a threat for SI staff (could be “in the wrong place at the wrong time”).
