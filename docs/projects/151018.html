Project Summary

This project aims to increase access to sustainable safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene education for drought affected communities and areas that have extremely low water and or sanitation coverage in Somalia. In line with the Somalia Resilience Recovery Framework, long-term displaced populations in prioritized locations in Bosaaso, Garowe, Galkacyo, Qardho, Kismayo, Baidoa and Dollow will be provided with durable solutions to WASH services where land is availed by the government. Project also targets both IDPs and host communities in Kismayo, Baydhaba, Qansax Dheere, Buur Hakaba, Madina, Deynile, Hodan and Darkenley districts districts as these populations remain at high risk of AWD/Cholera outbreak due to limited and overstretched water and sanitation facilities resulting from continued influx and subsequent overcrowding in IDP settlements. In rural areas, use of unprotected water sources, open defecation and failure to wash hands with soap at critical times contribute largely to frequent AWD/Cholera outbreaks. Access to safe water will be increased through construction and or rehabilitation of water sources including 7 boreholes(2 new), 26 shallow wells and 2 river water intake system with water treatment, 2 desalination system using existing sources, and extension of piped water network. To enhance quality of output new borehole construction will be preceded by hydrogeological survey undertaken by competent hydrogeologist and once drilled, pumping test will be undertaken to design appropriate pumping equipment to ensure safe abstraction of the ground water. All new boreholes will be installed with piezometric tube and main water meter to allow periodic monitoring of the aquifer level and abstraction volume respectively. Project will also support the government in development or strengthening of water policy and water law to safeguard the natural resource. River water intakes will be preceded by study of the hydrology to define safe sustainable abstraction. In all cases, an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) whose scope will vary by type and scale of water source will be undertaken for new water sources to define and implement environmental mitigation measures for negative impacts. All water systems will be equipped with elevated water tanks, distribution systems with communal tap stands or household connections when possible and hybrid energization systems. Water infrastructure will be designed appropriately and in line with existing standard designs. Pipeline extensions will also be made where pipeline exists to increase access to safe water. Taps stand will be located within a radius of at least 500m to reduce distance and enhance security of women and girls who often collect water for the household. Project will also construct 2 major improved water dams in locations that lack other options. To ensure safety of the water, the dams will be installed with protected infiltration well where treatment of water will take place and water drawn using solar powered system. As DRR measure, all well heads will be raised at a minimum 0.5m to reduce risk flooding and contamination. Design of infrastructure will take into consideration the needs of special groups in the community including the disables, old, children to ensure they have equitable access to the services. To enhance sustainability, women and men will be involved in identification of permanent solutions, design and implementation of the activities. For mini water supply systems at village or settlement level, water committees will be trained on management, operation and maintenance through sales of water at affordable prices. Committees will comprise of both men and women from all community groups including the minority and the marginalized for equal representation. Local technicians will be trained and provided with O&M tools to undertake repairs whereas consumers will pay for the technician’s services. For major water supplies including river water intake with treatment plant, desalination systems linked to existing boreholes and boreholes water supply in urban areas, Private Public Parternship (PPP) management system will be adopted for effective management and possible expansion of systems in future.
Adequate sanitation in 10 rural villages will be provided through construction of sanitation facilities, using Community Led Total Sanitation approach when feasible. Project will construct 300 latrines for families reintegrated through the durable solutions. NRC has developed a water safety plan and hence undertakes sanitary surveys periodically to identify contamination risks for timely mitigation. All latrine will be installed with handwashing equipment and community sensitized to use soap for handwashing. Design of latrines will take into account the needs of special groups such as the physically challenged hence depending on needs some will have a smooth ramp, handle and or side railbars, toilet seat and adequate in size.
Life-saving WASH interventions will be implemented in case of sudden disaster including floods, displacement leading to overcrowding in locations that lack basic WASH services and outbreak of water-related diseases to reduce the related mortality and morbidity especially among children. This will be through temporary provision of safe water through direct delivery or restricted cash depending on context and market functionality, provision of hygiene kits through cash or in-kind, household water treatment and/or point of use water treatment to 6000 hhs, construction of 700 simple design latrines, latrine emptying (600 latrines) and safe fecal sludge disposal and hygiene promotion. Schools and health facilities will be provided with adequate water and sanitation facilities including private space for menstrual hygiene (100 institutional latrines) while hygiene promotion will be through group practicing on good hygiene behavior. As a DRR measure, all latrines will be raised above ground level to reduce the risk of flooding during rains. Women and girls will be involved in identification of sites for facilities to ensure their safety and security is taken into account. Latrines will be gender separated, provided with lockable doors and handwashing equipment. Solar street lights (30 posts) will be provided near water points and latrines to improve the security and safety of women and girls especially in crowded IDP settlements. Throughout the implementation period the beneficiaries will be provided with adequate information on NRC’s complaints, response and feedback (CRFM) mechanisms to enable them channel complaints or share feedback to be incorporate as necessary.

Needs

Populations affected persistently by recurrent severe drought, those affected by floods in April/May 2018 and the IDPs living in overcrowded and WASH resources stretched settlements remain in dire need of support. JMCNA reports widespread severe WASH scarcity where majority of HHs are not able to access the minimum amount of water at 15l/p/d as Nugaal (79%), Sool (66%), Sanaag (62%), Mudug (53%) and Gedo (49%). Only 7% of hhs in Bay and 8% in Galgaduud access more than 15l/p/d. Majority rely on unprotected water sources. Distance to water sources is far especially in rural areas. IDPs in Bosaaso, Garowe, Galkacyo, Qardho, Dollow, Kismayo, Baidoa, and Mogadishu districts remain at high risk of AWD/Cholera outbreak. Use of unprotected water sources, open defecation and failure to wash hands with soap at critical times contributes largely to frequent AWD/Cholera outbreaks. Water scarcity is as result of: limited permanent safe water sources due to poor ground water quantity and quality; poor management of water sources leading to malfunction; low local technical capacity to operate and maintain water sources; poorly designed infrastructure. NRC’s assessment in Banadir, Baidoa and Togdheer in June 2018 indicates: 50% of the population do not have access to a latrine and those that have share at minimum 1:50 people. 70% surveyed rarely have soap. Importance of handwashing with soap is unknown to 65%. Most latrines in IDP camps are in filled up, unhygienic and a risk to women and girls as they are not gender segregated, some have unlockable doors and are dark at night. To address this needs, project aims to construct/rehabilitate water sources and establish sustainable management structures, undertake sanitary surveys, desludge latrines, facilitate construction of latrines using CLTS (rural), increase knowledge in good hygiene thus reduce AWD/Cholera as well as address protection risks that women and girls continue to face as they access the WASH facilities.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Project will contribute the durable solutions efforts particularly in Kismayo, Garowe, Galkayo, Bossaso, Baidoa, Dhusamareeb and Burao where the government has shown keen interest and provided land to settle the many displaced people. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many IDPs reintegrated. Through various donors including DFID, UNHCR and NMFA, NRC has been at the forefront of this agenda and has supported thousands of displaced people acquire land and issued them with land title documents. In the target locations, the IDP are protracted and have therefore intermingled with the host community over time and established positive cohesion. Project contributes significantly to the Resilience Recover Framework and the National Development plan which envisions "Adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation for all". Project contributes to achievement of the NDP 4 of the 6 NDP WASH targets whereby it will: • Increase equitable access to safe and affordable drinking water for 111,500 individuals• Increase access to gender sensitive WASH facilities with menstrual management facilities for 7 schools • Increase the percentage of people living in open defecation free environment• Increase the use of hand-washing practice with water and soap Water infrastructure will be designed taking into account environmental sustainability thus safe abstraction of ground and surface water. New water sources will be installed with equipment to monitor abstraction at the sources. Water systems serving large population will preferably be managed through private public partnerships to enhance functional sustainability thus long term solution. Mini water supply system at village level will be managed by water management committees to be selected by the community and trained by NRC’s competent WASH staff. All water supply systems will be designed, implemented and periodically monitored jointly with the beneficiary community and the government authorities. Once completed, the facilities will be handed over formally to the community and the government and a certificate of hand-over signed by all parties. Construction of latrines through the community driven approach, CLTS, leads to immediate ownership and sustainability of the facilities. It is envisaged that promotion for use of hygiene items through provision of restricted cash will instill a culture of purchase of this vital items amongst the beneficiaries and also support the market chain that can run even after the project ends.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Under or non-representation of minority or marginalized communities in consultative meetings or amongst WASH community structures Likelihood: High; Impact: highMitigation Measures: • Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all• Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among households clusters. • Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be, outside the settlements.• Condition implementation to an equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepersRisk: Forced Evictions and Government unwillingness to provide land for sustainable infrastructure development for the displacedLikelihood: highImpact: High Mitigation measures: • Align project to government’s resilience recovery agenda to gain support and sustainability for project• Advocate for legal rights of the IDPs in relation to the due process to be followed to relocate with dignity• Sensitize and support the government on developing policies and enforcing laws on land to protect the displaced

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: gender-based violence and discrimination against women and girls as they access facilities Likelihood: HighImpact: Very highMitigation: - Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety- Ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. - Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement- Install lights near communal WASH facilities- Where possible avoid central distribution on hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneficiary to purchase items at own convenient time- Construct gender separated latrines, lockable from inside, and add lighting - Increase number of water points to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness- Avoid community tensions, by providing the host community with access to WASH where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic WASH services and facilities.- Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities Risk: Rigid infrastructure that is difficult to be used by persons with reduced mobility (eg. persons with physical disability, older persons)Likelihood: mediumImpact: mediumMitigation: - Make infrastructure adaptions to WASH structures to make them accessible to this category of people (proportion of latrines with ramp and side rails, handlebars, toilet seats, - Ensure that beneficiaries know about WASH services, how to obtain hygiene kits, where and how much they cost

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: limited access to affected communities and or destruction of WASH infrastructure through armed conflictLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: Most areas are accessible to NRC staff and there hasn’t been cases of vandalism of infrastructure. However, in armed conflict, this remains to be a risk. - Conduct security risk assessment in target locations and follow security Standard Operation Procedures to ensure staff and beneficiaries remain safe- Conduct in depth consultations to understand factors that could pose security risk to beneficiaries, e.g unwanted visibility on WASH facilities - For extremely hard to reach areas that are inaccessible to internationals, implement project through local partners with supervision by NRC’s national staff hailing from the target locations
