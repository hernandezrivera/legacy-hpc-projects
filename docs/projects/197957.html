Project Summary

The project will provide access to quality, survivor-centered service to women and girls in host communities, IPDs settlements and hard to reach and underserved conflict affected communities focusing on provision of life saving GBV specialize service to address the negative impact of conflict and economic crisis on women and adolescent girls by promoting their access to humanitarian assistance (Provision of Case management, PSS, Dignity kits, Referrals & Safe spaces), and reducing their vulnerability and building their resilience (Skills development, Economic empowerment & Livelihood).
The project aims to promote access to integrated GBV prevention and response services to conflict affected communities in Damaturu, Gujba Geidam and Bade LGAs of Yobe state. The project will achieve this using integrated community-based and survivor centred approach through direct and indirect interventions such as establishment of 2 Dignity Kits production centres in (Damaturu and Bade) and Establiment/Renovation safe spaces in 4 underserves areas, production of Dignity kits and distribution to women and girls of reproductive age, Sikks acquisation on production and packaging of dignity kits, economic empowerment and livelihood support to GBV survivors, GBV case management, PSS and referrals, capacity strengthening for service providers and government agencies, Community system strengthening for PSEA including provision of tool free line for access to information on GBV service, and complain and feedback mechanism. GBV prevention through reporting, behavior and communication change (BCC) through Peer2Peer community sensitization, and support for LGA level GBV partners coordination meeting.
Strategically, through collaborative efforts of relevant government/sectoral partners, women and girls from informal settlement and host communities will be trained safety and GBV risk mitigation through access to space spaces and adaptive business skills that are relevant for income generation, local markets and Voluntary Savings and Loans, including self-employment during crisis situations.
Additionally, The project will intervein in Damaturu, Gujba, Geidam and Bade in Yobe State focusing on women and girls from informal settlement and host communities aiming to reach GBV survivors, marginalized and vulnerable women female-headed households, single women, widows, female adolescent without a family and women with physical disabilities, etc. Other beneficiaries include; community members including local leaders, young people, men and boys and duty holders will also be part of the intervention through sensitization activities among others.
Furthermore, the project will support institutional strengthening for enhanced GBV service provision through capacity building for partners on GVBiE, GBV mainstreaming, WGSS, OSC, and MPSS. AFRYDEV will further support operationalization of MPSS centre at the State Specialist Hopsiptal by engaging service of MPSS providers to address gap in shortage of manpower. The project will also support community system strengthening for GBV prevention through reporting, Peer2Peer community sensitization to promote behavior and communication change (BCC), and local government level GBV coordination. AFRYDEV will put in place flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in areas prompt to flooding in Geidam and Bade that are badly affected by flood to mitigate worsening of humanitarian crisis in this areas.
The project will ensure context specific approach to the production and distribution of dignity kits through standardization of the dignity kits and at the same time ensure localization of the contents by patronizing the local markets. The project will ensure mainstreaming and integration by providing linkages to other protection sector interventions such as NFIs, WASH and ER&L for a more holistic response.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in NE Nigeria is now in its twelfth year, and an increase in violent attacks over the past year has resulted in further deterioration of the security situation causing mass displacement and worsening of humanitarian needs. The Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2022 estimated about 8,4million people need assistance across the BAY states while the report indicated that gender based violence (GBV) continues to be a major protection concern facing IDP women and girls in north-east Nigeria. The HNO /PIN 2023 estimated about 4,572,018 need protection assistance in the Northeast while According to the GBV SS Strategy documents, about 1,829,002 people are in need of GBV services in the Northeast out of which 337,341 in Yobe State . The protracted nature of the conflict continues to displace millions of people, the majority of whom are women and girls. Many of them find themselves trapped in a vicious cycle of violence including sexual or gender-based violence (SGBV), exacerbated by pre-existing gender inequalities. Incidences of GBV, especially intimate partner violence including sexual violence, continue to be reported across the BAY states. There are limited prevention mechanisms and response services to address GBV and sexual and reproductive health for women and girls, including mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Analysis of MSNA data indicates that a large percentage (78%) of households with GBV survivors lack access to psychosocial support services. There is also a huge need for legal services for women, girls, boys and men affected by GBV (of whom 65% reported needing such services, per the HNO) and a similar percentage (61%) of women and girls who reported needing safe spaces within their community. An estimated 1.3 million individuals need GBV prevention and response services across the BAY states in 2022 (of whom 46% are IDPs, 23% returnees, and 31% host communities; 82% are women or girls). The majority (63%) of those in need are in Borno State, 22% in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe.The GBV severity ranking analysis for the GBV AoR to calculate the number of people targeted in each state and LGA to about 1,030,000 people (46% IDPs, 23% returnees, 31% host communities; overall 82% are women or girls) with GBV prevention and response interventions in 51 LGAs (25 in Borno, 15 in Yobe and 12 in Adamawa) with the severest needs, as informed by the JIAF and other analyses from the GBV Information Management System. This indicated majority (63%) of those to be targeted are in Borno State, 22% are in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. As highlighted by the GBV Information Management System data, GBV continues to be a major protection concern for women and girls in north-east Nigeria with at least 1% affected people with disability, thus the reason why they remain the main target. As previously mentioned, vulnerable people at risk including women and adolescent boys and girls have been forced to start or increase their wage-earning works at an earlier age to cope with economic instability. Adolescent boys and girls, and vulnerable women are also reported increasing pressures to adopt negative coping strategies, with 13.4% of adolescents reporting that they had engaged in at least one negative coping strategy at some point since the start of 2020. Common harmful coping mechanisms among adolescent boys include begging, stealing, and drug abuse, and among adolescent girls harmful coping mechanisms include begging, stealing, engaging in sex for survival, and dropping out of school. Adopting harmful coping strategies has increased safety and protection risks (Nigeria Rapid Gender Analysis 2022) Women and Adolescent girls focus groups reported facing risks such as exploitation, abuse, and sexual violence because of the overlapping crises. To manage rising food insecurity, women and girls reported adopting harmful coping strategies such as reducing their number of meals (70.2%), limiting meal portion size (70.8%).

2 - Activities

To achieve the objectives of this project, AFRYDEV will support provision of GBV daily case management PSS and referral for specialized service to survivors of GBV in Damaturu, Gujba, Geidam and Bade LGAs through engagement of case worker at the women girls safe spaces as well as working collaboratively with general hospitals and Primary health care for PSS service for survivors. AFRYDEV will establish 2 Dignity Kits production centers in (Damaturu and Bade) LGAs and equip the center to support empowerment and women and girls with skills acquisition on tailoring/production reusable menstrual pad and other content of dignity kits, and knowledge of GBV including risk mitigation, safety and security. AFRYDEV will further support renovation 4 women and girls safe space in hard to reach and underserved communities of Damaturu, Gujba, Geidam and Bade LGAs. AFRYDEV will also conduct capacity building strengthening for Health care workers on provision of MPSS to women and adolescent girls survivors. AFRYDEV will collaborated with Ministry of Health and State Primary Health care Board to train 20 health workers from SARC center and Trauma Unit in the two LGAs on MPSS and GBV Case Management. AFRYDEV will conduct Training of government officials and humanitarian partners/women led organizations on GBV Sector Coordination, including GBV sector core functions, SOPs and referral pathways, while will further support update, validation and dissemination GBV referral pathways. AFRYDEV will support training of 20 women and girls survivors on toiloring and production of dignity kits, and facilitate production and distribution of 500 dignity kits to women and adolescent girls. AFRYDEV will conduct safety and audit vulnerability assessment to inform the development of flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in Geidam and Bade LGAs. AFRYDEV will support economic empowerment of 20 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivors and provide them with training and start up materials and mentorship on Voluntary Savings and Loan (VSL) including access to market. AFRYDEV will provide 40 stakeholders with training on Community system strengthening for PSEA, and GBV prevention through reporting. AFRYDEV will conduct Community outreach/sensitization on the principles of GBV prevention that address negative social norms to promote behavior and communication change (BCC) through Peer2Peer community sensitization, and Awaereness on PSEA. AFRYDEV will further support for LGA level GBV partners coordination meeting to strengthen GBV prevention and response efforts. AFRYDEV will collaborate with the Yobe State Justice Reform team to conduct 3 days familiarization workshop to government and GBV partners in Damaturu on VAAP Act and Child Protection law to strengthen access to justice for women and girls survivors. AFRYDEV will work collaboratively with FIDA and the Criminal Investigation Department and Gender unit of the Nigerian Police force to provide escort support fund to GBV Survivors for transport from remote areas to health facility, police facility for investigation as well as court for legal proceedings. AFRYDEV will also conduct 1 days women summit to commemorate World menstrual hygiene day and 3 days event to commemorate the 2023 16days of activism.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Fundamental to the operation of African Youths for Peace Development and Empowerment Foundation is respect for the dignity and basic human rights of people within Nigeria and throughout the world. AFRYDEV has a PSEA policy it ensure strict adherence. A robust communication strategy has been pur in place ensuring every person who represents AFRYDEV reflect the organization principles and values in their professional conduct, regardless of whom they are dealing with, or where they are working. AFRYDEV PSEA policy aims to provide a safe and trusted environment that safeguards everyone from SEA including beneficiary communities, workers, program participants and partner organizations. AFRYDEV is committed to safeguarding the people it helps and who it works alongside. We recognize that the nature of AFRYDEV’s work places our workers and program participants in positions of authority and trust in relation to the communities we work with, especially vulnerable adults and children. AFRYDEV workers and program participants have an obligation to uphold high standards of personal and professional conduct at all times and must not abuse this position in order to exploit or abuse another person. AFRYDEV will actively prevent and respond to SEA and maintain an organizational culture that prioritizes safeguarding against SEA so that it is safe for those affected to come forward and report incidents and concerns with the assurance they will be handled sensitively and appropriately.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AFRYDEV has a strong Feedback & Complaint mechanism that ensures Capitalization of information coming from the beneficiaries is taken into account during project implementation in order to adapt the action based on the new knowledge acquired. Project activities are tailored to meet the specific needs of all groups (gender/age/people with disabilities) and M&E system is designed to include the perspectives of these groups, to examine the specific issues in the local context and to determine how interventions have a different impact on them. In collecting data, participants should be treated fairly and with dignity, and their interests should be protected. Three basic principles must be adopted: respect, do no harm and non-discrimination. AFRYDEV shall ensure that the beneficiaries are sensitized and informed of their rights to complaints about the implementation from the onset through APP system reporting.
