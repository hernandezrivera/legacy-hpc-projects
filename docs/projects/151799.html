Project Summary

Human Appeal aims to provide protection monitoring response in hard-to-reach and under-served areas identified by the Protection Cluster as in urgent needs of increased protection presence in Banadir, Bay and Bekool regions.
Through two mobile protection teams, Human Appeal will provide regular coverage of Banadir, bay and Bekool regions which hosts almost 68% of Somali IDPs. Human Appeal will deploy mobile protection team that will conduct household and community-level assessments through KOBO toolbox and focus group discussions, develop and provide timely and reliable information and key protection messaging out of the existing information from different actors, monitor returns and the camp consolidation/eviction processes, and provide information on services in areas of return to IDPs intended to return through joined “Know before you go” campaigns with the government. The Human Appeal teams will provide regular reports and updates to the National Protection Cluster, Banadir, Bay and Bekool Protection Working Groups, Ministry of Humanitarian and Disaster Management, and other relevant cluster mechanisms to ensure a timely and coordinated response in the locations visited.
The teams will choose locations for monitoring based on recommendations of the Protection Cluster as well as based on community level assessments and connections established with local authorities and beneficiaries. In addition to the protection activities targeted at beneficiaries, the Human Appeal team will conduct capacity-building activities for local NGOs through training in order to bolster the strength of the local NGO response to ensure longevity of comprehensive protection services in the targeted areas.
Human appeal will ensure to work in through the existing inter-agency referrals mechanism and referral pathways in the targeted communities however where there aren’t any referrals pathways then we will try to create a localized referral pathways. Human Appeal protection monitoring teams will develop 5Ws matrix for each single IDP settlement to ensure smooth referral pathways.

Needs

Human Appeal’s Protection Team has been conducting protection monitoring in Bay, Gedo and Banadir regions since August 2018, through HQ fund to support highly vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach locations. As such, Human Appeal has in-depth on the ground knowledge of the situation in the proposed locations, both through the team’s own work and through information gathered from partner organizations with whom the Human Appeal team regularly coordinates. In addition to the known needs and gaps in services in the proposed locations, the Human Appeal team has been monitoring the situation in Bekool through the deployment of assessment teams whose work is reflected in the analysis below. As per the recent Human Appeal rapid protection assessment in Banadir and Bay regions, loss of legal documentation and access to protection services represents a major protection concern for IDP families cited by 92%. The majority of Key informants (72%) reported that only 1-25% of IDP families are self-sufficient to meet their basic needs in the immediate term. 54% of Key informants reported that protection services are insufficient and need immediate action. Boys in conflict areas (the areas where the IDPs came from) are being forcibly recruited by armed groups and some have been used as suicide bombers. None of the displaced people Human Appeal spoke with has any immediate intention of returning home. This is mainly due to fear of attacks and lack of resources to rebuild their lives and livelihoods from scratch upon return. Insecurity and psychosocial stress was notice among children, accompanied by the sense of exclusion from their long years of undocumented status. Provision of immediate food rations (ready to eat food), clean drinking water, basic shelter/NFI items and psychosocial support to the new comers are identified as the most priority for the time being. GBV and sexual abuse and/or exploitation related to food exchange was reported by 24% of the interviewed women.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Key informants in Banadir and Bekool regions reported having difficulty in accessing justice, including access to legal documentation, HLP cases, and criminal cases, issues that would be monitored and assisted through Human Appeal’s proposed mobile protection team in the these regions. The limited number of actors working Bekool means many protection needs as well as multi-sectoral needs are unaddressed and growing in urgency. Main protection-related needs:1. Sexual exploitation2. Discrimination in access to assistance3. Rape/sexual abuse4. Diversion of distributions5. Tension between IDPs and host communities6. Poor access to lifesaving and basic humanitarian assistance

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Results of HA needs assessment highlight that children and older persons are considered most in need of assistance. The chronically ill, persons with disability and child-headed households are identified as being the most vulnerable social groups at risk of health concerns. Children are, however; perceived to be one of the most vulnerable groups across all sectors, with children under the age of 5 and children aged 5-12 years having been identified as the first and third most vulnerable groups. In the 3 proposed locations; Key Informants also ranked infants/toddlers under 5 to be the age-group most at risk of health concerns followed by older persons (60+ years) and children between the ages of 5 and 12 years. The assessed locations are under-served in all aspects with no hope to have access to proper services in the near future. Human Appeal applies Protection Mainstreaming principles to all programming. Human Appeal ensures and improves the safety of all beneficiaries who participate directly in projects or benefit indirectly by Human Appeal programming. Human Appeal will ensure the four key Protection Mainstreaming principles are met, those specifically of safety and dignity in implementation, meaningful access to services, participation and empowerment of affected communities to the services provided, and accountability. Human Appeal will ensure that safety and dignity will be assured for beneficiaries by maintaining confidentiality from first contact through assessments through referrals and provision of assistance and distributions. Safety and dignity will also be established in that all sites are free of hazards, are in safe locations, and are clearly humanitarian spaces. Human Appeal will ensure meaningful access to services is met by establishing distribution locations that are accessible to persons with disabilities, in close proximity to where beneficiaries reside.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Human Appeal's safety and security approach to its operations in the proposed areas has been defined through specific Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Human Appeal has classified areas according to their security status, mapping the whole operation areas as a) partially secured (e.g. center of Mogadishu), b) moderate risk (e.g. newly retaken areas from Al-Shabab or even the IDP camps) or c) high risk zones (e.g. locations adjacent to areas under Al-Shabab control). The definition of these areas has been based on field risk assessments performed by Human Appeal security teams combined with a close relation with other actors, including UN-OCHA RSO, INSO, NISA (Somali National Investigation and Security Agency), local authorities and the host community with whom Human Appeal has established a network of informants able to update the organization in a timely manner on the security context. For locations where the risk of security incidents is high, Human Appeal establishes movements in convoy with multiple communication mechanisms, tracking capabilities based on GPS and Thuraya Satellite phone systems as redundant communications, as well as hourly communication checks in order to allow an on-time monitoring of the teams.1.
