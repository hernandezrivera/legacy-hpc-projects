Project Summary

This project aims to increase the food security and enhancing the livelihoods of IDPs in western rural of Aleppo and Idleb Governorate based on the following activities :
A- Free bread distribution in camps:
ONSUR will test the flour before receiving it from the supplier to ensure the following technical specifications are set:
• Shall have a natural taste and smell.
• Moisture content: 14 % maximum.
• Ash: 0.65% maximum of dry matter.
• Protein: 11 % minimum of dry matter.
• Wet gluten: 28 % minimum.
ONSUR will contract with bakeries to bake daily 26.3 MT of flour in project areas based on their capacities to bake flour to produce bread based on specific criteria for bakeries and bread as following.
Bakery specifications:
•a warehouse that is isolated from the production hall to avoid exposure to moisture, insects, and rodents,
• a water tank and a reliable source of clean, healthy water.
a cooling conveyor belt [at least 40 m].
Additionally, selected bakeries must adhere to the minimum hygiene standards and should be no more than 15–20 km away from the distribution points in the targeted camps.
Bread Specifications:
• the rate of bread production should be no less than 1.150 kg of bread per 1 kg of wheat flour,
• the baked bread must be cooled on conveyor belts,
• cooled bread should be put into plastic bags. The intervention details and complaints mechanisms shall be printed on these bags,
• selected bakeries should be provided with tables and metal racks for carrying bags of bread and keeping it in good condition,
• the bread bundles shall be put in plastic baskets (with a capacity of no more than ten bread bags),
• the weight of each bundle must be at least 800g, and the diameter of a loaf of bread between 28-30cm,
• the number of loaves of bread should not be less than 8 in one bundle,
• the loaf of bread should be fully cooked,
• bread shall be free from any other impurities,
• and bread must be delivered in baskets, each basket contains 10 bread bundles
Additionally, through this proposed project, ONSUR will provide work opportunities for 60 non-skilled daily workers of the IDPs (women and men) in targeted camps. The workers will be contracted to handle daily bread distribution, control, verify beneficiaries' documents and undertake loading/unloading activities. They will work for 6 hours/ day, for 7 USD/day.
ONSUR will Support the IDPS by daily free bread bundles (1000g/ for each family of 4 people / 6 days a week (except for Fridays) in rural Idleb and Aleppo camps through blanket distributions that cover all IDPs in the targeted camps for twelve months as following:
-10250 daily free bread bundles for 5900 families in Salqin camps
-5500 daily free bread bundles for 3300 families in Harim camps
- 9000 daily free bread bundles for 5000 families in Aghtrain and - Azaz camps
-5500 daily free bread bundles for 3500 families in Jandaris camps
B- Food basket distribution:
ONSUR will distribute 2000 food basket for 2000 vulnerable of IDPs families in rural Idleb and Aleppo camps regularly for eight months and the selection of beneficiaries of vulnerable families in camps . The beneficiary selection stage will consider the vulnerability degree and will give priority to food-insecure families, especially women-headed families.
The Calculation of food basket based on (family size of 5 people for a period of 30 day). The food basket will be at a minimum 70% of the daily kcal intake which is around 1550Kcal per person per day in a monthly family food.
ONSUR will inform the beneficiaries on the distribution points locations and distributions time with the telegram/WhatsApp channel.
ONSUR will conduct the distributions in different distribution points. ONSUR will provide each distribution point with a banner that includes the following information (project name, distribution point number, complaints mechanism).

3 - Needs

Syria faces a multitude of challenges that make it one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies. According to (HNO, 2023) 12.1M people are estimated to be food insecure. Over 2M people live in informal settlements and planned camps, a 5% increase since 2021. Moreover, 85% of households are unable to meet basic food needs in 2022. • Based on the 2022 Food Security Assessment (FSA) and 2022 Food Security and Livelihoods Assessment (FSLA) findings, WFP’s mobile vulnerability analysis and mapping (mVAM) and food prices data, an estimated 13.5 million people need some form of food and agriculture-based livelihoods assistance. • It is important to note that both agriculture and non-agriculture-based livelihoods have been affected by the prevailing economic challenges. However, the constraints in agriculture-based livelihoods will have negative consequences on both incomes for households and national food production. Taking these challenges into consideration, this project seeks to support IDPs families who in need and suffer from difficulty in providing their food needs by providing their basic food items needs.
