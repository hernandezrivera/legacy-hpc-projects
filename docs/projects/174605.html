Project Summary

Populations affected by displacement in conflict areas in the, NW/SW regions are lacking shelter and NFI assistance to improve their physical protection during displacement or return. The NRC programme will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, households who need shelter assistance to improve their living conditions in the project implementation locations. Particular attention will be given to households that meet the following vulnerability criteria:
• IDPs and returness living in makeshift camps with shelters exposing them to bad weather;
• IDPs and returness living in host communities where they scramble for the very limited space available and share sleeping spaces;
• IDPs, Primary, Secondary Multiple Displacements and vulnerable Host community members
• Returnees (former IDPs), where houses have been vandalised or destroyed due to the crisis;
• Schools in conflict affected areas that are used as collective housing by the displaced communities.
Target group
Beneficiary targeting will also aim at reaching the most vulnerable households based on agreed upon selection criteria. Priority will be given to female headed households (HHs), unaccompanied minors and child headed HHs, and HHs with disabled members. Having clear beneficiary selection criteria will ensure that deserving beneficiaries have equitable access to NRC’s shelter assistance, and hence mitigate the risk of tensions if the assistance is solely addressed to a particular group.
The Shelter response in the South West and North West regions aims to reach a total of 5,000 Households, some households will benefit from different services at the same time hence the households are with double counting. While 11,028 households with benefit from this project, some activities will be double counted. In Total, the shelter response is expected to reach a total of 25,360 (12,426 Male 12,934 Females) persons.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The shelter infrastructure in the North West and South West region remains insufficient to meet the needs of the displaced populations (IDPs and returnees) due to either having been destroyed during conflict or poorly maintained. Majority of the affected population do not have legal documents (with majority of the affected population having lost their documents when fleeing) proving that they are the owners of these properties. This makes it difficult for them to claim ownership to the property and thus they find themselves unjustly thrown out of their properties. This is particularly the case for women. The situation in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon deteriorated in August 2020 compared to the previous three months with an upsurge in violence and attacks against civilians. Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) have increased the use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) with increasing numbers of civilian casualties. According to the UN-OCHA Situation Report Last updated on 8th Oct 2020, the displacement situation has been on the increase, with the number totalling up to 679,000 IDPs in the NW/SW regions as at the end of September 2020. This depicts a 76.4% increase in the displaced population as compared to 160,000 IDPs in September 2018. The humanitarian needs in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon are concentrated in divisions such as Fako, Meme, Ndian, Lebialem, Kupe Manenguba and Manyu Divisions in the SW region and Mezam, Ngoketunjia, Momo, Bui, Menchum, Donga Mantung and Boyo Division, in the NW region. The main subdivisions are already characterised by poor living conditions, especially for the population that has fled to the bush. The instability in the area has affected timely humanitarian interventions especially where access to IDP sites in the bush is hampered by different security developments which remain harsh and unpredictable. The deteriorating security situation has resulted in multiple population displacements across the NW/SW regions during the month of September 2020 with over 11,718 people forced to flee their homes to seek shelter in nearby bush and villages. The NW region has been most affected with over 10,200 displaced people affected in the month of September 2020. These movements are often temporary and the displaced return to their homes once the security situation allows. The locations where the IDPs have fled for safety include areas where they are living with relatives and friends in homes whose space is insufficient to provide the needed separation and privacy between the different ages and sex. In some situations, it is still common to find a home hosting almost 22 persons as depicted by NRC’s profiling data. The situation is reported to be worse in the bush where the IDPs sleep on the bare ground with no privacy and are exposed to harsh weather conditions and protection issues. Their vulnerability is further compounded by the lack of adequate household items, and the use of the provided plastic sheets (tarpaulins) to act as walls, which can easily be cut to allow unwanted intrusions. Besides, the plastic sheeting requires replacement after every few months as it is easily degraded by the hot climate. Inadequate lighting at night coupled with weak doors and windows and wall cladding materials for the IDP shelters, increases the risk of theft, night attacks and protection concerns. In addition to limited access to livelihoods, IDPs, and their host/surrounding communities face high unemployment rates and low income. As a result, most IDP households cannot afford appropriate shelter materials or rent.
