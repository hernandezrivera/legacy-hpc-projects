Project Summary

The IRC child protection program support children and adolescents in Rohingy and host communities to be safe at homes and in their communities and receive support when they experience any harm. Thus, the project will anchor on support to children and adolescents aged between 0-18 years with comprehensive case management, psychosocial services, life skills and business skills development to meet their safety needs and wellbeing. The project will be implemented in the following camps- 1E, 1W, 5, 8E, 14, 15, 18, 22, 23, 25 and Tecknaf, Sadar, Holidiapalang and Ratnapalong Upzilas in Ukhia host communities. IRC's Staff are highly trained with enormous experience to support diverse needs of the children and facilities regardless of the differences. IRC prevention program is largely local partner led, where IRC provides technical and handholding support to partners to implement IRC’s flagship program on Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS). Children aged 6-11 years are provided with age specific structured PSS through Social and Emotional Learning (SEL), SEL is nine months curriculum delivered in SHLS through trained facilitator (separate sessions for boys and girls) to help children cope with and heal from trauma; and participate in recreational activities to return to normalcy. Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) for adolescents aged 12 to 19 years with separate sessions for caregivers on building their resilience and parenting skills, Alternative care arrangement to have safe and supportive mentors to be more resilient to threats to survival and development (Training of caregivers and working with DSS to ensure placement of separated and or unaccompanied children), comprehensive case management services to children aged 0 to 18 years. Adolescents aged 15 to 19 are supported with "Learn to Lead" structured curriculum that support building resilience and civic engagement, particularly on issues related to leadership, social cohesion, advocacy, conflict resolution and peace building. They are also engaged in "Learn to Earn" sessions to equip them with transferable business and life skills. Adolescents who complete the sessions receive a start-up support in the form of tools/kits such as sewing machines, electrical repair took kits etc. to start their self-employment initiatives in camp and host communities. In addition, linkage support to government extension services is provided to the adolescents in the host communities. Community outreach designed based on the child protection needs identified through CPIMS, and regular child protection situation monitoring in each camp and host communities. Outreach session includes natural disaster and cyclone risk and preparedness issues on a regular discussion using IEC materials. IRC and local partner support establishing inclusive community structures, build their capacity and support in action planning so that they identify children at risk or those harmed, handle minor cases, and conduct timely referral to available service points, and ensure participation of women and adolescent, people with disabilities in the planning and decision-making process. IRC ensures that the local partners including volunteers/incentive workers are trained on the Safeguarding and PSEA, community and child friendly accountability mechanism is in place, clients are empowered through regular sessions to report. The project is designed in consultation with the clients and Interventions are designed to address age, gender, and disability specific needs of children. Community will be also part of the monitoring and result harvesting process over the year. IRC will continue close collaboration and coordination with CPSS to ensure the program delivered are in accordance with sector objective. Regular on ground coordination with relevant government mechanism will be a key approach to deliver program in camp and host communities.

1 - Identified Needs

The IRC child protection monitoring data from January to March and October 2021 (resumed after lockdown) showed that the protection concerns for children has heightened during COVID. Children experienced violence and abuse at different form due to their age, gender, disability, sexual orientation, social status and other identities. The monitoring data revealed a worrying trend; children 0-11 years has increased physical abuse (24%), emotional abuse (20%) and neglect (18%), which has long-term impact in their psychology and brain development. The data also revealed that parents using negative coping mechanism that include verbal abuse, leaving children long hour without adequate supervision, sharing misinformation, aggression and physical punishment. Resulting distress children with poor skills and capacity to control their emotion and building meaningful relationship with family, friends, and peers. Children aged 12-18 years has experienced the incidents of emotional abuse (18%), sexual abuse (15%), physical abuse (15%), child labour (14%), child marriage (11%), psychosocial distress (8%) at higher level. Child labour and child marriage found as a result of negative coping strategy of the caregiver to cope with livelihood challenges that impacted them highly due to COVID. Children with disabilities found at the greater risk of emotional abuse (25%) physical abuse (17%), neglect (13%), discrimination (13%) and psychosocial distress (12%). Social and cultural norms found as a major barrier for the caregiver and children with disabilities accessing services and facilities at equal level. The safety concerns for girls found higher than boys, 3% respondent mentioned about the security concerns of girls is the main reason to put restriction in their movement, engaging them in household chores and performing marriage at early age. According to Child Protection Assessment in Rohingya Refugee Camps in Cox’s Bazar, October 2021, UNICEF & CPSS also showed an worrying trend In the camps, the most reported child protection concerns perceived by respondents are Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%), risks of trafficking (32%), depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse (23%).Programing supporting adolescent has not been enough over the years forcing many adolescent boys and girls not to be engaged in any meaningful skill development programming. This is leading the adolescents, especially boys to easily engage with negative coping mechanism. Therefore, programming should create more opportunity for adolescents to build their leadership and business skills for income generating activities. With current programming approach there is no or limited opportunity for adolescent boys to understand gender concept, they are representing negative masculinity that is reinforcing gender stereotypes. There is limited access to national protection systems including response services (case management) formal, non-formal and tertiary education in both Rohingya and host community children. Lack of material support and high level of poverty are also cited among causes of children protection risks. The current level of interventions for adolescents that are out of school is inadequate to fully empower them with skills and mitigate the tendency among them, to engage in high-risk behavior. The outbreak of COVID-19 has proven to have a significant impact on parental stress, with caregivers and parents expressing their level of anxiety and psychological distress, economic hardship, and competing priorities, make it challenging for caregivers to fulfil their responsibilities. Data collected through pre and post-tests from our parenting programs indicates that caregivers and foster parents are under mental, behavioral and relationship stress, which affects provision of children care.

Targeted training for government and local services providers on child protection technical aspects based on their capacity gap i.e., gender sensitive case management, case management coaching and supervision, PSS, caring of child and adolescent survivor, gender sensitive protection programming, PSEA and Safeguarding etc. 4. Provide age, gender and disability sensitive case management services to children and adolescents5. Conduct gender and age-appropriate life skills session with adolescents and young people-Through SAFE approach6. Provide Economic skills development opportunities to children and young people aged 15 to 24 years with structured session on "Learn to Learn". This is a four-month curriculum that support young people developing life skills and business skills with an individual business plan by the end of the module7. Conduct age and gender appropriate PSS services to children and adolescents through Social and Emotional Learning (SEL).8. Conduct positive parenting sessions with caregivers
