Project Summary

The Bahar Response and intervention addresses the urgent humanitarian needs in Northwest Syria, exacerbated by the aftermath of the February 2023 earthquakes and the ongoing crisis. With escalating violence, displacement, and economic challenges, vulnerable households struggle to meet their basic needs. The initiative targets 4,430 vulnerable households, providing multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) through four regular rounds, each with a transfer value adjusted to local market conditions. By aligning with the HNO 2024, conducting organizational and market assessments, and implementing rigorous targeting mechanisms, the project ensures a tailored and effective response.
Identified Needs and Assessments:
Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024: The HNO 2024 underscores the dire situation in Northwest Syria, necessitating a targeted response to address escalating humanitarian needs.
Organizational Needs Assessment: Bahar conducted an organizational assessment to ensure its capacity aligns with project requirements, enhancing efficiency in assistance delivery. and contract with FSP through FAW, the transfer value is 5%.
Market Assessment in Proposed Locations: Comprehensive market assessments were conducted to understand local economic dynamics and access constraints, informing the design of cash assistance to suit beneficiaries' needs and local markets. This assessment enables the project to tailor its cash assistance to align with local market conditions, ensuring effective utilization by beneficiaries to meet diverse need.
Activity Description:
The intervention will provide multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 4430 vulnerable households in Northwest Syria through four regular rounds. Each household will receive a transfer value (100$ per month for four rounds) determined based on their specific needs and the local market conditions. This assistance will enable beneficiaries to address various essential needs such as food, shelter, clothing, and medical expenses.

CVA Risks and Mitigation Measures:
Potential risks associated with cash-based interventions (CVA) were identified and addressed using the CVA Risk Matrix and PSEA guidelines.
Market Distortion: Cash injection may distort prices, making essentials less affordable.
Mitigation: Regular market monitoring and adjusting cash transfer values.
Protection Risks: Cash assistance may expose vulnerable groups to theft, exploitation, or abuse.
Mitigation: Strict adherence to PSEA guidelines, safe delivery mechanisms, and awareness sessions.
Financial Risks: Mismanagement or misuse of cash may lead to instability.
Mitigation: Providing financial literacy training and implementing monitoring mechanisms.
Inflationary Pressures: Large cash injections may cause inflation.
Mitigation: Phased distribution and coordination with stakeholders to address inflation
Security Risks: Cash distribution may attract criminal attention, endangering beneficiaries and aid workers.
Mitigation: Security assessments, coordination with authorities, and community leaders for safe distribution.
Harmonized PDM Tool and Monitoring Activities
The project will utilize a harmonized Participatory Design, Monitoring, and Evaluation (PDM) tool to track progress and assess the impact of interventions. Regular monitoring activities will be conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of cash assistance delivery, ensure accountability, and adapt strategies as needed to address emerging needs and challenges.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Implemented MPCA through four regular rounds, the MPCA affords beneficiaries the autonomy to allocate funds according to their most pressing requirements. This flexibility is particularly vital in a context of widespread disruption and uncertainty, empowering households to make informed decisions based on their unique circumstances. Beyond immediate relief, the intervention also seeks to foster resilience within the community by bolstering economic stability and social cohesion. By alleviating financial burdens and enabling access to essential resources, it aims to mitigate the long-term impacts of the crisis. Through collaboration with local partners and stakeholders, the Bahar Emergency Response and Recovery intervention embodies a collective effort to extend compassion and support to those facing the greatest hardships. Objective:To provide financial assistance to vulnerable households impacted by the earthquake and ongoing crisis in Northwest Syria, thereby mitigating economic challenges and offering a lifeline to those in acute need. Outputs:Multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) provided to 4,430 vulnerable households for four rounds with value 100$ per HHs per month that mean the total amount will be 400$ for each HHs.Distribution of aid conducted in accordance with established protocolsMonitoring and evaluation mechanisms in place to ensure effective implementation Outcomes:Improved access to basic necessities such as food, shelter, clothing, and medical expenses for beneficiary householdsEnhanced economic stability and resilience within the targeted communities.Strengthened social cohesion and support networks among beneficiaries.
