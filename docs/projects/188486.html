Project Summary

UNHCR's project aims to increase resilience and livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable people affected by the Syrian crisis. This is to be achieved in collaboration with a wide range of stakeholders, including local and international NGOs, UN agencies, and local communities through a range of livelihood interventions delivered in the underserved communities. The project aims to assist a total of 135,700 IDPs and returnees across Syria. The project directly contributes to the following Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector Objectives
1- Sector Objective 1: Support the Maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services
2- Sector Objective 2: Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities

4 - Needs

According to the draft 2022 HNO, around 13.8 million people are in need of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. This number is expected to increase to 15 million in 2023. An estimated 85% of Syrians live below the poverty line, becoming increasingly vulnerable due to the loss or lack of sustainable livelihoods. Food prices have increased dramatically by the end of 2021 as WFP's standard food basket registered the highest ever recorded price since monitoring started (2013). The situation is critical for the most vulnerable who may be compelled to resort to negative coping mechanisms, e.g., child labor due to lack of meaningful income opportunities. The gap between income and expenditure per year is increasing among all population groups coupled with deterioration in the access to basic services, e.g., electricity.The project will consider the above factors to improve the accessibility of the most vulnerable groups to livelihood and economic support. Where possible, socio-economic assessments will be undertaken to better understand the socio-economic profile of target communities and the targeted population's capacities and needs to better identify livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following main activity will be undertaken as part of the project in collaboration with local and International NGOs:1) Rehabilitation of key local and community level economic and social infrastructure such as marketplaces, vocational training classes/workshops, bakeries, irrigation channels.2) Provision of essential ancillary goods and services to support farmers, fishers, and others dependent on the natural resource base for their livelihoods.3) Provision of business advisory services (including referrals) regarding business startup processes, business management, and pursuing business opportunities. 4) Provision of support to Small Start-up business projects. Basic essential assets and tools will be provided to returnees in rural and urban areas to help them resume their former profession or previous means of living or start a new business.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination when selecting beneficiaries of livelihoods interventions, and risk of the most vulnerable being left out (GBV, persons with disabilities). Mitigating measures: • Needs assessment will be conducted to gather information about the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees to be targeted through community centres and outreach volunteers. • Participatory assessments and community consultations will be applied in all stages of the programme cycle to enable beneficiaries to actively engage in the assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation of the project.• Capacity building of Partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD;• UNHCR will clearly inform community on beneficiary selection criteria and ensure that community leaders, local council, elderly people are engaged in community sensitization and beneficiary selection process. An active and informed complaint mechanism will be in place. • Enhance monitoring and evaluation of livelihood programmes Protection risk: Existing labor market opportunities limit the participation of youth, women, and persons with disabilities resulting in lower remuneration and return to negative coping mechanisms. Mitigating measures: • Analyze access to market opportunities with an age, gender, and diversity lens. Compare this information with protection needs assessments.• Conduct socio-economic field surveys to identify viable livelihoods interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closelywith other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA networkto ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regardto the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition tomandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment hasbecome mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet thestandard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership withUNHCR in the following year.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk description: Lack of access hampering needs and impact assessments and protection monitoring of the programs results in continuous reliance on estimations, third party reports, and information provided by partners for planning purposes, which may not accurately reflect needs and impact being assessed largely through proxy reporting.Mitigation measures: • Capacitate Partners to capitalize on information collected through livelihood staff and in the community centers and systematize methodologies used for focus group discussions in community centers;• Expand monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring e.g. KOBO• Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. • Maintain and expand UNHCR field presence and monitoring capacity.
