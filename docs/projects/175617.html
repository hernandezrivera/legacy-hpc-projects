Project Summary

CRADA aims to reduce the mortality and morbidity of people affected by the conflict and other vulnerable people such as IPDs and host community by improving access to quality health care services and lifesaving services to vulnerable people including IDPs and host community through Mobile clinics and Static Locations and outreach activities by emphasis on primary health care including provision of outpatient consultation. immunization, comprehensive reproductive health service, laboratory test, response to disease outbreaks through disease surveillance intensification and timely response to confirmed epidemic-prone diseases.
In addition, CRADA will conduct mobile clinic with mobile heath team of qualified health workers to provide mobile health services to people located to hard to reach areas and especially the displaced populations cut off from health services by the floods that have affected health service delivery in Jonglei and Upper Nile states ..
With this project, CRADA will improve the Immunization services coverage as most of the areas have low coverage due to the unavailability of vaccines and lack of cold chain facilities. This will be done by offering mobile vaccination and vitamin A supplementation services to children under five years. Vaccinators will be identified, trained, and equipped to conduct outreach immunization and referral for defaulter tracing.
CRADA will work hard to increase reproductive health services such as ANC, health facility delivery done by skilled birth attendants, Folic acid supplementation, family planning by emphasizing sensitization for women and girls of the importance of utilization of RH services, as well as by engaging men and community leader on the availability of reproductive health services in the health facilities and the advantages of the utilization of these essential services.
CRADA will ensure the quality of services by mitigating medical commodities and equipment shortage due to weak supply chain, interruption of medical supplies affecting the quality of preventive and curative services in all locations. In order to mitigate delays and also access challenges during the rain season, CRADA will preposition essential to and train health workers on the rational use of drugs and proper drug prescriptions.
CRADA will address the high stigmatization of mental illness at the community level which affects case detection and management. CRADA will also offer SGBV services to the vulnerable and affected populations to ensure that the survivors of Sexual abuse and gender-based violence have access to health services.
CRADA will also do nutrition screening and referral to nutrition centers for the children identified with malnutrition in collaboration with nutrition actors in the target counties thus improving GAM rates and nutrition status of the vulnerable and food insecure populations.
CRADA will target 20000 vulnerable and affected populations in Panyikang, Maiwut, Akobo, Uror, Pibor, Duk, (Jonglei State), Koch, Panyinjar, Pariang Counties (Unity State). This will include 9210 males and 10790 females, of which 5640 will be children under five years, and 2640 people with special needs including People with disabilities and the elderly.

1 - Needs

2020 has been a very challenging year for South Sudan as the country has experienced multiple shocks that have greatly affected health service delivery in the country.The country has experienced one of the worst floods and has affected the targeted counties with high destruction of health infrastructure and medical supplies and equipment. This compounded with the ravaging COVID 19 pandemic has brought health systems trying to recover from years of political unrest to their knees.The country is synonymous with a lack of medical supplies to the hard-to-reach areas and the targeted counties of Akobo, Duk, Pibor, Uror, Pariang, Panyijiar, Koch, Maiwut, and Panyikang have had reported stock-outs leaving the vulnerable and affected populations in serious health challenges.Pibor County has been affected by recurrent measles outbreaks. In 2020 the county was one of the 8 counties that experienced a measles outbreak and this can be attributed to low immunization coverage and lack of cold chain facilities (IDSR Bulletin week 52 2020). This county was also hard hit by flooding with flooding snapshot issue 3 2020 showing that more than 20000 people had been displaced by the floods (Flooding snapshot issue 2 Sep 2020) and this also caused a surge in acute watery diarrhea and malaria.Duk county was also highly affected by the floods and a similar situation was experienced in all the other counties. This affected health service delivery as mass displacement of populations meant overcrowding and overutilization of the available meager health services.The country is also experiencing severe food insecurity with all the target counties being in IPC phase 5 and actually Pibor in IPC phase 5. More than 6.3 million people are in critical condition of food insecurity which is likely to bring about malnutrition and hence vulnerability to diseases (IPC Nov 2020 Report). This calls for efforts to improve health service delivery and therefore CRADA will improve the health service delivery by having statistic and mobile health services in the target counties to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations have access to integrated health services

2 - Project activities

CRADA will carry out health activities in line with the health cluster objectives as follows:Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities in Akobo, Uror, Pibor, Duk, Koch,Pariang, Maiwut, Panyikang and Panyijar Counties by• Timely detection and response of health alerts through timely reporting to EWARS and IDSR to the county department• Immunization of the children under five against vaccine preventable infections• Vitamin A supplementation for children under five yearsImprove access to integrated health services in the target Counties• Management of communicable diseases like malaria and acute watery diarrhea with appropriate treatment protocols• ANC services and skilled birth attendants and in the health facilities• Folic acid supplementation of pregnant mothers• Health education with key COVID 19 prevention messages• Training health workers on infection prevention and control• Mental health and psychosocial support• Provision of SGBV services to the vulnerable and affected populations• Offer reproductive health services like family planning• Screening and management of malnutrition including referral.
