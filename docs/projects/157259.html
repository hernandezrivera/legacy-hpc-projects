Project Summary

This project aims to provide rapid response to the urgent food needs of food insecure population in the Northwest Syria, providing 1) in-kind, ready-to-eat (RTE) emergency rations for 14,843 newly displaced individuals, and 2) 3 months regular food assistance for 10,160 long-term displaced population and vulnerable local residents.

Needs

8 years into the crisis, the prolonged conflict is continuously causing a deterioration of the humanitarian situation in Syria. The large-scale displacement and a mounting economic recession have caused disruption of livelihoods and erosion of resilience capacity. There're 6.2 million people remained internally displaced within Syria, with over 1.5 million population movements registered between January and November 2018. Latest estimates indicate that at the end of 2018, approximately12.8 million are people now in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance inside Syria. Although security and access constraints hindered the ability to conduct a complete nation-wide assessment, analysis of available data highlighted a deterioration of the food security situation in the country. Some 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. The main causes of food insecurity are long-term displacements, loss of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which resulted in limited food access, high market prices and inflation causing depletion of assets for vulnerable populations. More than 80 percent of the population living in poverty, and $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector, there has been also an inseparable relationship between poverty and food security. In addition, the worst drought in 30 years severely affected agricultural production during 2017 - 2018 winter in Syria, resulted in the lowest wheat production since 1989 at 1.2 million tonnes. Reduced food production has resulted in elevated prices of foods commodities comparing to pre-crisis levels. Amid on-going conflict causing economic crisis and widespread unemployment, vulnerable population faces increasing constraints in accessing livelihood opportunities and many of them are exclusively reliant on food assistance to cover their needs. With continuous armed clashes and influx of IDPs, the Northwest Syria has been one of the highest food needs areas, more than 2.5 million people are in need of food assistance, of which over 1.4 million people are in acute people in need. According to Mobility and Needs Monitoring conducted by HNAP, as of December 2018 approximately 60% of the assessed households answered food assistance as their priority needs. Sources: - Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 - Syria IDP Task Force, December 2018 - WoS Food Security & Livelihood Assessment - FSLA & CFSAM, December 2018 - Food Security and Livelihood Cluster: Response Gap Analysis, December 2018 - HNAP: MOBILITY AND NEEDS MONITORING - DECEMBER OVERVIEW, December 2018 - FAO/WFP: CROP AND FOOD SECURITY ASSESSMENT MISSION, December 2018 - FAO: Early Warning Early Action report on food security and agriculture, December 2018.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There’s a potential risk of deterioration of the security situation preclude access to our areas of implementation. For the mitigation measure, our implementing partner has experience implementing actions in such context. We'll maintain a regular monitoring of conflict dynamics in areas of interventions and undertake periodic assessments of the operational contexts in order to respond to changes. There’s also a risk of more constrained cross-border access or that the regulatory environment in Turkey will not facilitate the operations of INGOs and Syrian NGOs from the north inside Syria. For the mitigation measure, contingency plans are in place by our implementing partner to relocate our operations in case operational access is reduced. Assumptions are: - Security and political considerations allow for the safe movement of our field staff. - Local institutions, community, and armed groups remain open to external project interventions that alleviate the conditions in which the targeted population lives.
