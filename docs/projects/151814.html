Project Summary

This project is live saving project to contribute to the decrease of water scarcity and improve the access to sanitation facilities in Baardheere. The project will increase the access to safe drinking water through rehabilitation of 3 strategic boreholes and 20 hand dug wells in the target location. Also, the project will decrease the percentage of open defecation by contracting 100 communal latrines in Baardheere.

Needs

About 2.9M people need urgent WASH assistance in Somalia in 2019. The people in need include IDPs, host communities, Population in rural drought hotspots, Population living in AWD/Cholera hotspots, Natural disaster affected/vulnerable (un-responded) and Women and children, and man in need of protection in relation to WASH.REACH Initiatives has recently conducted an assessment on Bardera. The assessment covered 5 IDP settlements in Bardera where most of the people have proper access to potable water. Also, the assessment reports that very few people have access to proper sanitation facilities where women and/or girls do not feel safe as latrines are not designed as gender separate. Handwashing behavior in the assessed settlements is not totally practiced as per assessment of REACH Initiatives.Heavy rainfalls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018. WASH infrastructures suffered from important damages in more than 25 districts including Bardera. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, many parts of Jubbaland state including Bardera continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2018 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots.Civil wars in this period destructed most of the water infrastructure/sources in Bardera where people have no access to clean and safer drinking water. People in Bardera have only access to unclean water fetched from the river where children and women become vulnerable to the water borne disease like AWD/Cholera. Water treatment at household level is under average where displaced people have no proper access to water chlorination. Open defecation is common practice in most of the IDP camps in and around Bardera because of inadequate sanitation facilities in the camps. With the very limited number of latrines in target IDPs in Bardera, disabled people still remain unassisted in utilizing latrines properly as they face challenges.

Activities

1. Rehabilitation of 3 boreholes to improve access to safe drinking water for 15,000 people (5,000 people benefit from each borehole as per the Guide to WASH Cluster Strategy and Standards, 2018) and extension of pipelines to 3 IDPs settlements, 5 schools and 3 MCH/OTPs. 3 strategic boreholes will be rehabilitated to improve access to sustainable safe water supply for vulnerable IDPs, host communities, Urban Poor and Rural communities with chronic water shortages. 2. Rehabilitation and upgrading of 25 Hand dug wells/shallow wells to increase access to safe water for 10,000 people (400 people benefit each shallow well as per the Guide to WASH Cluster Strategy and Standards, 2018) and chlorination of 60 unprotected HDWs. Shallow wells will be rehabilitated by deepening, protecting the walls from collapsing, construction of well head that is at least 0.5m above ground level, drainage channel that is at least 10m from the water delivery point, construction of soak away pit, installation of hand pump and shock chlorination after work is completed to reduce risk of AWD. 3. Construction of 100 gender sensitive latrines in IDPs, returnees and host communities to increase access to 5,000 people (50 people benefit each latrine as per the Guide to WASH Cluster Strategy and Standards, 2018). The design of latrines will ensure privacy and security of the users especially women/girls shall incorporate lockable door, hand washing facilities and use of locally available materials. They will be raised at least 0.3m above the ground level to ensure they are flood proof and clearly marked based on gender. 4. Train 40 Community hygiene Promoters (at least 40% to be women) who will conduct Comprehensive Hygiene Awareness Promotion including demonstration on use of hygiene kits and training on simple HH water treatment and safe storage.5. Water treatment through chlorination and awareness at household level (HHWT).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Following displacement, the supply of potable water and sanitation services where IDPs find themselves displaced may be inadequate or non-existent – especially in informal camp settings, unfinished buildings or collective public spaces not intended for shelter purposes. The lack of sufficient safe drinking water can have a negative impact on IDPs, both in terms of their health and well being, and in terms of the additional strain placed on IDP-host community relations if local water resources are contested. The target beneficiaries of the proposed project usually face the following risks:1. AWD/Cholera and water borne diseases as direct result of missing access to clean drinking water2. Spread of diseases among the IDP communities in the informal settlements3. Potential risk of sexual exploitation when using the toilets as they are not classified based on sex, away from the shelters and miss the required privacy and security aspects4. Risk of tensions and breaking of social cohesion bonds if the intervention target one group and miss the others5. Access to humanitarian interventions remains unequal with limited protection principles mainstreamed in such interventions6. HA is no longer perceived as a neutral organization Risk prevention/mitigation:1. Allow for more concrete participation by the target beneficiaries in the project's implementation/monitoring activities2. Mainstream the protection principles in all the project activities (proper beneficiary feedback mechanism, key messages that adopt the local context, community approaches,...etc)3. Neutrality and impartiality are part of HA’s approach, and HA asserts its commitment to the humanitarian principles. HA has a communication strategy with beneficiaries that is designed to increasing acceptance and understanding of the purpose of our activities with affected populations

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

HA MEAL staff will review and update the AGD commitments, implement and report against the set indicators. Specific commitments include ensuring staff and local stakeholders are trained on AGD (Age, Gender and Diversity) approaches, assessments and responses take into consideration the diverse needs of men, women, boys and girls. HA will ensure gender balance in the recruited project staff to allow for more traditionally accepted approach to females (particularly FHHs). Potential to contribute significantly to gender equality. A gender and age analysis is included in the project’s needs assessments and is reflected in one or more of the project’s activities and one or more of the project’s outcomes.HA will address the different impacts of the humanitarian situation on the beneficiaries, men, women, boys, and girls, in order to promote the potential for positive transformations of gender norms within the affected populations. HA will analyze, plan, and respond to the communities’ needs in ways that address practical gender needs and promote women’s rights and gender equality as part of any response. This will be achieved by:1. Mainstreaming gender and age equality, basing interventions on gendered analysis of the causes and impacts of crises, the practical needs of women and men, boys and girls. 2. Looking for opportunities to support women and men in ways that contribute to the transformation of gendered power relations, laying the groundwork for gender equality and greater resilience to crises by actively supporting women’s leadership and by challenging attitudes and beliefs that undermine women’s rights (including by engaging with men and boys)3. Coordinating effectively within the humanitarian system at international, regional, and national levels to ensure gender-sensitive responses4. Promoting the full participation and leadership of women and girls, men and boys, in all aspects of programming, from planning to evaluation

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict is one of the major challenges in humanitarian setting that can restrict proper intervention for needs in Somalia. People have experienced armed conflict in various stages as they currently face challenges in the fighting between government and insurgents where vulnerable mothers, girls and children are the most ones affected. In this project, Human Appeal will put into practice its risk mitigation plan where it adopts accordingly. Human Appeal will collaborate government of Somalia and its local authorities to ensure safe and dignified humanitarian response where people feel safe and secure.
