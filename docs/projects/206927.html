Project Summary

The objective of the project is to strengthen the coordination of the humanitarian response in Cameroon to save lives, protect the most vulnerable and improve the resilience of populations. Since the worsening of the crisis in the North-West and South-West regions, Cameroon faces three complex humanitarian crises affecting eight of Cameroon's ten regions. The impact of these three crises on the living conditions of the affected populations is reinforced by pre-existing structural vulnerabilities that hamper their recovery, in the three affected regions: Far North, North-West and South-West. Widespread insecurity in the Lake Chad basin and border area with Nigeria continued to affect the Far North region in 2023. Attacks against civilians, State security forces (SSF), including army, gendarmerie, police, and their bases, continued and non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and armed criminals continued to carry out lootings in crisis affected areas. NSAGs continue to use improvised explosive devices (IEDs) in the Far North, North-West, and South-West regions, primarily targeting military convoys. In the Far North region, particularly in the Lake Chad basin area, the economic context is marked by poverty, the lack of access to and sustainable management of natural resources, limited income-generating and market opportunities, as well as a drastic reduction in agriculture, livestock production and touristic activities due to prevalent insecurity.
In the North-West and South-West regions, high levels of insecurity continued. High levels of criminality and impunity lead to a climate of insecurity whereby civilians and humanitarian workers face risks of kidnapping for ransom, abduction, assault, unlawful detention, carjacking, and threats of violence. The line between targeted, politically motivated acts of violence and criminal activities is increasingly blurred. Violence, regular lockdowns and closure of roads continue to have a major impact on the local economies.
The complexity and the scope of these crises therefore require more effective coordination, adapted to the different intervention areas and based on humanitarian principles. OCHA Cameroon's coordination project will thus make it possible to achieve a humanitarian response that is better funded, more strategic and that centralizes human rights and the protection of people in the Far North. It will also enable a better complementarity between emergency actions and development programs, in order to fight together the humanitarian needs and their underlying causes. To this end, OCHA will provide the essential services of disseminating key information products, coordinating the Humanitarian Programming Cycle and facilitating joint multisectoral needs assessments. Its presence in Yaoundé, Maroua, Kousseri, Bamenda and Buea will enable it to provide decisive support to coordination mechanisms and promote coherence between operational issues in the field and strategic decision-making bodies. This key position will also enable it to advance the overriding considerations of the Centrality of Protection and Cross-cutting Protection, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and nexus humanitarian development and peace. From its sub-offices in the Far North, North-West and South-West, OCHA will also support the scaling up of the response in these regions, including through the management of CMCoord and humanitarian access responsibilities, as well as capacity building of local responders.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Cameroon is the most affected country by the conflict in the Lake Chad Basin, after Nigeria. Violence in the Far North has led to a steady rise of displacement since 2014. Meanwhile, violence continues to displace people within the North-West and South-West and to other regions. The coordination needs are related to: The definition of an overall coherence between the intersectoral coordination system in force at the national level and in the Far North and the NW and SW; and the adaptation of access and protection strategies. However, the need for civil-military coordination remains. There also remains a need to improve efforts related to the collection and analysis of data on the displacement, needs and vulnerabilities of affected people, in order to propose the most appropriate interventions possible. Finally, in the Far North, it is still essential to strengthen the links between the humanitarian and development spheres through harmonized programming and common coordination mechanisms. The growing trend towards the return of IDPs to the Far North, North-West and South-West has served as a reminder that strategic collaboration between the authorities, development partners and humanitarians will be essential to enable IDPs to make progress towards durable solutions.
