Project Summary

Under-nutrition is a public health issue and has adverse consequences on immediate child survival and long-term well-being. Acute and chronic malnutrition in children and women causes irreversible damage to intrauterine growth of fetuses and leads to physical and mental disabilities in children under the age of two years. North West(NW) and South West (SW) regions of Cameroon are characterized by an alert situation on the prevalence of acute malnutrition with a high prevalence of chronic malnutrition, micronutrient deficiency and poor indicators related to infant and young child feeding practices. However, deterioration of nutrition situation is foreseen since the ongoing conflict continue to inflict immense pressure on the underlying factors to malnutrition. The Kumba and Konye health District are areas highly hit by the ongoing crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon with little or no access to adequate food and health facilities especially in some hard to reach areas like Matoh in the Konye and Kumba III Health Districts.
Main Objective
The purpose of this project will be to prevent and manage acute malnutrition for children under 5 years to help reduce infant morbidity and mortality in the Meme Division of the South West region of Cameroon.
Specific objective
- To prevent acute malnutrition by implementing the BSFP program in Collaboration with WFP in the Kumba and Konye Health District
- Sensitise community on IYCF-E and COVID 19 preventive measures through Door-to-door visit and at the mobile clinic
- Conduct Active Screening to identify malnourished children in Konye and Kumba health Districts with the use of trained community health workers, mother led screening approach and mobile clinic sites
- To manage uncomplicated Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) at the OTP through the provision of RUTF in a COVID 19 context
- Refer and manage complicated SAM cases at the IPF
- Share reports on a monthly bases to Nutrition cluster UNICEF
This project shall be implemented in the Meme Division of the South West region of Cameroon precisely in the Konye and Kumba health Districts. These areas however, have recorded very low access to health care services with most caregivers and mothers resorting to the use of traditional methods in managing malnutrition. It is extremely important to manage this diseases at the community level since the hospital of these vulnerable communities are faraway from their homes and the highly impoverish communities lacks the financial capacity to pay hospital bills. The numerous ghost towns imposed by the belligerents and constant confrontations in these communities are also important factors affecting the access to nutritious foods.

1 - Besoins / Needs

With the foreseen nutrition situation deterioration due to the crisis in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon, as anticipated by the nutrition cluster, 189,000 children, boys, girls and women in the NWSW regions will require emergency nutrition services in 2021. This includes an estimated 108,000 children and 81,000 women with an estimated 8,000 children under five will be severely wasted and require life-saving treatment and if left untreated, it can lead to an elevated increased risk of death. Konye and Kumba Health Districts in Meme Division in the South west region of Cameroon is one of the main areas highly hit by the crisis with numerous displacement of inhabitants. The communities however, live under precarious conditions with severe low level of food security and poor access to healthcare facilities. Base on the strength of the forgoing, there is a high risk of acute malnutrition in these communities and hence a probable surge in the rate of morbidity and mortality for infants under 5 years. The following needs shall be addressed through the activities listed below; - Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme to prevent acute malnutrition hence reducing the burden of treating malnourished children. - Active Screening to Identify Malnourished Children: 15000 beneficiaries to be reached These will be done with the use of Trained Community health Workers (50) and Caritas Nutrition team - Family MUAC Approach: Need: 400 caregivers will be trained Mothers and caregivers will be train to effectively screen for MUAC and oedema and hence widen the scope of the screening. - Sensitisation of IYCF-E: 14,200 caregivers shall be sensitized on the importance of Infant and Young Child feeding Practice at emergencies with the use of counseling cards- Treatment and referral of complicated 100 SAM cases in the Areas of intervention with the use of RUTF. Malnourished children without complication shall be treated at the OTP while complicated Children shall be referred to the IPF; St. John Hospital Fiango Kumba.
