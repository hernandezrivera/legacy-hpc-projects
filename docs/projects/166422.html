Project Summary

This project is adapted to the different needs of women, men, girls and boys in line with the COVID -19 emergency response. Under the COVID-19 response programme, core activities are included such as food assistance, livelihood grants to the women and men of host communities and as well as training, essential inputs and extensive support for mask production following health sector guidelines for the Rohingya refugees.
Oxfam will organize training sessions for the target women groups on social distancing aspects and ensure proper handwashing facilities established at the training venues, protective clothes, masks, globes and other necessary materials are available with the participants. Equipment/tools such as sewing machine and seating facilities (e.g. chair) in their own house or Oxfam’s meeting place in the camps will be provided. Mask production activities will be implemented directly through Rohingya women’s groups in the camps. 200,000 cloth masks are planned to be produced by the women groups and distributed (10 masks per household) in the camps in coordination with the cooperating partners. 10 members will make around 2000 masks/per day. The most vulnerable women’s groups will be supported by some remuneration in kind/cash in exchange (based on CiC and Site management approval) as wage to will generate income opportunity. The payment and collection of masks will be weekly basis. The products will be disinfected/cleaned before distribution following health sector guidelines.
The targeted host community beneficiaries will receive BDT 4,500 in cash for food support and BDT 10,000 for Business support in Cox’s Bazar. These inputs will be provided based on assessment of different needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, girls and boys with age, disability considerations. It will help to mitigate the gendered impact of Covid-19 to the local economy, food security and access to basic needs. Proposed activities are targeted for both Rohingya and host communities to improve the social cohesion aspects.
Oxfam will implement the proposed interventions with the support of local partners YPSA and MUKTI which have longstanding connection to key stakeholders of government, civil society and community level during the project life. Oxfam has already distributed food and hygiene materials packages among 1,800 household in Ukhiya, Teknaf and Cox’s Bazar Sadar through local partners who established accountability and feedback mechanism system both for refugees and host communities. The partners gained valuable experience of ensuring social distancing and proper interaction methods during the distribution process. Besides, Oxfam GB will coordinate with WFP GFD partners to possible distribution the masks. An SOP of Mask production and distribution process will be developed in coordination with implementing agencies and following health sector guideline. The project activities will consider DRR and environmental aspect, especially on the disposal of used masks, following the WHO disposal guideline and technical support of Oxfam WASH.
A user friendly, safe system will be in place ensuring age and sex groups. The MEAL system will use the community feedback response mechanism to collect feedback/complaint to ensure accountability and satisfaction with the implementation/impact of the project of the affected community. Post distribution Monitoring will follow the FSS standard indicators to inform programme team and based on feedback measures will be taken.
Additional resource mobilization is currently being undertaken by Oxfam GB. Considering the current needs of food security & livelihood opportunity as well as Government policy stands, cash injection and risk prevention activities related with livelihood would support in strengthening local economy to revive rapidly from COVID-19 pandemic. These interventions will further contribute to food security and restore income options of both Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar district is ranked by the need’s assessment working group as one of the most prioritized geographic areas based on five key composite indicators which are risk of demographic and social vulnerability, economic and physical vulnerability, and recurrent disaster vulnerability. The breakdown of food systems during the COVID-19 crisis could lead to years of decreased agricultural productivity and worsen of food and nutrition indicators for large swaths of the population. Significant impacts are being observed and further anticipated food value chains (especially those relying on import and export). Prolonged impact scan includes limited access and distribution, reduced food diversity, impact on upcoming planting seasons, and even potential collapse of some sectors. Limited livelihood options due to disruption to food value chains (where most vulnerable groups rely on for daily or seasonal work), compounded by limited access to food will lead to increased indebtedness and negative coping mechanisms.Moreover, according to Rapid Gender Analysis, A COVID-19 outbreak in the refugee camps and neighboring communities would disproportionately affect women and girls and other vulnerable populations. Gender norms and roles in both refugee and host communities have limited women’s and girls’ ability to protect themselves from the virus and will have a significant impact on prevention and response efforts, if not adequately taken in the account. Gender norms place the burden on women and girls for care work, such as food preparation but are the last to eat in a family (Oxfam, 2018 Gender Analysis). The majority of refugees incur debt for food or health needs with an increasing trend from 35% in 2018, 45% in 2019, and 69% in 2020. A wide gender gap is seen for those who do have access to stipend or income, where 27% of men have access to income, only 2% of women do - despite women having the interest to earn their own income (Oxfam, 2019 Gender Analysis).The District Administration of Cox’s Bazar predicts more than 700,000 people in Cox’s Bazar District will be immediately jobless due to the ‘Stay at Home’ directive. This loss of livelihoods coupled with a decrease in access to the local market has disrupted activities and resulted in extra support needs for Food Security Sector. Considering the different assessment findings from Need assessment working group, FAP assessment in Cox’s Bazar, WFP flash market information, and Food Security Sectors (FSS) recombination, Oxfam is proposing the above-mentioned activities to support the completing gaps and minimize the negative impact on refugees and host communities.
