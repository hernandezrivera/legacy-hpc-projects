Project Summary

through the proposed interventions HIHFAD is planning to continue the provision of general and specialized health services through a network of its facilities into a comprehensive module including Primary health services with 2 primary health Centers and secondary health services with 5 hospitals 2 of them care about women and child health while the rest 3 hospitals will be general hospitals .the mentioned facilities are also putting into consideration the easy access to people with disabilities through the prepared infrastructure of those facilities to make sure of full access. in addition, HIHFAD will continue operating 2 rehabilitation centers to enhance the quality of life to people with disabilities by the provision of physiotherapy sessions and a solid referral mechanism to health care points if needed. adding to the previous interventions HIHFAD is focusing from a comprehensive perspective on providing specialized health services connected to the hospitals and outpatient services such as a dialysis unit and operating Tuberculosis center which is the only of its type in Idleb governorate. MHPSS services will be connected to each facility as a complementary component to make sure of covering the health needs of the people within the targeting areas

4 - Needs

As the Syria crisis approaches its tenth year, the health system remains heavily disrupted. While attacks on health care in 2020 fell by 67 % compared to 2019, the COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with the economic downturn in the country, has pushed the health system to the brink. By mid-2020, more than 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below three critical emergency health standards and a localized household survey in NW Syria found crude and under-five mortality rates were below international standards.(NGOHealthPartnerSurvey,Q42020)-HNO-2021 P74As in previous years, the health sector remains focused on five key vulnerable groups: displaced persons, children under five years (U5), WRA (15-49), older persons (60+), and people with disabilities. Among the 12.43 million people in need of health services in 2021, 1.41 million are children U5 while 3.32 million are WRA, including 498,480 women, are expected to become pregnant in 2021. An additional 536,753 older people are likely to require health services and those with early-onset NCD, which is estimated to account for 45 percent of all mortality in Syria.351 In Syria, the prevalence of disability is estimated at 25 %,352 nearly double the global average.Not only do persons with injuries and disabilities require specialized services and measures to ensure access, but they are disproportionately at risk from COVID-19 and the economic situation on account of increased reliance on caregivers, costof transport, andgreater household vulnerability.Persons with major-to-complete functional difficulty in at least one Washington Group question reported financial barriers to accessing health services morethan 90%of the time as compared to overall of 56% HNO 2021 P75

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Key Protection risks : Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:mitigations: Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM), as well as Health Cluster Flash Updates.- Continuing advocacy efforts by the health sector and UN agencies with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). Support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership is critical in highlighting protection challenges facing health workers across Syria.- Providing preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents (GBV)mitigations :- Standard operating procedures for medical referrals should include provisions such as:o Ensuring parent or guardian is identified to accompany all minor patients when referred to a new location for care;o Engaging child protection actors when a mother is in medical distress and cannot return home to care for her minor children; ando Advocacy with relevant authorities must be carried out before and during referral to avoid separation of a child from his/her guardian.- Ensuring confidentiality of all patients – for exams, communication and record-keeping.- As part of prevention and early response efforts, health actors should consider supporting local women’s organizations to raise awareness about GBV, its consequences and where to get help.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Considering the high vulnerability of the project beneficiaries, HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per facility. In addition, the services will be provided on a free-of-charge basis for all those in need regardless of their individual gender groupings. HIHFAD will ensure the privacy of the patients, where special attention will be paid to ensure the availability of safe and comfortable examination settings that supports the patient’s privacy. Furthermore, sex-separated toilets and breastfeeding corners will be ensured available as well. HIHFAD will make sure that the individuals of vulnerable groups are always accompanied by either their own caregivers or by pre-defined facility staff, so such individuals are additionally protected against any type of harassment or abuse while at the supported facilities.The facilities will be ensured to be well lighted and ventilated and the electric outlets will be rendered either high enough or provided with protecting means, so they are inaccessible to children

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

below the potential risks and the mitigation depending on the area situation1-Attacks on the civilian population and health infrastructure, which may result in the destruction of the facility or the death of the project staff. Possibility: Low Management:Review and adjust security measures on a daily basisParticipation in the UN de-confliction mechanism: communicating and pinning projectsites as independent neutral humanitarian locations, in order to prevent bombing by international actors.Emphasis on neutrality, independence, and the adherence to humanitarian principles and IHL2-Intensification of the conflict, leading to delays in implementation, due to access restrictionsPossibility: Low Management : Temporary suspension of project activities and minimization of staff movementAdjustment of time and the activity plan3-Drastic changes in the context of the Syrian conflict, such as extensive changes in the front lines, leading to mass expulsions and/or access restrictions Possibility: Low Management:- Provision of emergency plans e.g. through the deployment of personnel in mobile clinics in the event of a sudden IDP influx- Constant and accurate context analysis and situation assessment by the security focal point of the project partner HIHFAD4- interruption of WHO supply line-Temporary closure of the border crossing between Turkey and Syria for humanitarian aid, leading to delays in implementation.Possibility: low management:- Adjustment of time and activity plan- - Adjustment of time and activity plan- close contact with local Turkish authorities, in particular for border crossing of goods and personnel- advocate for continuing the current Cross Border mechanism
