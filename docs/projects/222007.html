Project Summary

This project will improve the livelihood and resilience of food insecure, and crisis affected populations by providing gender sensitive lifesaving and livelihoods assistance in our project operation locations. Particularly, the aim of this project is to alleviate hunger and destitution among the communities that we serve. Plan international will implement the following activities to achieve this goal, including cash-based interventions, distribution of livelihoods inputs and youth economic empowerment activities such as skills training, entrepreneurship development and access to finance. This project will be implemented in some counties of the six state that Plan International has presence including Lakes, Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei. In total, this project will benefit 132,419 populations (boys, girls, men, and women, VSLAs groups established, setting up vegetable demonstration gardens, and distribution of seeds and tools to the vulnerable people at the community level. The community volunteers and community representatives and local government staff in counties will be capacitate in different ways, including training, involving them in support supervisions, feedback and complaint meetings, and review meetings so as to ensure effectiveness and project sustainability.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analyzed) will likely experience Phase 3 or above. Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4, and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).The slow implementation of the revitalized peace agreement has led to increased insecurity in some parts of the country and therefore, continued people displacement, loss of assets, disruption of livelihoods including market and adaption of destructive copping strategies such as cutting trees for firewood and charcoal, child labor, abandonment of children. (IPC April 2022 projection report). In order to prevent the continued deterioration of food insecurity, malnutrition and associated social crisis and child protection risks, the need to provide timely and appropriate recovery and resilience building humanitarian and livelihood assistance in 2024 is more needed and important than ever. In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).Based on our previous experience in programming in areas of our operation informed by the various assessments and review meetings done with various stakeholders plus discussions with communities, other NGOs and local government in Malakal, Maban, Renk, Rumbek, Torit, Rubkona, Mayendit, Baliet and Leer counties.
