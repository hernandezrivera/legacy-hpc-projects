Project Summary

Tdh has been implementing child protection activities in South Sudan since 2014. Tdh has demonstrated adaptability and creativity in its CP programming, implementing in some areas where there are no other CP actors. As one of the principal CP actors in South Sudan, Tdh is noted for its commitment to effective coordination and communication within the sector. Tdh is an active member of the Case Management Task Force in Juba. Tdh has the tools, guidelines and policies in place that ensure we are able to implement and monitor quality programming. Tdh has embraced a holistic approach to programming whereby integrated, multi-sectoral programs aim to improve outcomes for children and contribute to improving the broader child protection outcomes, including a focus on improving children’s sense of security and self-confidence through physical, social and educational program activities. Tdh’s integrated approach links CP interventions with its FSL, WASH and education interventions.
CP/FSL integration is a priority for Tdh to improve the child wellbeing, as caregiver/household economic vulnerabilities can lead to direct and indirect negative protection coping strategies and CP risks. Improving household food security and access to livelihoods, alongside school feeding and school gardens, has previously proven to help children to stay in school and decrease school dropout due to emergency shocks and negative coping strategies to survival including early marriage, child labour and exploitation.
Similarly, WASH/CP integration will be achieved by increasing sustainable use of protection sensitive WASH services such as water supply and latrines, alongside comprehensive hygiene promotion and ensuring appropriate, sustainable WASH in schools, with the aim of ultimately leading towards improved child protection outcomes: children are able to live and learn in a clean and safe environment. Child friendly areas and schools can be assisted to meet minimum standards of WASH facilities, separated by gender and have adequate hand washing facilities installed. Additionally, WASH promotion in communities can decrease rates of open defecation especially prevalent among children, by promoting community solutions for increased access to child friendly latrines, where small children do not use adult latrines for fear of injury. GBV risks can also be reduced by increasing safe access to WASH for female community members.
The Tdh CP approach will prioritize rigor in assessments and ensuring that programs are directly addressing the specific needs of the targeted beneficiaries. In addition to implementing direct child protection activities, including case management and psychosocial support for specific vulnerabilities among children, particularly UASC and CAAFAG, Tdh will also ensure mainstreaming activities, including training other actors on CP concepts.
In Juba an inter-agency CP assessment on street children has been discussed but is still pending, and Tdh could consider spearheading this assessment, to explore the uncovered needs with respect to juvenile justice, street children and the worst forms of child labour. Tdh aims to reduce the protection vulnerabilities among children and youth in the IDP and host communities. For the year to come, Tdh sees great opportunities for this multi-sectoral, integrated approach to CP as a means to expand our geographic presence in South Sudan, to respond where priorities emerge.

Needs

Civilians including children have been facing extreme events with high impact, including destruction of homes, possessions and lands, witnessing casualties of war, the death of loved ones, kidnappings and many other forms of abuse. Long term displacement has led to economic hardship and widespread food insecurity as land and livestock are inaccessible. Continued fighting and insecurity mean that displacement is still ongoing. IDPs face many challenges as resources and access to services are limited, especially in Yei town.Children are often separated from caregivers as a result of conflict and can be forced to care for younger siblings or to work to support families, as well as being exposed to risks of neglect and psycho-social challenges. Many children still experience high levels of stress caused by witnessing traumatic events and increased hardships in living conditions. As they are unaccompanied they are more at risk to face abuse and exploitation. In Yei, 3 of every 10 children drop out of school because they are separated from their families and cannot support themselves. An assessment conducted in July 2018 has revealed than 68% of the children of the community were not going to school. The majority of children in Yei are in need of material support for basic needs, especially among the UASC, and of a social supportive and protecting environment that promotes their emotional, psychological and physical wellbeing. Despite the alarming incidence of child rights’ violations and abuses, the protection system in Yei is still in need of strengthening. People have limited access to legal services, justice, and police: arbitrary arrests are frequent, the police lack the means to run investigations and the judiciary system has not regained its functionality fully after the crisis in 2016. Currently there are no judges permanently seated in Yei. Cases are left a long time without trail and there is not adequate support for children in conflict with the law. Even though an SGBV referral pathway exists and a police officer is mandated to collect complaints of SGBV in Yei Civil Hospital, women do not report for fear of stigmatization and negative repercussions on their personal and family relations within communities. A Tdh assessment conducted in Mvolo in May 2018, identified child protection and GBV risks such as early marriage and early pregnancy among girls aged 12 or 13 years old. Such issues are also recurrent in the Juba PoCs where sexual violence is a frequent concern. Many unaccompanied children have found spontaneous foster care families within community structures. Nevertheless, the heavy burden that fostered children represent for foster families have not been addressed and some negative coping strategies and risks have already been identified. Strengthening the capacities of these foster families through basic services support such as food distribution and protection awareness raising & prevention messaging will prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanism leading to child protection concerns. Also, unaccompanied and separated children will be prioritized by the program, among other vulnerabilities. Vulnerability criteria are adapted to each area of operation based on SOPs developed at national level, in order to ensure that the most serious protection concerns are being addressed. Children with disabilities are exposed to social isolation and lack access to education or any recreational activity. There is also a need to support caregivers with children with disabilities to increase their access to services. There is a need to increase efforts towards demobilization in Yei River State where changes in context mean that more areas may open up to humanitarian assistance and more can be known about the situation of CAAFAG. Many children are still associated with armed groups. Vocational training, education opportunities and FTR services can help such children to avoid re-recruitment after demobilization

Activities

Tdh will provide a comprehensive CP program while building capacity of humanitarian actors on CP and ensure understanding of international standards and best practices. Tdh will respond to the protection needs of vulnerable children through provision of CM and access to community-based PSS. Tdh will conduct in depth analysis to understand and respond to underlying factors in order to design interventions that aim at reduce the cause of the protection concerns. In coordination with UNMISS and UNICEF, Tdh will aid CAAFAG after demobilization and target their specific needs in order to support their reintegration through access to services and material support. For UASC cases, Tdh makes best use of community mechanisms of offering support for such children, linking with community leaders who are knowledgeable about the traditional and existing fostering systems. Tdh engages with the FTR networks (community-CPIMS) in order to restore family links and trace families. Cross border separation is high in Yei and Tdh will link with ICRC and SCI services to restore family links where possible. Tdh will identify and train foster carers to provide interim alternative care arrangements for children, in close collaboration with the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare. Tdh will conduct comprehensive household vulnerability assessments among foster care families and CM beneficiaries to ensure integrated protection and economic targeting for FSL programming, with the aim of understanding and responding to caregiver/household economic vulnerabilities. Although foster caring is a practice in communities during crisis, this level of training and support to foster carers can add value to community coping mechanisms. Tdh will maintain CFS to ensure children access a safe environment for playing and learning. Sites in need of CFS will be mapped along with other CP actors, to ensure maximum coverage of areas in need. PSS activities provided in CFS include lifeskills and basic education are gateways to empowering children to gain confidence and building social cohesion skills among peers. Tdh PSS animators will run activities to improve children’s sense of security and self-confidence through physical, social and educational games. Parenting support groups will be established to minimize risks children face at home and in the community, and female friendly spaces and activities will be targeted at times appropriate for girls to attend. These spaces will allow working with girls and women to identity areas of risk to them and agree on ways to mitigate such risks. Awareness sessions on issues such as menstrual health and family planning will empower girls and women make decisions regarding sexual health. GBV issues will also be discussed with male adolescents in order to increase awareness for both genders and identify the common concerns and causes of early marriage. Tdh will reinforce community based protection networks in host and IDP communities and strengthen their awareness on child rights and prevention of violence and abuse against vulnerable children. Working closely with community based protection networks will also increase the number of safe referrals to Tdh for child protection concerns. Referrals will also be encouraged from key civil actors such as health workers, police gender desk and teachers, To increase awareness of child protection prevention and response in schools, helpdesks will be established that are capable of making safe referrals to relevant service providers as well as Tdh for CM services. Tdh will strengthen community networks to operate complaints and feedback mechanisms, including through Help Desks.Tdh will support CP coordination so as not to overlap with other CP actors. In Juba, Tdh proposes to support the juvenile justice system, integrating CM and PSS for incarcerated children, and advocate for legal support and legal representation for children

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Communication events will be conducted at the beginning of the project to sensitize beneficiaries on the intended assistance to be provided. These will include awareness sessions and information sessions held in community areas such as schools and churches. Project kick-off and project closure meetings with stakeholders, are also planned to present Tdh, the objectives, activities and outcomes of the project. In addition, community based protection networks and community leaders in Yei will be especially sensitized on the project in order for them to best communicate the available services to beneficiaries. Where possible, posters will referral pathways and other information can be provided to communities and schools to present the information in a visual manner. Where possible and appropriate information will also be aired on radio shows to reach beneficiaries who obtain their news this way, as many do in the affected areas.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Taking into account low literacy rates among beneficiaries, the project ensures to disseminate information both verbally and visually as well as with written information. The programme will target both male and female (children, adolescents and adults) and will focus on ensuring that gender and equity are promoted throughout its implementation and remain a central message. Given the high concern for GBV issues in the context,girls’ engagement in the activities, especially for adolescent girls will be prioritised and messages framed around positive reinforcement of access to education and livelihood for females. The focus on gender equity approach will be taken into consideration in order to avoid harmful interventions. As the project implementation planning will be participatory with the community, Tdh will prioritize with community input both how to best reach specific target groups and what messages are most adapted to men, women, girls and boys. Bearing in mind the role women play in caring for households, and the common occurrence of single female headed households, messages will focus around empowering such beneficiaries into social and economic security as well as psycho-social wellbeing. In some cases it may be beneficial to provide gender separated awareness sessions, depending on the topic. When promoting child rights within a community, messages must be delivered accurately to elders and caregivers in the community regarding the roles and responsibilities of children as well as of adults in protecting rights of children. In line with the principle of do not harm, such messages should be adapted to the context so that they harmonize with existing familiar structures and not be misunderstood by the community as eradicating traditional practices, unless harmful to children.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

As a child protection actor, Tdh puts a specific focus on the prevention of exposure to protection risks (particularly child trafficking, smuggling, delinquency and sexual exploitation and abuse), through comprehensive access to services. Tdh regional experience will support a mainstreamed strategy of internal and external capacity building on trafficking, SGBV and sexual exploitation and abuse prevention and response mechanisms as relevant to each area of intervention, as the extent of each of these practices may vary in each area of operation. Through strengthening the existing foster care networks and putting basic criteria in place for carers in communities, Tdh will reduce the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse in alternative care PSS activities such as focus group discussions with adolescents and adults will also seek to increase awareness of GBV risks and empower female beneficiaries to increase their own sense of safety in communities. Furthermore, Tdh internal policies include child safeguarding policy and a prevention against abuse policy, which all staff, volunteers and partners are required to agree to prior to engagement with Tdh. This measure reduces the risk of abuse during implementation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Awareness sessions are planned to present Tdh, the objectives & activities of the project. Focus group discussions are conducted to discuss the vulnerability concept and agree on the selection criteria. Finally, consultations with communities are conducted to tailor the project to the needs (e.g. definition of the content of kits). Community members are mobilized based on the objectives of the consultations, thus, foster care network consultations in Yei were conducted with existing foster carers in the communities, whereas vocational training needs consultations were done with youth of both genders.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The locations proposed by Tdh were previously assessed and a multi sectorial analysis was conducted. Tdh is deeply engaged to ensure comprehensive analysis of protection and child protection into all its programmes. To make sure all groups were consulted and that the risks they faced were taken into consideration assessments and beneficiaries selection process were done through focus groups discussion with IDPs, host communities, women and children. Tdh recognizes CBI can increase protection risks and will mainstream protection elements throughout the program that mitigate these risks and account for the local contextual challenges. Additionally, taking into account the protection considerations, including risk analysis related to child labour (road construction or increased farming capacity requiring children to work.) and opportunities mapping, including early childhood development/CFS training and paid jobs for mothers In Yei through its Child Protection activities and involvement in Protection coordination Tdh is also well informed about the protection risks faced by the distinctive groups. From previous experience in operational areas, Tdh is aware of the potential risk of creating factors for vulnerable caregivers to seek to benefit from services by creating unstable environments for children. Tdh mitigates these risks by strong sensitization with communities, adapting the direct support given to families closely with the context and taking measures to ensure harm is not done to a children in order for them to benefit from material support.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Tdh context and security analysis provides a broad picture of the access barriers faced by populations to access humanitarian services and by humanitarian actors to access populations in need. The prevailing context, coupled with the scale and duration of displacement the conflict have severely impacted the livelihood and overstretched resilience of affected communities. Further, the conflict has exacerbated tension along ethnic lines, vulnerability of women and children through sexual assault or kidnapping and discrimination and deprivation of basic rights. Tdh regularly updates a risk analysis of its area of operations looking at context and conflict evolution, involved actors’ strategy and perception of Tdh’s interventions, impact of its activities on the communities it serves or does not serve, and expectation of the same communities and actors. For the proposed program design, implementation and monitoring, including complaints and feedback mechanisms, will ensure stakeholder input into the access analysis. Tdh will also aim to provide humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations in areas that have received limited humanitarian response and hence prioritizing people having limited/no access to assistance.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Through FGDs, feedback and complaints mechanism, and PDMs Tdh will ensure beneficiaries provide direct feedback regarding access barriers, including assessing their ability to access services in safety and dignity, and analysis of the appropriateness of Tdh’s ability to access beneficiary communities in a manner that does not negatively impact on them. This information will be used to adjust implementation modalities as needed.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through FGDs, feedback and complaints mechanism, and PDMs Tdh will ensure beneficiaries input to design safer and “Do no harm” programming methodologies. In order to mitigate any identified risk, place of distribution will be chosen in coordination with heads of communities and in compliance with Tdh security policy. Additionally, safety considerations for the most vulnerable people (elderly, children head of households and disabled people) will be tailored according to their needs. During Tdh intervention protection mainstreaming sensitization will be undertaken in order to mitigate the risks that children and women face on a regular basis (worst form of child labour, GBV). In addition, surveys designed to evaluate feelings of safety at intervals of the project can be conducted in order to observe shifts in trends or improvements in community level safety, especially among children.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

To ensure dignity of the beneficiary the project will adopt a community based protection analysis by using participatory methodology in the beneficiary selection and will engage with the community in the design and implementation of the project and will strengthen the community prevention and response mechanisms at the local level through its child protection project and protection mainstreaming measures. Moreover by including local partners in its intervention Tdh will ensure the project is designed and implemented accordingly to the local needs. The different stakeholders (community members, local actors, state representatives and local partners) will have access to feedback mechanism that will be signed to assess whether dignity of beneficiary have been respected, Moreover surveys and final evaluation will also assess this component.
