Project Summary

The Gaza Strip after 14 years of strict blockade, is witnessing a deterioration of the socioeconomic and mental health status of its population aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the recent violent escalations in May 2021, and consequent decreased economic opportunities. The most impacted families include already vulnerable population groups such as economically fragile households on the waiting list for the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD); young graduates or unemployed workers who cannot secure financial opportunities with further deteriorated wellbeing, and children and women facing difficulties in accessing specialised protection services. To respond to this situation and cover the most pressing needs, Action Against Hunger in partnership with AISHA will support 6,050 individuals to: Support a total of 520 families within the most impacted population groups described above with unconditional and conditional cash transfers to cover their basic needs; 2. Provide tailored case management and assistance plan for households in need of protection related services. Services will be provided for identified gender-based violence (GBV) survivors and specific attention to the needs of people with disabilities, referrals to community-based safe spaces and legal services will be ensured. Female headed households and households with members with disabilities will be prioritized through the intervention with a comprehensive protection assistance based on needs identified to improve their psychological wellbeing. Financial wellbeing will be increased to ultimately improve food security through unconditional and conditional cash assistance, including emergency cash for protection for severe cases identified. This intervention will answer the second specific objective of the Food Security Sector, namely supporting households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs. It will also address the second objective of the Protection cluster namely: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment within all planned activities. Ultimately, the project aims to increase beneficiaries’ coverage of basic needs as well as GBV and protection cases management, for households particularly exposed to a wide range of protection issues.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic declared in March 2020 in the occupied Palestinian territory added an additional layer of vulnerability in the Gaza Strip, already crippled by a protracted crisis resulting from the Israeli blockade of 14 years, previous wars, a quasi stalling economy and an internal political division between the Palestinian Authority and the de-facto authority. As of mid-September, COVID-19 cases increased significantly in communities reaching 34.5% of positivity rate (Ministry of Health). Additionally, the violent escalation on the Gaza Strip between the 10th and 21st of May 2021 worsened further existing vulnerabilities among the population. As a results of the escalation, 260 Palestinians were killed, including 67 children, including at least 129 civilians. Over 2,200 Palestinians were injured during the hostilities, including 685 children and 480 women, some of whom may suffer a long-term disability requiring rehabilitation (OHCHR, 2021). Consequently, the mental and psychosocial health of the population, along with resources to meet basic needs and opportunities to secure livelihood opportunities, are further weakened. As per the recent Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted in July 2021, 22% of surveyed households reported a temporary loss of employment and 6 % a permanent loss of employment as a result of covid-19 measures. 50% reported a decrease in their monthly income due to COVID-19 and 50% reported that the escalation has had an impact on their livelihood and assets. The lack of income is a main driver of food insecurity, limiting financial access to food and other basic needs, and negative coping strategies by chronically and newly vulnerable households. In the second quarter 2021 there was 68% of male participation in the labour force and only 17% of female participation (PCBS 2021). An average of 61.4 %HHs reported increased debts as a result of COVID-19 in Gaza, considering about 75% reported buying food on credit (MSNA, 2021). As per the HNO (2022), about 1,1 mio of the population in Gaza is considered food insecure and in need of assistance. In addition to decreasing economic power, associated protection issues have increased within the Gaza Strip with 1,152,000 people in need for protection services in Gaza (HNO, 2022). Consequences of the escalation, on top of the worsening of the socio-economic situation have led to the deterioration of mental health status, increase of stress anxiety and depression, lower educational outcomes as chronic and distressing issues among vulnerable households. An aggravating concern is mental health of young people, according to some surveys 82% of adolescents in Gaza reported overall poor-very poor mental well-being (WHO and Juzoor, 2021), noting that 67% of youth (18-29 years) are without training or employment in Gaza (PCBS, 2021). Women as primary caregivers have been considerably affected by the increased mental load within households that recently lost their source of income. Based on results of the emergency response to the escalation in May led by AAH, callers for remote protection services are majorly men (58%). Yet women callers increased of 40% since the start of covid-19 with an increase of 58% of GBV cases (Mathieu-Tahboub, et al. 2021). Main needs concerned ‘psychological violence’ for both sexes, women then mostly request support against physical and legal violence (SAWA report, August 2021). A report from UNWOMEN in 2020 showed that women have higher stress levels (82%) than men (71%).According to AISHA’s recent assessments (June and July 2021), interviewed women (280 women and 53 staff members) reported that their main problem is having limited access to community-based integrated MHPSS and child protection services. This impact on the population’s wellbeing is further aggravated by the stress associated to loss of income and has led to increased domestic violence, GBV and child protection concerns (Care – OCHA, September 2020).

2 - Activities

The project will achieve its objectives through:(1) Selection of 400 Households for MPCA: targeted households to receive assistance will be households whose main bread winner has lost their livelihoods due to the COVID-19 restriction measures or as a result of the escalations of May 2021. AAH will be responsible for the household selection and will identify the target group in coordination with the MoSD referring to registered households waiting to benefit from the Social Safety Net (SSN) program. Households will be selected and prioritized considering the criteria among others: female headed household, women breadwinners, income and expenditures variations, source of income, debts holdings, food security level, daily worker status, household composition, coping strategies, financial stress. (2) Unconditional unrestricted cash support for basic food and other needs (MPCA): 6 months of MPCA will be transferred to selected households to provide financial access to cover their basic needs. The transfer value of 328 USD corresponds to recommended transfer value (1’046 NIS) by the Cash Working Group (CWG) and the FSS. Assistance will be received through Palpay system where right holders will be able to redeem their assistance through the Bank of Palestine ATM locations or/and direct purchase in accredited stores.(3) AISHA will support all households receiving MPCA with tailored household visits to assess decision-making and tasks repartition among the household and ensure that the cash assistance and associated responsibilities in deciding spending priorities and access to markets do not create further harm within the household. Counsellors will conduct regular visits to households to assess household dynamics, detect cases in need of child protection, MHPSS and/or GBV support and provide conflict and stress management techniques.(4) Selection of 120 unemployed graduates or workers (70 women and 50 men) to benefit from temporary work opportunities by AAH. The selection process of young graduates will be conducted within the HHs supported with MPCA by AAH and referring to partners’ channels to identify unemployed workers in coordination with the MoL and MoSD. Selection and prioritization criteria will include among others: breadwinner status, number of years without employment, relevant skills for the market and enterprises, socioeconomic status of the household. Temporary work placements will last between 4 and 6 months (340 USD/Month). Insurance at the place of work will be covered by AAH and minimum decent work conditions within the hosting company will be verified by AAH employability team. (5) AISHA will support placed graduates and workers within their host company by providing counselling at the place of work. During these visits, counsellors will detect cases in need of MHPSS and/or GBV support and verify that no abuse in the place of work is taking place, and that both women and men feel safe in their tasks and responsibilities as per all parties contractual obligations. PSEA standards from both AAH and AISHA policies will be followed through with selected employers. (6) Protection and GBV case management by AISHA:. AISHA will conduct a diagnostic assessment with case managers to identify protection concerns and tailor activities according to sex, age and needs observed and expressed by household members. Mental health and psychosocial support will be provided along with services for identified GBV survivors, with specific attention to the needs of people with disabilities and referral to community-based safe spaces and legal services.. Access to information about protection services through permanent hotlines and social media will be shared and accessible to participants during the intervention and beyond. Additionally, AISHA will train all non-protection specialist staff with AAH teams to activate and improve identification of protection needs, PSEA and referral of cases in coordination with child protection and GBV working groups of the protection cluster.

3 - Indicators

AAH will develop a comprehensive Monitoring and Evaluation Framework for the action using gender and age disaggregated data analysis. The AAH MEAL team will support AISHA’s staff responsible to ensure the monitoring system relating to impartial beneficiary selection and effective implementation. Joint MEAL planning through the development of project tracking tool will be conducted through participatory approach including AISHA as local partner. In addition, AAH technical & MEAL teams will conduct regular meetings with AISHA to follow up the progress and ensure activities are implemented according to the project work plan developed together. AAH MEAL team will be responsible to collect data and monitor the desired outcomes and impact of the activities on households’ satisfaction, relevance and use of the assistance and protection services. The AAH robust monitoring and evaluation system, and beneficiary feedback system will ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner, beneficiary selection prioritizing vulnerable families. The main MEAL activities will be the baseline and endline assessments of all households on both food security and protection dimensions, 3 Post Distribution Monitoring surveys for cash assistance will be conducted to assess quality, safety and accessibility to basic food needs and other basic needs, taking into account protection factors like safety and dignity. The 2nd PDM will be conducted after the third cash instalment to compare it with the results of the 1st one. A final PDM will assess the improvement in families’ food consumption as well as reduced negative coping mechanisms like using debts, the impact of cash assistance on minimizing protection threats on women and vulnerable household members and implementation of AISHA MHPSS and case management activities. On site or remote monitoring (according to movement restrictions and current context) will be implemented by the MEAL team during the action to obtain beneficiaries’ feedback and collect observations on quality of services provided and BNFs satisfaction as well as draw learnings for activities. The following indicators will measure the intervention: Food security dimensions will be measured through the following indicators: # of individuals benefiting from other conditional cash support (target: 660); # of individuals benefiting from unconditional cash support (targets: 2,200) through the PDM surveys the satisfaction of assistance received and work placement will be also measured through the indicator "% of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable, and participatory manner (target:80%). At the end of the intervention the socioeconomic wellbeing of supported households will be measured through the use of coping strategies and the proxy indicators: % of households with decreased value of Livelihoods Coping Strategy Index (L-CSI) (target: 60%) % of HHs reported satisfaction on safe and dignified access to AAH and AISHA services” (target: 80%), % of households with reported decreased financial stress) (target: 60%). Protection dimensions will be measured through the following indicators: # of vulnerable men and women benefiting from structured psychosocial services (Target: 520); # of GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving MHPSS services (Target: 100); and # of humanitarian staff (non-GBV specialists) who have received training in risk mitigation, psychological first aid, and referral (Target: 60). In order to evaluate the satisfaction of the beneficiaries of the interventions regarding their needs and wellbeing, the following indicators will be used: % of individuals satisfied by MHPSS activities received (target: 70%) % of women reporting feeling safer having access to new services (target: 60%) All indicators will be disaggregated by sex and age.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

According to the MSNA conducted by REACH in July 2021, 77% of surveyed individuals replied to preferring assistance through cash modality. This finding is in line with AAH baseline assessments and PDMs conducted in 2021 where majority of households reported preferring cash over e-voucher or in-kind assistance. Cash assistance will be provided through the Bank of Palestine with ATM cards if the option of financial inclusion bank account would not be available with the Bank at time of implementation. Financial inclusion bank accounts would allow households to manage their assistance and savings according to their priorities and timing of their needs and building without being limited by the expiry date of the delivery mechanism itself. Transfer values are in line with recommendation of the CWG. Protection support, namely mental health and psychosocial support will be provided through mixed modalities of in person and remote sessions taking into consideration current movement restrictions.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike will be able to reach out to the project with complaints, suggestions, and any other feedback through the mechanisms managed by AAH MEAL Unit and AISHA. FCMs channels will be promoted through through information sessions and information material (e.g., flyer) handed over during projects’ activities (e.g. assessments, distributions, training sessions etc.). Our team consults and meets with rights holders when carrying out needs assessments in relevant beneficiary areas and employs a series of methods to ensure that their perspectives and priorities are addressed in the project design. These methods used to receive feedback include interviews with key informants, home visits, group discussions (separating men and women to ensure a safe space when discussing sensitive subjects), questionnaires and regular monitoring sessions to ensure that the project is appropriate and relevant to their needs. The information collected through FCMs or directly in the field will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm is done and to guarantee continuous improvements of assistance delivered respecting internal humanitarian standards, including informing the budget. Beneficiary feedback will be analysed and presented in a systematic way, including to project partners that we work with. The AAH MEAL unit will be in continuous contact with the Program and Coordination teams to analyse the data, address concerns, respond to complaints and work with the project team to make adjustments to the project. AAH MEAL unit produces a regular report and gender sensitive data analysis of the complaints and feedback on a regular basis. It is shared internally for learning purposes. AAH and AISHA internal PSEA channels will be activated when relevant claims are transmitted and re-directed to HR departments.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

AAH and AISHA pay particular attention to the way vulnerable groups are included in their work. Information on the feedback and complaints mechanism is given to every beneficiary individually when assessing households and later while distributing assistance at household level. To ensure that feedback mechanisms are accessible to all, AAH and AISHA have a variety of different methods so that people can choose which they prefer (e.g. email, phone, field, office), AAH promotes mainly the use of a free phone hotline to give feedback and complaints to avoid having vulnerable population spending time and money in coming to AAH or AISHA offices. To accommodate and cater to some of these different groups, home visits or private spaces can be provided, to investigate and discuss a complaint received. During regular household visits (during hours agreed with and convenient with household members most at risk) AISHA counsellors and AAH field staff will ensure that all members had access to the information about the FCMs and are well understood. Field staff will record mobility, visibility, educational or other difficulties impairing access to the information to adjust channels and content of the communication.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. AAH has mechanisms in place to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct and our held accountable if they do not respect it. AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Risks assessments and analysis of the protection context are let for all the projects, and even more so when implemented with at risk groups (e.g. minors) or in areas where risk of doing harm is exacerbated (e.g. inside health facilities in COVID-19 times). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, which focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication is in place. COVID-19 protective measures and equipment will be applied throughout all interventions to further prevent harm being done to beneficiaries, communities and staff members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most in need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE ́ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviours to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
