Project Summary

Peace Corps proposes an Emergency life-saving integrated quality Education for conflict and flood affected school going children(3-17 years) from IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities in Jonglei, Lakes and Warrap States. This project targets 4,768 beneficiaries of which 4,570 (2,230 boys, 2,340 girls) will be school going children aged 3-17 years and 198 adults (126 Male, 72 Female) including disabled and marginalised children from IDPs, Returnees and host vulnerable communities. This project will run for a period of 12 months covering the three terms and the targeted 12 schools in selected counties. The proposed education project will incorporate and complement life-saving components such as psycho-social support, disaster risk reduction mechanism, and availability of gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools is essential for keeping children in the classroom and ensuring their safety and well-being, with clean and secure separate toilets for girls and boys and hand-washing facilities to both students and teachers. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Health and Protection (CP&GBV) to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations. The key considerations underpinning the design of this project were: the selection of locations with existing gaps in current programming, a targeting criterion that is responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable groups, and an approach that ensures complementarity with ongoing interventions to maximize efficiency and impact. The key activities to be implemented are: Conduct Community mobilization to the communities persuading parents to enrol 4,570(2,230 boys, 2,340 girls) school going children and youths in school (51% girls), Establish/Rehabilitate 12 temporary learning spaces of 2 classroom each in line with INEE's Minimum, Establish/Rehabilitate 16 gender sensitive segregated WASH facilities fully equipped with hand washing facilities in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard. Train 66(24female) of teachers on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills, Distribute learning and recreational materials to children and youth (6-17 years), Distribute of ECD kits to 240(130girls) Children aged (3-5 years old), Distribute sanitation and hygiene kits (MHM) to 240 adolescent girls, Train 400(240 girls) adolescent and youth on life skill and vocational courses, Train 132 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Establishment of School safety audits aimed at identifying and mitigating protection risks on the way to school and inside school. The project will also mainstream gender, GBV, conflict sensitivity and protection with hopes of contributing towards protection of children from risks, GBV and other protection challenges that have a potential of disrupting their education, enhance their psycho-social development, improve hygiene and sanitation and alleviate strains on their academic performance. PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and also the CCCM Cluster Coordinator for Pibor GPAA. PCO has been implementing School Meal Program in Counties of Wau and Jur River Since 2018 to 2025 supported by WFP, also implemented integrated Education and Nutrition project in 5 schools in counties of Twic and Pibor GPAA under SSHF SA1 funded by UNOCHA in 2023 and has been implementing MYRP Education Programme funded by ECW since 1st May 2020 to 30th June 2026 in Counties of Gogrial West,Tonj East and Tonj North of Warrap State.

1. Needs

The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection services in South Sudan (According to HNO- 2024).The HNO estimated that 2.64 million people, about 50 per cent of the 5.59 million people in need of emergency life-saving integrated quality Education services. The Education system and infrastructure in south Sudan are fragile with limited equitable access to quality education for boys, girls and youth aged 3-17 years who represent 44% of the population (According to HNO 2022 report). This includes an estimated 3.4 million (51% are girls) school-aged children in needs of education services estimated to be out of school representing one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world, have no access to pre-primary, primary and secondary education in 2022. South Sudan’s education system has been negatively affected by protracted conflict, economic crisis, inadequate access to quality learning spaces, teaching and learning materials, inadequate teachers training, limited sanitation facilities at school, low enrolment and high drop-out rates. Many schools have been destroyed, children and teachers are too scared to attend because they fear for their life, while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of a chronically devalued currency. The continued influx of returnees from Sudan has continued to stretch the already dire resources. These factors have led to a decrease in enrolment rates of school age children and an increase in the drop-out rate of school age children and also exposed them to different form of GBV including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies especially amongst girls. The negative impact on children has also increased as the education system is under severe strain due to the government’s inability to pay teachers regularly or provide any learning materials to support them to perform their functions or incentive to keep them motivated. Similarly, the overall food security situation has further deteriorated. The intervention logic is that given the situation of the (largely man-made) disaster in South Sudan, there is an urgent need to support schools with interventions for ensuring equitable access to quality education and preventing school dropouts. Increased access to quality learning opportunities helps increase enrolment of Out of school children and improve retention in Schools. This project is linked to the general strategy of saving lives and providing timely integrated multi- sectorial assistance and reinforcing protection, particularly connected to the 2024 education cluster strategy by ensuring that children and youth (3-17 years) have access to quality formal and non-formal education and that girls and boys already in school are retained, improved school attendance and by promoting emergency WASH in schools and mainstreaming child protection and GBV. The available limited sanitation facilities are not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. The project will also contribute to lower the risk of abuse and exploitation of children including early marriage, child labour and forced recruitment into armed groups. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, FSL, Protection (CP&GBV), Nutrition, Health to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations.

2. Project activities

The overall objective of this proposed plan is to focuses on Emergency life-saving integrated quality Education for conflict and flood affected to 4768 school going children and youths aged (3-17 years) and adult from IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities through implementation of the 3 Education cluster objectives. The key activities to be implemented are: Conduct Community mobilization to the communities persuading parents to enrol 4570(2,230boys, 2,340 girls) school going children and youths in school (51% girls), Establish/Rehabilitate 12 temporary learning spaces of 2 classroom each in line with INEE's Minimum, Establish/Rehabilitate 16 gender sensitive segregated WASH facilities fully equipped with hand washing facilities in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard. Train 66(24Female) of teachers on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills, Distribute learning and recreational materials to children and youth (6-17 years), Distribute of ECD kits to 360(130girls) Children aged (3-5 years old), Distribute sanitation and hygiene kits (MHM) to 240 adolescent girls, Train 400(240 girls) adolescent and youth on life skill and vocational courses, Train 132 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Promote enrolment and recruitment through “back to school” and “back to teaching” through use of radio talk show campaign, Train teachers on life-skills, psychological first aid and disaster risk reduction, Establishment of School safety audits aimed at identifying and mitigating protection risks on the way to school and inside school. PCO will also ensure that normal routine monitoring of all the activities implemented throughout the entire project life cycle is conducted to ensure activities are in line with the project design document and are successfully achieved. Reporting will also be done as required by donor and by the cluster through use of 5Ws shared monthly.
