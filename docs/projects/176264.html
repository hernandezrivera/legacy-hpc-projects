Project Summary

The food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate because of unresolved conflicts at national and sub-national level, recurrent natural disasters, compounded by climate change and poor disaster management capacity. In 2020, several acute crises hit South Sudan, further aggravating the pre-existing protracted crises and creating an unprecedented humanitarian situation: a desert locust upsurge, several episodes of flooding, and the COVID-19 pandemic. These further exacerbated the already fragile malnutrition situation in country.
In response to the high nutritional needs in South Sudan, WFP's project will facilitate the provision of curative and preventative nutrition services to populations affected by conflict and displacement. The project will ensure that IDPs, PoC populations, returnees and residents have access to emergency nutrition services in order to prevent morbidity and mortality due to moderate acute malnutrition. WFP will continue to work with cooperating partners to support an emergency nutrition response through a preventive supplementary feeding programme and treatment services for moderate acute malnutrition targeting children
aged 6-59 months, pregnant & lactating women, and people living with HIV/TB across
South Sudan.
In hard to reach areas, WFP will conduct direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will be coordinated closely with the Nutrition cluster and use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, education, FSL, WASH).

1 - Needs

The project aims to address the nutritional needs of vulnerable women and children in populations affected by food and nutrition insecurity resulting from conflict and displacement. The Food Security Monitoring System Round 25 (data collected in December 2019) showed that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was at 12.6 percent while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 3.3 percent, reflecting a slight increase over the same period in 2018 when the prevalence rate was 11.6 percent for GAM and 2.3 percent for SAM. According to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report some 5.8 million people (48 percent of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity from December 2020 to March 2021. This figure includes around 1.73 million people in IPC Phase 4 and 105,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. With the onset of the lean season, food security levels will drop. Between April and July 2021, the IPC estimates that 7.2 million people (60 percent of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 108,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity.Almost 70% of the counties in South Sudan are classified to be in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 3 and above, with the largest burden in the Greater Upper Nile followed by the Greater Bahar el Ghazal states.All this translates to approximately 1.8 million children under 5 years old and pregnant and lactating women suffering from acute malnutrition in 2021, which is the highest caseload since the start of the crisis in December 2013. Out of this, over one million children and nearly half a million pregnant and lactating women are expected to suffer from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM).

2 - Project activities

In collaboration with cooperating partners, WFP will:1. Provide children aged 6-59 months, PLHIV/TB and PLW screened in communities and identified as moderately malnourished with an appropriate treatment protocol, inclusive ofspecialized nutritious food (SNF), through the Targeted Supplementary Feedingprogramme (TSFP) under the CMAM approach. The expanded criteria will be used asstrategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. 2. Provide preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U5 and PLW in counties withGAM rates above 10% through a preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme in theaim of preventing acute malnutrition. The Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme will be implemented in combination with the General Food Distribution in areas of high food insecurity.3. Support an outreach network of community nutrition volunteers to provide nutrition and health message, outreach services, actively screen and identify those at risk or with acute malnutrition and ensure proper referral to health and nutrition services. 4. Ensure the project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (Health, FSL, Education, WASH, Protection, NFIs). 5. Ensure revised delivery modalities will allow for prepositioning in locations with irregular access year-round, in strategic hubs with the aim of reducing pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to UNS, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programmes are not disrupted. 6. Monitor and provide joint supervision through a network of field monitors, programme and nutrition staff to ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are meeting the high quality and reaching the most nutritionally vulnerable individuals.7. Contribute to enhancing the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information through supporting the the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System. WFP undertakes the FSNMS twice a year, once in the lean season and once post harvest and collects a range of food security and nutrition indicators to inform the situation in country and thereby programmatic adjustments as needed.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WFP is cognizant of its potential to advancing protection outcomes for the population of South Sudan due to the breadth of WFP access to populations, extensive size and coverage through operations and programmes and, the highly valuable commodity of food and cash assistance. WFP conducts regular protection assessments to capture information on WFP’s current and possible impact on dignity, integrity, safety, AAP, conflict (and Peacebuilding) and other key information. This is then utilized to inform activity design, adjustments and implementation that reflects community realities and preferences (as much as possible). WFP operates with the assumption that GBV is ongoing in all locations of assistance as a product of gender inequality and the social, political and economic environments in which it is operating. As such, WFP applies a Do No Harm approach through participation in interagency GBV Safety Audits and checklists to identify areas requiring attention and mitigation for GBV harm minimization. WFP has increased its investment in researching, partnering and focus on conflict sensitive programming.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WFP monitoring and data systems have been adjusted to include the Washington Group Questions to increase visibility and response to persons with disability. With regards to disability inclusion, WFP is finalizing guidelines on disability inclusion that can be used by WFP and its cooperating partners. The document will also list Food Security and livelihood activities that Persons living with disability can be actively engaged in line with WFP’s Policies. The guidelines will also provide a pool of indicators that Programme units can adopt to measure progress.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To strengthen community engagement, WFP will continue to support the formation and engagement of functioning of project management committees (PMC), comprising women, men, youth, persons with disabilities, and other individuals identified as vulnerable, thereby ensuring that the PMC is representative and inclusive, and empowering the most vulnerable to have their voice heard. The committees facilitate engagement, problem resolution, and communication with WFP to facilitate effective delivery of life-saving food and nutrition assistance.Before and during distributions, WFP and its partners provide information to the affected community on programme objective, activity, eligibility criteria, entitlement, and where and how to lodge grievances and suggestions. This information is disseminated through various communication channels, including radio broadcasts in the PoCs; and through community meetings, help desks, hotlines and appointed community mobilizers in remote locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Certain aspects of project assistance such as proximity of nutrition sites, availability of shelter, inclusion of particular topics during awareness raising, are informed by community needs/situation. Feedback from beneficiaries will be considered and where feasible changes are made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

WFP will mitigate contextual risks by monitoring the political situation and updating its business continuity plan accordingly. Conflict, protection analysis and rapid food insecurity analysis will help ensure that WFP's activities do not exacerbate conflict or tensions and do not have unintended negative effects on people living in conflict areas. Supply chain management and innovative programme modalities will provide flexibility to respond to changes in conditions. Advance financing, procurement through the Global Commodity Management Facility and pre-positioning will help to ensure that food and nutrition products are available as needs and transport conditions change. Transport capacities will be augmented and means of delivery adapted as needed. WFP will lobby for more functional corridors.Inadequate funding could prevent WFP from fully meeting the needs of vulnerable people.Operational shortfalls and pipeline breaks may damage WFP's reputation and its relationships with communities. To mitigate these risks, the country office has been continuously engaging with partnershighlighting needs and emphasizing the importance of allocation of resources early in the year. A refined prioritization strategy will also serve as a risk mitigation measure in case of resourcing shortfalls. To mitigate programmatic risks and facilitate the timely delivery of food and nutrition supplies to people in need, WFP carries out regular conflict and protection analysis. Through its dedicated access team it negotiates with local authorities to ensure access to affected populations and staff security and works with local cooperating partners to ensure access to affected populations and to improve targeting. Risk assessments for transport, storage and delivery help to ensure that operations continue with minimal disruptions and food assistance is timely delivered.
