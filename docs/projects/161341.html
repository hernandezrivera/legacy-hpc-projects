1 - Needs

The crisis that exploded in South Sudan, the world’s youngest and one of its poorest, most fragile countries in December 2013, has exacerbated Health infrastructures and services that have remained consistently below global standards leaving vulnerable communities with little resilience. These communities had limited to no basic services before the crisis began. As conflict becomes protracted, host communities remain vulnerable with limited coping abilities to withstand the shocks. The security situation in Mayendit and Manyo Counties has slightly improved this year though sporadic incidence continues to be reported, an example was in Mayendit, on 7th September 2019 in a primary school in Jaguar, youths from different clans clashed, leaving 8 Casualties who were brought to Rubkuay PHCC and further referred to ICRC after stabilizing the patients. Some incidences of high way attack and robbery continued to be reported with the latest incident reported in September 2019 (CASS, 2019). In Manyo county June 2017, fighting reached Wadakona and remaining civilians were evacuated to Renk (Radio Tamazuj, 2 February 2017). Mayendit and Manyo are one of the Counties with difficult terrain and very poor road infrastructure especially during the rainy seasons. The two counties have been affected by flooding with a notable example of Malkuer Payam of Mayendit where 459 households were heavily affected as reported through an assessment report conducted by the education cluster. Most facilities in Mayendit South are not accessible during the rainy season and others involve 6 to 7 hours walk through swamps, flooded areas to reach them. While for Manyo, most of the health facilities are located along the bank of the river with very poor WASH facilities making the area prone to water borne diseases with high incidences of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea (UNICEF, 2019). There is very poor referral network exacerbated by lack of telephone network in most parts of the Counties, no ambulance to facilitate referral. In Manyo, in addition to the poor road and net work connectivity, there are no humanitarian agencies present in the town apart from CASS. Most agencies provide nutrition services through mobile teams from Renk leaving most services such as WASH, FSL lacking in the county. According to the IPC ANM, the counties of Mayendit and Manyo are in the IPC phase 4 as of June 2019. This indicates that the two counties will face serious food insecurity in 2020. This situation has been made worse by the flood that has affected the two counties. A SMART survey done in Mayendit by Samaritan Purse indicates that the county’s GAM rate is at 27.3% and the one of Manyo county is also very high given the neighboring Renk county have a GAM rate of 32.1%. Through this project, CASS will improve access to essential primary health care services through the mobile teams, support to referral and strengthening services at 2 PHCC, 3 PHCU’s and 2 mobile team. CASS hopes by implementing this project, will build resilience of the population to cope during emergency. Being a national organization with better understanding of the local context, CASS hopes to be able to maintain access to basic nutrition services even within the mix of reduced humanitarian corridor. The project will target children and PLW as well as the elderly and all the vulnerable community. It will have little or no negative impact on the environment.

2 - Activities

The project will focus on the following activities;• Support static primary health care services at 2 PHCCs and 3 PHCUs . • Establish 2 mobile teams, 1 in Manyo and 1 in Mayendit. • Provide services for SAM with medical complications at Kaka PHCC. • Improve the Wash facilities in the health facilities by providing safe water, provision of cleaning materials, general infection prevention and control. • Provision of CMR services at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Provision of BeMONC services at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Recruitment of skilled midwives, CO’s at all the facilities to be supported. • Training of skilled birth attendants. • Provision of routines outpatient consultations at the PHCC’s, PHCU’s and the mobile teams. • Training of health workers on CMR, IPC, IMNCI, disease surveillance and response. • Provision of Rape kits at Kaka and Mayendit PHCC’s. • Provision of sensitization and referral services of SGBV cases from all the supported health facilities and mobile teams. • Sensitization on MHPSS services and referral of cases. • Conducting routine surveillance on attack on health care. • Reporting on vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, drugs etc., assault on health personnel among others.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The displacement-affected communities are the primary beneficiaries of this project. They will all access the services offered through this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. CASS is currently implementing government owned project in the two counties through UNICEF and HPF. The HRP project will be supplementary to the two projects, this will allow sustainability and the smooth continuity of the project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned to the national objectives since it will target IDPs, IDPs returnees, Refugee returnees and the the vulnerable host communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. For now the situation is calm with the exception of the minor revenge attack especially in Mayendit county. To ensure safety, CASS will conduct risk assessments regularly and all movement to project site will be coordinated with the relevant Cluster leads, UNDSS, UNMISS and local authorities/RRC to ensure clearance for safe passage, flexible response and effective team work. The CASS Juba security advisor support will be available at all times to respond to queries and provide advice. CASS field teams will also be sensitive towards staffing and the underlying conflict tensions. Deployment of national staff will be done within their home areas, where the risk of ethnic violence is minimal. In addition, all staff working in the field is already provided with hostile environments awareness training (HEAT) and regular security briefings. Surge capacity will be used where appropriate in order to reduce risks in areas with a high potential for ethnic conflict.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. Once the project ended, it will be handed over to the CHD for sustainability purpose.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Implementing partner CASS will develop a culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholder workshops will be organized in the location identified for implementation of the response. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing community elders, Women, Youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries.The communities will be encouraged to express their concerns, views and provide regular feedback to the implementing partner in a regular structured modality. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered. These feedbacks will form part of the project performance reporting to the Health cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience.
