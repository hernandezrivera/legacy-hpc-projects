Project Summary

According to JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The methodology used for the PIN calculation estimated that 4.6 million people are in catastrophic (10%) severe (9%) and extreme (18%) need for humanitarian WASH assistance. This project aims to reduce people’s needs, risks, and vulnerabilities and increase access to durable and sustainable safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene education to the crisis-affected IDPs, returnees and host communities and areas that are a high risk of AWD/Cholera due to extremely low water and/or sanitation in Somalia. Under this project, CoDHNet proposes to improve access to safe water, sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices to improve the health status of 21,600 disaster-affected communities and IDPs in Lower Shabelle of South West, Gedo and Lower Juba regions of J/land States. Lack of access to safe and adequate WASH facilities coupled with poor hygiene knowledge and practices put these communities at risk for waterborne diseases, and COVID 19 disease, particularly women, children, and disabilities.
The target population of the project is 3,600HHs benefiting 21,600 people (5400 women, 3888 men, 6480 girls and 5832 boys) including disabilities and minorities. CoDHNet will conduct community consultation meetings with the community stakeholders including women, disabilities, IDPs, and returnees to familiarize them with the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement, and roles for accountability and transparency. Also, side meetings with girls, boys, men, and women, people with disabilities and minorities will be convened to ensure their different needs are addressed. CoDHNet will therefore achieve the level of reduction through Integrating Health, Education and WASH component to better Health education awareness in water sources, health centers, and schools.
CoDHNet will provide 30 days of lifesaving safe water to 3,600 IDPs and host community households in L/Shabelle (1200), Gedo(1600), and L/Juba (800) benefiting 21,600 crisis affected men, women, and children including people with disabilities and minorities who have no access to safe water. The project will rehabilitate/construct 19 flood-proof shallow wells with hand pumps, Upgrade 2 public diesel-powered boreholes equipped with hybrid systems. To enhance sustainability and increase the ownership of the water sources women and men will be involved in the identification of permanent solutions; design and implementation of the activities whereby CoDHNet will conduct capacity building training to 21 WASH committees and 42 well operators shared equally by men and women, HPs training to 60 CHPs, and 60 who will conduct hygiene promotion campaigns.
As cross-cutting, CoDHNet will conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms to 200 Community Recourse members shared equally by men and women.
To improve the sanitation of the target locations and institutions, CoDHNet will construct 260-flood proofs, desludging, and gender-sensitive latrines with lockable doors to IDP settlements and 12 twin block latrines complete with hand washing basins in 6 schools and 6 health facilities with equal access of men, women, boys, and girls including disabilities and minorities, to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls and discourage open defecation and to curve CODIV19.
60 CHPs trained will carry out HP and COVID 19 prevention campaigns to reach 21,600 people. CoDHNet will distribute HP kits to 3000 HHs. 2 KAP surveys will be carried out at the inception of the project to identify the gaps of hygiene and factors that improve hygiene and sanitation practices to the beneficiaries and inhibiting factors that put the people at risk of COVID19 and AWD.

Project Activities

1.1: Conduct Community Consultation workshops to 180 community stakeholders shared equally men and women including minorities, and disabilities to familiarize them with the project objectives, activities, feedback, and complaint mechanisms for accountability purposes;1.2: CoDHNet will provide 30 days distribution of lifesaving safe water through vouchers to 3,600 IDPs and host community households in Bay(800HHs), L/Shabelle (800HHs), Gedo(1200HHs), and L/Juba(1800 HHs), benefiting 21,600 (5400 women, 3888 men, 6480 girls, and 5832 boys) including disabilities and minorities who have no access to safe water due to drought, floods, and conflicts. 1.3: Rehabilitate/construct 19 flood-proof shallow wells with handpumps, benefiting 9500 persons (2375 women, 1710 men, 2850 girls, and 2565 boys) who have no access to safe water due to drought, floods, and conflicts. 1.4: Upgrade 2 public diesel-powered boreholes equipped with the hybrid systems; benefiting 12,000 (3000 women, 2160 men, 2700 girls, and 3240 boys);1.5: Chlorinate 21 water points and conduct daily monitor the free chlorine residual testing (FCR) to prevent waterborne diseases and reduce AWD/Cholera outbreaks. 1.6: CoDHNet will conduct capacity-building training for 21 WASH committees shared equally men and women, each committee is composed (3 women and 4 men).1.7: CoDHNet will conduct capacity-building training for 42 well operators shared equally women and men (21 women and 21 men). 1.8: As cross-cutting, CoDHNet will conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms, COVID 19 control measures, AWD/cholera, and floods preparedness to 200 community members including teachers, CHWs, disabilities, and minorities (100men and 100women). 2.1: Construct 260 flood-proof, desludging, and gender-sensitive latrines with lockable doors and hand washing basins to IDP settlements to reduce open defecation. Benefiting 7,800 people (1950 women, 1404 men, 2340 girls, and 2106 boys), 15% of the latrines will be designed as disabilities and child friendly. 2.2: Construct flood proof 12 institutional twin blocks latrines (1 for male and 1 for female) with lockable doors and handwashing basins at 6 public schools and 6 health facilities with equal access to men, women, boys, and girls including minorities and disabilities. 2.3: Conduct cleaning campaigns (waste removal and disposal) program to 6 IDP settlements. Distribute 24 sanitation sets to the six IDP settlements; each settlement will receive 4 sanitation sets. Conduct sanitation awareness campaigns to IDP settlements before the start of the cleaning campaigns, participating men and women. Each sanitation kit contains (1 wheelbarrow, 1 pickaxe, 2 shovels/spades, 4 brooms, 2 rakes, and 1 barrel for collection of the garbage). 3.1: CoDHNet will conduct HP training for 60 (24 men, 36 women) CHPs, schools teacher, and CHWs for hygiene promotion practices using HP materials. 3.2: Conduct Hygiene Promotion activities to reach 21,600 people (5400 women, 3888 men, 6480 girls, and 5832 boys) including disabilities and minorities. CoDHNet will conduct two KAP surveys at the inception and completion of the project, to identify the gaps of hygiene and factors that improve hygiene and sanitation practices to the target beneficiaries and inhibiting factors that put the people at risk of AWD and the impact of the hygiene promotion activities carried out during the project activities respectively. 3.3: Distribution of hygiene promotion kits to 3000 HHs at the highest risk of AWD/cholera diseases in the project target locations. Each HP kit contains (200 aqua tabs, 1 Jerry can (20L), 1 bucket (20L), 3 bars of soaps, and female sanitation materials). This will benefit equally benefiting 18,000 (4500 women, 3240 men, 5400 girls, and 4860 boys) including people with disabilities and marginalized communities.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services:Mitigation: CoDHNet will locate water sources visible, central locations, and not more than 500 meters from settlement and install lights near communal WASH facilities which significantly increase the security of women and girls fetching the water. Involve women and girls insisting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety and ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. At the same time, latrines will be lockable and the location of the new latrines is discussed with the women and girls to increase security in regards to the risk of GBV. CoDHNet will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from the use of or lack of WASH facilities.2) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: Mitigation:CoDHNet will include women, men, boys, and girls including disabilities and minorities in all the consultation and decision-making meetings at the inception of the project, and during the project implementation. In the WASH committees, well operators, and community hygiene promotions, men and women will share equally. All the WASH services will be accessible to men, women, and children including disabilities and minorities.3) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms:CoDHNet will establish a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on the expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of the project services. The complaint mechanism contributes towards creating a safer environment for beneficiaries as well as increasing to listen to their voice during the implementation of the project. CoDHNet will make set up accessible, confidential and well-understood feedback mechanisms and ensure all beneficiaries are mobilized and the information reaches them regularly.CoDHNet will conduct an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures, and make clear the entitlements for all. Emphasize inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings at the household level. Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be, outside the settlements. CoDHNet will make it very clear to gatekeepers the condition for implementation to equal and direct access with all communities. CoDHNet will establish call center telephone contact, which everyone can report concerns without any external stakeholders’ interference.

Please provide further explanation

During the project design, all the stakeholders including the government representatives were consulted and agreed that the land of constructing the WASH facilities should be secure from the eviction of the IDPs. Water systems serving a large population will preferably be managed through private-public partnerships to enhance functional sustainability thus long term solutions. The water supply system at the village level will be managed by water management committees and well operators selected by the community and given training on management and maintenance of the facilities by CoDHNet’s competent WASH staff. Once completed, the facilities will be handed over formally to the community and the government officers and certificate of hand-over will be signed by all parties. Construction of latrines through the community-driven sanitation and hygiene promotion campaign approach leads to immediate ownership and sustainability of the facilities.
