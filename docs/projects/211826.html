Project Summary

A project that aims to provide several educational services through implementing some activities in some Homs rural areas, this activity includes:
-Education support classes for children at risk of dropping out of education for boys and girls students IDPs and Returnees students
-Providing cash amounts to caregivers of children who dropped out due to their financial situation to purchase basic supplies that will enable them to return to learning
-Provision of education supplies for both students and teachers by stationery, school bags and teaching materials to mitigate the financial load of targeted beneficiaries.
Monitoring and evaluation activities will be as follows:
- Field visits
- Opinion questionnaires for beneficiaries
- Complaints box in the centers
- Periodic meetings with project staff, including administrators and teachers
Islamic Charity Association AOUN will provide SHF with monthly reports on numbers of students assisted, locations of assistance, and any other information pertinent to the SHF monitoring needs which will be reflected in the 4Ws report in addition to the interim technical progress report and financial
statements as well as the required final technical and financial reports.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The protracted crisis in Syria, persistent economic crisis, further compounded by the aftermath of the earthquake have prompted a 4% increase in the People in Need (PiN) for education services compared to the previous year (2024 Syria HNO Education Sector). As Syrians grappled with these shocks, economic indicators took a further downturn, driving some population movement in Syria, as families moved in search of livelihood opportunities and better access to basic services (2024 HNO Summary). Insecurity and economic hardship heighten gender-based violence, child marriage and incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse (Humanitarian Action for Children/UNICEF2024). Similarly, Out of School Children (OOSC) areexposed to a broad range of gendered protection risks like child labor and child marriage. While young people in Syria have missed out opportunities to develop and learn. They have limited access to skill-development opportunities and basic services including safe transition from school to work.The project provides its services to the most vulnerable children who are at risk of dropping out of education through two parts:- Educational classes for children who dropped out to enable them to make up for what was lost and return to learning.- Providing cash amounts to dropout children who dropped out of education due to the inability of their caregivers to buy basic supplies for them (stationery, school clothes....) to enable them to return to learning.- AOUN will ensure the safety of students with special needs - Safe working space for all project staff and students.- Project Facilitator/staff selection and training will be from both genders (male and female) taking into cultural sensitivity.- project will ensure the availability of gender-sensitive WASH facilities- The safety and dignity of beneficiaries and the Do No Harm principles will be anticipated by the team and the administrators who have a direct association with beneficiaries.AOUN is adopting strict and organized methodology to enhance and develop protection mainstreaming across the project’s targetedareas through the following:1- Provide expertise to the education cluster to address the most prevalent and severe protection risks that intersect with the sector-specific response. Suggest protection mainstreaming activities based on the outcomes of the project’s Protection analysis, including offering to review assessment surveys or questionnaires of other clusters to ensure that protection is mainstreamed.2- The project manager and assistants will make sure to contribute and provide feedback to the HRP documents and identify areas where protection can be better mainstreamed in these documents.3- Make sure protection is regularly discussed as a standing agenda item in staff meetings, and staff members are aware of the importance of mainstreaming protection in their programs.
