Project Summary

South Central Somalia is experiencing multiple humanitarian crises, including a desert locust infestation, Covid-19 pandemic, consecutive years of severe drought, conflict, seasonal flooding, and disease outbreaks. IDPs, especially young children and pregnant and nursing women (PLW), are especially vulnerable to communicable diseases due to poor sanitation, limited access to safe water, crowded living conditions and underlying health conditions.
Covid-19 has contributed to significant disruptions in essential health services, including acute and primary care, reproductive and sexual health (SRH), as well as gender-based violence (GBV) and mental health services.
SOS’s health records showed that primary health care visits and vaccination coverage have sharply declined amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, raising concerns of increased preventable diseases, and malnutrition going untreated.
SOS will provide basic life-saving primary health care services to 150,000 highly vulnerable IDP, host-community and hard-to-reach populations in Banadir, Afgooye Corridor, Lafole Baidoa, Xudur, Diinsoor, Balcad with a strong focus towards women and children. SOS will facilitate access to target populations in identified catchment areas safely access comprehensive primary healthcare at no cost, including health and social behavior change messaging and community outreach through CHWs, designed to reduce the spread of communicable diseases. Disease outbreak surveillance, reporting, response and prevention to reduce morbidity and mortality from common diseases. Integration of MHPSS in to the primary health care to improve the phyiscial and mental wellbeing of communities. Clinical management of rape services to SGBV survivors to reduce rape consequences. outpatient consultations will primarily focus on clinical diagnosis and treatment for communicable diseases, treatment of common ailments, Antenatal and Post-natal care visits (ANC/PNC), immunizations, skilled birth attendants specialized obstetric and newborn care and ambulance referral service.
The same beneficiaries that will be accessing the out-patient consultations will also be receiving health education and behavior changes education at health facilities through trained community health workers and health care providers.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.- Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.- Identify central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.- Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points.- Consider the frequency of distributions – the greater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.Consider separate waiting areas for males and females. -Have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available. -Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children- Install lights outside and on the access route to the facility5. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups -Identify individuals who lack documentation to prevent exclusion from services.-promote active participation and consultation with target beneficiates including women, girls and disable persons during project planning, implementing and monitoring. The project will identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities in humanitarian contexts.• The project will identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities• People with disabilities and/or their representative organizations will be consulted during the design and implementation of the project.• The project will disaggregate beneficiary data by disability (preferably using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions (WGSS).
