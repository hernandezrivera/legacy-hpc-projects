Project Summary

The prolonged drought in southeastern and southern Ethiopia is the result of three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020. The impact of the drought is deepening and according to April 2022, UNOCHA update at least 81 million people in Oromia, Somali, SNNP and Southwest regions are affected. Livestock are suffering from lack of pasture and water. Estimates from regional Governments indicated more than 1.46 million livestock deaths (67 % in Somali, 31 % in Oromia, and 7 % in Southwest and SNNP regions). Surviving animals have a significantly deteriorated body condition which has decreased their market value which has led to decrease of the purchasing power of households. According to the most recent seasonal forecast by ICPAC, a below average belg season is expected. The situation seems not expected to improve rapidly.Thus, the affected pastoral communities require continuous support. According to the humanitarian response plan, Humanitarian partners are targeting 12.8 million people for food assistance (out of which 8.0 million people also receive other types of assistance). To response to the situation CARE proposed three major intervention that include Livestock feed provisions, Animal health provision and restocking with shoat to Liben of Somali region and Borena of Oromia region.
East Hararge DRM office Early warning department report showed the rain delayed by 48days this show in FY22 no longer expect of belg crop (short season) planting and delays may happen on Meher crop planting. Zonal Agriculture office noted to partners the need to support the farmers on provision of short season maturing seed. Similarly, the shortage of seeds noted in West Hararge Zone. In response to this CARE proposed the provision of agriculture inputs such as improved seeds and /or vegetables to both Eastern and Western Hararge zones.
The political dynamics have changed dramatically in Ethiopia's Tigray Region following the unilateral ceasefire declaration by the Ethiopian Government on 28 June 2021. However, the breakdown of essential services such as the blackout of electricity, telecommunications, and internet throughout Tigray region will only exacerbate the already dire humanitarian situation. According to the UNOCHA the shortages of cash and fuel in the region can compromise the duty of care of aid workers on the ground. Despite the dynamic and uncertain situation, it has been reported the security situation in Tigray has been generally calm over the past few days, with limited humanitarian activities being implemented around Mekelle and Shire. If the situation improves there will be a possibility of accessing the affected woreda.
According to UNOCHA recent updates, in Amhara region areas bordering Tigray, namely in Wag Hamra, North Wollo and North Gondar zones, situations remained unpredictable but relatively calm during the reporting period. Several locations remained hard to reach for partners’ humanitarian operations due to security concerns, including Abergele and Tsagibji kebeles, parts of Zequala kebeles in Wag Hamra Zone, large sections of Kobo in North Wello Zone, and in Addi Arekay in the North Gondar Zone. However, the livelihoods of the people in the above zones are highly affected by the conflict that leaves many affected people for need of agriculture input support. CARE proposes the agriculture input provisions which includes improved seeds and /or vegetables, provision of hand tools, and the provision of Draught power for the Amhara and Tigray region affected woredas. The recent UNOCHA updates shows in Afar, the humanitarian situation continued to be dire with a high number of IDPs in need of support while the response is still insufficient to compare with needs in the region. Because of the conflict the main livelihood and income source from livestock highly affected. To respond the situation CARE proposed Animal feed provision, animal health and restocking interventions

1. - Projects activities

To address the needs of the crises-affected peoples and respond to the situation CARE proposed different agriculture activities that will implement in Afar, Amhara, Oromia, Somali, and Tigray, and enhance the livelihoods of the people. 1. Provision of improved locally adapted drought-tolerant crops (cereals, pulses, and vegetables) with essential farm tools and equipment 2. Support treatment and vaccination of animals to mitigate the large-scale loss of livestock 3. Provision of supplementary livestock feed to vulnerable pastoral households and agro-pastoral households4. Support Livestock asset restoration (restocking of core breeding animals) in areas where the large-scale loss of livestock is experienced following disease out-breaks and climatic shocks5. Draught power support for effective land preparation activitiesThe activities and the target per region are depicted as follows:1. In Afar region CARE will provide animal health, Animal feed, and restocking support for 8168 individuals.2. Provision of agricultural input and draught power for 87,532 (23,000 in Amhara and 20,766 individuals in Tigray will receive agricultural input and 23,000 and 20766 individuals in individuals in Amhara and Tigray regions respectively, will provide with draught power.3. In the Oromia region in the Borena zone, CARE will provide animal health, animal feed, and restocking support for 32,035 individuals.4. Provision of agricultural inputs for 55,575 individuals in the West and East Hararghe zone in the Oromia region.5. Provision of animal health, animal feed and restocking with small ruminants of the drought affetd people in Liben zone Somali regions All the actions will be supported with awareness and trainings as appropriate. In addition to the input provision activities CARE will provide GBV and PSEA training to CARE staffs, government staff and community members engaged in targeting and response activities. The training will aim to increase the understanding of what protection mainstreaming means in practice, to enable and encourage participants to mainstream protection principles in their programming; and enhance the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries. A key component of this training will be a focus on encouraging both service providers and beneficiaries to report any claim of sexual exploitation and abuse committed by staff of CARE or affiliated partners. CARE will link reporting procedures with complaint and feedback mechanisms or anonymous suggestion boxes available at GBV service provision locationsAll the proposed interventions will not undermine or negatively affect the environment. No intervention will have a negative impact on the environments/ ecosystems.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

CARE coordinates closely with the clusters and other stakeholders at all levels. Hence, CARE will continue to work with disaster Prevention and Food Security Coordination Bureau/offices and coordinate with other UN agencies, NGOs, and other operational partners on the ground to ensure complementarity and avoid overlap with assistance provided by other partners. CARE regularly attends the monthly DRM-ATF meetings and share project progress update through the 5W matrix. CARE is a new co-chair for the Ethiopian Cash Working Group and will coordinate with other agencies and support the cash program at the national level. CARE will use this platform to continue reporting and sharing the outcome of this project.The humanitarian program managers at the head office will responsible for overseeing the overall implementation, project management, and monitoring of project progress and will work with the LDM managers on monitoring & evaluating, and capturing the learning that arises from this project. Similarly, Field-based staff will submit project reports on progress achieved as well as challenges encountered on a weekly, monthly, and quarterly basis. In addition, the program staff at head office will conduct regular monitoring visits and conduct a further review and prepare midterm and final reports to be shared with the donor. CARE will follow community-based beneficiary targeting by using targeting committees to identify the most vulnerable households However, to ensure the inclusion and exclusion errors, we will conduct an independent verification assessment. Post Distribution Monitoring will be conducted after two weeks of distribution of inputs to measure the beneficiary satisfaction in terms of response timeliness intervention and will share with the Agricultural task force.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CARE will mainstream Protection principles while designing and implementing the project. During beneficiary targeting, selection, and item distribution, CARE will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. In addition, the project staff will conduct regular oversight including monitoring visits to ensure access and service delivery to vulnerable groups who need special attention. IDP or crisis-affected peoples representatives will be involved in the beneficiary selection, verification of beneficiary lists, and monitoring activities where feasible (incorporating gender, age, and ability diversity). Accessible and user-friendly feedback collection and complaint mechanisms will also be put in place and used as an instrument to ensure access by the vulnerable groups throughout the project period. The community mobilization will be held with IDP/ crises affected people’s representatives including women and will include information sharing through banners and announcements to ensure that community members are aware of the project aims and planned activities, and how to access the feedback mechanism. The project will identify safe and secure locations and mechanisms for the input distribution.
