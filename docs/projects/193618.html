Project Summary

The 2023 inter-agency Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Sudan has been revised due to the high needs spurred by the current crisis. The revised HRP (May – December 2023) to provide emergency support to 18 million people in Sudan until the end of this year. This project is proposed under the 2023 Country Refugee Response Plan (CRP) appeal submission and has been reviewed and pre-vetted by the Refugee Consultation Forum (RCF). The proposed interventions focus on integrating multi-sectoral lifesaving assistance targeting approximately (185,240) refugees and asylum seekers in Sudan, including South Sudanese, Ethiopian, and other refugees from Chad and Central Africa Republic residents at Kassala, Blue Nile, and North Darfur States. This project is guided by the decision of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), which indicated that the cost of the 20223 HRP will only focus on lifesaving activities. Therefore, interventions of this project will address life-saving WASH, Food Security, and livelihood assistance. The targeted refugees are located in refugee camps in Kassala, Blue Nile, and North Darfur states. While focusing on refugees, IDPs and host communities around refugee camps will also be supported during the response to prevent conflicts, promote peaceful coexistence, and ensure sustainability.
The project outcomes are expected to be achieved through high-quality program delivery of activities, including FSL activities: General food distribution (full ratio) for most vulnerable households throughout the planned year. WASH activities: Operating and maintaining available water sources. Provision trucks of potable drinking water to targeted people suffering from insufficient drinking water. Provision of multi-purpose soap for households' consumption. Construction of new hand washing facilities in communal set ups. Provision of personal hygiene materials for women and girls of reproductive age. Conduct vector control activities to reduce waterborne diseases. Dispose of 300 MT of solid waste and support its management. Solarization of water pumping systems to ensure sustainability. Provision of clean and sustainable energy solutions for domestic use of HHs. Provision of training to use clean energy sources for HHs. Provision of new solar lighting for public spaces to increase the protection of women and girls in access to facilities. Afforestation of 80 hectares through tree plantation. Solar torches and lamps for household lighting are provided to benefit HHs and increase the safety of vulnerable HHs, especially women, girls and people with disabilities. Consultation with target communities will be central to this project as part of community engagement and accountability to the affected population. Coordination with the UNHCR, RCF, RWGs, and COR at the national and sub-national levels will be prioritized.
Furthermore, Inter-agency coordination with all humanitarian actors will be an integral part of
our coordination mechanisms throughout the project lifecycle.

1 - Needs

The conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has entered the sixth week. Hundreds of people have died, thousands have been wounded, while thousands more families are on the move, both within Sudan and across the borders of neighbouring countries. Practical Action is among the first respondent NGOs to the Ethiopian refugee influx from the Tigray region in November 2020. PA has led a multi-sectoral emergency response consortium with KPHF and JASMAR- targeted Tigray refugees and asylum seekers at Hamdayet reception centre in Kassala state in February 2021, where emergency WASH and Health assistance was delivered. PA is adopting a holistic approach in its programming by assisting refugees, IDPs, and host communities where it operates. Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa, with 1.14 million refugees and asylum seekers as of 30 June 2022, including 255,237 new arrivals; 56% of them are females, and 44% are males, according to Sudan Situation, UNHCR report, May 2023. Out of the overall refugee figure, South Sudanese are forming (70.7%), Ethiopian (6.4%), CAR (2.5%), and Chad (0.4%). The sustained economic decline continued to affect Sudan, with the annual inflation of the Sudanese pound recorded at 148% lower-than-average harvests have reduced cereal availability, increased food prices and exacerbated the conflict. Like Sudan, Ukraine mainly depends on Russia and Ukraine for wheat imports. Similarly, fuel prices have increased significantly. Rising costs, high inflation, and challenges in supply chains combined have considerably reduced household purchasing power and resulted in a projected 11.7 million persons across Sudan likely to be in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 or higher between June and September 2022. Refugees, largely cut off from formal economies, are particularly vulnerable to the effects of declined purchasing power, heightened food insecurity, associated negative coping strategies and protection risks. The CRP was implemented by 28 partners in the first half of the year and delivered assistance to address protection and humanitarian needs, working across 15 states and benefitting an estimated 533,243 refugees. Of those reached, an estimated 47% of beneficiaries are male and 53% female, with 48% of the total caseload comprised of children, 46% adults, and 6% older people—source (CRP 2022 Mid-Year Report). For the 2023 CRP, the projected planning figures are: 926K, with assumptions of the continued presence of refugees in and outside camps, continued challenging economic situation, competing global crises, continuing civil unrest and tribal conflicts, natural hazards (floods & drought), COVID-19, Sudan continues to follow an open border policy and the legal framework for refugees and asylum seekers remains the same, work towards durable solutions, limited development funding, and an increased need to support host communities. While the needs/gaps remain high, the FSL, WASH, Energy & Environment sectors have identified the urgent needs and response priorities for the 2023 CRP.

2 - Activities/Outputs

To ensure timely lifesaving assistance, Practical Action will fundraise against this appeal to provide improved access to lifesaving and essential services for refugees and asylum seekers to reduce suffering and stress on refugees and host communities while promoting durable solutions for refugees. This change ambitious will be collectively achieved through working with humanitarian partners and donors to contribute to the 2023 CRP strategic objectives to benefit refugees, asylum seekers and people of concern. PA will adopt a multi-sectoral approach to the urgent needs of (185,240) refugees, IDPs, and vulnerable host communities in targeted areas. WASH interventionswill improve safe access to clean and adequate drinking water and promote personal hygiene practices while strengthening sanitation systems in camps and the surrounding areas. FSL interventions will enable refugees, IDPS, and host communities, especially at-risk women and girls, to restore income-generating assets and employment opportunities and support youth capacities. Energy & Environment interventions will focus on women and girls to improve their health andwell-being and protect them from risks associated with collecting firewood, cultivating drinking water and utilising latrines at nighttime. Also, it will pay close attention to the impact of climate change impact on the environment by restoring forests to ensure the sustainability of natural resources and avoid tensions and conflicts over natural resources. The proposed intervention will address the identified needs of people of concern through the following activities; FSL) Provision of food distribution complete ration in cash for (3000) individuals monthly throughout the project lifecycle. WASH) operating and maintaining 6 water sources, provision of potable water trucking (10KLTRS) =30 days x6 months; 1 truck @5000/month which covers 10 locations at an average =500USD/location/month (PC1); provision of soap for 4000 households, construction of 300 handwashing facilities in communal setups, provision of personal hygiene materials for 15000 women and girls, do 36 vector control activities, dispose of 300 MT of solid waste and support its management, solarisation of 3 water pumping systems, provision of clean and sustainable energy solutions for 1200 HHs, provision of training to use clean energy sources for 1000 HHs, provision of 600 solar lighting for public spaces, afforestation of 80 hectares through tree plantation, and provision of solar torches, lamps for household lighting to benefit 3500 HHs. Implementation will be in consultation with all stakeholders, including target communities, UNHCR, COR, HAC, INGOs, NNGOs, CBOs, and other line ministries. Interagency coordination will be anessential mechanism to ensure synergy and avoid duplication.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Practical Action recognizes that extreme climate conditions and the mismanagement of the natural resources contribute to poverty in Sudan. Most of Sudan's people are reliant on their farming for their livelihoods, despite the fact that Sudan is one of the most fertile lands in Africa, yet the productivity is low due to rain scarcity, desertification, deforestation, absence of proper irrigation methods, and a lack of knowledge and awareness on natural resources management and climate change. Practical Action through its "Energy that transforms" Change Ambitious have invested in activities that promotes safe impact of environment such as introducing clean cooking stoves for households in Darfur, BN and Kassala states, which improved women and girls' health and reduced the burden on the environment. The "Farming that works" Change Ambitious helped reducing conflicts over limited resources at a local level, enabled farmers and pastoralist adapt to new climate reality and improving their resilience. "Cities fit for people" Change Ambitious has contributed to environment-friendly waste disposal practices, in a holistic community-focused approach to improve sanitation and waste disposal.Therefore, Practical Action 3 Change Ambitious are realizing and focusing on imposing positive impact on environment, addressing climate change challenges, building the local capacity and awareness on adopting best practices related to environment conservation and climate resilience and mitigation. Practical Action has a long experience in adopting Climate Resilient approach that ensures equal access to water for different users while maintaining ecosystem balances and enhance environmental restoration. Practical Action has a track record of implementing environment projects focusing of forestation and environment conservation. Interventions such as applying IWRM, replacing diesel operated water yards into solar-operated systems to reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping. Interventions of this project will ensure and promote positive impact on environment and natural resources, by reducing the deterioration of forests, through the provision of clean cooking stoves, afforestation through tree plantation, and building the capacity of target communities on the use of clean energy sources.
