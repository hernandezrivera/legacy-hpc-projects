Project Summary

To address the identified critical needs of the Rohingya refugees facing the deterioration of shelters and the lack of adequate internal lighting, as well vulnerable host communities facing a lack of adequate shelter and access to sustainable energy sources; the twelve-month NRC Shelter Programme will provide safe and dignified shelter and energy solutions to Rohingya refugees in the camps, and vulnerable crisis-affected populations in the host communities of Teknaf Upazilla, Cox's Bazar District.
In the Rohingya refugee camps, NRC will distribute TSA II kits as part of the Shelter Sector’s TSA II blanket coverage strategy and distribute SHS kits that will target the most vulnerable in the Rohingya communities. Throughout the process, the shelter beneficiaries will be engaged through awareness campaigns, training, focus group discussions and feedback sessions, including training and awareness on DRR. NRC will also contribute technical and operational capacity in emergency circumstances and will ensure DRR shelter coverage of TDKs in Camp 25 as part of 2021 monsoon preparedness. NRC Shelter will also provide repair and maintenance to all other NRC Programme facilities in the camps and host communities, ensuring that they remain in good condition and they are accessible to all PSNs. All repair and maintenance costs will be provided by the relevant NRC Programme user of the facilities.
In the host communities, NRC will offer cash-based shelter assistance for household repair or upgrades and distribute SHS kits to the most vulnerable members of the community. NRC will engage the host communities in all levels of the decision-making of the activities, to promote ownership and increase the empowerment of vulnerable and marginalised individuals. NRC will also provide training and awareness on DRR, to increase the host community’s resilience during emergencies.
NRC will undertake these activities using a professional training team that will be fully trained in gender and disability inclusion and PSEA. Gender equality will be mainstreamed throughout the project and each of the activities will include sensitization activities on abandonment of gender-based violence (GBV) and the promotion of women and girls’ rights. NRC will also ensure that there is an independent and impartial complaints mechanism availble for all activities. Alongside meeting minimum internal and ISCG Shelter Sector standards, NRC will conduct this intervention following the latest Covid-19 guidelines for distributions, ensuring that vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host communities are shielded from the risk of catching the virus whilst receiving shelter assistance.
NRC will at all times coordinate all activities with the Shelter Sector, other partners operating in the camps and the SMSD Sector.
NRC Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) programme will support the HLP TF with HLP assessments, development of guidance notes and advocacy papers as identified by the HLP TF in its workplan and HLP Strategy 2020-2023. NRC M&E will provide access to an independent and impartial complaints feedback mechanism. Furthermore, NRC Shelter will undertake all repair and maintenance construction works for NRC Education Rohingya and host community learning facilities.

1 - Identified Needs

The needs of the Rohingya refugees and host communities have been identified as a result of the 2020 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessments, which identified shelter materials as the critical priority need in the Rohingya camps and the third most critical need in the host communities (after food and cash); where 69% of Rohingya households reported facing issues with their shelter (in the 6 months prior to data collection), with 51% of those households identifying the roof as their biggest concern. In the Host Community that figure was marginally lower at 59% of households reporting issues with their shelter, with 46% of those households identifying the roof as their biggest concern. In addition, 39% of host community households reporting not having enough money to pay for shelter materials or labour to undertake repairs. Electricity was also identified as a key need, with 58% of Rohinya households reporting not having enough access to light, and its position as a gateway item to contributing to improving other priority needs such as Education, Protection and Livelihoods was highlighted, as well as its ability to increasing the wellbeing of beneficiairies. The identified needs were supplemented by NRC'S internal Bangladesh Response Analysis which highlighted the Teknaf area as having critical Shelter needs as well as experiencing gaps in assistance coverage.Although the 2020 JRP target for cross-cutting Sector HLP documents has been met, according to the mid-year 2020 JRP, HLP partners identified numerous risks and challenges, including increases in rent, pressure to increase in-kind payments and threats by the host community to evict refugees because of COVID-19-related restrictions and limited livelihood opportunities, which requires development of further HLP guidances that will advance the Sector’s strategic objectives.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Distribution of 1,550 TDKs to Camp 25 ensuring blanket coverage, including awareness and technical training and during the emergency responses (with consideration and culturally appropriate training for female HHs)Activity 2: Distribution of 3,000 kits of the second level of TSA assistance, TSA II, with further replacement of damaged materials and shelter upgrades, to Camp 24 and 25. Include in kind and voucher assistance and technical verification that essential materials were installed correctly Activity 3: TSA II capacity building awareness and training on shelter reinforcement (with consideration and culturally appropriate training for female HHs) for all beneficiaries receiving TSA II kits. Activity 4: Repair and maintenance of exisiting community facilities, specifically but not limited to Education in Camps 2E, 9, 10 and 13 and the host communities of Teknaf and Nhilla. Includes accessibility improvements for PwSNActivity 5: Host community shelter repair/reinforcement/upgradation based on the technical assessments to 500 households in Teknaf and Nhilla. Assistance will be provided through cash, vouchers or in-kind assistance. Targeting to be based on vulnerability criteria as determined by Protection actors and NRC's vulnerability matrix and assessment.Activity 6: Host community-led participatory shelter programming (including consultation with leadership from women’s groups) for all beneficiaries of cash based shelter repair and SHS kits. CBOs will help select beneficairies and inform of the process of the shelter activities. Priority will be given to female and marginalised voices.Activity 7: Provision of 2,000 HH level solar home system (SHS) energy that follow the Minimum EEWG requirements for HH level lighting to Camp 24 and 25. Targeting to be based on vulnerability criteria as determined by Protection actors and NRC's vulnerability matrix and assessment.Activity 8: SHS energy capacity building awareness and training on SHS maintenance and repair (with consideration and culturally appropriate training for female HHs)Activity 9: Provision of 500 Host Community HH level solar home system (SHS) in Teknaf and Nhilla, that follow the Minimum EEWG requirements for HH level lighting. Priority will be given to that are vulnerable and those not connected to the electricity grid. Targeting will be the same recipients of the cash-based shelter repair. Activity 10: Support to the HLP TF with HLP assessments, development of guidance notes and advocacy papers as identified by the HLP TF in its workplan and HLP Strategy 2020-2023, provided by NRC Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) programme.
