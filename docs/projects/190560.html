Project Summary

Caritas Switzerland (CACH) is engaged in humanitarian responses in the country to help support the most vulnerable populations (host communities, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees) following either conflicts and/or natural disasters. In addition to the pure humanitarian response CACH is engaged in advancing the Humanitarian Development Peace (HDP) triple Nexus concept which aims to link humanitarian aid, development cooperation and peace to increase effectiveness, efficiency and support local government, civil society actors and people in a demand- driven response.
The response plan at hand is an Agriculture Humanitarian Response aiming at protecting and sustaining core agro-pastoralist and agricultural livelihoods. It is a combination of a CACH project under implementation in Oromia region (Emergency Agriculture response for IDP and host communities in East Hararghe Zone, Oromia Region ETH-21/DDA-3379/SA2/A/INGO/21083) with a 249,851.57 $ secured fund, and CACH 2022 planning for other areas in Oromia (Borena zone), Amhara (North Shewa), and Tigray (Western).
The humanitarian environment changed throughout the country with a surge in conflict and conflict-induced displacements. The DTM Ethiopia (Displacement Tracking Matrix) Event Tracking Tool 112 (12-19 March 2022) has identified a reduction on the number of IDPs. However, it identified an increase of returning IDPs (1.5 million) with very high concentration of returnees in North Shewa, North Wollo and South Wollo. The same report highlights the increase in the number of IDPs due to new conflict and drought in West Guji and Borena zone.
To better design the emergency response a joint CACH ECC SDCO rapid assessment was conducted in North Shewa zone (April 2022), and a CACH remote assessment was conducted in Borena zone (March 2022). In Borena, the current prolonged drought and conflict situation is affecting the agro-pastoralists and pastoralists in multiple ways: failure of three consecutive rainy seasons, desert locust infestation, resource sharing with neighboring kebeles of the already depleted rangeland, and movement restriction towards areas with better grazing and water available.
In North Shewa, the fighting has resulted in large-scale displacements from South Gondar, Waghimra, South and North Wello, and North Shewa Zones as well as in and around cities, neighboring urban and rural woredas. According to the Amhara regional Disaster Prevention Food Security Program Coordination Commission (DPFSPCC) report, over 2.3 million people were displaced from their original place, of which more than 1.4 million IDPs remain displaced across the region (DPFSPCC multi sectoral response plan, Dec. 2021). The majority of IDPs are women and children. Main cause of displacement is the conflict, whereby the fleeing persons lost their jobs, farmland, and livestock, and thus their livelihoods. The critical humanitarian situation forced most returnees and IDPs to live in extreme conditions with relatives and friends where shortage of any form of assistance is a big gap. The conflict has adversely affected agricultural production, crops, and livestock.
Activities and target areas are selected in close coordination with all concerned government actors and aligned with projects and programs of other actors in the same area as well as with the 2022 HRP cluster specific objectives, indicators and framework. Moreover, activities have been developed considering ongoing and planned projects and programs of CACH (2021-2025).Ongoing and planned projects complementing the humanitarian activities include a. Integrated Response - Humanitarian Aid to the most Vulnerable people in Moyale, Borena, Oromia (ETH-21/DDA-3379/SA1/A-WASH-NFI/ES- E/INGO/18557); b. Lowland COMPASS projects in Borena Zone (foreseen start June 2022); c. Lifesaving NFI Rapid Response to Displacement Affected Most Vulnerable People projects in North Shewa and Western Tigray (ongoing).

Implementing Partners

CACH works closely with its longstanding national implementing partners based on their geographic presence, to ensure continued delivery of services in areas and to build sustainable capacity of partners, and in adherence with the Grand Bargain localization agenda and the National NGOs’ Engagement - Strategy Paper.
Specifically, CACH will work with the Ethiopian Catholic Church Social and Development Commission (ECC SDCO) in the northern parts of the intervention (Amhara, Tigray). ECC SDCO’s major sectors of interventions are Food Security and Resilience; Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH); Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Environmental Protection; Health; Education; Women in Development; Migration; Emergency and Integrated Community Development Programs. ECC SDCO has a very strong presence in the Country and is currently implementing with CACH a rapid response intervention in North Shewa and Western Tigray.
In Oromia’s Borena Zone CACH will implement the project together with ACORD Ethiopia. CACH and ACORD collaborate, have built and maintain a strong partnership since 2017. Through this partnership, projects in the sectors of basic needs, protection, livelihoods, WASH, and shelter have been successfully implemented. Both ACORD and CACH have been operational in the targeted woredas over the past years and maintain a good collaborative relationship with both the local government line offices and the communities. CACH’s and ACORD’s access to the operational areas is ensured through its long-term operational presence with sub-offices in Yabello (CACH) and Mega (ACORD). CACH and its implementing partner thus maintain excellent working relations with other NGO as well as government offices and maintain a broad network providing important information (i.e., project implementations, security information).

1. - Projects activities

1.1 Provision of improved locally adapted drought tolerant crops (cereals, pulses, and vegetables) and fodder seeds together with essential farm tools and equipment. This activity is cross cutting in all the project locations where the reserve crop seeds are depleted due to drought or conflict.1.2 Support of Livestock asset restoration (restocking of core breeding animals) in areas where large-scale loss of livestock is experienced following disease out-breaks and climatic shocks. The restocking focuses on shoats because of their high reproduction rate in short time and their higher resistance to drought. This activity is to be implemented in Borena Zone of Oromia region.1.3 Resuscitation of off-farm income generating activities targeting households affected by the climate change and returnees.1.4 Drought power support for effective land preparation activities.1.5 Support treatment and vaccination of animals to mitigate large-scale loss of livestock. As part of enhancing the livestock health delivery system in all targeted locations the project will provide voucher-based veterinary drugs. 1.6 Provision of supplementary livestock feed to vulnerable pastoral households and agropastoral households. The Genna rainfall performance outlook indicates that it will be below normal. Therefore, the current drought situation in Borena zone is expected to continue limiting the availability of pasture and water. 1.7 Forage seed provision for improved fodder production.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher-based interventions reduce extra administrative and logistics costs, it enables returnees/IDPs to participate in the local economy and to self-confidentially determine household needs in order to increase their own level of dignity and self-reliance. The implementation of Cash Transfer Programming (CTP) will be linked with bank accounts, and each beneficiaries’ amount of cash or denominated voucher is allotted based on the nature of the activities and each cluster’s guidance/National Cash Working Groups (NCWG) standards.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

To build the capacity of relevant local stakeholders and actors at all levels as an integral part of aid localization is an important objective of CACH. Local capacity of all stakeholders will be strengthened by committed participatory partnerships. This requires the delineation of clear roles/responsibilities with representatives, community leaders and committees. Based on these agreements, the establishment of selection criteria for distribution procedures and policies including tracking of resources, and the planning and implementation of response activities are defined in a comprehensible way. In such manner, CACH will closely cooperate with local government line offices right from the start of the project to align the action with local efforts and foster the localization of aid, as well as to ensure sustainability. Relevant stakeholder and community engagement and involvement will be part of the process from assessment stage to project planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The project approach is community centered, which is why communities will be actively involved in decision-making steps (e.g., during beneficiary selection). Both CACH and national implementing partners will ensure to incorporate specific activities to address all cross-cutting matters such as Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP), Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and combating Gender Based Violence (GBV) especially for IDPs.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Enabling people to have a voice is considered as a key component of accountability to the affected communities. Therefore, and as an inherent part of CACHs Code of Conduct, a Complaint Handling Mechanism (CHM) is put in place and made accessible to all stakeholders of the project. Among others, this involves instituting a relevant platform through which affected populations can voice concerns, general feedback and complaints. Considered a mandatory step in the predefined project cycle, the dissemination of the information about the mechanism to all stakeholders is crucial. The feedback loop is closed by incorporating voiced concerns into the decision-making management system. Furthermore, the project team commits to address such matters within a specified and quick turnaround. All CHM are specifically calibrated to the project environment and the prevalent context. For the proposed action at hand, the CHM consists of 1) dedicated feedback phone number, an email/a postal address, 2) feedback box for written complaints/suggestions installed at CACH and at project locations, 3) community meetings, entailing also women-specific meetings, 4) verbal feedback received by staff members and 5) Close collaboration with local stakeholders to keep them informed about plans and the implementation status. This helps to establish and monitor realistic project goals. The CHM shall be open and accessible to all people. The use of different feedback mechanisms allows for people with impairments or specific needs to use the channel most appropriate to their needs. Potential protection risks stemming from project implementation are considered crucial components as well. This includes preventive actions to decrease potential tensions among communities arising because of this intervention.
