Project Summary

The project seeks to support the food insecure households in the four districts of Matabeleland South and North provinces as well as nearby Umguza and Byo Peri Urban areas of Zimbabwe through the provision of food assistance, crop and livestock production, and management including training of beneficiaries and Agricultural Technical Extension Officers to effectively, efficiently and sustainably manage the intervention. This is justified by the number of people in need (PiN) of food assistance and other basic needs which continues to increase in the country due to various factors including droughts, climate change impacts, cost of production, economic decline, policy inconsistences, limited capacity and technical knowhow at many levels. For instance, it is estimated that a total of 7.03 million people (2.34 in urban and 4,68 in rural areas needed food assistance based on the PiN linked to the ZimVAC assessment. This has negatively impacted more on women and children and other social groups such as the orphans, elderly, people with disabilities and people living with HIV and AIDS. Considering that Zimbabwe is an agro based economy anchored on capacity of smallholder farmers predominantly practicing subsistence farming in rural and marginal areas, this intervention will target these as they influence the food supply chain from production to consumption. Women usually bear the brunt of food insecurity at household level and especially in the current prevailing situation where many men in the targeted four districts (Insiza, Umzingwane, Umguza and Bulawayo peri urban) have migrated mostly to neighbouring South Africa and Botswana in seek of better livelihood and income options. The majority of the remaining young men have moved away from home to mining areas scattered across all the Matabeleland provinces. The launch pad of the project will be Insiza where ZimPro is already implementing a food and income security project with support from Trocaire and Irish Aid. For ZimPro implementation and coordination of project activities in Umguza would be easier as ZimPro has a permanent field office in Insiza which shares boundaries with Umguza.
More specifically, the project seeks to address two strategic objectives (SO) under the Humanitarian Response Plan, i.e.
1. SO1: Saving lives through support to food access to meet basic food and nutrition requirements for acutely food insecure population during severe seasonal shocks or other disruptions. This will be in the form of food assistance in Umguza and Bulawayo Peri urban during January to March 2021. There is an assumption that the performance of the 2020/21 season would be far much better than the just ended 2019/20 as basing on current weather forecasts, hence no need for food assistance during the last quarter of 2021.
2. SO2: Prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population, by providing emergency agriculture support and building resilience to multiple shocks. Under this SO, which will be implemented in all the four districts, there will be provision of drought tolerant crop inputs for field crops for the 2021/22 season distributed before onset on rains, i.e. by October 2021. Some targeted households will be assisted with improved forage seeds to improve their pastures, whilst other households will benefit from rehabilitated dip tanks then supported to vaccinate their livestock. All beneficiaries of agric inputs are expected to attend training covering issues such as good agricultural practices, climate smart agriculture, crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing. Government Technical Extension Officers and Project Officers will also be refreshed on similar issues.

Activities Notes to the activities:• Cash transfer are designed for each individual in the household to receive US$13 per month up to a maximum of 5 individuals per HH during the period January to March 2021.• The seed pack for field crops will comprise of a minimum of 10kg cereal; 10kg legume; 50 kg Basal fertiliser and 50kg top dressing.• Seed pack for horticultural crops of a minimum of 5 types of garden seed varieties, herbs for pest and disease control. • Trainings are beneficiaries, Agricultural Extension Officers and direct Project personnel focusing on good agricultural practices; climate smart agriculture/ pfumbudza; Integrated pest and disease control and management; post-harvest handling, storage and processing. The planned activities for district are as follows: 1. Umguzaa) Food assistance through cash transfers to 1010 individuals that are acutely affected by drought for three months a) Provision of drought tolerant crop inputs for field crops to 1000 hhs for 2021/22 season to be distributed before onset on rains, i.e. by October 2021. b) Provision of horticultural packages to 250 hhs to be distributed by April 2021 to enable vegetable production at onset of winter.c) Training of 12 extension officers at inception of project and 1000 individual beneficiaries during project implementation 2. Bulawayo Peri urbana) Food assistance through cash transfers to 3 250 individuals that are acutely affected by drought for three months. b) Provision small agric inputs packs for and a variety of garden seeds before onset of winter, i.e. by April 2021 targeting 350 households with an estimated 1 750 individuals with access to water. c) Throughout out project duration, provide training to 350 hhs as part of the inputs package to enable them to practice good agricultural practices.d) Training of 9 extension officers during the onset of the project, followed up with refresher training during project implementation. 3. Insizaa) Provision of drought tolerant crop inputs for both field, 1860 households and horticulture production for 465 households (field crops for 2021/22 season to be distributed before onset on rains, i.e. by October 2021 and garden seeds to be distributed by April 2021 to enable vegetable production at onset of winter.b) Provision of training to (1820 households), and extension and advisory services throughout the project duration to 40 extension officers c) Support targeted households with forage seeds to 1000 households by November 2021 d) Rehabilitation of 5 dipping facilities serving 600 households to commence immediately after the 2020/21 rain season,(May 2021) which will be followed by supporting dip tank and livestock management committees with acaricides, vaccines and drugs as subsidies. e) Support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management throughout the project duration benefitting 1 860 households f) Provide agriculture or livestock inputs to households while working on assets rehabilitation / construction to 350 households as from April to November 2021. 4. Umzingwanea) Provision of drought tolerant crop inputs for both field and horticulture production (field crops for 2021/22 season to be distributed before onset on rains, i.e. by October 2021 to 992 households and garden seeds to be distributed by April 2021 to enable vegetable production at onset of winter to 248 households with access to water for irrigation). b) Provision of training to beneficiaries and agric technical extension/ advisory services throughout the project duration.c) Provision of training to farmers (980 households), and extension and advisory services to 12 extension officers.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

National reports/assessments like ZimVAC and OCHA influenced the formulation and design of this project based on the PiN of food assistance. ZimPro is already in the province, more specifically in Insiza district where it is implementing a livelihoods and income project with support from Trocaire and Irish Aid. Some of the needs to be addressed in the proposed intervention were identified during the traditional annual stakeholder consultative meetings which ZimPro has been holding at district and ward level. The project will be introduced at district level to key stakeholders (District Administrator, Agritex, Department for Livestock, DDF, Social Services Committee), ward councillors, traditional leaders and the community. At inception, the project will be introduced at provincial level, then cascaded to district and ward level through meetings with stakeholders. ZimPro has worked in all these targeted areas before implementing different projects, therefore it is very familiar with all the relevant and necessary protocols. MoUs will be signed with respective RDCs at district as a formalisation process for the implementation of the project. At these stakeholder consultative and information sharing platforms, the project goal, objectives, target populations, entitlements, timelines, expected results and budgets will be shared. It is at these forums where contact details of who to contact should one needs to complain about ZimPro, are given publicly. Internally, ZimPro has a complaints handling mechanism with governs how complaints from communities we work with raise their concerns and how they are handled. It is at these meetings where key aspects of the Core Humanitarian Standards that govern the relationship between beneficiaries and implementing agencies are shared. SASE and Programme Participants and Child protection are covered by internal policies of the organization and these will be adhered to. In addition, suggestion boxes, help desks and localised project committees will be accessible for project participants to utilise.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will work closely with the department Agritex in order to disseminate updated information on climate change and weather issues in general. The project will also continue to encourage communities to embrace their Indigenous Knowledge Systems so as to build community resilience to climatic shocks. Beneficiaries would be trained in responsible stewardship and management of their environmental which will include in reviving of as well as application of local environmental action plans. The project will also promote climate proofing strategies like agro ecology, pfumbudza, etc. as environmentally friendly practices and sustainable farming concepts. Equally critical will be the promotion and support of water and soil conservation techniques in crop production and holistic rangeland management in livestock production. Beneficiaries would be trained on and supported with resources for agro-ecology interventions such as soil conservation practices, soil reclamation techniques and restoration of degraded landscapes, soil fertility, trenching, solid as well as liquid organic manuring, soil compositing, diverse cropping patterns such as rotation, companion cropping, alley cropping, gulley reclamation, soil testing kits, water-shed/catchment area management etc.More, so the farming method encourage balancing of the ecosystem and is less costly for small holder farmers as it does not rely entirely of externally supplied inputs like synthetic fertilizers, chemicals and hybrid crop varieties. The above issues are significant in the context of climate change impacts and the need to embrace climate adaptation and mitigation strategies face with increased threats from droughts.
