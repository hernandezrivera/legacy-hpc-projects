Project Summary

The humanitarian consequences of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine are significant. The east-ern region, weakened by hostilities, is the epicen-ter of critical humanitarian needs.
The COVID-19 outbreak not only directly af-fects the health of conflict-affected populations, but has a significant impact on the well-being of individuals and communities.
During a pandemic of coronavirus infection, one of the most vulnerable categories is the elderly 60+ and people with disabilities, who cannot pro-vide on their own an adequate standard of living and need constant outside help. They are in the main risk zone.
According to the Assessment of the Needs of Elderly People, which was carried out by HelpAge International in 23 settlements in the territory con-trolled by Ukraine in Donetsk and Luhansk regions, 71.31% indicated that they need PPE (anti-septic tanks, medical masks, latex gloves) during the COVID-19 pandemic. 92.38% of respondents need-ed basic hygiene items (given that there is current-ly no research on this category of beneficiaries ac-cording to NGCA, we use the existing data, realizing that the humanitarian situation on NGCA is 2-3 times worse ).
To address the urgent humanitarian needs caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, this category of beneficiaries desperately needs to take urgent measures to reduce existing risks.
The project will focus on supporting the most vulnerable elderly and disabled people in the NGCA.
The main goal of the project is to reduce the risks of the spread of infection, protect and miti-gate the negative impact of the pandemic on the most vulnerable categories of the population, and ensure emergency access to vital services.
As part of the project, it is planned to support 1,750 beneficiaries (elderly people, people with disabilities, families with disabled children) and 180 social institutions (preschool institutions, nursing homes, boarding schools, medical institutions with inpatient departments, hospices, etc.). The project activities will include the provision of support through the distribution of hygiene kits, personal hygiene products, cleaning and disinfection kits.
The proposed project cannot cover all existing needs, but will alleviate the difficult humanitarian situation of the most vulnerable people and help preserve and prolong full life for the elderly and people with special needs, minimize the risks of infection and prevent further spread of infection.

1 - Needs

For the population affected by the military conflict in eastern Ukraine, the COVID-19 pandemic is an additional threat to the long-term humanitarian crisis and daily physical danger. During military conflicts, the most affected are the elderly, children, people with disabilities, marginalized populations and displaced persons.The elderly make up almost a third (32%) of those in need of assistance - the highest rate in the world. Women make up more than half of people in need of assistance (56%), and many of them take care of their families themselves. Also, women who lead families and / or caregivers for the elderly, children and family members with disabilities experience additional stress and are especially vulnerable to many of the problems that surround them.Despite the fact that all age groups are at risk of contracting COVID-19, the elderly and people with special needs, whose immune systems are weakened by chronic diseases, are at a significantly higher risk of death and serious illness after infection. Moreover, those over 80 die five times more often than in other age groups. Epidemiologists estimate that 66% of people aged 70 and over have at least one underlying medical condition, putting them at increased risk of serious exposure to COVID-19. The rapid spread of the virus is overwhelming healthcare and social protection systems, and the death rate among the elderly could rise even higher.Elderly people with limited mobility and those with special needs who are self-isolating with family members or caregivers may also face a higher risk of violence, abuse and neglect. Women with physical disabilities often experience psychological difficulties and discomfort when receiving outside care.The COVID-19 pandemic has exposed insufficient funding for care and support services for people with limited mobility of all ages. The types of care and support services available to people with disabilities are often limited and unavailable to those on low incomes. For most of them, family members are the only providers of care. In most cases, women provide unpaid home care. Often times, household roles are distributed in such a way that women are also responsible for most of the household chores.People with disabilities who live at home are critically dependent on outside help for nutrition and hygiene. Low incomes of such families often force them to reduce spending on personal hygiene and disinfection products in favor of medicines and food. Especially vulnerable in this respect are women who have a need for additional feminine personal hygiene products during menstruation.In addition, children and adults with disabilities who need daily support from relatives and social workers, for example, people with limited mobility and residents of social institutions, cannot isolate themselves and use social distancing. As a result, they are in high risk areas of coronavirus infection. People with disabilities are at high risk of contracting COVID-19 because they face barriers to accessing proper hygiene and disease prevention information and can become infected from physical contact with caregivers. Difficulties for people with disabilities can arise due to the physical inaccessibility of basic infrastructure necessary for hygiene, such as sinks, as well as the physical difficulties of thorough hand washing. Moreover, these people constantly need to touch technical means of rehabilitation (wheelchairs, crutches, etc.), which means disinfecting not only their hands, but also auxiliary devices.Workers (90% of which are women) and students of preschool institutions are not less at risk of infection. Despite the fact that children of primary school age are not included in the high-risk group, they can come into contact with sick family members and be carriers of viruses.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

As a result of the project activities, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results:1. Purchase and distribution of hygiene kits for the elderly. This form of intervention is intended for 1,500 people. Priority – lonely people, people with limited mobility and people with disabilities.2. Purchase and distribution of hygiene kits for per-sons with special needs. This form of intervention is provided for 250 people.3. Purchase and distribution of cleaning and disin-fection kits for 180 medical, social and preschool institutions.Planned coverage- 100 kindergartens, the average number of stu-dents is 100 people.- 50 medical institutions, the average number is 60 beds- 30 social institutions, average number - 60 bedsAll kits will be completed in accordance with the recommendations of the Cluster.During the implementation of the project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. For this, communicative approaches will be used, assuming a transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information on the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.

3 - Indicators & targets

During the implementation of the project, it is planned to achieve the following goals1. 1,500 of the most vulnerable elderly people (approximately 840 women and 660 men) will receive assistance in the form of hygiene kits, which will improve the living standards of the vulnerable population and minimize the risks of infection among the elderly population.2. 250 (approximately 140 women and 110 men) beneficiaries with special needs will receive assistance in the form of specialized hygiene kits.3. 100 preschool institutions will receive assistance in the form of cleaning and disinfection kits (200 kits) (approximately 5100 girls and 4900 boys)4. 50 medical institutions will receive assistance in the form of cleaning and disinfection kits (150 kits) (approximately 1,690 women and 1,310 men)5. 30 social institutions will receive assistance in the form of cleaning and disinfection kits (90 kits) (approximately 1000 women and 800 men)
