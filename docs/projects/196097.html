Project Summary

"The project aims to contribute to create an environment conducive to ensure the basic rights and well-being of Rohingya refugee children in Cox's Bazar. Specifically, it will guarantee quality education with inclusive and gender focus for 900 children aged 3-4 years and 1800 vulnerable children, adolescents and youth aged 11-24 years affected by the humanitarian crisis in camps 14, 17, and 8E in Ukhiya. The project objective will be achieved through the achievement of following results- Children and Adolescent’s access learning opportunities in a protective environment and Children and Adolescents receive a quality and continuous education adapted to their needs. 900 children will have access to pre-primary education through ECD interventions in camps of Ukhiya in community based ECD center. 900 children (100% girls) aged 11-14 will have access to safe learning opportunities on Myanmar Curriculum (Under ALP) in community-based learning facility, with a gender and protection approach. 1200 youth and adolescents aged 15-24 years (50% girls) will receive education through Youth and Adolescent literacy numeracy curriculum with life skill training in Multipurpose center in camps of Ukhiya. The capacities of 84 Rohingya teachers and 57 Host teachers will be improved. The target group of children, adolescents and youth will be chosen based on their risk of exposure to exploitation and all forms of violence, mobility limitations, limited access to basic services (protection, education), protection risks due to C-19, and due considerations for children and adolescents with specific needs.
The project aims to explore Community based and cross sectoral learning solutions for children, adolescent, and youth, aligned with Education sector quality standards. Teaching will focus on academic content but will also include curricula actions to increase live saving and life sustaining skills and personal resilience of children and adolescents. Facilitating immediate access to equitable gender responsive learning opportunities in a protective environment for the crisis affected Rohingya children and adolescents within safe and inclusive learning spaces (including WASH facilities), who receive age and context appropriate education and play materials, supplies and equipment that align with the Education Sector standards, in the context of the post COVID-19 situations and in a way that prompts higher attendance rates. The primary language in all curricula will be in Burmese, in adherence to the Myanmar Curriculum Scale up (JRP 2022) to ensure the sustainable return and reintegration of the children in Myanmar when conditions become conducive. Additionally, the intervention will ensure that quality education has been adequately delivered by testing for the grade level competencies of children aged 11-14 years. Teacher training will also be a major focus of the activities, so that they can sustain the delivery of quality education and lessons. Youth and Adolescents group will receive education in basic literacy and numeracy that will better prepare them to receive vocational training in future as well as are able to access future opportunities and livelihoods, therefore increasing their resilience towards future protection risks and lessening the likelihood of adopting negative coping mechanisms. If situation allows, technology-based education and digital learning solutions will be provided as an alternative education delivery mechanism to make up for the gaps in education due to the impacts from COVID-19, as well as prepare the recipients for future risks which may hamper traditional learning methods. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"There is strong evidence that the early years of life play a major role in a child’s development. Risk factors for poor development include poor maternal nutrition, lack of stimulation and learning opportunities, and exposure to violence, but protective factors such as breastfeeding and maternal education can reduce these negative effects. Over 50% of children less than five years in the camps are experiencing developmental delays which may become irreversible. So, investments in pre- primary in camps would be an effective means of ensuring that all children will be prepared to start LC at the right age and to combat their developmental barrier. Impact of covid 19 pandemic has significantly affected the education service delivery in both communities (Host and Rohingya). Nearly 6000 learning facilities across all camps disrupting the learning of over 325,000 girls and boys for over 1.5 years. This caused substantial dropouts, learning outcome reductions, and contributed to an increase in child marriage and child labor. On the other hand, for the adolescent and youth (15-24 years) age group there are multiple sectors who are offering services such as livelihood/Food security services (FSS), GBV, protection etc. who provides supports mainly on skill development. On top of the other response, there is need of basic literacy-numeracy and soft skills for youth and adolescent (specially for women and girls) specially who those has never been to be learning before. By looking at the enrollment range between the age group on 15 to 24 of boys’ and girls’ trends shows boys enrollment is 2.5 times higher than girls’ enrollment (JMSNA 2021). Among the Rohingya Population, over 55% are below 18 years age and more than half are women and girls. Individual with disabilities also been identified as 1% population. (REACH May 2021). After the closure of LCs from March 2020, 14% household with children previously in education reported not planning to send their children back to LC’s when reopen (RGA-2020). Educational attendance of adolescent girls remains extremely limited in both camp and host communities. In the camps, among those 11-14, girls are less likely than boys to attend education facilities (Learning centers), with this trend increasing with age. Similar challenges are faced by adolescent girls in host communities. This trend is enhanced by social and religious barriers that restrict mobility of girls in public once they reach adolescences well as prevalence of early marriage. This includes the upholding the purdah or practice of keeping women separate from and preventing contact or communication with men they are not related to. This contributed to social isolation and exclusion, low levels of foundational literacy, psychological distress, exploitation, and abuse. It is found that, parents are more likely to send their girls to education facilities if there are female teachers and sex-separated classroom. Significant challenges also exist for children with disabilities contributed by insufficient skill and training of educators, poor awareness, negative attitudes, and stigma related to CwD. LF also commonly lack ramps and accessible seating arrangements, toilets, and water facilities, combined with surrounding terrain that is often steep and enough and difficult to navigate and there is poor availability of assistive devices."
