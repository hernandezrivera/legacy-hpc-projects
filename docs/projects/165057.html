Project Summary

As part of IOM’s contribution to effective coordination and the UN's localization efforts, IOM aims to support capacity building initiatives to strengthen the quality of humanitarian programming in Syria. With UN agencies depending heavily on the network of Syrian NGOs to implement activities, it is vital to support these partners to continue to grow and improve in the delivery of humanitarian assistance to people in need. As such, IOM aims to work with national and international NGO partners working in the Turkey cross-border response, and increase access to sector-specific and thematic technical capacity development of humanitarian staff. Based on the feedback and priorities expressed by NGO partners, IOM will organize 8 trainings targeting SNFI cluster partners in Turkey and 10 in Syria. Also 68 policy and procedure development to IOM Implementing Partners. Furthermore, 68 coaching sessions on the jointly developed policies and procedures.

4 - Needs

Based on a study that was conducted by Feinstein International Center, depicting a case study on the cross-border operation for Syria with regards to partnerships and remote management, one of the main findings was that many local organizations are relatively new, therefore need organizational and operational capacity development in international humanitarian standards and resource management, amongst others. On this basis, IOM integrated capacity building into its 2019 HRP activities, and undertook a series of initiatives - in close coordination with OCHA and other UN partners - to support local and international NGO partners. Assessments were carried out after each event to gauge partner feedback, and an assessment was conducted to understand the overall impact of these capacity building activities. The importance and continuing the capacity building activities was confirmed, with a strong demand for practical and continuous on-the-job training which focuses on how to apply policies, frameworks, guidance and lessons learned. In a separate survey carried out by a reputable NGO, 56% of respondents asked for more support to develop their financial planning and management skills – an area where IOM’s partner capacity assessment showed most room for improvement.

5 - Implementation of activities

For humanitarian partners, IOM is proposing to improve knowledge of key humanitarian workers in sectoral and cross-cutting technical areas, through the implementation of 5 NFI ToTs in Turkey targeting 120 participants and 5 trainings in Syria also targeting 120 field staff. In addition, delivery of 3 trainings in Turkey targeting 60 participants in shelter repair and rehabilitation and 5 trainings in Syria targeting 120 field staff. IOM will also continue to work closely with its partners on joint capacity development initiatives. Based on these plans, IOM will aim to improve partners' institutional processes and procedures. This will include the revision and developing 68 internal organizational policies and procedures in a variety of areas, such as human resources, procurement and finance. IOM will also conduct Quality and Accountability with its Turkey-based partners, targeting 100 participants. Activities are coordinated in SNFI cluster, for cluster related trainings, through joint planning between IOM, cluster leads and SAG members on the types of modules and knowledge included in training for effective operation. Through survey and consultations with different SNFI cluster members, IOM is ensuring the proper coordination with relevant stakeholders. IOM is part of the advisory group in the Partnership Working Group (PWG) and conducts activities in line with the PWG plans. IOM also coordinates all its activities and plans with the PWG to avoid duplication. Moreover, IOM also coordinates with other UN agencies such as UNHCR by crosschecking participant databases of trainings being conducted to mitigate duplication. Given IOM’s active role in the different coordination forums and its wide range of projects with numerous IPs, IOM can strengthen the capacity development initiatives already being done by reaching a wider audience and enhance partnerships between UN, INGOs and LNGOs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection mainstreaming is integrated in training curriculums of the capacity development activities. More specifically protection risks include the limited participation and representation of females in the trainings conducted and hence limited inputs on protection mainstreaming from participants. In order to mitigate this risk IOM is applying a quota of female participation in each of the trainings aiming to reach 50% females and 50% males in each cohort. Furthermore, in order to provide protection mainstreaming insights to the trainings and capacity development activities conducted, IOM aims to include female facilitators in the training teams to mitigate the risk of limited participation of females. Another risk is the high turn-over of NGO staff and loss of organizational knowledge and capacity, therefore IOM is aiming to have organizational capacity development rather than just individual capacity development through revisions of SoPs and enhancing the system of operation rather than the individual staff capacities. In addition, through the revisions of policies and procedures IOM is closely coordinating with the PSEA network to ensure integration of PSEA perspective in organization policies and procedures. Other risks include, lower than expected impact of capacity building initiatives, limited impact at field level due to lack of follow up mechanisms and challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development. Part of the measures that will address these challenges are: measuring impact through Organizational Capacity Assessments and monitoring score cards; targeting staff in Syria and Turkey through different methodologies such as ToTs, and coaching.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Capacity development is a collective effort and requires buy-in from senior management in both donors and partners. Awareness raising and advocacy on the needs and importance of capacity development can mitigate this risk. The identification and recruitment of qualified candidates to conduct capacity development initiatives can be a challenge. However, with a transparent and clear recruitment processes IOM will ensure the best qualified candidates to deliver capacity development activities. Finally, another risk would be the rapid change in security environment where an external condition might change the direction of the response and immediate interventions will limit the time available for trainings and capacity development. A mitigation measure would be ensure flexibility with regards to the timeframe of the project so that activities can be implemented when there are relevant stable conditions in the response.
