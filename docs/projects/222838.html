Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face a critical humanitarian situation that is ongoing due to a combination of sporadic armed clashes and intercommunal violence, food insecurity, outbreak of localized and national outbreak of infectious diseases and climate shocks. In the last quarter of 2024, food insecurity affected over 7 million people, exacerbated by flooding and conflict. Access to humanitarian assistance is severely restricted by violence, bureaucratic hurdles, and operational interference, making the delivery of essential services challenging. The conflict in neighbouring Sudan also spilled over, further destabilizing the situation and complicating aid efforts.
These factors have severely affected people’s livelihoods and hampered access to basic services including health, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and education. The Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) estimates that 9.3 million people, or 69% of the country’s 13.4 million population are in need of humanitarian assistance. The Health Cluster, in collaboration with its partners, has set a target of providing life-saving health services to 3.2 million people, including 800,000 women of reproductive age. This target includes internally displaced persons (IDP), refugees and returnees from neighboring countries, mainly Sudan. UNFPA and its implementing partners will contribute to achieving this target by improving equitable access to quality integrated health care services, covering maternal and sexual and reproductive health (SRH). UNFPA will also contribute to strengthening resilience and promoting humanitarian-development linkages that will lead to the recovery of the health system and coping mechanisms. To this end, UNFPA will provide quality integrated SRH services to 533,517 vulnerable women, men, boys and girls including 266,666 women of reproductive age with 64,637 live births. In addition. UNFPA will use the full implementation of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) and universal access to emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) services to reduce preventable maternal deaths. UNFPA will continue to lead the delivery of the MISP in acute SRH services tailored to the specific needs of the crisis. This will focus on the six key components of the MISP:
1. Leading the coordination of MISP activities at the national/local level in collaboration with the Ministry of Health.
2. Preventing sexual violence and responding to the needs of survivors.
3. Preventing the transmission of HIV and other STIs and reducing morbidity and mortality from these diseases.
4. Preventing excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality.
5. Preventing unintended pregnancies.
6. Planning for comprehensive SRH services.
In addition to service delivery, UNFPA will continue to lead the core pipeline for inter-agency emergency RH kits in coordination with the Health Cluster and WHO. To this end, UNFPA will provide coordinated supply support to the Health Cluster providing frontline SRH services.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is dire, driven by a combination of conflict, floods, food insecurity, outbreak of infections diseases and restricted humanitarian access. In Quarter 3 and Quarter 4 of 2024, more than 7 million people faced acute food insecurity. This was primarily due to disruptions in agricultural activities from persistent violence, climate shocks, notably flooding, and economic instability. Some areas, especially those affected by intercommunal conflict or floods, were at risk of famine. Seasonal flooding, which began in July 2024, affected millions, particularly in states like Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, and Jonglei. An estimated 3.3 million people were expected to be impacted by the floods by December 2024, further straining resources. The inter-communal violence and flooding led to mass displacement, with hundreds of thousands of people moving either to IDP camps or across borders into neighbouring countries, especially in response to the spillover from the conflict in Sudan.Access to healthcare was severely limited, and many displaced populations, particularly children, are at high risk of malnutrition. The combination of food shortages and limited access to clean water made disease outbreaks a persistent threat in the country. The currently is currently facing multiple diseases outbreaks at national and local levels. The massive influx of returnees and refugees from neighbouring countries such as Sudan and Ethiopia have also increased the burden on the country’s already overstretched health system. This has resulted in limited and/or no accessibility to essential services, contributing to the country’s poor health indicators due to overcrowded health facilities, making it difficult for existing health infrastructure to meet the increased demand. South Sudan is one of the countries with the highest maternal mortality rate, standing at 1,223 per 100,000 live births, according to United Nations data from 2020. The United Nations Cooperation Framework (UNCF) report for 2020 highlighted that only 19% of births in the country were attended by skilled health personnel, and the rate of home deliveries was particularly high at 87%. Access to emergency obstetric care services is limited, with only 40% of health facilities functioning, and international organizations play a significant role in providing health services. Various factors have contributed to the poor health indicators, such as reduced financing opportunities, a weak health system with a low percentage of the national budget (less than 2%) allocated to the health sector. In addition to the challenges mentioned, there is very low interest in implementing health policies such as the family planning policy, the draft law against gender-based violence and severe shortages of health personnel, particularly doctors (with only one doctor for every 65,574 people) and midwives (with one midwife for every 39,088 people).The Health Cluster estimates that 3.2 million people will be in critical need of health services in 2025. This target includes 525,484 adult women and 488,970 adult men, 399,338 adolescent girls and 401,215 adolescent boys aged 10-17 who will need health care. An additional 800,000 are women of reproductive age (15-49 years). UNFPA projects that only one third of these women and adolescent girls will have access to high-quality sexual and reproductive health.

2. Project activities

The project will support equitable access of crisis-affected population to essential, high-quality SRH services using the MISP model. Therefore, the project will support the following activities:1. Provision emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) services: The service will be delivered through fixed and mobile health facilities in priority counties 1, 2 and 3. To achieve this, UNFPA will support the deployment of mobile teams in emergencies. In addition, UNFPA will support existing health facilities to ensure the provision of MISP in the event of a crisis. UNFPA will ensure that the provision of EmONC services is coordinated with existing health services provided by other partners as part of the overall Health Cluster coordination. UNFPA will support referral of cases to the next level of services. Through the provision of EmONC services, UNFPA will contribute to the reduction of excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality and prevention of unintended pregnancies as well as planning for comprehensive SRH services.2. Provision of Supplies: UNFPA will procure, distribute and pre-position inter-agency emergency reproductive health kits in hotspot areas. The procurement and distribution of the kits will be in line with quarterly supply needs forecasts. The supplies will support the provision of clinical delivery assistance, management of STIs, access to family planning services, reduction of HIV transmission, management of complicated miscarriages and fistulas, support for surgical interventions such as caesarean section and clinical management of rape cases. The supplies will support all health facilities providing the MISP at all levels. UNFPA will ensure that 30% of health facilities receiving supplies meet all WHO IPC minimum requirements.3. Support clinical management of rape: UNFPA will support the provision of clinical management of rape (CMR) services by strengthening the capacity of service providers and providing supplies for post-rape treatment. In coordination with the GBV sub-cluster, UNFPA will ensure that CMR services are provided at all levels using fixed and mobile facilities. To this end, UNFPA will integrate CMR and psychosocial support into GBV and health services. 4. Integration of family planning: UNFPA will support the integration of family planning services into SRH service delivery at all levels. During health education sessions, UNFPA will introduce different family planning methods and family planning messages will be integrated into antenatal and postnatal care services. UNFPA will support partners by providing family planning commodities as part of the interagency emergency sexual and reproductive health kits. 5. Capacity building training: UNFPA will support capacity-building training that will contribute to the quality of services and the motivation of service providers. Capacity-building training modules will be developed following capacity needs assessments. 6. Supportive supervision: UNFPA will conduct routine support supervisions to ensure the quality of health services and promote accountability to the affected population. This will involve working closely with facility staff to identify and resolve potential operational challenges, provide guidance on best practices, ensure compliance with established protocols and promote patient-centered care. It will also provide a platform for open communication, feedback and capacity building, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and responsive health care delivery. 7. Coordination of Sexual and Reproductive health in Emergency: UNFPA will continue to support the SRH technical working group at national and subnational levels, under the overall leadership of the Health Cluster. Through this coordination forum, UNFPA will ensure that all stakeholders, including line ministries at national and subnational levels, NGOs and health care providers, are well trained and equipped to respond quickly and effectively to emergencies.
