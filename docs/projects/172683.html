Project Summary

The project is titled FOSELIP-Food Security & Livelihoods Program. The project seeks to improve livelihoods,food and nutrition security and build the resilience of 18,000 vulnerable households translating to approximately 90,000 people in Buhera and Mutare districts. This will be achieved through increased access to inputs; increased access to water for crop and livestock production; and capacity building of farmers to increase production and productivity. The activities will run from 01 April 2021 – 31 December 2021. The total budget for the project is USD$1,290, 615.00
The main focus of the project is to assist vulnerable households with access to labour and land to improve crop and livestock production through increasing access to inputs, training and extension services. Labour-saving and climate-smart agriculture (CSA) practices will be promoted through the establishment of demonstration sites by lead farmers as well as mentoring farmers in good agricultural practices.The vulnerable farmers will be provided with a starter pack of key agricultural inputs to prime the production of crops and small livestock in the two districts.The project will link private sector input suppliers and output market buyers with smallholder farmers. Private sector suppliers and buyers will engage with market facilitators. Given the increasing levels of vulnerability in the two districts, the food security and nutrition status of many families has significantly reduced. LEAD realized the need to support the drought affected farmers with nutritious and fast growing vegetable seeds to bridge the food gap under the household nutrition gardening activities. The inputs for crop and livestock inputs will be provided to vulnerable households including the elderly, women headed, child headed, the disabled and people living with HIV and Aids with access to labour and land but with no capital. Greater emphasis for small livestock will be placed on youth, women, child headed and women headed households.

1 - Activities

Improving access to crop and livstock inputs.Holding of inception workshops to sensitize beneficiaries and stakeholders on the project objectives, implementation modalities and setting up of the community project implementation, monitoring and evaluation teams. Using the OCHA criteria, District stakeholders will identify, select wards from which beneficiaries will be mobilized, selected and registered. Setting up and managing the grievances redress mechanism. Procurement, setting up and managing input distribution points. Each HHs to receive input pack consisting of cowpeas-2.5kgs, either sorghum seed 5kgs or maize seed 10kgs,basal fertilizer-100kgs and top dressing fertilizer-100kgs). The crop inputs will targeted 6,232 HHs. For Community Gardens each household will receive a pack of the following seeds: tomato, butternut, carrots, onions, covo and NUAA45 sugar beans which is rich in Iron and Zinc. The Household Nutrition gardens will target 1968 HHs. Provide subsidized livestock inputs- supplementary feeds -each household to receive 6 birds and 505 kgs poultry feed and will reach out to 5,976 households. Household targeted to receive goats will each get 3 animals and will reach out to 980HHs , households targeted to receive survival stockfeed gets 15 bags of 50kgs and will reach out to 2,844 HHs. In order to facilitate the provision of agronomic and animal production training, LEAD will promote the formation of farmers groups from the farmers assisted with crop, gardening and livestock inputs and the establishment of demonstration plots by farmer groups. Capacitate and train farmer groups on Good Agricultural practices and group dynamics. Train farmer groups in processing and transforming under-utilized indigenous plants that have a food and nutrition security and global ecological importance. Rehabilitating Dip tanks Facilitate the formation of livestock production groups (Made up mostly of Households receiving crop, livestock and gardening inputs as well as other members benefiting from the rehabilitated assets) and coach them on the importance of rehabilitating, maintaining and raising resources for the repairing the dip tanks. Train beneficiaries to rehabilitate dip tanks as well establishing household nutrition gardens and water troughs for livestock. Household Nutrition gardens (as mentioned above) to be established in areas with good water supply and accessible to the households . . Activities: Identification of non-functioning dip tanks, assess the materials required and select the dip tanks that can be rehabilitated at a minimum cost. Communities in their groups to contribute the local materials and only procure non local materials. Communities to participate in the rehabilitation of the dip tanks. Pilot a user pay system in order to save money for future repairs. Mutare: 5 dip tanks and , Buhera: 5 dip tanks Extension and Advisory Services- Farmers will be encouraged to form groups and establish demonstration plots which will be used as centers of excellence to promote good agricultural practices Strengthened monitoring, coordination and knowledge management. Participate in vulnerability assessment and monitoring studies. Documentation, knowledge management of project activities, food security information and dissemination.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

LEAD organized a design workshop that involved key district stakeholders as well as an assessment of the food security priorities of two districts. The workshops identified the major drivers of food insecurity and the interventions needed to improve agricultural production and productivity. The design was driven by the aspirations and vision of the community which guarantees project sustainability and financial contributions from the beneficiaries. District project implementation teams comprising members all key stakeholders to guide, resolve conflicts and monitor the implementation of the project activities will be set up. The team will meet monthly and receive feedback as well as making sure that the project remains on track. In the wards the beneficiaries will be capacitated to facilitate the participation of all community members in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project. Members of the committee for each farmer group will be drawn from all the groups( Women youth, men and disabled) to ensure equal representation and more women will be encouraged to take up leadership positions in the various committee and sub-committees. The project will also establish suggestion boxes within the communities as well as establish a telephone hot line for beneficiaries to give feedback.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmentally safe and clean practices that conserve and protect the environment will be promoted in the two districts. The involvement of the Ministry of Agriculture, Mechanization and Development of Irrigation, as well as the Environmental Management Agencies (EMA) will convey the importance of the attention to the sustainable use of natural resources and will facilitate the sensitization of beneficiaries on environmental issues and the correct information of beneficiaries. The project will implement recommended and updated environmental conservation strategies based on the latest scientific discoveries, able to guarantee that the beneficiaries have an appropriate understanding of the activities and their cultivation methodologies. This includes the promotion of conservation agriculture (CA) and climate resilient agricultural practices, awareness of the impacts of agricultural activities on the environment, including the safe use of pesticides and herbicides and the impacts of climate change. The project will raise awareness and discourage communities from establishing community gardens on wet lands as well as cultivating along river banks. The rehabilitation of dip tanks and practicing conservation agriculture will reduce soil erosion as well as promoting hygiene practices among the beneficiaries.
