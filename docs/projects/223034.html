Project Summary

According to Health Cluster Inputs for the Humanitarian Response Plan 2025, In 2025, the health cluster plans to reach 3.2 million people with essential lifesaving health services. The current health status of the population is poor. Malaria is the leading cause of death and illness, affecting half of the population. Measles vaccination is low, and outbreaks are frequent. Cholera and Hepatitis E have also been reported in several areas. Infant and child mortality rates are high. According to IOM Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) Urban Component’s Quantitative Assessment Report 2022 for Juba County, 48.9% of households were unable to access healthcare in the Internal Displaced Persons camps, of whom 51% were female and 49% were male members. Furthermore, the report reveals that 77.3% of the children under 5 years were vaccinated of whom 65.4% for polio vaccine, 61.7% Measles vaccine, 41.1%tuberculosis and 29.9% for Tetanus vaccine respectively. The assessment report stipulated that 43.5% of pregnant women access antenatal care services. The report stated that 64.4% of the households needs access to protection such as MHPSS among others. WASH services are limited in the IDP camps where 15.9% of households collect water from rainwater and only 3.2% of boreholes were protected exposing the IDPs to water borne diseases like diarrhoea, typhoid among others. Tambura County of Western Equatoria saw significant human rights violations and abuses in 2021, including targeting civilians based on ethnicity and gender. At least 440 people were killed according to the UNMISS Human Rights Division, and over 80,000people were displaced by the Tambura conflict (HNO 2022). In coordination with the Health Cluster, PDP aims to provide quality health care to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system. These goals support the 2025 HRP’s (Humanitarian Response Plan) objectives of saving lives, reducing health risks, and enhancing coping and recovery.

1. Needs

According to the Inter Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA) 2024, the main barriers to access and utilization of care services were long distance to health facility and lack of transport (50%), long waiting time for services (36%) and insecurity or security concerns (6%) (floods, and high cost of healthcare (mentioned by host communities, IDPs, and returnees). These barriers have resulted in low access and utilization of health care services, especially for women and girls who face additional challenges. Only 56% of the population can access health facilities within one hour’s time, while 66% of the population has difficulty in accessing health services. The functionality and capacity of health facilities are also limited and inadequate. As of September2023, only 12% are fully functional, offering the full package of Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS)available and open at the required hours. The quality of healthcare services is also compromised by inadequate health workers, limited supplies, long waiting hours, water scarcity, poor maternal health services, and lack of follow-up care. South Sudan has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in the world, with 1,223deaths per 100,000 live births (according to the United Nations interagency estimate) . The access and utilization of maternal health services are suboptimal, as only 39 percent of women receive the recommended four antenatal care visits and 14.7percent of births are attended by skilled health personnel. Currently there 6,708 people who need health services in Tambura County.

2. Project activities

Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives. Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static health facilities (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population. Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly, implementing feedback mechanisms and ensuring compliance IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using scorecards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities. Strengthen reporting mechanisms; Capacity building for comprehensive management of rape (CMR) in health facilities and strengthen referral pathway.Conduct routine immunization services at health facilities and Community. Conduct U5 and Over 5 curative consultations at health facilities. Conduct level 3 MHPSS services
