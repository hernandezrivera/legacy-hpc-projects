Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Karat Zuria, Kena and Segen Zuria woredas of Konso Zone SNNP Region, where due to conflict 39,048 persons are displaced and in urgent need of humanitarian assistance, especially in the area of access to water, sanitation and hygiene services. The project will last for six months and will benefit all the 472 internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the target area and 38,576 host community members. The project activities will focus, water tracking for both IDPs and hosting communities which will benefit 39,048 people in total (472 IDPs and 38,576 Host community) construction of 10 semi-permanent latrines benefitting 2,000 IDPs and host communities, hygiene promotion and delivery of 500 essential WASH Non-Food Item (NFI) packages, including provision of 500 Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits for adolescent girls, benefitting 2,500 IDPs, rehabilitation and maintenance of 3 water schemes (motorized scheme) and training of WASHCOs, providing access to safe water to 39,048 people. The proposed project will also promote the practice of adequate hand washing and environmental sanitation to mitigate potential outbreaks of water-borne diseases, including cholera, and curb the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic.
To further promote equity, the project will ensure regular consultation of women, PWD (17.6% of the total beneficiaries), elderly people and other vulnerable groups. Staff will follow the Core Commitments for Children in Humanitarian Action framework to uphold the rights of children. Project events on WASH will include sessions on gender, children rights, and equity to create long-lasting awareness. The project will promote equitable access to humanitarian assistance regardless of beneficiaries’ sex for example through accessibility of project sites to pregnant women, elders and people with disabilities. The project will also identify potential negative effect on different gender and age groups and put preventive measures in place. As a standard, HEKS follows strict Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH) rules when interacting with beneficiaries. Participation of women was key during pre-project consultations and project design and will continue playing a central role during implementation and evaluation. HEKS has been implementing in the target areas WASH project, COVID-19 prevention, which is emergency and have office in Karat Town. Mixed humanitarian teams including male and female members will carry out activities following a gender-sensitive approach.
The project will be implemented from the HEKS Office in Karat town in Konso Zone of SNNP region. HEKS has qualified staff at field level and equipped facilities to carry out the intervention. HEKS is participating in the local Government-NGO Forum and has been very active in other forums and platforms, including the humanitarian coordination platform since late 2019, and the WASH Cluster SAG. The organization is closely engaged with other key humanitarian actors in the zone including implementing partners CISP, LWF. The zonal and woreda offices, the DRR office and WASH Cluster have been providing information, data and advice in the process of developing this project for coordinated efforts. HEKS will further engage with locally active stakeholders to avoid duplication of effort and maximize efficiency. HEKS will work with relevant government offices/agencies and community leaders that have roles and responsibilities in humanitarian crisis preparedness and response. Coordination will occur through regular information sharing at both local (woreda, zonal and regional) level and at national level for example through 5W reports to different clusters as well as through participation in inter-agency reviews of needs and priorities

1. - Projects activities

Water trucking service will be carried out for a period of 30 days providing safe and clean water of at least 7.5 litres per person/ day that is enough with SPHERE minimum standards for drinking water supply. HEKS will also ensure that minimum drinking water quality standards are met and in accordance with WHO Drinking Water Quality Guidelines. The project will chlorinate the water in an appropriate storage facility prior to pumping it into the water tanker or treat water in storage at the drop off point and or provide appropriate household water treatment chemicals. The activity is planned to start immediately upon grant release. The main water sources in all three target woredas are boreholes which are currently being also used by other WASH partners and government interventions. In total 39,048 people will benefit from the tracking out of which 38,576 host community members and 472 IDPS.Construction of 10 semi-permanent latrines: 10 communal semi-permanent latrines with four stances will be constructed at IDPs collective centers and selected community places. WASH cluster standards will be followed during the construction, in order to provide proper sanitation facilities to the target communities. Latrines will be sex disaggregated and will have hand washing facilities. The design of the latrines will consider the need of people with disabilities and all latrines will be lockable from inside for privacy purpose during use. The latrines will have 4 stances and 2 shower rooms. Sphere standards of on sanitation (50 people/stance) will be respected when the activity will be planned and implemented. This will benefit 2,000 IDPs and host community. Delivery of 500 essential WASH Non-Food Item (NFI) packages: Essential life-saving WASH NFIs including water treatment chemicals distributed. For the composition of the kit HEKS will follow the WASH NFI Note V3 which include Dignity kit (1/female). During the distributions, an induction on the correct use of items, including WTC, will be implemented by project staff, to ensure that items are used effectively. Wherever needed, a leaflet will be used and distributed to facilitate the process. Local language will be used to ensure proper comprehension of the messages disseminated. HEKS will ensure the respect of the distribution guidelines in the context of COVID-19 developed by the Shelter and NFI cluster. Those include, among others, recommendation on how to select distribution points, how to organize the sites and recommendations for counterparts participating to the activity. This will benefit 2,500 IDPs and host communities. Hygiene promotion: 40 Hygiene Promoters, selected within the target communities will be trained for three days to carry out hygiene promotion activities. Their performance will be reviewed regularly by experienced staff. The training for hygiene promoters will focus on community mobilization, door-to-door visits, dissemination of good H&S practices, and use of IEC materials. Moreover, the training will boost the trainees’ knowledge on water borne disease transmission, prevention and treatment, and appropriate use of WASH facilities. To develop good/safe hygiene practices, prevent diseases, and encourage positive health behaviors, hygiene promoters will conduct door-to-door visits and will disseminate information within the target communities. This will benefit 39,048 IDPs and host communities. Rehabilitation and maintenance of 3 water schemes (Motorized water schemes): The project will provide spare parts and repair/maintenance works for 3 water schemes (motorized water schemes) serving IDPs and the host community, in order to enhance the continuity of the service, as the water supply system in the target area has been severely damaged by the conflict. This will ensure water supply both for the IDPs, the hosting communities and the user community at large. This will benefit 39,048 persons (472 IDPs and 38,576) host community members).

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

HEKS has a strong commitment to receive and respond to beneficiary feedback in a safe timely manner, including being contextually relevant and in line with literacy levels and language diversity. Local leadership and community members contribute to assessments, monitoring and evaluation, as part of key informant interviews (KIIs), focus group discussions (FGDs), and post-activity / end line data collection. Such participatory data collection mechanisms will ensure that all categories of affected persons (including women, youth, persons with disabilities and older persons) are actively involved providing information and feedback about whether the interventions are effectively addressing their specific needs. Anonymity will be guaranteed in this process. Collected field data will be analyzed in a gender and age disaggregated form by M&E and technical teams. Community sessions (potentially including single-sex sessions) will be organized to share and discuss the monitoring results. These sessions will also be used to assess the opportunity of adjusting the interventions (e.g. content/format of hygiene promotion sessions) to better address priorities/needs of different social groups. Different groups of beneficiaries/project participants will thus have the opportunity to provide feedback as appropriate. This will entail informing beneficiaries, either directly or through their community leaders, during regular community sessions or ad meetings, of any change in project strategy or activities that follow specific feedback / complaints (that is, closing the feedback loop). Since 2018, HEKS is a member of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS Alliance) and is thus committed to complying with the nine standards of the voluntary code to improve the quality and effectiveness of all its humanitarian aid projects worldwide. HEKS will make sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, where and how to obtain it. In line with the organization’s Human Rights Based Approach, HEKS is committed to ensuring that the proposed project is based on a participatory and empowering approach that enables and encourages affected populations, especially marginalized individuals, to play an active role in the project and in decision-making processes that affect their lives. This translates into putting place in the targeted locations specific mechanisms to provide timely and accessible information, and to receive and respond to beneficiary feedback throughout the duration of the activity. Gender-appropriate feedback will be ensured through ensuring feedback locations are safe and accessible to women and girls; through deployment, where possible, of female staff; separate FGD with women. Age-appropriate feedback will be enhanced through ensuring feedback locations are safe and accessible to people from different age groups, including children and the elderly. HEKS will also provide opportunities for women, people with disabilities and older people to hold positions of responsibility and be represented on community-based committees including as community focal points for assessment teams. An accessible, well understood and confidential mechanism, including during Post-Distribution Monitoring, will be set up for receiving feedback (suggestions and complaints), with a clear follow-up system on all reports. HEKS’s feedback and complaint mechanism includes information sharing on: project objectives, outcomes and outputs, implementing agency and donor; selection criteria and mechanisms for beneficiaries; composition of the WASH kit and prioritization; WASH activities to be carried out. The validation and agreement with the main stakeholders on the selection of sites and beneficiaries of distributions will take place through meetings with community members, local authorities and cluster representatives
