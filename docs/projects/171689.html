Project Summary

Somalia continues to grapple with multiple shocks that exacerbate existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. The 2020 Gu seasonal flooding (river flooding) contributed to loss and damage to property, housing, farmland and infrastructure. The riverine areas of Hiran, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Middle Juba and Lower Juba were some of the worst-affected areas. The COVID-19 pandemic and associated socio-economic impacts also contributed to the food insecurity situation in Somalia. As a result, the FSC put and average projection of 3.5 million Somalis to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through 2021.
Further, with an estimated 2.6 million women, men, girls and boys currently displaced, the IDPs remain in need of multi-sectoral support services including protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions. The most vulnerable groups, who are exposed to the highest risks and are consistently socially excluded, include women, children, the elderly, child- and female-headed households, the physically and mentally disabled, people living in camp and camp-like settings
Lower juba remains a hub of the most vulnerable returnees from Dadaab refugee camps and over 6400 IDP’s in 144 sites in Kismayo alone, displaced by multifaceted conflicts, floods, drought and government evictions. This intervention targets IDPs, urban poor and poor agro-pastoral households that have been hit by the successive droughts, ﬂoods, conﬂict and displacements and are in dire need of immediate food assistance and livelihood support.
SFH will address the immediate household food needs in the target locations through direct unconditional cash transfer activity targeting 400 most vulnerable IDPs households including women and children headed households for a period of 5 months at the rate of 70 USD per beneficiary in Lower Juba. Besides, to build resilience and prevent sudden shocks, this intervention will contribute to household food security through income generating activities while restoring productive communal assets such as Communal water pan and fedder access roads. 200 unskilled, but most vulnerable, able-bodied men and women who will work for 80 dollars per months and 8 supervisors/Forman’s @120 for 3 months with benefit from the conditional cash grant.
This response will also provide seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and tools such as: - nutritionally dense Crop and Vegetable seeds to 400 IDP farmers that reside in Kismayo but left their farms due to floods or conflicts in Gobweyn, Wirkoy, qamqam, yontoy and Sanguuni locations, this will reduce the dependency syndrome on food aid while contributing to increased agricultural production at the community level.
To protect key livelihoods assets of IDPs and to preserve future means of livelihoods, 400 vulnerable pastorals from pastoral communities within the district will be supported with livestock treatment drugs and fodder vouchers for their lactating animals to replenish their physical body and enhance their market value. In general, the following will be achieved through this response:
1. Provide unconditional cash grant to 400 most vulnerable households in Kismayo @70 dollar for 5 round;
2. Provide seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs (Crop and vegetable) to 400 urban poor and small-scale poor farmers along river juba in Kismayo district.
3.Provide assorted agricultural hand tools to 400 poor farmers to enhance their farming and production ability.
4. 400 pastoral households will be provided with livestock treatment and fodder vouchers
5. 200HH supported through Unconditional cash transfer for restoration of communal assets
6. Conduct Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), DRR and Early warnings

Project Activities

The proposed intervention aims to improve the critical food security situation, nutritional status for IDP's, women, men, boys and girls facing critical humanitarian needs by providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance while prioritizing the most vulnerable members of the target communities in Kismayo. Due to the complex humanitarian situation, the IDPs remains one of the most vulnerable population groups constantly facing acute food and nutritional insecurity when disaster hits. Kismayo has over 70000 IDP in 144 Sites with nearly 5000 IDPs in IPC 3 and 4. The project aims to improve access to immediate food, protect livelihood assets, and reduce vulnerability to shocks through provision of unconditional grant, conditional cash, livelihood inputs and tools and livestock treatment and fodder in line with the cluster strategy. Access to immediate food for the most vulnerable households will be done through and unconditional cash transfer. This will provide quick impact and improve diet diversity to IDP Households and urban poor households with malnourished children while ensuring access to other needed service at the household level. The unconditional cash will improve immediate access to food and other much needed services. Apart from providing immediate food access, vulnerable communities require to restore their productive livelihood asset to prevent and withstand sudden future shocks. Both agriculture and livestock production will therefore be enhanced. Many Urban poor, rural and IDP households have access to large farmlands but are incapacitated by lack of access to farming tools and seeds. The proposed interventions aim to support these groups to engage in agricultural activities through provision of agricultural inputs, tools and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) training. The proposed activity envisages the promotion of fast growing nutrient dense crops for improving the nutritional condition. The activity will also enable women to sell the surplus from their produce in the local markets thereby enabling them to earn extra income. The intervention will target livestock intervention which is the dominant livelihood of the target population, through provision of emergency livestock treatment and fodder through vouchers. These livestock services will mitigate the effects of desert locust and drought risks and prevent them from deteriorating into emergency phase.In line with the FSL strategy, this proposed intervention will give priority to households with DISABLE persons, Minority groups, women and child headed households. MEN, WOMEN, BOYS AND GIRLS are all given appropriate priority. 30% of the CFW beneficiaries will be women. Households with malnourished child, elderly, pregnant and lactating women or sick member will be prioritized for the Unconditional grant.In Summary the following summarized activities will be undertaken:1. Provision of unconditional cash grant to 400 most vulnerable households in Kismayo @70 dollar for 5 round; 2. Provision seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs (Crop, vegetable and Fishing kits) to 400 urban poor and small-scale poor farmers along river juba in Kismayo district. 3.Provision of assorted agricultural hand tools to 400 poor farmers to enhance their farming and production ability.4. 300 pastoral households will be provided with treatment and fodder vouchers. 5. 200 HH will be supported through CFW activity that involves restoration of communal asset6. Training of 100 farmers on Good Agricultural practices (GAP), DRR and EWS. SFH banks on multiplier effect to cascade the training content to other farmers by the trained farmers.

Project Indicators

1) Number of Households who received 70 dollars per month for 5 months. Target: 400HH. SFH will provide unconditional cash transfer to the most vulnerable IDP (new and among the protracted), to save lives and livelihood assets. Priority will be given to most vulnerable IDPs affected by flooding, conflict displacement and are in IPC 3 and 4. The unconditional cash transfers gives flexibility to the beneficiaries on the use of cash. SFH will use biometric system for registration and validation of beneficiary. The beneficiaries will be given sim card with their details to receive the money and redeem at their nearest Hormud (Money transfer company) at the end of every month for 5 months. PDM will be conducted after 1st and 3rd round of cash transfers to monitor the usage of cash and assess the impact on the market. The beneficiaries will be given USD 70 which approximately contributes to 79% of the CMB, for 5 rounds.2) Number of vulnerable Household farmers that received 8 variety and nutritionally dense vegetable and crop seed. Target: 400HH. Provide nutritionally -dense assorted vegetable and crop seed as well as farm tools to 400 neediest urban, peri-urban and IDPs households in Kismayo district.400 agropastoral dropout and riverine farmers who have access to farm land will be supported with high quality and nutritionally dense vegetable and crop seed. They will also be provided with farming tools for land preparation, weeding and harvesting in order to increase productivity. Each identified household will be provided with 240g of 8 seed varieties such as (Amaranthus (30 grams), Carrots (Karoota) (20 grams), Mustard (Khardal)(25 grams), Okra (Baamiya) (15grams), Onion (Basal) (60 grams), Sweet Pepper (30 grams), Tomato (15 grams) and Watermelon( 45 grams) ) and crop kits consisting of 20KG maize and 15KG beans per farmer.3) Number of Households who received assorted agricultural hand tools. Target: 400HH. 4) Number of pastoral and Agro-pastoral households who received treatment and fodder vouchers. Target: 300HH. 5) Rehabilitation of communal water pans and feeder road through CFW. Target: 26) Number of Individuals who participated in the Good Agricultural training sessions, DRR and EWS. Target: 100 farmers. SFH will Conduct 4 sessions of 25 participants each (Cognizant of COVID-19) on Good Agricultural practices (GAP) for the 400 livelihood input beneficiaries to enhance their capacity of good farming techniques and to improve food production.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. Mitigation: a) SFH will Identify central locations to ensure beneficiaries do not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. b) Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. c) Farm tools will be distributed in an appropriate sack/bag that can be carried to allow beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 2) Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance Mitigation: a) All SFH staff and vendors are required to PSEA policy and its violation will lead to disciplinary measures including termination of contract. b) SFH will ensure appropriate gender balance of registration and distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. c) SFH has accessible and functional response and feedback mechanism include real-time free hot lines and information desks at the distribution sites to receive timely complaints or compliments. 3) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities a) Targeting will be guided by needs, access and vulnerability and the vulnerable groups include women headed household, child headed households b) We will establish separate male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. c) SFH will make special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits. they will be assisted and their safe arrivals ensured.
