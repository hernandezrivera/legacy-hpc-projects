Project Summary

The main target of this 12 months project will be to contribute to the strategy objective 3: strengthen the resilience of affected populations, promote early recovery and voluntary and safe durable solutions to displacement, and support social cohesion. GSF project team propose to reached 1500 women and adolescent girls in the host community and IDP across Borno State. The project will use vocational/ skills acquisition training, coaching, mentoring and other access to income generating activities to support livelihood stabilisation and income recovery which will be targeting conflict-affected Adolescent youth and vulnerable women. The project will also provide employment opportunity and empowerment to beneficiaries in the selected LGAs of Borno state in the North east of Nigeria, thereby contributing directly to the SDG Objective and Borno State development plan (Support Livelihood Stabilisation and income recovery).
The intended activity will basically be; to provide vocational skills training to the most vulnerable victims of the insurgency some selected IDP camps and host communities within Maiduguri/ Jere LGA of Borno State; thereby empowering the beneficiaries so that they can be independent and cater for immediate household needs. In vocational training: the vocational training response plan includes three flexible approaches that can be selected depending on the context. In the urban areas, we will identify the most vulnerable in all the IDP camps/ Host communities and get them enrolled into the vocational training Centre. GSF will now use the expertise of artisan to support the training of the conflict affected adolescents, young people and vulnerable women to undergo tailoring, leather work (making of belt, Shoe, Purse, bag, etc.), bead making, Tie/dye, cosmetology, paint making and catering. For rigorous employability training, especially in parallel technical training and apprenticeships, GSF will offer training and mentoring to enhance the ‘employability’ skills of adolescent girls/ boys and women to increase success. At the end of the training, GSF might empower all the beneficiaries with start-up kits/ machines so as to establish their small-scale enterprise of choice to make earnings and be able to find alternative livelihood income. Finally, we shall put in placed appropriate tracking tool in monitoring the performance of beneficiaries and to also check for sustainability of their business.

1 - Needs

Getting to 12 years protracted conflict in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States of North East Region of Nigeria has make Borno State as the epicenter of the crisis that has been characterized by complex displacement, which has left the already vulnerable populations in need of rapid life-saving emergency and developmental assistance. Most of the affected people seek shelter in urban centers or newly accessible local Government Areas (LGAs) in search of protection, livelihoods and safety, increasing competition for basic services between displaced and already vulnerable host communities as been a huge burden to the general population. The current situation is a major protection crisis, as well as a food crisis. There have been many reports of killings, human rights violations, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation, abuse, forced child abduction and recruitment, child labour, and arbitrary detentions. Movement restrictions and curfew in some LGAs have also led many people to negative coping mechanismsWith the ongoing crisis, efforts have been made to mobilize support and seek solutions to the crisis. National governments along with state and regional governments, have made significant headway in responding to the needs of the affected people in the State. For instance, the State Governments have embarked on a large-scale rehabilitation effort of the LGA in Borno State– dubbed the “Borno Development plan” - to lay the foundation for the safe and voluntary return of displaced persons. As of January 2018, it was reported that about 30,000 houses, 57 hand water pumps, 154 classrooms had been renovated or built by the Government, Telecommunications have been restored, with the reconnection of a major mobile network operator in December 2017.As of April 2019, estimates put the numbers of returnees at around 70,000. However, the IDP camp is still operative and today hosts an estimate number 700,000 IDPs. The security situation however remains alarming, with civilians living in LGAs being allowed to move only within a radius of up to five kilometers outside the town.
