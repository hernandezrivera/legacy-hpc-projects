Project Summary

Friendship Support Association (FSA) is a local non-governmental organization established in 2007 with registration number 0624 at the Agency for Civil Society Organization (ACSO). With strong partnership and support from many international donors and governments partners, we have now grown to larger group of 150 committed staff and hundreds of community volunteers. The number of projects we are implementing annually have also grown to 19 (10 of which are related with humanitarian response). Humanitarian response intervention is one of the strategic focus area of the organization. Afar region as a whole is highly prone to recurrent natural disaster that seriously threatens the livelihood of the pastoralist communities. Therefore, FSA has been implementing WaSH and Shelter in districts like Afambo and Amibara and Koneba, which were highly vulnerable areas to severe 2020 flood and the recent conflict. FSA proactively, participate in national and regional cluster coordination platform (i.e. WaSH, ES/NFIs, Agriculture, Education, Protection and Cash working group as well) to contribute the coordination effort of the clusters and avoid duplication of activities. In this regard, FSA actively participate the cluster coordination meetings and regularly provide 5Ws reports. FSA ensures that all sectors complement each other to produce programming that saves lives, is cost-effective and bring about positive behavioral changes. The main purpose of this project is to create improved access to education, health, dignity, safety and security of conflict-affected internally displaced and returnee people through adequate and appropriate live saving and living conditions. FSA wants to address 27,439 internally displaced and returnee individuals (6,098 households) (18,925 women and girls; 8,514 male and boys) from which 700 are school students. As such the project has the following specific objectives
Specific objective 1: Improved access to basic education for conflict displaced and returnee school age children (girls and boys) through establishment of temporary learning shelters, provision of basic education materials and capacity building to existing and new teachers.
Specific objective 2: Improved access to save drinking water, adequate hygiene and sanitation services and prevention of risks of water-related diseases for conflict-affected and returnee people.
Specific objective 3: Improved protection of conflict-affected internally displaced and returnee people against threats from weather, gender-based violence, theft or disease through emergency shelter solutions and items necessary to support daily life activities.
Specific objective 4: Improved Information Management Capacity of humanitarians’ actors and government sector bureaus of in Afar.
- Sector(s) of activity
1) Education in Emergency: Conflict displaced school age children (girls and boys) and disconnected their schooling calendar. The proposed action aims at improving access to education services through re-established/renovated learning schoolrooms, provision of basic education materials and capacity building to existing and/or new teachers.
2) WASH (Water trucking, Sanitation and Hygiene): The most vulnerable conflict affected internally displaced and returnee people in two Woredas (Chifra and Dewe) of Afar region will be supported with provision of save drinking water, hygiene and environmental sanitation promotion, and basic hygiene/dignity kits.
3) Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items (ESNFIs):15,000 Conflict affected internally displaced and returnee people of Dewe Woreda in Afar region will be supported with emergency shelter and NFIs (such as temporary shelter materials and kitchen kits) as per the national ES/NFI cluster standards. The aim is to reduce climate sufferings and increased access to quality and equitable shelter and household items to have meaningful access to essential and lifesaving services.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

During the project designing process, FSA program team strictly examines the “Do No Harm” principle to protect human and its environment and this will be discussed with the target community where planned project is to be implemented. In addition to this, FSA gives orientation for all newly appointed staffs prior to start of the actual duty. In the process of orientation, one of the guiding documents that should be briefly explained is “FSA’s code of conduct” which is mandatory to be considered by everyone. And the target community will also be communicated about what do’s and don’ts of the organization and channeled with appropriate information system. FSA will never work against the standard of humanitarian intervention (CHS). The fact that this is an emergency response project, the beneficiaries are IDPs, Returnees and host communities. All the IDPs, Returnees and host HHs are clearly identified by the local government. Although FSA is working in Afar in order to fill the gap in information and resources in the region; it also abides to the Guiding principle of humanitarian action to provide humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable wherever their origin is. FSA provides priority for lactating and pregnant mothers, children under 5 and people living with disabilities (PLWD). And in the process of implementation, the implementer of this project will establish targeting and appeal committees and establish clearly defined beneficiaries selection strategies. As long as the community participation in the planning of project is concerned, there are two scenarios; the first one is during problem identification by engaging in community conversation groups and the other one is during launching and implementation of the project. During the project kick-off, objectives of the project are explained and sharing of responsibilities for implementation is also addressed. This means all the concerned bodies (community groups, local government sectors and FSA) will share responsibility of project implementation and consensus will be reached to be accountable for every action. In addition to this, monitoring of the project and close supervision will be conducted with representatives of the community members, stakeholders and FSA to review if the planned project is going well or not. So, community participation is ensured from the beginning and throughout. On the other hand, the project will establish complaint handling committee where members are nominated by the beneficiaries. Then committee members will be communicated either in person or through telephone whenever beneficiaries have any concern of complaint. The committee will discuss the matter with FSA and local authorities and solve the issue in a short period of time.FSA strictly observes gender issues in implementation of all its projects. The organization understands the beliefs and traditional practices in the field with respect to gender. FSA works on gender empowerment to address reduce burden on women and girls. Project supports by themselves contribute to a reduced vulnerability of women and girls because they are the ones in charge of taking care of the whole family by making food, collecting firewood, making houses, looking after children and livestock and so forth. Besides, women and men will be participated equally in the planning and implementation of this project and both women and men have increased meaningful access for the support being provided. Participants in “Integrated Lifesaving Response to Conflict Displaced People in Afar Region of Ethiopia” programming have increased awareness of and sensitivity to gender equality issues. And strategies, skills and tools from the implementer of this program and partners’ experiences in gender sensitive planning, implementation and monitoring will be transferred and shared with key stakeholders. FSA and volunteers of this programs and services will effectively meet the specific needs of women and men.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

FSA has operational presence in five zones and 22 woredas of Afar Region. FSA has also a code of conduct in place and Committed to core humanitarian Standards (CHS). All FSA staff, in Addis Ababa and field offices staffs at Aysaita, Semera, and Awash 7 have been trained in the code of conduct, and which is being repeated on a yearly basis. FSA code of conduct is complemented with specific policies, among them, Child Protection Policy, Sexual Violence Policy and Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) Policy. Complementarily, several ways of communication are in place, and complaints are received by the HR and Admin department in Addis Ababa in addition to the field office managers. Complaints in this project intervention can be either reported directly to the Complaints compliant handling committees of the project through the established complaints mechanism, which includes staff from FSA, community groups, and community representatives.FSA has different mechanisms in place aiming at ensuring community engagement throughout the whole project cycle. At the initial stage of implementation, FSA mobilizes the communities in order to disseminate information on the project as approved by the donor (objectives, activities, modalities, monitoring) and conducts further consultations in order to finalize the beneficiary selection criteria and modality, when possible, and set up the CRM on the base of the preferences of all people, men, women, children, adolescents, people with disabilities, people with special needs, elderly, so that they have access to easy, safe and confidential way of providing feedback and communicating complaints. FSA will orient beneficiaries on accountability measures, particularly on CRM. Namely, community members will be reassured on the fact that the mechanism is safe and confidential. Information is diffused through banners and pamphlets, complemented by oral dissemination during the mobilization phase and the project and monitoring activities along the implementation phase. When required by the vulnerability of the community members, FSA communicates with the people on a home basis. FSA will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for all projects to ensure that the target groups can provide direct feedback or pass on their concerns in a confidential manner to the organization. Beneficiaries will be informed during the community mobilization phase about their rights. Although the physical implementation of the CRM differs per area the principle of enabling feedback is the same.Specific members of staff are assigned to analyse the complaints and to assure a timely response to the beneficiaries and communities. Complaints are usually dealt with immediately after receiving them. The findings of “gaps, shortcomings and community feedback” is immediately shared with the project team and FSA management to ensure that action is taken to address the challenges identified. FSA will return to beneficiaries giving feedback or making a complaint with information, updates on investigations and whatever input results from the process.FSA is taking feedback of the beneficiaries seriously and if project adaptation is required, it will be done accordingly. However, given the beneficiary involvement in the project design from the beginning and the close contact between the target communities and FSA staff, it is expected that most concerns are dealt with directly. Staff dealing with CRM are trained to distinguish between sensitive and non-sensitive complaints. Sensitive complaints (e.g., sexual harassment by project staff or government employees) are only shared with senior management. Lessons are identified and incorporated into the project cycle on a systemic basis. To conclude with, feedback from the communities is not only taken from the CRM. Regular monitoring surveys, including post-distribution monitoring, provide information for the progressive cyclical adaptation of the project and its modalities.
