Project Summary

The country like most other countries in the world, is currently affected by the COVID-19 pandemic and as at 21 May 2020, 51 positive cases had been recorded and four deaths. This pandemic has worsened the situation in the country straining an already compromised health delivery system and also depleting households livelihoods. Considering that the outbreak affected the country at a time the country was reeling from the effects recurrent drought induced food insecurity, the nutritional status of children and PLW is now being threatened. Hence, this project seeks to reduce the risk to COVID-19 and deteriorating nutrition status in light of the double burden of COVID-19 pandemic and drought induced food insecurity.
Despite the risk communication efforts underway, there are still gaps especially in these rural communities on COVID-19 awareness, especially on nutritional issues in the context of this pandemic. The current estimates indicate an increasing SAM and MAM case load in the target areas, without significant lifesaving nutrition interventions, the nutrition situation will continue to deteriorate. In addition to food insecurity and macro-economic challenges, inadequate knowledge among mothers/caregivers on appropriate and healthy diets and poor IYCF practices compounds the situation. Poor hygiene practices prevalent in the communities also contribute to the increasing malnutrition rates. Micronutrient deficiencies for children under five years and PLW (iron) also exist in the target districts. With regard to health systems, there exist capacity gaps within MOHCC staff and community health workers to deliver high impact nutrition services to community members in the context of COVID-19 and the current food insecurity. In addition, there are also reporting and coordination gaps as some of the ordinary ways of doing business are no longer feasible, which lead to duplication, wastage of resources, slow responses.
To address these gaps the project seeks to enhance awareness on COVID-19 and IYCF-e in the context of COVID-19 through printed materials, sms, mobile media among other convenient and effective ways. To enhance reach the project will work with the community health workers (VHWs, Lead mothers), facilitating their capacity building and support with tools of the trade to enable them to reach out to the caregivers. MOHCC capacity building will also be pursued to address the capacity gaps in light of nutrition issues in the context of COVID-19, the training will complemented by support with job aids and mentorship visits. Distribution of essential nutrition commodities will be supported to ensure the health facilities do not record stock outs. While utilization of mobile and electronic platforms will be enhanced to improve reporting and coordination.
The project will ensure that affected populations are involved throughout the course of the project life, with community dialogues conducted and feedback mechanisms established. In addition, specific needs of the vulnerable groups in the communities will be considered e.g for people living with disabilities, PLWHIV, teen mothers, children, PLW. Gender dynamics have been considered from design and will be taken into consideration during implementation since these power dynamics have an impact on the decisions with regard to use of household resources and uptake of the recommended health services.
