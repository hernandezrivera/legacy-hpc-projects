Project Summary

As the Syria crisis moves into its twelfth year its impact continues to blight the present, and the future prospects of its population, with the impacts felt most negatively on the lives of vulnerable civilians across the country. In response to these needs, People in Need's (PIN's) project proposes to contribute towards saving and sustaining lives of targeted conflict-affected communities in Northern Syria through the provision of food assistance (multiple modalities). Responses between immediate needs and resilience- building will be adapted according to continuous assessments and analysis of the local contexts within a flexible and responsive framework.
Activities will be underpinned by emergency preparedness and response plans developed in coordination with relevant clusters, working groups, and partners – to promote timely, efficient and joined-up responses that seek to maximize geographic and sectoral coverage and minimize gaps. PIN will also focus upon assisting vulnerable, conflict-affected to meet basic food needs. Activities will target the most vulnerable households in communities chronically affected by the adverse impacts of the conflict, using common assistance modalities and selection criteria, per the recommendation of the working groups.
PIN will plan to reach beneficiaries with monthly food assistance (food baskets, food vouchers, MPCA), agriculture vouchers, and farmer field schools, and bakery support through reduced-price bread on a community level through providing contracted bakeries with 50% of flour and 50% of yeast to reduce the price of the bread price up to 40% in comparison with the free market bread price, while another activity targets only IDPs in camps free bread distribution where PIN provides 100% of flour and yeast needed and the baking cost to bakeries in order to distribute the bread for free to the targeted camps. Beneficiaries are targeted from both IDPs and host community members, meeting eligibility and vulnerability criteria to avoid creating tensions within and across communities. Selection criteria was elaborated in consultation with the affected communities, as well as based on evidence from previous project monitoring and community feedback obtained through the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and include low-income families from the following groups: Female-headed households (FHH); Child-headed HH; Elderly-headed HH; HH with disabled and chronically ill members; HH with high dependency ratio; Unemployed male and female youth; Poor farming HH. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance by employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet the distinct needs, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritise the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are coordinated with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border to hubs to district and village levels. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming; including consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at projects' planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2023, 15M people need food and agriculture assistance with 44% being children and 28% being people with disabilities. 12.1M Syrians are facing food insecurity and another 2.9M are at risk of becoming food insecure. This is an alarming increase of 1M in comparison to 2022, demonstrating a dire need for food assistance. Not only that the purchasing power continued to weaken following the COVID-19 pandemic which has contributed to the depreciation of the Syrian pound, but also problems with the supply chain and deliveries following Russian invasion of Ukraine worsened the food security of HHs in Syria. Additionally, the climate crisis is making it more challenging for farmers to produce agricultural goods to support the population. So far, PIN has met these needs through in-kind and cash-based modalities, better enabling populations across Northern Syria to meet their basic needs. Additionally, PIN continues to support bakeries across NWS with flour and yeast with the hope that they will be able to one day operate independently. PIN delivered over 5,000 MTs of food support, including bread across Northern Syria. Based on the needs listed in FSA HNO 2023, PIN will need a total of $29,000,000 for its food security project in 2023. PIN has already secured all $29,000,000 to cover these needs.

4 - Implementation of activities

PIN activities utilize in-kind and cash-based responses, focusing on food insecurity and negative coping strategies: 1. In-Kind Food Baskets, enable HHs to meet 70-78% of needs (1,575kcal/person/day). 2. Monthly Cash Value Food Vouchers to achieve the nutritional value as food baskets, projections require an expected value of approximately $60 monthly, equivalent 1,575kcal/person/day for HH of 6 members; 3. For Rapid Responses, provision of RTERs to IDPs; 4. Support Bakeries by distributing flour and yeast to bakeries monthly subsidized priced bread packs for local vendors linked to PIN-supported bakeries;5. Nutritional Top-Up given the general level of Monthly Food Assistance is below 100% of dietary requirements, PIN proposes $30 top-up via cash-based modality with the general food voucher. Improving Agricultural production by supporting small-scale farmers and micro-gardening for home consumption. The assistance to small-scale farmers is with Agricultural vouchers, to seed packages, tools, drip-irrigation, and water storage. The inputs' training is tailored to the targeted farmers allow them to purchase essential agricultural inputs (seeds, tools, fertilizers, water harvesting and storage equipment). For highly vulnerable HHs with access only small land areas, PIN will deliver inputs and training to engage in micro- gardening, prioritizing vegetable production to increase dietary diversity and reduce expenditure. Additionally, PIN provides one-off emergency MPCA.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN's protection mainstreaming strategy carries out protection risk assessments, focusing on cash and other assistance modalities, which feeds to the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)Risk: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: needs-based targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; advertisement in targeted public places; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs.;100% beneficiary verifications; FGDs with representatives from the community, communication channels for PIN’s CFRM, 2)R: intergroup tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization packages across partners, assistance to host communities and IDPs.3) R:PSEA 3)MM: Staff training; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection-related questions, Clear process to address PSEA cases, Signs are posted in project sites that assistance is free. 4)R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with security teams for timing and location, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered areas, with seating if possible; Different queues for men/women, home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility difficulties; Food rations are grouped into lighter boxes to facilitate easier transportation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA strategy: PIN aims for its country program capacity building in the area, ensuring staff in each hub is connected to the wider PSEA response/ strategy according to each context. PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. PSEA trainings are conducted to all new staff as part of the protection mainstreaming trainings. PSEA is also integral part of the Code of conducted signed with staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to other areas; and/or switch to remote management, through local partners. (A2/R2) staff, offices are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, temporarily suspends activities; investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention ;(A3/R3): LCs/others collaborate inline with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access WG) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted from transferring goods, PIN suspends activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift funds to Cash program.
