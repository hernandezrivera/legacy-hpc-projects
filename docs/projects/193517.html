Project Summary

This project will be a new phase of PHC’s Housing Rehabilitation Program (HRP) that will provide emergency shelter rehabilitation to 90 vulnerable families (about 468 Direct beneficiaries), 50 families in East Jerusalem (EJ), and 40 in Area C of Jerusalem Governorate whose shelter is unfit for weather conditions, does not meet the Minimum Shelter Standards, overcrowded, and unsafe with a total budget of ($847621). The overall objective of the project is to respond to the immediate shelter needs of vulnerable populations in EJ and Area C of the Jerusalem Governorate. Specifically: (1) Promote self-reliance, resilience, maintain, and support for the most vulnerable families with shelter-related solutions. (2) Enhance the safeguard, the protection, and reduce the likelihood of forced displacement. (3) Contribute to solving the problem of overcrowding shelters by creating new spaces through rearrangement. (4) Promote more environmentally friendly shelters, cost, and energy-efficient building practices. (5) Enhance the economic situation for vulnerable families so reducing the probability of renting and/or regular maintenance. (6) Enhance the shelter's weather conditions for vulnerable families. This project provides financial support in an average of $7,500 to shelters in EJ and an average of $7,000 to Area C of Jerusalem Governorate. This response will follow the PHC Results-based management strategy through a self-help support approach for the most vulnerable families to provide shelter repairs and weather solutions for shelters. PHC’s proactive approach to emergency preparedness is in place to ensure early action is taken when required. It includes risk monitoring, the establishment of coordination and management arrangements, and preparing for joint needs assessments along with the Palestinian official bodies CBOs, SC. PHC Program Committees track and monitor the project progress and identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries according to their shelter conditions and their socio-economic situation. PHC involves beneficiaries in the different stages of the project cycle to determine their needs, especially FHHs, the PWDs, and the elderly, to gather their feedback, complaints, and lessons learned to take suitable action accordingly. PHC provides beneficiaries with the tools to be protected from PSEA, GBV and/or human rights violations. PHC seeks to improve the living conditions of the targeted beneficiaries as a result of shelter rehabilitation services that will be provided through the project.
This project is in line with PHC Theory of Change & PHC’s strategic plan (2019-2023) through its 2nd strategic objective “Contributing to the development of the housing sector, to secure sustainable urban environment”, specifically: “Developing residential areas to secure sustainable urban development that ensures the provision of appropriate infrastructure and environment”. & “Increasing the availability of affordable, adequate, healthy and accessible houses for Palestinians, especially vulnerable & limited-income families”. Also, the project follows PHC HRP policies and procedures. This project will be built on the best practices and lessons learned from previous phases of the HRP. This project is in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan (SO2) and the HNO-OPT. This project is related to the shelter cluster priorities and objectives CO2 and CO3. PHC’s previous experience in the identified geographic area confirms that the HRP has no cultural concerns, is appropriate and is relevant to the needs of vulnerable families. PHC since the generation of HRP, gained trust and support to replicate several phases of the program. This project is directly relevant to SDG11 and indirectly relevant to the other SDGs. PHC integrated approach took into consideration the environment & energy efficiency as a major component during rehabilitation through awareness sessions, raising the beneficiaries capacities & inclusion of related items in the BOQs

1 - Needs

Palestinians in East Jerusalem (EJ) and Area C live in an increasingly coercive environment in which the Israeli government is attempting to control the demographics of the entire Jerusalem area to restrict the percentage of the Palestinian population since the unilateral illegal annexation of EJ in 1967. Since then, Palestinians have been utterly discriminated against in social services, planning, and development. The Israeli municipality has severely limited services provided to Palestinian areas, including the provision and maintenance of infrastructure, schools, and health services. Furthermore, issuing building permits for Palestinian residents of EJ is made severely expensive and difficult with lengthy and complex procedures that rarely result in obtaining a permit. In addition to escalated demolition orders in the recent year and political deportation. As a result, Palestinians are forced to build illegally or forced to be displaced outside EJ due to 1) Only 15% of the total EJ area being available for residential use to the Palestinians according to ‘B’tselem, 2017’. 2) The vast majority of East Jerusalem’s land is unregistered. 3) From 1991 to 2018, only 16.5% of the building permits were issued for construction in the Palestinian neighborhoods according to ‘Peace Now, 2019’. This had led residents to have a dual lifestyle; small, unsuitable, and shared dwellings usually used as an address inside EJ while the actual dwellings are located outside. In addition to the separation wall imposed social and economic isolation on EJ from other Palestinian areas, that led to protection risks which include; the risk of forced eviction and displacement, demolition of shelters, in addition to the risks associated with relocation or revocation of residency rights imposed by the Israeli authorities, increasing in settler violence, harassment, and settlement-related restrictions on movement and access to land.The HNO-OPt 2023 demonstrates that over 72,000 Palestinians are in need for shelter assistance and over 214 residential structures were demolished between Jan. and Sep. 2022. The lack of financial capacity for maintenance makes vulnerable families no longer able to maintain their shelters without having the opportunity to benefit from external financial support. The accumulated housing shortfall increased the demand and growing forced displacement from Jerusalem day by day, because of the enormous rise in the prices of residential lands, the scarcity of adequate building environment, the high rates of rented houses, and the social and psychological problems caused by the overcrowding in residential apartments and neighborhoods. As a result of the above-mentioned situation, The PHC is dedicated to helping to alleviate the problem of housing and focused on developing and implementing more phases of its programs to identify Palestinians’ needs in general and vulnerabilities needs in particular according to its strategic plan 2021 – 2023, and the Networking and Fundraising plan 2022 - 2024. PHC continuously receives applications from vulnerable families and conducts regular assessments by PHC projects team and as a joint work between PHC and the MoSD. In June 2022 PHC conducted an assessment in EJ, and updated the previous PHC MoSD assessment. PHC found that 220 families – around 30% of them are FHH and 52% of them are Females – are in emergency shelter rehabilitation in 2023, 180 of them in EJ and 40 in Area C of Jerusalem governorate.Through this project, PHC will be responsible to rehabilitate 90 shelters, 50 in EJ and 40 in Area C of Jerusalem Governorate. The assessments were divided into 1) technical assessment; insight of minimum shelter standards being completed 2) social assessment; insight of separate individual interviews with women, men, boys, and girls with different age groups.The assessment targeted families who are:1. Vulnerable Palestinians (poor, needy and marginal, divorced or widowed FHH, elders, and/or handicapped), 2. Vulnerable Palestinians suffering from chronic diseases or disabilities.3. Families living in poor shelters' physical conditions; especially shelters are unfit for weather conditions.4. Families live in highly dense and crowded shelters;5. Families living in shelters need main basic facilities. During the mentioned assessment, PHC found the followings: 1. Inadequate shelter conditions increase the likelihood of displacement from area C to areas A & B, and from Jerusalem to outside the city in particular for FHH families.2. Weak capacities for self-protection and limitation of the Palestinian Authorities in Area C.3. The nullity of issuance of building permits in Area C made keeping & maintaining the existing shelters in that area very significant, especially for vulnerable families.4. FHH often has less capacity to do the basic repair work themselves.5. Some HHs have family members with disabilities reducing the chances for the active members to seek employment and limiting their income mostly due to social welfare related to the disability.6. Some families lack the safety, health, and comfort requirements due to overcrowded shelters & poor living conditions that affect family members’ privacy and dignity & children’s welfare resulting in the intergenerational transmission of social inequality, increase in GBV & PSEA.7. Families invest all spaces in the shelter especially in the old city due to the limitation & cost of building permits, restrictions, and land confiscation.This project will target the most vulnerable families in the identified areas according to the conducted assessment. Through the selection criteria based on PHC grading system higher scores will go to divorced, widowed women or FHH, larger family members, PWDs, also to shelters lack the availability of basic facilities and lack safety and protection. Families shall provide ownership documents or approval from owners to rehabilitate their shelters to avoid future disputes

2 - Activities

The overall objective of the project is to respond to the immediate shelter needs of vulnerable populations in EJ and Area C of the Jerusalem Governorate. This response follows PHC's RBM through the self-help support approach. The project seeks to achieve the optimum results regarding beneficiaries by defining the aptness for support for emergency vulnerable families at a reasonable cost. PHC has its internal Program Committee (PC) (50 percent are females) that is tasked to set the eligibility criteria of applicants and track and monitor the project's progress. The PC ensures that all shelters will have at least the minimum shelter standards in their estimated BoQs and recommend the most vulnerable cases to the External Advisory Committee (AC). The AC (30 percent are females), consists of community representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and familiar with their needs and challenges. The AC supports the program implementation and participates in the selection of the beneficiaries according to shelter conditions and socioeconomic situation of families. To increase beneficiaries' coping capacity and contribute to building resilience, PHC involves all family members (especially FHH, PWDs, and the elderly) in all project stages starting from determining their needs and ending with measuring their satisfaction and the changes in their lives due to the project implementation. Workshops with beneficiaries are conducted to discuss the project components and to take into account any recommendations from the beneficiaries’ side in consideration in the project design and implementation, in addition to gather their feedback, complaints, and lessons learned to take suitable action accordingly. Workshops are also conducted to provide the beneficiaries with safety access to different channels and tools, i.e. hotline, e-mail, , satisfactory surveys, individual field visits. also the workshop aims to raise the beneficiary’s awareness concerning GBV, PSEA, housing legal status, and how to implement and use safe electrics in their shelters, all are supported with related materials on PSEA and safe complaint channels. The methodology for identifying the level of interventions for rehabilitation follows the policy of PHC’s HRP that is based on the current physical conditions of shelters, poverty level, and the income level of people living in. The interventions will include, based on identified needs, but are not limited to roof sandwich panels, ventilation, insulation, flooring, windows, internal walls, toilets, sinks and kitchens, and disabilities access where needed. This project will respond to the urgent shelter needs in EJ and Area C of Jerusalem Governorate and will offer an integrated approach to responding to the housing and resilience needs of the vulnerable residents.At the beginning of the project, PHC approves the PC & AC Committees and their mandates. A Project Inception meeting is followed with the project team to take ownership of the project’s goals and objectives. The project team visit the potential beneficiaries and conducts a thorough social and technical assessment to ensure their vulnerability for immediate response. The Project Team discuss and gather their needs across their gender and age levels and prepare the required reports along with a BoQ and cost estimate for each potential shelter. This assessment and the related documents from line agencies are treated through the approved selection criteria of the PHC, which depend on local committees and the PHC Grading System when needed. The PC studies, recommends and raises the most vulnerable cases to the AC for their final selection. The AC studies, reviews and lists the end eligible cases.The Project team prepare the technical documents (BoQs and specifications), the self-help agreements and all the required annexes, then, PHC sign the agreements with the eligible beneficiaries. The grant amounts approved by the AC are included in the agreements along with the time frame needed for having decent homes for all direct beneficiaries. At the time of signing the agreements, the project team clarifies the action process, the responsibilities, and rights, and provides beneficiaries with all financial regulations and procedures. The Project team also conducts bilateral meetings with beneficiaries to prioritize the works that will be done in each shelter. A down payment that represents around 25% of the grant amount is disbursed to beneficiaries with a commencement order. The other payments are paid according to their progress in the rehabilitation of their shelters. PHC team then monitor the work regularly, to ensure full technical support and oversight for PWDs and widowed, separated, or FHHs. And to ensure that the work is being implemented as scheduled and according to the required quality and specifications, this will be reflected by technical consultation documentation, photos, and reports. The approval of the final payment is conditional on the full implementation of the shelter rehabilitation. The workshops for beneficiaries will be held in order to: (1) clarify the project methodology, guidelines, PSEA, feedback and complaint mechanism, rights, and obligations, reduce the risk of GBV. (2) train beneficiaries on technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy-efficient and environmentally friendly living environments. (3) encourage beneficiaries to get voluntary work from the local community to increase the positive impact of the project. (4) respond to all the beneficiaries' concerns and inquires. (5) encourage project beneficiaries to share feedback on the provided assistance, potential encountered issues, and/or remaining needs to be addressed. (6) support to access to legal aid through establishing a proper linkage between them and the ICLA, or PHC legal advisor. (7) raise the beneficiaries awareness in safety & energy efficient practices.

3 - Indicators

The project monitoring is a continuous and consistent process occurring throughout the project’s life span, led by the PHC project manager and PHC’s project technical staff and field coordinators. The project’s Monitoring and Evaluation Framework identifies performance measuring indicators and their associated baselines, milestones, and targets (BMTs) for the results (outcomes and outputs). PHC monitoring process ensures that the project implementation is conducted in a manner that ensures the delivery of the program outputs. This is achieved through continuous tracking of indicators as well as baseline and end-line surveys to measure progress. The monitoring tools and systems are designed to focus on both upward accountability to donors, and downward accountability to beneficiaries, whereby targeted beneficiaries are directly engaged in the monitoring and evaluation of the program. Monitoring tools are designed in accordance with the data source identified in the M&E Framework and include, but are not limited to: field visits, direct observations, documents review, individual meetings, group meetings, monitoring of the progress made in delivering the outputs, as the tool for results monitoring who documents review, individual meeting, group meetings, focus group sessions, and surveys. PHC staff use data from monitoring to take corrective action when needed and keep records of the implementation of monitoring activities that inform PHC to check the validity of the theory of change through measuring outcome level indicators. PHC further monitor the implementation process in terms of the applicable standards and procedures; monitor changes in context and how this could increase the risk associated with contextual changes, and monitor the financial status of the project. Additional monitoring will be undertaken by PHC’s communication and advocacy officer, who reports to the project manager. PHC project manager and staff are responsible for the quality of all outputs. A final evaluation for projects are conducted to assess the efficiency, effectiveness, relevancy, and sustainability of the project. The evaluation also assesses the extent to which impact could be delivered. Lessons learned from the evaluation will be utilized to enhance the overall programming at PHC and recommendations for replications. The indicators of the project will be:1. # of individuals protected and have improved access to adequate shelter. 2. # of PWDs have improved access to shelter3.# of people exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported with adequate shelter assistance4. # of individuals living in substandard shelters, overcrowded conditions supported with Shelter assistance to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities.5. # of legal aid sessions conducted6. # of awareness raising sessions conducted.7. % of households who report satisfaction with the shelter intervention. Previous phases of the program showed that small grants make a significant difference in the lives of vulnerable families at several levels: At the community level, PHC formed the Advisory Committee, which consists of community female and male representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and familiar with their needs and challenges; to support the program implementation and participate in the selection of the beneficiaries according to housing conditions and socio-economic situation of families. PHC involves beneficiaries in the different stages of the project cycle. One of the assessment criteria is to involve the families in determining their needs according to their gender and age (e.g., vulnerable FHH’s, PWDs, the elderly…etc). At the monitoring and evaluation level, PHC will monitor the progress of works on a daily basis. The monitoring process will be based on the above-mentioned procedures. The PC will share feedback and information concerning challenges and issues raised on-site to partners. PHC always aims to provide assistance in ways that are supportive of recovery and long-term development, and do its best to “do no harm” by concealing them from the additional risk Evaluation process will be based on a questionnaire, financial audit, meetings, and quality assurance process. It is worth to note that the questionnaire is a tool designed and used in the recent phases of the program to measure the satisfaction and to indicate the impact on the living conditions of the most vulnerable families, the degree of safety and adequacy, quality of work, and the progress achieved. And in order to assess the changes that were attributed to the project intervention, as well as to examine whether targets have been achieved. Knowing that one of the Project committee mandates is to implement and monitor the project to be gender age-balanced and to be offered equal opportunities. Through the evaluation process, the changes occurred in terms of safety, dignity, and integrity will be measured separately for women, men, girls, and boys where according to this project 468 are direct beneficiaries; 93 Females, 62 males, 131 boys, 155 girls, and 27 elderlies. 32 out of the 468 beneficiaries are PWDs. PHC pays special attention to the environmental issues; neutral impact on the environment with mitigation or enhancement, to achieve this PHC improved technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy-efficient and environmentally friendly living environments. PHC will take into consideration during the implementation of the project measures to promote more environmentally friendly housing, cost, and energy-efficient building practices.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PHC in coordination with the community will prioritize intervention to provide the most appropriate assistance to beneficiaries without discrimination or exposing them to additional risk, in addition, the project team have sufficient experience to be aware of protection concerns identified during rehabilitation activities in a safe and confidential manner Thus PHC will pay attention to the following issues:In terms of safety, dignity, and do-no-harm:1. Provide efficient lighting throughout the site by visiting the site regularly.2. Respect minimum space standards in shelters to minimize risks of exploitation and abuse by visiting the site regularly.3. Ensure accessibility to disables and elderly by preparing appropriate BOQ.4. Include partitions and door locks to better protect women and girls, particularly FHH’s by preparing appropriate BOQ.In terms of meaningful access:1. Prioritize people and groups based on their needs by applying the PHC accountability framework.2. Ensure that the housing unit is accessible and appropriate to all groups and individuals, in particular, people with physical or mental disabilities, elderly and women by preparing appropriate BOQ.3. Ensure that bathrooms are accessible to persons with disabilities and elderly, and safe for small children by preparing appropriate BOQ.In terms of accountability, participation, and empowerment:1. Maximizing transparency and setup accessible feedback and complaint mechanism by applying the PHC accountability framework.2. Ensure that the local community is involved in project planning for convenient implementation by conducting workshops.3. Provide technical and construction assistance by conducting workshops and by visiting the site regularly.4. Reinforce the community’s self-help capacity: encourage traditional construction methods, particularly in areas where transportation of materials is restricted. Women will have the opportunity to equally participate in the process; equip beneficiaries with knowledge of safe construction practices by conducting workshops for beneficiaries.5. Monitor the implementation process to avoid exploitative and fraudulent practices by visiting the site regularly and according to PHC M&E procedures as stated in the indicator section.6. Involve persons with disabilities and the elderly in all project cycle in order to have accurate information about their specific needs and constraints by applying PHC accountability framework and by conducting workshops with beneficiaries.7. Avoid and monitor any exploitative labor especially child labor on construction sites by visiting the site regularly.8. Conduct regular discussions with individuals of different ages, gender, particularly those with specific needs on housing unit issues, to ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and resolved by applying PHC accountability framework and by conducting workshops with beneficiaries.
