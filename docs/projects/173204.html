Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh has Planned for Site development & improvement Program for 2021 with the targeted 12,465 families (57,221 individuals) in their successfully operating existing Camp-4(2110 HHs),Camp-4 Extension(509 HHs),Camp-5(1647 HHs),Camp-13(5275 HHs),Camp-19(2924 HHs), and host community under Rajapalong union, Palongkhali union, Holdiapalong union & Jaliapalong union considering most vulnerable location of this unions, due to lack of solar street light and poor road communication. Accountability to affected people(AAP) principles, Covid-19 safety measures, protection guidelines will be followed throughout the project. Caritas aims to provide a wide range of interventions using the community-led approach for both the Rohingya refugees and host community, which will ensure protection mainstreaming across the activities through close collaboration with community engagement including women, men, youth, elderly peoples & peoples with disability (PWD) promoting community-led decision making, GIS risk mapping with assessment, Monitoring, implementation for sustained results and finally community will take the responsibility. Caritas will engage daily laborers prioritizing women/PWD with very light work under CFW. Project will ensure Coordination with Government authorities, RRRC, Camp-in-Charge(CIC),UNO,SMSD Sector coordinator, Site management agencies. Project will include small scale bamboo infrastructures such as bamboo bridge, retention wall with drum sheet, Stepping with Geo-Text Bags including handrails, drainage, slope protection/stabilization etc. in the new sites. In the old site of camp/host community, Caritas will Construct/repair of sustainable Low cost brick/masonry infrastructures of different sizes Brick pathways, vehicular road, drainages, stepping, low height retention walls etc, including replacement of damaged bamboo infrastructures. CB will also focus on aforementioned activities to ensure safe access to CIC offices, health centers, distribution centers, law enforcement, WFS, CFS, learning centers etc. Caritas will also provide Capacity building orientation to the community on Community led approach, DRR & repair / Maintenance of the aforementioned site development/DRR activities to strengthen their capacity and enhance feelings of the ownership. CB will ensure pre-post monsoon site development/DRR activities including Installation/maintenance of solar streetlights to enhance safety for women’s, adolescents and children’s. CB believes, above Community-led intervention with sustainable infrastructure will improve social cohesion and strengthen household resilience.

1 - Identified Needs

Small-scale weather events cause recurrent damage to shelter and site development activities resulting in an almost constant need for repairs due to the limited use of durable materials in the camps. The main materials used – untreated bamboo are not long-lasting. Households located in a landslide-prone area are at additional risk of losing their shelters due to heavy rain, and strong wind. Between June and August, weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure, and flooding partially damaged 23,023 Rohingya shelters and totally damaged 1,854 (NPM, 08/2020).This is a more than 100% increase in damages compared to the same period last year. This is likely due to the combination of monsoon weather conditions, light-weight temporary shelter/site development materials, the reduction of shelter and site development programming and monsoon preparedness activities due to COVID-19 containment measures (Shelter and Infrastructure Damage Report 08/2020). It is observed bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation, use of untreated bamboo which requires to be upgraded. Safe access to the key community facilities like CIC offices, health centers, distribution centers, law enforcement, WFS, CFS, learning centers could not be repaired due to COVID-19 restrictions and non-essential activities in the camps. In J-MSNA for Camp infrastructure 46% HH reported infrastructure got worse,22% has no change, and 32% has improved. Targeted camps area are densely populated with limited compliance to plot standards or good site planning and service provision in the past. settlements are largely in hilly typography with exposure to risks such as landslide, cyclone, flooding, erosion and in some location’s elephant attacks. The humanitarian situation in the camps is further exacerbated by heavy rainfall during monsoon and COVID19 which complicates safe access to the most important facilities within the camps and due to lack of sustainable site development & improvement activities. Concern of protection have a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees. There are gaps regarding protection mainstreaming across activities, including collaboration to promote community-led decision making, where equity issues among men, Women, adolescent girls, boys, elderly peoples, peoples with disability are to be addressed. Lack of adequate light in the camps and the host community is one of the reasons adult women & adolescent girls are uncomfortable going out at night to use latrines. Some cash-for-work activities are available to men,but women and vulnerable groups have fewer options due to cultural restrictions of working outside their home. Especially in the Kutupalong-Balukhali Expansion Site,serious protection risks due to congestion and the terrain (steep slopes and flood-prone low-lying areas) make it challenging to improve access, mitigate environmental risks, and install essential infrastructure for basic service provision. After rains the soil becomes slippery which reduces mobility and access. Deforestation exacerbates the risk of landslides and flooding which occurs during monsoon season.Major weather events including heavy rain, flooding, and wind cause widespread destruction across all locations. Solid waste management is a issue and drainages often get blocked with solid waste. There are pockets in camps without adequate drainage systems.Wastewater from shelters,bathing facilities and washing sites get stagnated around the settlement causing health risks. Sustainable site development & improvement intervention like slope protection and stabilization, dredging of waterways, construction of foot bridges, pathways, drainage with discharge points, retention walls, stair steps are the key site improvement elements, which need to be in place to facilitate safe access inside the camps to improve their living condition with safety & dignity.
