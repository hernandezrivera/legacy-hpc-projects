Project Summary

This project will complement the nutrition cluster response objectives through implementation of emergency nutrition program mainly using four main intervention strategies: 1. Increase accessibility and Provision of quality nutrition treatment services, 2. Capacity building of the response team including CHD nutrition service providers. 3. Promote Protect and support of safe and appropriate feeding for infants and young children affected by the crises or emergency
4. Increased access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH, FSL and protection interventions/responses in all nine counties (Akobo, Bor, Nyirol, Magwi, Torit, Lopa Lafon, KN, KE and Abyei) in 96 Payams across the nine counties (the list of Payams is attached herein). The overall objectives of the project is to contributes to reduction of morbidity and mortality of under five children and PLW due to acute malnutrition in former Jonglei, EES and Warrap state through provision of timely and quality management of acute malnutrition, promotion of maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) and integration of nutrition response with different sectors that includes WASH, Health FSL and protection.
The project will reach a total of 55,526 (12,404 U-5 SAM, 27,303 U-5 MAM, 15,819 MAM PLW) acute malnourished beneficiaries through management of acute malnutrition and MIYCN interventions which includes hygiene and sanitation promotion .The response will be focusing to achieve the following three results R.1. Children and PLW screed end detected and referred to appropriate nutrition centres. This will be done through active case finding using CNV/HHP, provision of acute managements of nutrition service trough 96 OTP and 94 TSFP static sites supplemented by RRM and mobile team to the area where service is not accessible.

Needs

Though the current IPC 2018 indicates that there is slight improvement in prevalence of acute malnutrition in the country, still malnutrition remain public health problem in South Sudan. According to Nutrition cluster estimation in 2019 936,581(477,656 Girls and 458,925 boys) children and 596,944 PLW with acute malnutrition will be need of nutrition on assistance in the country. According to SMART survey conducted done in all state most of counties nutrition situation remain at critical status with GAM rate of > 15%. Save the Children conducted SMART Surveys in various counties with the SAM and GAM rates as follows; (KN – SAM of 4.50% and GAM rate of 17.3%, Lopa Lafon (SAM of 1.9% and GAM of 9.7%), Bor South (SAM rate of 2.2% and GAM of 16.1%), Akobo West (SAM rate of 4.80% and GAM of 18.8%), Nyirol (SAM rate for 6.6% and GAM of 25.7%) and Abyei (SAM of 3.6% and GAM rate of 17.7%). SCF conducted a survey in Kapoeta East June 2018 which recorded a SAM rate of 1.8% and GAM of 10.4%. IMC also conducted SMART Survey in Akobo East with statistics of 2.5 SAM and 16.1 GAM. According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS), Magwi County is at relatively stable at 05 SAM and 4.10% GAM. These surveys were conducted between July 2017 and August 2018. (GAM matrix attached). Multiclausal aggravating factors of malnutrition remain high in all counties. Proper Care is fundamental for child growth, behaviour, and practices of caregivers (mothers, siblings, fathers, and childcare providers) who provide the food, health care, psychosocial stimulation and emotional support necessary for the healthy growth and development of children. In South Sudan, most of the care practice are sub optimal and have considerable contribution to high prevalence of acute and chronic malnutrition. Of the major care, practice in the counties, sub optimal Exclusive Breast Feeding (EBF) and Complementary Feeding (CF), poor hygiene practice, poor maternal nutrition, poor psychosocial support and poor home health practice are the most common among others.Malnutrition in South Sudan has been chronically high as a result of unsustainable livelihoods, limited health care services and limited social support mechanism for maternal and child care among others which is highly attributed to years in conflict. The issues of household food and nutrition security has not only affected particular locations but has extended to other areas that are said to be stable i.e Magwi county. Recent assessments conducted in the country by the humanitarian organizations highlighted the fact that the nutrition situation in many areas is at critical and emergency level requiring rapid intervention to treat existing cases and prevent more cases from becoming acutely malnourished. At the same time, addressing both immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition is very crucial in reducing the likelihoods of child mortality and morbidity rates due to malnutrition. The causes of malnutrition are directly linked with inappropriate care practices, substandard levels or access to health services, water supply, hygiene and sanitation, inadequate health education and a poor understanding of the importance of food quality, quantity and diversity.Unavailability of latrine, poor hand washing practice among community and inaccessibility of potable water and less practice of treating water was among the aggravating factors in the area. Though data are not available, the prevalence of micronutrients deficiency and especially iron, iodine and vitamin A are the most common problem however, effort and commitments to address these problem among stakeholders is not sufficient. As a result, the stunting rate in South Sudan remain high with 31% prevalence and it need immediate response that focus on both nutrition sensitive and specific intervention that enable to break the inter generation cycle of malnutrition in the country.

Activities

To achieve the intended objective the following major activities will be implemented in the implementation period.Establish strong community mobilization screening and referral mechanism: Community outreach activities the backbone of OTP/SC and TSFP. Strong community mobilization, screening and referral of detected children and PLW will be established using existing community structure mainly primary health care and Boma Health Initiative approaches. Hence community participation. HHP, MtMSG and community nutrition volunteers will be highly engaged in the mobilization screening of acute malnutrition that ensure early detection, prevention and referral of identified cases. CHD and BHI committee will be involve in selecting and coordinating of HHP Strengthen static OTP/TSFP sites : To ensure the accessibility, timely provision of appropriate nutrition therapeutic care and quality nutrition service 35 OTP/TSFP sites in all four county will be supported and strengthened Capacity building and nutrition surveillance: The primary focus of this project will be in building the capacity of the MOH, Sate MOH and county health department as well as SCI emergency nutrition team. Training on CMAM components, nutrition in Emergency and developments, and MIYCN will be the major topics to be provided for emergency nutrition team to be included in this project (both MOH and SCI)Promotion of Optimal MIYC : This activity will also focuses on supporting optimal MIYCF as this provides the greatest protection for infants and young children integrated with CMAM program offers an opportunity for identification of inappropriate feeding practices that may have contributed to the development of malnutrition, correction of those practices during the course of treatment, and follow-up post-discharge to prevent relapse and also most of baby friendly space are attached to OTP/TSFP sites. Complementary feeding demonstration will be also done. To ensure community participation and engagement most importantly for social behavioral change activities community approaches will be used by establishing of at least five Mother support group per each OTP/TSFP sites. Cultural appropriate IEC materials including counseling cars, posters, will be utilized for behavioral communication messages dissemination.Establish and strengthen integrated response: integrated response will be strengthened in each county, referral linkage and prioritization of target beneficiaries will be done with multi sectoral response, and addressing the underline cause of malnutrition will be prioritized and emphasized. Nutrition workers (HHP/CNVs) will be trained on early childhood education.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Save the Children South Sudan has a robust accountability system that enables beneficiaries to provide continuous feedback in a safe and accessible way and where the feedback is acted upon to improve the quality of our programming. Beneficiaries will be provided with information during project start up, including joint planning, and monitoring. The key information to be provided are project objective, source of fund (donor), type of activities proposed, duration of support, targeting and beneficiaries selection procedures, their entitlements, roles and responsibilities of stakeholders and procedures about compliant feedback mechanisms.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Save the Children will conduct SMART Survey to understand the context and analysis of socio-cultural issues of the target communities. Based on findings, key messages will be provided to beneficiaries considering the context and also specific information that take into account the age, gender, sex and disability status. Proposed activities in this submission are context specific and tailored to the needs of men, women, boys and girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Save the children enforces Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by adhering to four commitments in order to ensure the safety and well-being of adults and children. Save the Children prevents and responds to PSEA by implementing and enforcing our child safeguarding and PSEA policy of zero tolerance; assigning focal point for reporting and training in each field office and location we operate in that feeds to the national system for further investigation and action; raising awareness and engaging communities we work with; conducting risk assessment of our activities; ensuring safe reporting mechanisms are in place and responding to internal and external reports to SEA through different internal and external steps and channels. The four commitments, awareness, prevention, reporting and response, stipulates that:• Awareness: All staff, partners, representatives, volunteers are aware of issues of child abuse and sexual exploitation and the risks to both adult and children. Children and families (beneficiaries) are aware of standards of behavior they can expect from SC staff and representatives.• Prevention: We will ensure that staff partners, representatives, and volunteers minimize the risks to children. We will create an environment where children’s rights are respected and child abuse and sexual exploitation of both adults and children is prevented. • Reporting: We will ensure that staff partners, representatives, and volunteers are clearer as to what steps to take when concerns arise regarding the abuse or exploitation of adults and children. • Response: We will ensure that action is taken to support, safeguard and protect adults and children where concerns arise regarding their possible abuse and exploitation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SC has will engage beneficiaries to review performance of its program and get feedback on the areas of improvement. SC will also used program MEAL information from its current program to help design this project. National and district stakeholders will be consulted through cluster meetings and individual consultations to help shape the design. SC will be conducting consultative participation sessions through focus group discussions that will be seeking the views of children, adults, elderly and women in order to increase our knowledge and understanding of their lives and the issues affecting them. The consultations will be part of the information that will go into monitoring and evaluation system of the program. In addition, community feedback and review meeting will be conducted on a quarterly basis to discuss the program progress and get feedback from the communities on how the progress can sustain or improve its performance. The country office MEAL also generates various dashboards including complaint and feedback dashboard, quality benchmark monitoring dashboard, project management dashboard and indicator analysis on monthly and quarterly basis so that program implementing staff will get information for informed decision and improve programming for the beneficiaries and meet project objectives.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Multiple channels for providing feedback such as the toll-free hotline (subject to service availability for those locations), suggestion boxes, appeal committees, help desks will be used at all project meetings and distribution sites. Save the Children will also conduct focus group discussions, sampled feedback collection, onsite and post distribution monitoring surveys with different groups; men, women, children and elderly to get their feedback on the program performance. The first post-distribution survey will also collect information from non-beneficiaries to get information on their perception on the fairness of targeting process. The feedback will be compiled in a database developed and maintained by the MEAL team which alerts and escalate for every outstanding action on the feedback provided. All the feedback will be collected and analysed by our MEAL staff for monthly analysis and feed into program review meetings to be discussed with the program staff. For issues that are related to service quality (rather than project design), an “action tracker” maintained by MEAL will be utilized for deciding actions and tracking progress in implementing the required actions. From our MEAL database, we have observed that beneficiaries are more likely to provide feedback through focus group discussion and feedback collection sample surveys.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will pay special attention to persons with disabilities, PLWHA, elderly, PLW, those with chronic illnesses, and children under 5 while measuring access by use of MUAC, weight for height z-score and capturing these in the SMART surveys, programme reports via our monitoring, evaluation, accountability and Learning system. The project will employ standard monitoring tools that track beneficiaries reach through different interventions in a regular basis disaggregated by sex and age. Consultation with the beneficiaries will be conducted during post distributions and post harvest assessments. As part of the targeting and beneficiary selection process, communities will be consulted through community meetings to ensure that the families most affected by chronic food insecurity are included in the program.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Field security survey will be conducted prior to the start of the implementation period. The findings will be used to inform implementation of the project making sure that beneficiaries and staff are working in areas ensuring their safety. For instance, during distributions, community watch volunteers will maintain order and provide added protection for the beneficiaries. Safety and Security of beneficiaries will be guaranteed by bringing services closer to the people to minimize exposure to long and risky treks.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure that beneficiaries are served with respect and without any form of discrimination.
