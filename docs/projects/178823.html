Project Summary

Emergency food and cash distributions are tailored for immediate support for the most vulnerable IDPs, their host communities, returnees, and their communities of return, including in hard-to-reach areas, in line with SPHERE and Core Humanitarian Standards. Based on market size and structure, the modality used will be either cash, vouchers, or in-kind.
RFs' team will identify needs, gaps, challenges, and opportunities and provide immediate support through cash transfers. Cash is instrumental in supporting beneficiaries to access a variety of basic goods and services while it also strengthens local economies. beneficiaries will use the money to access basic goods from the local markets, simultaneously the cash works as a stimulus for boosting market economic activities. Oxfam will also consider introducing vouchers to provide food, it contributes to the fragile economy especially if there is a risk of inflation when the markets are recovering from shocks. Vouchers are also a useful modality where there are no banks or financial service providers.
Emergency life-sustaining food assistance, As may be necessary, the team will carry out emergency responses for crisis-affected vulnerable communities consisting of short-term food assistance. As a first-line response in an emergency, and in cases of non-functional markets, especially in the first three months of a disaster, RFs' will distribute food rations for 3 days, one week, one month, or three months, based on how acute and/or chronic the conditions are. Should RFs' observe cases of malnutrition they will make referrals to appropriate partners and agencies
RFs’ will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate in its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle. RF’s staff, volunteers, and implementing partners are trained in safe programming and risk assessments. This includes the identification of the risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation, and management measures: this is then implemented and reviewed regularly. Particular attention will be paid to addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse and aid diversion, and a conflict sensitivity approach will be adopted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

AL Resala Foundation (RF) is committed to mainstreaming protection into all of the projects and does so through the use of several mechanisms. All activities in this project will inherently mainstream protection principles, prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and ensuring their well-being especially in times of forced displacement and hardship. All proposed facilities will also respect the principles of neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries without discrimination.These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff concerning the protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation, and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. The collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries. Furthermore, the RF policy of documentation, including photography strongly considers protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity. Concerning this particular project, the activities involving item supplies will place priority for beneficiaries that are members of vulnerable groups, namely single-income households (widowers or divorced persons) and families with individuals with special needs or disabled persons, in addition to large families (over 6 members), or impoverished or unemployed families.The IDP population consists of a significantly higher number of women and children (24% women, 60% children, 16% men) than the average population, indicating a high number of female and child-headed households.
