Project Summary

This project aims to provide emergency assistance to recently displaced populations by providing Multi-Purpose Cash Grants (MPGs) to the most vulnerable IDP families which displaced within the last two months due to the continuous dispute and conflict in their areas, which caused big displacement influxes towards western and norther Idleb and northern of Aleppo.
The project is considered as a rapid response for those affected IDPs to help them cope with the harsh conditions and secure their needs in a dignified way, by distributing one-times cash grant with a total value of 120 USD per family (aligned with SMEB value). The project and the grant amount were designed to allow the beneficiaries to cover their basic needs of food and NFI, since this cash modality will give them flexibility in choosing items they need and to give the project an ability to meet all changes in beneficiary needs.
Through the proposed project, GAN seek to target 4500 newly displaced families toward Idleb governorate due to the last escalation in northern Syria. The beneficiaries will be selected in close coordination with the local councils and civil authorities with 100 % verification from MEAL team in the targeted areas based on the vulnerability and eligibility criteria including displacement duration criteria since the newly IDPs are the most vulnerable groups to the shocks. Also, the project will prioritize the women-headed households as well as the households with children, disabilities, pregnant, and lactating women.
GAN will benefit from exchanging of experience through sharing the lessons learned and the solutions to overcome the challenges with SFNI cluster and CWG.
GAN could cover as much as can of the geographical areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, such as Afrin, Harim districts that have overcrowded IDPs to reach 1200 vulnerable and newly IDP in urban, rural or even camps. Since GAN, through its ‘on the ground’ operation team and its prior experience in Idleb governorate will be able to access the locations and markets during the project implementation.
Due to the lack of the banking system in northern Syria, GAN will identify the money transfer agents through conducting a competitive process after assessing the markets and the vendors' capacities in the local markets. where the identified vendors will be responsible for delivering the MPG's amount to the beneficiaries in the different locations.
prior to the MPG distribution, a rapid market assessment in terms of availability of the food items, prices, and the accessibility will be conducted to make sure the local markets are functioning and to ensure all beneficiaries have a good and equal accessibility to the different goods and services.

4 - Needs

4M people live in northwest Syria, Women children are estimated to make up for the 76% of this population, humanitarian situation across northwest Syria, continue to deteriorate at an alarming rate due to ongoing violence, which has a devastating effect on the 4M people in NW Syria.From 1 Dec19 to 2 Feb20, some 586,000 people fled from their homes in northwest Syria as a result of ongoing hostilities. Many of them have been displaced many times throughout the Syria crisis, eroding the resilience of families and communities, in the NW, there are critical levels of overcrowding putting significant pressure on overburdened communities.Shelter situation has continued to deteriorate due to the cumulative impact of the crisis. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services, the need for humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining core and seasonal assistance remains crucial as households are still unable to meet basic needs, particularly during harsh weather.A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 90% of an unskilled laborer’s average monthly salary and between 50-80% of the average monthly salary.MSNA data also shows that female-headed households, youth, elderly and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services including shelter, as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs, more than three-quarters of those paying rent are unable to afford it.Families report that they are in need of fuel, clothing, and power/heating sources. Over 60 %of people state a preference for receiving assistance in the form of cash or vouchers rather than in-kind as they can select specific items needed for their families.According to CCCM data, all newly displaced people are in need of non-food items while 79% needs shelter assistance and 57 percent needs food and cash assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

- beneficiaries selection:Selection criteria will be finalized in coordination with local stakeholders, and communicated to local councils and community members. Potential beneficiaries will then be identified through specific outreach and referrals from local organizations and the relief coordination administration. The field teams will be primarily responsible for the selection of beneficiaries: a scoring system will be used to prioritize based on weighting assigned to the selection criteria. Based on past experience, GAN will develop an appropriate mechanism to verify from the registered households in close coordination with the relief administration and the local councils.- Distribution of multi-purpose grants to vulnerable affected beneficiaries:e-voucher card will be distributed for beneficiaries and WhatsApp channels will be created for each group of beneficiaries to communicate with on time, distribution approaches will be selected with local councils, a dedicated female staff will be maintained to ensure female access and protection. At distribution, each beneficiary will be required to present his/her ID form. Cash vouchers will be designed and distributed before. Accompanied with the distribution process, MEAL team will be existed to observe the process and raised the beneficiary awareness to the complaint mechanism.- Post-distribution monitoring to monitor receipt, use and impact of assistance providedFollowing every distribution round, GAN and SR monitoring team will conduct a PDM and a price monitoring to gauge beneficiary feedback in relation to timeliness of provision of assistance, quality and quantity of assistance provided, the distribution process, behavior of the distribution staff, perceptions on the targeting process (and knowledge of criteria), and the use of the cash provided (e.g. used, kept, shared.. etc.) as well as to keep an eye on the market any price inflation to adapt with better timeline and mechanism of intervention.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members:Likelihood: high, Impact: medium.Mitigating Measure: - Use the recommended minimum standards of cash amount. 2-Tension between host communities, IDPs and returneesLikelihood: high, Impact: high.1.Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2.Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make efforts to target % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support IDPs.4. Establish feedback mechanisms and conduct follow-up visits if needs be.5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 3- physical harm at distribution points (security, safety): Likelihood: medium, Impact: high.- Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or canceled.-Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding-Training to staff on establishing and managing distributions and crowd management.-Application of checklist at distribution sites.-Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk. 4-corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection:Likelihood: medium, Impact: high.1. Strengthen over-sight mechanism. 2. ensure that their staffs have read/areaware of the Code of Conduct.3. Outreach to affected population.4. Presence and information of complaints mechanism. 5: Sharing personal data of HC, IDPs or other affected individuals or households with third parties, potentially putting them at risk of violence, detainment or discrimination:Likelihood: high, Impact: medium.Mitigating Measure:-Data protection policy dissemination and adherence to data protection principles.-Contracts with service providers include provisions in line with data protection policy.-Beneficiary consent forms.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- Markets are functioning properly in the targeted areas.- The money transfer agent / Hawala services are functioning.- Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries Risk:1- risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high.Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allow the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, GAN would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are met. 2- Risk: Money transfer agent / Hawala services are no longer available:Mitigation strategy: Proactive identification of alternative cash transfer agents, Investigate further the possibilities of vouchers systems as an alternative. 3- Risks3: Closure of the borders between Turkey and Syria:Mitigation3: As a registered NGO in Turkey, GAN is expecting that this legal framework will give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, GAN will adapt its modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure continued assistance. 4- Inflation – price increases for staple items due to lack of supply to meet demand (as cash transfers increase purchasing power and demand), causing harm to all affected people and other community members who use the market, the mitigation measures:- An estimate of potential above-average inflation through market analysis, and compare with normal price fluctuations, seasonal shifts, and other existing data.- Market analysis, participation of local communities, the participation of IDPs and host communities- Monitoring for a better understanding of market reactions and to quickly mitigate issues.
