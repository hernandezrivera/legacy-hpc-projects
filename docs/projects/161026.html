Project Summary

violence and other abuses have continued largely unabated since the conflict began, spreading to areas previously considered less affected. Tens of thousands have been killed or injured, many hundreds of thousands have lost their houses, property, and livelihoods. people are exposed to severe human rights violations and there is a lack of accountability and absence of a functioning justice system. more people sought safety in PoC sites, spontaneous settlements, and in neighboring countries. but many more people are living in sites offering little personal protection or basic services. The conflict affected to freedom of movement whether people have fled their homes of find themselves entrapped in displacement sites due to fear, their right to move freely is impacted. as the conflict continues, the range of threats continues to multiply: food insecurity and the breakdown of family linkage and social structures leads to frustration and, at worst, trauma. Displacement separated families and caused unaccompanied children. Many children are at risk of recruitment in armed forces and groups, or other forms of exploitation and also abuse. Given the negative coping mechanisms and intra-communal violence that characterize the conflict, community-based psycho-social support and mental healthcare are needed. CRO will therefore seek to ensure that all vulnerable groups especially children, girls and women, the disabled and elderly and in some cases young boys or men are protected from violence, abuse and exploitation. CRO will improve collection of dis-aggregated and gender data to improve analysis of these groups in order to improve actions. The project will have particular focus on groups that have been excluded from humanitarian services and from communities themselves. the elderly, persons with disabilities, youth, persons at risk of sexual violence, and children. CRO will ensure that IDPs and conflict-affected populations facing protection risks and threats are provided with timely protection response and prevention services. CRO will provide needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict-affected people are identified through effective protection monitoring, reporting and response, including promoting safe movement and durable solutions. CRO will ensure vulnerable people affected by violence have the skills, opportunities and positive coping strategies required to return and reintegrate into their communities in safety and dignity. CRO will conduct awareness raising of child protection in order to facilitate prevention, through of risk and vulnerabilities as well as engagement with communities to reduce risk and exposure, promote early warning and coordinate response to protection threats in Longchuk and Maiwut. CRO will conduct community training on child protection. CRO will train community on conducting psycho social support to the children who are unaccompanied and demobilized . Project target is 30,000 people, 12000 children, boys and girls shall be targeted in this project, 4500 women will be targeted, 10,000 men shall be targeted in this project.

1 - Needs

The crisis caused displacement, protracted conflict emergency, poverty, and livelihood insecurity are the main drivers leading to child protection risks to boys and girls in many parts of South Sudan including Maiwut and Longchuk Counties in Upper Nile state. Child protection in South Sudan has continued undermining not only the safety, dignity, overall health status, and human rights of the thousands of boys and girls who experience it, but also the , economic stability, and security of the nation .Due to this conflict, More children have experienced child protection risks being a human rights violation, a public health challenge, and a barrier to civic, social, political, and economic participation of children in the Country. Moreover, predominant cost drivers are primarily the targeted populations boys and girls demanding for protection from violence which has been directly been driven by these crises. Considering these challenging predicaments in the operating environment and limited access to protection services for the targeted IDPS , returnees and host community men, women boys and girls and their demand for prevention and response . In addition, the recent influx of spontaneous refugee returnees from the neighboring countries, and the returning of IDPs to their original residential areas are demanding access for protection services. The REACH survey in South Sudan revealed that 40% of children facing violence as the main protection threat (Reach survey, 2019). There are some awareness raising activities on `Child protection, but they tend to be ad hoc, short term and small scale. Strengthening of community-level prevention actions is therefore needed alongside response interventions to sustainably reduce child labor in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties –Upper Nile state. This proposed project seeks to address the problem of Child protection before it occurs, hence the investment in younger populations of male and female youth, younger women and girls and boys in and out of school in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties Upper Nile state. There is discrimination and barriers in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties of preventing Children from exercising their rights, accessing services and other opportunities, which greatly increase their risks of experiencing violence. The lack of accountability in the county in comprehensively addressing violence against children boys and girls is a significant obstacle to ending the problem. Generalized acceptance of recruitment of Children in the armed forces, lack of political will, inadequate legal protections and enforcement, insufficient resource allocation, contribute to pervasive impunity. This is particularly evident in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties of Upper Nile state, where community has weak justice systems and where customary law practices are more widely-used which contradict international human rights standards. Lack of advocacy for boys and girls going to school, insecurity and poverty are beyond the intangible suffering and impacts on quality of life and well-being of children include costs to the survivors , family reunification and provision of Psychosocial support in terms of improving their health (mental and physical), employment and finances, and the effects it has on children. Out of ten selected causes and risk factors for disability and death among Children between the ages of 7 and 17 suffer child labor mistreatment rated higher than cancer, motor vehicle accidents. Some of the consequences include: immediate injuries such as fractures and hemorrhaging and long-term physical conditions (e.g. gastrointestinal, central nervous system disorders, chronic pain), mental illnesses, such as depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, attempted suicide, Lack of education , displacements ,Unaccompanied siblings , sexual and reproductive health problems, such as sexually transmitted infections (including HIV) and other chronic conditions .

2 - Activities

1. CRO will conduct assessments on child protection concerns in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties to identify key drivers of the protection risks for children found in these counties 2. CRO will conduct community based monitoring and surveillance on the Cases of child protection needs in the two counties Maiwut and Long Chuk 3. CRO will conduct capacity building of the community volunteers on family tracing and Reunification of unaccompanied children in the community.4. CRO will establish child friendly spaces in Maiwut and Longechuk Counties to accommodate the Unaccompanied and separated Children from their parents.5. CRO shall provide both individual and group PSS counselling of the traumatized children in the child friendly spaces 6. CRO shall train the community volunteers on providing the Psychosocial support to traumatized and demobilized children from the armed groups 7. CRO shall strengthen the referral pathways for children with cases which need other partners attention eg education, health and Nutrition needs. 8. Undertake family tracing and reunification in line with the minimum standards. 9. Facilitate the release of children associated with armed groups to communities in the dignified manner through dialoguing and negotiation with the armed groups . 10. CRO shall train the health care givers CMR and PSS to ensure complementary services are provided11. CRO will continue monitoring and reporting mechanism of grave child rights violation in two counties 12. CRO will conduct peace building training in Maiwut and Longchuk Counties

The community will be involved right from the planning and in the implementation process because the project is directly linked to the community . Chiefs and the local authorities will be consulted for any eventualities of the project .

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

No the environment is not safe and not stable .The two locations of this project are affected by climate change where by weather changes are brought about due to heavy rains causing flooding and hence promoting soil erosion. This will increase when more returnees and IDPs occupy the rural settings which will involve cutting down of trees and burning bushes for resettlement Due to poverty some people who will settle in the rural locations will not have latrines which means open defecation will be done to contaminate the environment. The women will be collecting fire wood and some times burn charcoal for fuel depleting the environment .Poor agricultural practices applied by farmers will make the farms and soil to be infertile .Fishing activities in the rivers and swamps will deplete the fishes in the water due to poor fishing methods

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project include both sustainability and handover mechanism in place since the line ministry and the local authorities are engaged in the planning , implementation and monitoring of the project. CRO will build the capacity of the local authorities and the community at large to ensure that they get the concept of managing the project on their own once CRO leaves at the end of the project period. CRO will officially hand over the project to the community and the local authorities who shall be present . So the capacity building is done simply to ensure and strengthen the sustainability and hand over mechanisms.
