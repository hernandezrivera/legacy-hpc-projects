Project Summary

The project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (February 14, 2018 - June 13, 2019) aimed at addressing the most urgent food needs of the conflict affected, food insecure populations residing in the government-controlled areas of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, particularly in communities within 5 km of the Line of Contact. Since markets are operational (see e.g. ACTED-led Market Monitoring Reports), the main project modality was cash assistance (unconditional grants to cover basic food needs). However, bed-bound beneficiaries and beneficiaries with limited mobility, who were unable to shop on their own, are provided with in-kind assistance. Until 13 June 2019, the project was implemented by ASB in consortium with ACTED and SSU and was funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.
The follow-up project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021), funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany, will be implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine (food, hygiene and winterization components) and Samaritan Union of Ukraine (psychosocial support component).

Needs

The conflict in Ukraine continues to have a negative impact at all levels of socioeconomic indicators, particularly in Eastern Ukraine (FSLC report “Analysis of Impact of Conflict on Socio-Economic Situation in Eastern Ukraine”, Sep 2017). As a result of the pension reform (Oct 2017) and real salary growth in Ukraine, the food security situation in Ukraine has demonstrated some improvement. According to the FSLC Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) summary, the number of food insecure people in Luhansk and Donetsk GCA decreased from 410,000 in 2017 to 267,000 in 2018. However, 51% of household members in the 5-km zone report that the pension increase was insufficient to meet increased prices (REACH Winter Assessment, 2018). Thus, between September 2017 and February 2018, food prices increased by 11% (Joint Market Monitoring study, Feb 2018). Out of 116,000 individuals in Donbas who are severely food insecure, 13,000 people reside in GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts (FSLC HNO summary). The proportion of moderately or severely food insecure population is much higher in the localities close to the contact line (15 %), with the majority of the population (57%) in the 5-km zone (GCA) being marginally food secure (FSLC HNO summary). As demonstrated by REACH data (Humanitarian Trend Analysis, 2018), the proportion of households with borderline or poor food consumption scores (FCS) increases the closer one gets to the contact line (>20km = 9%; 5-20km = 10%; 5km = 15%).Given Ukraine’s particularly cold winters, food needs increase during winter and spring seasons, thus further deteriorating food security status of particularly vulnerable households (FSLC’s Guidance Note on Winterisation (2017-2018) in Food Security). Thus, in GCA, the share of households with poor and borderline FCS increased from 17.8% in June 2017 to 20.9% in February 2018 (FSLC report “Food Security and Socio-Economic Trend Analysis in Eastern Ukraine”, Mar 2018).As noted in the Shelter Cluster’s Winterization Recommendations for 2018-2019, late winter programming is still relevant, as certain households may become more vulnerable during the winter months because of increased expenditure on heating. Data collected for post-distribution monitoring under the ACCESS consortium shows that shelter expenditures get prioritized over food expenditures during winter, negatively impacting households’ food consumption scores and leading to the use of negative coping strategies.REACH’s 2018 Winter Assessment suggested a link between food security and unemployment. In February 2018, food insecurity had increased to 26% (up from 11% in June 2016) for households with a head of household aged 40-60 – a group with a high proportion of unemployed heads of households (FSLC report “Food Security and Socio-Economic Trend Analysis in Eastern Ukraine”, Mar 2018). Overall, for the population residing in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA), FCS had decreased from 21 % in February 2018 to 16 % in July 2018 (REACH 2018, Trend Analysis). Based on the primary data from the project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (2018-2019), the profile of 3500 beneficiaries of cash assistance demonstrated the following vulnerabilities: •62% are women; •24% are older people (60+);•average monthly income is 1,303 UAH (40% of actual subsistence level);•32% of beneficiaries have poor FCS;•68% of beneficiaries have borderline FCS;•70% of household have chronically ill members;•49% are women-headed households;•37% are households of older persons;•26% are households with children;•16% are households with immobile members/members with disabilities;•13% are households with unemployed members aged 50-59;•10% are households with single parents;•9% are households with IDPs.ASB will continue to respond to the persisting urgent food needs of the most vulnerable households in the 5-km zone near the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (GCA).

Activities & outcomes

The consortium was implementing its emergency assistance project in 25 locations in the 5-km zone along the contact line (GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts) (February 2018 – June 2019). The ACTED teams operate in Sloviansk, Sievierodonetsk and Mariupol, whereas ASB is present with its office in Sievierodonetsk. All visits to the 5-km zone are coordinated with CIMIC. If security situation deteriorates drastically, ASB will consider changing the target area or the target group to respond to new humanitarian needs. If local markets are disrupted, ASB will consider switching partially or fully from cash to in-kind modality. Purpose: To meet food needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine.Output: 3000 beneficiaries receive cash assistance to cover their food needs.Outcome: target beneficiaries improve their food security situation.Beneficiary identification Beneficiary assessment will take place in summer 2019, to identify caseload for July 2019-February 2020)Beneficiary assessment will be based, among other things, on FCS. Beneficiaries’ food situation will be assessed before andafter the aid distribution. If PDM data shows that FCS of certain beneficiaries from the first caseload remains poor or borderline, those beneficiaries will be automatically selected for the next phase of aid distribution. The target localities may also be adjusted after the next round of assessment. Beneficiary selection will be undertaken in coordination with the FSLC, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. Local populations will be consulted in the various assessment phases, as consortium will undertake community mobilization in the target areas to seek referrals and identify vulnerable population groups/individuals in target communities. ASB will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2019-2020 (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households, IDPs).Distribution of assistanceASB will provide monthly assistance to 3000 beneficiaries. Cash transfers to 3000 beneficiaries will take place once in 2-3 months, covering food needs for the respective period. The proposed cash intervention will follow the FSLC’s September 2018 recommendation (UAH825/person/month to cover 1575Kcal/day). Cash is the most appropriate aid modality as markets are functioning. For the population in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis, 2018). ASB will use Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta’s cash home delivery system. Thus, the modes of cash disbursement are in line with the needs and preferences of the affected populations. The project will specifically focus on women, who represented 63% of the overall (cash and in-kind) caseload of beneficiaries in the project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine”, and older people (38% of the caseload). Specific attention will be paid to households with children under 18 (around 18% of the overall caseload) as well as to households who have members with disabilities.

Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85 % of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85 % of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70 % of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms;• 75% of programme beneficiaries have improved food consumption scores;• 3,900 individuals are supported with food assistance;• At least 3000 beneficiaries are supported with cash assistance;• 400 beneficiaries during January-June 2019 are supported with in-kind assistance;• 63% of program beneficiaries are female;• 38% of beneficiaries are older people;• 10% of beneficiaries are homebound and/or persons with disabilities;• 18% of beneficiaries are children. A hotline feedback mechanism will be available for community members and assessed/selected individuals to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. ASB will aim to close the feedback loop on all requests made through the hotline. Communities will be provided with information on how their feedback was addressed.ASB will also engage with local actors and beneficiaries on an ad hoc level and undertake spot check monitoring to gauge beneficiary satisfaction and potential project challenges. All complaints will be recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up (hard copies, filed at the area/capital level). All complainants will be treated respectfully, whether it is felt that the complaint is justified or not.Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the utilization of the assistance provided. All assessments, are designed to allow age and gender disaggregation.
