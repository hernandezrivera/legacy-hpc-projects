Project Summary

IMC has continued to expand access to preventive services and treatment of acute malnutrition through its Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) programs.
• 19 active Out-Patient Therapeutic Centers (OPT) established in Damboa and Maiduguri. In these areas IMC works through a network of over 200 Community Nutrition Volunteers. IMC has also established a Stabilization Center (SC) in Damboa that is the sole provider of in-patient treatment for acute malnutrition in the area.
• 8,470 children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) have been treated in Damboa and MMC OTPs. The OTPs have all achieved, or surpassed, the recommended OTP performance.
• 1,650 children have been treated at the SC in Damboa. IMC is supporting the caregiver’s access to food during the time their children are admitted at the SC.
• 14,506 (1,624 M, 12,882 F) people were reached through the Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) intervention. IMC continues to provide IYCF messages during Care Group supported women’s discussions; community dialogue with selected community leaders; and through nutrition and health education sessions at facilities based level.
• 200 Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNV) received training and are currently supporting activities in all the nutrition centers in Damboa and MMC LGAs.
• CNVs screened 123,970 children aged 6-59 months (SAM rate of 10.2%).
• Through collaboration with SMoH and WHO in fostering capacity building in the sector, 15 (7M, 8F) national nutrition technical staff received training on guidelines and protocols for Stabilization Centers, OTPs and IYCF.
IMC’s WASH interventions address the urgent needs for access to water, sanitation, and hygiene in IDP camps and host communities by improving infrastructure and providing training and capacity building. The program targets all household members within the intervention sites and is implemented in close collaboration with the local governance structure (BOSEPA) and local community WASH volunteers. More than 80% are IDPs in formal camps and host communities in Damboa and Konduga LGAs.
Currently, the WASH program is providing access to clean water to approximately 65,742 IDPs and host communities. In Damboa, IMC is providing over 36,000 liters of water/day through water trucking to 2,500 people at the Damboa General Hospital IDP camp. In addition, IMC has rehabilitated 11 motorized water pumps and distributed hygiene kits to over 2,700 households. IMC has established 150 local Water and Sanitation Committees and continues to train and supervise these community cadres to provide governance and management of the water and sanitation facilities currently in place. To integrate with food security and livelihood, excess water flow is utilized by the IDPs to grow vegetables as a micro garden and fill into a fish Pond which supports households in adding nutritional value to their food and also provide an income during sale of surplus production.
IMC works to prevent and respond to GBV cases in four LGAs of Borno state. Community volunteers reached approximately 98,794 people in 2021 with basic information on GBV and its consequences, as well as on services available to them, including women’s and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) that offer healing and empowerment for vulnerable women and GBV survivors. The WGSS are safe environments, usually situated in centers where women and girls can socialize and make small handcrafts (such as embroidered traditional caps and beaded handbags) that they can sell to generate income for their families. Our case workers are also in the centers to provide one on-one comprehensive case management, offer counseling services and facilitate referrals as needed for specialized and more advanced care. IMC also trains healthcare workers from government and partner agencies on GBV case management, referral pathways and available legal services, to ensure that survivors receive appropriate care when referred by case workers and volunteers.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021’ Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP, an) estimated 8.9 million people in the North East remain in need of life-saving interventions to survive, including access to basic WASH, health/nutrition, and Protection services. IDPs and affected host communities continue to face the same needs, and require the same emergency interventions in WASH, nutrition, and protection. This can be achieved with the provision of good and sufficient infrastructures, which will increase their dignity. This includes, for example, providing enough sanitation and showers to reduce the number of users per facility. International Medical Corps has been providing WASH services focusing on IDP camps, while Nutrition and Protection services target both host communities and IDPs in camps. International Medical Corps will continue to provide WASH interventions to improve IDPs' lives in camps, ensuring they have enough water, live in a clean environment, and receive health/hygiene promotion focusing on key behaviors to prevent avoidable water-borne diseases and COVID-19. With the crisis ongoing, protection issues remain critical to be addressed in the overcrowded camps. IMC has been providing response and prevention services to affected people in camps and host communities. The establishment of the ten Women and Girl Safe Spaces (WGSS) have allowed women and adolescent girls to find a safe place where they discuss and participate in psychosocial activities. Women and girls were received in these hubs. The 2021’ HRP shows a need to strengthen and scale up nutrition services to reach the maximum number of children possible. The food security situation doesn’t improve as communities have been cut off their means of living. In Damboa, for example, IDPs are regularly killed by insurgents when they go to fetch firewoods. In other locations, farmers are killed on their farms, which makes it difficult for them to access their main source of survival. The COVID-19 pandemic has also put additional pressure on the already fragile socio-economic situation exacerbating food insecurity. According to the IPC acute malnutrition analysis, Damboa and Konduga LGAs are in IPC AMN Phase 3 while MMC and Jere are IPC 2, with the situation expected to worsen to IPC 3 in the coming months. Thus, the critical need to maintain nutrition services as they save lives.

2 - Activities

The WASH, nutrition, and protection proposed activities are designed to improve the quality of life, improve child survival, reduce WASH, and nutrition-related morbidity and mortality among the conflict affected population in the target areas of interventions. The program is embedded and coordinated so that the population in the target area benefits from all three sectors (WASH, Nutrition, and Protection) that IMC is proposing to implement. This program is also designed to provide an integrated response to breaking the vicious cycle linking malnutrition, avoidable water-related diseases, and gender inequalities. International Medical Corps’ global and country mission’s vision is to build local capacity to transition from relief to self-reliance, preparing for the sustainability of the outputs and outcomes after the project’s closeout. To realize this, all activities are designed to utilize locally available resources, promote community leaders' participation, and build the capacity of local stakeholders. International Medical Corps will work closely with respective line ministries to transition responsibility for implementing the operations and services, as appropriate. For example, the nutrition program will continue to utilize government-owned health facilities, and outreach sites which will be supervised by the State Ministry of Health staff to sustain functionality, while ensuring the government’s full support and participation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The different interventions will be conducted under the protection lens to ensure no additional harm is done to beneficiaries and to guarantee that their dignity remains paramount. International Medical Corps’ organizational non-discriminatory ethic will prevail throughout the project’s implementation to ensure that all beneficiaries, especially the most vulnerable persons including those with disabilities and the elderly, have equal access to services based on their needs. International Medical Corps will ensure that our project follows the “do no harm” principles and does not contribute to suffering. Because of the on-going insecurity, International Medical Corps will ensure, to the best of its ability, that the proposed activities are located as closely as possible to the beneficiaries. International Medical Corps will roll out the CBFRM at all its program sites to ensure beneficiaries can safely provide feedback and complaints on the services and goods they are provided by the program, and that responses and information is provided to the beneficiary in a timely and appropriate manner protecting and ensuring confidentiality. Personal information about beneficiaries gathered throughout the project’s implementation will be managed confidentially and only general demographic information will be shared for operational reasons, e.g. planning, implementing and evaluating the program. International Medical Corps will ensure that the rights of the beneficiaries are protected at all times, and will always advocate that their voices are heard. Further information about IMC’s protection mainstreaming strategy is included by sector.
