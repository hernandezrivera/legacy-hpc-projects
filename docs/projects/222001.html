Project Summary

Proposed Interventions1 HERO will target 19,172 vulnerable individuals in Fangak County, especially women and children under 5, as well as mobile clinics and Boma Health Teams in hard-to-reach areas of Fangak. This intervention will prioritize support for essential healthcare including prevention and treatment of communicable diseases, improved reproductive, maternal, child, and newborn health, improved care for noncommunicable diseases and trauma related injuries, as well as emergency community-based healthcare through BHI. BHI team Fangak county will be trained and tasked with integrated community case management, health promotion and risk communication.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces severe climate vulnerabilities, ranking as the second most vulnerable country globally to natural hazards according to the 2023 INFORM Risk Index. In September 2024, according to humanitarian snapshot, more than 755,000 people across 41 of South Sudan’s 78 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area are affected with Flood. Thus, communities facing a critical situation because of the flood and compounded by existing food insecurity, conflict, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. Fangak County in Jonglei state is one of the counties which has been hit hard by flooding, particularly with new wave of the flood, as a result it caused displacement of the host community to high grounds. According to IRNA Report 2024 for Fangak county, nearly 80% of the villages have been affected and the population faced great challenges coping with the shocks caused by the flood. It has been estimated that 25, 668 households/154,005 individuals affected out of which (29%) of from the total affected residents; 7,486 households/44,915 individuals were displaced to high grounds in Old Fangak, New Fangak, Paguir, Pulita, Manajang, Barboi, Toch and Mareang Payams. The affected population especially the displaced have been immediate basic and lifesaving service such as WASH, Health, Shelter NFIs, Food, Livelihoods and protections. The major health needs are Inadequate of health facilities and the most of Health Facilities has been affected by flooding, inconsistent medical supply chain resulting to drug shortages or stock out, increased disease outbreak, especially stagnant water and poor sanitation and waste management. there is increased water and vector borne disease. In September 2023, the South Sudan Ministry of Health declared an outbreak of hepatitis E virus in Fangak County, Jonglei State. From April to November 2023, MSF identified 169 hepatitis E cases, among them 45% pregnant women. Cases reported at the hospital were severe and the case fatality ratio (CFR) was high with 18 deaths, 53% were women of reproductive age and 42% were pregnant women. As of December 2024, total of 17 cholera suspected cases have been reported in Jonglei state, 15 Canal Pigi County, 1 from Fangak and 1 from Ayod. The congestions in IDP settlements in Fangak due to flooding and conflict, pose a potential risk of spreading of communicable diseases such as measles, Cholera, Malaria and other Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTD) such as Kala-Azar, Schistosomiasis, Tuberculosis (TB). There is no referral way in case of critical illness outbreak. Pregnant and lactating women, elderly people and the children under the age of five are more vulnerable during flooding thus increase the risk of childbirth complication of which there are not enough maternal staff in the health facilities.

2. Project activities

The response activities will aim to reduce morbidity and mortality, particularly the most vulnerable group Children and women through life saving and Essential primary health care services. The project will target 19,172 across the 18 payams of Fangak county. HERO will use the following approaches to reduce the morbidity and mortality in Fangak county1- Improve access to health care services: by supporting mobile and outreach clinics in hard-to-reach areas. CHD staff support with incentives, transportation of drugs and vaccines, and ICCM through the Boma Health initiatives as well strengthening referrals.2- Capacity Building HERO will train frontline workers in IMNCI, Rational Drug Use, and infection prevention and control and provide them with continuous mentorship in order to build their capacity of responding to emergencies. BHI workers in the three counties will equally be trained in Integrated Community Case Management, Health Promotion and Risk Communication.3- Social Behavior Change Communication – HERO has developed a pictorial community health dialogue tool, that will be utilized in creating awareness about health seeking behaviors. HERO will also record and translate health messages into the local language. The messages will be put in portable solar radios and utilized by Boma Health Workers to pass health messages. HERO will aim to change the health seeking and social behavior of the local populations through targeted health and hygiene promotion sessions. 4. Monitoring, Evaluation & Knowledge Management – HERO believes in evidence-based programming and will continue conducting disease surveillance, recording information in the DHIS2, rapid needs assessments, surveys and gap assessments in order to document lessons learned and best practices and improve program outcomes. 5. Advocacy – HERO will continue advocating for improved healthcare services by working with the County Health Departments in Fangak County and the State Ministries of Health of Unity and Jonglei States and with donors for resource mobilization.
