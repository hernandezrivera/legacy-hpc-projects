Project Summary

The Overall Objective of the proposed intervention is to support vulnerable communities in Area C of the West Bank (WB) affected by the deteriorating socio-economic conditions, Israeli human rights violations, and COVID-19 by protecting the productive capacity of their agricultural activities. Oxfam with three local partners (ESDC, PARC and LRC) will directly target 1,052 vulnerable households (HHs) (5154 individuals, 1422 adult men, 1412 adult women, 2165 children under 18 years old, and 155 elderly) in Hebron and Bethlehem Governorates in the WB. The proposed intervention is aligned with the country strategic objective three (SOP3), “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project is aligned with the FSS objectives 1, “The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected;” 2. “Households suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs,” and 3, “FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, including civil society organizations, key national ministries and concerned donors’. Oxfam and partners will conduct three main inter-related activity clusters contributing to the 3 FSS objectives. Aligned with O1, Area C farmers will be supported by enhancing and restoring their productive capacities. Oxfam and partners will rehabilitate agricultural lands and roads. The project will improve access to water and decrease water wastage for agriculture through installing water carrier lines and rehabilitating water wells. Farmers will have efficient access to water using renewable energy, rehabilitated land, roads, and livestock sheds. Aligned with O2, Oxfam and partners will provide temporary cash for work (CfW) opportunities for herders/ farmers and agricultural workers linked with O1 activities. Aligned with SO3, the project will provide legal awareness and legal support to target communities, in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission, land and water authorities, Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and FSS partners. Legal files will be prepared, including pick stressing legal cases supported financially and logistically. Oxfam will carry out a refresher training on safe programming to its partner staff who will be responsible for the implementation of the project activities to ensure there is solid understanding to the risks and ways to mitigate them throughout the project on the different groups of the project population. Oxfam in its orientation meetings with communities,, will provide Oxfam’s feedback mechanism, and its policies that safeguards beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

Herding communities living in Area C of the WB have been increasingly subject to continuous stressors perpetrated by Israel, especially land/property confiscation and the demolition of homes and structures. Hence, Continuous human rights violations severely deplete the households’ resilience capacities and deepen their socio-economic crisis. Oxfam will respond to the immediate needs of 23 communities in Hebron (19 communities) and Bethlehem (4 communities). The target population and proposed sectors of intervention are informed by Oxfam’s, and it is partners qualitative needs assessment (August 2021) conducted in the WB. Oxfam and its partners carried out 11 FGDs in 8 herding communities in Area C of Bethlehem and Hebron with a collective livelihood perspective rather than an individual household perspective. The assessment attempted to identify some of these communities' current livelihood, protection, access to services, and food security conditions. A total of 104 individuals (61 men and 43 women), including herders, community leaders and village council members, were selected from the following localities: Hebron: Ar Rakeez, Ar Ramadin, Haribat an Nabi, Idhna, Imneizil, Isfey al Fauqa, Isfey al Tihta, Jinba, Jurat al Khiel, Khirbet al Fakheit, Khirbet al Majaz, Khirbet Ghuwein al Fauqa, Khirbet Jamrura, Khirbet Shuweika, Maghayir al Abeed, Mantiqat Shib al Butum, Mirkez, Qawawis, Beit Ummar/jala. Bethlehem: Al Khadr, Umm Salamuna, Wadi Fukin, Al Jab'a, Beit Skarya. According to OCHA (unpublished report, July 2021), “Impact of increased settlers’ violence on livelihoods of Herding Communities in Southern West Bank, reported harsh socio-economic conditions within their HHs suffering from daily challenges in accessing basic livelihood needs, health services, water, energy, and infrastructure. Communities are located near Israeli settlements, and others live in the middle of the “firing zone” with extremely difficult access restrictions. Communities, in general, lack basic facilities with no sewage infrastructure, paved roads, water and electricity networks. For electricity provision, 95% of the HHs rely on small scale solar energy systems which provides them with 35% of their total energy needs inside the household. There are massive shortages of water supply for domestic and animal needs that makes the supply that reaches them around 40% of their needs. 95% of the communities rely on livestock farming and use dry fodder due to the lack of pasture lands. The priorities highlighted by the communities were as follows: (1) A pressing need to enhance access to their land through opening new roads and/or rehabilitation, land rehabilitation, animal sheds, water wells/cistern rehabilitation, water carrier lines, mobile tanks, and solar energy. Around 70-80% of participants were reported the high risks of movement near their villages due to the poor and risky infrastructure. (2) There is an increase in morbidity and mortality among livestock, mainly newborn livestock, due to poor living conditions. This includes poor feeding, insufficient veterinary services that worsened during the Covid-19 outbreak, in addition to poor and old animal sheds. In some villages there has been drastic decline by 50% of livestock production due to the climatic changes coupled with the Israeli restrictions affected the natural ecosystem, for others there is gradual but slow decline reaching to 20-30% in some villages. Overall, the decline in pasture lands was reported to have resulted in increase in livestock farming inputs by 80%. (3) legal awareness and support were also raised as a pressing need. The meetings revealed that the communities’ needs have increased due to the continuous violations, restrictions, harassment and daily attacks by Israeli forces and settlers. Needs sharply increased during Covid-19, during which demolitions and seizures of Palestinian property and humanitarian aid, including Palestinian homes, continue, despite previous commitments from Israeli authorities not to target Palestinian residential structures during the pandemic. Area C communities continue to face legal challenges summarized as follows: a) absence/weak centralized administrative body providing legal counselling and response mechanisms to address military orders; b) limited knowledge about technical steps to prepare legal cases to challenge military orders, especially new military orders since many amendments to Israeli laws are made; c) presence of several entities managing such cases, causing confusion, the lack of accountability and mistrust of people receiving military orders towards service providers; d) continued and sharp increase of IHL violations by Israeli authorities against Palestinian land and housing rights; e) loss of large areas of land, especially agricultural land, due to increasing Israeli stop-work orders over the last five years; f) failure to address hundreds of Israeli military orders due to frustration, lack of knowledge and awareness, g) increasing costs of legal aid to defend the right to land and housing

2 - Activities

ESDC, LRC and PARC will implement the shed rehabilitation, road and land rehabilitation, cistern rehabilitation including the CfW activity based on geographical divisions. ESDC and LRC will implement activities in the north and south of Hebron, while PARC will implement in Bethlehem and the western line of Hebron in addition to the water related water which includes water tanks, water carrier pipelines. ESDC will be responsible for the solar panel units. All project activities were coordinated with the MoA and will be in detail coordinated and agreed with MoA at the national level and at the directorate level at the start of the project. Oxfam will be responsible for coordinating activities in the field and with the MoA and DoA, will provide overall oversight and tracking of activities, will provide guidance and training on safe-programming and safe delivery of implementation, and harmonize and lead procurement processes. Project community committees (PCCs) will be established at the project start and will include representatives of village councils (VC), MoA/Directorate of Agriculture (DoA), project partners, women and youth CBOs. Oxfam will lead on harmonizing procedures, standards, tools and monitoring along the project cycle. ESDC, PARC, and LRC will directly implement activities. Output 1: The livelihood of 1,052 vulnerable herders/farmers and their households (HHs) in Bethlehem and Hebron Governorates (5154 individuals, 1422 adult men, 1412 adult women, 2165 children under 18 years old, and 155 elderly) is protected by enhancing their access to and rehabilitating their agricultural lands, wells, roads, livestock sheds, carrier lines, water mobile tanks, and installation of solar energy. Beneficiary selection criteria include small scale male and female farmers and herders owning. Priority will be given to female herders whenever available. 5-10 dunums of open land in Area C or 30- 100 heads of small ruminants. Affected farmers/herders registered in MoA’s database will be prioritized, especially those not supported by ongoing projects. CfW is the chosen modality to conduct rehabilitation works to support 400 farmers/workers and unemployed agricultural workers in the community. CfW beneficiaries will be selected based on family size, families with PWD members, women-headed families, income and employability status. Workers will be paid 22 USD per day/per dunum for up to 26 working days. Oxfam’s three partners will implement activities under output 1 in Hebron. A1.1:Rehabilitate 950 dunums (250 dunums in Bethlehem district) prioritizing affected crops, land, and assets through providing inputs and services (seedlings, grains, machinery work, fencing): Land rehab. will include levelling, planting seedlings and/or seeds, installing irrigation networks, retaining walls, and fencing. A 1.2: Rehabilitation of 28 water wells (8 in Bethlehem district): Selection of wells will be based on MoA’s priorities in coordination with VCs, WASH cluster, the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and Oxfam’s three partners operating in the target areas. A1.3: Rehabilitate 15 km of agricultural roads: Selection will be aligned with the MoA and VCs prioritized geographic areas and those not included in any other FSS project. PCCs will be supervising the works that a local contractor will carry out. A1.4Rehabilitation of 50 animal sheds: The selection of beneficiaries will be conducted through a public announcement by VCs with the engagement of PCCs. The selection criteria will include but not limited to; socio-economic parameters, the degree of exposure to risks (due to location) and those not supported by other interventions. Women headed HHs will be prioritized. Rehabilitation of 50 animal sheds will be conducted through a local contractor and supervised by the PCCs until they are handed over to MoA and the VCs. ESDC, LRC and PARC will implement this activity according to the geographical divisions mentioned above. A1.5: Install water carrier lines (7 km of 6- and 4-inches )pipes): PCCs will develop selection criteria to select communities/areas, farmers/herders with pressing needs. Selection of carrier lines will be developed in consultation with the MoA and VCs based on a comprehensive assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project by the project team, MoA, VCs, and PCCs. A local contractor will be selected through a competitive tendering process to install carrier lines. A1.6: Five mobile water tanks: The selection of herders will be based on: socio-economic parameters, level of exposure to risks due to location and those not supported by other intervention, prioritizing female (headed HHs) farms. A 1.7 Install 5 solar system units (15 kW) for HHs and mobile water pumps to discharge water from water wells: Based on electricity needs, the selection of HHs will be conducted through consultations with VCs and other actors (community leaders, and PCCs). ESDC will hire a qualified contractor to develop the systems. Contractor will provide training on water conservation & sustainable agricultural practices. Oxfam & partners will coordinate PWA & the WASH cluster during implementation to follow WASH technical specs. Output 2:139 farmers/herders and their HH are supported through legal aid Oxfam and LRC will support targeted farmers/herders to prepare legal files with MoA, CWRC, land and water authorities, MoSD, FSS, and legal aid organizations, i.e., JLAC and Saint Yves, who will submit the files to Israeli courts if needed. A 2.1: Conduct 19 awareness workshops targeting 380 herders and farmers (out of the targeted 1054) in the Hebron and Bethlehem districts. A 2.2: Prepare legal files for approximately 100 farmers and herders. A 2.3 Provide legal and financial support to 20 cases out of the 100 legal file cases (A2.2): LRC will support victims (farmers/herders) of land and housing right violations, including hiring lawyers and other associated costs to submit files to the Israeli courts

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam's Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive online feedback through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam's MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam's Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam's confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam and MA'AN have adapted accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19. Oxfam's helpline will be primarily used to report any misconduct, fraud, exploitation or abuse incidents, which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries by making their numbers available to all beneficiaries. This mechanism will help resolve issues in the field, such as prices, access to shops, changing contact information, and others. At the beginning of the project, the beneficiaries will be invited to groups of 10-15 to receive an MoU that will include information about the project, the amount they will receive, and the project designated number to call in case of inquiries and complaints

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries’ feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. The action responds to Oxfam’s and partner’s needs analysis whereby women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilization sessions will be implemented encouraging the participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also serve as an awareness-raising tool to sensitize households and the community to accept women’s participation. This will contribute to mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam’s gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action’s response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, including PWDs, youth, children, and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, essential in the context of Area C, where social and political instability is evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project. This will enhance community engagement and will reduce potential protection risks or exclusion. The committee will include VC representatives, MoA/ DoA, project partners, women and youth CBOs. Oxfam will lead on harmonizing procedures, standards, tools and monitoring along the project cycle. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in Oxfam’s humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming vulnerability and the need for the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a transparent process with selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, PWDs and children will benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age, or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people’s concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access, whether physical or related to the process of implementing the intervention and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality. Women in the farming and herding community will be directly targeted in the project. Women will be invited to orientation meetings to become aware of the feedback mechanism and the project activities. Through the PCCs, and from experience in the villages planned in the proposal women and youth will take part in the oversight of the project activities which will also enable the team have better access to the beneficiaries and will be a channel to ensure open communication with them to the project team.
