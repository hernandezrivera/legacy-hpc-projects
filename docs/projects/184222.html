Project Summary

There is approximately 2.7 million IDPs in Somalia, Conflict, insecurity, and impact of political divisions continue to drive humanitarian needs, displacement and protection concerns in most regions of Somalia. The situation has been made worse by the outbreak of COVID 19 pandemic which led to restriction of movements which heavily affected the country’s economy. The situation has been worse for the minority clans, the elderly and those living with disability. There has been increased displacement and especially in Afmadow district due to the current drought. Increased displacements have also been reported in Berdale due to the ongoing conflict in the neighboring districts. The number of IDP have continued to increase and especially in Berdale and Baidoa due to these displacements. Access to basic services has been a major concern for the protracted and the newly displaced IDPs in the urban and the peri urban crowded IDP settlements in Berdale, Baidoa and Afmadow Districts. The IDPs have been exposed to harsh climatic conditions as most of them lack proper shelter, and access to NFIs has been a major challenge and especially for the newly displaced IDPs. An estimated 3.7 million people HNO 2021 need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. According to the JMCNA 2021, shelter is one of the top three priority need of more than half of the entire population and of 58 percent of IDPs. An estimated 3.7 million people need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations.The needs for shelter and NFI needs in 2022 is expected to increase compared to 2020, this is as a result of extensive flooding that is expected to damage shelters, loss of NFIs, displacement due to conflict, drought and forced eviction. The inability of households to repair and construct shelters or buy basic non-food items due to limited financial resources. WRRS is proposing to support 49,800 beneficiaries (men 18,410,women,20,390,boys 5490,Girls 5,510) with NFI,ESK and transitional shelters in Lower Juba, Afmadow, Kismayo, South West, Berdale and Baidoa Districts. Beneficiaries receiving NFIs will not receive ESKs. The project will target IDP women, men, boys and girls, priority will be given to people living with disability the elderly and those from the marginalized clans. WRRS will consider cash/voucher modality, this will be based on market survey that will be done to ascertain availability in the market. WRRS will coordinate with other organization to prevent overlap and duplication of activities. Assistance will be provided to the protracted and the newly displaced IDPs in South west and Lower Juba regions.WRRS will ensure that the assistance provided to the beneficiaries is appropriate to the affected population. WRRS will target the beneficiaries in Lower Juba and Bay region and especially in Baidoa and Berdale Districts. The organization will conduct community consultations before the start of the project this will be to ensure that the beneficiaries in need are consulted from the onset of the project. WRRS will also establish a community feedback mechanism in Baidoa, Berdale and also in Afmadow and Kismayo Districts

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Use of Vouchers is more convenient compared to using cash, use of cash is associated with multiple risks and especially when dealing with bulk cash.Transportation of in kind is also cumbersome due to the poor road network in parts of Somalia and also restrictions of movements and bad weather.
