Project Summary

The overall objective is providing lifesaving WASH assistance to most vulnerable population groups in particular for the regions in the south struggling with scarce access to humanitarian assistance and having a number of Persons in Need that is over 80.000. The project will target DCs, schools and health centers through the following activities: rehabilitation of WASH facilities to ensure a good quality and quantity of water, provision of hygiene kits, NFI distribution and implementation of hygiene sessions, solid waste management system, capacity building of local service providers and implementation of vector diseases control (fumigations).

1 - Needs

It is estimated that over 242.000 people are in need of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene assistance in Libya. Although IDPs are the most vulnerable with regard to WASH needs , Returnees, Non Displaced, Migrants and Refugees also are critically in needs on this subject. Since April 2019, given the intensification of the clashes from the LNA, the availability and efficiency of WASH facilities have been decreasing and keeping and the accessibility of these services has become a growing challenge for the local authorities. Therefore, the following needs have been highlighted:• Lack of safe access to water and sanitation facilities and access to basic hygiene items, notably as a life-saving or emergency intervention. These needs are especially present in detention centres, schools and health facilities;• Lack of WASH facilities, NFI/Cash assistance;• Lack of a solid waste management system;• Poor capacity building of local service providers. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities are critical infrastructure elements in educational and health facilities. If inadequate, they can cause diseases to spread at a rapid pace, while discouraging the population, first of all persons with specific needs, to access the services provided there. This is especially true for women, girls, and persons with disabilities, whose safety and dignity can be put at risk due to inadequate WASH facilities. The number of gender segregated latrines, as well as facilities designed and built according to principles of universal design is generally low in Libyan schools, with major consequences in terms of girls’ and children with disabilities school attendance. Girls are particularly affected by inadequate WASH facilities, as the lack of hygiene and especially menstrual hygiene supplies can discourage girls from attending and staying in school. In general, lack of lighting, locks, privacy and sex-segregation increases the risk of GBV against women and girls when using public WASH facilities. According to the map of needs by region identified from the WASH cluster, GVC targets the regions that have the most critical needs which have a PIN varying from 80.000 to 100.000 (Ubari) and from 100.000 to 120.000 (Murzuq; Sabha and Tripoli). Three out of four targeted regions are located in the south. Indeed, the lack of INGO’s reinforce the needs in WASH intervention. Among those targeted mantikas, identified actions will be prioritized according to the results of a multi- sectorial assessment conducted by GVC at the beginning of the project. Indeed GVC has developed a Community Protection Approach (CPA), which is a community engagement and empowerment instrument used to design Integrated Protection Programs (IPP).

2 - Activities

Through these different activities, GVC aims to provide lifesaving WASH assistance to the most vulnerable population groups. Based on its expertise and with the contribution of its implementing partner in Libya, Migrace, GVC plans to implement activities in host communities, especially in schools and health facilities and in DCs. The activities that GVC plans to lead in the DCs are the following: rehabilitation and/or Installation of new water facilities. Notably water tanks and treatment units will be installed in the targeted DCs.Rehabilitation of new sanitation facilities respecting those parameters in addition to the sphere standards: (Sex segregated toilets; children-friendly and universal designed wash facility; Lighting and locks to assure safe access; etc.)Furthermore, a smoke fumigation system will be set once per month in the DC. Distribution of cleaning materials will be led to promote both the vector control system and the solid waste management system.Distribution of gender-specific hygiene kits and sessions on the importance of personal hygiene and the proper use of cleaning materials will be conducted and, trainings to the implementing partner will be delivered on WASH and protection/gender mainstreaming through wash activities.All the activities will be designed and implemented in accordance with the DC principled Framework. The host communities present critical Wash needs especially in health facilities and schools. the multi-sector questionnaire (MQ) will be implemented in order to decide which are the top priority facilities that will need to be targeted for intervention. The activities implemented will be same as the first ones plus:Hygiene sessions and global awareness days will be lead in targeted health centers and schools. They will integrate the provision of information about hygiene, GBV related issues, and services through the inclusion of health and protection information. Finally, GVC will conduct training for health centers

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project will be accountable to the affected population at each phase of the cycle project. Indeed the affected population will be involved from the first stage thanks to the implementation of GVC’s methodology CPA. Population engagement is designed as a process of empowerment, following the Ladder of Citizen Participation and is adaptable to each context. A mechanism for identification, assessment and referral of families and individuals ensures additionally meaningful access to assistance and services for these peopleFurthermore, a selection committee will be composed from one hand of theheads of targeted schools, and the hospitals directors and some of keys staffs and on the other hand of GVC technical staff and its implementing partner Migrace to design the project and determine the needs. They will also be included in the design of the monitoring and evaluation tools. In addition, a monitoring system will be in place throughout the project implementation, encompassing i) a feedback and complaint mechanism; ii) a reporting mechanism for cases of violence and/or abuse; iii) regular (group and individual) meetings with parents and students. Moreover, the beneficiaries will be included in the implementation of the activities, especially in the Hygiene Session. A final evaluation will be conducted in order to measure the impact of the intervention.At last, an exit strategy will be design in order to assure the durability of the project and the re appropriation of the different activity by the beneficiaries and the stakeholders. Throughout the whole cycle, different Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) groups in the community will be engaged, including the most vulnerable ones. More specifically, different AGD groups will be identified based on a sampling approach and classified according to a scale of prioritization, with a rationale that focuses on the characteristics of each groups and the specific protection risks associated with them. The widest possible representation of AGD groups will be sought. Attention will be paid to ensure that potentially underrepresented groups, such as migrants, IDPs, single female- headed households and persons with disabilities/caregivers of persons with disabilities will participate in this phase.Moreover, GVC Libya will pay attention to apply GVC Policy such as the “Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy”.
