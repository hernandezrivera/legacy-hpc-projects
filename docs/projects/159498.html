Project Summary

According to the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine, more than 1.4 million people were displaced by the conflict in Donbass and the annexation of Crimea.
Launched in March 2016, the NMS has proven to be an effective tool to monitor the situation of the IDPs: the NMS reports are used by a wide range of state and non-state actors in Ukraine to develop strategies and plan activities with real-time data about the most pressing needs of IDPs and host community members in Ukraine.
The NMS will collect, analyse and share information on socioeconomic characteristics of IDPs: displacement trends and movement or return intentions, employment and livelihood, access to social services, preferred durable solutions and assistance needs as well as information on the situation of returnees to the NGCA.
The proposed project will contribute to strengthening the capacity of the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (MoSP), the Ministry of Veterans Affairs, Temporarily Occupied Territories and Internally Displaced Persons of Ukraine and other humanitarian actors to conduct assessments on IDPs in a harmonized and systematized manner.

1 - Needs

According to the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine, more than 1.4 million people are registered as internally displaced persons (IDPs). Half of the registered IDPs permanently resides in the GCA in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts beyond the 20 km area along the ‘contact line’. In line with the latest NMS Survey findings, 73% IDPs have been staying at their current place of residence for over three years thus the internal displacement can be considered protracted. The situation of protracted displacement requires actions of the humanitarian actors to prevent the negative effect of the conflict. The provision of humanitarian assistance is highly dependent on trends and patterns of human mobility. Humanitarian actors require information on the location and composition of the affected population in order to deliver services and respond to needs in a timely manner;The monitoring of displacement requires a flexible information management system and adaptable methodologies with the capacity to regularly provide adequate information to facilitate the delivery of services and target responses in line with needs in a timely manner. IOM has gradually improved the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), a system designed to regularly capture, process and disseminate information to provide a better understanding of the evolving movements and locations, numbers, vulnerabilities and needs of the displaced populations.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Ukrainian authorities and humanitarian partners are able to plan strategically for assistance programming based on information on the situation of IDPs. Data collected and presented in the NMS reports will benefit Ukrainian authorities and humanitarian partners in planning of the programmes targeted for various target groups as the information presented will show the needs of women, men, boys and girls. Capacity building of national and local authorities that provide support to IDPs and conflict affected population on strategic planning and methodology, sample design, questionnaire development, data collection, data processing and report preparation will be conducted by IOM through number of sessions and field visits. Output: Data on the socioeconomic characteristics of IDPs in Ukraine is available and humanitarian actors have up to date information on displacement trends and movement or return intentions, IDPs employment and livelihoods, access to social services, preferred durable solutions and assistance needs as well as information on the situation of returnees to the NGCA. Activities for output:  Prepare the questionnaires in coordination with government authorities, humanitarian actors and in line the strategic focus of the NMS round; Carry out data collection, including but not limited to telephone survey, focus group discussions and face to face interviews;  Combines data obtained from multiple sources, namely: administrative data; data from focus group discussions and key informant interviews; data of sample surveys of IDP households and local population; telephone interviews with IDPs (numbers are from National IDPs database provided by the MoSP); and available relevant data from other sources; Analyse the data collected, including disaggregation by gender and age parameters, and produce the report; Present the key findings of the NMS round and disseminate the report to relevant stakeholders. Through telephone and face-to-face interviews, focus group discussions and interviews on entry-exit checkpoints (EECPs) along the contact line, IOM will collect and analyze the data on socio-economic characteristics of IDPs in all 24 regions of Ukraine and Kyiv. Taking into consideration that Vodafone mobile network operates in non-governmental controlled area (NGCA) Donetsk and Luhansk regions, the information among IDPs that returned to the area of residence will be gathered and presented in a separate chapter. Data collected within NMS will be desegregated by gender, age and type of the settlement. The NMS is to be implemented during the whole duration of the project. NMS reports in English and Ukrainian will be issued on a quarterly basis and will provide the updated information on the situation with IDPs in all oblasts of Ukraine. Based on findings of each quarterly phase, the tools of the NMS can be adjusted to study the emerging trends. The scope of the system could be also extended to include elaboration of the methodological instructions, automation of data calculation and quality control as well as visualization procedures to be transferred to the potential implementer of the NMS from amongst the state authorities of Ukraine.
