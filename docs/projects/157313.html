Project Summary

To contribute to reduction of mortality and morbidity among the affected persons (IDPs, returnees, and host community members) through provision of integrated Nutrition , Health, and WaSH emergency and recovery interventions to vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls in selected sites in Blue Nile, White Nile, and South Kordfan States during 2019. The nutrition interventions aim to improve the nutrition status and reduce the nutrition-related mortality and morbidity rates among the most vulnerable groups of the population, especially children under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women at the selected geographical areas through provision of timely and comprehensive preventive and curative nutritional services.

Needs

According to Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2019, the total number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance is more than 6 million with an increase of 15% compared to 2018. This increase in number can be justified by several factors such as large scale population displacement, influx of refugees and climate and socioeconomic conditions across the country. Host communities and residents are estimated to be 1.94 million which represent 34% of the total people in need of humanitarian assistance. Sudan has one of the highest rate of wasting with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 16 % among the under five children with stunting rates of 38.2%. There are 3.1 million people in need for basic nutrition services; 49% of them are female, 51% are male ( by sex) and 100% are children (by age). There is clear gap in the nutrition services across the country by which only 25% of children with malnutrition have access to treatment services. About 2.3 million children suffer from wasting annually; 694,000 of these suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 523,243 pregnant and lactating women are in need for counseling on optimal infant feeding and care practices. ADD nutrition project aims to provide comprehensive preventable and curative nutrition services at both community and health facility level in order to alleviate the suffering, maintain their dignity and increasing their self reliance.

Activities

The proposed activities are in line with the Strategic Response Plane and the Nutrition sector objectives. These activities are the following:- The projects aim to respond to nutritional need in the selected areas through provision of comprehensive package of both preventive and curative services through the following activities: - Strengthen the Case management of moderate acute malnourished (SAM/MAM) girls and boys of 6 59 months and PLW through improved outreach and referral. - Support routine screening and periodic MUAC screening campaigns for active case finding. - Training for technical capacity building of Ministry of (CMAM, IYC, NiE , Monitoring and Reporting). - Provision of IYCF education and counselling for mothers/care takers. - Support the provision of micronutrient through CMAM program - Conduct meeting with key influences at sub national levels to raise their awareness and support their engagement and development and reviewing of the key nutrition documents -Organizing meeting and training for CBOs at localities level to contribute to sub national plan and processing, and to increasing their capacity on nutrition aspect -Consultation work shop for CSA members at national and sub national level to ensure the civil society organization in able to contribute in nutrition plan-Hosting SUN-CSAs planning and review meetings.at national level (4 meetings)

Impact on environment/natural resources

ADD will pay attention to integration of environmental issues during any nutrition project assessments will ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. When assessing environmental issues, understanding the specific context is critical. the in Sudan, deforestation is the one main environmental problems, and projects that could potentially affect forests and other land vegetation and water sources need to eliminate and/or mitigate negative impacts as much as possible.: key environmental concerns should be built into the project. To support land vegetation would be the introduction of alternative reuse material for nutrition supplies bagging such as cloth bags to replace plastic bags. ADD through Monitoring and evaluation of the outcomes of a response is important to ensure that the effects sought for were indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time to allow for an adjustment of activities. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of Do No Harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, evaluation will help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. ADD will be Working with local authorities, CBOs and communities: Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management, including working with the national and state governmental authorities. Civil society organizations including Universities, and local NGOs should be consulted, promoted and their capacities enhanced. People in these organisations have invaluable knowledge and skill in the management of the local environment, and will be responsible for building the systems for equitable sustainable management in future
