Project Summary

According to JMCNA, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The methodology used for the PIN calculation estimated that 4.6 million people are in catastrophic (10%) severe (9%) and extreme (18%) need for humanitarian WASH assistance. The 10% put under the catastrophic severity is mainly due to lack of access to water both in terms of quality and quantity accompanies by lack of access to functional sanitation.
According to Inter-Agency Joint WASH Assessment that READO participated in Baidoa, Hudur and Berdale, there are gaps in access to clean and safe water as most strategic water points were destroyed by floods and in some areas they are non-existent. Flash floods caused by heavy Gu rains in April 2020 led to contamination of water sources that led to increase in the number of cholera cases in Bay region with 681 cumulative cases (Week 1-39) with 3 deaths and a CFR of 0.4% (Outbreak update – Cholera in Somalia October 6th 2020)
Also, only 8% and 58% of respondents in Berdale and Baidoa districts respectively mentioned that they treat their water before use. (Inter-agency Joint WASH Assessment 29th August 2020)
On the access of latrines, 33% and 58% of the respondents in Baidoa and Berdale respectively reported that they don’t have access to a latrine in their HHs. While only 12% of the respondents in Baidoa reported having access to communal latrines. In Hudur, only 40% have access to communal latrines.
There is a need for gender-segregated latrines in both districts as only 56% and 21% of the respondents in Baidoa and Berdale respectively reported that they have access to gender-segregated latrines. In Hudur, only 16% have access to gender-segregated latrines.
Concerning latrines being accessible to people with disabilities, Berdale has a dire need for latrines for people with disabilities. 0% and 32% of the respondents in Berdale and Baidoa respectively reported that there is access to latrines designed for people with disabilities. In Hudur, only 19% have access to latrines designed for people with disabilities. (Inter-agency Joint WASH Assessment)
In response to this, READO intends to provide access to safe clean water, sanitation, and hygiene for vulnerable populations in Baidoa Berdale and Hudur, Bay/Bakool region. This will be done through emergency latrine construction, providing temporary access to water, hygiene promotion and kits distribution, and rehabilitation of water points. READO will also maintain safety, equity and accessibility of the vulnerable groups (including women, boys, girls, elderly and people with disabilities) when accessing WASH services. In regards to people with disabilities, 15% of latrines will be constructed to meet the needs of people with disabilities and the elderly (ramp, handrail, wide door, the easy lock should be considered as appropriate). The latrines will be constructed in a way that can be used safely by all sections of the population, including children, older people, pregnant women and persons with disabilities.

Project Activities

Objective 1: Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities (including men, women, boys and girls) in a dignified manner in Baidoa, Berdale and Hudur in Bay/Bakool region Somalia. Activity 1.1.1: Support provision of temporary access to safe water for 1,500HHs (9000 people) of affected communities in the emergencies (IDP's) through water trucking/vouchers, O&M services and householdwater treatment HHWTs. This will also involve the installation of emergency water tanks (water distribution points) in the Southwest state of Somalia. The water trucking will last for two months Activity 1.1.2 Support provision of Emergency VIP latrine construction for 200 latrines and desludging for IDPs communities. Considering the scale of internal displacements, and to contain the AWD/cholera outbreak, additional emergency latrines, and garbage disposal will be pertinent. Activity1.1.3; READO will distribute key emergency WASH supplies and equipment including hygiene kits (including Jerricans, aqua tabs, soap) to meet WASH emergency needs of 1,500HHs vulnerable communities in Baidoa especially IDPs. Activity 1.1.4; READO will train CHWs and volunteers who will in turn conduct community sensitizationand hygiene promotion around drinking water collection and storage; hand washing, hygienic sanitation. Objective 2: Continue to restore access to safe and equitable basic WASH services in targeted locations(including non-IDP men, women, boys and girls) in a dignified manner Activity 2.1.1 READO will assess key community water sources of shallow wells, boreholes and other water sources and rehabilitate 3 strategic water points in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor that will benefit 7,000 people. Also, READO will establish Water Management Committees (WMCs) at the community/IDP level to manage WASH facilities and conduct hygiene promotion campaigns. The WMCs will also comprise of people with disability as a focal point for that group. WMCs will be trained on the operation and maintenance of the rehabilitated water points. READO will consult the WASH Cluster focal point to ensure that all activities planned are integrated into the WASH master plan, endorsed by the Federal Authorities. In regards to people with disabilities, READO will ensure participation and active engagement with people with disabilities in all stages of the program to ensure that WASH services and facilities are tailored to needs and preferences. Furthermore, READO will raise awareness amongst WASH staff on the need for and approaches to data collection on children with disabilities. Moreover, during needs assessments and PDMs, READO will disaggregate all data collected by disability, age and gender and present findings with these considerations. Objective 3: Maintain safety, equity and accessibility of the vulnerable groups (including women, boys,girls, elderly and people with disabilities) when accessing WASH services 10% of latrines will be constructed to meet the needs of people with disabilities and the elderly (ramp, handrail, wide door, easy lock should be considered as appropriate). The latrines will be constructed in a way that can be used safely by all sections of the population, including children, older people, pregnant women and persons with disabilities. READO proposes to construct Ventilated Improved pit latrines (VIP).The latrines will also be gender segregated for men and women with lockable doors and within reasonable walking distance. Also, HH consulted in design, location and delivery of sanitation services (considering ageing and disability as a vulnerability as well).

Mitigation Measures

In regards to insecurity risk in locations and routes to WASH sanitation facilities, READO will identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossﬁre or violence. READO will also consider installing lights near communal facilities. If lighting is not possible, consider coordinating with the NFI sector for the provision of solar lamps/torches for each household. Furthermore, READO will locate sanitation facilities invisible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from the settlements for easier accessibility to people with disabilities. 15% of latrines to meet the needs of people with disabilities and the elderly. WMCs will also comprise of people with disabilities to make sure their voices are being heard and considered in making disability-friendly WASH facilities. In light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings and consider staggering of the distribution of hygiene kits for between 5-10 days. The distribution sites will set up a supervised hand washing area with an adequate supply of hand wash solution and each site will allocate areas for health screening/ body temperature checks by health officials in coordination with the MoH. During distribution, READO will also allocate specific areas for the elderly, pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities and individuals with pre-existing/chronic underlying conditions & give priority. In regards to the distribution of hygiene kits, READO will identify central locations for distribution points so that the beneﬁciaries do not have to traverse long distances or pass through conﬂict zones to access distribution points. As far as GBV is concerned, READO will sensitize women to accompany each other in groups especially when going to fetch water to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls. Besides, READO will build separate toilets for males and females. READO will ensure they are marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with the community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. For privacy, READO will provide secondary enclosures around facilities or put locks on the doors to latrines. This will be discussed with beneficiaries to consider their preferences. In regards to safety risks to children, READO will consider the size of the drop hole; provide smaller jerry cans for children to collect water to avoid potential injury. In regards to Accountability to Affected Communities, READO will establish a complaint response mechanism that will be accessible, conﬁdential and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneﬁciary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. This will be done through the use hotline number where beneﬁciaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. During the distribution of hygiene kits, READO will establish mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with a disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. For more gender inclusion, READO will consider the gender balance of distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. READO will consult through FGDs women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their speciﬁc needs and preferences for WASH target locations, design and services. Besides, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about WASH need and assess whether WASH interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the two communities through employing the holistic DO NO HARM approach.
