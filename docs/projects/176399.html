Project Summary

Most vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states continue to endure multiple shocks that have continued to erode and undermine lives and livelihood with consequent in poor food and nutrition security outcomes. A combination of poor harvest, conflict with recent escalation of hostilities in the Equatorias, economic meltdown, and now floods in Bor South among other counties have conspired to drive food and nutrition insecurity to unprecedented levels. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above emergency threshold of 15% in the target counties (IPC December 2020). The situation of vulnerable communities is compounded by COVID 19 outreak that has resulted into significant impacts on businesses including negative economic implications, that have further eroded livelihoods.
Latest December 2020 IPC report has projected severe acute food insecurity in 2021 which is expected to remain among the highest recorded since 2014. This coupled with increasing levels of inter-communal conflict, especially along Bor South- Pibor border; in addition to livestock related fighting in the Equatorias, a macroeconomic crisis, and the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and preventive measures are leading to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and worse outcomes (IPC December 2020).
Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) proposes actions targeting 10,000 households who make up 60,000 beneficiaries (10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 girls and 20000 boys) includes people with disabilities and will respond to prioritized activities via livelihood protection support through prepositioning crop, vegetable and fishing livelihood kits supplies through FAO pipeline and livestock interventions. CARD has been operating in the targeted counties since 2014 and has is implementing a FAO funded FSL project in Western Equatoria and previous and pipeline actions in Jonglei. CARD has other programs with competent staff, logistics and community goodwill; good relations with communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account GBV mitigation measures.

1 - Needs

Most vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states continue to endure that have continued to erode and undermine lives and livelihood with consequent in poor food and nutrition security outcomes. A combination of poor harvest, conflict with recent escalation of hostilities in the Equatorias, economic meltdown, and now floods in Bor South among other counties have conspired to drive food and nutrition insecurity to unprecedented levels. An estimated 1.4 million people are projected to fall within the IPC classification of crisis, emergency and catastrophic acute food insecurity, with attendant World Health Organization’s (WHO) Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates above emergency threshold of 15% in majority of assessed counties (IPC December 2020). The situation of vulnerable communities is compounded by COVID 19 outbreak that has resulted into significant impacts on businesses including negative economic implications, that have further eroded livelihoods. Flooding extremes in Jalle, Baidit, Anyidi, Kolnyang and Makuach in Bor South Counties submerged at least 230 hectares of crops and vegetables in addition to spurring livestock disease outbreaks and heightened risks of Rift Valley Fever, further exposing additional vulnerability among agro-pastotalist communities (FAO Cluster Updates, 2021; REACH/ Flood Reports, 2020). Latest December 2020 IPC report has projected severe acute food insecurity in 2021 which is expected to remain among the highest recorded since 2014. This coupled with increasing levels of inter-communal conflict, especially along Bor South- Pibor border; in addition to livestock related fighting in the Equatorias, a macroeconomic crisis, and the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and preventive measures are leading to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and worse outcomes (IPC December 2020). Mvolo is among other counties of Western Equatoria, where flooding in August 2020 damaged first-season harvests and displaced thousands of people, coupled with reduced access to inputs, localized floods and desert locust damage, portray a dire situation in 2021 (FEWNET Outlook August 2020). Further, deteriorating currency has affected the livelihood of most households as the prices of most food commodities becoming expensive in the local markets such as Magwi, Mvolo and Mundri. Coupled with insecurity incidents along key supply routes is likely to lead to more criminality especially along key roads (WFP Market Price Monitoring Reports, 2020). 2 - Project activities

Prepositioning through Cluster Pipeline and distribution of agricultural livelihood kits - crop, vegetable and fishing kits as well as farming tools to 10,000 households to facilitate emergency food production and rehabilitation of livelihoods. 2. Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. Desert locusts/ FAW surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication 3. Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security and multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality 4. Provision of livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns targeting 100,000 livestock including training and equipping of 120 community animal health workers to reduce livestock mortalities, morbidities and increase productivity and access to animal source foods 6. Installation and maintenance of six cold chain facilities for livestock vaccines storage.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD staff and associated personnel have signed a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on thei rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project including throughout implementation in addition to formation of implementation committees to spearhead beneficiary messaging on the project including collection of feedback and complaints that will inform implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration.
