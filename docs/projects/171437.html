Project Summary

In a context characterized by prolonged strain and economic isolation-de-development-, vulnerable families, including those with a large number of children, female-headed households, and families with people with disabilities, have adopted different coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities. The deteriorating socio-economic condition in Gaza has a negative consequence on children’s rights and their ability to access education and protection services as manifested in an increase of school drop-outs and child labor (Tdh, 2020; OCHA, 2020). This project involves multi-sectoral interventions that are consistent with the HRP Strategic Objectives and with the objectives of the Protection and Education Clusters and the Food Security Sector. The project aims to decrease the prevalence of child labor in the Gaza Strip and to increase children's access to the formal education system and other vocational training opportunities through the provision of child protection services, psychosocial services, educational reintegration, family support, and increased community mobilization toward the issue of child Labour and drop out of schools. Along with protecting children's rights to access to education, this project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be hazardous and could negatively affect their overall wellbeing and development. The project will target 7,150 beneﬁciaries, including 1,000 working children, dropped out of school children, or at risk of drop out (500 boys and 500 girls). The proposed activities include (1) provision of individual case management and MHPSS services to child laborers, out-of-school children, and children at high risk of drop out, (2) referral to specialized services, as needed, (3) re-integration of out of school children (75 boys and 75 girls) in the official education and vocational education, (4) provision of school stationery, uniforms, and transportation to children who have accessibility and affordability issues, (5) provision of catch up classes to out of school children, (6) conducting training sessions to school staff focusing on different issues such as dealing with children with low academic achievement and how to discipline a child for misbehaving at school, (7) provision of direct access to food to 100 households for 6 months through food e-vouchers through the WFP platform, (8) provision of restricted cash-based support to 50 households to establish income-generating activities. In total, 870 persons will benefit from the food security component of this project.
Finally, training sessions to 6,000 community members, mainly caregivers, on the importance of eliminating child labor. The project team will provide services to the identiﬁed cases through the child protection centers, distributed across the Gaza Strip, one in each governorate. A high level of coordination with relevant ministries and relevant clusters, including Protection and Education. Finally, Tdh will report its intervention to the Protection cluster, UNRWA, Child Protection Area of Responsibilities Working Group, and FSS. Tdh will be actively involved in the cash-programming working group and will closely coordinate with the FSS team, relevant ministries, and all other relevant actors. The total project budget is USD 480,000. The primary cluster for this Project is the Protection Cluster.

1 - Needs

For the last decade, the socioeconomic situations in the Gaza Strip have been in steady decline. The deteriorating socio-economic condition has a negative consequence, particularly on vulnerable groups such as children, women, and people living with disabilities, as it limited their ability to access education, protection services, along with experiencing food insecurity. Considering Gaza's vulnerability, authorities have focused their efforts on preventing the spread of the COVID-19 virus through imposing contaminant measures that involved closing schools, tightly controlling the movement in and out of Gaza, and closing all public spaces. The pandemic is expected to have wide-reaching economic and social impacts; earlier this year, the Palestinian Ministry of Social Development (2020) estimates that at least 53,000 families have fallen into poverty in recent weeks, due to the loss of a source of income. In Gaza, the share of poor households is expected to reach 64%. According to reports from the World Bank (2020), the pandemic has pushed a greater number of households into poverty and food insecurity, with an increase of at least 10% in the current poverty rate which is more than 53%. Consistently, preliminary findings of an internal assessment conducted by Tdh (October 2020), revealed that the current containment measures have contributed to an increase in the level of poverty, violence against children, increase in the number of children displaying symptoms of distress, limit affordability of securing basic life necessities, and limit learning opportunities for poor children. In such a context, vulnerable families, including those with a large number of children, families with an unemployed head of household, female-headed households, have adopted different coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities (OCHA, 2020; Protection Cluster, 2020). The most commonly used coping mechanisms are reducing the quality and quantity of consumed food, sending children to work, and withdrawing children from schools (Tdh 2019; Tdh 2020). The COVID-19 pandemic is substantially impacting the education system, the well-being of school children, and the learning environment of children and young people. The Education Cluster-oPt estimates 489,009 children need educational assistance, including of whom 9,780 are children living with a disability (September 2020). Recently, Tdh has conducted a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) to assess the impact of COVID-19 on vulnerable children and their families. The study involved collecting primary data from 603 households beneﬁting from Tdh projects (352 households) and the Cash Transfer Program of the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) (251 households). The Assessment has covered the impact of COVID-19 on different areas, mainly child protection, coping mechanisms, education, and food security. The findings of the RNA have shown that only 35.9% of vulnerable families indicated that their children followed education programs at home since March 2020. The main causes of such a low percentage are limited availability of laptops, smartphones, limited affability to pay for an internet connection, and lack of student’s interest (Tdh, 2020). Additionally, Tdh and partners work has revealed an increase in the number of children who require casemanagement and MHPSS services mainly due to an increase in the prevalence of distress among children, an increase in the violence against children, along with an increase in the number of children engaged in child labor and in the number of children being out-of-school or at risk of. The number of identified cases of child laborers has significantly increased after the emerge of the COVID-19 pandemic compared to previous years (Tdh, 2020). The most affected groups at children aged between 10 to 16 years old. In general, children with disabilities and girls are more subjected to GBV however, boys are at higher risk to be engaged in child labor. Access of vulnerable children to comprehensive child protection services that involve the provision of individual case management needs to be enhanced. Additionally, activities that could empower their families like cash-assistance, and food vouchers. Under this Project, Tdh and partners will continue to provide protection services to vulnerable children mainly out-of-school and working children. Additionally, under the Action, the needs of children living with different types of disabilities will be met through the provision of protection and educational services provided by the Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children.

2 - Activities

Identiﬁcation of vulnerable working and dropped out children is a priority for the project team, the aim is to identify about new 1000 working, out of school children, or at risk of drop out distributed as 500 boys and 500 girls. The process of identifying children will involve conducting ﬁeld visits to working areas and the most deprived areas in Gaza. It will also involve visiting governmental schools and obtaining a list of dropped out of school children. Data obtained from the government schools will be validated by home visits, case by case, as the reliability and validity of MoEHE data on drop out of school children is not high. Concerning working girls, special attention will be dedicated to identifying them through home visits. Main activities under the Protection Cluster will include (1) provision of individual case management and MHPSS services to child laborers, out-of-school children, and children at high risk of drop out, (2) referral to specialized services, as needed. The main activities under the Education Cluster will include (1) re-integration of out of school children in the official education and vocational education, (2) provision of school stationery, uniforms, and transportation to children who have accessibility and affordability issues, (3) provision of catch up classes to out of school children, and (4) conducting training sessions to school staff focusing on different issues such as dealing with children with low academic achievement and how to discipline a child for misbehaving at school. The main activities under the Food Security Sector are (1) provision of direct access to food to 100 households for 6 months through food e-vouchers through the WFP platform and (2) provision of cash-based support to 50 households. In total, 870 persons will benefit from the food security component of this project. From Tdh experience and the recent RAN-2020, the average household size for vulnerable children (8.7 persons per household) is higher than the average of the Gaza Strip (5.5 persons per household), (Tdh, 2020). The cash-based support will be conditional to reintegrating out of school children into schools or vocational training centers and not to engage children in any form of child labor. The cash-based support will be restricted to establish a micro project. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to select projects that have short-life cycles such as mini-markets to quickly generate income. At least 10 female-headed households will be targeted and priority will be given to families who have children disability and chronic diseases. The project interventions will be carried out by Tdh and 4 partners. Tdh will liaise with MoSD, Protection and Education Clusters & FSS to provide inclusive quality protection services. The targeted beneficiaries, including children and adults from different age groups and genders, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project by identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according to the specific needs of children, taking into account their gender and their age groups. Over the past years, Tdh and the partners' team have identified a good number of child laborers and out-of-school children aged 7 years as such children were not enrolled at school at age of 6. Tdh has also identified cases that will not be enrolled/have not yet enrolled in schools due to the absence of a birth certificate. Tdh works closely with UNRWA to make sure to issue a birth certificate for such children. It is unfortunate that the main cause that prevents families from issuing the birth certificate is the limited financial ability to pay the registration fee and the accumulated penalty fee of not registration children earlier.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh and partners will fully respect, protect, and fulfill beneficiaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh and partners will achieve this by protecting beneficiaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and Id numbers. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data that could reveal any identification data, all publications/reports will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneficiaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access to humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment that maintains and respect beneficiaries' rights, including respecting and maintaining their privacy and not demeaning them. Tdh and partners have zero-tolerance for exposing beneficiaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measures will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Codeof Conduct, and condential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP,PSEA, and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identiﬁed cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and ability status will receive protection services according to their assessed and identiﬁed needs. All Project beneﬁciaries will have equal access to services, including beneﬁciaries with disabilities and beneﬁciaries living in the Access Restricted Areas. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. To overcome access problems related to COVID-19 mitigation measures, Tdh and partner will ensure access to children living with disabilities through obtaining lists of children with disabilities from organizations that provide services to children with disabilities. Also, Tdh and partners have already a list of children with disabilities in need or previously beneﬁted from MHPSS services. Those children will be contacted using different blended methodologies that involve in-person modalities and remote modalities. If needed, Tdh and Partner might conduct MHPSS services in places that are accessible to children with disabilities that could involve home visits and provision of services in grassroots organizations across the Gaza Strip. Also, Tdh and partners will develop the MHPSS manual that could be used by children with disabilities themselves. Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children has already developed such tools for children with hearing disabilities. The proposed activities are based on the different needs assessment done by the Tdh and partner team in which beneﬁciaries engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneﬁciaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews. Beneﬁciaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as with any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products.
