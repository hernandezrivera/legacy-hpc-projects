Project Summary

The project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable IDP families in Banadir and Galgaduud to mitigate risks faced through Shelter/NFI interventions such as timely provision of 8000 NFIs including appropriate provision of 3500 ESKs to IDPs. The most vulnerable beneficiaries will be drawn from the areas with the support of community support groups and owing to their needs, the project aims at improving their living conditions by providing decent housing and other useful materials for shelter and NFIs. The people living in the camps are prone to protection related incidents, it is therefore prudent to enhance their lives particularly women and girls whose rights are violated. Moreover, Community priorities, demands and needs in terms of shared infrastructure are addressed through construction of new facilities or rehabilitation of existing community structures that can benefit an estimated 20,000 persons. Post Construction Monitoring/Post Distribution Monitoring (PCM/PDM) exercise in this project is aimed at providing feedback on the effectiveness of the distribution and construction. Response and Feedback Mechanism (RFM) established with the affected populations in this project provides accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the communities with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. The proposed intervention will create synergies with existing Protection, CCCM, WASH projects in districts in Benadir and Galgaduud and focusing on access to dignified basic services in IDP hosting sites/communal facilities, 8000 benefecires will receive Household Non-Food Items (NFIs) through the voucher modality 3500 of them benefiting from Emergency shelter kits simultaneously.

Project Activities

It is expected that a total of 28,000 beneficiaries in Benadir region and Galgaduud state will receive the NFIs. Provision of emergency assistance packages to the newly displaced persons and benefit from community structures/facilities which is aimed at improving the living conditions of the IDPs. Provision of 8000 NFI kits to the vulnerable newly displaced members in the IDP camps, 3500 of them will be provided with Emergency Shelter Kits from the local markets. These households will be selected based on vulnerability basis that will ensure transparency and effectiveness in the whole process. In Banadir the ESKs will be subjected to the Statutory/legislative access rights to land is advocated for with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction and permission obtained before constructing ESKs on any land or property, in writing, where possible. Technical staff will consider the appropriateness of the layout of the shelter to leave adequate spaces for other necessary facilities like roads, water places, latrines etc. This will improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable persons and ensure sustainability.Construction/rehabilitation of community infrastructure. Community’s priorities, demands and needs in terms of shared infrastructure is addressed through construction of new facilities or rehabilitation of existing facilities. Community mobilizers will hold focus group discussions at IDP camps with SMS and/or protection to gather feedback and infrastructure prioritization from the community perspective. Approximately 20,000 beneficiaries will benefit from comm infrastructure that be constructed or rehabilitated in the project sites.This will be done by consultation.Post Construction Monitoring/Post Distribution Monitoring (PCM/PDM) is conducted and the exercise will be provide beneficiary feedback on the effectiveness of the distribution. 70 % of the beneficiaries will take part in the exercise to ensure proper representation and exact feeling is recorded about the process and quality of the distribution/construction.Community Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) established with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. Accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct by placing feedback surveys in NFI kits, feedback box at distribution site and phone lines. The project will use the voucher assistance model in its distribution of non food items and emergency shelter kits. This is the most appropriate modality in distribution because the markets operating and accessible and the items to be distributed are available in the local markets. With in-kind distributions the agency bears the cost and hassle of procuring and transporting the goods to the final distribution point. With cash and voucher distributions the cost of procurement and transport is the responsibility of the trader. When planning and implementing the resource transfer programme, consideration will be given to how the form of resource transfer interacts with the power dynamics of the household. In the event of preparing vouchers gender balance can be maintained. In the voucher asssitance SSWC will assess the capacity of local traders and markets putting in place agreements with local traders. Vouchers will be prepared and distributed ensuring that the vouchers are redeemed at vendors points in accordance with the programme guidelines. Payment will be made for confirmed redeemed vouchers.

Mitigation Measures

Protection risks likely to impact this project and affected communities can be2(a) Exclusion and marginalization where marginalized community’s/ minority groups might not be included in consultations and/or excluded from humanitarian assistance. To mitigate this, SSWC will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Shelter/NFI staff and committees will be representative of all groups (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) as they play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and will be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. The project will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups noting which group may have greater difficulty finding accommodation or more prone to eviction. A systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries for distributions will be used otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered and finally SSWC will ensure that NFI packages include suitable items to cover the specific needs of women, girls, older persons (e.g. sanitary pads, adult diapers) and provide additional clothing and blankets for the frail and weak.3(b) Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance is another protection risk. SSWC will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. Central locations will be identified so that female beneficiaries will not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points to prevent the likelihood of sexual violence as well as making sure distributions take place in daylight hours giving female beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Distribution points will also not be placed near military installations to prevent threats such as attacks, stray bullets, and crossfire or conflict related sexual violence (CRSV) and weapon bearers will not be present inside distribution points as well. Additionally, appropriate design and construction of shelters will preserve the safety and dignity of the affected population; Space arrangements, shelter structures and distance between dwellings will respect privacy and cultural norms between men and women and also allow for family unity. Partitions and door locks will also protect women and girls, particularly single women and female headed-households, from risks of exploitation and abuse. Moreover, provision of solar lamps/torches for each household in settings where lighting of communal areas is not available will provide security for women. 4(b)Exposure to violence during registration/distribution is another protection mainstreaming risk likely to impact the project and SSWC will consult both displaced and host communities about shelter and NFI needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities in the initial stages of the project. SSWC will also work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising due to crowded shelter conditions. Distribution points will not be near military installations to prevent threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Likewise, weapon bearers will not be present inside distribution points. Systematic outreach process will be used in registering beneficiaries for distributions and SSWC will make sure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution.
