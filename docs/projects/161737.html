Project Summary

In 2018, a total of 8.8 million children died before the age of five and half of them are in Sub-Saharan Africa due to preventable disease such as Pneumonia, diarrhea, malaria, HIV/AIDS, cholera and measles . These diseases caused up to 44% of deaths in under five children. Although the impact of WASH related infectious disease episodes and deaths is disproportionately felt among the populations of low- and middle-income countries. (Public health to infectious diseases, 2016). However WASH related/Infectious diseases and humanitarian crises frequently go hand in hand. Cholera, malaria, malnutrition, hepatitic -E, trachoma, schistosomiasis, worm infestation, HIV-Aids and measles are some of the diseases that pose a serious threat for internally displaced persons (IDPs) in camps and host communities. These opportunities allows for emergence and spread of WASH related infectious diseases. For example, the forced displacement in Borno state communities has exacerbate transmission and/or introduced preventable WASH related disease to new areas. Disruption of WASH facilities and migration of the people from the crisis zone have led to shortages of medication, reagents and trained medical personnel, as well as breakdown in routine health services such as vaccination. Destruction of infrastructure can not only reduce direct access to health services and care, but can create new environmental conditions and habitats that facilitate the spread of vector-borne and zoonotic diseases. (Center for Global Health Science and Security,2018) Furthermore, the crisis in Borno state have contributed and exacerbate the emergence of disease out break that are WASH related. Therefore, the need to increase awareness, sensitization and some preventive measures and control among the IDPs in camps and host communities in order to promote healthy living. The total population of Borno is 5,445,607, out of which IDPs are numbering up to 1,104,812 and the people in need are up to 2,599,925. Among which Women and girls were most affected (Health sector, Nigeria 2019). SAHEI will educate and sensitized the displaced affected people on safe WASH practices, build capacity of health worker on WASH related diseases prevention, conduct house to house visit to remind them of safe practices that would make them live healthy and would refer patients for treatments if found during sensitization. Profile data for sector coordination and information management. The organization will also collaborate and work with surveillance and risk communication group to response, control, mitigate /prevent outbreaks of these devastating infectious diseases among the internally displaced people in camps and host communities to better understand infectious WASH related diseases transmission and preventive measures. This will go a long way in promoting their health.

1 - Needs

Water sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities install in host communities are over stretched because of the influx of the returnees to their various LGAs or safer zones. Moreover, some of the houses in host communities lack proper toilets and latrines in camps are filled and need to be dislodged.Thus, a lot of open defecation and poor hygiene practices are rampart and common. Such behavior may lead to outbreak of WASH related diseases such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) especially among children which has become a thing of concern even now. Currently it is one of the prevalent diseases affecting both returnees and IDPs children in host communities and camps. Record from the Health facility/clinics in camps and host communities has shown that, source (EoC, 2019, Maiduguri). Cholera which is already endemic in the state since 2015 is causing morbidity and mortality among both children and adults. The internally displaced peoples (IDPs) have become so vulnerable and susceptible to WASH related diseases during these years of insurgency. Inspired of the commitment by Government, UN agencies and other donor and funding agencies toward Health promotion activities. There’s still huge gap that needs to be filled. Majority of infectious diseases are Water and sanitation related and can be prevented. However, needs assessment has shown that people lack knowledge on basic health education and prevention and control of water and sanitation related diseases, therefore SAHEI has identified needs of these communities hosting IDPs and returnees; That basically they need to be educated on health promotion that relate to WASH infectious diseases prevention; such as Cholera, Malaria, Yellow fever, Warm infestation, Schistosomiasis, Hepatitis-E, Vomiting, Trachoma and Diarrhea in these selected local governments and therefore needs quick intervention in which this project will aim to address through the following objectives:To establish, expand and strengthen the communicable disease surveillance, outbreak prevention, control and response. To strengthen health sector coordination, information management , health system restoration and strengthening linkages through Humanitarian-Development Nexus (HDN), with an emphasis on enhancing protection and increasing access to health care services.

2 - Activities

SAHEI will carry out the following activities during project implementation:1. Training for health workers from various health facilities in those communities on WASH related diseases that could be transmitted to patients while in the hospital and general safe hygiene practices in the health facilities..2. Data sharing with information management unit to strengthen humanitarian development nexus3. Distribution of aqua tablets and water quality monitoring / testing.4. Awareness raising and sensitization on prevention and control measures to community leaders and stakeholders in host communities.5. Advocacy calls to community leaders and stakeholders for support to educate their domains on prevention and control measures of infectious/ WASH related diseases.6. Provision of hand washing stand/hand washing demonstration to youth, women, men and children in communities.7. Training health community volunteers and traditional birth attendance on prevention and control measures of infectious/hygiene related diseases during delivery, most especially home delivery.8. Monitoring and Evaluation to ensure indicators are met.9. collaborate and working closely with partners on ground and the state surveillance team.
