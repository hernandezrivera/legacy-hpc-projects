Project Summary

The project will implement activities as per the following description to achieve the targeted objectives for all women, men, girls, and boys (including all age groups and persons with disabilities) in the targeted Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Access to WASH services will be achieved through the three sub-sectors of intervention that are presented below:
Improved water services: IOM will continue the Operation & Maintenance (O&M) and upgrade the existing deep tube wells and water networks in camps 09, 10, 11, 12, 18, 20, and 20 extensions.
In the targeted area, non-repairable deep tube wells will be decommissioned, and new deep tube wells will be installed where necessary to ensure improved access to the most vulnerable people in need and meet humanitarian standards.
5 small-scale solar-powered piped water networks will be newly installed in camp 9, 13, and 20Extn. to provide safe water to the targeted beneficiaries. Moreover, the existing solarized water supply systems in camps 09, 12, and 13 will be upgraded or rehabilitated where necessary.
Access to Sanitation: To ensure dignified and safe access to the latrine facilities, IOM will continue to upgrade the existing poorly functional latrine and bathing facilities with gender-inclusive and disability-related features in consultation with the community. IOM will provide assistive devices (i.e., commode chairs, commode chairs with potty, urinal bottles for males and females, etc.) to people with disabilities or physical impairments to ensure their specific needs are met based on the assessment. Moreover, IOM action will continue the construction of durable household latrines to cover the sanitation gaps at the targeted host community villages in Ukhiya.
Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems (DEWATS) will be built, and lime-based faecal sludge treatment plants will be upgraded to the DEWATS system to meet the coverage gaps. In addition, IOM will continue to build 14 Intermediate Sludge Transfer Systems (ISTS) connecting with the FSM units to ensure cost-effective sludge transfer mechanisms in targeted areas. In addition, IOM will collaborate with UNHCR and DPHE to link the existing faecal sludge treatment approach to the mega FSTP built by UNHCR and DPHE for easy operation to address recommendations of WASH Sector FSM Strategy and mega rationalization.
To ensure appropriate waste segregation at the source and engage the community effectively in the chain of solid waste management (SWM), IOM will ensure garbage bins (red and green) in each household. Existing material recovery facilities (MRF) operation and maintenance will be ensured, and a new MRF will be installed. The complete chain of the SWM monitoring system will be continued and strengthened at all levels.
Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials, etc.) will be provided through community mobilization activities targeting all age groups, genders, and persons with special WASH needs. IOM will continue the utilization of the SCOPE distribution system in all camps to ensure duplication is avoided and a systematic distribution is ensured for people in need. Sector-standard MHM kits will be distributed to the reproductive age group (10–55 years) based on the field assessment and needs across all the IOM-managed camps every six months. The community will be further engaged in O&M of WASH facilities, waste segregation, prevention of mass transmission, and waterborne diseases like Acute Water Diseases (AWD), scabies, COVID-19, etc.
Notably, IOM will procure, replenish, and replace the emergency stocks for cyclones, fires, floods, or other emergency responses, and real-time service monitoring with community feedback mechanisms will be continued and strengthened throughout the project period.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Water: There are a total of 80 water networks in IOM AoR, serving 76% of the population, although equitable access has not been assessed and assured. According to the IOM WASH facility monitoring dashboard (September 2023), a total of 59% of tap stands were found functional, 38% had repair needs with various critical issues but were functional, and 3% were completely non-functional.It has also been found that 18% (source: IOM water quality monitoring database) of the water networks do not comply with sector standards on FRC presence at distribution points. Frequent repair and maintenance of hand-pumps is necessary to keep the water supplies for meeting the daily needs of the targeted beneficiaries. It was experienced during the fire outbreak at Camp 11 that a rapid provision of water supply or a viable source of drinking water with supplemented Aquatab distribution is vital. Sanitation: Latrine coverage at a camp level should provide access at 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but data shows that at least 4 of the 11 IOM AOR camps {9 (1:40), 10 (1:36), 11 (1:41), and 18 (1:36) } are unable to facilitate the necessary minimum ratio (the current average is 1:23) due to the high population density.Current functionality has an average of 98% and is a continual process for partners. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in the privatization of bathing spaces, with 24% of households reporting private bathing spaces inside (source: the IOM PDM survey October’23). Often, those spaces are unhygienic, poorly maintained, or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with a majority not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence, with 95% preferring private bathing spaces (Source: IOM PDM Oct’23). A continual concern regarding sanitation is the coverage of fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements, or scale-up. Solid Waste Management (SMW) remains one of the main challenges, as only 70% of the refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated area (source: KAP survey's April 2023). 43% of households report having both red and green waste bins for organic and non-organic segregation. (Source: KAP survey’ April 2023)It's worth mentioning that, according to the IOM KAP survey's April 2023 findings, 18% of HH don’t have any waste bins and manage waste with polyethylene. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Community engagement strategies will focus on changing identified high-risk behaviors through participatory approaches and capacity building such as safe water usage and solid waste containment, reducing waste entering the surrounding environment. According to the IOM KAP survey report, although 97% of respondents were able to cite three critical hand washing times, only 47% of HH have access to handwashing devices, which is a barrier to strengthened hygienic practice and follow-up. IOM would like to ensure that people who are in need of clean water, soap, and hygiene products aim to reduce their public health risk. IOM would also like to continue working with health, education, and other sectors to find out the needs and causes of diseases linked to safe water or poor hygiene. IOM will utilize the existing established market system to get the supplies, such as soap, water containers, and MHM kit items, and will ensure delivery of the supplies on time, with good quality and enough quantity.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"IOM is working very closely with the WASH Sector in the rationalization exercise to achieve cost efficiency in its WASH operation.IOM started and will continue to engage NGO’s who managed to raise funds to provide comprehensive WASH services for the targeted beneficiaries. In October 2023, IOM engaged a new NGO partner to provide WASH services in 2 camps. IOM aims to engage more NGO partners to provide WASH services in 2024. Through this process, IOM will reduce its current caseloads by 30% in the 2024 JRP, which is a significant achievement towards the forthcoming rationalization plan. IOM however will continue to serve as the area-focal-agency of the camps under its WASH AoR to ensure the quality of WASH services. IOM will continue to deploy 1 CFP (camp focal point) in each camp to monitor the service quality and facilitate the camp-level coordination mechanism. In addition, IOM will continue to provide WASH NFI to all beneficiaries within IOM AoR, as well as provide necessary technical guidance to implementing NGO partners. IOM will also coordinate and closely liaison with DPHE to extend support for building major-scale WASH infrastructures to meet the comprehensive services to secure a healthier environment within the Rohingya refugee camps. The funds saved by engaging NGO’s to oversee the operations and maintenance of WASH facilities shall be used to support the distribution of WASH NFI’s.IOM will intensify community engagement in the coming year, strengthen the technical skills of WASH user groups and block-level WASH committees, and equip them with the necessary equipment. Consequently, the community will be skilled enough to do repairs to the WASH facilities themselves and will ensure the functionality of the facilities in the long run. In this way, community ownership and sustainability will be enhanced.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection: Being beneficiaries of a crowded distribution mechanism is always a challenge for women, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Considering the difficulties in accessing the centralized distribution centers and the risk of exposure to potential negative impacts such as threats and robbery (do no harm), IOM will continue distributing all the NFI WASH items to the household level by using the SCOPE system. During the project design and implementation phase, IOM will ensure safe and dignified access to resources by ensuring service is community-preferred, feedback is addressed, and cultural norms are respected. It's worth mentioning that, at all stages of latrine and bathing facility construction, upgrade, or rehabilitation, IOM and its partner will ensure that the facility is safely accessible to all targeted groups, regardless of their age, gender, sexual orientation, and ethnicity. Gender: IOM will be improving and upgrading the WASH facilities to ensure equitable access by women, men, girls, and boys, inclusive of all age groups and persons with disabilities, in consultation with the users. In consultation with WASH actors, IOM action will be continued to form and strengthening the MHM user committees at the camp level to address the accurate needs of MHM kit content, such as quality of undergarments, sizes, pads, packing modalities, etc Diversity: IOM and implementing partners will be working to build the capacity of female and male staff and community members on hygiene promotion and practice monitoring to ensure the needs of women, men, boys, girls, LGBTI, the elderly, and persons with special needs. IOM and implementing partners will continue working on environmental protection through capacity building of diverse population and integrated ways of solid waste management, for instance, door-to-door waste collection, compost production, support plantation programs, wrap-less soap distribution, etc.

3.3 - Age & Disability

IOM will continue to accommodate unified disability and elderly features during wash facility construction and upgrade, which will increase access to and practice of improved sanitation. Implementing partners will continue reaching children and different age groups to strengthen hygiene practices at all levels through different innovative modalities. IOM and implementing partners will continue working to enable equitable and appropriate access to all WASH services for all age groups.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM will continue its actions to ensure that all IOM WASH staff and IP staff are skilled in accountability to the affected population (AAP) and involve the communities in all aspects of WASH program design and implementation. The established complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) system will continue to receive complaints from the affected community. All complaints will be responded to within the standard timeline by IOM WASH staff and IP staff. Community consultation will be strengthened, and access to information by the community will be enhanced in different ways (like putting up billboards, setting up information desks, etc.) in all stages of the program.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

To reduce Gender Based Violence (GBV) and enhance protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, IOM and its partners will ensure WASH staff, volunteers, and hygiene promoters are trained on gender sensitivity and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). SEA referral pathways will be strengthened by providing appropriate responses within the set timeline. To prevent the potential risk of being a victim of sexual abuse, IOM and implementing agencies will ensure consultation with users, especially women and girls; and will construct privacy fencing to ensure the safety and privacy of the users.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, IOM will upgrade 5 existing water supply systems in camps 9, 13, and 20Extn. to the solarized system to reduce fuel consumption and, at the same time, reduce the negative impact on the environment. Solid waste management is one of the key priorities in camps to minimize negative impacts on the environment and keep the camp environment clean and healthy for the camp dwellers. Implementing partners will continue operating MRF to manage solid waste. Continuous household waste collection and transfer to the MRF site will be ensured; organic waste will be converted into compost, inorganic waste sent to sanitary landfills, and recyclable waste sent to scrap dealers. Compost will be distributed to camp dwellers and the host community for homestead gardening or vegetation. IOM will continue testing influent and effluent from fecal sludge treatment facilities (FSTP) and will take corrective action to meet the standards.
