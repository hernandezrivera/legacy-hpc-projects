Project Summary

7 MMUs shall aim to provide the basic minimum primary health care services free of charge in 7 Districts in both Idleb and Aleppo Governorates in NWS. Almost 285880 individuals in 53772 families in the outlaying areas are planned to be reached throughout BINAA MMUs. The MMUs will be linked to the nearby PHC and hospitals supported by partners recognised by the health cluster/Turkey Hub.
The MMUs will be functional to provide the services in the same targeted areas of which BINAA provides Nutrition services, and will complement the interventions required for maternal, neonates and infant’s health and nutrition, and will work for:
Advocate for the immunization of children in hard-to-reach areas of under 5 years old children. Ensure that the vaccination referrals cards are provided to track for follow-up doses.
Ante-natal care for pregnant women and referral of all pregnancies, especially women at high risk for complication, to the nearest PHCs for specialist cares, laboratory investigations and ultrasounds. Provide information of supported hospitals for delivery in order to make delivery plans by the pregnant women.
Family planning services (contraceptives and condoms) and referral to PHCs for IUD insertion. MMU staff will counsel the women in reproductive age on various modern contraceptive methods for them to make an informed choice of family planning methods.
Growth monitoring among under 5 years old children and record in the growth monitoring chart. The standardized MOH growth monitoring chart will be used.
Nutritional screening by measuring MUAC among under 5 years old children. Malnourished cases identified in MMU will be referred to designated PHCs for detailed nutritional assessment (weight for height, weight for age and height for age etc.) and treatment by trained health personnel. Pregnant and lactating women should also be assessed.
Medical consultation for common acute illnesses and dispensing acute medications free of charge. Treatment of uncomplicated infectious diseases (ARIs, watery diarrhea, dysentery etc.)

4 - Needs

159 camps out of 182 targeted ones in 39 Communities are not reached with health services as per the last update shared within ISIMM Plus report. 425,716 individuals are in need for health services in the targeted camps, and 19,983 of them are people with disability.The provision of the services in the targeted areas is very critical due to COVID-19 pandemic, the economic downturn in Syria, and the heavy hostilities to the health care centers, that had exacerbated weaknesses in the functionality of almost 48% of the primary health care centers.Disrupted medication supply chains, were previously identified as a key gap in the health response and a contributor of child mortality. In order to promote quality, accountable health services, including limited health care staff, there is a need to support primary health service packages that guarantee comprehensive reproductive health care including first line response to GBV; child health and routine immunization services; integrated maternal health and nutrition programming; management of NCDs and mental health conditions; diagnostic and screening services; and availability of essential medicines. In areas lacking health governance, leadership and resources for health facility operations are required for response.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to support mobile medical units by provision access to healthcare for the most vulnerable children under the age of 5, neonates and pregnant and lactating women through lifesustaining interventions at the facility and community level (MISP). reaching neonates with quality health and nutrition services,,< 5 children with targeted health and nutritional interventions, pregnant lactating women with RH service. Additionally, the project will provide access to essential primary healthcare in the Northern West of Syria, the services includes: (outpatient consultations, NCDs, antenatal care, reproductive health), in addition to the referrals to essential secondary care services of (medications provided/prescribed, diagnostic procedures, medical interventions including dialysis) for acutely vulnerable boys, girls, men and women in need for mental health and PSS (cross reference with Protection Cluster). The MMUs will be functional and supplied with the required medicines, medical supplies, PPE, IPC items, and with qualified health workers. M&E framework will ensure optimal provision stabilization services of emergency health conditions (e.g. severe diarrhoea with dehydration, suspected pneumonia, injuries, bleeding, high fever, convulsions and severe hypertension, SAM cases etc.) and immediate referrals to nearest PHCs or hospitals after stabilization. Dispensing of ORS sachets to parents., Identification of potentials of CD outbreaks and report immediately on alerts to the MoH. Provision of health education / personal hygiene promotion, propritizing PWD in the provided services with linkage to the specialized nearby care providers.The 7 MMUs will be recognised by HC/Turkey Hub throughout HeRAMs codes.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks againsthealthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA)1 in 2020 fell by 67 per cent compared to 2019. Mitigating measures: - Applying the designated de-conflictionmechanism for health facilities, and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). -Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare. - Reporting from relevant hubsregarding health situation in reconciled areas. Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate therequired care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical thathealth providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory,quality health services. Mitigating measures: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence. Accordingly, affected population should haveaccess to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package. -Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support. - Identification ofsafe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connectinghealth services and clinical management with social services, and privacy of the survivor. -Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to healthservices.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

During the course of the work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community.Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploitand abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcareproviders should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both onorganizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describesstandard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbiddingsexual. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about theorganization. - Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartialassessment of needs and risks.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- The supply chains are maintained for the delivery of adequate logistic supplies.- Qualified staff are available for contraction.- The targeted communities to accept BINAA and the planned intervention.- Safety and security conditions remain stable in the areas of implementation.Risks:• Shortages on the required qualified staff.• Challenges to provide the required supplies.• The cross-border operations are suspended.• Safety and security conditions deteriorate.•COVID-19 outbreakMitigations:.• BINAA will ensure adequate level of coordination with Idleb health directorate to mobilize the required HRs to the MMUs.• BINAA have already assessed the availability of the requested items within both Turkey and Syria. Also, BINAA will ensure high level of coordination and collaboration with Turkish ministry of health MoH for the facilitation of obtaining the required approvals, procuring the needed items and delivering them through the cross-border into Syria. Also, BINAA can make use of the local markets within Syria in case the provision of the needed items from Turkey is not feasible for some reason.• BINAA will ensure high level of coordination with the border-crossing authorities to promote the needed access. Also, BINAA will seek to provide the majority, if not all, of the required items from the very beginning of the project implementation to avoid any unwanted cross-border related circumstances. However, in case the cross-border operations are suspended or terminated and resulted in impeding the delivery of the needed items into Syria, BINAA will consider the provision of the needed logistical supplies from within the country of implementation.
