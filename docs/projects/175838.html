Project Summary

The provision of integrated emergency lifesaving Nutrition services will target a total of 12,320 vulnerable individuals; Children U5s; 3,730 Boys, 5,047 Girls, and 3,543 Women (PLWs) through fixed sites and mobile approaches in Fangak (Jonglei), and Pariang (Unity) during 2021 to pursue Cluster objectives to decrease suffering, Mortality and Morbidity related to malnutrition in South Sudan.
Hold the Child Organization (HCO) proposes to contribute to the reduction of mortality and morbidity related to malnutrition in Pariang of Unity and Fangak of Jonglei, South Sudan in order to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition specific service delivery targeting Children below the age of five, U5s, Pregnant and Lactating children, Internally Displaced people living within communities and returnees seeking refugee provided they meet the admission criteria indicated in the South Sudan CMAM guideline adopted in 2017.
During 2021 of the project cycle, Hold the Child is anticipated to utilize 428,960 $; this project will contribute to the 4 cluster objective; (1) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2021;
Contribute to the reduction of mortality, mortality and child suffering from protection threat through increasing access and equitable utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery through SAM and MAM for Children U5s and pregnant & lactating mothers (PLWs) meeting the admission criteria will be enrolled into the CMAM program, Community Mobilization through the involvement of Community Nutrition Volunteers, Mother to Mother Support Groups who will be used to support Vitamin A and Deworming supplementation campaigns during National Immunization Days, (NIDS), Active and timely identification of nutrition status through active surveillance to monitor Proxy GAMs, and Community Health Workers; Cluster Objective (2) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
Provision of special nutritious foods, Maternal infant Young Child Nutrition, Micro nutrients supplementation and behavioral change communication approach; (ii) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment; Active and passive screen for malnutrition at community level through the community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition Promoter focusing on the prioritized locations of Pariang (Unity) and Fangak (Jonglei) by the end of 2021.
Cluster Objective 3; to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2021;

1 - Needs

According to IPC analysis of January 2020, 5.29 million people (45.2% of the population) are estimated to have faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.11 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. About 40,000 people were classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the counties of Akobo, Duk and Ayod in Jonglei State. In Jan 2020, about 1.3 million children will need nutrition assistance in the country, including about 300,000 and 1.0 million children projected to be severely and moderately malnourished, respectively. It is, therefore, necessary to adopt a paradigm shift and move from clinical and crisis approach to preventive and community-based approach based on the results of the food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys trends for 2019.Compared with the same time last year, the January 2020 levels of food insecurity reflect a 9% reduction in the proportion of population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The cumulative effects of flooding and associated population displacements, Covid-19 Pandemic, lack of equipped health care, WASH facilities, localized insecurity, the economic crisis, and prolonged years of asset depletion continue to drive the high levels of acute food insecurity in the country. Low crop production is also a contributing factor, with the 2019 cropping season production meeting 63% of the 2020 national cereal needs (comparatively, 2018 cereal production met 57% of the 2019 national cereal needs). Isolated insecurity incidents displace populations, disrupt livelihoods and impede households’ access to other food sources, such as wild foods, fish, and livestock products. The high food prices and continued currency depreciation have also consistently reduced the purchasing power of vulnerable households who are reliant on market purchases for their food and other basic needs.In the projection period of February to April 2020, 6.01 million people (51.4% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 20,000 people in the counties of Akobo and Duk estimated to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) neighboring with Fangak County. In the projection period of May to July 2020, 6.48 million people (55.4% of the population) will face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, which is 5% lower than was projected for the 2019 lean season.In January 2020, 15 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with Greater Upper Nile region having 12 (Longochuk, Maban, Maiwut and Ulang of Upper Nile State; and Akobo, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor and Uror of Jonglei State); Greater Bahr el Ghazal region having 2 (Rumbek North of Lakes State, and Aweil North of Northern Bahr el Ghazal); and Greater Equatoria region having 1 (Kapoeta North of Eastern Equatoria State).The proxy prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among under-five children ranges from 1.3 per cent to 33.4 above the emergency threshold of 19.59% Fangak and 20.27% Pariang. Pariang County has reportedly increasing number of Covid-19 cases with high infection rate; IDSR report Week 5. February 2021. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Hold the Child’s progressive evidence based programing projects 2020 intervention in communities (Pariang and Fangak) which have recently been characterized with GAM rates above the WHO threshold of 15% and the need to Scale up and improve access to basic services: WASH and health service delivery year-round; plus emergency nutrition, especially during the lean season; and Close monitoring of at-risk counties where food insecurity is high, with populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and above

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This project will ensure PSEA related incidents will be mandatory through the project cycle. All staff, associates and volunteers shall benefit from regular and refresher training on child safeguarding and PSEA, do no harm, psychological first aid, and risk assessment. Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan is facing; through this protect specific measures and activities will be tailored. Through our child safeguarding policy and other related policies like PSEA will be maintained and always will be referred to for guidance. All staff must sign a code of conduct before providing frontline services and engagement with beneficiaries. Through awareness of other services and referrals will all contribute to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will demonstrate accountability to affected population will be key during the project design. A set of streamlined and designed approaches will be set in place on entry and exist of the program centers. All beneficiaries with complaints will be addressed with high level of confidentiality and more inclusively. Focused group discussions, Joint evaluations, Home gardening and recipe demonstration sessions will be platforms to understand the program impact on how its meeting the beneficiary needs and objective while giving appropriate feedback. Special cases of disability will be handled on cases by case basis and priority given to those in severe situation and while encouraging safe referrals to access of essential services without discrimination. Sex segregation will be key during data collection and age break down.IEC materials shall be printed to represent a certain group of people with No education/ Learning skills and disseminate knowledge to each individual in the community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will periodically convene Focused Group Discussion, Regular visits with key stakeholders Youth leaders, Village chiefs and elders to strategically provide in depth understanding of the area and provide double feedback both to the Project and to the beneficiaries for consolidation and tailored improvement for better goals attainment.Through Inceptions, Community leaders are inclusively involved to ensure collective participation with direct support from the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through Focused Group Discussions, regular joint monitoring visits, outcomes or recommendation from such discussion will quickly be adopted within the project to acknowledge project ownership and involvement. Such forums provide tailored and real time pointers for improvement. Dialogue meeting organized at all levels shall be key to redirect the project goals.
