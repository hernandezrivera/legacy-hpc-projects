Project Summary

Purpose and overall goal:
Aligned with the 2025 health cluster objectives (C01, C02 and C03), South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan and with the Health Sector Objectives, the project seeks to provide quality health care to crisis affected people, prevent and respond to health threats and strengthen the health system in Manyo and Fashoda Counties. This will be achieved through implementing the basic Health and Nutrition package that includes maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV services to girls, boys, men and women in emergency situations with a specific focus on increasing accessibility for persons living with disability and the elderly. In collaboration with the County Health Departments, the project will support cholera response through static and mobile health activities for the targeted population in hard-to-reach locations, AAHI will support two 5 static health facilities (2 in Manyo and 3 in Fashoda), 2 integrated mobile medical units (1 in Manyo and 1 in Fashoda). AAH will also support the Boma Health Initiative in 8 Bomas of the 2 Counties. To respond to the cholera cases reported, 3 Cholera treatment centers will be established along the high risk – returnees/refugees transit areas at Kaka PHCC, Kodok Hospital and Wadakona PHCC, a multifaceted approach involving the combination of water, sanitation and hygiene, surveillance, risk communication and community engagement through social mobilization, treatment and oral cholera vaccines will be implemented.
Methodology and target:
AAHI is the lead Health partner in Manyo and Fashoda Counties implementing the South Sudan Health sector transformation project (HSTP), the proposed activities under this project will provide essential primary health care services in areas of high need and where other existing services are inadequate to avoid duplication. Activities include; cholera response, the provision of curative consultations in primary health facilities for high risk and flood affected communities; community-based treatment of common illnesses in children under five; routine vaccination; reproductive and sexual health including the provision of ANC, skilled delivery, PNC and clinical management of SGBV and mental health treatment. AAHI will primarily target children under five, girls, boys, women from both returnees, IDPs and people with disabilities and mental health conditions due to their unique vulnerability.
AAHI will build the capacity of CHD staff through mentorship, coaching, joint supportive supervision visits and training to effectively improve the quality of care. AAHI will work closely with the community leaders to prepare them for future shocks. The proposed health activities will be integrated with other humanitarian activities in targeted areas to enhance emergency referrals and access to protection and nutrition services to collectively address underlying causes of morbidity and mortality.
105,893 beneficiaries are targeted comprised of 56,358 in Fashoda (10,884 residents, 24,997 IDPs, 20,478 Returnees, 8728 Male U5, 6228 Female U5) and 49535 in Manyo (1,049 Residents, 3,828 IDPs, 44,658 Returnees, 7819 Male U5, 7895 Female U5) and 1165 people with disabilities.
The project will promote the proactive involvement and participation of local communities and health authorities which will facilitate the appropriate selection and implementation of the most acceptable, feasible and responsive intervention options focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring accountability to affected populations.

1. Needs

Between 6th – 10th November 2024 two Initial Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA) were conducted in Manyo and Fashoda Counties of Upper Nile State by Action Africa Help International (AAH-I) together with other inter-Agency Cluster Working group partners for a quick overview of the emergency situation and to determine the immediate needs of the affected population. The assessment in Manyo was triggered by the continuous influx of returnees from Sudan to Wadakona and in Fashoda it was triggered by the unprecedented rise in Nile water levels resulting into widespread flooding in Lul, Dethwok and Kodok Rural Payams of Fashoda County and disruption of services and livelihood. Flooding damaged health facilities, schools, latrines and contaminated community water sources with increasing cases of Malaria, snake bites and Cholera outbreak.Below is a summary of key findings:a) Flooding of River Nile which gradually started in September 2024 escalated in late October destroying drinking water sources, latrines, health facilities, farmlands, submerging houses & School facilities resulting into population displacements to highlands in Fashoda.b) Increasing cases of snake bites, malaria and acute watery diarrhoea which are higher than caseloads of similar duration over the past 3 years, Malaria consultations increased by 20% in comparison with same duration in 2023.c) Acute shortage of trained health care professionals and lack of essential drugs and medical supplies in flood affected areas. Health workers in the catchment health facilities had not been paid incentives for the past four months.d) Underutilization of reproductive health services particularly family planning service across the sites. This is evidenced by the low mid-year progress against indicators in the DHIS.e) Girls, women, disabled and the elderly people are particularly at higher risks of adverse effects of the crisis by virtue of gender and physical disability. Required Actions;Fashoda Countya) Support 3 static Health facilities, 1 mobile team and BHI in 4 flood affected Bomas.b) Establish cholera response with treatment center at Kodok Hospital.c) Establish river side vaccination at returnee entry points at Kodok and Lul.d) Support ambulance services for timely referrals of obstetric emergencies.e) Supply of essential drugs and medical supplies to aaddress shortages and stock out.f) Recruit additional manpower to reduce the ratio of consultations per clinician to <50 per consultation per clinician per day. Manyo Countya) Support 2 static Health facilities, 1 mobile team and BHI in 4 Bomas with returnees.b) Cholera response with treatment centers at transit areas of Kaka and Wadakona PHCC.c) Establish river side vaccination at returnee entry points at Kaka and Wadakona.d) Support ambulance services for timely referrals of obstetric emergencies.e) Supply of essential drugs and medical supplies to address shortages and stock out.f) Recruit additional manpower to reduce the ratio of consultations per clinician to <50 per consultation per clinician per day.

2. Project activities

• AAHI will support 5 static health facilities (2 in Manyo and 3 in Fashoda), 2 integrated mobile medical units (1 in Manyo and 1 in Fashoda) and Boma Health Initiative in 8 Bomas of the 2 Counties.• To respond to the cholera out break, 3 Cholera treatment centers will be established along the high risk – returnees/refugees transit areas at Kaka PHCC, Kodok Hospital and Wadakona PHCC, a multifaceted approach involving the combination of water, sanitation and hygiene, surveillance, risk communication and community engagement through social mobilization, treatment and oral cholera vaccines will be implemented.• Health worker recruitment of required to fill gaps at facility and community level to respond to the primary health care needs of the flood affected population including EPI, OPD, mobile clinics, RH, referral system, and cholera treatment center.• Health worker training on cholera response, outbreak response, Immunization in practice, Infection Prevention Control, rational use of drugs, BHI training and Malaria case management.• Support medical referrals for obstetric emergencies, suspected cholera cases among the returnees and flood affected communities.• Integrate priority reproductive health services of the MISP into PHC and make available antenatal consultations (ANC) for pregnant women, postnatal consultations (PNC) for mothers and new-born and family planning (FP).• Minor repair to health facilities to improve capacity to respond flood affected populations and returnees from Sudan including integration of Nutrition and WASH improvements in health facilities.• Ensure regular provision and supply of essential drugs, medical equipment and medical supplies to supported health facilities to mitigate shortage and lack of essential drug and improve the quality of service.• Ensure regular community, awareness and sensitization, case identification, defaulter tracing, and appropriate referrals.
