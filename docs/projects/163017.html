Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe is deteriorating, with the economic crisis (rising food prices, under-employment, high unemployment among the economically active group) compounding the impacts of erratic rainfall which has been characterised by recurrent El Nino-induced droughts and the recent Cyclone Idai. The 2018/19 agricultural season was severely affected by these weather phenomena resulting in delayed start of the season which was followed by prolonged dry spell. The Eastern and parts of Southern parts of Zimbabwe was affected by Cyclone Idai. Approximately 3.58 million people (38% of the rural population) are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse and require urgent action to reduce food consumptions gaps and save livelihoods. Approximately, 2.47 million people face Crisis conditions (IPC Phase 3) whilst 1.10 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An additional estimate of 2.68 million people faces Stressed conditions (IPC Phase 2). The prospect of significant deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the communities cannot be ruled out unless support to these communities is delivered. The prevailing boycotting of work by healthcare workers combined with the prohibitive cost of medicine has become a barrier for the poor and vulnerable families in accessing health care. Additionally, Zimbabwe’s healthcare budget is being eroded by escalating prices of essential medicines. Stocks of essential medicines, diagnostics and supplies have been depleted due to foreign currency shortages.
Women and children particularly girls are disproportionately affected by as they are the first to drop from school and assist in seeking for food, women carry double burden of feeding the family and caring for the children at home especially in time of crisis like the current drought and the recent cyclone idai disasters. Communities tend to walk long distances to access basic health care services thus also exposing women and girls to various forms of abuses.

In response to this emergency, Save the Children proposes to implement health and nutrition interventions in 12 Districts across the 7 provinces thus Binga, Kariba, Gokwe South, Wedza, Chimanimani, Chipinge, Bulawayo, Shamva, Mt Darwin, Mazowe, Rushinga and Hurungwe districs. Save the Children seek to improve access to quality health care services within the selected districts.
This will be realized through capacity building health workers and community volunteers on mass screening for early case identification of moderate and severe acute malnutrition, identification and referral water borne/communicable disease, provision of ORS etc Health workers will be trained/refreshed on Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), management of medical consequences of malnutrition and common communicable diseases. The response also seeks to support immunization campaigns to reduce the prevalence of vaccine preventable diseases within the 12 selected districts for a period of 12 months where necessary support to daily running of RHCs including drugs use and storage, basic medical equipment,. Train staff in IPC and ensure have equipment and materials to maintain high levels of IPC. key consumables gloves etc.
If necessary Save the children has the capacity to provide health surge support to CTUS CTCs and Mass vaccination campaigns. (would seek alternative funding if becomes necessary).
