Project Summary

The Project aims at improved access to quality response services and GBV risk prevention by advancing knowledge, engaging men and empowering women and girls in refugees, and the affected host community populations in Cox’s Bazar district. In line with the three strategic objectives of the 2020 JRP and Objective-5 of the GBV sector, the proposed GBV interventions by BRAC are aimed at community-based preventive services to facilitate behavioral change, GBV risk mitigation and quality response services to empower GBV survivors. All activities will be conducted directly by BRAC in 3 camps and 2 host community areas through holistic prevention interventions as well as expend and strengthen Entry point for the GBV survivors. BRAC will provide GBV survives based on survivor-centred approach and guiding principles of GBV to ensure safety , dignity of the survivors and maintain principle of “Do No Harm”.
Interventions:
BRAC will provide SGBV services in 3 camps and 2 host community area. In order to deliver effective and appropriate SGBV services the project will be built around the following areas: Safe Spaces for Women and Girls, Outreach, Case Management, Psychosocial counselling and Men and Adolescent boys programming and ASRH services .
The interventions focus to ensure community participation and engagement through community based approaches. The project targeted 44386 individuals ( 19457 Host community and 24929 Rohingya ) and Key interventions are :
• Establish and operate additional Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (SSWGs) to provide GBV survivor services and other support needed to reduce GBV risk.
• Provide case management and referral services for survivors including psycho-social support and additional support aimed at addressing survivor needs
• Increase outreach of community-based activities and organize awareness and sensitization sessions to reduce the risk of GBV.
• Sensitize men and boys to create positive behavioral change towards women and girls through EMAP approach .
• Provide a holistic solution for Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services that address the need of the adolescent girls.
• Provide vocational training and distribution of start-up materials to women and adolescents in the growth of life skills.
• Establish Women Leadership Group (Shanti Mohila Group ) and community watch group to promote the active involvement of women in decision making processes.
• Provide capacity building training for protection actors on GBV minimum standards, GBV mainstreaming, caring for child survivors, case management, PSEA to deliver survivor-centered GBV services.
Focused Host Community Interventions:
BRAC will operate one SSWGs in Ratna Palong and proposing one SSWGs in Baharchhara .There are gaps and need from community of GBV Service Entry Points in these area . We will implement community-based self-protection and enhance social cohesion initiative. BRA will empower 3080 women and adolescent girls in host community through structured empowerment programme and provide holistic solution for Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services that address the need of the 2730 adolescent girls in host community.

Needs

With the crisis now in third year, the dynamics of the situation have shifted from one defined by an ongoing, massive influx to ensuring the day-to-day living needs of the Rohingya communities in the context of a more stable, but still quite fluid and dynamic, environment. According to the GBVIMS data (GBVIMS report of June 2019), domestic violence constitutes 76% of total reported GBV cases. The JRP midterm review shows only 29% of survivors who reported rape were referred and received medical care within 72 hrs. There is a need to Increase holistic interventions on domestic violence along with GBV mainstreaming in health sector. According to MSNA household assessment in August 2019 in the camps, 57% were unable to identify any of the GBV service points, while 85% mentioned ‘Majihis’ as their preferred point of referral for cases of sexual violence. To address this, referral pathway training and awareness-raising on GBV services will be priority . Women and Adolescent girls, who are more vulnerable to child/forced marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence face heightened obstacles to access services. On the other hand, interventions in sexual and reproductive health (SRH) are seen as key and critical in the response due to inadequate services and lack of understanding of the needs and challenges of adolescent girls and women in Rohingya community. Despite the fact that, the main challenges remain government restrictions on the right to work and access to economic opportunities outside the camps for refugees. Both the community needs to create income and livelihood activities for women, girls, men and boys. But not enough opportunities were created to generate income to meet the needs of refugees and host communities. The skills development initiatives have largely overlooked men and boys, leaving them idle. In spite of being among the poorest, people with disabilities, especially women, were largely excluded from these schemes. In contrast, skill development programs have not always taken gender barriers into account (double time burden of women and girls, cultural restrictions, access to technology, information and skills). Barriers women and girls face in accessing economic empowerment activities needs to be addressed in the interventions. Working through Safe Space for Women and Girls (SSWGs) can be an effective method for delivering skill development for women and girls in a safe and enabling environment. (Gender Reflections: two years of the rohingya refugee response -september 2019)In host community, 60% of women reported ever being physically or sexually abused during their lives. Their husbands were the most common perpetrator. Two-thirds of the abused women have never talked about their experience of violence and almost none accessed formal services for support. To address this major public health problem, the prevailing attitudes that permit and encourage male violence against women must be directly addressed ( icddrb , Knowledge Repository) . In reference to Gender Profile No.2, For Rohingya Refugee Response, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh (as of March 2019) , the inclusion and training of men and adolescent boys in the promotion of gender equality and positive masculinities is a practical strategy to end SGBV. More services are needed for male survivors of GBV, including increasing entry points and male staff for these services. I addition the prevention and response to SEA need to be strengthen. In contrast, emergency shelter remains extremely limited and inadequate. In addition, there was lack of GBV mainstreaming activities. The need of Emergency shelter and GBV mainstreaming activities need to be address.

Activities or outputs

•In line with Performance Indicator 2 & 4, BRAC will expand and strengthen case management entry points where women, boys, men and girls can safely access case management services and build capacity of the case workers in clinical management of rape and caring for child survivors. We will collect, manage and analysis GBV incidents through GBVIMS+. Moreover, the structured PSS services will be provided through trained PSS counselors to 1790 GBV survivors in the camps and host community. • In line with Performance Indicator 2, BRAC will establish community-based groups and Community based Girls Protection mechanism and organize awareness and sensitization sessions to prevent violence against women and girls. In addition, Implementation of EMAP approach we will engage men and boys to promote positive power and masculinities in Host and refugee community. • BRAC will engage and educate adolescents girls to claim and negotiate their SRHR rights which include personal and menstrual hygiene and train local health workers on menstrual hygiene and involve adolescent’s girls as role models to raise awareness and change community views on menstruation.• BRAC will provide Life Skill and livelihood training to adolescents’ girls and women those are affected on early marriage and survivors of GBV. We will also Provide financial and material support and create market linkage for host community women. In addition, we will establish and promote Shanti Mohila and Balika Groups to promote youth leadership, peace building and support girl’s participation in decision making.• BRAC has its own Safeguarding policy and SHE policy and investigation units for protection from SEA and Sexual Harassment. However, BRAC has feedback mechanisms in place include a 24-hour hotline number , letter boxes, face-to-face and email complaint mechanisms . In 2020 1560 humanitarian workers will receive PSEA and Safeguarding training in 2020. We will ensure transparency and accountability in monitoring and reporting towards effected population. • BRAC will provide capacity building for partners and refugees on search and rescue, contributory stocks of 5000 dignity kits. BRAC staffs will respond to emergency situation through Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU). • In coordinating with GBV subsector and MOWCA, BRAC will trained 250 service providers on GBV minimum standards, caring for child survivors, psychosocial first aid (PFA), case management. • BRAC will build social cohesion through organize dialogue between Host and Rohingya community to reduce social tension, violence and conflict. • BRAC is as an active contributor to the GBV Sub-Sector as well as GBV IMS Task Team, Male Engagement Task Team, GIHA, MHPSS working group, PSEA network and NGO platform. BRAC will continue to work in coordination with other sectors to ensure multi-sectoral approach for effective GBV response, prevention and risk mitigation services. BRAC will mainstream Gender and GBV with health and WASH sector and will do Gender Analysis within the community, introduce gender lens based on gender marker and conduct periodic gender auditing which will contribute to GBV subsector. • Community based support initiative will be developed to strengthen the inclusion of girls and youth with disabilities in GBV prevention and response activities as well as awareness raising and increase accessibility to address the needs of persons with disabilities.
