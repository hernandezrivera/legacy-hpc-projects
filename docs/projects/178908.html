Project Summary

The purpose of the Shelter and NFI intervention is to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, and contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure in Syria. The different components of NRC’s Shelter project are intended to contribute to HRP Shelter & NFI Sector Objectives 1 and 2.
NRC will deliver this project through direct implementation modalities.
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities, IDP and refugee returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the conflict. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators
In line with the NRC Syria Strategy, the Shelter programme will include emergency, transitional and recovery response options. These options are not intended to be “standalone” and may be delivered in conjunction with one another. In South and North, provision of NFIs will be predominantly focused around schools and learning facilities. These activities will be done using holistic approaches incorporating WASH and education. Other community infrastructure such as markets will also be rehabilitated in coordination with early recovery and livelihoods sector. The proportion of emergency, transitional and recovery related outputs will vary between different parts of Syria, depending on context, needs, access and implementation. Activities 1 (NWS only), 2 (NES only) and 3 (NES and NWS) will be implemented cross-border. These activities will also be implemented cross-line along with activity 5. Shelter and WASH activities will be delivered as a fully- integrated joint programme where relevant. The implementing modality will be defined based on access constraints and NRC capacity and will include cross-border emergency (unconditional cash, in-kind tents and NFIs), transitional (roads, lighting and other camp infrastructure) and recovery (housing rehabilitation) where relevant.

4 - Needs

2021 HNO estimates a total of 5.9M people are in need of shelter assistance, 4% increase comparing to 2020 due to unavailability of adequate and affordable shelters, limited response, C-19 challenges for communal shelters and displacements in NWS and NES. 31% of the overall population living in inadequate shelters. In addition, it estimates that 4.7M people are in need of NFIs, which has increased by 20% comparing to early 2020; the drop of Syrian pound, fewer income opportunities, rising prices, economic restrictions and C-19 drove the increase. Up to 1.8M people are living in IDP sites across NWS (1.5M) and NES (almost 300,000), often with inadequate access to basic services. For the urban populations, where population density is high, damage to shelter, basic services infrastructure is extensive, and livelihoods are limited. Further, spontaneous returnees often return to damaged, unsafe or inadequate shelters. Female-headed households, youth, elderly and PwD have less access to basic services including shelter, livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs, especially in the winter. Lack of access to safe and adequate shelter increases vulnerability of households and forces adoption of harmful coping mechanisms such as selling assets or keeping children out of education. The 2021 HNO estimated that 2.4million children are out of school which could be due to lack of access to adequate learning spaces. Based on NRC 2020 Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) households report they need fuel, clothing, and power/heating sources during winter. In NWS 90% of people would prefer to receive this in the form of cash rather than in-kind, and in NES 85% preferred a mix of cash and in-kind fuel and stoves.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will contribute to: SO1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support through HRP activities; (1) Targeted crisis-affected households, including in underserved communities, have their core and essential non-food item needs met, including (a) the provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, etc.) (b) Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including winterization and fuel distribution in NES; through appropriate modalities to newly displaced and/or highly vulnerable households. This will comprise of a combination of NFI kits and unrestricted cash; NRC is planning, where appropriate, to distribute Cash for Winterization MPC as it was preferable by the beneficiaries based on NRC PDMs from previous actions . The composition of the kits or items selected will be done in discussions with the community and close coordination with the Sector. NFI support will also be provided to students during exam periods. (2) Targeted crisis-affected households are provided with timely life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support (medium-term/temporary shelter). This will include the provision of emergency shelter (e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure) and NFI kit distribution. The registration will be carried out using local council lists verified by NRC, where beneficiaries will be selected based on the needs. PDMs using Household questionnaires will be conducted for all the activities, with a sample of the targeted population. Payments are made using financial service provided and distributed by Hawala agents during distributions set up by NRC.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Centralized, large-scale distributions can create security risks for beneficiaries and staff. They may be inaccessible for people affected by impaired mobility or those with mobility restrictions. In-kind NFI distributions might lack appropriate items for women, girls, and boys and/or the quality/type is inadequate resulting in unmet needs. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) Priorities household-level distributions where feasible. 2) Selection of centralized distribution sites will include risk assessment and alternative distribution methods. 3) Market-based approaches will be used in place of in-kind assistance when possible. 4) Complaints/Feedback mechanisms in place for beneficiaries to voice recommendations, suggestions, or concerns. Protection Risk (2): Lack of adequate understanding of HLP rights risks of harmful consequences, including risk of eviction and lack of security of tenure for vulnerable populations. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) DD carried out by specialized staff to ensure understanding of rights and responsibilities and strengthen the security of tenure.2) Complaints/Feedback mechanisms are put in place for beneficiaries to voice their recommendations, suggestions or concerns. 3) Community tension: awareness raising, community outreach and messaging.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC’s Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, and child safeguarding policies. Several sessions took place in 2020 to raise awareness of PSEA and child safeguarding to staff, in addition in sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC participates in PSEA network meetings, regularly reports and shares information on good practice and challenges related to PSEA. NRC’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and child safeguarding reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams. In addition to inquiring during post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs) whether people were asked to exchange anything for aid. Attempts by non-traditional humanitarian actors (e.g. local councils and authorities) to interfere in beneficiary selection and eligibility are reported and addressed as they come. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks who recipients of aid think provided the assistance, in order to identify possible areas of concern. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM have started and are expected to conclude by mid-2021. Tents/NFIs/Hygiene kit distributions: Issues related to exchange of assistance for sexual favours, threats or actual removal from beneficiary lists.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Threats to health, safety/ security of staff, partners and beneficiaries linked to conflict or criminal activities: dedicated staff to ensure follow-up and implementation security policy, regular risk assessments, updated security and contingency plans, relevant training for all staff. Limitations by local authorities prevent NRC access to specific areas and to independently select beneficiaries/staff: networking with peer organizations to encourage unified policy positions, regular meetings with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent recruitment policy and criteria for selection of beneficiaries, clear red lines identified, careful liaison work. Border closure: Market analysis for local procurement, Advocacy at UN level for humanitarian exemptions, Stockpiling of basic needs items when possible and relevant, use of different modalities, in-kind/ cash. Neighboring states push for large-scale return of refugees to Syria including coerced return: Collaboration and coordination with other NRC offices in the region. Development of contingency plans. Programmes adaptation . Advocacy efforts. Fraud/Corruption/Bribery: Strengthening of procurement/operating procedures, promote “whistle-blowing” policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. Loss of access in targeted areas due to developments, legal restrictions/loss of acceptance: good understanding of context, contextual analysis, security plans/procedures, community acceptance and good relations with local authorities. C-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during C-19; adapted modalities. Currency fluctuations and rising prices: transferring funds to Syrian bank for immediate needs/monitoring.
