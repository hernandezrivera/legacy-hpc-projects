Project Summary

To meet the educational needs of out-of-school children in Afrin and Jarablus, Northwest Syria, through the provision of non-formal education (NFE), which will prepare children for reintegration into the formal education system by developing basic Arabic and English literacy and numeracy, building social and emotional learning skills and improving psychosocial wellbeing.

4 - Needs

2.6 million children have been displaced across Syria since the start of the conflict, and across all age groups, educational needs are very complex, encompassing literacy and numeracy, skills development, livelihoods and income generation, as well as psychosocial support. Since December 2019, the intensification of the conflict between opposition groups and groups supporting the Syrian government have led to the displacement of 831,920 IDPs from opposition controlled areas. Out of this total of 831,920 IDPs (approx. 138,666 households) displaced since December 1st.,167,822 IDPs (approx. 60,000 households) have been displaced to the 4 Turkey-controlled districts of Northern Aleppo where our partner Bonyan is working. Therefore, 20% of that total have been displaced into areas where Concern’s partners have access. 81% of these IDPs are women and children. (170,881 women; 240, 519 boys; 260,193 girls). Compared with the Education Directorate in Afrin and Jarablus, there are 73,500 total school-aged children in Afrin and 40,000 in Jarablus. These areas have high numbers of internally displaced people (IDPs) (50% of school-aged children are IDPs in Afrin, while 72% of school-aged children are IDPs in Jarablus) and are likely to receive increased numbers of IDPs as the conflict intensifies in Idlib.

5 - Implementation of activities

Selection Criteria: Out-of-school children, with equal priority given to girls and boys and preferential selection for children from IDPs and children affected by disability. NFE activities will be designed to enrol children into the formal education system as soon as possible and not to create any additional pull factors which might encourage children to drop out of the formal education system. In order to promote a conflict-sensitive approach, Concern and Bonyan will ensure that project staff and participants represent the broadest range of different ethnic and tribal groups from the targeted communities. Bonyan team diversity in Northern Aleppo will reflect that of participants. CurriculumBonyan will use UNICEF's Curriculum B to prepare students to enter formal education, and to assist them in developing basic Arabic and English literacy and numeracy, It will also look to build social and emotional learning skills and to improve psychosocial wellbeing. Structured psychosocial support (PSS) will be integrated using War Child's I DEAL programme. NFE teachers and PSS facilitators will receive ongoing professional development throughout the project period in order to ensure the quality of the implemented education and PSS activities. Back-to-school campaigns will be conducted and vulnerable children participating in Bonyan's NFE programme, who have specialized needs, will receive referrals and material support. A Caregiver Engagement programme will also be implemented.Key informant interviews will be undertaken by the Bonyan team with the following Education Directorate officials and organizations in Jarablus and Afrin districts Bonyan Education Needs Assessment in Afrin REACH Humanitarian Situation Overview in Syria, Northwest Syria Syrian Arab Republic Humanitarian Needs Overview Assistance Coordination Unit Schools in Syria Thematic Report Bonyan Rapid Needs Assessment (Education) in rural Aleppo Data analysis of NFE programming in Azaz & Al Bab

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Concern and partners have a code of conduct which controls the expected behavior of the project staff and restricts any abuse of their authority in relation to distributions and the provision of services. A complaints response mechanism will be shared with programme participants so that they can communicate with the senior management of Concern and partners and report any breach of the code of conduct. 2. Access restrictions to the geographic area due to active fighting and/or shifting of front-lines would prevent the humanitarian actors providing educational services to the affected population.In the event that the conflict restricts access to one of these districts then the project has been designed with enough flexibility to move the project activities and project team to another district.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: • Cross border humanitarian assistance from Turkey continues.• Local and International NGOs registered in SE Turkey are allowed to continue their project activities in Turkey and NW Syria.• Safety and security in the project area will remain relatively stable.• No major front lines/military operations near to the targeted areas, and no major change in the lines of control.• Markets in Turkish controlled areas of NW Syria remain functional and all the services and goods required in this project can be easily procured locally.• Continued strong coordination and cooperation with local authorities and educational directorates in the project areas. Risks:1. Interference of armed parties to the conflict and local councils in project implementation process including recruitment, procurement and implementation of the project activities. (Likelihood: Moderate. Impact: High.)2. Changes in security situation in the targeted areas. (Likelihood: Moderate. Impact: High)3. Poor participation of parents in PTA meetings and Caregiver engagement courses. 4. Parents refuse to send their girls alone to the NFE centers. (Likelihood: low, Impact: high)5. Children dropping out of formal education to attend NFE centers. (Likelihood: low, Impact: high)6. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to inequality of services/opportunities provided (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium)7. Diversion of project funds and materials through conflicts of interest between Bonyan staff and suppliers or programme participants.8. Risk of exploitation and abuse by Concern and Bonyan staff towards the programme participants in return for access to services.
