Project Summary

World Vision plans to support 60,000 individuals (6,000 men, 12,000 women, 18,000 boys and 24,000 girls) comprised of IDPs, returnees, and affected people in host communities with shelter and non-food items in some of the locations of severe needs. These include some counties in Unity, Upper Nile, Western Equatoria and Warrap States. This project will also benefit some locations through cash-based interventions through the unconditional but restricted modality due to the likely presence of functional markets. The specific areas to benefit from this are Tambura and Yambio in Western Equatoria State, and Tonj North in Warrap State. The cash based-intervention modality will contribute to rebuilding of local economies, hence improving community resilience and coping mechanisms, which on the other hand contributes to achievement of Cluster’s objectives of supporting the most vulnerable, returnees, IDPs, host communities with lasting solutions.
World Vision will use approaches that enhance effectiveness of the project. This include close coordination with the S/NFI Cluster at both national and sub-national levels. Prior to NFI distribution, the Pipeline Unit will be kept informed on caseload targets especially during needs assessments and desk reviews to enable adequate prepositioning of NFI materials. All requests for NFI will be done one week in advance to give ample time for Logistics planning and delivery of items to communities. Also, the response team will closely coordinate with the Logistic Cluster and community on daily basis to enable timely deliveries. Community engagement will be boosted by identifying and strengthening the existing community structures responsible for local issues. Further, Local community committees will be formed and trained on community resilience, coping mechanisms and mobilization of community members for local projects. This local committees will help cascade the knowledge widely in the community. Also, multi-sectoral integration will be the cornerstone of the project which will help communities to realize durable solutions. World Vision's mobile team will take part in multi-sectorial interventions in order to leverage the efforts of the various sectors. Joint missions will be planned together especially during community awareness exercises, where the World Vision mobile team will provide messaging on health, WASH, nutrition, and food security and livelihood (FSL) sectors. Gender and protection issues related to House, Land and Property (HLP) will similarly be identified and addressed accordingly during the project lifespan. During joint mission responses, World Vision and partners will make the use of the Humanitarian Hubs like Ayod, Nasir and Fangak to support hard-to-reach locations. These Hub locations will be used as logistics and communication centers to urgently respond to emergency needs, for example, planning of missions, preposition of NFI materials, and supplies of other sectors like Health, WASH. World Vision will also collaborate with CCCM partners by sharing information regarding the both formal and informal settlements, as there is a likelihood of increased movement of people across communities. Where possible, Biometric Registration (BMR) will be used to verify beneficiaries before distribution takes place.

1 - Needs

As a result of the 2018 signed peace deal in South Sudan, communities have enjoyed relative peace for the last year. However, parties of the agreement have expressed obstacles slowed down the implementation. While the political contexts are fluctuating, the prevailing humanitarian context has increasingly remained overwhelming. 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview predicts a more complex wave of humanitarian challenges in many counties of Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap and Western Equatoria States. This situation continues to challenge humanitarian actors with matching limited resources to increasing humanitarian needs of possible IDPs and Returnees. In addition, still majority of the population in both rural and urban locations are struggling to survive because the accumulative effects of the conflict has hurt livelihoods and people's coping mechanisms. While the stability of communities is expected to result into excessive return of people to their homes of origin, this will come along with rapid House, Land and Property challenges. It is observed in this circumstances that most of IDPs and returnees with female headed households, people with special needs such as elderly and/or disabled people, child headed households are exposed to high level of vulnerability. Therefore World Vision aims to assist 60,000 vulnerable people affected by the protracted conflict. The assistance would be delivered to the counties in the above mentioned four states where they are identified as either in ‘Severe’ and ‘Extreme’ in 2020 HNO and HRP Severity maps. However, 10% of the targeted beneficiaries will be served through the unconditional but restricted modality of cash based-interventions. This is because of the likely presence of functional markets. Using this approach will speed up the efforts of community resilience and coping mechanisms. Target communities will be engaged through every step of the implementation, such as community consultation from the inception, project information sharing, formation of Local Community Committees representing all categories of the people including women and children. The members of the committees will be trained on various topics like community resilience and coping mechanisms, HLP issues and mobilization of communities for local projects. This would improve the existing coping mechanisms like use of local building materials and cooking with traditional pots. We will ensure active participation of local authorities and faith based institutions through identifying and strengthening the existing structures. Community Feedback Mechanism will be set up to collect feedback of project participants from the onset. Vulnerable people in the target communities will be supported with long-term solutions by integrating the S/NFI project and other sectoral programs. Our health partners will be contacted for joint responses including messaging on malnutrition issues and the importance of mosquito nets. In possible spots of Ebola, sensitization on the epidemic will be conducted. Messages on hands washing, proper disposal of human wastes will be conducted. Locations with Nutrition centers will be targeted to carry breastfeeding and deworming of children messaging. As movement of people will be high in both formal and informal settlements, World Vision will work closely with CCCM partners. This will be done by exchanging information on IDPs and returnees, establishing community-led initiatives, formation and training of leadership committees, distributing community mobilization package, setting up a monitoring system for IDP movements and service delivery, and participating in contingency planning. In certain locations, the biometric registration will be used to verify beneficiaries’ prior to distribution. Through this integration, community issues would be addressed holistically, thus contributing to durable solutions as resilience and coping mechanisms are strengthened throughout the project cycle.

2 - Activities

World Vision will deliver shelter and non-food items to target counties with the following activities.1. Conduct needs assessment through direct and indirect inter agency rapid needs assessment (IRNA) missions to ascertain community need for both in-kind and cash interventions. During this process community views will be collected by holding focus group discussions in separated groups of women, children, elderly, and community leader groups to cross check the needs of the most vulnerable groups in the communities. Community feedback mechanism such as Complaint Desk will be set up to collect community feedback throughout the project period. 2. Conduct beneficiary verification and registration. World Vision will use the agency’s Vulnerability Map which identify the most vulnerable people by certain sets of criteria to target the right vulnerable beneficiaries in the target area. As indicated by the agency’s own assessment conducted in most of the target counties, the project will focus on IDPs and returnees as the primary target group. 3. Conduct S/NFI distribution. During the NFI distribution, World Vision will ensure that all aid distribution is based on the needs of the disaster affected population and will ensure that structures are in place to ensure that on site distribution monitoring are done. Local committees will support planning for the distribution, mobilization of beneficiaries, and establishing and managing complaints desks. 4. Deploy World Vision Mobile Teams to deep field locations. The mobile team will engage and respond to the emergency needs of disaster affected population in hard-to-reach areas where life-saving commodities are needed. The deployment will be both bi-sectoral and multi-sectoral. This includes use of Humanitarian Hubs to serve hard to access communities. 5. Conduct Rapid and Post Distribution Monitoring. This activity will inform in particular on the level of dissatisfaction or satisfaction of the affected population who received in kind NFIs and cash transfers. However, World Vision will ensure continuous monitoring for the cash interventions to understand market dynamics throughout the project lifespan. 6. Lead the S/NFI activities at the state level in Western Equatoria State. World Vision will continue to coordinate as State Focal Point(SFP) the activities of the S/NFI cluster ensuring that various partners fill the humanitarian gaps in Western EquatoriaState. 7. Co-lead the S- NFI Coordination at the national level. The areas with cash interventions, the following modalities will be applied. 1. Hold inception meetings with community 2. Conduct needs assessment 3. Conduct Rapid Market Assessment 4. Conducting of feasibility service providers5. Setting up and training of local committees 6. Community sensitization and awareness meetings 7. Conduct beneficiary registration 8. Verification of beneficiaries9. Cash distribution 10. Conduct rapid and post monitoring

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, this is always considered and therefore that is why the needs analysis is done before any response to take into account the needs of the affected population and their input is always important aspect.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

In all the response locations, the Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items response works with the government/local authorities, the community leaders and the community representatives including the beneficiaries. The responses are however short term in nature as it is hoped that once these locations become peaceful, the government and the community structures would be able to build from this assistance and fully rebuild their livelihoods.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes the project is aligned with national objectives as it will provide support to newly displaced IDPs, returnees, and the most vulnerable population in the disaster affected host communities including those hard-to-access areas.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

If the signed peace deal agreement continues to hold, the environment for the project will generally be stable. The assisted households are likely to remain in these locations and rebuild their livelihoods as the environment would be considered safer.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The usage of the locally available materials would continuously be advocated during this project. When this project comes to an end, the partner communities would continue to use the locally available resources and this would make the project handover to communities more sustainable.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Where there is contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards, World Vision will provide Mine Risk Education which will be delivered by the highly experienced team on the subject. Mine Risk Education Team of World Vision has been accredited by UNMISS for its teaching module and capacity.In case communities leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of getting assistance (humanitarian assistance as a pull factor), World Vision will disseminate accurate information about the activities and benefits of the project to the people on eligibility criteria to reduce expectations during the community consultations in early stages of the project in each location.Where there is possibility of exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, the project will be aligned with the community based protection structures like local chiefs, churches, Community Based Child Protection Committees, Women and Youth groups, formed in the target settlements and take precautionary measures to minimize exposure to security threats posed on vulnerable women and girls. Measures will be taken especially during distribution process for women and girls to be protected.Risks related to HLP will be mitigated by incorporating protection questions during assessment in order for the communities to understand House, Land and property dynamics. Community members including their leaders will also be trained on how to solve this issues.
