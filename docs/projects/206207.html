Project Summary

Since the start of the Sudan conflict in April, over 1 million people displaced from Sudan to neighboring countries, of whom 364,818 arrived in South Sudan as of 7 November. Of these, 83 per cent are South Sudanese returnees and 16 per cent are Sudanese refugees. The percentage of Sudanese refugees has doubled from 8 per cent in early October, indicating a significant increase in Sudanese refugees in the past few days. Northern Bahr El Ghazal bordering East Darfur in Sudan at the onset received thousands of refugees and returnees as of 26 May 2023. As Sudan’s crisis continues to escalate, the number of refugees and returnees in Northern Bahr El Ghazal keep on increasing. The Commission for Refugee Affairs and UNCHR declared Wedweil Transit centre to be Wedweil Refugee Settlement (WRS) As of 22nd of July 2023, Wedweil of Aweil West received 6578 refugees while the entire state was reported to have received over 7,000 returnees – with an average daily influx of 150 individuals. The inflow to the Northern Bahr El Ghazal has been through: Kiir Adem, Gok-machar, Majoknyinthiou and Jaac (UNHCR, 2023), IRNA Report 1-7th August 2023.
According to IPC projection as of Sept 2023, Between July and September 2023, four counties (Aweil South, Aweil center, Aweil East, and Aweil North) are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) and one county (Aweil West) is classified in IPC MAN Phase 3 (Serious). An estimated 46% of the state’s population are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023. According to UNOCHA as 28 Oct 2023, Protection services are in demand due to the increased number of the elderly and children arriving without caregivers, Food, shelter, non-food items and health services are needed as the host communities have exhausted their resources.
GBV is a prevailing issue in South Sudan, ranging from domestic violence to sexual violence in conflict. Despite gender equality being guaranteed in the Constitution of South Sudan, women and girls remain marginalized and subject to social cultural and patriarchal constraints. These constraints, along with the low status of women at an individual and systemic level, contribute to an environment that hinders women’s access to the justice system and threatens their physical security. Even with the continuous awareness and campaigns being staged by most agencies within the country, there is still no much change to GBV especially at the grass root level. There need for continuous awareness and support for women and girls against GBV in the country.
Children and families who have arrived in Abyei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal in search of safety after fleeing the intensifying conflict in the neighboring Sudan, now face needs and challenges as they seek refuge in the states of Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal that are already some of the most deprived as a result of interconnected shocks driven by the intercommunal conflict and floodings. Thousands of South Sudanese returnees and Sudanese refugees have crossed the border into the two states of Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Over 90% of women and children and thousands more are expected to arrive as violence escalates in the Sudan hence very alarming.
Access to essential services such as water, shelter, education and protection is extremely limited and now communities are having additional pressure to share very scarce resources. Due to the ongoing conflict, trade with Sudan has been completely suspended and the prices of food and commodities have also gone up substantially. As the influx of returnees and refugees fleeing conflict continues unabated, protection actors continue to receive reports of returnees and refugees assaulted during their flight to from Sudan. In addition to this, many areas are facing significant humanitarian needs and humanitarian risks. This requires investment in child protection and Gender-based risks prevention and risks mitigation as a great priority.

1. Needs

In Aweil and Abyei administrative Area, the effects of economic shocks as a result of depreciation of the currency led to increase in the food and commodity prices. This has greatly affected families by impairing their ability to care of themselves and their children leading to other stressors such as gender-based violence and negative coping mechanisms like forced marriages and alcohol abuse. This in turn has led to increase in child labour among children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal state. Young children are involved in informal activities such as hawking clothes, pushing carts or wheel barrows, washing cars, shoe shining, and selling charcoal to generate little cash for themselves and siblings. It has been observed that Northern Bahr el Ghazal has the highest number of children living and working on the streets. This has culminated into delinquency. Many children have been engaged in dangerous activities such as alcohol and substance abuse and perpetrating violence against other children. Due to this, a considerable number of children are in conflict with the law. A recent joint assessment by TOCH and other CP actors in Northern Bahr el Ghazal found out that 30 children were in detention in Aweil main prison, Lack of Food or shortage at the household level as daily meals have drastically. Returnees reported that they have one meal a day or zero meal a day. The Price per maluo of 3.5kgs of sorghum ranges from 3,000 to ,500 SSP at the local markets, Returnees lack cooking utensils, sleeping materials, washing soaps, clothes, water containers (CRIs/NFIs): Since their arrival, they have been trying to annex themselves to the host communities, but the mentioned items could not be enough. A 36-year-old woman said “we are even causing conflict within the existing peaceful families. This is because we also come as families of different characters from different backgrounds of life. Sharing cooking items is the worst thing in a women’s life. Imagine my children waiting for the food which will be cooked when the other family is done. My children will be watching the other children as they eat!”, Children do not go to school due to lack of school fees and other children refuse because of hunger. There are few cases of orphanages. For example, there are 3 orphans in Marial-baai and Yargot (2boy & 1girl), Vulnerable returnee-women and girls are at risk of exposure to Exploitation and abuse following the nature of set-up with limited shelter services The combination of the triple shocks of economic decline due to depreciation of the local currency, South Sudan’s Pound, conflicts and climatic shocks has led to the deterioration of people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms. This also presents significant risks to children as well as the women. Women and children are experiencing many different forms of violence in the humanitarian contexts such as Physical violence, Emotional or psychological abuse, Sexual abuse, Sexual exploitation, Harmful practices (such as Forced and early marriage), Economic exploitation of a child (including harmful work) and Neglect. TOCH always work with diverse and most vulnerable communities to strengthening Accountability to the affected populations (AAP) by establishing a complaint, response and feedback mechanism (CRFM) related to the operation of the Women and girls’ friendly space and apply PSEA and anti-corruption measures during and after project so that the community members (who are the primary beneficiaries), can give feedback on the service provision and launch their complaint. In addition, awareness and training on PSEA, AAP and safeguarding will be provided to staff, volunteers, beneficiaries and stakeholders.
