Project Summary

This project aims to bolster the WASH cluster’s capacity to respond to emerging humanitarian response in locations with greatest needs through the management of the portion of the WASH core pipeline. In 2020, IOM will continue its commitment to support the WASH Cluster by aiming to procure and manage core pipeline supplies to benefit 722,844 people targeted to be assisted with lifesaving WASH emergency assistance. The project activities are in line with Strategic Objective (SO) 1, 2 and 3 of HRP 2020, as well as the WASH Cluster Objectives 1, 2, and 3. IOM Pipeline will procure, transport and manage the supplies needed for the WASH Cluster minimum packages for quality interventions integrated across sectors, targeting the acute vulnerabilities in South Sudan. Supplies procured will include items indicated by WASH Cluster as key basic supplies needed in WASH frontline response, such as household water treatment and storage, water quality testing supplies, bulk water treatment and storage, hand pump spare parts and tool kits, latrine materials, basic hygiene supplies Type of responses to be supported will include both static and mobile responses, covering response locations within and outside PoCs in South Sudan.
IOM Pipeline will work closely with the WASH Cluster and State Focal Points to ensure prioritized and coordinated coverage across the country, minimizing gaps and overlap of services. IOM will also maintain strong coordination with other Clusters such as the Shelter/NFI, Health and the Logistics to ensure more vulnerable and underserved populations have access to WASH core pipeline supplies to maintain their basic WASH standard. The common pipeline system implementation in South Sudan has been effective as it optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies. Through the management and provision of core pipeline supplies, IOM supports the inter-sectoral collaboration which mainstreams the key-cross cutting issues such as gender equality, AAP, protection, disability inclusion and GBV prevention and mitigation, including SEA prevention effectively in every response. IOM common pipeline ensures the provision of reliable and quality gender-sensitive items such as menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kit and hygiene kits

1 - Needs

According to the 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for the WASH sector, an estimated 5.2 million women, men and children are still deprived of access to adequate safe clean water, sanitation facilities and basic hygiene, all across South Sudan. The estimate number covers all population groups, namely internal displaced population (IDPs), including persons that have been displaced more than once, returnees and the Host Communities (HC). The widespread lack of safe drinking water, developed sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable WASH related diseases. WASH needs are widespread and remain immense as many communities who have had their infrastructure destroyed during the prolonged conflict are unable to rebuild without assistance. Remaining infrastructure in many remote locations are unable to provide adequate services due to the population increase putting a strain on existing services. WASH Cluster has also stated that in 2020, with the continuous flow of returnees, including people returning to their place of origin in South Sudan, demand on water and sanitation infrastructure will increase. Access to water in particular, may become a driver for conflict as numbers of people attempt to access insufficient improved water sources increase. As with previous years, women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products. Appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need. IDPs in Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites do not have sufficient hygiene and sanitation and are at risk of disease outbreaks in the congested conditions. Without WASH infrastructure, the potential presence of water or vector borne diseases as well as the ability to contain them, may impact the wellbeing of the local populations at risk of disease outbreaks in the increasingly congested urban setting.Accessing many remote locations in South Sudan with lifesaving humanitarian cargo is still very challenging, due to conflict and increased road attacks, combined with a highly undeveloped road network, drastically slows movements. This difficult task is made more problematic during the rainy season when road transport is not an option and all supplies must be moved by air. Maintaining a robust and high performing core pipeline to ensure adequate prepositioning of WASH supplies in key locations throughout the country is necessary. This effort reacts to the need to strengthen the humanitarian planning and response among the large number of aid agencies providing assistance in the country. Core pipeline will provide crucial support to ensure swift access to WASH emergency relief items to support integrated WASH responses in a coordinated fashion. Since 2014, IOM has managed the core pipeline in South Sudan and successfully supported WASH Cluster to meet objectives. Many partners rely on IOM as pipeline manager for the provision of materials from the Cluster’s common pipeline. Maintaining a steady pipeline is essential to meet needs in a timely manner as they are identified. IOM’s pipeline manager works closely with the WASH Cluster and State Focal Points to ensure prioritized and coordinated coverage across the country, minimizing gaps and overlap of services. IOM also works closely with other Clusters such as the Shelter/NFI Cluster, Health Cluster and the Logistics Cluster to ensure more vulnerable and underserved populations have access to WASH core pipeline supplies to maintain their basic hygiene standard. In 2020, IOM will continue its commitment to support the WASH Cluster by aiming to procure and manage core pipeline supplies to benefit 722,844 people in need.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Pipeline response agency partners conduct assessment in locations of need before any response is planned and carried out. In this way, the scale of the emergency is well-established with a visit to the field site and accumulating all needed information to decide if a response is needed. The cluster approach also works as a way of cross-checking information and approves any request for supplies for all response before IOM Pipeline carries on with supporting partner agencies. Before any registration/distribution, the community is also consulted so that they can take ownership of the processWASH Need Assessment toolkit and Pipeline PDM’s categorically incorporates questions on inclusion, participation, and protection in order to measure effectiveness of the response in addressing the needs of the affected population. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed for future response. During the response of partners, a complaint mechanism is established, usually by means of a complaints desk or box. In emergency response with only very limited time in the community, a robust complaints mechanism is a challenge to setup. Post-distribution monitoring is a way that IOM Pipeline ensures feedback from beneficiaries are incorporated in reports that are used for future planning. IOM Core Pipeline adheres to the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) framework and encourages distribution partners to ensure participation of all groups. Also in line with its commitment to AAP and mainstreaming protection and equity of access to humanitarian response, women and girls are required to be included in consultations with the community in decision-making process. IOM also has in place policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA has been rolled out.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Pipeline distributing partners inform beneficiaries before distribution on items distributing and their usage. Partners will utilize WASH Cluster’s set standards for information sharing with beneficiaries, which include community meetings, and information disseminated through radio and local chief/community elders. Post-Distribution monitoring will be conducted to collect feedback, from both partners and beneficiaries. on material appropriateness and quality. This feedback will later on feed into planning for improvement and future procurement. Pipeline also conducts partner satisfaction surveys on management of Pipeline. In addition, partners are also encouraged to conduct the post distribution monitoring, to evaluate the quality of their overall responses. Pipeline partners are required to include the community in monitoring and evaluation of the quality and utility of supplies through post-distribution monitoring and community feedback. IOM team have all been trained on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse which makes them aware of all important and relevant concerns related to project design and implementation prior to rolling out of any intervention. Pipeline team also works closely with the IOM Protection Officer to ensure protection is mainstreamed in all activities. Pipeline partners have well established procedures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
