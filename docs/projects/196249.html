Project Summary

To respond to the needs of described above groups, PAH will conduct short and long-term interventions across the country, especially in the areas where cross-sectoral needs are dire. Shelter/NFI project may complement other responses and PAH will always work to ensure synergies with own projects or other partners’ programs. PAH will use both in-kind (light and standard kits distribution) and CVA modalities to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the newly displaced persons and the ones with new vulnerability, returnees and other living in conditions of high vulnerability in coordination with protection team. PAH will strengthen its CVA interventions in 2023 to approximately 30% of the project. It will be implemented in areas of returns with functional and accessible markets. In-kind response will be conducted through mobile and static teams in close coordination with cluster partners. PAH will use the established humanitarian hubs for logistic coordination, information center, organize response to reach remote locations. Where such modality is not available PAH will use its own procurement and transportation solutions, especially for last mile transportation.
PAH conducts regular JMMI assessments, which inform the actions. PAH is an experienced organization with broad portfolio of Shelter/NFI projects successfully implemented across the country.
PAH plans to implement both static and mobile responses. Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.
In the provision of transitional and essential shelter kits, PAH and protection partners will conduct in-depth HLP rights analysis using the unified cluster tools/questionnaires in the areas of shelter constructions.
Land tenure consultation meetings and Community sensitization meetings on HLP rights will be organized throughout the project cycle. Such meetings will result in identification and agreements on land ownership for the shelter construction and provision of essential shelter kits. Through support of HLP focal point, UNHCR, IOM and NRC (protection and HLP partners), the beneficiaries will be supported to obtain communal land tenure documents in order to avoid the risk of being forcibly evicted.
PAH will consider the formation of shelter committees in the areas of project implementation. The committee will include representative from local authorities, traditional chiefs/elders, women and youth to ensure equal right representation to every age and gender group. These committees will work throughout the project cycle in monitoring the implementation progress and giving complaints/feedbacks. Before the construction of any transitional shelter, PAH will require the beneficiary to provide proof of land ownership with legal documents with close consultation from local chiefs and ministry of land and housing more specially from urban settlements to avoid escalation of land related disputes.
In areas/plots of collective centers, PAH will sign agreements (MoUs) with Ministry of Water and physical Infrastructures, Housing and Land utilization, Land tenure/local authorities to guarantee a minimum period of land use to avoid eviction of beneficiaries. Wherever possible, PAH will set follow up strategy to support in identifying land ownership.
PAH will continue to identify HLP related issues throughout the project phases among the affected population and do refer them to the HLP focal point and other stakeholders.
As part of flood resilience and sustainability of the project PAH plans construction of transitional and flood resilience shelters for both returnees and flood affected population.
PAH implement multisector responses to ensure covering the diversified needs of affected population.
Taking into consideration Ebola preparedness plans due to the cases in neighboring Uganda, in case of outbreak in South Sudan PAH will follow the preparedness plans and procedures of respective authorities.

1 - Needs

In 2022, approximately 8.9 million people, including 24% women, 15% people living with disabilities and 4.7 million children, need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. 2.4 million were in need of shelter and non-food items assistance. Nearly 2.3 million require NFIs assistance alone. According to 2023 Intercluster Severity Scales for Humanitarian Needs Overview vast majority of the country (67 out of 78 counties) was classified as extreme severity plus 1 county was classified as catastrophe. Based on the food security analysis (IPC Acute Food Insecurity & Malnutrition Snapshot South Sudan) 7.8 million people, are expected to experience severe food insecurity in the first half of 2023.The magnitude and severity of food insecurity is is worsening gradually in the country. 23 counties classified with high severity of: Jonglei, Lakes state, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states, more than half of the affected people requires emergency shelter support, particularly in areas with the highest concentration of displaced people due to localized outbreaks of sub-national violence and floods cause displacement and the economic downturn. Lack of flood-resilient construction and continued depletion of natural resources for temporary and transitional shelter continues to drive vulnerability, placing households in a continuous pattern of emergency need without the opportunity for recovery. Another factor of dire humanitarian needs is the high rates of returns across the borders of South Sudan. More than 270,000 returnees from neighbouring countries were foreseen in 2022. Returnees usually arrive with minimal resources after walking for days or weeks and need shelter and NFI support . Additionally, 329,000 refugees and asylum seekers also require shelter and non-food item (NFI) support. Displaced families are squatting in informal areas, unfinished buildings and marginalized neighbourhoods. Inadequate shelter and critical NFIs can affect people’s wellbeing, dignity and health, including mental health. This can result in negative impacts on education, health, mental health, livelihoods and food security, and can generate increased protection risks particularly for women, children and people living with disabilities. The Covid-19 pandemic combined with flooding, negatively influenced market functionality, access to natural resources such as timber, poles and grasses for shelter construction. Flooding caused further destruction of shelters and depletion of household goods and may further drive temporary and permanent displacement that will generate conflict, increased risk of forced eviction and localized violence as well as increased protection risks as women and girls venture further a field to collect increasingly scarce materials. According to a key informant assessment from protection partners (CSRP), nearly a quarter of assessed households have HLP issues. The security of tenure among IDPs and returnees is not protected. The returnees continued been forcedly evicted from their property, lose assets and shelters they have accrued. Women in particular face considerable challenges in asserting their HLP rights, which continues to drive vulnerability for women, children and people living with disabilities. Despite the legal recognition of women’s rights to land and property under formal South Sudanese law, cultural norms, customary land tenure law and manipulations of the system pose barriers to women securing their rights. According to the findings of an assessment on people’s priority needs conducted in 2020, shelter and NFI was identified as the priority need by 50 per cent of women assessed settlements in the country, after food assistance, livelihoods and health. For children in assessed settlements, shelter and NFI was identified as the priority need by 53 per cent and for people living with disabilities, by only 15 per cent.

2 - Project activities

1. PAH will provide emergency shelter kits and critical NFIs materials in areas of newly displaced people due to localized outbreaks of sub-national violence, areas of returns and where there are protracted displaced population with protection risks and new vulnerabilities especially people living with disabilities in the prioritized locations. PAH will request items from core pipeline for distribution through coordination with cluster and SFPs. PAH resources will be used to supplement in kind distributions through the use of local functional markets. 2. PAH will provide emergency shelter and critical NFIs kits to newly displaced population with floods. PAH designs a separate emergency shelter and live-saving NFIs kits targeting only newly floods affected population. The floods kits will be distributed based on cluster coordination team recommendation. Unified Cluster separate reporting templates will be used during floods responses in close collaboration with cluster partners and SFPs. 3. PAH will provide in-kind essential shelter and NFIs kits for replacement. to improve living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced especially people living with disabilities, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs. PAH will increase long-term responses in coordination with WASH, FSL and Protection clusters in delivering multi-sectoral responses. PAH will collaborate with CCCM partners to increase informal and formal settlement planning in 2023. All distributions will be coordinated and jointly conducted with other cluster sectors. PAH will establish CFMs in all the project locations to ensure AAP throughout the project cycle. Agreed and clear targeting criteria will be communicated to the leaders, beneficiaries. PAH team will disaggregate the population by, gender, age, vulnerability and disability. Where necessary, PAH will provide the transportation of items from core pipeline to the project side.4. PAH will provide Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees, in coordinated manner: PAH will conduct rapid market assessment and protection risk analysis to determined the delivery mechanism before the provision of CVA; a) CVA interventions will be implemented in areas with functional and accessible markets b) JMMI reports published by REACH will be used for monthly price monitoring supported by IA-CWG partners. PAH plans to increase the CVA response modality to 30% where there will be multi-purpose cash assistance of unconditional with both restricted and unrestricted delivery mechanisms. CVA team will encourage the usage of local available materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional manner to increase resilience and coping mechanism. PAH is active member of IA-CWG and JMMI south Sudan. 5. PAH plans construction of transitional and flood resilience shelters for both returnees and flood affected population. PAH plans piloting construction of transitional and floods resilience shelters in the areas of returns, with involvement of women, men and people living with disabilities to promote community participation and empowerment. PAH will strongly strengthen and advocate for flood-resilient response where that is the main driver of displacement and vulnerability in order to decrease year over year humanitarian needs. PAH will also follow HLP due diligence protocols and conduct area specific conflict analysis to undertake and determine ownership of land so as to avoid legitimizing illegal occupancy or marginalizing real owners of land and property which may exacerbate conflict. There will be community sensitization and awareness on HLP supported by protection cluster and HLP partners.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and it always takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: % of people with access to safe emergency shelter; % of people with access to safe life-saving Non-Food Item. Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, inc. clusters.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics PAH will monthly conduct the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) in accessible locations and will use the data to inform the project activities. Additionally PAH will conduct monthly price monitoring using cluster unified tools. PAH will conduct protection risk analysis as part of market functionality assessment. PAH is a member of the Inter-agency Cash working group.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Where markets are functional with physical access and when it is appropriate Cash and Vouchers Assistance (CVA) modality is encouraged instead of In-kind responses. PAH recognizes the weak financial systems and markets across the country to support S/NFI needs. However, PAH will upgrade and provide shelters and live-saving NFIs using a combination of conditional restricted vouchers and unrestricted cash in areas of return and collect sites. PAH will implement 30% as CVA in the overall response in 2023. There will be unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance with both restricted and unrestricted commodity vouchers for meeting needs at household level. CVA team will encourage the usage of local available materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional manner to increase resilience and coping mechanism of displaced population. CVA activities will include: market functionality assessment, protection risk analysis, needs assessment, price monitoring, vendor selection and capacity review, vendor trainings, communication sessions to community leaders and beneficiaries, registration and voucher/cash provision, distribution and redemption, ongoing monitoring. Support capacity building and S/NFI CVA strategy building and improvement. The vouchers and cash assistance will be used after careful assessment of the market accessibility and availability of the products. Due to high inflation, the market assessment will give information on cost-effectiveness of the activity. Voucher system supports local economy and empower the beneficiaries to purchase items they find most necessary.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of people with disabilities, elder, with limited mobility, PAH officers will visit them directly to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged locally to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. PAH will also translate the IEC materials to local language where the project activities are implemented. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for needs analysis, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support. In collective centres, PAH programme team will collaborate with CCCM team for formation and training of the leadership structures and CFMs. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based caseworkers in GBV referrals, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted throughout the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, protection trainings, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback are kept in Kobo system and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of Senior Management, if necessary, conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback. Where necessary, PAH will close the Complaint and feedback cycle by giving answers to the beneficiaries on the raised complaints or make referral to other service providers.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PAH will conduct protection risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Protection risks are most often higher for marginalized/minority groups’ members, so they will be the main focus in the project. All vulnerable groups will be targeted with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to find out about any gaps and address them. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.
