Project Summary

Over years the prevalence of GBV cases in NES had been high as consequence of the war and COVID 19 effects. The displacement and the harmful gender norms and stringent patriarchal societies that enable GBV have played a greater role in escalating effects to women and girls. The risk of gender‑based violence for women and girls in particular has increased dramatically due to the armed conflict and the displacement, the subsequent breakdown of law and the erosion of social structures and norms, as well as limited access to basic formal and informal protection mechanisms. Reports of sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home. In addition, the changes in traditional gender roles and responsibilities within the families, due to the displacement and the current living conditions, resulted often in an increasing of stress and tensions within the families leading to an increase of GBV episodes. Also, conflict and multiple displacements contributing to poverty, desperation, and unfamiliarity with legal systems, are significant factors increasing the vulnerability of women and girls. women are more vulnerable to violence and adolescents risk early and forced marriage; in addition to a growing number of children who lose one or the two parents due to random shelling and displacement. These have a long-term negative impact on women, children and their families’ psychosocial wellbeing. GBV services will include the provision of GBV case management, provided within exclusive women safe spaces within or adjacent to health facilities. We shall coordinate and strengthen referral pathways with health, mental health, and with other partners to provide comprehensive services to survivors. We will also deliver structured PSS activities for women and girls to allow survivors identify pathways to raise their confidence, relieve stress, build up their social network and develop positive coping mechanisms.
The impact of protracted conflict and displacement is also extremely severe on mental health (MH) and psychosocial wellbeing with WHO reporting an estimated prevalence rate of 22% . In addition, the crisis continues to expose civilians to multiple and severe protection risks. The MHPSS project will offer wide range of services that will prevent, assess and treat Psychological and emotional disorders and promote psychosocial resiliency for the affected population affected by trauma as a result of war in the North East Syria (NES). Services will include: comprehensive case management services, psychoeducation, community outreach services, capacity building and structured peer group support. There will also be upscaling of problem management plus intervention through training of trainers and providers, who will implement to the clients in order to reduce mild and moderate conditions of anxiety, depression, stress, alcohol and drug abuse etc. The project will integrate MHPSS services into primary healthcare facilities while making it possible for accessibility of services to all community members, both host and displaced, regardless of age, gender or impairment. This will improve the wellbeing of the vulnerable populations and community.

4 - Needs

The prevalence of GBV cases in NES had been high for years as a consequence of the conflict, the displacement and the harmful gender norms, and stringent patriarchal societies that enable GBV. This has been exacerbated due to the COVID-19 pandemic with increasing preliminary evidence from the countries most affected by the virus that indicates a negative impact on the trends, prevalence, and intensity of GBV. Protection risks, lack of access and availability of basic services and resources as well as family, community, and sectarian tensions has compounded to more issues. GBV clients lacks specialized MHPSS services which are vital for healing. Huge gaps exists in provision of mental health services; lack of mental health professionals, lack of psychotropics, limited community and family supports. These contribute to different MNS conditions such as severe emotional disorders, depression and anxiety, epilepsy, psychotic disorders, intellectual and developmental disorders are common in the setting. The conflict-affected population's MHPSS issues are compounded by worsening of pre-existing mental disorders; new problems caused by the conflict-related violence and displacement; and issues in adaptation related to the post-emergency context, for example living conditions in the countries of refuge. Due to the Turkish invasion in 2019 and COVID-19 pandemic, there is an increase in psychological distress and mental health conditions amongst women and girls, as well as a potential escalations or negligence of the needs of those living with chronic and severe mental, neurological, and substance use (MNS) disorders. 22% of conflict-affected populations experience a mental health conditions, including GBV survivors(WHO,2019).

5 - Implementation of activities

GBV,MHPSS and health will be integrated at Raqqa, Kisrah, Tal Baider, Qataniah and Abo Qoibeh facilities. GBV services will include: case management for women and girls in safe spaces within or near health facilities; coordinate and strengthen safe referral pathways with health, mental health, and other partners to provide comprehensive services to survivors; Engaging Men through Accountable Practice. Survivors will also benefit from PSS structured case management through trained and supervised Psychosocial Workers; referral/linkages to other specialized services, and utilize interventions such as, problem management plus (PM+) to improve wellbeing; Supervision and capacity building will be provided to ensure quality services are offered; Community psychoeducation and peer group sessions will also be provided to meet the needs identified by community members which will include topics such as self-help skills, problem management plus techniques, resiliency, detection, referrals and Psychological first aid; Community based outreach activities to identify of persons with limited access to PSS services and make appropriate referrals, create MHPSS awareness and advocacy. This will lead to reduction of emotional and psychological effects in women, girls, men and boys and inaddation create an avenue for collective and collaborative social support emanating from psychological distress.MEAL(Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning) will also be employed. There is an Indicator Performance Tracking Table(IPTT)tool for each active grant supporting in monitoring of the projects according to implementation plan and timelines.This assists in tracking indicator and targets progress, know gaps and spot opportunities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

GBV/PSS protection risks include: High levels of violence and criminality are a risk to the safety of staff and beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and humanitarians face a real risk to their physical safety and security due to ongoing violence, targeting by different groups, and being caught up in conflict-related clashes.• Resistance or backlash against GBV and PSS staff from sections of the community leaders on GBV/PSS messaging, from family or community on women participating in empowerment, and family members, perpetrators, and local community when dealing with sensitive cases. There is the potential of receiving threats as well as bodily harm in retaliatory incidents.• Working with children vulnerable GBV survivors, women and girls, encompasses the risk of potential sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), physical abuse. The lack of quality services and referral increases the risk of further stigma. The NES context has a high degree of poverty that may make clients resort to commercial sex, sex for food, and other related abuses to security needs. Risk of violence/harm toward staff during PSS sessions-certain clients may have mental disturbances that can potentially lead to abuse to staff. This can happen as well due to lack of income to most the mental patients who due to their mental status can’t be able to secure meaningful employment and thus demand financial support from the case managers, who are not in a position to help.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

-Beneficiaries seeking special treatment to access services: The beneficiaries will be explained to on the policy that we don't charge for services and any engagement.-Dual relationship between helpers and clients: The clients may become emotionally entangled to the client thus making a window for exploitation. To mitigate this all staff shall be trained on PSEA and disciplinary measures will be employed incase of any violation.MEAL team has set up Community Based Feedback Mechanism (CBFRM) in all project implementation locations and seeks views of the affected population to provide us with their feedback on our interventions and establish community trust. MEAL team regular conducts community consultations with affected people to know what is their preference way to raise their feedback/complaints through interviews. The MEAL team has different safe, accessible and available channels such as Hotline number or text, WhatsApp, Complaints box, Feedback Email and face to face with MEAL team on the ground.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The overall security situation remains stable across NES, The NGO is working closely with the security cluster and other INGO/NGOs security units and maintaining the daily duties accordingly. * Change of military/political control of geographic locations where the NGO implements programs; the risks a mitigated through maintaining strong lines of communication with global and regional security in order to pre-empt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. *Inflation and increased cost of commodities has resulted in a rise in civil unrest which have weakened the self-administration governance in rural areas or Raqqa and Deir ez Zor resulting in a rise in the tribal conflict. Mitigation will be by short-term stops on some programs with tensions became violent. *COVID-19 has placed restriction of movement, use of public facilities for program implementation and workshops and has required at times for staff to work from home to avoid risk of infection; per Self Administration decree, medical staff can only visit one medical facility to avoid cross-contamination risks. *Inadvertent or unintended engagement with actors listed under international terrorism lists; Bridger Insight (BI) checks are conducted for each vendor/supplier/partner. The NGO has multiple sources for procurement and maintains procurement plans and processes to mitigate delays.
