Project Summary

This Project is meant to contribute to transition returnees, IDPs and Host communities to resilience by supporting emergency food Production through distribution of vegetable Kits, and crops seeds and reduction of dependency on agriculture by supporting income generation activities, livestock restocking and support to fishing communities. As well by supporting the returnees, host communities and IDPs, it Promotes peaceful co-existence among the population whose social fabric has been destroyed by the conflict in South Sudan. This Project will also seek to integrate the current Cordaid WASH program being implemented in Koch, and Pigi/Canal Counties of Unity and Jonglei state respectively. Cordaid already has a long history of implementing similar projects which has yield significant results in transforming IDP, Host communities and returnees livelihoods.
Cordaid is geographically well placed because it has at least 10 field offices in the Equatoria region, Upper Nile region and Bahr el Ghazal region, well equipped with vehicles, Motor bikes and quad bikes to facilitate project implementations including internet connectivity. Cordaid is currently working with a budget of about $16 Million covering Emergency Response, Resilience, Health and strategic partnership. Cordaid has well experienced staff both at the Country office and the field offices among which include: FSL Manager, Agronomist and Value chain specialist, Micro finance specialist, M&E manager staff and roving FSL officers that can be deployed to any field location of need.
Cordaid also has been part of the HRP 2018 and received SSHF grant for implementation of Vegetable and fishing distribution in Koch County of Unity state for a project that ended in March 2019. Apart from FSL Cordaid has been implementing SSHF project under the HRP 2019 and received grants for implementation of WASH activities (Drilling of boreholes and Hygiene Promotion) in Koch County of Unity state and Canal/Pigi of Jongolei state and Health Projects on Ebola Response in Nimule border crossing point of Eastern Equatoria state.

1 - Needs

Many areas in Unity state have been constrained by road access and protracted conflict between the opposition and government, coupled with inter-clan conflicts which makes populations ever unreached by services. While the Corridor of Jonglei Bordering Upper Nile has been constrained by access to reach the communities trapped in the middle as the only route is by water as such markets are made dyfunctional. However with the recent peace deal signed between warring parties and the government in South Sudan, there is relative stability in the country as such some Internally Displaced persons (IDPs) and refugees have started returning to their homes and if the peace deal implementation will continue to hold, more people will return to their homes as hope for stability continue to reign. These beneficiaries normally come with nothing to start from, as such all the burden of feeding the returnees is soldered by the relatives as a receiving population. According to IOM Round 6 tracking data of June 2019, a total of 1,271,487 returnees were recorded. while according to IPC report of September 2019, a total of 6.35 million people are in need of urgent intervention in South Sudan (IPC Level 3+) as a result of these scenarios (returnees, inter-clan conflict and displacement, natural socks like drought and floods etc), according the IPC report 88,000 people are in level 3+ by September- December 2019 while 113,000 people will be in level 3+ by January-April 2020 in Guit and Koch Counties. The IPC target for 2020 being 98,200 people( 37710-Guit, 60490- Koch). While in Canal/Pigi of Jonglei 58,500 people being targeted under FSL allocation for 2020 allocation out of the total the 65,000 people which are projected to be in IPC 3+ between January -April 2020. Thus his Project targets the population within the HRP population figures for 2020. In 2019 Cordaid carried rapid need assessments in Yambio and Canal/Pigi which generated the need to implement the FSL Projects in 2020. We applied household surveys using Kobo apps on smartphones, as well as conducting FGD and KII in selected communities. During the assessments, their needs were prioritized and community members also shared their preferred options in response. From the HH survey, we analysed key indicators like Food Consumption Score (FCS), No. of meals per day, access to water, etc. These supported our formulation of response plans. This Proposal is meant to transition returnees and IDPs to resilience by engaging them in: (1) livelihoods activities such as Agricultural production on quick maturity crops, Fishing and small scale businesses and training on the selected livelihoods options. (2) By supporting both returnees and Host communities the project will enhance interaction and Peaceful co-existence between returnees and receiving communities to bridge the gap that has broken social fabric which used to exist in the communities. This Project will integrate and complement other Cordaid Projects like WASH under the SSHF and Food Security and Livelihoods Projects funded by other donors including SSHF which was implemented in Koch under the HRP 2018 and ended in March 2019, and its health Projects. Cordaid will works in collaboration with other partners which deliver Programs in Peace building, GBV, Nutrition and Protection, this is meant to produce a greater impact.

This project will contribute to two FSL Cluster Objectives, including:CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 5, 4 and 3)1.1- Community Mobilization 1.2 - Beneficiary identification and selection1.1- Vegetable and tools distribution to 3000 household beneficiaries (18000 individuals)1.2- Crop seeds distribution to 3000 household beneficiaries (18000 individuals)1.3- training on agronomic practices of vegetable and crops to 6000 beneficiaries (36000 individuals)1.4- set up demonstration garden for practical learning at 30 groups (15 for vegetable and 15 for crop) CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (in IPC 5, 4 and 3)For the below activities, Cordaid has applied the modality and we successfully implemented in Wau, Koch, Rubkona, Malakal, Aburoc, and Pigi/Canal in recent years. From evaluations, it was found that most beneficiaries supported under this modality have livelihoods skills improved and income increased. Thus Cordaid will replicate the success in this Project. 2.1- Community Mobilization 2.2- Beneficiary identification and selection 2.3- support beneficiaries to establish small scale businesses mostly women -1000 beneficiaries 2.4- support communities along fishing areas with fishing kits to increase household food and income- 800 beneficiaries 2.5- Conduct livestock restocking through livestock faire 1500 beneficiaries 2.6- Form Village savings and loan associations to enhance sustainability of the small scale businesses and offer training on VSLA cycle (at least 10 groups)2.5- Train beneficiaries on Business management skills- 1000 beneficiaries 2.8- Train beneficiaries on livestock management practices (health, feeding and breeding)- 1500 beneficiaries 2.9- Project post distribution monitoring 2.10 Market assessment The project beneficiaries will be linked to other sectors e.g. WASH Project implemented by Cordaid for Vegetable Production, Malnourished children and GBV cases linked to Nutrition and GBV/Protection Projects by other partners.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries will have their rights respected by having choice of which livelihoods to participate. 6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This Project targets affected communities like returnees, IDPs and host communities and above all gives incentives for voluntary return of IDPs and refugees by creating livelihoods means start life with in the environment of return.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, this Project is linked to the government structure of Relief and Rehabilitation Commission objective, secondly the Project is linked to the objective of creating stability and community peaceful co-existence.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this Project is aligned with the national objective of supporting return of IDPs and refugees which is part of the Peace agreement.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

This Project is meant to transition the target population into Resilience by engaging the communities in active livelihoods activities to reduce dependency on food aid.The Project is also meant to diversify sources of livelihoods to increase household food and income through engaging income generation activities, livestock restocking and fishing apart from agricultural production. By supporting both the returnees and Host communities, the project creates harmony in the community of return.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, this Project will work hand in hand with government line departments and ministries in order take over after the project end. Secondly this Project will work with Project committees which will be involved in beneficiary selection and Project feedback mechanism from the community, apart from the other feedback option like post distribution monitoring. The committees will be empowered to take up efforts created by the Project. Chiefs in all the Project Cordaid work, they are normally part of the Project committees. This enhances recognition of the local authority and powers for Project sustainability. The Project beneficiaries will be linked Cordaid Resilience program such as Agri-business and DRR projects.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Cordaid will ensure during establishment of demonstration plots, the piece of land dedicated for the demonstration plot purpose will be contributed voluntarily by the community with consent of the chief of the location and agreement of the beneficiaries. To avoid interference of stakeholders in beneficiary selection Cordaid will ensure that beneficiary selection committees are formed and briefed about their role in mitigating selection biasness. Even after the selection process is completed Cordaid will conduct a verification exercise in a public meeting to ensure the community confirms that the selected people belong to the vulnerability criteria.During distribution of project inputs, Cordaid will ensure supplies are transported to the closest locations possible to avoid beneficiaries traveling long distance leave their households unattended for longer hours.
