Project Summary

Sudan continued needing urgent humanitarian assistance throughout the year 2022, the extreme needs are triggered by conflicts, economic crises, decease outbreak (covid-19 & monkeypox), floods and instability of political situation. High food prices and reduced access to cash income continue to limit poor household purchasing power and food access. Increased intercommunal clashes and flooding also continue to limit households' access to their main livelihood activities. According to Sudan situation report released by OCHA Oct.2022 the number of people affected by seasonal rains and flash floods across Sudan reach 349,000 and 4,800 heads of livestock being lost in addition to destruction of 12,100 feddans of agricultural land moreover about 211,000 people were displaced across the country due to inter-communal conflict and armed attacks between January and September 2022.
According to the report the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry report late planting and below-normal cultivation in most traditional and semi-mechanized rain-fed and irrigated sectors due to delayed rainfall in June and July and the high cost and shortages of agricultural inputs. However, heavy rainfall and flooding in late July and August continue to impact pasture and crop development in many areas of, Kassala, and South Darfur states. The compounding impact of late planting, high input prices, and flooding will likely compromise the upcoming harvest. Between July and August, staple food prices increased seasonally by 10-15 percent, remaining approximately 170-235 percent higher than August 2021 and about seven times higher than the five-year average across most monitored markets. The increase is driven by seasonally reduced market supply, increased demand, high transportation and production costs, and the continued devaluation of the SDG.
The project will cover 5 states of Alight operation areas include East Darfur, South Darfur, Kassala, Gedaref and West Kordofan states. In total project target 9,500 Households (29,640 Female & 27,360 Male) will benefit. The intervention will target the most severely affected people under IPC 3 and IPC4 mainly from IDPs, Returnees and host communities under the three SOs objectives.
In South Darfur the project will work in three localities; Kas, Gereida, Ed al fursan targeting 2,165 households (6,755 Female & 6,235 Male). In East Darfur the project will cover Adila, Abukarinka and Sharia localities targeting 2,590 households (8,100 Female & 7,476 Male). In Kassala the project will target 1850 HH (5,772Female & 5,328 male) in Reifi khashm elgirba, and Reifi Wad Elhello. In Gedaref the project will work in Al mafaza , Galabat ash-shargiah with total target 2,175 HH (6,786Female & 6,264 male) I West Kurdofan the project will cover Babanusa and Al dibab localites targeting 720 HH (2,246 Female & 2,074 Male).
The communities in these areas are Mainly IDPs, Returnees, Pastoralist and host communities and vulnerable individuals affected by conflict, disease outbreaks such as Covid-19, Monkey pox, floods, communal conflicts, exposed to food insecure due to unavailability and lack of access in addition to small-scale farmers who face major constraints in farming because of high costs of production, post-harvest losses, access to farmlands and etc. Thus, the proposed Project strive to deliver results in the following three FSL sector objectives;
SO1- Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through lifesaving and life sustaining food assistance.
SO2- Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.
SO3- Improve communities’ capacity to sustain household’s livelihoods through the rehabilitation / Building of productive infrastructure as well as training services
Relevant activities will be implemented to realize the above objectives.

1 - Needs

Sudan Continued facing high need of humanitarian food assistance throughout the year 2022, the extreme needs are triggered by instability of political situation, economic crises, decease outbreak, floods. Reduced access to income continues to limit purchasing power also high prices affect households' food access. Increased intercommunal clashes and flooding also continue to limit households' access to their main livelihood activities. According to Sudan situation report released by OCHA Oct.2022 the number of people affected by seasonal rains and flash floods across Sudan reach 349,000 and 4,800 heads of livestock being lost in addition to destruction of 12,100 feddans of agricultural land moreover about 211,000 people were displaced across the country due to inter-communal conflict and armed attacks between January and September 2022. According to the report the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry report, due to delayed rainfall in June and July and the high cost and shortages of agricultural inputs, it is witnessed late planting and below-normal cultivation in most traditional and semi-mechanized rain-fed and irrigated sectors. Moreover, heavy rainfall and flooding in late July and August continue to impact pasture and crop development in many areas of Kassala, and South Darfur states. The compounding impact of late planting, high input prices, and flooding will likely compromise the upcoming harvest. Between July and August, staple food prices increased seasonally by 10-15 percent, remaining approximately 170-235 percent higher than August 2021 and about seven times higher than the five-year average across most monitored markets. The increase is driven by seasonally reduced market supply, increased demand, high transportation and production costs, and the continued devaluation of the SDG.In 2023, HNO 2023 estimates, the number of acutely food insecure people continued to increase for the third year in a row, reaching a record 11.7 million people in the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 and above between June-September 2022 due to dry spells, reduced grain production, high prices and eroding livelihoods due to the multi-year economic crisis. This Food security and Livelihoods project will target 5 states (South & East Darfur states, West Kordofan, Kassala, and Gedaref states) and 12 localities (Kas, Gereida and Ed al fursan in South Darfur, Adila , Abukarinka and Sharia in East Darfur , and Reifi khashm elgirba, and Reifi nahr atbara in Kassala , and Al mafaza , Galabat ash-shargiah in Gadaref state , and Babanusa and Al dibab localites in West Kordofan) which are in IPC 3 & 4 category and within Alight operation areas. South Darfur is the most populous and has the highest number of people in need and internally displaced persons (IDPs), hosted in 31 camps and 10 neighborhoods (according to IOM Mobility Tracking). East Darfur State has been experiencing continuous inter-tribal conflicts, protracted displacement, chronic poverty, economic crisis, food insecurity, poor basic social services, flash flooding, and refugee influx. It also hosted about 606,700 people, mainly IDPs, returnees and asylum seekers who need humanitarian assistance. West Kordofan also experienced tribal conflict and flooding in 2022, several people displaced within and outside of the state. Kassala State is affected by conflict, flooding, drought, disease outbreaks and economic inflation. Gedaref State is also affected by flooding, displacements and high refugee crisis. The economic crisis, food insecurity and conflict are the main drivers of humanitarian needs in all targeted states and localities while Conflict, protracted displacement, food insecurity, climate-related hazards (floods, dry spells) and inadequate access to basic social services are the exacerbating factors.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Overall, the project activities are designed to address the immediate food security needs of people in IPC 3 & 4 status and strengthen agricultural production and food security by supporting IDP farmers (both in rural areas and IDP camps), Returnees and host communities those have access to the agricultural land. Also, it supports semi-sedentary people to improve food security and livestock productivity. Moreover, the project helps targeted communities to preserve their existing assets than sell for buying food. The Project activities will contribute to the general humanitarian response objective and the three cluster specific objectives; SO1- Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through lifesaving and life sustaining food assistance. SO2- Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives. SO3- Improve communities’ capacity to sustain household’s livelihoods through the rehabilitation / Building of productive infrastructure as well as training services To attain the above objectives; Under SO 1 the project will implement the following activities: lifesaving & life-sustaining cash assistance (Voucher/Cash)' At least total 3000 HH aggregated by (1530 Female /1470 Male ) will be targeted through cash transfer activity to fulfill their family needs.Under SO2 the project will target 5000 HHs (2250F/2450M) through the implementation of food security activities include. 1, Procurement and distribution of agricultural certified seeds, 2, Purchasing and Distribution of agricultural local tools , 3, Provision of extension service trainings, Animal husbandry training , 4, Purchasing and distribution of goats mother goats, 5, Purchasing and Distribution of Salt lick, 6, Purchasing and distribution of ready-made fodder, 7, Purchasing & Distribution of Donkey Carts and distributing cash for Livelihoods. Under SO3, the project will engage affected communities to rehabilitate community assets that may include rehabilitation of schools, health facilities, roads and drainage structures, cleaning of public gathering places such as markets, sport fields, etc. In these activities labor intensive jobs are created that engage local communities in mass. Daily wages will be paid to people who participate in these activities that will support them in getting additional income as they improve their communal assets.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project includes gender mainstreaming activities recommended by the FSL cluster. Each activities will be tuned with gender sensitivity. All distribution activities in this project will be done according to the tradition of the people, ensure privacy and dignity of women and girls. Targeted beneficiaries will be selected in consultation with community, and according to the FSL cluster prioritization criteria, gender is key factor, for example HH headed by women will be prioritized for support. Gender inequality is striking in Sudan, as evidenced in significant disparities in education, literacy, and quality of life. The gender inequality index in Sudan is 0.708. The maternal mortality ratio is 750 women per 100 000 female live births and 48% of women are illiterate. Moreover, Sudan has neither signed nor ratified the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW). Divorce proceedings are unequal in that while both husbands and wives may ask for divorce, obtaining it is much more difficult for women. There are no laws prohibiting domestic violence, and its occurrences is common. Police intervention is rare, unless contacted. Sanctions exist at the level of the community to deal with such situations; however, they are ineffective as a deterrent. While in traditional communities' women are often expected to stay at home after marriage, among educated women, particularly in the cities, it is increasingly common for women to remain employed after marriage, therefore supporting and focusing on women on any intervention can help build peace across cultural divides and in re-building civil society in unstable regions. Gender informs the power relationships and social bridges or divisions that are drivers of peace or conflict in specific contexts. This intervention will be an opportunity to incorporate gender equality in post-conflict reconstruction and improve gender relations. The project will implement some activities that are gender focused, such as agriculture startup kits and peanut butter making focusing only on women as key food security actors at household level. The project will also be focusing on empowering women by building their capacities and skills through trainings so that they able to participate in the decision-making structures which are shaping the events and outcomes in their own.Alight will continue mainstreaming gender in implementation of FSL, the women play vital production role than the men in the field of agriculture in Darfur, as stated by FAO gender reports, moreover the women participation is over 60% in all phase of agriculture, planting, harvesting, and chiming. The projects will ensure that the staff receive trainings in all areas that mitigate the risk for women and young girls include protection mainstreaming, humanitarian codes of conduct, child protection policies.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and plans all through the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern. The Project will ensure at all persons of concern have equal and non- discriminatory access to protection, assistance and solutions, this will build on their resilience and recovery. The project will engage much better with boys, girls, men and women while in their safe spaces. Integration of other Alight protection projects will be enhanced such that activities like awareness raising, dialogues, will be integrated in our FSL programming approach. Alight will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions and complaints) using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non- sensitive feedback e.g. use of complaints boxes, toll free hot lines. In regard to PSEA; All Alight staffs have committed to and signed PSEA policy, Child Protection policy, Code of Conduct as well as the engaged community based networks Alight is a member of National and State levels PSEA task force and regularly coordinates with other partners in relevant PSEA forums. Under the Project, we will conduct Safeguarding and SEA risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. Alight has contributed to Inter Agency Action Plan for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and will ensure Zero Tolerance - No Excuse to SEA in all its operations and project. Jointly with the PSEA task force, we will develop relevant SEA IEC materials and distribute them in communities, raise awareness on SEA, strengthen community-based complaints mechanisms, enhance reporting and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.
