Needs

The protracted protection crisis in the oPt, largely attributable to Israel’s ongoing occupation, now in its 52th year, the ongoing internal divide and violations of international law, remains the driver of humanitarian need in this context. Despite years of relative absence of armed conflict since the 2014 hostilities, there has been a sharp deterioration in the protection situation in the Gaza Strip, as a result of the ongoing crisis at the fence, and increased vulnerabilities in the context of the 11-year blockade including an economy in free-fall. In the West Bank, a coercive environment intensifies, driven by demolitions, forced evictions, discriminatory planning, access restrictions, settlement expansion and settler violence, generating a risk of forcible transfer for many Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem and the Israeli-controlled part of Hebron city, H2. New legislation and administrative steps potentially limiting the ability of individuals and human rights organizations to challenge Israeli policies and practices, has been introduced in tandem with an increasingly restrictive space for civil society. As in Gaza, the use of excessive force by Israeli security forces remains a concern. This project will contribute directly to the implementation of the oPt Humanitarian Response Plan, and support the HCT in ensuring the centrality of protection in the humanitarian response in this context. The PC has identified the following key protection concerns: lack of respect for IHL and IHRL, including grave violations against children, killings and injuries, protection threats arising from the blockade, recurrent hostilities and human rights violations arising from internal political divisions in Gaza, ERW contamination from recurrent conflicts in Gaza, risk of forcible transfer faced by Palestinians in the West Bank, child protection needs, including psychosocial distress, protection concerns arising from the presence and expansion of settlements, including settler violence, gender based violence driven by the protracted protection crisis and the prevailing patriarchal culture in Palestinian society, and increasing need for protective measures targeting human rights defenders and humanitarian actors. The PC has identified the different impact that these protection concerns have on women, girls, boys and men, persons with disabilities, and how certain groups are more vulnerable than others due for example to their source of livelihoods, (herders, Bedouins) or location (ARA or certain refugee camps in the West Bank). Against this background, the protection cluster is working towards two key results in 2018: Increased respect for IHL and HRL and accountability for violations; and protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.OHCHR, as PC lead, has overall responsibility for ensuring coordinated protection responses. The Protection Cluster Working Group (PCWG) meets in Ramallah and Gaza City. The PCWG has the following sub-groups: Child Protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Working Group, Working Group on Grave Violations, Legal Task Force, Settler Violence Core Group, ARA Core Group, Legal Advice Group (on international legal analysis), and GBV Working Group. OHCHR is responsible for ensuring that all protection responses are coordinated taking into account gender and diversity, and the differing protection needs of various groups within the population. Effective coordination between the sub-groups of the cluster is essential and is achieved through the Strategic Advisory Group, chaired by the Cluster Coordinator which also focuses on achieving a one Palestine or national approach to the protection work. Gender considerations are integral to the protection response. Notably, the need for strengthening both gender analysis and gender sensitive responses across the Cluster's sub-sectors.

Activities

Given the large membership of the cluster and the broad scope of the issues coordinated under the cluster, sufficient human resources are essential to ensure leadership and coordination, as detailed in the project budget. In line with below outlined activities, and working with the chairs of the Protection Cluster sub-groups and with the members of the PCWG, the coordination team will: 1. Provide vision and leadership for the Protection Cluster, based on consultations with members and stakeholders;2. Undertake day-to-day coordination of the PCWG, including convening meetings, information sharing and ensuring PCWG input to humanitarian processes (e.g. HPC); 3. Chair the Core Groups on Settler Violence and the ARA, and the Legal Advice Group;4. Ensure coordination with the work of the PC sub-groups and AoRs. Related activities include ensuring coordination between working groups to ensure complementarity 5. Identify and propose action to address gaps, including in information gathering, management, analysis and coordinated response to priority protection concerns, with a focus on increased analysis on gender specific vulnerabilities and gender specific impact of ongoing violations of IHL and IHRL; 6. Strengthen detection and referral for protection cases between protection actors and for protection actors to make and receive external referrals (from other clusters/ sectors)7. Strengthen Protection Information Management under the protection cluster, including by providing support and capacity building for regular (quarterly) reporting on 4Ws within the relevant Working Groups of the Protection Cluster (GBV and Child Protection/MHPSS).8. Coordinate joint monitoring and information analysis settlement related issues and in the ARA;8. Coordinate joint human-rights based advocacy for affected populations under relevant priorities of the HCT Advocacy Strategy (e.g. accountability for settler violence, violations in the ARA, forced displacement and forcible transfer), including through liaising with UN human rights procedures and mechanisms, and link Protection Cluster advocacy to the work of the HCT Advocacy Working Group; 9. Through the Legal Advice Group, coordinate the discussion of questions of international human rights and humanitarian law, and the provision of feedback to PCWG members on possible legal arguments to assist in their advocacy and other protection work; 10. Through the Legal Task Force, which OHCHR will co-chair with NRC in 2019, ensure that up-to-date information, legal advice, and analysis on emblematic cases, trends and protection risks is available to the ICCG and the HCT. Similarly, the LTF will continue to provide legal advice on the provision of material assistance in the West Bank;10. Work with the other clusters and sectors to mainstream protection, in order to ensure that Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments are fulfilled and that protection is made central to the humanitarian response in all stages of the HPC. Activities in this regard include trainings and technical support to the provision of guidelines, checklists, and coordination of operational responses to protection issues;11. Build capacity of cluster members to strengthen communication with and accountability to affected populations, including through beneficiary feedback on protection responses;12. Needs assessments: work to identify baseline data and needs of persons with disabilities in the context of humanitarian action; support the efforts of working groups to address assessment gaps as identified in the HNO;13. Under the lead of the Protection Cluster gender focal point, provide support to cluster partners in gender mainstreaming all their activities, including by providing capacity building support to partners to enhance gender sensitivity in the planning, implementation and monitoring of projects submitted to the HRP;13. Support resource mobilization by cluster partners
