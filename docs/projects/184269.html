Project Summary

Somalia is among countries recording the highest number of grave violations against children in the world. The ongoing conflict, poverty and lack of opportunities have driven many Somali youths from their homes with the aspiration to find better lives, sources of income and even education. According to the 2022 Somalia Humanitarian Needs Overview, up to 66 per cent of the population in need of protection services in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence and, 1.1 million are IDPs. Adolescent girls are at risk of child marriage, sexual violence, and other forms of gender-based violence. Additionally, children with disabilities experience exacerbated vulnerabilities because of societal attitudes, environmental, and institutional barriers that they continue to face. Existing child protection systems, structures and services are inadequate, especially in hard to reach areas, despite concerted efforts by communities, government, donors and partners to deliver life-saving services. This has resulted in widespread failure to promote and protect the rights of children and has intensified child protection risks because of the negative impact on their household livelihoods thus exposing them to negative coping mechanisms such as child labour.
Against this backdrop, WV aims to provide age-appropriate, disability-sensitive quality CP services that will be focused on prevention, mitigation and response. WV will prioritize hard-to-reach areas and will aim to provide these services in Somaliland, Puntland, South West State and Jubaland where the organization has a physical presence with ongoing leverageable interventions in health, nutrition WASH, GBV and FSL. This project will focus on increasing awareness on prevention of all forms of violence against children by the creation of a safe and protective environment both at home and in the community. Tailored messages packaged for different audiences will be disseminated in a culturally sensitive and inclusive manner. For instance, the young people and adolescents will be reached through social media and influencers while the conventional radio spots will be used to reach the elderly caregivers. Campaigns for school enrollment has been factored in this project for children as a strategy of protecting children from violence associated with being out of school. The schools will therefore also be targeted with capacity building on positive discipline measures, life skills and PFA training for teachers and CEC members. The project will be intentional in ensuring the participation of people with disabilities by working closely with DPOs to circulate child protection messages. Most importantly, the schools and learning institutions will continue to be used by this project as an access point to children and teachers to access referral to child protection services. Community-level awareness-raising will continue to be utilized to increase the capacity of the community to identify child protection violations and take timely action for response. Community child protection committees, caseworkers and caregivers capacity building will also form an important part of the project. To meet individual children's needs, case management approach has been adopted to provide services to children affected by armed conflict, unaccompanied and separated children as well as children exposed to SGBV. To reach children with group PSS, the project will utilize existing community resource centres, child-friendly spaces to provide structured recreational and educational activities for psychosocial support. Direct MHPSS services will be facilitated by caseworkers both physically and remotely. WV has robust safeguarding policy framework and measures in place that will ensure all interventions are safe for children and adult beneficiaries. WV employs a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash injected provides greater choice and dignity by allowing the targeted beneficiaries to have purchase power and choice on prioritiesed individual needs, while at the same time stimulating local markets and improving the market cash flow. WV will prioritize unconditional cash transfers to enable families with an alternative to meet the specific household supplies needs and unique gender needs of the children. Cash assistance will also revitalize the local economy by promoting local businesses and providing a source of livelihoods.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

WV is a member of the cash working group and participates in market functionality assessments conducted by the FSL projects already existing in the proposed districts covered by this project.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

*1. Risk of Minority clans/tribes excluded from humanitarians assistance and consultations*This risk will be addressed by providing for multiple awareness raising mechanisms on available project services including factoring in mobile outreaches to far and hard to reach locations to ensure timely access to services. To understand the demographics within the districts, assessments will be conducted which will inform targeting and beneficiary selection criteria. A mapping of the available CP service providers alongside the telephone of their focal points will be displayed in public places accessible to all such as health facilities and other social amenities like schools in the Somali language. *2. Risk women and girls exposure to SEA in relation to accessing humanitarian services* WVI already has an existing community response mechanism which will be integrated in this project to increase access to feedback and complaints options existing in the project areas. The toll free number 364 will be displayed in all service delivery sites in the Somali language. Monitoring of the response to complaints in this system usually forms part of the senior leadership team monthly updates until the issue raised is resolved. Suggestion boxes will continue to be used in schools alongside the setting up of help desks where boys and girls share concerns about WVI service delivery including reporting SEA concerns. WVI also has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy that strongly communicated WVI's zero tolerance to SEA which all staff are trained on annually and sign a self declaration to adhere to the behaviour protocols which covers PSEA guidelines. Staff found culpable of SEA usually face termination of contract. The various means to reports all forms of staff misconduct is usually shared during community meetings which includes reporting to trained safeguarding focal points, and anonymous toll free line to WV Global Safeguarding Unit. *3. Risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance and indiscriminate attacks from armed actors*WV staff has a fulltime security team (composed of a Security Advisor and field-based Security Officers)who continuously monitor the security context, liaising closely with United Nations Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS) International NGO Safety Organization (INSO) Somalia and the local security apparatus to keep abreast of the evolving context and make informed decisions. The project will also make use of staff weekly updates on the regional security situation to inform activity implementation.
