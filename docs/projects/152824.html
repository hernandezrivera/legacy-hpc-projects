Project Summary

This project aims to implement GBV prevention and response services in Five Payams in Yambio county i.e. Hai Masia, Saura, Gbangasu, Gangura and Remenze Payams in Western Equatoria State and also Four Payams in Kapoeta East County i.e. Kauto, Mogos, Narus and Natinga Payams in Eastern Equatoria State aims to contribute to two protection cluster strategic objectives
1. Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas
2. Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
The plan has six activities (case management targeting 200 GBV survivors, Psychosocial support targeting 440 beneficiaries, Establishment and programming of five (5) Women and Girls’ Friendly spaces in Yambio County (Hai Masia, Saura, Gbangasu, Gangura and Remenze Payams) and four (4) Women and Girls’ Friendly spaces in Kapoeta East County(Kauto, Mogos, Narus and Natinga Payams)to benefit 2,200 women/girls and out-reach awareness targeting 5682 women, men, girls and boys. The project will also carry out six mobile and static protection assessments and continuous monitoring whose reports will be sharedto assist in informing protection and multi sector response and advocacy, establishment of new referral mechanisms and supporting the existing to help referral of 100 beneficiaries and protection awareness raising and prevention messaging. This project plan targets in total eight thousand, six hundred and twenty two (8622) beneficiaries (men 1293 =15%, women 3018= 35%, boys 1,293 = 15% and girls 3,018=35%). 50% of beneficiaries will come from Yambio County and the rest from Kapoeta East County. The project is developed following grassroots consultations in Yambio and Kapoeta North Counties. The total Project cost will be USD 250,000.
The project has established mechanisms aimed at enhancing accountability to affected populations through provision of information about assistance, protection from sexual exploitation and abuses, continuous consultation with affected population beyond community leaders, Feedbacks and complain mechanisms and community engagement monitoring and evaluation are put in place.
To ensure the centrality of protection in humanitarian actions, this project is based on protection risk analysis that considered specific needs of different groups. It also seeks to Identify and address barrier to access that different population could face. These project activities are made in a way that promotes gender equality and addresses protection threats such as violence, coercion, exploitation etc. The project tools are tailored to specifically measure beneficiaries’: accesses, safety and dignity.

Needs

Since the eruption of the violent conflict in South Sudan in 2013 and renewed fighting in Juba in 2016; South Sudan has been undergoing catastrophic humanitarian crisis. Millions of the South Sudanese population were/are displaced both as internal displaced persons and refugees in neighboring Countries in dire needs for humanitarian assistance. In accordance with the UNOCHA Humanitarian Bulletin issue 01/18th January 2018, more than 1.9 million South Sudanese were internally displaced and over 2 million had fled as refugees to neighboring Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Sudan and Democratic Republic of Congo. There have been total breakdown of rule of law. The peace agreement that was signed between different warring parties in 2015 collapsed months after formation of transitional government in 2016 leading to more fighting especially in Equatoria and Western Bahr el Gazala Regions. Though the revitalized peace agreement was signed in September 2018, it is yet to translate on ground. The crisis have made millions vulnerable and depending on humanitarian relief aids. The economy has since collapsed with hyperinflation. Humanitarian workers have been targeted by armed groups. There are huge humanitarian needs all over South Sudan including Yambio and Great Kapoetafor which this project is targeting. In Greater Kapoeta area,the major humanitarian issues include recurring food insecurity to thousands of civilians most of them internal displaced persons due to on-going insecurity in surrounding villages caused by cattle raids, fighting among the communities in Pibor areas forcing many people to seek refuge in Kapoeta East. There are high cases of malnutrition in Kapoeta area especially among children under five years due to inadequate access to nutritious food. There are prevalent cases of physical and sexual violence against women in Kapoeta East and Yambio due to reasons such as alcoholism among the men, weak rule of law and Justice System, emergence of armed groups, cultural norms etc. The displacement of communities makes displaced women/girls more vulnerable to exploitation, sexual abuses. Local cultural norms do not encourage respect for women/girls rights. Still some women/girls in both areas do not report the cases of GBV to the partners as such; there are needs to continue to create awareness. While there are referral mechanisms in Yambioand Kapoeta Eastsuch services are limited. There are prevalent cases of child pregnancy, forced marriage and most girls do not continue beyond primary three levels particularly in Kapoeta East. While there are quite good number of agencies working on livelihood and nutrition projects, there are nospecialized agencies rendering GBV services in Yambioand Kapoeta East and the needs continue to exceed the available services in the two Counties. This project will enable GREDA to address the critical gaps in GBV programming in the two targetedcounties. In the two targeted Counties;GBV needs remain high and requires continues actions. Adolescent girls have particularly been targets of multiple forms of violence including forced marriages, sex slavery, child pregnancy and denial of opportunities to go and finish their education. GREDA’s experience of working on GBV project inthe Kapoeta and Yambio indicates that majority of adolescent girls are out of school, experiencing growing levels of sexual violence, early and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse. Women/girls have limited access to GBV services especially in the far away payams. The local cultural norms do not allow women to freely discuss GBV issues publically. The fear of cultural norms contributes to failure of women to report cases of GBV. Culturally;the adolescent girls as early as 12 years are considered mature and ready for marriage. Women and girls are rarely involved in decision making including on issues that affect them and their wellbeing.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project intends to continuously and timely provide beneficiaries with information regarding assistance provided by the project and others that require referral. There will be monthly meetings to inform the beneficiaries. Information can also be made available through beneficiary groups like women groups. In places where there are local radios, there will be radio talk show pass information to the beneficiaries. Use of announcements in social functions such as funeral prayers to inform will be prioritized. Equal announcements will be done in schools and health centres to reach many beneficiaries. Referral pathway will be developed or updated and disseminated to wider beneficiaries. Visibility materials such as T shirts, sign posts, brochures and where possible billboards will be developed to provide beneficiaries with information about assistance. Prevention messages with emphasis on referrals to services will be developed in context specific ways. Volunteers will visit homes to inform beneficiaries about assistance provided. Persons that have benefited from the assistance will be made ambassadors to go and speak to others. Focus group discussions will be organized for different groups to help pass information conveniently to different groups. Reports will be published and made public to beneficiaries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages to specific population groups will be tailored for example women and girls’ pictures will be used to advertise existence services. Literate group of population will need posters or brochures while illiterate group will need verbal or symbolic communication. Efforts will be made to translate messages for example to Zande and Balanda languages to be used in or Toposa or Didinga in Kapoeta East. Sensitivities to local cultures, religion or gender will strictly be taken into consideration. Staffing priority in specific location locations will be to local population to help in contextualizing messages to specific areas. Local artists such as local band groups will be used to pass specifically contextualized messages.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

GREDA exercises zero tolerance policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse whether in development, humanitarian and peace building. All staff sign commitment to that guarantees that they will not engage in sexual exploitation and abuses. GREDA has a robust policy on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. No staff, partner or volunteer is allowed to engage in any sexual relations with beneficiary. Any staff or volunteer who commits or fails to report cases of sexual exploitation has committed gross misconduct and faces a disciplinary commit and if proven guilty will be dismissed. GREDA prohibits any sexual activity with a person under the age 18 and considers any such relation as non consensual and therefore punishable by the organization laws. All Managers working for GREDA are obliged to create and maintain environment which prevents sexual exploitation and abuse.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Over the last few months GREDA has engaged extensively in consultation with local communities in both counties. Different approaches were used to consult with different sex and age groups. Separate focus group discussions for women were organized. Key Informant Interviews were conducted to get specialized view on issue of protection. Rural Participatory approach was used with different groups to identify needs. Once needs were identified participants got involved in prioritizing the needs. The needs prioritized through community consultations are the once used to design this proposal. Feedbacks from different groups have been incorporated in the needs analysis and activity development for this project proposal. In Yambio GREDA team visited and consulted with affected population from following Payams: Hai Masia, Saura, GBangasu, Gangura, Remenze etc. Similar consultation were made in Kapoeta EastPayams i.e. Kauto, Mogos, Narus and Natinga. This plan is informed in large part by contributions from the affected population. The following numbers were consulted Men 187, Women 392, Boys 161, and Girls 273. In total in two counties Yambio and Kapoeta East one thousand and thirteen individual were consulted.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

GREDA considers feedback from beneficiaries as important part of performance monitoring and result evaluation. All Beneficiaries form important arty of monitoring and free and encouraged by GREDA to report to any concerns they have regarding the project activities/team through a suggestion box at the site concealing their identity. A group of beneficiaries may select representatives to have face to face meeting with the management. All managers are accessible who have a feedback or complain. Contact of head office especially Program Coordinator, Monitoring and Evaluation Coordinator will be made available to beneficiaries to launch complain against any GREDA team member. Regular surveys will be conducted to get feedback on satisfaction of beneficiaries. All feedback will be documented. Feedbacks constitute lessons learnt and will be used to improve performance towards achievement of the results.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project is based on protection risk analysis taking into account specific needs of different population groups. It puts focus on identification of specific risks for a specific population group for example sexual exploitation and abuse of women and girls rights by project staff, volunteers, other humanitarian actors and partners. This explains why GREDA has a strict code of conduct relating to any sexual relation. It also has put emphasis on setting mechanism that reduces the risks to the population group. This project also identified sexual violence against beneficiaries as a possible risk that seeks to mitigate that by establishing friendly spaces in areas where women and girls will not be targeted. The project also in its analysis identified some possible risks to staff including ambushes and burglary against project supplies. Safety policies are put in place by GREDA to mitigate possible.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project has identified number of barriers to access for example women and girls from outside Yambio face really problem in accessing friendly spaces in Saura as such friendly space will be established in Remenze for women/girls there. Other barriers to accessing services may include bad roads in parties of Kapoeta East especially areas around Narus payam due to heavy rains. Areas like Nadingere Payam in Yambio due to the distance from Li Rangu and remoteness poses real challenge to people. In security caused by cattle raiding in Kapoeta East may cause serious access problem relating to access. Presence of armed groups in the areas may cause accessibility problem. Cultural norms may discourage GBV survivors from accessing case management and psycho social support services. This project has put strong mechanisms in place to address all access changes.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project tries to address inequalities that exist between women, men, girls and boys, between rural and urban communities, different age groups by including all groups to access services. It seeks to establish services more close to rural populations in hard to reach project areas. The project intends address inequalities caused by lack of skills among girls and women. This project is designed in a way that addresses protection threats such as physical violence, coercion etc by educating communities on dangers of committing GBV including domestic violence, sexual violence, negative cultural practices.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project activities such as awareness raising are meant to discourage violence, promote equality among different sex, ages and areas. Women and Girls friendly spaces will reduce protection threats such as sexual violence that may be planned against women and girls when they do not have where to associate with each and get exposed to people who can take advantage. Women and Girls friendly spaces are meant equip beneficiaries with skills that make them more self reliant that will address the issues relating sexual exploitation and abuses. Protection assessment and monitoring is meant to identify issues that affect protection of beneficiaries and share the reports with humanitarian actors to inform planning. Referral mechanism is established to help reduce acute impacts of GBV incidents.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Without measuring access of beneficiaries to services it will be difficult to know whether the project has achieved its intended results. In line with this project priority intends to monitor access of beneficiaries. Data related to access will be collected and analyzed. The result of this will be used for corrective actions aimed at improving performance toward achievement of planned outcome

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The overall project is to achieve safety for all beneficiaries of the assistance. In line with the project monitoring is tailored to capture safety status of the different. Any measurement of performance towards achievement of intended has to take in account the safety of the beneficiaries. An indicator to monitor safety is developed and data collection and analysis tools have been modified to include safety of the beneficiaries. Results from this safety monitoring will be used to improve performance.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The essence of protection work especially GBV is to safeguard dignity of beneficiaries. So to achieve project monitoring takes in to account the safety. Data related to dignity will be collected and analyzed to measure performance towards achieving dignity for every beneficiary. The project monitoring tools for this project are customized to capture variables that capture and measure achievement of dignity for beneficiaries.
