Project Summary

The proposed intervention is to strengthen case management and referral services for survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). This includes providing training and mentoring to the GBV and CP staffs of WRRS, The project provides immediate assistance to vulnerable populations. GBV survivors will receive case management and psycho-social support (PSS), including emergency care, referral to WRRS medical teams trained in case management of rape (CMR), tailored material assistance, basic emotional support, and follow up on the services will be provided .Capacity building with formal and informal authorities will be strengthened and improved. Further, the intervention provides training and technical support for health workers engaged in CMR, as well as for additional health and protection staff, in order to equip participants with the skills to provide compassionate, confidential, and competent clinical care to survivors of GBV. WRRS will be closely working within the cluster system across the six regions training staff from different clusters on GBV mainstreaming and distribute gender mainstreaming booklet relevant to their clusters.

Needs

Those particularly vulnerable to sexual violence are displaced populations, minority clans and female headed households. However,WRRS baseline assessment in September 2018 shows that there are significant barriers for survivors seeking support that needs to be improved: Limited awareness of the consequences of Gender Based Violence and Child Protection and available services; Fear of retaliation by perpetrators; Blaming of survivors and lack of confidentiality in the community and service providers. Furthermore, there are low levels of knowledge among humanitarian workers to respond and mitigate GBV in-line with the Inter Agency Standing Committee guidelines on GBV. Gaps to address are: strengthen community engagement and awareness of GBV and services available, and promote community-based mechanisms for; improve the quality of response services, particularly medical care, Psycho social support, case management and safe spaces; Engage with formal and informal security and justice actors on human rights and protection of survivors and support in referring cases; strengthen knowledge and practice of humanitarian actors on GBV and CP. Displacement and family breakdown means children are at high risk of separation which puts them at risk of trafficking, Child labor, sexual exploitation and recruitment in armed groups. Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) girls are often hidden as they are absorbed into community structures and words as a servant. There is a crucial need to increase and sustain the provision comprehensive case management, psycho-social support, interim care and tracing services for these children

Activities

Case management and psychosocial support for GBV survivors (women and girls) in the targeted locations provided by 2 GBV case workers To support case management process and the most vulnerable GBV survivors and CP cases tailored material assistance to 600 GBV survivors (women and girls), dignity kits for 600 rape and sexual assault survivors and vulnerable IDPs and referral support (transportation costs to health centers for 200 survivors) will be providedCase workers will provide accompaniment and transportation support for survivors to access health facilities Community-led GBV awareness campaign to improve access to response services will be supported by the community mobilization officer. This will include initial identification and training on GBV core concepts, safe referral, and basic emotional support and community mobilization methods for community volunteer focal points in each target location. The awareness campaign will utilize community awareness resources developed by WRRS on referral and service availability (inter-active GBV mainstreaming curriculum components and GBV/CP IEC flip books) targeting also community education committees (CECs). At least three awareness sessions per month will be supported, in addition to general peer-to-peer awarenessEducation facilities are a key site for identification of vulnerable children requiring psychosocial support. Therefore, teachers (men and female) in the target regions will be trained on GBV awareness, and psychosocial support for children and safe and compassionate referral. In order to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors to mainstream GBV and CP in emergency response based on the updated IASC guidelines (particularly from Health, WASH and Education clusters) WRRS will conduct training in potential displacement in the target areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In Somalia the average house hold has 5 members meaning an approximately 45,000 IDPs living in poor less protected condition these overcrowded IDPs triggers their susceptibility to violations of their rights. In this regard an approximate of 10,000 Men, women, Girls and Boys will indirectly benefit from the project especially through community sensitization campaigns in relation to GBV causes, Consequences, Importance of timely reporting of cases, Services available in different service providers and prevention child separation and tracing availability for UASCThe proposed project will target the most vulnerable women, and girls from the IDP settlements and the most vulnerable members of the host community. Those particularly vulnerable to sexual violence are displaced populations, returnees, minority clans, female headed households, children under 18, elderly and persons living with disability. These beneficiaries will be the prime project target with the aim of improving access for GBV survivors and UASC (women, girls and boys) and tailored interventions to promote equal access to services for women, girls, boys, timely, competent, confidential and compassionate case management, psychosocial and medical care for IDPs and host communities in target6regions. GBV trend analysis (April 2017- April 2018) show that those most vulnerable are displaced persons (77% cases) and women and girls (85% and 14% cases this clearly show that IDPs more susceptible to violations of their rights compared to the host communities. WRRS will ensure that services are offered to those in need to prevent further exposure to risks and vulnerability.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

WRRS believes in the full participation of beneficiaries throughout the implementation of the project. The beneficiaries will be involved in targeting and WRRS will use local knowledge to identify needy households and/or review selection, beneficiary liaison of both elected and un elected members of the community, beneficiary representative, community communication forums, monitoring of program implementation to ensure transparency, and management and/or monitoring of supply and quality of services. The participation of beneficiaries also allows the project interventions to strengthen community based institutions that organize the poor & build their capabilities to act collectively in their own interest which in turn increases the sustainability of intervention through fostering ownership of program objectives and processes. In the Somali context the beneficiaries will be consulted at local level, through community, clan and religious leaders regarding project location sites and the quality of services to ensure that all the vulnerable people can actively participate in the project. Due to the complexity of Somalia, SPHERE standards may not be fully met, but, women have not been allowed by religious and cultural norms to take a lead in community development activities; until recently when the local leadership structures began to promote women’s participation in community based activities. Women do participate actively but are frequently side-lined when it comes to decision making processes despite playing a vital role in household and community dynamics. The project will seek to encourage the increased participation of women in project meetings so that they can provide input and feedback regarding the quality of services they will be receiving from the interventions. Encouraging increased participation by women and girls in the project structures does not in any way diminish the role to be played by their male counterparts
