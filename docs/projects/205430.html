Project Summary

Concern will continue providing life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services in Aweil North County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) and Guit County in Unity state. In Aweil North, Concern will provide nutrition services across 22 sites and in Guit; Concern will provide nutrition services across 11 sites. The nutrition services include Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) for severely malnourished children 6-59 months. The two Inpatient care services (SC) will provide inpatient care services for children 0-59 months, Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) for children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating women (PLW) and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) services. Concern will use the national Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach and the national guideline. Concern will continue to promote the prevention of malnutrition through a combination of interventions including Social Behavior Change (SBC) through group sessions and individual counselling, promotion of home gardening, cooking demonstration and promotion of good hygiene practices. To ensure community participation and improve access and coverage, Concern will involve community volunteers for community mobilization, screening, follow up cases and dissemination of recommended nutrition behaviors. Concern will continue its success with pre-emptively reducing risks of malnutrition through supporting mother and father groups in improving MIYCN. The SBC curriculum mother/fathers groups promotes gender equality and male involvement in maternal and child nutrition and health. The nutrition service provision will be integrated with WASH, health, FSL and GBV interventions to achieve broader outcomes. In 2024, in Aweil North County, the management of severe and moderate acute malnutrition will be integrated with the existing health services and CHD staff will be trained to treat acute malnutrition as part of BPHNS package. Concern, CHD and SMoH have signed a strategy on exiting from NGO direct nutrition service delivery to integrated health and nutrition service delivery. Concern will provide technical and logistical support to CHD/SMOH to carry out nutrition activities at the facility and this will support the national strategy of integrating nutrition with the health service delivery and ensure sustainability.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate with the number of people in need is growing due to multiple factors. The factors include continual climate shocks, conflict induced displacement, the rise in food prices and influx of refugees / returns due to Sudan crises and fifth years of consecutive flooding is likely further deteriorated food and nutrition security. According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children and women at risk of wasting in 2024 and will need urgent support. As per FEWS NET 6.9 million people (about 60% of the population) are projected to face acute food insecurity. While the October to February harvest is anticipated to reduce the size of food consumption gaps for many households, millions will still be unable to meet their minimum kilocalorie needs. By April, food stocks will become depleted; meanwhile, sporadic conflict will likely continue to disrupt income generation and purchasing power will remain low. The high burden of returnees that fled Sudan will likely also place pressure on food and income sources. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is expected in counties bordering Sudan and other parts of Jonglei, GPAA, Unity, Upper Nile, and Eastern Equatoria. In the Jonglei-Upper Nile border region, there is a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) if conflict or flooding isolates households from food sources for a prolonged time. The admission level at nutrition centers across all locations indicate an increasing trends, potentially due to impact of flooding, food insecurity, hike in food prices and disease prevalence. According to the nutrition cluster county priority map 2024, 18 counties are with high priority and Concern’s proposed counties (Aweil North and Guit) are among the 18 priority counties and categorized as priority 1, where high malnutrition prevalence, high food insecurity, high morbidity rate, poor WASH conditions and high GBV concerns. Both Aweil North and Guit counties are at an emergency level (IPC 4) of food insecurity. The latest SMART survey conducted by Concern in Aweil North and Guit counties in April and May 2023 respectively revealed high prevalence of GAM. Therefore, in Aweil North, a global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 24.7% (20.8 - 29.0 95% C.I.) and a severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence of 4.1% (2.7 - 6.4 95% C.I.). Similarly, in Guit county, a GAM prevalence of 22.5 % (18.2 - 27.4 95% C.I.) and a SAM prevalence of 3.9 % (2.3 - 6.4 95% C.I.) recorded. Therefore, the nutrition situation in both counties remain as serious concern and deteriorated and classified as ‘very high’ according to WHO malnutrition classification and requires emergency intervention. The situation is likely to further deteriorate due to the disruptions in imports, food production and the related surge in food prices induced by the recent conflict in Ukraine and Sudan. The key drivers of high level of malnutrition include, inappropriate feeding practices; poor health seeking behavior; inadequate health services; women’s heavy workload; and lack of availability of food or unequal food distribution skewed towards male family members. Due to the prolonged crises, the local coping capacities depleted and the need for the continued nutrition services has increased. Children and women in South Sudan continue to suffer from unacceptably high levels of malnutrition due to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, healthcare and WASH services. It is therefore imperative that Concern continues its life-saving work providing gender-responsive nutrition services, integrated with Concern other donor funded multi-sector interventions for wider outcomes. The programme will also contribute to Concern Country Strategic Plan 2022-2024 with a specific focus on quality nutrition services and addressing the root causes of malnutrition.

2. Project activities

CO1: In order to prevent malnutrition, Concern will strengthen the social behavior change intervention through individual counselling and group sessions at nutrition sites and community level using the national MIYCN guideline. Moreover, mothers and fathers groups will be established for repeated sessions. Gender transformative topics and the recommended behaviors on MIYCN are included in the curriculum for both mothers and fathers groups to improve the knowledge and commitment of men in maternal infant and young child nutrition. The primary targets are caregivers (mother or fathers) of children, pregnant women and mother in laws/grandmothers and community leaders. Demonstration gardens will be established at nutrition sites for mothers/caregivers to learn on how to produce vegetables. In collaboration with MoH and UNICEF, Concern will implement the bi annual Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns in the two counties. CO2: Concern will continue to respond to the management of wasting and nutritional oedema through the CMAM approach for the treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition. In Guit County (Unity), Concern will provide nutrition services from 11 sites. Similarly, in Aweil North county (NBeG) Concern will provide nutrition services from 22 sites. The CMAM programme includes management of severely malnourished children (MUAC<115mm or WHZ score <-3SD or presence of bilateral pitting oedema) without medical complications will be managed through the weekly OTP while those with medical complications will be referred to the inpatient care until/SC until the medical complications resolved and return to OTP to complete treatment. Children discharged as recovered from OTP will be referred to TSFP to prevent relapse. Children 6-59 months of age, pregnant women from second trimester/visible pregnancy, lactating women with infants below 6 months with moderate acute malnutrition (children with MUA >=115mm or WHZ score >=-3SD and <-2SD and for PLW MUAC <230mm)) will be enrolled in TSFP. To maximize the coverage, CNVs will conduct screening, follow-up of absentees and defaulters. Concern also will continue to scale up family MUAC approach by training mothers of children under 5 years and providing with MUAC tapes to monitor and screen their own child for referral to the nearest nutrition site.CO3: Concern has CMAM database to monitor the admission trend and exit performance indicators on a monthly basis. Therefore, Concern will continue to contribute to the national database by providing monthly NIS data and 5Ws to the nutrition cluster: The nutrition cluster analyses the admission trend and exit performance indicators and shares with partners during the bi-weekly coordination meetings. In order to monitor the nutrition situation and to evaluate the impact of the nutrition intervention and to contribute to IPC analysis, Concern will conduct SMART surveys in the project locations.CO4: The nutrition service provision will be integrated with facility WASH, promotion of hygiene and sanitation practices and provision of WASH kits for SAM cases, provision of soap for OTP/ TSFP beneficiaries. With health through integration of nutrition services in health facility and strengthen referral pathways. Moreover, all SAM cases at admission and SAM and MAM cases presented with malaria symptoms will be tested and treated for malaria, those who not vaccinated will be referred to health facility and children and PLW will be screened by health workers and referred to nutrition section. Nutrition beneficiaries will be prioritized for FSL interventions and nutrition staff, CNVs and mother and father group members will be trained by FSL team on agronomy and they will provide technical support on vegetable production/home gardening. The staff will receive refresher training on GBV pocket guide on mapping of protection partners, referrals/linking and providing basic support to survivors of GBV without doing further harm.
