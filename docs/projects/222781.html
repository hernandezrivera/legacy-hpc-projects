Project Summary

This proposed project aims to strengthen the protective environment for both refugee and host community children by delivering child protection services and prioritizing the psychosocial well-being and resilience of children, adolescents, families, and communities following the socio-ecological model of child protection. It will follow Tdh's Global Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) framework.
Vulnerable and at-risk children and adolescents will have increased access to safe and age and gender-sensitive child protection services, including specialized case management and referral services, alternative care as well as family tracing and reunification services for unaccompanied and separated children. A gender-sensitive approach will ensure that the best interests, non-discrimination and diversity responsiveness, and specific needs of boys and girls are addressed, considering different gender risks, vulnerabilities, discrimination, and inequalities. This project will provide structured and non-structured psychosocial support (PSS) for children and adolescents. The focus of PSS will be on promoting positive coping mechanisms and empowering self-protection. The Structured and non-structured PSS will be provided to Rohingya, and host community children and youth, and the case management support will be provided to Rohingya children only. Positive parenting support will be provided to Rohingya and Host community caregivers through the “Positive Parenting Skills” program to strengthen families' capacity to protect and promote the well-being of their children. Level 3 MHPSS support by One-on-one PM+ counseling will be offered to caregivers from Rohingya and Host communities (16 years and above) experiencing stress, anxiety, depression, or difficulties in coping with daily challenges. Referrals to Level-4 services will be made for high-risk cases. The project will support harnessing and developing the capacity of four existing Community-Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs) to protect and promote the well-being of children, as well as prevent and respond to Child Protection cases. Two CBCPCs will work for camps and two CBCPCs will work for the host community. CBCPCs will implement community-led initiatives to ensure sustainability and community ownership for child protection. Adolescents from the Rohingya and Host communities will be empowered through active participation and Engagement in arts-based projects led by themselves following Participatory Action Research Methodology (PAR).
The staff and volunteers engaged in this project will work in both static and mobile modalities considering the beneficiaries' needs and availability. The static activities will be implemented in two existing MPCCs and 2 PHC’s Child Play Areas, and mobile activities will be implemented at preselected community spaces. To address language challenges, IEC materials will be published in English, Bangla, and Burmese. To mitigate the risk of trafficking, trained Community Volunteers will raise awareness of trafficking-in-persons following Tdh’s existing guidelines. In addition, the CBCPCs will be mobilized to monitor TIP cases and develop response plans. Moreover, Tdh will provide counseling support through PM+ to the victims of trafficking, aiming to facilitate their long-term recovery. The capacity of the staff and volunteers will be strengthened through a capacity-building assessment.
This project will take a comprehensive approach, providing services to people over the age of 18 as well as children and adolescents aged 0 to 18. Services such as case management, structured and non-structured psycho-social support, positive parenting, problem management plus (PM+), life skills, pre-vocational skills, and service provision for children with disabilities will be provided using a holistic approach. To increase the overall child safety measures from the system level, it will also involve the local community and other significant stakeholders.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the CP Situation Analysis, in October 2024, nearly 1 million refugees reside in 33 highly congested camps, with children making up 52% of the refugee population. Reports indicate that a greater number of boys are engaged in hazardous labor, while girls are more vulnerable to child marriage and sexual violence. Family separation also heightens the risks of abuse and exploitation, with many children lacking adequate supervision and care. As per the CPSS partners' reports, CRMM, and Joint Protection Monitoring Report (JPMR), The main child protection issues in the camps during the first and second quarter of 2024 are child trafficking, child recruitment and use, and abduction, which are on the rise. Both boys and girls are at risk of being trafficked or recruited by armed groups. Since February 2024, the escalation of the conflict in Myanmar – as well as the tension in camps has severely impacted the protection of refugees, particularly women and girls, and contributed to raising child protection incidents, such as child labor and exploitation, child marriage, and missing children. Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+) data (June 2024) shows that 34% of children experience neglect, 11% face physical distress, and 8% are engaged in child labor.The Joint Protection Monitoring Report Q2 2024 and CPSS Situation Analysis 2024 further highlight child protection concerns: children aged 14-17 are at risk of coercion into armed groups, facing torture, forced labor, and mental distress. Child marriage affects 4.53% of cases, primarily impacting girls and leading to early pregnancies and disrupted education. Psychological distress affects 11.57% of children, indicating widespread trauma within the community.In addition, the GBVIMS Q2 2024 report shows that Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) constitutes 73.8% of reported GBV incidents, with 98% occurring in survivors' homes, impacting children who witness the violence. Child marriage remains a concern, though less frequently reported, and child sexual abuse cases account for 4.2% of all sexual violence cases in the camps. While women and girls are most affected by GBV, studies also indicate that men and boys also experience significant levels of violence, including sexual violence.It shows that an integrated, multi-sectoral approach is needed to ensure more equitable and higher-quality child protection services for the targeted beneficiaries in the Tdh Targeted camps as well as surrounding host communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Tdh ensures protection is mainstreamed throughout all its projects and endeavors to uphold the Sphere Protection principles and mitigate potential protection risks. This will be achieved by:Prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm: Community sensitization will be core to Tdh’s endeavors to do no harm and mitigate the impact of any unintended negative consequences. Confidentiality in case management cases will safeguard survivor information and identity, ensuring safety and dignity. Meaningful access: Special attention will be given to vulnerable individuals having difficulty accessing assistance and basic services. The barriers that prevent children, adolescents, and adults, especially women and girls and people with disabilities, from accessing services will be assessed and addressed accordingly. Menstrual hygiene kits will be distributed to case management project participants to address adolescent girls' needs. Outreach activities will promote greater awareness, and referral mechanisms with a focus on sensitizing community members. Case management will serve as the mechanism for children to access crisis and longer-term psychosocial support. Accountability: Appropriate CFRM mechanisms are in place for affected populations to provide their feedback on the quality of services provided, as well as receive timely and appropriate responses. These include ongoing monitoring activities (KIIs, FGDs, etc), as well as formal evaluation exercises. Participation and empowerment: Tdh’s child protection approach focuses on developing self-protection capacities and enhancing well-being at Individuals (case management, MHPSS), families (Positive Parenting, PM+), and the wider community (CBCPCs) levels. Outreach activities will enhance the knowledge and capacity of community people to improve hygiene practices, identification of CP risks and positive health-seeking behaviors, as well as knowledge of referral pathways to access essential services.

2.3 - Age & Disability

All participants in camps 26 and 27 and host communities in the catchment area are considered vulnerable based on humanitarian and social criteria. Under child protection, Tdh will prioritize women and girls with disabilities, early married girls, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) survivors. For case management, the selection criteria will be children at high risk of violence, abuse, exploitation, and/or neglect. Community outreach sessions will also target the most vulnerable and incorporate age-gender- and disability-inclusive practices.To strengthen the capacity of the local partner on disability inclusion, training on the safe identification, inclusion, and referrals of persons with disabilities will be provided to the partner staff and its volunteers.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Following the IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases (1) Taking account: By updating baseline data, conducting participatory stakeholder analysis, risk assessment, and communication strategies, Tdh will begin gathering information from participants to better understand their needs and involve them in decision-making processes. This will ensure the project aligns with participant needs and priorities and fosters a sense of ownership. (2) Giving account: Tdh will hold Community Consultation sessions and short interviews with the beneficiaries. These interviews aim to gather reflections on the quality of Tdh facilities, services, expectations, areas of improvement, effectiveness, and preferred communication channels for Complaints, Feedback, and Response Mechanisms (CFRM). Tdh will establish CFRM channels for participants to provide complaints, feedback, and suggestions through a 24/7 hotline number and feedback boxes placed in all Tdh facilities. (3) Being held to account: To hold program people accountable to the target population, Tdh will clearly define the roles and responsibilities of all project stakeholders. Tdh will conduct project reviews through formal and informal meetings, allowing participants to assess the project and offer feedback on its performance.Tdh will continue using culturally appropriate, inclusive, and accessible CFRM to collect and analyze feedback. CFRM boxes will be placed at accessible level for children, and pictorial and emotional reaction-based feedback forms will be used. Tdh will introduce randomized phone calls to beneficiaries to assess their satisfaction. CFRM instructions will be displayed at all Tdh sites to aware and sensitize the community people on it.. Tdh will circulate CFRM cards with hotline numbers to the community level to sensitize community people on CFRM. Tdh’s CFRM focal point is the custodian of keeping records of all feedback and complaints received from the community.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Terre des Hommes (Tdh) has a comprehensive Policy on the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The implementation and enforcement of this policy are led by the Ethics and Compliance Technical Specialist in collaboration with the Tdh PSEA focal point. They oversee its daily application and ensure that all project participants are informed and compliant. Regular PSEA training sessions will be conducted for all staff, volunteers, and partners involved in child protection, emphasizing their roles in preventing and responding to SEA. Child-friendly and confidential reporting mechanisms will be established to allow children and their caregivers to report concerns without fear of retaliation. Periodic risk assessments will be carried out to identify and address potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse in child protection programs. Facilities and activities will be designed with PSEA measures in mind, ensuring they are secure, monitored, and managed by trained personnel.Community engagement will be reinforced by involving children and their parents or guardians in discussions and decisions about PSEA policies and protection strategies. As co-chair of the PSEA network, Tdh contributes by developing strategic initiatives. The integration of PSEA standards into child protection monitoring frameworks will ensure continuous adherence and accountability. Ongoing feedback from children and their families will be collected to enhance the effectiveness and reach of PSEA measures. By embedding these practices throughout all phases of child protection programming, organizations can uphold PSEA principles and safeguard children from any form of exploitation or abuse.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, EPR measures are embedded in this project. Considering the possible natural disasters and seasonal/sudden outbreaks, Tdh has trained and capacitated team members to respond during emergencies. The outreach team will conduct awareness and campaigns on seasonal/sudden outbreaks following community outreach guidelines by Tdh. The PSS and Case management team will also provide PFA on a need basis during disasters. The staff are also trained on basic fire response and the project sites will be equipped with fire extinguishers. Tdh has its business continuity plan and contingency funds to continue support to the affected population in need. The multi-purpose community centers will be kept ready to be used as emergency shelters for beneficiaries during any kind of emergency and Tdh and its partner will maintain a stock of dry foods to support them during an emergency period.The project also focuses on community resilience by disseminating awareness messages about disaster preparedness and climate change adaptation, empowering residents to take proactive measures during emergencies. Tdh and its partner will also disseminate the early warning message and awareness to the children and their caregivers as an emergency preparedness.

The Selected youths under 18 are members of Community-based Child Protection committees as a part of their capacity-building and representation in the decision-making process.This project offers an activity that is mainly designed to empower the youth through Participatory Action Research and arts-based projects led by themselves.Participants 15-25 years will be engaged through different Engaaged methodology based stryctured session. in addition, A good number of youths (i.e. 19-24-year-olds) will be engaged directly in the implementation of this project activities as volunteers. Moreover, the youth parents will be included as project participants to enhance their capacity in parenting skills and to ensure positive care arrangements for their children at home.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Tdh promotes the self-resilience and resilience of Refugees. Tdh plays a crucial role in working with girls, boys, their families, and communities to improve the sustainable improvements to living conditions of the most vulnerable children. Through its intervention, Tdh aims to support the capacity of individuals and groups to anticipate, cope, adapt, and transform in the face of shocks and stress and contribute to social change.In this project, Tdh will not just focus on the needs and challenges, Tdh will recognize, value, and harness the strengths and resources of the individuals, families, and communities in order to empower them. In addition, Tdh will focus on promoting the resilience of individuals (Case Management, Structured PSS), families (Positive parenting, PM+), and communities (CBCPCs) throughout the project cycle. Tdh recognizes that there are different elements of making up resilience: Coping, Adaptive, and Transformative capacity.
