Project Summary

FSNAU September (2021) report figuratively projected that 3.5 million people in Somalia will be in crisis (IPC3) and Emergency (IPC 4) up to December 2021. The report further predicted that close to 1.1 M IDPS will face moderate to severe food consumption gaps throughout 2021 in the absence of large scale humanitarian assistance. Additional 3.7 people will be in (IPC 2) stressed, adopting negative coping mechanism to meet their food and none food needs bring the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 7.2 M. The situation will be worse among rural population where huge number of households are experiencing significant decline in food and income sources. This is due to poor performance of 2021GU harvest which was 60 % lower than 10 years’ average. Other factors at play included civil insecurity, floods and shortage of farm inputs and pests. Pasture and water sources which are fundamentally indispensable for pastoralist communities have either dried up or are significantly declining to an alarming levels. Also, current projections are not providing favorable outlook as Deyr rains did not start well in the country. This grim situation is compounded by high poverty rates as a result of limited livelihoods assets, few income earning opportunities, lower community support and heavy reliance of external aid as well as climate shocks which is challenging traditional rural livelihoods and forcing good number of households to drop their traditional coping mechanism, further increasing food insecurity in many parts of the country. Crisis of this nature usually have deferential effects on women, men, boys and girls. Women and girls carries the bulk of the burden as they seek opportunities to provide food and water which do not only increase work load, but exposes them to further risks. Young population are facing the risk of diminishing opportunities for income as activities like agriculture negatively impacted by drought. All groups are likely to face protection concerns, majorly manifesting as negative coping strategies, particularly young men and women from extremely poor households as household food security continue to diminish. The proposed intervention targets in Galgaduud, Gedo, lower Juba, where currently there are 781,330 people in IPC 2 (stressed), 337,290 people in IPC 3 (crisis), while 28,740 are IPC 4(emergency) with essential livelihood packages such as cash, voucher, livestock treatment and livestock feed in order to strengthen and protect the livelihood assets of the poor and most vulnerable households.). The intervention will support 1,450 HHS with total population of 8,700 people consisting of (Women 2510, 905 men, 2,940 girls and 2,345 boys) where 900 HHS will receive cash and 550 HHS will be supported with livestock treatment. The transfer value will be based on specific regional MEB. $75 in Gedo and lower Juba and $84 in Galgaduud. Which is 80% of MEB. The use of cash is informed by the availability and accessibility of well-functioning market system. Safe distribution mechanism such as mobile money operators and other financial institutions. It also responds well to local needs during crisis. The organization intends to put in place delivery mechanism that will ensure accountability. HOD will use know your customer strategy where mobile money operator will verify the owner of the SIM by the name in the SIM registration before transferring the money. This is to ensure the right beneficiary receive the money. Beneficiaries data will be safe guarded and will only be used for the intended purpose only. The organization will continuously carry out risk mitigation exercise such as post distribution assessment to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism will also be put in place for beneficiaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneficiaries are receiving the right amount.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

cash is preferred due to availability and accessibility of well-functioning markets. secondly our choice of cash is informed by the existence and availability of mobile money operators and other financial institutions. Thirdly cash is very effective in responding to lifesaving and emergency interventions. Cash is cost effective compared to in kind. It is also easy to monitor. cash enlarges recipient choices unlike in kind. The organization intends to put in place delivery mechanism that will ensure accountability. HOD will use know your customer strategy where mobile money operator will verify the owner of the SIM by the name in the SIM registration before transferring the money. This is to ensure the right beneficiary receive the money. Beneficiaries data will be safe guarded and will only be used for the intended purpose only. The organization will continuously carry out risk mitigation exercise such as post distribution assessment to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism/grievance mechanism will also be put in place for beneficiaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneficiaries are receiving the right amount. Market data will be collected on regular basis to monitor cash impact on the market, as well as inflation & impact of seasonal changes

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

HOD will apply Market Functionality Index (MFI), with specific adoptions of the new Business Process Model for cash-based transfers. This will ensure HOD to identify possible market risks and establish mechanisms for 'Market situation monitoring' processes of cash-based assistance operations. This will help HOD to carry out proper and effective assessment in the market with regards with the following market parameters (a) food availability, (b) Price stability, (c) market competition, (D) access to markets and protection (E) supply chain resilience as well as (F) infrastructure availability. Further on that HOD expects the proposed cash transfer will assist normal operation among the target households without causing panic buying .

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Protection of civilians: i) Distribution points and routes shall be located in central areas that are known to be safe and have been cleared of any landmines, not near military installations or near conflict areas or in areas known to be infested with robbers. (ii) The distribution will be done door to door as much as reasonably possible. (ii) All distribution will be done during daylight between 10.00 AM to 3.00 PM. iv) Route guides shall be stationed at various locations to safely guide the beneficiaries to the distribution points. v) The shortest route to the distribution point shall be used. 2. Exclusion and marginalization risks: i) Data about beneficiaries shall be segregated by age, gender, physical ability/disability and group identity (minority or dominant) ii) During invitation to community sensitization meetings, messages will be sent through door to door, social media and posters to youth groups, parents, community elders to invite boys and girls to the meetings. ii) During meetings an opinion of women, boys and girls will be sought before conclusions are made on a matter. Iv) Beneficiary registration will be done according to categories such as women, people beyond 60 years old, youth, physically disabled, pregnant and lactating women and minority clan. v) Those who are most likely to be marginalized or excluded in distributions such as women and those who do not have documentation will be made part of the project implementation team to champion the rights of such groups. vi) All stakeholders will be involved in the selection of beneficiaries so that no vulnerable and deserving beneficiary is left out and all who can benefit are informed of the details of the project. 3. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: i) All project workers will sign a code of conduct which prohibits interference in the beneficiary selection process, involvement in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. ii) A stakeholder engagement plan will be prepared and all stakeholders will be invited for community sensitization meetings for the project. Selection of beneficiaries will be conducted after an agreed-on criteria by all stakeholders iii) At distribution points, female staff shall be in charge of women and girls’ queues iv) A feedback and grievance mechanism shall be set up to pick out issues arising from stakeholders v) Distribution teams shall be rotating among the various groups of stakeholders. vi). 4. Protection mainstreaming risks: i) Beneficiary lists and personal data shall be kept confidential and safe from unintended use. ii) The location of distribution points and access routes shall have enough space for orderly distribution and will be guarded by security staff to ensure the safety of beneficiaries. iii) Distribution will be done twice in a month and during the day. To minimize safety risk and to ensure beneficiaries do not face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their rations home, undercover escorts shall be deployed during distribution days. Avoiding community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets, and inputs.
