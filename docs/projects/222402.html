Project Summary

Continuous heavy rains since May, combined with controlled water releases from Lake Victoria, have significantly raised Nile River levels, affecting about 710,000 people across 30 of South Sudan's 78 counties and Abyei Administrative Area. The floods have devastated homes, destroyed crops, disrupted education and health services, and restricted access to essential facilities. Reports indicate rising malaria cases, respiratory tract infections, acute watery diarrhea, and snakebites. Flood-affected people were unable to tend to their lands and livestock, further aggravating their vulnerabilities. (OCHA floods snapshot, 5th September 2024)
RCO through our vast experience in emergencies proposes viable and holistic actions that aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality attributable to the consumption and use of unsafe water, the lack of or inadequate sanitation facilities during emergency situations and inadequate hygiene practices. The project will respond to the prevailing WASH gaps affecting the vulnerable populations in most complicated emergency zones – replicating our past experiences and lesson learnt in both WASH in emergency and WASH in development. RCO’s project seeks to target 15,600 beneﬁciaries amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs, returnees from Sudan and host communities directly affected by the floods & conﬂict with integrated response in line with 2025 HRP WASH cluster strategic objectives.
Jonglei and Northern Bhar el Ghazal states are among the other states in the country where WASH needs are dire particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from Sudan. This is attributed to by the impact of inter communal clashes and the ongoing conflict experienced Sudan that led to influx of high number of returnees that have overwhelmed the already limited existing WASH infrastructures.
The project seeks to Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition (WASHCO1) by providing WASH services at OTP/SC centers and PHCC/PHCU health facilities as well as addressing the root causes of active of WASH related diseases and protection especially among IDPs, Sudan-returnees, women and girls who face GBV, a key protection concern while they trekking for long distances in search for water among others.
To ensure this succeeds, the project will train WASH hygiene promoters on core WASH’GBV competencies including referrals and case reporting and they will in turn train others and conduct community mass hygiene promotion campaigns. (WSH/CO2)
Boreholes and hand dug wells will be rehabilitated with a flood-resilient design (WASHCO2)
by the trained pump mechanics and Water Management Committees who will be flushing out to ensure they (bore holes & hand dug well) are well cleaned and removed turbidity by air compressors and treated with chlorine for disinfection and improve water quality as well as replacing the worn-out parts.
RCO will address the sanitation situation both at the communal level and institutions like OTP/SC, schools and PHCC/PHCU health centers through rehabilitations of gender/disability segregated latrines with a flood-resilient design (WASHCO2) with doors and locks as well as installation of a hand washing facilities at each latrine.
Finally, the project will synergize and integrate multi sector response by coordinating with counterpart agencies on ground to ensure effectiveness and referrals of other cluster objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches like COVID-19, AAP principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention and sustainability.

1. Needs

Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. (. Reports indicate rising malaria cases, respiratory tract infections, acute watery diarrhea, and snakebites. Flood-affected people were unable to tend to their lands and livestock, further aggravating their vulnerabilities. (OCHA floods snapshot, 5th September 2024).By 30 September 2024, the ongoing crisis in Sudan had resulted in a significant influx of over 821,000 refugees and returnees (including over 413,000 children) into South Sudan since the conflict started. Many of these individuals arrive in dire need of humanitarian assistance, exacerbating an already precarious situation in a country that is grappling with its own humanitarian challenges. Most of the returnees settle in areas that are already under-resourced, placing additional strain on local economies and services. The majority of these arrivals have high nutritional needs and lack any means of income, further complicating their ability to integrate and thrive especially among communities that are already facing severe food insecurity due to ongoing conflict and climate-related disasters like the recent floods. (Humanitarian Situation Report No. 9 2024) Uror, Duk, Aweil East & Aweil North counties are historically under-developed and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict, now exacerbated by influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan and hard hit with floods that has rendered them with limited access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation facilities. This has contributed to deteriorating IPC 4 and beyond as well as acute malnutrition situations in these counties. These counties are currently characterized by limited access to clean water, relying on seasonal streams, unsafe ground water, unprotected wells, and rampant open defecation. Which has been the main course of waterborne and diarrheal diseases in the region. Notably, poor sanitation coverage and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation which constitute to nearly 50% of the total AWD and cholera cases. (South Sudan Situation Reports on Cholera 2024). Latrine coverage in the target counties remains critically low (3%); thus, open defecation is predominant in these areas and considering the current heavy rains and flooding, water sources may get contaminated by fecal matter hence potential for water borne disease epidemics such as cholera. hand washing, poor disposal of solid waste etc being widespread. (FSNMS round 28 report). Deteriorating WASH conditions are further exacerbated by increased cases of protection concerns among girls, boys, men and women with subsequent conflict related sexual violence and abuse. Should these conditions persist, the food security, WASH and IPC situation is expected to deteriorate further.

2. Project activities

1. Conduct project introductory sessions with the community beneficiaries including their representatives to share the project objectives and buy in. 2. Carry out GBV risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs and GBV staff in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and market centers and map safe areas of implementation so as to select the safe locations and rehabilitate 60 flood-resilient boreholes and water points to ensure fully functional and equitable access to safe and suﬃcient water to vulnerable and affected 15,600 beneﬁciaries in the target counties. 3. Carry out risk analysis with girls and women and RCO female staffs in the community, schools, OTP/TSFP centers and market centers and rehabilitate of 120 safe and lockable latrines with flood resistant nature that are gender and disability tailored and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to ensure 15,600 targeted beneﬁciaries practice safe sanitation that is digniﬁed and gender appropriate. 4. Sanitation - Carry out gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers in in target counties. 5. Provision of WASH NFIs, including WASH/Hygiene and dignity kits to 5000 parents of children admitted for SAM and discharged at OTP/SC till the household level 6. Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities 7. Carry out hygiene promotion campaigns among 15,600 beneﬁciaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases. 8. Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 7,600HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the speciﬁcities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in the target counties. 9. Train 60 Water Management Committees to manage and maintain the 60 rehabilitated water points. 10. Train enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells with a flood-resilient design to ensure sustainability of the project. 11. Distribute WASH NFIs including Pur, Satchets, MHM kits (to women and girls at adolescent stage) and jerry cans to the identiﬁed vulnerable communities in the targeted states. 12. Training of adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene management by female WASH staff as well distributing of menstrual hygiene kits both at household and institutional level.13. Establishment of hand washing stations both at the community and institutions to enable hand washing practices are adhered to. The hand washing facilities will encourage change of attitude and behavior towards hand washing and hence improved hygiene in general.
