Needs

A total number of 13.2 million people are in need of health assistance of which 51.3% are women, 48.7% are men and 41.5% are children under 18 years old. Access to health care remains poor, due to a lack of functioning facilities; shortages of health staff, drugs and medical supplies; and persistent clashes between various armed groups. Referral systems for trauma, emergency obstetric care and surgical services are insufficient, while there remains limited capacity for mobile clinics to reach out to fluid temporary settlements and host communities. The HNO 2018 identified several health needs including access to primary health care through an Essential Package of Primary Health Services (EPPHS) to cover essential health care needs of vulnerable populations and access to Secondary Health care including trauma care. It was indicated that there is also a dire need to increase availability of mental health services. According to the health cluster inputs to HNO 2019, 37% of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services and the need for secondary health services is still very high: one functional hospital bed is available per 1,000 population (compared to global average of 3 per 1,000 population) and only 50 gynaecologists serve a population of about 4 million in NWS. HeRAMS Q3 data in NWS results showed that 377 health facilities were functional out of 452 health facilities that reported. Of those that reported, 74% of health facilities (HF) were fully functional, 9% were partially functioning and 17% were out of service; 15% were reported damaged, 60% of the hospitals are without intensive care service, while 27% of total HFs offered mental health services. Based on the Community and Mental Health Needs Assessment conducted by the IRC, 86.81% of respondents indicated that transportation costs were a barrier to accessing health services, 22.2% of the children in the families of the assessed sample suffered from diarrhoea in the past 7 days, and 10% of children didn’t have access to routine vaccination services yet; 44% of the survey participants have a member of their family with chronic disease (the main prevalence was for hypertension at 20%); 31.32% indicated that safety and security were barriers to access; 99% of respondents were not aware of MHPSS services in the area, while 96.74% of respondents indicated that self-harm was one of their coping strategies. The health infrastructure inside Syria has been severely affected due to continued attacks on health facilities, leaving only 48% of them fully functional throughout the country. For the first 10 months of 2018, there were 137 targeted attacks on health care compared to 122 verified attacks in 2017. Yet limited funding remains a challenge, with only 15.4% of the funding requirements for the sector for 2018 having been met after the first 6 months of the year. Primary health (PH) care, reproductive health (RH) care, and child health care (including immunization) have been identified as the largest priorities for the health response. Implementation of activities

The IRC will continue to support 17 health facilities through direct implementation and through partners; ranging from supporting primary health clinics, secondary hospitals and mobile clinics, in addition to community, mental and integrated GBV services and referrals. The IRC will also provide the facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies, in line with the Essential Package of Primary Health Services (EPHS). Trainings and capacity building plans are put in place (in coordination with other actors/partners and WHO) to train medical staff on communicable (CDs) and non-communicable diseases (NCDs), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), and mHGAP. CHWs will be linked to the facilities, working in the catchment areas and the surrounding camps and clinics. They will conduct household awareness sessions and follow up on referrals, including for GBV cases. CHWs will receive relevant adapted trainings to assist with their health awareness raising in the community including in CDs, NCDs, hygiene promotion, and communication skills CHWs will also screen patients for hypertension, diabetes and malnutrition, and will follow up on their treatments and clinic visits. GBV interventions will also be mainstreamed throughout its clinical and community health programming. Women who have experienced, or are at risk of GBV, will receive case management services through GBV case managers and be referred to PSS, recreational and livelihoods services as available, resulting in a more coordinated approach that will increase access to services. MHPSS workers will also conduct referrals to more specialised services and carry out awareness sessions on mental health disorders. Medical doctors in the hospital will be trained on mHGAP with one doctor as a focal person to better identify and manage cases that need mental health support. Support of facilities will include coverage of running costs, staff salaries, and provision of pharmaceuticals, technical support and capacity strengthening. The IRC partners will coordinate with the Health Cluster and Health Directorates and the IRC will ensure no overlap in activities or services.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers. Gender Based Violence. Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Duty of Care Community Engagement and Participation. Provision of health care for vulnerable groups and equitable access to health services. Mitigating Measures: -Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. -Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. -A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. -Inclusion of vulnerable and minority groups needs throughout the program cycle (analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) through gender, age, and disability disaggregated analysis of beneficiaries with identification of risk factors impacting service provision.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Further escalation of the conflict and destruction of health infrastructure. Mitigation: Coordination with partner to monitor the situation closely and use the IRC conflict analysis systems. Share regular information with UN to plan for any unanticipated changes in populations and security, Support partners in the development of risk mitigation including prepositioning of medical supplies. Utilize partner systems for effectively operating facilities in high conflict areas. Border closure and insecurity. Mitigation: Partner to lead procurement and transportation of medical supplies. Preposition medical supplies and materials. Coordination with Turkish border authorities. Community rejection of GBV and community outreach services. Mitigation: Ensure GBV services are provided in confidential manner. Ensure referral to GBV services is with informed consent of the GBV supervisor.
