Project Summary

International Medical Corps will assure the provision of the basic package of primary health care to refugee and the host populations through support to two health centers and implementation of one mobile clinic (the mobile clinic will focus on localities with high concentration of IDPs and after an assessment is done). This package will include curative consultations, integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), childhood vaccinations, reproductive health care (antenatal and post-natal care, assisted deliveries prevention of mother to child transmission of HIV/AIDS (PMTCT) and clinical care for survivors of sexual assault (CCSAS)). These services will adhere to national protocols including case management protocols and the free health care policy for children under five years of age, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs and households hosting IDPs). Core activities for improved access to primary health care will include:
Curative Consultations
• Staffing – IMC will work with the MoH through the health Districts to ensure health centers are fully staffed with qualified essential service providers including head nurses to maintain oversight of all structures and support staff.
• Training – Staff will receive refresher trainings (including: Child Health, Clinical Management of rape, management of common illnesses as per National protocols and Pharmacy management) as needed on national protocols and guidelines of appropriate case management
• Provision of Drugs and medical supplies – IMC will procure drugs and medical supplies from an international supplier and distribute them to the 2 health centers to assure that they have the necessary stock to treat common illnesses. In addition, support will be provided to staff to reinforce the supply chain, distribution, proper storage and management of stock to minimize ruptures, avoid expiration or loss of drugs due to improper storage, and decrease the risk of mismanagement.
• Referrals – The referral network for secondary care will be reinforced through communication with the Goré district hospital in Gore. Health Center Staff will be trained on recognition of cases that require referrals for secondary care according to national protocol as well as the specific steps to follow if a referral case arrives
Reproductive Health
• Staffing – The project will ensure there are skilled staff including trained midwives in the health centers and mobile clinic to assure the provision of quality antenatal and postnatal care as well as assisted deliveries when women arrive to give birth.
• Training: Trainings will be provided on Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care: International Medical Corps will train health staff and provide supportive supervision to staff in targeted health facilities on topics such as new-born resuscitation and other lifesaving BEmONC signal functions, and active management of third stage labour (AMTSL) to prevent post- partum haemorrhage.
• Drugs and medical supplies – Drugs and materials for Basic Emergency Obstetric and neonatal Care (BEmONC) will be available in all project supported sites
• Referrals – Referrals will be made for secondary care in case of the need for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (CEmONC).
• Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission (PMTCT)- See HIV/AIDS Programming
• Clinical Care for Survivors of Sexual Assault – Survivors of sexual assault will receive care according to international standards for the time frame in which they arrive at a service delivery point including receiving Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP), reference to psychosocial counseling when possible, testing and treatment for STIs, emergency contraception and pregnancy tests.
Child Health
• Cold chain support - Support will be provided at every step to ensure maintenance of the cold chain during shipping and avoid unnecessary loss of vaccine stock. Components of the cold chain at the health center level including functional refrigeration and thermometers will also be assured.
• Vaccination campaigns – The project will assist the district health authorities in mass vaccination campaigns, when this is organized.
• Growth monitoring – Staff will be trained on growth monitoring if necessary and materials made available to assure routine growth monitoring is available.
• IMCI: For improving child health services the project will train the service providers on IMCI and ensure basic medications and supplies are available for IMCI in the health facilities.
HIV/AIDS Programming
• Awareness Raising – IMC will strengthen awareness raising activities on HIV/AIDS by health education sessions led by CHWs and focal points within the communities. HIV Testing and Counseling – HIV testing and counseling will be available in IMC supported facilities. All patients testing positive for HIV/AIDS will be referred for treatment.
• Assurance of ARVs – Project staff will liaise with DHMTs and UNFPA to ensure ARV availability to prevent increase in individual viral loads and decrease risk of transmission in the communities.
• PMTCT – The project will assure provision of ARVs to HIV positive pregnant women to prevent the transmission of the HIV virus to infants. Infants born to HIV positive mothers will also receive treatment for the recommended period (diagnosis four to six weeks after birth and ARV treatment within the first 12 weeks of birth for HIV exposed infants).
3) Strengthen community-based health activities
The project will 1) provide support the District health service to establish community-based Health Management Committees (HMCs) to ensure accountability of the health center at the community level and 2) to benefit from the existence of Community Health Workers (CHWs) in the communities to reach beneficiaries.
• Establishment and training of CHW network - Links with Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be strengthened through supervision where they already exist and in areas where they don’t the project will support the community to select and train 50 CHWs (25 per Health area). This network will benefit from regular trainings and a capacity building program aimed at strengthening their knowledge base and communication techniques.
• Mobilization of CHW network for health and nutrition education – CHWs will promote positive behavior change for key health and nutrition behaviors through home visits and group educational sessions. Topics for these health education sessions will include the importance of vaccination, benefits of proper hygiene, signs and care for common communicable diseases, danger signs during pregnancy, importance of exclusive breastfeeding and benefits of seeking care at health facilities and mobile clinics clinics and HIV/AIDs awareness.
