Project Summary

This project aims at building an active social and learning environment that permits children to enroll, learn and complete the school cycle in 6 schools in 3 subdivisions. The project also will enable teachers to conduct research and self-training as part of capacity building to improve their performance and enhance the quality of teaching.
UNICEF goal is to connect 03 subdivisions in the Far-North region with Internet services precisely Mora, Makary, and Mokolo.
Presently the Spanish Natcom for UNICEF is financing the connection for 8 schools in East Region with Internet services. A field visit was conducted to visualize how the children are using the tablets with both online and offline content. The children showed a mastery of the usage and this will go a long way to improve on learning and ICT capacity of the underprivileged children in these schools. The tablets with both online and offline content are improving the learning of children. This project will provide teachers and learners not only with learning ICT, but also learning through ICT, it will also aid actors of the educational supervision chain to ensure a better supervision by relying on tools existing through ICTs. This will contribute in improving the quality of education. It will then be a question of revitalizing the internal and external participatory management structures in the schools so that they can mobilize for children in general and most especially girls and other vulnerable children. Thus, it will consequently improve the school retention in the locality of the project.
ICT (Information and communication technology) tools will be installed, these tools will be used to provide children and teachers with learning platforms that gives opportunity not only to learn ICTs, but also to learn using ICTs and through ICTs as prescribed by the National policy of teaching ICTs in the primary cycle.
The ICT equipment will also support the implementation of the pedagogical supervision model and monitor the attendance of pupils and teachers thereby making information available to the authorities quicker for a quick decision making. This sustainable and attractive method in making changes in the access and retention of pupil in school will be supported by the entire educational community. From this, the project will focus on activities to strengthen the technical and logistical capacities of the internal participatory Management structures (Parent Teachers Association, Association of Pupils’ mothers, School Councils, Management committees, Children's Government) and external (community-based organizations) at the school for the enrolment, monitoring attendance and retention of children in school, especially girls.
The project will consist of constructing e-containers in the 06 selected schools that will serve as multimedia center for the sites and the communities. These schools will be connected with internet using solar power and connectivity system to provide electricity. Materials such as desktops, tablets will be handed to the selected schools. The school administrators in collaboration with the community will make sure the materials are well kept and managed. The teachers will be trained on the use of ICT in schools, on the usage of the tools to improve learning. They will also be trained on how to manage these materials and on the sustainability side of the project.
The project will be monitored by UNICEF in collaboration with the Government, computer start-ups and the community.
At the end of the project, it is expected that in each school: students will demonstrate learning improvements, learning ICT based platforms will be ameliorated, teachers will use ICT to improve their teaching, school enrolment and retention for learners and teachers will be improving.

1 - Besoins / Needs

About 440,000 school-age children (47% of girls) need assistance in the education sector in the Far North region, particularly in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava, and Mayo-Tsanaga. Due to the displacement of families, the host schools integrate additional displaced students, which constitute a burden for already week education services. According to the 2018 report of ministry of basic education, the student/teacher ratio in the Far-north region is 127 children per teacher. In this region, in addition to insecurity with ongoing sporadic attacks forcing communities to move regularly with consequences on children's access to education, there are other factors slowing down children's access to school. These include the lack of birth certificates, inadequate/lack of school structures (classrooms and latrines, water points), cultural beliefs and lack of economic means by parents. As a result, many children find themselves out of school with little or no access to education or training. Girls and children with disabilities are the most affected by this situation. A joint evaluation in education is underway and will make it possible to define the level of concern of the various children and adolescents deprived of their education and exposed to protection risks including GENDER-based violence, drug use and recruitment into armed groups. Some partners in the sector have put in place educational activities, including accelerated education to support groups of out-of-school children, but efforts are still needed to reach a significant number of children and adolescents and thus reduce the risk of their exposure. In addition, UNICEF organizes remedial education classes for out-of-school children in order to reintegrate them into school. This program is complemented by an Education through Radio program to reach a larger number of children and youth with educational and other awareness-raising activities on barrier measures to fight against the COVID-19 pandemic. In addition, the level of school attendance is also very low in both primary and secondary schools, and the quality of education is affected by the lack of qualified teachers. According to the recent MINEDUB report, the teacher-pupil ratio is 1/126, which is a real obstacle to good teaching and learning. In addition, most teachers are teacher-parents without the proper pedagogical training. Furthermore, 67 schools (16 in Logone and Chari, 34 in Mayo-Sava, 17 in Mayo-Tsanaga), are still closed and could not reopen because of the insecurity and about 18,000 students are unfortunately affected by this situation and therefore deprived of their access to education. Equally, 50 schools (15 in Logone and Chari, 25 in Mayo-Sava and 10 in Mayo-Tsanaga) have been destroyed over the years, depriving 17,000 children of access to education and 17 schools are still occupied by armed forces exposing children to protection risks. The 'Connext my School' project will improve education for children in the targeted schools and will serve to attract more children to school activities as well. Teachers will use the program for continuous capacity building to improve their performance with a significant impact on the quality of teaching and learning.
