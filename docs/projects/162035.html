Needs

"As of 30th September 2019, UNHCR population fact sheet indicates 55% of the total Rohingya population being children, 7 percent of which are adolescents between 12 to 17 years. Majority of which require education services including pre vocational training opportunities. Despite improved services over the last 2 years, the education needs remain enormous for children and adolescents and new risks are emerging for children, adolescents and. The 2019 Multi Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) Report indicates 31 percent of children aged 5 to 17 years to be out of all sort of learning opportunities within the refugee camps. Only 2 percent of adolescents girls and 13 % of adolescents boys between 15 to 18 years are reported to be attending Temporary Learning Centers. Adolescents represent one of the most neglected group in accessing education opportunities. The REACH Education Needs Assessment March 2019 indicates that for children aged 15-18, 1% of girls and 9% of boys were reported as attending learning centres. This is likely linked to the fact that centres are not generally set up to provide services for this age range, with only 3% offering services for children above 14. These adolescents are at high risk as they are the least reached population category with education services coupled with several bottlenecks. The joint Multi-sectoral needs assessment 2019 report indicates 36 percent of refugee children not attending learning opportunities due to Child Marriage while 23 percent are affected by cultural reasons. 15 percent of the children population reported the lack of adequate learning materials within the existing learning centers. Child marriage has become a coping strategy as families look at it as a protective measure in ensuring their social, economic and physical security. This often exposes adolescents’ girls to early pregnancy, sexual and physical abuse from their husband as well as abuse from the community which increases stigmatization. For boys, child labor, drug abuse and engagement in criminal activities is the alternative in the absence of Education. Many households in the camps rely on the unpaid household labor including caring for younger siblings, cooking meals, collecting water and firewood, among other household chores. The MSNA 2019 report indicates, 5% of household reporting a child working to earn income, 5% household reporting presence of early marriage and 2% households reporting children working under hazardous working condition. There is also need to address community perception and knowledge gap on child protection, education, PSEA and adolescents’ participation. This will address child protection concerns such as child marriage, child labor, trafficking, sexual harassment, physical abuse, access to education, social cohesion, peace building and participation among others. The MSNA report indicates, 21 percent of Rohingya children not accessing learning centers because they don’t find education useful, while 20 percent children are engaged in household chores and not accessing learning centers or available alternatives.

Activities or outputs

"The project goal will be to provide quality inclusive education and pre vocational services for Rohingya adolescents (15 to 18 years) while working with parents and communities. Three objectives as guided by the education sector strategies will be achieved. Output 1: Access - Expand and strengthen immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, in a safe and protective environment, for crisis-affected refugee girls and boys aged 3-24 years old. The project will capitalize on using its existing multi sectoral facilities to ensure boys and girls of 15-18 years access learning opportunities. The Guidance on Informal Education Program (GIEP) previously known as LCFA will be used to guide learning for boys and girls. Adolescent boys and girls between 15-18 years of age will access pre vocational training, numeracy and literacy. The Guidance on Informal Education Program (GIEP) core materials will be used to deliver learning opportunities including level 5 materials once approved and provided through the education sector. Teachers and pre vocational instructors from both the host and Rohingya communities will be recruited, mentored and trained. Capacity building for teachers and access for children with special needs will be improved. Student’s assessment will be conducted to inform promotion to another level or grade. Students and teachers will be equipped with learning and tutoring materials. Output 2: Quality - Provide quality inclusive education to refugee girls and boys aged 3-24 years old, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunities. The project will focus on Rohingya refugee children in providing quality inclusive education for boys and girls between 15 to 18 years of age. Staffs, teacher and pre vocational instructors will be targeted for teaching related professional development opportunities in alignment with education sector standards. Activities will include: Competency assessment for Rohingya and host community teachers and prevocational instructors; In-service trainings on Pedagogy, code of conduct, child safe guarding, inclusive teaching-learning strategies, inclusive teaching learning materials, peace building, disaster risk reduction, PSEA and gender inclusion; Staffs capacity trainings (Master Trainers); On job mentorship and monthly meetings with teachers and pre vocational instructors. In consultation with the sector field contextualize trainings will be conducted to ensure inclusion and participation of Rohingya male and female. With guidance from education sector: Guidance on Informal Education Program (GIEP) teaching and learning materials will be distributed to the students and teachers, intermediary materials for prevocational skills; and assessment on learning outcomes will be conducted. Output 3: Community Engagement - Ensure refugee ownership and active participation and engagement in the education of girls and boys aged 3-24 years’ oldCommunity engagement and participation will be ensured through working with parents, centre management committee male and female members as well as boys and girls. Activities will include; capacity building of the centre management committee members education, child safeguarding and PSEA, orientation on inclusion, accountability & feedback mechanisms; awareness raising campaigns on education and violence against children where men, women. Parenting sessions will be conduct to ensure parents engagement in children and adolescent education. Peace building activities will be integrated within the learning activities as well as community engagement activities. World vision will use its empowering peace builder approach to ensure child led initiatives for social cohesion. Preparedness and response activities to monsoon and cyclone will be integrated within the project activities for children and communities. This will be in a form of preposit
