Project Summary

The project seeks to provide a regular program of Food Baskets (6 rounds ) and bread distribution (6 months) for most vulnerable people in Aleppo and Idlib governorates.(the project is planned)
UFUK Int in coordination with LCs will select about 1500 HHs as benefciaries for food baskets and 3000 HHs as benefciaries for bread in 6 sub-districts.
The beneficiaries will be selected according to many vulnerable standards, four distribution center will be provided in each targeted communities to facilitate distribution process.
UFUK will take care to avoid overlap or duplicating process through good coordination
with FSL cluster and other stakeholders among the region, as well as verifying the names of benefciaries.
Who can benefit of this project?
Vulnerable people affected by the confect will be the benefit of this project, in Syria situation, particularly in NW Syria. We set many vulnerable criteria to select the beneficiaries of this project as the following:
1 Female Headed Household/child-headed household
2 Injured/Disabled-headed households
3 Households with at least one pregnant women
4 Households with at least one elderly, chronically ill or disabled person
5 displaced families
6 Households living in collective shelter/abandoned building or equivalent poor living
conditions
7 Households caring for a separated or unaccompanied child
Regularly distribution program:
This project will provide monthly Food Baskets and daily bread bags for selected beneficiaries for six months, that’s will be necessary for achieving not just food security, but also the stability and empowerment for these vulnerable people that will help them to revive and recover again as well avoid them to resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, child labor and recruit in armed groups as sources of livelihoods.
Replace or exclude names of beneficiaries will be available if some of them didn't meet the criteria or provided wrong information or moved to other areas, anywise the aid will provide based need.
Regularly distribution will be in coordination with LCs and IDPs committees in the targeted communities, Through distribution center will take the place in each targeted community; UFUK Int will take in consideration the safety and security of distribution place, UFUK team commit to apply the humanitarian and protection principles in all projects as well as set a complaint system including complaint box, WhatsApp number and monitoring officers to ensure the services are delivered to beneficiaries as dignity and transparency.
For elder people and women or people with special needs; distribution team will deliver the baskets food door to door as well specializing corner for them in the distribution center.
Procurement and Distribution:
UFUK have developed its financial and procurement policies as appropriate with Syrian context, to contribute in reviving the economic NW Syria, UFUK prefer to purchase the required procurement from local markets, if the goods are not available in local markets UFUK have ability to purchase from Turkey and shipped to Syria by Turkey-Syria cross borders. The procurement process including tenders, quotations, confecting interest will be under transparency ways, sharing all document with donors.
The beneficiaries are being registered and verifications have been finalized prior to the distribution.
Distribution team have already lists of beneficiaries who have to provide the required documents such as ID and family card to get the baskets and sign, as well UFUK will invite the represent of communities (LCs / IDPs committees / Women associations) to monitoring distribution process
What is Food Basket content?
The food basket consists of a number of items ( cereal, pulses, oil, salt, sugar, canned meat can be added). The general basket is normally delivered as a package of dry items. For families have children 1-5 years; will get additional items that considered nutrients are essential for children.

4 - Needs

(According to HNO Syria-2021) The protracted crisis in Syria has intensified needs over the past year through a complex and deepening socio-economic crisis, degradation of livelihoods, knock-on effects of Covid-19, ongoing conflict in the North of the country and major losses in the food production sector (due to conflict and climate change). These have combined to severely compromise the national food security situation.an estimated 14.2 million people need some form of food and agriculture-based livelihoods assistance. Most IDPs and other vulnerable crisis-affected people in Syria remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, including food, education, health care, shelter and water, sanitation and hygiene services. The national assessment showed that 52.8% of IDPs were food insecure indicating a higher level of vulnerability than residents (49.7%) while returnees were found to be the most food insecure at 60.3%. Highlighting specific vulnerabilities of IDPs, an additional Food Security Sector (FSS) assessment on Newly Displaced IDPS (NIDPs) in NW Syria , found that 41.5% % of all NIDP are food insecure and in need of assistance, 50.1% had experienced hunger, and 41.2% of households had unacceptable food consumption.Debts and credits are also a common coping strategy with 52.9% of households having taken on debt, since displacement in NWS, with an average amount of debt per household at SYP 701,443. The primary reason reported for taking debt is to buy food . Over 1.7 million people currently living in last resort camps are among the population groups most affected by the deteriorating food insecurity situation with no access to livelihoods and total reliance on humanitarian assistance.
