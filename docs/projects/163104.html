Project Summary

Agriculture is at the heart of the Zimbabwean economy, contributing significantly to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and as the main source of livelihood for about 60% of the population. The performance of agriculture is a key determinant of rural livelihood resilience and poverty levels. In Zimbabwe, smallholder agriculture is the core contributor to agricultural production and therefore vital for achieving food, income and nutrition security goals for improving livelihoods and responding to climate change. During the last decade, smallholder farmers have been faced by a myriad of constraints related to low and erratic rainfall; low, harsh macro-economic environment; low investment in agriculture; shortages of draft animals and poor physical and institutional infrastructure, including energy shortages. This has resulted in national production levels falling well below the national demand. Farmers are still using conventional farming methods such as monocropping, conventional tillage and burning of crop residues which result in a decrease in soil fertility leading to a decline in yield and eventually serious grain deficit. As the soils are depleted of nutrients CRS through this project shall provide an input pack and implement the integration of green manure cover crops and grain legumes into the farming systems to help in replenishing the nutrients.
Given increased risks to the effects of catastrophic droughts, especially in districts located in Matabeleland South and North Provinces, there is urgent need to demonstrate visible progress in the affected areas. The current 2019 devastating drought has resulted in a huge livestock mortality in the Southern region hence fodder production and supplementary feeding interventions shall play a pivotal role of reducing livestock poverty deaths. CRS understands the importance of achieving rapid results while laying the foundation for lasting change. Based on CRS experience and its proven capacity in Zimbabwe to work on agricultural development, the proposed project will ensure that communities and their respective local based institutions have the necessary crop and livestock protection, production, and marketing skills and techniques. To gain these skills, CRS proposes a series of trainings in addition to the critical inputs of seeds, fodder, and livestock. The trainings will consist of vegetable production (April - May), small grains production (January OR October), fodder production (January for Beitbridge, and October for Binga and Gwanda), goat production (February - March), indigenous chicken production (February - March), and post-harvest handling and storage (April). Vegetable garden implementation will target the most vulnerable affected population with current access to a perennial stream or borehole. Project targeting, design, and implementation will be informed by stakeholders, other partners, and participants, particularly women.
