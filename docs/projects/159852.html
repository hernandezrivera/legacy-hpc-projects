Project Summary

More than 5 years into the conflict in eastern Ukraine, humanitarian needs remain significant, especially in areas close to the contact line. Vulnerabilities related to poverty, disruption of critical infrastructure, and ongoing armed hostilities continue to influence the affected population negatively. Elderly (over 60), households with unemployed members, female-headed households with children, people with chronic illnesses or with disabilities and IDPs in host communities constitute the most vulnerable groups at risk and in need of humanitarian assistance.
The project employs a multi-cluster approach in order to provide comprehensive humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable households in Luhansk region in the 0-20 km zone (GCA). The project aims to address the most basic needs such as providing immediate access to food, securing personal hygiene, and meeting the most acute protection needs (legal aid and psychosocial support) of conflict-affected people.
Food items and hygiene kits will be delivered to 800 of the most vulnerable and food-insecure households over 6 rounds. This will allow to meet the short-term needs of vulnerable populations (elderly with minimum pensions, persons with disabilities, female-headed households with children) in 23 targeted settlements. The needs will be addressed with mixed interventions: both life-saving in-kind food baskets and critical WASH-related hygiene supplies for residents of 3 isolated villages and vouchers for residents of the remaining communities where it is feasible and cost-effective due to presence of some infrastructure. These measures will alleviate immediate food and hygiene needs of conflict-affected people, preventing further deterioration of food insecurity and the spread of hygiene-related diseases in the region. A focus will be placed on the elderly, people with disabilities and low-income households as they are found to experience more frequent problems with accessing quality food, maintaining their hygiene/sanitation and spend less on hygiene items according to the HNO.
A mobile team of lawyers and psychologists will carry out visits to the isolated settlements providing legal and psychological assistance to local residents. 500 beneficiaries will receive legal counseling in groups or individually, depending on the needs, and will receive secondary legal assistance if such is needed. Lawyers will pay particular attention to human rights violations, GBV and child protection assisting victims in filing cases to court. Additionally, brochures on violence against children (VAC) risks and GBV, MRE Information, documentation, IDP residence registration, social benefits, legal assistance or entitlements will be distributed.
The work of the psychological assistance will cover at least 1000 people in 23 settlements. Mobile groups of psychologists will work with locals outdoors, in community buildings and in their homes. This format has been tested during the summer of 2019, demonstrating a good coverage and effectiveness. In addition to directly providing psychological assistance, the project team will train at least 100 local residents to provide primary psychological support. This will reduce overall stress levels in frontline communities.
New water sources will be created for 4 medical facilities in remote settlements without centralized water. Sanitation facilities (including off-site sewage collection and disposal) will be repaired/upgraded in 5 medical facilities with inadequate sanitary conditions and/or water sources. 2 yearly awareness raising sessions on hygiene and sanitation practices will be conducted in these ambulant clinics for the local children and patients of the clinics.
Based on the project outcomes, a policy-paper will be prepared. It will contain an analysis of the actual problems of isolated settlements and recommendations for solutions. The document will be presented to the leadership of the regional state administration and to a wide audience.

1 - Needs

East-SOS is constantly present in settlements near the contact line and has its system of collecting information and assessing the needs of the local population. The organization collects information through the operation of its hotline, conducting regular monitoring missions, carrying out humanitarian and development projects and by taking part in the OCHA general coordination meetings and the work of clusters (FSLC, WASH and Protection). Due to the organization’s continuous presence in the 0-20 zone, the established network with local activists, close communication with centers of activity, social institutions and the local administration and MCA, full accessibility of East-SOS to the whole 0-20 zone, the East-SOS team is able to reach the most vulnerable beneficiaries like the elderly, persons with disabilities and limited mobility, and low-income households at risk.According to HNO 2020 for Ukraine, a significant number of people, particularly in the areas close to the contact line, experience major difficulties accessing quality and nutritious food, especially in winter. More than 180,000 people in the (GCA) of Donetsk and Luhansk regions remain food insecure with people residing in the 0-5 and the abovementioned groups being the most at risk. 10% of households which include an elderly person or a person with a disability are falling into the food insecure category. The female-headed households also tend to be more food insecure than those headed by men. The life-saving food assistance aims to address the immediate needs of these groups to meet their monthly food requirements.The 2020 Ukraine HNO identified that the need for WASH assistance remains high for settlements in the 0-20 km zone. 26% of all households in GCA need help with sanitation and 14% of households have unsatisfactory hygiene practices. People residing in isolated settlements are at higher risk due to the lack of or damaged critical infrastructure such as medical facilities, water supply systems and adequate roads. The need for WASH assistance is greater for rural households living within 20 km of the contact-line, particularly among certain vulnerable groups like households with below average income, elderly and persons with disabilities. The latter two groups are disproportionately vulnerable when it comes to sanitation and hygiene due to their limited physical mobility. Elderly people make up 42% of the population in the 20 km area of the contact line which is why special attention is given to them in this project.Some medical facilities in rural areas do not have access to water to centralized water which limits the access to clean water and creates the risk for the spread of waterborne diseases. The personnel in these ambulant clinics (e.g. in Muratove, Valuiske) are forced to carry water from wells nearby.Humanitarian needs within the protection sector such as freedom of movement, issues of official documentation, payment of pensions and social benefits and the need for psychosocial support remain acute for conflict-affected people in isolated settlements. Violations of international humanitarian law and human rights law are widespread and affect mostly civilians living close to the contact line.According to statistics from East-SOS legal receptions, at least 1,500 residents of the above mentioned 23 settlements have a need for legal assistance. In most cases, telephone or in-person consultations are enough to help such clients, that is why the emphasis during this project will be placed on the legal assistance embedded into the work of mobile legal and psychological assistance groups.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activity 1: Distribution of food kits (200) and food vouchers (600) to 800 of the most vulnerable beneficiaries with the highest needs through 6 distribution rounds) disaggregated by sex and age. Voucher transfers cover food needs for the period of 2 months. The intervention will follow FSLC’s recommendation (UAH 825 per person/month), covering 75% of minimum daily food needs (1,575 kcal).Outcome 1: 800 of the most vulnerable men, women and children will be able to cover their basic needs with life-saving assistance in the form of food A.2: Provision of hygiene items to households in 7 isolated settlements and providing vouchers for hygiene materials (including diapers for elderly and PwD) in the remaining 16 settlements. Voucher transfers cover hygiene needs for a 2-month period.O.2: 800 of the most vulnerable men, women and children will be able to cover their basic hygiene needs with the provided hygiene items which will prevent the spread of water and sanitation-related diseases. A.3:A mobile team of lawyers will provide legal assistance (primary and secondary legal aid) to 500 residents of 23 settlements O.3:The protection needs of women and men in isolated settlements will be addressed. Residents will be able to access quality legal assistance. Lawyers will provide consultations, information sessions, primary and secondary legal aid without discrimination.A.4:Psychologists of the mobile teams will provide psychosocial support to 1000 residents in groups or individual sessions depending on the needs.O.4: Psychosocial distress and risk behavior of residents of 23 target settlements will be lowered due to appropriate psychosocial support. Their wellbeing and dignity will be restored in an impartial and neutral manner.A.5:100 residents in 23 settlements will be trained by psychologists of the mobile team in delivering psychological first aid.O.5: The resilience of the local communities will be strengthened and supported, securing a sustainable impact in the settlements. Trained beneficiaries will be able to provide psychological first aid to residents in their communities.A.6: New water sources will be developed in 4 isolated rural medical facilities (FAP) and rural sanitation facilities (including off-site sewage collection and disposal) in 5 FAPs will be improved to the reliable provision of sufficient water of an adequate quality.O.6: Patients, medical personnel and residents of remote settlements adjacent to the 9 FAPs will have access to clean water in FAPs reducing the risk of water and sanitation-related diseases, also in possible emergency situations.A.7: Awareness raising and information dissemination on hygiene and sanitary practicesO.7: 360 children and adults will have knowledge on healthy sanitary and hygiene practices decreasing the risk of spread of diseasesA.8: Dissemination of information on protection issues such as trafficking, violence against children (VAC) risks and GBV, prevention, referrals, and availability of life-saving services, MRE Information, (MVA), HLP, documentation (including birth and death registration), IDP residence registration, social benefits, legal assistance or entitlements.O.8: Ensure that 5.000 conflict-affected people, including IDPs and vulnerable beneficiaries will have access to updated and relevant information on protection issues.AAP: East-SOS is in close communication with the beneficiaries at all stages of the project cycle. We have mechanisms in place for gathering feedback, including it into the project and ensuring that target groups can measure the adequacy of our interventions and will be able to address complaints and concerns through questionnaires, social media channels, phone hotlines and website. All feedback from beneficiaries are processed and addressed appropriately. The affected populations will be directly involved in the monitoring and evaluation activities reporting on addressing their needs, level of satisfaction, levels of stress/relief.
