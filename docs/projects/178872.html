Project Summary

The project is located in fıve sub-districts (Albab, Daeetaza, Dana, Ras Alain an and Tell Abiad ) targeting 8183 individuals (4090 M+4093 F) 3183 IDPs( 1565 M+1568 F) and 210 CWD (105 M+105 F) and consists of two key component areas: preparing currently out of school children from boys and girls for returning to formal learning, and securing equitable access to education provision for children and youth in these areas
A ‘Back to Learning’ campaign will be given alongside details of the upcoming rehabilitation works, ongoing support available to children and resources provided to families. As well as giving practical details of services, this will also inform parents of the project’s efforts to mitigate the costs of school attendance (eg: by providing books and stationery). This work also forms part of the recruitment strategy for the emerging Parent/Teacher Associations,to secure ongoing community support and participation in the provision of the stable education services.
For children not currently in school, non-formal education provision will be made in each of the five sub-districts. An initial cycle of ALP from June-September will target those children deemed most likely (based on assessments and time out of school) to be able to transition into formal provision for the start of the academic year 2021-2022. An additional cycle of ALP will be provided (up to February 2022) to give students with larger gaps in their learning experience extended support to ensure their later transition to formal learning Additional ongoing support will be provided throughout the academic year once children have transferred to formal schools; this will support their retention in this provision through remedial classes and homework support as appropriate. Children will be identified according to needs (difficulties with learning, access to learning spaces outside of school, at risk of dropping out etc
All NFE programs will integrate Child Protection activities (through provision of PSS sessions, Child Rights briefings etc) and referrals where needed, both within RW service network and other NGOs and service providers. In the formal component, a program of rehabilitation will be undertaken in 12schools (10 primary and 2 secondary) across the 5 sub-districts. This will include appropriate remedial works to the fabric of the buildings (doors, windows, lights, toilet facilities etc). For five schools (one in each of the sub-districts), additional building works will ensure the sites are appropriate and accessible for children with disabilities. Essential materials will be supplied to the schools, including whiteboards, classroom furniture, visual aids and other teaching materials. First aid kids and fuel during winter months will be supplied; security and safety plans will be established and resourced at each location. Stationery and textbooks will be provided for children boys and girls, CWD and staff females and males. Professional development and capacity building activities will enhance the performance and quality of the staff.
The project also includes provision of transportation for needed children and incentives for the volunteer teachers building on the truth that all targeted schools are not supported by any organaization and all education personnel there working as a volunteers .Through all these components, the project will therefore improve the access to and the quality of formal and non-formal education in NWS and NES which is align with the same objectives for HRP 2021 .
The project activities have been designed in accordance to responce to the main findings reported in the HNO 2021.
Till now RW has secured 31% of the planned fund for this project .
Finally all the COVID 19 preventation measures shall be taken into acount while implementing the project activities.

4 - Needs

Ten years into the Syria crisis, humanitarian needs are deepening, as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. Due to the large influx of displaced families, many schools were converted to temporary shelters, 44000 people reside in 290 schools in Daretezza , Al Bab sub-districts in Aleppo governorate and Maretemesrin and Dana in Idleb governorate and Tell abiad in Raqqa governorate and Ras Alain in Hasakeh governorate . An estimated 398,000 school-age children (5 to 17 years old) have been displaced in northwest Syria between December 2019 and February 2020. More than 300 schools are out of operation, impacting 117,000 children and more than 5,000 teachers. the drop-out of school percentage in Ras Alain and Tell abiad is 15% 42% from the families have 2 children doesn't attend school. The environment is not appropriate .The parents cannot cover the expenses of sending children to school the unappropriated education environment and Teaching resources or insufficient education (stationery, textbooks, and other) are the top 3 main reasons to send & keep children in families to schools.Tell abiad and Ras Alain communities are doted along the map, that is why there is a need to provide a transportation for the children who are based in the communities with no schools.The interviewed education personnel have indicated that they need to enhance thier capacities.In the targeted location of the assessment education authorities had decided to close all schools as a protective measure during separate COVID 19, which create a new kind of needs.List of sources:HNO 2021 Takaful Alsham Education N/A Oct 2020 Pease Spring area needs ,MSNA conducted by pease of hands Dec 2020
