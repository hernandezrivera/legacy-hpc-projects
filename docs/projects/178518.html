Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address survival needs of the most vulnerable crisis-affected households through the provision of rapid response, Multi- Purpose Cash grants. The project will target 3,185 HHs, representing an estimated 19,110 individuals, and will aim to provide short term or seasonal support to communities facing emergency gap in humanitarian service provision and the inability to meet their survival needs.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO for Syria, the economy of Syria has shrunk 60% since the onset of the conflict in 2011, leaving 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian support and 90% living below the poverty line. According to the REACH HSOS data set from January 2021, 87% of communities reported insufficient income of as the main barrier to meeting basic needs. Humanitarian access to conflict-affected communities continue to pose a challenge. In December 2020, approximately 40,000 HHs in Ar-Raqqa suddenly lost access to humanitarian assistance as a result of WFP losing access to communities in NES. An estimated 84% of the population in Ar-Raqqa is unable to meet their survival needs, as a result of dysfunctional basic services, including low functionality of health services and destroyed or damaged housing/shelters, and an estimated 75% is considered in extreme need of humanitarian assistance. In the sub-district of Menbij, 194,375 (out of a population of 260,000) are in need of humanitarian assistance. The conflict in Ein Issa and Deir ez Zor continues to displace families fleeing political and tribal conflict, thus reducing humanitarian access to the communities most in need. The proposed intervention will aim to address multisectoral, emergent needs of vulnerable, crisis-affected HHs through 3 rounds of MPCA, providing emergency or seasonal support to most vulnerable crisis-affected HHs for 3 months, until they can be linked to other types of assistance services or sources of income. In line with the Cash WG guidelines for Syria, MPCA will not fully meet the basic needs of targeted HHs. Rather, this will allow crisis-affected people to make choices and prioritize their most urgent needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

DCA will utilise MPC as a mechanism for providing short-term emergency support to conflict and crisis affected HHs who lack access to other humanitarian support. At the community level this will be defined in collaboration with coordination mapping, needs analysis, and severity score. At HH level, if not possible to cover entire community in need, HHs with the highest vulnerability score will be selected. In addition to the provision of 3 months of MPC support, DCA will utilise multi-sector assessment data collected at the HH and community level to support communities with linkages to other humanitarian support to ensure survival needs are addressed past the 3-month period. Support will be provided in line with CWG guidelines, in USD with a harmonized value of 100 USD per HH. By distributing in USD DCA aims to mitigate the impact of inflation on entitlements and subsequent outcomes, in line with CWG guidance. Assessment and PDM will be conducted prior to and after cash distributions and include HH level surveys as well as trader and consumer surveys, and KIIs to inform the relevance, feasibility, and subsequent outcomes of MPCA at household, market, and community level. DCA will seek to mitigate possible tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities through the engagement of communities in the identification of vulnerable HHs and clear messaging regarding entitlements. All community stakeholders will have access to DCAs feedback and complaints mechanism, through which beneficiaries will have the opportunity to request inclusion. DCA coordinates closely with the HAOs, city and district level authorities, and komins.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Tensions due to jealousies between IDPs, Returnees and Host communities. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Targeting, where possible, at community level where emergent needs are widespread and impacting the majority households; 2. Where required, targeting will be defined in collaboration with local community leaders who will also support in defining initial assessment lists; 3. Include consideration of conflict dynamics in the defining of targeting, and where possible ensure complementary support is provided to nearby communities not receiving MPC support.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Duty of Care policies and procedures are in place for national staff. Risk: Local authority-imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent project staff from moving and/or working, and/or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumption: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff), and Money Transfer Agents (MTAs) will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA will coordinate with other cash actors and monitor liquidity trends closely with MTAs to ensure continued operationality of the proposed action. DCA staff will follow local authority-imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should liquidity or other operational challenges create significant challenges in operational viability.
