Project Summary

This project is designed to contribute to helping Syrian returnee families especially for those who are already living in their apartments in bad weather circumstances without any type of security and privacy.
. Rural Damascus governorate has a high number of PiN (2021), more than 643,000 people and ranked at severity level 3 (WoS SNFI PiN 2021). Other areas such Moaadamieh Harasta and Adra AL Omalieh in high need of rehabilitation of damaged houses. The high rate of apartment rental forced people to return to their original damaged apartment or to stay in apartments illegally. These families are in need of emergency response such as Damaged houses rehabilitation.
In Deir Ezzor governorate more than 208,000 are in shelter need at severity level 3 (WoS SNFI PiN 2021). During March 2021, ADRA identiﬁed a high need for rehabilitation of damaged houses in the neighborhoods of Al Omal (wasat and est) where there is a high number of returning families because the governorate and INGOS are working to provide main services (electricity ,Water, Sanitation….) and the most of the buildings are structural safe.
ADRA is planning to respond to 750 individuals including(male, female, boys and girls) from the most vulnerable families (IDPs, Returnees and host communities)through the rehabilitation of damaged houses in the governorates of Deir Ezzor and Rural Damascus. A total of 150 apartments will be targeted under the shelter long-term solutions. ADRA will also provide 3,333 individuals with winterization NFIs to increase resilience against the cold in winter.

4 - Needs

The Syrian crisis has had a devastating impact on shelter and access to NFIs across Syria with more than 5.9 million people in need of shelter assistance (a four per cent increase from 2020), and 3,943,119 individuals remain in need of NFI winterization assistance. It is estimated that 50% of returnees lives in damaged house and 75% have no access to services, leading to secondary displacement (HNO 2021). Rent in public or private buildings and are increasing in number. These tend to be heavily overcrowded with insufficient services. Without access to collective shelters, the most vulnerable IDP population establishes makeshift shelters on vacant lots and vacant buildings that usually lack access to essential services. Based on ADRA’s ongoing shelter projects across Syria, IDPs are hosted mostly with families and friends, creating a severe strain on family relations, financial burdens, and overcrowding. IDPs and returnees cannot meet their basic needs, including shelter, due to already strained resources spent on food and clothes. The economic condition in Syria, including fluctuation in the exchange rate, the sanctions' impact in compound with COVID 19 also has contributed to increased poverty whereby families can no longer afford essential NFIs, commodities and rent. Between 94-98% of HHs in Rural Damascus have increased debt (HNO 2021) and according to WFP, the food basket has increased to 120,000 SYP ($96).

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will provide safe and private access to adequate shelter and winterization NFIs for the most vulnerable families through the repair of damaged houses, the distribution of NFIs, the rehabilitation of collective shelters and transitional shelters and the improvement of public infrastructure. ADRA will coordinate with the relevant governorates, MoLAE and the local municipalities for identification of the specific needs and identification of an initial list of beneficiaries. ADRA will then conduct a vulnerability assessment using vulnerability criteria in alignment with the sector to verify the need. The selection criteria will include IDPs, returnees, and refugees families registered with MoLAE and with the necessary housing documents. Additional selection/vulnerability criteria will be applied including families with a high number of individuals, female headed households, people with disabilities, families with elderly members are living or considering returning to their apartments. For NFIs, ADRA will utilize similar selection criteria yet will target areas particularly impacted by harsh winter conditions and the lack of income to afford basic NFI commodities. Post beneficiary selection, ADRA will complete a competitive procurement process according to ADRA’s Procurement Policy, select a contractor and complete works according to the technical specifications and BoQ based on the technical assessment. The targeted building will be the slightly or moderated damaged apartments checked by the ADRA engineering team. Shelter and NFI works will be monitored regularly by ADRA and handed over to MoLAE. Beneficiaries will be informed of ADRA’s complaints mechanism and a PDM will be conducted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Target areas are perceived to prioritize and empower one group over another or unfair selection of communities, causing community tension and increasing the risk of (S)GBV. 2. Vulnerable community members, particularly women and children are asked to provide services in return for humanitarian assistance. 3. There is unfair selection of apartments based on community connections. 4. Child labour used on project sites increases the risk of school drop out and protection risks such as physical injury in addition to GBV. 5. Vulnerable families do not have appropriate HLP limiting access to assistance. 6. Risk of security incidents and community tension at NFI distributions. 7. Risk of IED/UXOs found during shelter interventions.Mitigation: 1. Conduct detailed assessments at local level to ensure assistance considers community dynamics, explain selection criteria clearly to Ministries and ensure communities selected are based on needs assessments alone. 2. Train staff in PSEA and closely monitor all staff and contractors working in target areas. Ensure complaints’ feedback mechanism is available to bene ciaries. 3. Ensure detailed needs assessments are conducted in coordination with local stakeholders ensuring works are in areas promoting equal access to assistance. 4. Explain ADRA Syria’s policy on child labor to contractor prior. 5. Refer beneficiaries to appropriate authorities to obtain documents. 6. Monitor security situation and ensure adequate staffing and social distancing at distribution points. 7. Ensure houses are inspected carefully during assessment.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. Exchange rate and price fluctuation in the local market causes withdrawal of contractors during implementation. 2. COVID-19 pandemic and the preventative measures that can result in a delay of implementation of the project. 3. Lack of fuel due to the imposed sanctions, causing long lines to purchase fuel, may result in the inability of the ADRA team to conduct field visits. 4. Delay in obtaining necessary approvals from the MoLA, which may delay implementation of the project. 5. Delayed fund transfer resulting in delayed implementation. 6.Apartments have structural damage endangering residents. 7.Access to Deir Al Zor causes delays. Mitigation: 1.ADRA will try to shorten the procurement process duration in order to allow them to commit to their prices. 2. ADRA has a COVID-19 response policy that ensures the staff's protection during site visits including PPE and social distancing. 3. ADRA will ensure several cars are available. 4. ADRA has good relations with MoLAE and the relevant governorates. ADRA will conduct regular visits to follow up on the approval process. 5. ADRA will provide more than one transfer pathway, will closely monitor the situation inside and outside Syria, and keep close coordination with the donor. 6. Apartments will be excluded from project. 7. ADRA will monitor access regularly yet has been successful thus far.
