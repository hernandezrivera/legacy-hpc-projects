Project Summary

The programme has established Non-Formal Education (NFE) Centres in 2 locations in NE Syria (Ar-Raqqa governorate) to target children between 8 to 10 years who are currently out of school and have never attended school. Concern will also support formal education, by working with teacher in 15 schools who will be supported through Teachers in Conflict Context Training. These schools will also be provided with essential teaching and learning materials. Essential repairs to WASH facilities and infrastructure will also be carried out in these same 15 schools, creating a safe and conducive learning environment. Concern has conducted school WASH assessments in Tabqa and Ar-Raqqa and the rehabilitation of WASH facilities will include installing latrines, providing access to clean water (water network or trucking) and needs for minor structural fittings i.e. windows and doors in order to meet the minimum standards for Child Protection and ensure the children's safety in the buildings.

Needs

Children in Syria face numerous challenges, including limited access to education. The 2018Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) notes that 1.75 million children and youth are out of school,accounting for more than one-third of school aged children and 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out. There are gaps in infrastructure with 53% of educational facilities have been partially damaged and 10% have been destroyed and over 180,000 teachers and educational personal have left the formal system. Across Syria, schools account for an estimated 33 per cent of collective centres hosting IDPs. In the Ar-Raqqah city, as reported by the Education committee in June 2018, there are only 31 active primary level schools. These schools are run by 540 teachers (65% female teachers) and host 14,813 students (54% girls, 46% boys). From the 10,000 people surveyed by Concern in Ar-Raqqah cityin the first half of 2018, only 24.8% have declared that their primary school age children are regularlyattending school, which leaves 7,552 out of school children between 6 and 12 years of age.In Al-Thawrah, as reported by the Education committee in June 2018, there are 189 active school in Al-Thawrah. 49 other schools are in need of repairs and 20 have been totally destroyed, 63 are being used as accommodation centres for IDPs and 8 as military points. The active schools host a total of 47,476students (48.6% girls) for 1750 teachers (56.8% female teachers). A large displaced population has put additional pressure on schools that are already overburdened, and fragmented governance structures have struggled to cope. Many teachers have left Syria, many more have left the teaching profession and a significant number of male teachers have died in the conflict or been enrolled into the armed forces. The remaining teachers are overstretched and overwhelmed by the number of students and the lack of adequate resources to teach them. In the districts targeted by this action, 15% of the households assessed by Concern consider education as their highest priority need. In the 3 districts, of the total 20,433 households surveyed having primary school age children (6 to 12 years old), 79% declared that their children are not attending school regularly. Concern conducted an assessment for 681 children in Raqqa and Tabqa in January 2018 and found that 51.1% were not currently attending school and 39.2% had never attended school. Concern will in February 2018 conduct an The Early Grade Reading Assessment and an Early Grade Mathematics Assessment so assess literacy and numeracy levels of the children not attending school. Similarly, an annual State ofEducation (ASER) pilot was conducted by the International Rescue Committee (IRC) in November 2016 among 2,846 children (1,255 boys and 1,591 girls) for five schools in Idleb Governorate. The results highlight significant learning gaps with most children in 6-7-8 grades not being able to read a simple 7-10 sentence story or solve a simple subtraction problem, both of which were at 2nd grade level (IRC 2017, DFID 2017). With six different curriculum being taught at different, and often overlapping, times during the conflict(Government of Syria, religious curricula, Kurdish curriculum, ISIS system, Syrian Interim Governmentand Jabhat Tharir el Sham) huge disparities exist in the quality and form of education being delivered to children. Further divergence in the quality of education provided is evident between IDPs and hostcommunities (HNO 2018). While in many areas schools have remained open and the demand for education is high, most schools are not inclusive of the most vulnerable children, including for example orphans, children with disabilities and those engaged in child labour or formerly associated with armed groups. The gaps in access and quality are reinforcing the lack of means to address properly the children's safety and wellbeing.

Implementation of activities

The primary objective of this action is to increase access to quality education in a safe learning environments for Syrian children affected by conflict. Education support and services, to include elements of WASH and Protection considerations, will be provided in both NFEs and formal education establishments to help ensure a) out of school children are appropriately targeted and prepared for formal education transfers after 10 weeks; and b) children in formal education establishments are learning in schools and classrooms which are safe and managed by teachers who are adequately equipped to address the educational and emotional needs of children affected by conflict. Parents and caregivers will be encouraged to attend parent-teacher sessions which raise awareness about what the children are learning, formal school referral mechanisms, psychosocial support referral mechanisms and anti-bullying measures. Concern's global Education Strategy is closely aligned with the INEE handbook (Access, Quality and Wellbeing). This action will follow these principles, with some limitations on the quality aspect in formal schools due to the challenges posed by the self-administration restrictive control of curricula.This action will improve access to formal education for out-of-school children, through the establishment of three NFE centres for children in Al-Thawrah and Ar Raqqa and delivery of 10 week Accelerated Learning Programmes (ALP). Six ALP cycles will be delivered during the one-year intervention. The locations for the NFE centres will be established in consultation with local authorities, and the population targeted will include primary school age children who are out of school or have never attended school due to conflict. The objective is to create a safe place for primary school aged children to build the confidence and skills (literacy and numeracy) required to integrate back into formal education. The target is that 80% of the 720 children attending will return to school. School transportation services will be provided in each district for the most vulnerable children to ensure education support is inclusive for all children. With the aim of improving quality of education, the Teachers in Crises Contexts training package will be contextualized and used to better equip education professionals who are operating in 15 schools in conflict affected areas. The TICC package will be used to train both teachers in NFE centres and in targeted schools. The purpose of providing tailored trainings is to integrate basic psychosocial support within standard teaching and to help contribute towards broader learning outcomes. Integrating psychosocial support into the formal education curriculum is the most effective way of responding to the daily psychosocial support needs of the majority of students many not need more specialist support. Additionally, Concern will also rehabilitate the WASH facilities and provide teaching materials to the 15 schools supported.Teachers in Concern's NFE centres will also be trained on the ALP modules, whereas this will be excluded from the training of teachers in formal schools due to limitations posed by the self-administration. Wellbeing and safety in the learning environment will be addressed in both NFE centres and formal schools. NFE centres will be selected to include space for both academic and more social activities including outdoor space for playing games and for creative activities such as art. Teacher training on Social and Emotional Learning will also be included to help teachers provide basic psychosocial supports to students who pass through the NFE centres. This support is required for a student's psychosocial development and to help mitigate any effects of displacement, conflict and missed periods of education. A referral mechanism will be used to refer children who need more specialist psychosocial support to other service providers.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk- There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS or FSA groups supported by Turkey, especially in border districts such as Manbij and Al-Thawrah. In 2018, peaking at the close of the year, Turkey has taken a much more aggressive stance against areas controlled by the self-administration in North East Syria and is threatening to hold a third military campaign there to establish a buffer zone along the length of the border. There is also the risk that the fragile coalition of parties and groups that form the Syrian Democratic Council could unravel as a result of internal differences and/or external pressure, which would weaken the local authorities and likely spell increased conflict, criminality and insecurity. This situation might prevent Concern working in several areas in NES, could lead to the evacuation of international staff, scaling down to emergency assistance or closing down of some or all operations depending on the severity and scale of the conflict. - Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioriation in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. In these instances, partial or complete access to Der Ez Zor - where a significant proportion of this Action's activities are targeted, would be prevented. AssumptionsThe security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling Concern to establish a field office in Basira in the first quarter of 2019 and to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Al-Thawrah. - Concern will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating MesuresConcern has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict - particularly in the wake of the likely US troop withdrawal in early-mid 2019. Concern's response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localised insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, Concern would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly dispaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), Concern would remotely manage staff and the project from its support office in Dohuk and using its remote-management team based in Turkey
