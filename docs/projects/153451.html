Project Summary

The overall project aim is to ensure that children at risk of, or exposed to violence, exploitation, and abuse access immediate and quality life-saving assistance while prevention and response interventions are strengthened in emergencies. The project’s main target is children (boys and girls) in selected payams in Yambio and Nzara counties.
CMMB will work closely with all relevant stakeholders to deliver comprehensive child protection case management services including referrals to specialised services for 350 vulnerable children. To ensure quality service delivery, a total of 30 social workers and community volunteers will be trained child protection case management, mentored and supervised to effectively support children. To enhance protection of released/vulnerable children,50 foster/kinship caregivers will be identified, trained and supported to take care of children who need care as their families/caregivers are traced. Family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children will be strengthened through working with volunteers in the local communities. They will be trained to identify and refer UASC/released children for appropriate services. Provision of both mobile and community based psychosocial support will be enhanced through establishing 6 CFS sites. Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection issues, and awareness raising to address prevailing protection issues, and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment, and family separation will be part of this intervention. CP referral pathways will be developed to link children and communities with various life-saving services. Provision of life skills will enhance resilience of vulnerable boys and girls.

Needs

Security situation in and around Yambio county deteriorated in 2015 leading to numerous atrocities against children and their families. Even though the situation seems to be improving needs of children largely remain unmet. During a recent IDP verification exercise in Yambio (13-15th September 2018), they mentioned that there were cases of rape, forced recruitment into armed groups for children, destruction of infrastructure such as health centres, houses, schools, roads and cases of separated children. Orphaned children were also reported to be living within communities with no support exposing them to increased vulnerability to sexual and physical violence. A rapid needs assessment conducted in Lirangu on 1st of October 2018 was informed that there are unaccompanied, separated and missing children though there was no estimated figure. Due to lack of functional schools some of the children are engaged in smoking bang, sexual activities and alcohol drinking.During an OCHA facilitated joint assessment mission to Nadiangere on 4th August 2018 there were disturbing findings on child protection concerns. Survival sex was mentioned by the young girls as most of them preferred to elope with men to access food and other materials.90% of the young ladies interviewed 13 years -21 years were already married off exposing them to sexually transmitted infections including HIV/AIDs and missing opportunities to go to school.The interviewed community members mentioned that there are over 100 unaccompanied children living alone/non-relatives. Children expressed that they still fear attacks from armed group members and some were abducted especially young boys. Nadiangere primary school was burnt down and all the teachers ran way leaving children with no access to education. Those who dare to go to Yambio for studies face harassments and arrests forcing them to return to Nadiangere. Children are still psychologically affected by attacks carried out in May/June 2018. They also complained of sleeping under trees exposing children to risks of malaria and other health issues.High prevalence of mental health issues and limited access to services in South Sudan is a big challenge. Displacements have fragmented family and community support mechanisms. Compared to 2016, number of children reported as recruited/used by listed armed forces/groups, increased by 8 % in 2017. Norms instil generalized acceptance of some forms of violence. In April 2016, the National Military of South Sudan, the Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA), and South Sudan National Liberation Movement (SSNLM) signed a peace agreement allowing UNICEF, National DDR Commission and UNMISS advocate for release and reintegration of all children. 724 children have so far been released and received comprehensive reintegration services out of targeted 1000. The remaining numbers needs urgent support. Although no recent assessments have been conducted in Nzara county, child protection needs are enormous. Majority of displaced communities live in Nzara due to its relative calm at the height of the conflicts. Despite all these findings the level of response has been very low exposing children especially girls to early marriages, sexual exploitation and early pregnancies in both Yambio and Nzara. Adolescent girls remain targets of multiple forms of violence including being taken as wives of the rebel members and forced to have sex. Negative cultural norms remain a menace for young girls who are married off. Lack of adequate CP actors after prolonged conflict has not only raised the no of CP concerns but left many children exposed to violence, exploitation and abuse.

Activities

The project activities will focus on case management including family tracing and reunification,psychosocial support through CFS and awareness raising on key child protection issues as well as strengthening community based CP systems as elaborated below.Children formerly associated with armed conflict/released children and other vulnerable children require comprehensive case management services including referrals to specialized services to identify their vulnerabilities and jointly work with them and their families to find solutions. Children formerly associated with armed forces/groups will be identified, referred/documented and provided reintegration services in line with standard practice and guidelines for reintegration of CAAFAG in South Sudan. Identified cases will be documented using the appropriate case management tools. A total of 150 released/vulnerable children will benefit from case management services including family tracing and reunification in Yambio and Nzara counties. To ensure quality service delivery, a total of 15 social workers and community volunteers will be trained on child protection case management, mentored and supervised to effectively support these children. To enhance protection of released/vulnerable children,20 foster/kinship caregivers will be identified, trained and supported to take care of the children who need care as their families/caregivers are traced. They will be identified from within communities where the vulnerable children come from to ensure they quickly settle into foster families. To adequately support children, 30 child protection staff and non-CP staff as well as government staff will be trained to understand preventing and responding to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children in times of emergency given the volatile conflict environment. Community based child protection structures will also be trained on CPIE to strengthen their knowledge on identification and refer UASC/released children for appropriate services. Provision of both mobile and community based psychosocial support will be enhanced through establishment of 4 CFS sites and delivery of structured psychosocial support activities that will target 800 boys and girls. Appropriate parenting skill sessions will be provided to 200 caregivers and other community members through the CFS to strengthen their support to a safe environment for children.Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection issues through awareness raising to address prevailing child protection issues, response and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment, and family separation will be emphasised.1500 adults and children will be targeted for sensitization through campaigns, leaflets and focus on specific groups. CP referral pathways will be developed/revived and disseminated to link children and communities with various life-saving services. Establishment of adolescent peer to peer support groups will strengthen their coping skills towards various life issues and build strong social networks.500 youths are targeted across the 2 counties.CMMB co-chairs the state child protection working group that will be key in addressing duplication across various locations ans activities.Appropriate coordination with all other CP actors will be given much emphasis to ensure no duplication of services.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Community entry meetings with both children and their caregivers at the onset of the intervention ensure that they are aware of which services will be provided, for how long and where. During these sessions they are able to seek clarification and answers on the assistance. Regular meetings with children at the child friendly space ensure they are well informed on the activities and any changes/gaps addressed. Child protection committees identified within the communities are also sensitised on available services who in turn educate communities.Quarterly review meetings further provide platforms for information sharing as well as targeted radio messages and general community sensitization forums.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Since the intervention targets children, messages are designed to be child friendly considering their context. Role plays, drama and songs are employed to simplify and break down complex messages for children to understand considering their age and sex. Pictorial messages are also easily understood by children hence their common usage. Children are guided to draw and paint pictures that express their feelings, dreams and aspirations in life. Messages on appropriate management of menstrual cycle target adolescent girls and are delivered to this group only.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

To reduce potential risks of SEA all staff will be trained on PSEA. Appointment of PSEA Focal Points across the project sites will be given priority. The trained focal points will then be assisted to conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men of communities of concern ensuring that they are aware of their rights and entitlements. Various information sharing fora will be used that included posters, videos, community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas for children and recommending protective actions to combat against SEA will be a key focus throughout the project life. Working with targeted communities the Focal Points design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints (action plans for who what will do, where, and how) with a contact for reporting any suspicions, concerns, issues, questions, and cases. Children feedback mechanisms take various forms including using of pictures to express information. All staff working with children sign a code of conduct that spell out what is expected of them as relates to SEA matters and how to protect children and help them report. Effective prevention mechanisms, including effective recruitment and performance management and establishment of internal complaints and investigation procedures is a priority. An effective and anonymous complaints mechanism to allow and encourage individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely will be strengthened. All such complaints should be immediately brought to the attention of the Program Director either directly by the beneficiary or by the person to whom such an incident is reported or through the PSEA focal points. Children will be assisted to report.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Children are consulted when designing the child friendly space programs and jointly work with animators to prepare activity timetables. Their opinions were central in selecting appropriate activities. Communities were also engaged through general meetings and focus group discussions targeting women, girls, boys and men. The community volunteers also play a key role in connecting communities with project activities at various stages e.g. needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation. Girls are encouraged to take active participation and provide leadership at the child friendly space and other relevant activities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Continuous community engagement and provision of feedback has been instrumental in identifying needs and guiding design of appropriate CP activities. Community feedback mechanisms will continue to be enhanced through the volunteers, community meetings, focus group discussions, displaying of available complaint options and suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations at the child friendly spaces. Project start up workshops involving all relevant stakeholders are platforms to share project details that enable them, and communities hold the project team accountable during implementation and follow ups. At this workshop roles and responsibilities of various stakeholders are clearly defined and appropriate feedback mechanisms shared. Available feedback options are shared including contacts where the beneficiaries can directly report their concerns without involving project teams. Regular monthly meetings at the CFS encourage children to share their concerns and jointly find appropriate solutions. The CFS sites are centrally agreed with beneficiaries including children to reduce conflict and cater for their individual needs.

Children provide feedback on activities conducted through smiling faces at the end of each session and one on one discussions. Community feedback mechanisms are developed through the volunteers, community meetings, focus group discussions, displaying of available complaint options and suggestion boxes where appropriate. Child protection committee members monitor day to day activities at the CFS and share any community concerns with the team. Project start up workshop involving all relevant stakeholders share project details that will enable the community to hold the project team accountable during project implementation.Joint quarterly progress reviews and meetings address key concerns/gaps and jointly develop appropriate recommendations/follow up actions that are reviewed in the next meetings. The community volunteers will also play a key role in connecting communities with project activities at various stages e.g. needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation. The project will also conduct mid term and end of project evaluations working closely with targeted communities.This will inform current intervention as well as future interventions.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project places special emphasis on affected population’s ability to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Community child protection committees are empowered to enhance the protection of affected and at-risk children as they better understand the local context. They work closely with caregivers of targeted children. Different segments of affected populations are meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being through focus group discussions, key informants, participation in specific activities etc. At risk populations have been identified, how and why they are at risk considering their specific vulnerabilities including those experienced by girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, persons with disabilities, and other minority groups.The project will endevour to respond to the needs of the various population groups based on their level of vulnerability as identified through various protection risk analysis strategies.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identifies and addresses barriers to access faced by various population groups. The child friendly spaces are within safe and central locations to ensure children do not walk long distances nor get exposed to unsafe locations. Lack of appropriate information sharing protocols that might expose sensitive information discouraging survivors seeking for services. Approved information sharing protocols and use of CPIMS tools will minimize this. Limited access to project sites by children living with disabilities and the very young ones. This will be addressed through engaging community volunteers who might identify and report these children whereby mechanisms are developed to take closer services to them and encouraging other children to support them. Adolescents are also supported to organize activities with young children.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities address issues of inequalities and protection threats by identifying the threats, who is affected, how they are affected and why. This is done through community-based protection structures e.g. child protection committees, adolescent girls and young women empowerment initiatives, self-esteem and rights awareness. Establishment of peer to peer support clubs for adolescents’ address inequalities among boys and girls enhance peaceful co-existence among children.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In all stages of the project cycle, protection risk analysis has been given special attention to ensure that targeted populations are not exposed to more harm through the services provided. Vulnerable children e.g. released children are provided access to safe environments and families/caregivers supported with necessities to keep them safe. Non-food items and food commodities provided to released children ensure they are able to offer appropriate support. Family tracing and reunification is conducted for those separated from their families as well alternative care placements done as tracing continues. Provision of a safe protective environment ensures that children are not recruited into armed forces/groups nor exploited. Appropriate parenting skills sessions to caregivers and communities support positive interactions with children and care for parents of young children and of children with special needs. There are agreed upon guidelines on identification of CFS sites that guide communities on identifying safe and central locations for CFS activities to reduce risks of attack on children. Sensitive child protection information is shared through agreed upon information sharing protocols and coding using the CPIMS tools. Efforts have been incorporated to avoid open discussions of sensitive child protection cases/information through case conferences involving on those responsible for offering direct services to vulnerable children.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Establishment of child friendly spaces that offer structured, supportive educational and protective activities for children go a long way in providing a safe environment for children. Community protection mechanisms include self-help activities that promote psychosocial well-being spearheaded by children and caregivers.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Risks and threats identified through protection risk analysis are minimized through advocacy with relevant authorities to secure children when providing assistance, facilitating their own efforts to stay safe and taking steps to reduce exposure to risk. Grave violations against children’s rights are monitored and reported while advocacy for the rights of affected populations with relevant authorities and child protection actors is enhanced through coordination meetings and case specific meetings with authorities reminding them of their obligations to protect children. Sensitive information is managed to avoid exposing vulnerable children to further harm. Through peer to peer support clubs’ children are empowered to address their child protection issues including safe reporting to caregivers/child protection staff. Child protection committee members support vulnerable children at the community level.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Culturally appropriate burials, religious ceremonies and practices, and non-harmful cultural and social practices are supported for children who have lost their dear ones to enhance their coping strategies/mechanisms. Child survivors of GBV are supported to participate in structured and protective activities at the child friendly spaces as well as receive nutrition and medical support where appropriate. CFS activities enhance their psychosocial well-being. Through the CFS, children have access to psychosocial support, social networks and self-help activities that boost their dignity.
