Project Summary

The project aims to address the humanitarian challenges of a classified affected hard to reach populations in Taleex and Lascanood districts of Sool Region. The emergency nutrtion proposal is intended to avert the present nutrition situation which is serious to critical with GAM rate of Malnutrition of 13.6%, and according to FSNAU Post Gu’ update, there are 20,000 persons in IPC4 (Crisis) living in Sool and region. There are 15.9 childhood morbidity rate in Northern inland pastoral of NW Region. The populations living In Sool region has been recently affected by several medium scale conflicts causing massive displacement of the already weakened and drought affected communities. The proposed intervention is combined emergency curative and preventive nutrition services to be in access to respond to the needs of malnutrition affected women, children, youth, and men along with the most vulnerable pregnant and lactating women, elderly and persons with disabilities. This will be achieved through recruiting and training Emergency nutrition teams on standard Somalia IMAM and IYCF tools with the purpose of early detection and treatment of under 5 children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition. Thereafter, the project will help and give integrated services such us screening, identifying, and treating children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition and complicated cases will be referred to the nearest TSFP service center. The pregnant/lactating women will receive multiple micro-nutrient supplementation, while benefiting Infant Young Child Feeding (IYCF) counseling services. Breastfeeding promotion is provided throughout the course of the project. The teams will also support equitable lifesaving health services to acutely ill children and mothers by referring to the nearest Health facility. In protection mainstreaming, HEAL fully involves IDPs, including from marginalized communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects.
The project components include:
1. The project will support 4 mobile teams (2 in Taleex and 2 in Laascaanood Districts) and four fixed Nutrition sites (Karingorfod of Laascaanod and Taleex MCHs) .
2. The identification, referral and treatment of 2,140 6-59 months children suffering from acute malnutrition through Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) facility based Services, referral to Therapeutic Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and community based approaches.
2. The project will strengthen local technical capacity for integration of Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) into the Primary Health Care system through utilization of Basic Nutrition Services Package (BNSP).
3. Strategies are strengthening supply chain management, well-trained health workers, financing, and use of existing community based nutrition programs in order to scale-up prevention, early identification and treatment of acute malnutrition of children and Pregnant and Lactating (PLW).
4. Service delivered will be Maternal and child nutrition components including IMAM, IYCF, NHHP, referral system, Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) supplies, nutrition surveillance and as well as mobilizing, educating and creating demand for service uptake.
5. Prevention services will include de-worming, counseling and promotion on infant and young child feeding including Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) in emergencies. Community mobilization, screening and referral, micro-nutrient supplementation for PLW (multiple micro-nutrients and Iron Folate), Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) and U5 (Vitamin A supplements) for children reinforced with Communication for development (C4D).
6. In collaboration with the nutrition cluster the project support to existing programs at community and facility level.
7. The intervention protects children and women by fostering resilience to prepare absorb shocks, through adaptive learning, collective action, cohesion and self-reliant

Mitigation Measures

The Emergency intervention will contribute into the protection of vulnerable and minor groups such as the poor, the socially disregarded or segregated groups, disabled persons and others. HEAL keeps in mind that the times of emergency there are increased chances of abuse and marginalization of these groups, therefore throughout the different stages of the intervention total inclusion will be exercised by all program staff. The beneficiaries will be served according to the prioritized needs assessment and therefore service provision will be based solely on their specific needs, with a total disregard to any other kind of influence. The program is build to ensure meeting humanitarian needs by embracing the SPHERE Standards. Rape is not so common in the target locations, however, HEAL being aware that its cultural practice to not reveal the information to avoid stigmatization of ones own daughter/sister etc. therefore is under-reported. These obscure perceptions can be overcome by mainstreaming and awareness raising and enhancement of fundamental right and dignity of everybody especially the vulnerable and the disadvantaged .HEAL will ensure increased partnership and mainstreaming of protection is aligned in all Humanitarian response operations. The project will prevent any form of abuse and maltreatment of women and children, especially sexual violation and child labor in ex-change of project services, job opportunity and aid donation, through Do-No-Harm and Interest sensitivity. HEAL will Train all project staff and IERTs, promoters and will advocate for the prevention and elimination of any form of human rights violation and abuse, neglect and maltreatment of the project target beneficiaries, and instead will prioritize safety and dignity of beneficiaries, through community mobilization and awareness campaign. The emergency response project will facilitate Support mechanism and referral services for the victims and survivors
