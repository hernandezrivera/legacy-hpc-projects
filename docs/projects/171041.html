Project Summary

AYUUB’s assessment in August 2020 shows new floods affected people are still suffering from lack of WASH facilities which has accelerated concerns on earlier WASH problems. The number of the flood affected people are about 5,077HH in Marka alone- almost the same as earlier ‘Hagaa’ flood affected Households. There is high risk of increased waterborne diseases including AWD due to open defecation since latrines are not available in the newly located camps. Other needs identified included the need to deploy manpower and cash for work to close river breakages and mitigate further flooding, drain flood waters in schools, social centers and key community spots with such tools as drainage dewatering pumps, drainage systems and man-power and rehabilitation of latrines to suit needs of disabled as well, water points, emergency water supply to the affected communities in Merka, Qoryoole, Qurtunwarey and Wanlaweyne.
South West State joint inter-agency WASH assessment at community, health facilities and public schools (17th-29th August,2020) shows that there were an estimated 7,833 families (46,998 individuals) in Marka affected by the most recent floods. The 280 assessed community water points revealed that 12.5% are not functioning. 31% of the water points assessed do not provide enough water to the targeted communities. 29% of the assessed public schools do not have water supply systems in their IDP camps and while 68% of the villages do have piped water, 10% of their water supply is through water trucking, 10% depend on shallow wells while 9% of them depend on harvested rainwater through berkads. 23% of the IDPs assessed do not have water supply system. Marka district has a 48% gap in the access to safe and clean drinking water as 3,845HHs need access to emergency clean water as the same number of people were displaced by the floods. Also, there are no water sources near the settlements in Marka, Qoryooley and Wanlaweyne districts where these people were displaced to. In Marka district has a 76% gap in access to safe clean water as there are no boreholes; 29% of the respondents from Marka mentioned that they pay for water. OCHA also reported that the flood waters are receding in Afgooye and Wanla Weyn whilst in Marka flooding is alerted for KM50 town and nearby villages in the last weeks of August 2020.
96% of Marka respondents indicated that they lack hygiene materials including soap (65% lacking) and hand washing facilities especially during these Covid-19 pandemic times and the looming AWD. A paltry 45% of respondents indicated using soap to wash hands especially after visiting latrines. This has led to reported cases of diarrhea among children <5 with Marka reporting about 43% of children above 5 years of age having experienced diarrhea in the last two-week preceding the assessment (Inter-agency Joint WASH Assessment 29th August 2020).
On the access of latrines, slightly over 30% of the respondents in Marka reported that they don’t have access to a latrine in their HHs. Only 25% of the respondents in Marka reported that they have access to gender-segregated latrines. 19% of the respondents in Marka reported access to latrines designed for people with disabilities. 95% of the latrines assessed were not usable by persons with disabilities.
In response to these needs, AYUUB will provide emergency access to clean water through water trucking and give sustainable access to water through rehabilitation of strategic water point and shallow wells. The default modality will be Market Based Programming (MBP) to strengthen local markets and this will involve cash for work for solid waste management including garbage collection and clean up campaigns. The hygiene promotion and provision of hygiene kits to target communities with gaps in hygiene promotion, construction of new latrines and rehabilitation latrine affected by floods, and distribution of hygiene kits will also form part of the interventions.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Construct Ventilated Improved pit latrines (VIP) including handwashing facilities in priority settings in Wanalweyn and Marka districts and in institutions including schools through CFW, health facilities to benefit 6000 people (30 persons per toilet as per the SPHERE standards) vulnerable IDP communities with equal access to sanitation facilities. AYUUB will identify skilled and non-skilled individuals from 500HHs from the IDPs and train them on latrine construction, latrine designs and required quality. AYUUB will purchase latrine construction materials for the trained individuals from the IDPs to do construction of the latrines with guidance and supervision of the WASH engineer and WASH Project Manager. 10% of latrines will be constructed to meet the needs of people with disabilities and the elderly (ramp, hand rail, wide door, easy lock should be considered as appropriate). This will also involve clear site of all debris and digging pit hole 1.5m diameter × 3.2m depth. The substructure will involve construction of rubble stone or concrete blocks foundation (300 or 200 mm thick, 600 mm deep), then a reinforced concrete slab at at-least 300 mm above ground level. The structure will be constructed using Galvanized Corrugated Iron (GCI) sheets, local poles, timber and GCI sheets roofing.Activity 1.1.2: Hygiene promotion conducted to 2,333 HHs. AYUUB will train hygiene promoters who will in turn train the rest of the community in groups on topics like hand-washing practices, Household water treatment and storage etc. Hygiene promotion will also be conducted in institutions including schools and health facilities etc to increase awareness safe hygiene practices. Activity 1.1.3: Distribute hygiene promotion kits and standard IEC materials to flood affected IDPs and high-risk households to prevent the spread of AWD in the IDP camps and among host communities. The hygiene kits will be obtained upon request from the WASH supply hub in Southwest State. This hygiene kits will include 2x Non-collapsible jerry cans (20L); 3 x pieces of soap; 3mth supply of aquatabs for the target of 2,333 HHs. The hygiene distribution will also target educational facilities. As mentioned above, AYUUB will ensure that all women, men, and children have access to information and training on the safe use of hygiene items that are unfamiliar to themActivity 1.1.4: CFW for solid waste management (garbage collection& general clean up campaigns). 500HHs will be paid a total of 40 USD ($2 per day for five days in a week for 4 weeks- 4hrs a day for 5 working days- total hours 20hrs a week ) per month for three months. Activity 1.1.5: Conduct On-site monitoring to ensure the distribution of hygiene kits are made as per the plan. Two weeks after the distribution of hygiene kits, AYUUB will carry out post-distribution monitoring to assess the use of and beneficiary satisfaction with distributed hygiene items. Activity 2.1.1: Provide emergency water trucking to 2,333 HHs in Marka and Wanalweyn district on a monthly basis through water vouchers.Activity 3.1.1: Rehabilitate one Borehole . Parts that need rehabilitation are solar, construction of elevated tank, piping and water kiosk. AYUUB will also rehabilitate 5 unprotected shallow wells in locations affected by the floods that require dewatering, disinfection and protection. Activity 3.1.2: Conduct water chlorination of shallow wells in Marka and Wanalweyn districts. All wells receiving chlorination will be tested for one week, a few times a day, to confirm the level of chlorination is effective to provide safe drinking water. In addition, Household water treatment with tablet (aquatabs, watermaker), Bucket chlorination at the water point (the point of collection), will also be conducted and facilitated by AYUUB. AYUUB will conduct proper sensitization/awareness before commencing these initiatives.
