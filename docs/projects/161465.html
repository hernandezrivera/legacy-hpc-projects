Project Summary

Food Agriculture and Disaster Management (FADM) is a national NGO working in Western Bahr El Ghazel in the counties of Jur River, Wau and Raja and is currently implementing WASH, Nutrition and livelihood programs funded by UNDP and the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund.
WASH needs persists in the locations targeted, with the majority of those affected females from especially female headed HHs. Food Consumption Score and HH hunger indices indicate a dire need for humanitarian support to avert a slide of the population into worse food insecurity levels and malnutrition often connected to WASH.
Over 50% of the populations in these areas are projected to be under IPC phase classification of 3 and above in the first quarter of 2020.
The main drivers of WASH related needs have been identified as protection and safety issues in accessing services, lack of inputs, erratic weather patterns culminating into floods amongst others.
FADM plans a 12 month response targeting 20,000 people with WASH in Health, WASH in Nutrition and WASH in GBV support inline with cluster frameworks and plans, with a focus on reaching women, the elderly and people with disability.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition continues to be a challenge in the locations of Wau, Jur River and Raga; the level of acute malnutrition are attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation and hygiene services. 95,590 (35%) 31,997 (55%) and 173,221 (55%) persons are classified under IPC 3 and above in the counties of Jur River, Raja and Wau respectively per IPC projections for the first quarter of 2020. Multiply displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecure households (over 53%) than host communities (35%). Female-headed households also have higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male-headed households. At the household level, access to safe water and sanitation facilities is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to torrential rains that have resulted into floods in parts of Western Bahr El Ghazel affecting HHs in Wau and Raja mainly. (OCHA, 2020). Latrine coverage remains low at less than 40%; more than half of the population practicing open defecation. Loss of livelihoods, economic challenges, lack of WASH essentials has all contributed to the dire WASH situation in Jur River, Wau and Raja. Annual Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 88.5 percent between June 2018 and June 2019; the increase was mainly driven by high prices in non-food items Households in both urban and rural areas in Wau and its environs remain unable to afford the minimum basic needs. Disease outbreaks such as AWDs have had significant effects of livelihoods, increasing cases of malnutrition amongst under 5s and PLWs. WASH access for PWDs and the elderly is limited, with majority of the WASH infrastructure not convenient for access by people with special needs and the elderly. Protection needs are also directly related to WASH issues.
