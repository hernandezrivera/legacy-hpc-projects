Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the autonomy of food security of displaced populations in the districts of Chiure and Mecufi. The intervention aims to strengthen both agricultural production (horticulture) and fishing activities, essential for the subsistence of families, providing them with the necessary means to guarantee sufficient food for consumption and surplus for commercialization. The project, in addition to providing inputs such as improved seeds that are resilient to drought and disease, tools such as hoes, machetes, watering cans, ropes and small drip irrigation systems, adapted to the context, fishing equipment (nets, hooks, coolers, small boats motor, etc.) also includes the training component in good agricultural and fishing practices, as well as the creation of fishing associations, and the creation of direct links between producers, fishermen and the local market, encouraging the commercialization of surpluses and the development of an agricultural and fishing value chain. The main objective of the project is to contribute to the food autonomy of displaced people by strengthening their agricultural and fishing production capabilities, increasing their food production and connectivity with local markets to generate income. With the project, it is expected to directly benefit 4390 displaced people in the districts of Chiure and Mecufi.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

A0: Identification of land for agricultural productionIn this activity, APDS will mobilize potential beneficiaries to identify land for agricultural production, in their backyards or on their farms, as long as they have water nearby to facilitate vegetable production. In this activity, APDS expects to benefit 3,890 people.A1: Agricultural production field preparationIn this activity, beneficiaries will be mobilized to prepare the land, creating flower beds in their backyards, but also creating an animal fence. Beneficiaries who have a field in a low-lying area will only prepare the production fields, as they do not need to fence their fields, as they are outside the communities, out of reach of animals such as chickens and goats. In this activity, APDS expects to benefit 3,890 people.A2: Purchase and distribution of agricultural and fishing inputsAPDS will seek better suppliers of agricultural and fishing inputs at market level. After identifying these suppliers through our procurement process, APDS will acquire the necessary inputs and then distribute them to beneficiary families. In this activity, as a way to reduce the risk of misuse of inputs by beneficiaries, we will deliver inputs in stages. In this activity, APDS expects to benefit 3,390 people with agricultural inputs and 500 with fishing inputs.For agricultural production we will have 6 phases, divided sequentially as follows:Phase I: beneficiaries will have to show us the land for production.Phase II – will consist of the delivery of some materials such as (hoe, machete, nest, thread, etc.).Phase III – families benefiting from Phases I and II will prepare the production fields and fence them according to the state of the field.Phase IV – families will receive other inputs such as seeds, manual watering cans and materials for small drip irrigation systems.V – families will do the planting.Phase VI - families organized into a group of 10 will be provided with sprayers to spray their crops if necessary.For Fishing – we will have 4 phases divided as follows:Phase I – Selection of displaced fishermen who have been in the area for at least 3 years and who have moved from a coastal district.Phase II – Formation of associations and training in first aid, prohibition period, prohibited species and registration of associations with community fishing committees (CCP).Phase III – delivery of materials (nets, hooks, life jackets, vessels with thermal boxes).Phase IV – Training in materials management and fish conservation.A3: Training in Good Agricultural Practices and marketing of surplusesIn this activity, training sessions will be held on sustainable agricultural practices, soil management, pest control and irrigation techniques for better harvests. To facilitate the training process, beneficiaries will be divided into smaller groups according to where they live, where they will receive training from our agricultural activists. The training will be carried out in a staggered and rotating manner in the areas of each beneficiary, explaining in a practical way all the steps of what must be done.The groups to be created and trained will be the same as those created to receive sprayers, with at least one watering can for each group. It will also be the same groups that will come together to, in an organized way, collect their (surplus) products in order to reach marketable quantities and channel these quantities to local markets. In this activity, APDS expects to benefit 3,890 people.A4: Carrying out fishing and marketingAfter providing all fishing supplies and training in the fishing activities described in activity A2. Groups of fishermen will fish and at the same time sell fish with marketable qualities.APDS will support beneficiaries in establishing direct links with markets (fishermen and buyers), mainly fish retailers. In this activity, APDS expects to benefit 1,390 people.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

MITIGATION OF GENDER-BASED VIOLENCE RISKS AND PROTECTIONAlthough APDS does not implement stand-alone protection projects, APDS remains committed to integrating protection into its operations and programming. APDS is therefore working to improve its tools and staff capacities in this regard. Hence the reason why each group will be consulted to mitigate any risk of gender-based violence.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

At this point, it is important to highlight that the principle of APDS is non-discrimination, regardless of color, race, gender, age and nationality. Based on this principle, APDS will encourage the participation of all beneficiaries expected to benefit from the project (women, children, elderly). Dialogue mechanisms will be created through the establishment of focus groups to allow all beneficiaries to participate and contribute to the identification and redefinition of priority needs.In this project, we will seek to ensure that children and elderly people without physical capabilities to actively participate in activities are represented by members of their families, thus allowing them to benefit just like other beneficiaries with capabilities. Because they are more vulnerable, they will be prioritized.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This project proposal, focused on the two sectors Agriculture and Fisheries, is committed to ensuring that all people, regardless of their physical or cognitive conditions, have equal access to the benefits of this project. APDS will adopt an inclusive approach, starting with mapping and identifying these people. To this end, it will involve local leaders and those responsible for displaced people, forming discussion groups to identify people with disabilities and understand their specific needs and study how to incorporate them into project activities, which will initially involve considering members of their families to provide them with support. representation, if the disability limits it, but in cases where the person is able to work, preparing fishing nets for example, or other activities, they will be considered to represent their families if that person is the head of the family. What we are going to guarantee in this project is the prioritization of these people so that they do not feel marginalized. Therefore, tools such as inclusive assessment forms will be developed to recognize all forms of disability — physical, sensory, intellectual or psychosocial.After this phase, the project will seek to raise awareness in the community through campaigns that address the rights of people with disabilities and the importance of their inclusion in project actions, whether development or emergency. Training will be carried out for volunteers, staff and partners, with the aim of eliminating stigmas and prejudices that may limit these people's access to the benefits of this project and others.To ensure equitable access, a monitoring system will be established to ensure that people with disabilities receive the support they need. Focal points within the team will be designated to continually monitor the inclusion of these groups, addressing potential gaps in services provided. Finally, the project will include an ongoing monitoring and evaluation process, collecting direct feedback from beneficiaries to adjust approaches, ensuring no one is left behind.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

As part of its Food Security strategy for the period 2020-2025, APDS is committed to taking into account the growing environmental challenges humanity currently faces, such as the consequences of climate change, rapid population growth and health risks. growing numbers linked to the environment and urbanization. Thus, APDS is striving to integrate, within its support programs and its governance, actions in the areas of Disaster Risk Reduction, to strengthen the adaptation of populations to climate change. This is done, for example, by analyzing the environmental impact of its projects and implementing solar panels to supply electricity wherever possible. Specifically for this project, APDS will raise awareness and discourage the community from carrying out uncontrolled fires to prepare agricultural fields, using research techniques that are harmful to the environment such as trawling, not only depositing garbage in oceans, cutting down mangroves, among other actions.
