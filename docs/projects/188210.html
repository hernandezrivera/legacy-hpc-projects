Project Summary

WV and IP will offer a multifaced project in continuation of the existing and expand in Idleb and Aleppo supporting the most vulnerable as requested by authorities and informed by needs, including CWD from HC and IDP camps between the ages of 5-17, by providing equitable access to quality and protective learning prospects to girls and boys in both formal education-Curriculum B-and in nonformal education through Accelerated Learning Programmes, remedial, catch-up, literacy and numeracy classes and Self Learning Programme. 7 areas with the highest severity rate (5) as per HNO 2022 will be targeted: Maaret Tamsrin, Dana, Bennsh, Armanaz, Salqin, Harim, Atareb, covering 37 (27 new locations). In IDP camps, nonformal education provided to 1540 females and 1470 males including 27 CwDs. In 15 formal schools, we’ll support 3428 female and 3286 male students including 20 CwDs. 355 teachers, 6084 male & 4555 female students will benefit from SPHERE alliant and CWD-considerate rehab and construction including heating fuel in 22 schools that were identified through a comprehensive WV needs assessment.
We’ll continue to work with and recruit local communities to ensure apt education services are due at a level that meets the cluster and authorities’ standards, which include local outreach, quality and inclusive education in formal and non-formal settings with lifelong learning chances with special focus on CwD. 8300 of parents (F 5300 M 3000) targeted in awareness sessions on the importance of education and CP. In compliance with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles, achieving improved learning outcomes through conducting BTL campaigns to encourage children to return and be retained in education while parents prioritise education. The sessions will be complemented in distributing learning supplies. We’ll also provide training to teachers and facilitators on F2F and distant ed, programmatic support throughout the project cycle. OOSC will be bridged back to formal education based on the term’s scores. Holistically, structured and semi-structured PSS sessions using WV’s models and expertise will be offered by training education staff (TICC- PSS- SEL- EiE- CSG) in the established/rehabilitated CFSs at formal and non-formal settings in all of the targeted locations, to enhance the resilience, coping mechanism and life skills of children, in addition to mine risk awareness sessions, hygiene promotion education, case identification and referrals for most vulnerable cases in need of services through the protection team and external pathways.
Ensuring the continuity of learning, adapting plan B for distance learning due to COVID or escalation in the security situation, where we’ll be contingent on sufficient pre-existing equipment (independent contribution) from pervious education project (tablets,cameras,laptops,internet equipment,teaching tools-valued at $80000). The project will provide heating materials (gas heaters in tents, fuel and heaters for block building schools). All projects will provide drinking and washing water, support to operational costs, cleaning materials, sanitisers and solar power systems as an electricity source in the rehabilitation works. Security equipment for education settings (first aid kits and fire extinguishers) given after training staff on first aid and emergency preparedness.
PTAs established to consult, inform of updates along with teachers support circles as experience sharing and care. Project will offer incentives to teachers, facilitators, guards, cleaners and admin staff as an important factor in securing stable education process and retention of teachers while securing jobs and income for about 348 families. WV and IP will facilitate experience exchange workshops with Aleppo and Idleb Education Directorate education personnel (30 Aleppo/70 Idleb) to improve the level of coordination and help build cooperation and partnership with the local councils and camp management.

4 - Needs

Results from 2022 HNO confirm the great need for WV’s interventions in NWS, where protracted crises continue to overstretch the education services, with 25 confirmed attacks on schools and 7 verified instances of military use of schools in 9 months. Existing services are unable to physically accommodate or meet the different learning needs of adolescents, IDPs, returnees and children with specific needs (2021 MSNA). With an estimated 18% of school age children not attending any form of learning, out of school children remain one of the most affected groups. Over 3/4 of 12-17year-olds who were not in school had dropped out. 20% of schools in NWS are destroyed, the largest proportion of non-functional schools were in Idleb constituting 13% of all schools assessed (Schools-in-Syria_IMU-2021). Children will bear the greatest responsibility for reconstruction and growth when the conflict ceases. Early 2021, WV spoke to children in NWS who have expressed concerns about their day-to-day lives and those of their parents. The reality they described to us is an unquantifiable loss of learning, safety and psychosocial well-being. Many Syrian children and young people told us they just did not feel equipped to contribute to the recovery of the country, even if the war would stop today. Many Syrian adolescents and young people told us that the 10-year mark of the conflict makes them feel that it is now impossible for them to achieve their hopes and dreams and support their communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be executed by WV directly and through an IP, where WV go through a screening, evaluation and selection process to select the IP. WV will work hand in hand with its IP throughout the project cycle beginning with the pre-design consultations, project design, building their capacity in policies and procedures to be followed, financial management, reporting and grant management etc. Being involved in all aspects of the project will build the competence of the IP in programming, monitoring, evaluation and future planning to enable them to be more self-reliant to lead education programming. For example, the BTL campaigns will be conducted by the IP and WV through F2F and virtual awareness messages, provision of learning supplies (school bags, stationery, textbooks, replenishment stationery each three months), in addition to administering teaching tools, kits, teaching resources and guides for teachers. Facilitators will receive recreational kits for conducting PSS activities at formal and non-formal settings. WV’s education field coordinator and the IP will coordinate with the education directorates of Idleb and Aleppo to facilitate transferring children to formal education on a termly basis. WV & IP will facilitate experience exchange workshops with Aleppo and Idleb Education Directorate staff in order to improve the level of coordination between the Education Directorate and local humanitarian actors while strengthening the IP’s position with the directorates.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked in the ongoing unstable armed conflict in NWS. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. Additionally, some facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19 and other communicable diseases. Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages. Unsafe learning spaces and insufficient/non-existent WASH facilities contribute to children not attending learning facilities. Learning facilities unable to put into place and maintain Covid-19 safety measures.At the wider institutional level, members of the community or local authorities may try to influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of provided support and the project may face pressure on selection, hiring or procurement processes. Delay or non-approvals of planned activities or planned processes. Negative attitude towards curriculum and/or the method of teaching. Negative attitude towards professional development of teachers and education personnel and/or the method of training.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Our absolute priority is protecting children, vulnerable communities and our own staff. Zero tolerance of incidents of violence or abuse against children and other people in the communities we serve, either committed by our staff or others connected to our work. WV abhors any misuse of power, status, or trusted position for any exploitative purposes. Continuous improvement of safeguarding efforts, which emphasises PSEA and other forms of violence or harm, is at the top of our agenda. We have mechanisms to enable communities, beneficiaries, and staff to report potential cases of abuse. Frontline staff are trained on how to recognise and respond to issues of SEA. We provide a confidential Whistleblower hotline. We continually monitor and update our policies to be accountable and improved. We believe we have a moral obligation to maintain the highest ethical standards in our work. The Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy expand upon WV’s standards, to cover all vulnerable people in one integrated policy. Community consultations used in the design of the project ensured adequate representation of WGBM. Female staff and enumerators are ensured for facilitation. We ensure local IPs follow measures and maintain gender representation amongst staff. Activities have been developed sensitively & consider Do No Harm (DNH) principles. To ensure a DNH Approach, protection is mainstreamed in all response areas & all staff have been trained in protection mainstreaming and the WV policies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WV and IP will remain up-to-date with the authorities’ COVID-19 rules and the project will have clear Covid-19 safety protocols based on the local and global procedures, including identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the school and the relevant health centre. These protocols will be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Hand washing stations in place, hand-sanitiser and face masks/shields, etc will be distributed. Students, school personnel and caregivers will benefit from awareness messaging of protocols and practices.The community will be consulted at all stages of the project and the design will be context and conflict sensitive. A situation and needs analyses will be done regularly to stay updated. The curricula used will be the one relevant to the context. The project will be inclusive of all children in need of the services, and where applicable, a clear exclusion criterion will be communicated. The project will have a clear entrance and exit strategy communicated to the community and local authorities. WV will inform donors and the Cluster if donor conditions contribute to tensions between WV, the IP and the community or education authorities, for advocacy purposes. WV has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify our and the IP’s responsibilities and obligations to the staff, volunteers and associated personnel such as facilitators, teachers and other education personnel.
