Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the elimination of cholera in Haiti, supporting the Ministry of Health’s long-term national elimination plan (2019-2022), through the detection, confirmation and investigation of cholera cases; and the community care through interventions to cut transmission.
The project targets 333,253 people (159,964 female and 173,289 male) to be reached by rapid response teams and benefiting from the cordon sanitaire, i.e. in average 15 houses around the affected household.

Needs

Since October 2010, Haiti has been battling an unprecedented and severe cholera epidemic, with over 819,000 suspected cases and 9,789 deaths (source: MSPP/DELR, epidemiological bulletin, week 47, 2018). However, since the initial outbreak, significant progress has been made in the fight against cholera: the weekly caseload has been reduced by 99,4%, down from over 14,900 cases per week to its current average of approximately 85. Deaths have also decreased by over 99.5%.UNICEF is one of the main partners of the Ministry of Health (MSPP) and DINEPA (Water and Sanitation Directorate) under the National Plan for the Elimination of Cholera (2013-2022), and contributes to the Plan through the provision of technical and financial support to the emergency alert and response component (via the MSPP rapid mobile teams and NGO partners). UNICEF’s role in supporting the rapid alert / response system is subdivided into four main areas: support to national and departmental response system coordination; support to the surveillance mechanism; rapid WASH response and prevention actions in communities; and permanent epidemiological analysis. Despite this progress, the fight against cholera in Haiti is not over. Cholera transmission continues, while the risk of sudden localized outbreaks at anytime, anywhere in the country remains high. The volatility of cholera should not be underestimated, as recently demonstrated by the outbreak in the border area between Haiti and the Dominican Republic. The cholera epidemy is still concentrated in Ouest, Centre and Artibonite. These three departments make up 71% of cases for about 60% of the population (IHSI) during the first semester of 2018.In line with the Haitian Government’s Medium and Long-term Plans for Cholera Elimination, the proposed project will support greater responsibility and autonomy of the national EMIRA teams (MSPP rapid response teams) and the creation of three mixed DINEPA-NGO teams dedicated to water quality control and water chlorination in the lasts affected areas.

Activities or outputs

1. Strengthening coordination with communities and local authoritiesUNICEF will continue to support the MSPP cholera coordination at national and departmental level. To reduce the risk of a spike during the rainy season (Jul - Nov), MSPP, UNICEF and NGO partners launched in July 2018 a massive community response and communications campaign in cholera persistent zones, especially in the Ouest department (‘operation coup de poing’). The results obtained three months after the launching of the operation are impressive with the lowest level of cases ever reached before in the most populated department of the country. With this project, UNICEF plans to expand this approach with the same intensity in the other cholera most affected departments.Mayors of selected communes will help identifying local partners and priority places (essentially markets places and bus stations) and the project will support the deployment and training on cholera transmission prevention of municipal agents in those gathering places. 2. Strengthening surveillance system and laboratoryUNICEF will continue its support to enhance the capacity of surveillance and decision-making of the National Technical Committee Against Cholera (MSPP and DINEPA). At the departmental level, two UNICEF officers will remain in support of the health department Directorates in the most affected departments to assist in ensuring the quality of the surveillance system and alerts responses by partners.At the community level, UNICEF intends to work with Health Department Directorates to identify and train 300 community-health agents who will be able to play a role of active surveillance of acute watery diarrhea and provide a first response. In addition, Haiti counts on a large network of community volunteers and committed local leaders. Furthermore, UNICEF plans to strengthen national laboratory facilities to allow for faster distinction between cholera cases and acute diarrhea, in order to improve real-time analysis and enable MSPP to determine when cholera cases are reduced to zero. 3. Sustaining rapid response to cholera suspected cases in communitiesThe huge reduction in suspected cases in 2018 compared to 2017 (approx. 70%), is the result of improved surveillance, coordination and rapid response at community level.Currently, 13 rapid responses teams of the MSPP (EMIRA) are supported by 60 UNICEF partners’ NGO teams providing rapid response. This mechanism must be maintained throughout 2019 to ensure the maximum effectiveness in controlling and reducing the transmission. 4. Supporting WASH interventions as prevention at community levelTo complement the rapid response and hygiene awareness activities, UNICEF has set up in coordination with DINEPA and the partner NGOs, Emergency WASH Mobile teams aimed at intervening on water systems in persisting outbreaks areas. The WASH mobile teams ensure systematic chlorination of all water networks at the source, monitor water point quality and promote the use of household water treatment among water points users. They also ensure water chlorination in densely populated areas located near affected communities to prevent a further extension of the transmission. Finally, the WASH mobile teams implement emergency water points and handwashing points in schools and health centres within affected communities. 5. Strengthening information and awareness raising on preventive hygiene behaviorsIn addition to the rapid response, UNICEF has increased the scope of action of NGO teams by mobilizing them for stronger community engagement & hygiene awareness. Indeed, as soon as 90% of the suspected cases are reached with a response, part of the teams return back to the neighbourhood in order to ensure follow-up, intensify sensitization, organize public sessions, as well as reinforce sensitization in markets, schools, churches, bus stations and other gathering places.
