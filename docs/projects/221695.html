Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at responding to the humanitarian needs of conflict-affected populations in Cabo Delgado (CD) province through emergency response and early recovery interventions aiming at building the resilience of Internal displaced peoples (IDPs), returnees and Host Communities (HC) toward improved access to water, sanitation and Hygiene services. Aiming at saving-lives and alleviating the suffering of those most-in-need, while promoting the dignity, health, wellbeing and empowerment of communities particularly women and children. This will be achieved by restoring access to essential Water Sanitation and Hygiene services, promoting health and environmental considerations. Activities include providing access to potable water (quick maintenance and rehabilitation of water points), sanitation facilities (construction/rehabilitation of communal and emergency latrine and bathing chambers), and hygiene NFI supplies to enhance dignity, improve public health, reduce the prevalence of waterborne diseases, and enhance the overall well-being of affected communities. Those activities are essential to reduce the prevalence of stunting, wasting, and other malnutrition-related issues for vulnerable groups, particularly children under five (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), who are at high risk of malnutrition during emergencies. The action seeks to enhance information management, providing critical insights into key humanitarian needs. This will foster a more coherent, harmonized and effective response by humanitarian actors. By addressing coordination challenges and mitigating fragmentation in humanitarian efforts, the action aims to improve the overall response to the crisis.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The intervention prioritizes improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services for vulnerable groups, with a specific focus on children under five (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), who are at heightened risk of waterborne diseases, malnutrition, and stunting. The project targets internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host community members to address critical needs and ensure equitable access to essential services. In collaboration with local partners, government authorities, and the WASH cluster, the project aligns with national strategies to ensure coordination and avoid duplication of efforts. Priorities include rehabilitating water points, constructing child- and women-friendly sanitation facilities, and distributing hygiene kits tailored to the needs of CU5 and PLW to promote dignity and health. By integrating community participation, the project fosters ownership and sustainability, while enhancing the resilience of affected populations. This approach ensures equitable access to lifesaving WASH services, mitigates health risks, and supports social cohesion among IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Cabo Delgado.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Building on ACF's extensive experience and knowledge of the humanitarian context in Cabo Delgado, activities will be prioritized in the districts of Macomia, Meluco, Metuge, Mueda, Muidumbe, Nangade, and Quissanga to improve access to essential, life-saving WASH services. All interventions will be preceded by thorough assessments to ensure context-specific and needs-based responses. Key activities include the flexible maintenance and rehabilitation of water sources, primarily those equipped with hand pumps and small water systems that can be repaired in quick manner. These efforts will be complemented by training for water sanitation and hygiene management committees to ensure the sustainability of the facilities. Additionally, communal latrines will be constructed or rehabilitated to enhance access to safe sanitation. Given the limited availability of markets offering essential hygiene products, such as handwashing soap, water storage buckets, and water treatment items, the project will prioritize either voucher assistance or in-kind distributions based on market assessments and prevailing conditions. Where markets are functional and accessible, voucher assistance will empower communities to procure hygiene items locally, supporting the local economy. In areas where markets are scarce or unreliable, hygiene and dignity kits will be provided in-kind to ensure immediate access to life-saving supplies. To promote the correct use of distributed items and encourage hygiene best practices, local hygiene promoters will be identified and trained, with active involvement from community members. This approach fosters community ownership and contributes to long-term impact and sustainability.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project identifies potential risks through a comprehensive risk assessment process that considers environmental, social, financial, and operational factors. This assessment includes consulting with local communities, stakeholders, and experts to understand potential impacts and vulnerabilities specific to the project location. Key risk areas, such as environmental degradation, community disruption, or resource overuse, are analyzed to determine the likelihood and potential severity of each risk. To avoid causing harm, the project adopts a "do no harm" approach, integrating risk mitigation measures throughout the project lifecycle. These measures include: Community Engagement and Transparency, Environmental Safeguards, and establishment of Feedback and complaint mechanism, Monitoring and Adaptation and Capacity Building.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The WASH intervention project ensures meaningful access to water, sanitation, and hygiene assistance for women and elderly people by identifying specific barriers, such as mobility issues and safety concerns, and designing services to be accessible and safe, with community engagement at its core. Women and elderly people are involved in planning and decision-making, with tailored approaches like convenient, safe water points and accessible sanitation facilities. Capacity-building programs on hygiene practices and water management specifically support women’s roles in community health, while elderly individuals receive targeted support for their needs. Social support networks are formed, allowing these groups to share experiences and provide mutual assistance. Continuous monitoring and feedback loops enable adaptive improvements, ensuring these vulnerable groups are empowered and fully benefit from the WASH initiatives.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify people with disabilities by conducting an initial needs assessment that includes household surveys, community consultations, and collaboration with local stakeholders knowledgeable about disability issues. This will help map individuals’ locations, needs, and specific challenges related to accessing WASH facilities. To remove barriers, the project will incorporate universal design principles in the provision of sanitation facilities, such as ramps, handrails, and non-slip surfaces. Accessibility training will be provided to staff to ensure sensitive and effective service delivery, while information will be disseminated in accessible formats (e.g., visual aids depending on needs). Regular feedback loops, such as community meetings and surveys, will allow persons with disabilities to voice any challenges they face, enabling the project to make adaptive changes to maintain inclusive and equitable access to all WASH services.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project considers its environmental impact carefully and plans to minimize potential harm through a series of mitigation measures. An initial environmental assessment will be conducted to evaluate potential impacts, particularly regarding water source sustainability, waste management, and local ecosystem disturbance. Mitigation measures include sourcing water responsibly to avoid depletion, implementing eco-friendly sanitation solutions, and ensuring proper waste disposal to prevent contamination.
