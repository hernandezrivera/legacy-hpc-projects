Project Summary

In 2023 PLAN International will focus on implementing integrated CP program in camp 17,20E, 21,24,25 and host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf for prevention, mitigation and response to Abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation to Children in Rohingya camp and host communities of Cox’s Bazar. The proposed project will include Awareness raising, CP Systems strengthening and Capacity building, Case management, Community-based child protection, Structured and unstructured PSS and Support activities for adolescents through life skill education. These interventions will ensure inclusion of Children/persons with disability, other children with specific needs. Special attention will be paid to child survivors of sexual abuse and GBV, early/forced/child marriage, child labor, trafficking as well as unaccompanied and separated children.
The project will reach 46350 people (Female-23186, Male-23164), It will closely involve the children and their caregivers from both Rohingya and host communities, they will be consulted throughout the project cycle from designing to Evaluation stage. The parents and caregivers will be receiving positive parenting sessions for strengthening their parenting skill through this project for addressing harmful practices and attitudes to children.
Project will establish/refurbish structure for providing CP services to the children and their caregivers including community members.
CBCP activities will be structured through feedback from community consultation and participation to ensure inclusion and ownership by both the host and the refugee community. Community-level interventions will include promoting social cohesion and strengthening self-protection of children. Workshop with duty bearers (government officials and local authorities), community leaders, parents, teachers and religious leaders within both community groups on developing a basic understanding of everyday peace, social cohesion, social trust and public accountability will also take place. PLAN will ensure that child friendly feedback mechanisms are in place with strong emphasis on PSEA and Child Safeguarding.
For strengthening the CP system, this project will coordinate with other sectors and government officials to enrich their capacity to protect and deliver quality services in a child friendly way, train the volunteers and other local and INGO CP staff. This proposed project will also train non-CP actors on age- and gender-sensitive child protection principles, approaches, child safeguarding PSEA. ECCD intervention will also be carried out in Teknaf area with the support from Education sector of PLAN International.
The project will offer gender responsive comprehensive case management services including referral, integrated Menstrual Hygiene management (MHM) services, MHPSS that covers structured, on-structured PSS and specialized services. LGBTI will be kept in special consideration.
Plan will establish adolescent groups / clubs (disaggregated by age, gender and disability). It will consider 10% person with disability in targeting beneficiaries.
PIan International is committed to coordinating the response through active participation in sectors such as Protection, Education, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sector and other working groups like Strategic Working Group (SAG), CMTWG to ensure meaningful access, prioritization of safety and integration of services. The services offered will be in line with CPMS, interagency tools and SOPs, CPSS strategy and will be ensured through structured training enhancing competencies in providing quality gender-responsive and timely services. This project will also include a contingency plan for natural disaster and will do best for keeping the environmental impact of the project will be minimal.
As the project will be implemented through national NGO, Plan will undertake various capacity strengthening initiatives for PNGO and community volunteer as well at every stage of the project.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Entering the 6th year of the Rohingya humanitarian situation in Bangladesh the needs remains dreadful. Technical Report of the Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5, June 2022) in the Rohingya refugee camps and the surrounding host communities reveals that overall vulnerability levels have remained alarmingly high since 2019 among Rohingya households. The latest findings showed that 95 percent of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. REVA also indicates that despite the current level of humanitarian assistance, 51 percent of Rohingya households cannot afford the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) that creates protection concern for the children and adolescent as the household often try to cope with situation by following negative coping mechanism like child marriage, child labour, prostitution etc. Community level child protection, Community System strengthening, justice for children, Case management, working across sector are the 5 priority areas identified in CP strategy ‘21 to guide it in coming years. As per CPIMS+ from Jan’22-June’22, it shows that case trend has increased in June compared to January , rate of cases for male (boy 54%) is higher than female (girl-46%). However, child marriage incidents for girls is much higher than boy and unfortunately the trend is increasing. Alternatively, child labour incidents for boy (93%) are higher than girls (7%). Interestingly, the trend is decreasing. Mostly, neglect is the key Child protection concern so far observe in CPIMS+. Other significant CP concerns are Child labour, physical abuse, separated from families .According to Recent findings conducted under Joint initiative of PLAN and UNHCR (February 2022), the most important issue that adolescent faces in the camp is safety. They face eve-teasing. Parents want to marry their girls as soon as they reach puberty as they think this will mitigate the safety risks. Some religious leaders promote early marriage as an obligation and a way to preserve girls’ sexual purity. Some religious leaders try to raise awareness of the harmful impacts of child marriage and promote waiting. However, parents are so concerned about girls’ safety and reputations that they marry them young no matter the harmful impacts. This kind of child marriage trend creates further protection concern like child pregnancy, intemate partner violence (physical and emotional) and over responsibility of a family that is relatively link with Child labour. Adolescent girls in the refugee camps face increased restrictions on their freedom of movement, making it harder for them to access services, including education. The greater restriction on girls is linked with parents’ fears around girls being sexually assaulted, which would “spoil their honour” and bring shame on the family. According to children consultation (September 2022) conducted by Plan International and Save the Children, Child protection situation across the camp communities is quite poor, as children and adolescents frequently mentioned living in constant fear of harassment, abuse, and violence inflicted by both community members and law enforcement agencies. Children living in extended communities, on the other hand feel vulnerable mostly due to natural disasters and its consequences. The consultation also indicates that Adolescent girls living in the Rohingya camps are among the most vulnerable as they have highly restricted mobility affecting their everyday life and wellbeing. They are also victims to eve-teasing and child marriage, unlike their male counterparts.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project incorporates protection principles into all services at every stage and ensure interventions targeting the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity and promote and protect the rights of people, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. The project will strongly follow below PRINCIPLES: 1. Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: Strongly prevent and minimize any adverse effects of its intervention. 2. Meaningful Access: ensure access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without discrimination, pay special attention to individuals who have difficulty accessing assistance. 3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can be involved in all aspects of project cycle and establish measures for affected populations to measure the appropriateness of interventions and organizations can address concern and complaints.

3.2 - Gender

The project will use gender lens throughout the project cycle from designing to evaluation stage. It will analyze and address the different needs, barriers, capacities, opportunities and aspirations of women, men, boys and girls, especially from vulnerable populations without creating further stress, danger or exposing them to undignified solutions Gender segregated indicators will be followed for monitoring, gender and age desegregated data will be collected, analyzed and used.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Plan International Bangladesh has a Safeguarding policy for children and young people and is committed to the 6 IASC principles related to PSHEA. All staff and volunteers will sign the associated Code of Conduct, Whistleblowing and Bullying and harassment policy. A do no harm assessment will be conducted for each sector of intervention, including assessment of solar fan installation as well as safe access to taps to ensure no overcrowding or risk to GBV. WASH standards for the tap installation will be followed strictly.

3.4 - Diversity

Plan always address and design project covering diversity in terms of disability, gender, ethnicity and religion. The project will cover 5% person with disability of total reach. We will try to ensure 50% male to female ratio. PIB will ensure that communication is inclusive and transmitted through multiple media options, including radio, visual guides, and community mobilization (respecting social distance recommendations), as well as in a diversity of languages like Rohingya, and accessible formats when using technologies. Through participation, community engagements and consultation of most affected groups, including women and youth groups, adolescent girls and boys groups, female community health workers, and women from indigenous and most vulnerable groups, surveillance-mechanism will be developed and implemented. Project will also promote safe spaces for all diversified groups to take part into the consultation, mass awareness programs etc.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

PLAN has developed an easy, regular, on-time and accessible information sharing mechanism as a part of AAP. It includes how the basic requirement of meaningful and ethical participation would be followed, what information will be shared at which stage and how children and the community can raise their complaints, feedback and suggestion on project implementation. Children friendly complaint and feedback mechanism will be established at the beginning of the project that includes three channels of data flow that are a hotline number, opinion box and community reflection session with children, youth, parents, and caregivers and other community members. PIB recognizes that CFM plays a key role in strengthening reporting mechanisms for PSEA and in safeguarding children and young people. Feedback received from different channels contributes in program adaptations in many ways including changing implementation strategies. At every stage beneficiaries will be encouraged to raise their issues.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Plan International and its partner will follow Protection Sector Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan that will include(i) Preparedness Activities (ii) Response activation, coordination and management and (iii) Response Activities. Plan International will support PERU and CPFP by assigning member, exchanging information and involving any assessment required for pre/during/post emergency. Plan and its partner will ensure dissemination of awareness message on disaster, developing, distributing and orientation on IEC materials as preparedness and mitigation strategy at community level. This proposed project will follow a contingency plan for natural disaster and will do best for keeping the environmental impact of the project minimal. Project will facilitate awareness message on preparedness of natural disaster, will respond to survivor of natural disaster. Budget allocation for natural disaster is ensured for this project.
