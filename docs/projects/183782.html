Project Summary

Through WFP’s decentralized evaluation, interviewees reported on prevalent poor nutritional habits amongst school-aged children including coming to school without breakfast or food, as well as the consumption of food with low nutritional value (high sugar, high salt).
Based on the success and learns learnt from the past three years of the Emergency School Feeding Programmes in Libya in 2019 [HLBY19‐EDU‐151894‐1], 2020 [HLBY20-EDU-162711- 2] and 2021 [HLBY21-EDU-172814-1] as well as the Emergency School Feeding Programme under the Education Cannot-Wait fund in 2021, where more than 20,000 School-aged children including some 1,000 migrants students have been reached with whether on-site school meals or alternative Take-Home Rations when schools closed because of the COVID-19 pandemic. WFP will re-activate the complementary school meals programme as means to ensure children’s access to food in times of crisis and enhance diets through increased micronutrient intake. Assistance will take into account protection and safety, COVID-19 precautions, and where possible, contribute to their broader protection and community awareness.
In 2022, WFP plans to reach some 40,000 school-aged children in need of the Education Sector's assistance, out of which 20,000 school-aged children will be prioritized for humanitarian-oriented assistance under the Humanitarian Response Plan for January 2022 - May 2022. This programme could also support and strengthen the current formal education system and advocate towards a safe back to school, in order to allow a greater and at scale-reach of those already in school, girls and boys out of school or at risk of dropping out and those who are most vulnerable in the society, minority groups including (but not excluded to) girls. It will also help promote and encourage the enrolment of the children of the migrant populations in urban settings. The programme would review implementation arrangements to assure COVID-19 precautionary measures, strengthen synergies with education and livelihood sectors and increase nutrition sensitivity, gender sensitivity, local procurement and community ownership. This would promote community infrastructure, rehabilitation, and reconstruction ﴾including schools and health centres﴿ as well as promote the reintegration of displaced populations and returnees, with special attention paid to the different needs of women and men.
While implementing the Emergency School Feeding under the HRP 2022, WFP will invest its resources in the capacity strengthening to enable national and local authorities to respond, and foster schools’ resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses stemming from existing and future crises through strengthened coordination, evidence-based needs analyses, and strategic preparedness. In October 2021, with the support from WFP Regional Bureau as well as UNICEF and UNDP, WFP and the Ministry of Education organized the National School Feeding Workshop in Tripoli with the participation of some 55 participants. WFP will also work with the Ministry of Education in conducting awareness-raising sessions for the local Education officials and school representatives on nutrition and cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, environment and the prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA).
The project will be monitored by the local Education officials and WFP’s Third Party Monitors. The outputs data will be collected by WFP through the partners’ monthly distribution reports, and the outcomes will be assessed the Post-Distribution Monitoring on an annual basis.
