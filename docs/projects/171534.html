Project Summary

Recent analysis by FAO-SWALIM indicates that the intensity and frequency of floods and droughts in Somalia have been increasing in the last 20 years, due to climate change and other factors such as environmental degradation and population growth. The last ten years has seen more than 50% of failed rainy seasons, two major drought events including a famine in 2011 and a severe drought in 2016/17. Around six major flood events have also been witnessed in the country during the same period in 2010, 2013, 2015, 2018, 2019 and 2020. Such sequential events have had hazardous impacts, eroding resilience, economic reserves, livestock herd size, health and livelihoods.
Currently, model climates are indicating a high likelihood of La Nina phenomena during the last quarter of 2020 and first quarter of 2021, which is associated with depressed rains in Somalia that may lead to drought conditions in the country. Further, FAO-FSNAU analysis indicates that an estimated 2.1 million people face food consumption gaps through December 2020 due to multiple risk factors. For instance, lower parts of the Shabelle River continue to be inundated since May this year, impacting negatively on the food security sector.
The project’s objective is to develop an interactive digital ‘climate information service’ for different users, such as the agriculture sector, to assist farmers and livestock owners to make real-time decisions. It will make existing information more relevant by generating useable information from data, and providing tools that can be applied in local decision-making about the impacts of climate change on future drought and flood trends. This will be achieved through the development of mobile applications with customized messages for different users, such as farmers, pastoralists, development agencies and others.
The project will also aim to scale up the use of modernized early warning systems and climate information to save lives and enhance livelihoods in vulnerable communities through the expansion of hydro-meteorological monitoring network that will generate data, which will be used in the production of early warning information for early action. To sustain this, FAO aims to work closely with the Multi-hazard Early Warning Center within the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management (MoHADM) for institutional and local use of Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Information (CI) in preparedness for response to climate-related disasters.
Also, the project will seek to enhance flood emergency preparedness through awareness creation on flood safety and environmental conservation to persons residing along river banks in Belet Weyne, Bulo Burti, Jowhar, Balcad and Afgooye districts and mapping of communities living in flood-prone areas of the Shabelle basin.
The project aims to improve the way different communities utilize and reserve water during rainy seasons and advocate for simple rainwater harvesting methods and soil conservation measures that will see optimal use of rainwater and thus reduce water scarcity in a selected pilot study areas.

Project Activities

Develop an interactive digital ‘Climate Information Service’ for different users, such as the agriculture sector, to assist farmers and livestock owners to make real-time decisions (January – December 2021). Work closely with the Multi-hazard Early Warning Center within MoHADM to strengthen institutional and local capacities in Early Warning Systems (EWS), provision of Climate Information (CI) and key related information products (January – December 2021). Expand networks that generate climate-related data to save lives and safeguard livelihoods from extreme climate events (January – December 2021). Map river embankment breakages and communities living in flood-prone areas, and create awareness on flood safety and environmental conservation to persons residing along river banks in Belet Weyne, Bulo Burti, Jowhar, Balcad and Afgooye districts (January – December 2021).

Mitigation Measures

Humanitarian access to some areas within Somalia is limited due to conflict/security threats including presence of landmines or explosive remnants of war. The use of high resolution satellite images will be emphasized in areas with limited access for ground truthing. FAO has also invested in local staff who know the security issues/risks of their respective areas. The staff also have very close relationships with the local communities. FAO will monitor closely the security of local staff and partner organizations with guidance from the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS) in Somalia. In times of floods and droughts, women and children are most affected in the community. The early warning information generated during this project will target the most vulnerable members of the community who are women and children. The participation of women in workshops targeted to promote early warning information will be encouraged. Vulnerable communities are the ultimate beneficiaries of the SWALIM early warning information generated through this project. In times of high floods, there is a high risk of vulnerable communities being excluded from receiving early warning information due to displacement. This project aims to scale up efforts to reduce disaster and climate risk, and to strengthen the resilience and adaptive capacity of people at risk of displacement by investing in EWEA information systems, while strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers. Further, the project will build capacity for preparedness and community-based early warning systems to prepare for safe and timely evacuations if displacement becomes unavoidable, thus ensuring the protection of all displaced persons.
