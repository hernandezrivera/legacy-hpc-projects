Project Summary

The proposed project will cement on the work SLI is already doing on the ground in Melut. This emergency Water, Hygiene and sanitation (WASH) project will assist the most vulnerable households from the Returnees, IDPs and Residents to have access to safe drinking water through provision of raft of measures. In order to address WASH related gaps, this project will provide lifesaving WASH interventions by distributing essential WASH items which include WASH Hygiene kits, buckets, collapsible, Aqua tabs, Pur sachets and non-collapsible jerry cans and other items with support from WASH Core pipelines. The project will rehabilitate 10 water points, establish 100 latrine stances, and ensure 4 nutrition facilities have access to water, and provide 6410 SAM children and PLW with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. A total of 16762 persons classified as severe, extreme and catastrophic in Melut County. Disaggregation of beneficiaries shall be informed by actual needs during the intervention. Community members will be mobilized through the established community hygiene promoters (CHPs) and encouraged to avoid bad practices such as open defecation. Hygiene promotion activities including hand washing after visiting toilets, before and after eating food will be shared with households to minimize common water related diseases such as typhoid, cholera among others in the IDPs, returnees and Residents living in poor hygienic conditions.
The project will address and mitigate risks related to Gender Based Violence (GBV), which has badly affected women and girls. These groups of beneficiaries suffer multiple problems including sexual assaults while accessing water points, sexual assaults and rapes due to distances travelled as well as intimate partners violence (IPV) where women are subjected to beating for failing to bring enough water home in time and suspicion of having affairs with other men if delayed by the long queues in few boreholes or far away stream or river. Women and girls will get support and access to WASH MHM kits to assist them during menstrual periods. The project is designed to incorporate knowledge gained during the trainings on the accountability to affected persons (AAP) and widely consultation of beneficiaries, before coming up with WASH activities. Overall, the project is designed in line with the WASH cluster’s objectives and overall, the humanitarian response plan for 2024.

1. Needs

The population of Melut have been left in highly vulnerable conditions due to intercommunal violence within the neighboring counties of Fashoda, Baliet, floods, Sudan war that escalated from April 2023 forcing populations to flee for safety, scarcity of food as reported in the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report in July 2023, and the economic crisis. In the last Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2023), 2783 households were classified as severe, extreme and catastrophe in the county. In the current IPC report, Melut is listed as emergency (IPC4) food insecure. In October 2023 to March 2024, Melut is expected to have 13000 and 3724 individuals in emergency and catastrophe respectively. Between April and July 2024, population facing food insecurity in the county will reach 23000 (IPC4) and 7590 (IPC5). This implies that vulnerable IDPs and residents who have lost household assets as a result of conflict and flooding and require in-kind wash nonfood items will seek to utilize the little resources, they get to buy food rather than WASH NFIs support. Due to intense rains and flooding's, routes to markets have been destroyed. Traders are not able to restock their shops. This would mean community members may not be able to access Wash NFIs during the current flooding season. Additionally, Melut is grappling with the impact of flooding and populations fleeing Sudan conflict. Due to flooding, the water sources such as boreholes and hand-dug wells are submerged and contaminated leading the population to resort to drinking untreated surface water. Displaced households have lost their belongings to floods such as cooking utensils and water containers. The general hygiene condition in internally displaced sites is worsening and there is potential water borne diseases such as typhoid, diarrhea and intestinal worms. Assessment conducted in the county have also noted that the menstrual hygiene condition of women and girls is poor due to lack of sanitary kits and soap. In locations with water pumps, lack of pump mechanics has made it impossible to repair damaged water pumps and boreholes triggering water scarcity. Further, dysfunctional markets, lack of enough WASH partners, absence of community-based hygiene promoters and water management committees has negatively affected the capacity of the affected population to respond to disaster. In light of this, there is a need to carry out WASH intervention in Melut in order to save lives and alleviate suffering of the most vulnerable people using cluster guideline on Phase floods. SLI will utilize the grant obtained through this call to conduct hygiene promotion at the community and household level messaging and distribute lifesaving WASH items such as PUR sachets, filter cloth, aqua tabs, jerry cans, soap and buckets and menstrual hygiene kits for women and girls affected by floods and are food insecure in targeted counties. The proposed emergency response is in agreement with the WASH Cluster Floods response and Humanitarian Response Plan for South Sudan. The assistance will cover only returnees, internally displaced persons and most vulnerable residents who fall under the following categories: IPC 4 and IPC5, elderly (given their advanced age, poor economic status coupled with inability to actively engage in income generating activities); pregnant and lactating women (they are exposed to various risks such as sickness, malnutrition and protection thus making them more vulnerable compared to any other group); children; unintegrated persons; people with no community links; severely sick persons; and individuals living with disabilities.

2. Project activities

1.0: Community consultation and sensitization. SLI project team will conduct community consultation and sensitization meetings on this project with local authorities, IDPs, residents, women, youth and chiefs to brief them about the project and enumerate the role of the community and SLI in the implementation. Activity 1.1 Form and train AAP committee. SLI will facilitate the formation of Accountability to the affected population (AAP) committees of nine members (5F and 4M) to ensure community participation in the implementation, enhance acceptability and sustainability. AAP will comprise of community leaders, women, youth and People with Special Needs alongside SLI’s staff will work closely to effectively deliver WASH response. Selection of AAPs will be done by RRC and community leaders. SLI will work with the AAPs to select enumerators, Hygiene promoters, beneficiaries, distribution and monitoring. Activity 1.2: Selecting and training of 30 (20 F, 10 M) hygiene promoters. SLI and AAPs will select 30 hygiene promoters (20 Female, 10Male) with previous experience and knowledge of hygiene promotion. CHPs must be versed with local culture and should demonstrate capacity to read, write and communicate either in English, Arabic and Local dialect to enable clear and widespread information dissemination. Their training will be done in line with WASH cluster hygiene messaging and will be facilitated by SLI WASH Manager and WASH Officers. Training topics: safe water chain, safe excreta disposal, environmental hygiene, hand washing with clean water and soap/ash, how to make tippy taps, food hygiene, Menstrual Hygiene, Cholera Prevention messages as part of mainstreaming. Activity 1.3: Conducting house to house hygiene promotion CHPs will conduct house-to-house hygiene promotion to develop good hygiene practices, prevent disease and encourage positive hygiene practices among IDPs and host communities. Hygiene promotion will entail; Water purification, treatment and storage, Hygiene in the home, Hand washing and Menstrual Hygiene Management; sustainable water purification methods and hand washing. Activity 1.4: Selection of beneficiaries for WASH NFIs SLI, AAPs and enumerators will select beneficiaries to support distribute and conduct post distribution monitoring. Selection criteria will include households with elderly persons, caregivers of separated and unaccompanied children, unaccompanied minors, severely sick persons, individuals living with disability, individuals with no community links and women headed households and youths. 1.6. Distribute WASH NFIs beneficiaries Distribution of the items to 28139 beneficiaries will be conducted through enumerators, Chief and AAPs. SLI WASH Officers will oversee the distribution process ensuring quality assurance and adherence to WASH cluster guidelines. Activity 1.7: Conduct post distribution monitoring. The PDM will be carried out using WASH NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires to assess the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed, selection processes and quality of distribution. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary household with focus on the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The project will employ a sample size of 20% of the beneficiaries who will benefit directly through the intervention. Activity 1.8: Provide standard kits to 6410 SAM children and 210 PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. Activity 1.9: Establish water points in 4 nutrition sites. Activity 1.10: Establish 100 latrines stances with flood resilient designActivity 1.11: Establish 10 water points with flood resilient design.
