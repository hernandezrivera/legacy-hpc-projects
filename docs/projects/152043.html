Project Summary

Since the beginning of 2015, the North-East of Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violation of human rights resulting into different forms of Gender Based Violence (GBV). More than 1.9 million individuals have been displaced due to the conflict and displacement continues to be a significant factor in 2018, with the highest number of displacements and returns occurring in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe States(IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 24). Despite the fact that some displaced individuals have started to return home after relative security has been restored and more areas are now accessible to humanitarian actors, the situation on the ground remains dire and several remain in inaccessible areas. The Displacement Management and Shelter/(Non-Food Item(NFI) Sector has recorded that over 40 camps out of the 148 camps in Borno state are over-congested, i.e. 1,000 individuals above camp capacity – the most congested camp has 36,000 individuals above camp capacity (Muna Garage).
To support displaced individuals and new arrivals under these contextual dynamics, IOM through this project will contribute to ensuring an adequate humanitarian rapid response to needs. This will include the continuation and scaling-up of shelter and NFI support for people in camp and camp-like settings (including for new arrivals), through the establishment of transit and emergency shelters, the distribution of emergency shelter kits and NFI kits, as well as care, maintenance and upgrading of existing shelters, in accordance with Sector standards.
To compliment ongoing emergency shelter response activities, this project further proposes to enhance recovery support, particularly as it pertains to supporting the more than 1.5 million individuals who have returned to their areas of origin in the three states. With the principal aim of promoting safe, dignified and voluntary returns and preventing situations of secondary displacement, IOM will support the rehabilitation of community infrastructure (including markets, hospitals, schools, etc.), shelter solutions for returnees and host communities (shelter repair kits, transitional shelters).
Clear-cut focus will be given to mainstreaming GBV into shelter and settlement response activities during site planning and distribution of shelter and NFI related kits. Designs of shelter solutions will also take into account privacy, security and comfort of beneficiaries. IOM plans to guarantee the participation of Women, Men, Girls and Boys as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age group, and specifically address women's and girls' specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance to the needs of women as well as men; (2) site plans will be developed to mitigate the risk of GBV. All activities related to this project will equally benefit women and men, girls and boys and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. In regard to assessment, monitoring and evaluation of gender specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be centered on Do No Harm principles.

Needs

The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. In the three-worst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9 million people. More than 80% (1,441,635 individuals) of internally displaced people (IDPs) are in Borno State, the most affected state in north-eastern Nigeria. Over 60% of the displaced population live in host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities (IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix Round 24, August 2018). Since military operations began in October 2017, the trend of new arrivals from hard-to-reach areas to accessible locations across Borno State has increased significantly, and at an alarming rate that has overwhelmed the capacity of humanitarian agencies.Between November 2017 and August 2018, Borno and Adamawa states have seen the movement of nearly 190,000 individuals (153,000 IDP new arrivals and 36,000 returnees) (IOM DTM Emergency Tracking Tool Data). Movements are in large characterized by displacement from hard-to-reach areas to accessible areas; on average 4,500 displaced individuals arrive in accessible areas per week. This is considered a result of heightened insecurity and increased frequency of military operations across the north-east. Prior to November 2017, the weekly average of new arrivals in accessible areas was 1,400. Displacement to accessible locations shows no sign of abating with ongoing military operations and non-state armed group (NSAG) attacks upon locations across Borno State continuing. These new arrivals are often fleeing conflict and insecurity and, as a result, many are in poor health conditions and malnourished and in need of shelter and NFIs. Moreover, the Displacement Management and Shelter/NFI Sector has recorded that over 40 camps out of the 148 camps in Borno state are in a situation of over-congestion, i.e. 1000 individuals above camp capacity – the most congested camp has 36,000 individuals above camp capacity (Muna Garage).Out of the 1.9 million displaced persons identified by IOM’s DTM 745,494 persons in 262 camps and camp-like settings in the three states. Additionally, 407,000 returnee families don’t have access to adequate shelter and are residing in damaged shelters in the return areas of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. In host communities, 916,000 non- displaced families are living damaged houses or hosting IDPs in overcrowded circumstances IOM aims to address the needs of 496,700 individuals through the provision of emergency and transitional shelter solutions, and NFIs, to support coping and recovery mechanisms of vulnerable families.
