Project Summary

There is an ongoing armed conflict in east Ukraine with daily shootings and shelling as well as localized flare-ups such as in Avdiivka and Novoluhanskoe in 2017 and Zolote in 2018. The conflict damages or destroys housing and civilian infrastructure, necessitating an emergency response in terms of shelter and basic needs, especially for persons with specific needs. To provide shelter and NFI support to displaced and conflict-affected persons with specific needs in a timely manner, UNHCR will preposition acute emergency shelter kits as well as non-food item (NFI) in the area most affected by shelling, which is the 'contact line'. In GCA, UNHCR’s emergency response will be coordinated closely with local authorities and complementary to their efforts to respond. UNHCR will also continue its shelter repair and reconstruction in 2020 and will prioritize households with protection and specific needs living on both sides of the 'contact line'. UNHCR will make sure that it will mainstream protection into its shelter assistance (including housing, land and property rights).

1 - Needs

Along the line of contact, over 50,000 homes were damaged by the conflict. In GCA, several humanitarian actors, including UNHCR, have conducted repairs; families, communities and local authorities have also contributed. It estimated that at present 3000 families in GCA are in need of humanitarian shelter repairs. If more people return, the figure could rise. Some returning families have damaged homes that had not been eligible for repair because the owner was absent, so returns could lead to a new demand for shelter interventions. In NGCA, there are few actors implementing humanitarian shelter projects in NGCA. Though it is not possible to conduct an address-level assessment of housing damage, the Shelter Cluster estimates 10,000 families in NGCA are in need of humanitarian shelter interventions. Since the outstanding needs in NGCA are considerably higher, and the capacity of humanitarian actors is lower, UNHCR will plan for a strong shelter component in NGCA in 2020, while implementing risk mitigation measures related to the complex operational context. Among both IDPs and conflict-affected persons, access to housing remains a top priority in terms of basic needs. Among IDPs, 59% reside in rented housing, while 14% live with a host family or relatives. The high cost of rent is a main concern for IDPs. The cost of utilities, especially for heating, has risen considerably, since Ukraine is raising the price of gas to market levels as a condition for its cooperation with international financial institutions. IDPs have difficulty to pay the high cost of heating in the winter months.

2 - Activities & outcomes

As the expected capacity of humanitarian actors will not meet all needs by the end of 2019, UNHCR plans to continue its shelter activities, albeit at a reduced level, in 2020. Prior to selecting locations for shelter activities, UNHCR will conduct a security and protection assessment of the location. UNHCR will conduct post-repair monitoring that incorporates protection issues in order to measure the protection impact of its shelter intervention. UNHCR will integrate protection (including housing, land and property rights) into all shelter activities, such as, for example, at the identification of beneficiaries phase. Based on limited funds expected for 2020, UNHCR will conduct light, medium and heavy shelter repairs for some 1,400 households. In line with the Shelter Cluster, the average household size in Ukraine is set at 2.4 for planning purposes. Based on the assessed needs, the focus of shelter interventions will be on NGCA, where the numbers of families in need with humanitarian shelter repair is much higher and fewer actors are able to deliver shelter assistance. In GCA, UNHCR will continues its shelter activities, although at a reduced level as compared to 2019 since local authorities have started to conduct light repairs, UNHCR will coordinate closely with them and support their increased role in meeting humanitarian shelter needs. In NGCA, procedures for contract management and shelter monitoring will mitigate the risks associated with the complex operational context. A small number of extremely vulnerable families will be assisted with heavy repairs. Since the crises in Ukraine disproportionately affects women and elderly persons, UNHCR will ensure that 60% of beneficiaries of shelter project are women and at least 40% of all persons assisted under this objective are older persons. To respond to the immediate needs of those affected by new shelling, UNHCR will preposition emergency shelter kits including tarpaulin, complemented by other materials appropriate to the Ukrainian context. The needs for this intervention are high; as a part of its comprehensive response, UNHCR plans to support some 1,500 families with emergency shelter kits. UNHCR will also support programs to offer alternative housing to households living in dangerous areas along the line of contact, ensuring that any relocation programs respect protection principles. UNHCR will support the incorporation of protection measures in housing projects (e.g., beneficiary selection, accessibility, social cohesion, etc.). This Project Proposal will also include logistic support for UNHCR's shelter project.
