Project Summary

RI will implement the nutrition program via an integrated approach with health that will increase access through the provision of health services in the 10 HFs (Binnish, Nour, Alrahman, Tilaade, Ras El Hisn, Salqin, İskat, Turkmen Bareh, Qatmet, Mare HFs) with nutrition centers. As the project is integrated with health activities, operational, program, and staff costs will be shared with the health project and the costs for the 51 CHWs in the 10 HFs will be covered fully under the health project. However, CHWs will be part of the nutrition project by raising awareness on nutrition subjects. The project will identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities by taking necessary measures for access to HFs and nutrition centers. CHWs will reach out to people with disabilities via outreach activities and the disaggregated data by disability will be collected by the MEAL team.
RI aims to provide nutrition services over the year 2023 targeting 66,492 as follows:
-Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergencies (MIYCN-E): RI will aim to prevent acute malnutrition through contextually appropriate social and behavior change communication messages promoting IYCF-E practices. Trained medical staff at HFs, nutritionists, and CHWs will provide screening, follow-ups, and counseling sessions at both facility and community levels, and referrals.
-Counseling and Group sessions will be conducted by the Nutritionists targeting children under 5, PLWs, and caregivers using a hybrid module to ensure adherence to COVID-19 measures and protocols. According to the nutrition cluster data and recommendation, the 51 CHWs will conduct MUAC screening and raise awareness about IYCF-E. The messages will be about exclusive breastfeeding (initiation of breastfeeding, the importance of breastfeeding for the babies and mothers, and complementary feeding starting from 6 months of age (introducing the complementary feeding in an appropriate quantity, diversity of the complementary feeding, preparing the meals in a clean way). Nutritionists and health workers maintain the services at the facility level during RH and pediatric consultations or vaccination clinics. CHWs will support surveys as required to address the need at the targeted location.
-Management of Acute Malnutrition Sub-Sector: RI will implement CMAM in 10 HFs according to NWS Nutrition Cluster Guidelines. CMAM will complement MUAC screening by receiving the referred individuals with malnutrition at the nearest HF with a clear structure in terms of the provision of services, internal and external referrals, and integration with other sectors in the same facility.
RI will provide outpatient nutrition services in the 10 nutrition centers. Nutritionists and health workers will implement MUAC screening for children and PLW at the HF level and malnutrition cases (SAM and MAM) will be registered and followed. Nutritionists will follow up on nutrition activities at the HF level, register and gather all nutrition data at the health facility, screen for acute malnutrition through detecting the nutritional edema, MUAC, and Z-Score, and treat and follow up with the acute malnutrition cases and provide counseling and awareness raising sessions about IYCF-E practices. Also, the Nutritionist will support all medical and non-medical staff in implementing nutrition activities according to the RI plan.
HFs will increase the accessibility of services at the community level in line with the CHW referral network. RI will ensure the provision of needed nutritional assistance at the discharge level by providing preventive supplements such as micronutrient powder, tablets, and nutrients (donated by UNICEF) to ensure continuity of treatment and prevent relapse.

3 - Needs

Scale-up of high-impact nutrition interventions is needed to meet the needs, especially for early detection, referral of cases, and integrated prevention interventions. According to the SMART survey 2022, the combined GAM rate showed a prevalence of 5.0% (3.3 - 7.5 95% C.I) with a combined SAM rate of 1.7% (0.6 – 4.4 95% C.I). The GAM rate by WHZ increased from 2.5% (1.3 - 4.5 95% C.I) to 3.3% (2.0 – 5.4 95% C.I.) and the SAM rate also increased from 0.4% (0.1 – 1.7 95% C.I.) to 0.9% (0.2 – 3.4 95% C.I.) (P=0.487). The SAM rate by MUAC increased from 0.8% (0.2 – 2.7 95% C.I.) to 1.3% (0.5 – 3.4 95% C.I.). Furthermore, according to the SMART survey-2022 findings and the global nutrition classification of severity ranking criteria chronic malnutrition rate in NWS is ‘serious’ and has not significantly improved since 2021 – 24.5% in June 2021 and 22.3% in 2022. Prevalence of anemia in children 6 – 59 months is ‘serious’. Only 4.62% of children 6 – 23 months are receiving adequate and nutritionally balanced food. Only 56.26% of infants 0 – 6 months were exclusively breastfed, and only 33.33% of children 12 – 23 months were continuing breastfeeding. Acute malnutrition of PLWs increased from 12.4% (June 2021) to 12.9% (2022), and increased for women of reproductive age from 10.8% (June 2021) to 11.7% (2022), with the WRA anemia rate slightly reducing to 52.73% (2022) compared to 2021 rate of 53.85%, noting that anemia rate among pregnant women was higher (58.59%).

4 - Implementation of activities

All activities will follow nutrition guidelines as approved by the nutrition cluster and UNICEF global guidelines. RI will support the prevention and treatment of malnutrition services through:- Outpatient care at nutrition centers where CHWs and mobile clinics will provide screening services, diagnosis, treatment, referral, and follow-up of MAM and SAM cases. The nutritionist will take necessary measurements (oedema, MUAC, weight, length, z-score), calculate the RUTF/RUSF dose according to standard criteria, and provide the dose with an explanation of how to utilize the RUTF/RUSF, appropriate diet, and the importance of weekly follow-up visits. The nutritionist will inquire with the mother about the child’s condition and consult with the doctor to determine the presence or absence of a medical complication. In the event of a medical complication, the nutritionist refers to the stabilization Centre. The mother is given advice on the importance of nutrition complementarity and continued breastfeeding for those under two years.- IYCF support through group and 1-1 counseling. Group sessions for PLW will be held at the HFs to provide awareness sessions about nutrition behaviors and correct practices- CHW teams will conduct oedema and MUAC screening for children under 5 years and PLWs during household visits- CHW teams will refer suspected cases to nearby Nutrition centers to confirm the diagnosis and follow-up treatment

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

First risk: Lack of quality services and doing harm. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Treatment for cases of malnutrition to be conducted by technical personnel only; 2. Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor-centered services; 3. Training CHWs and Nutritionists on CMAM and IYCF-E to make sure that the provided services are conducted according to the high quality and standardized framework.Second risk: Discrimination or perception of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with communities to understand relevant community perspectives; 3. Carry out safety audits for community members’ access to site locations.4.Training the team on child protection issues 5. adding child protection providers to the services map


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project aims to provide IYCF awareness messages and Acute malnutrition treatment, RI will work on mitigation measures through the following: • Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs • Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report • Establish good relations with local authorities, • Clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them.• Ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all women and girls. RI will mitigate the risk of exploitation or abuse by • Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. • Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.• Provide awareness-raising sessions on PSEA for boys, girls, and parents • Provide child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mitigation: staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible. Activate an emergency plan.
