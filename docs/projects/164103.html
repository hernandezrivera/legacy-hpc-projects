Project Summary

This nine-month intervention will contribute to Nﬁ and Shelter Sector Objective 1 providing life-saving and sustainable NFI items to 8625 targeted vulnerable HHs. INTERSOS will provide assistance without discrimination on status and will be able to support the 8625 vulnerable HHs, IDPs, host communities and returnees’ ones living in sub – districts of Suran, Hamra, , Oqeirbat, (Hama Governorate), in sub – districts of Khan Shaykun, Ma’arrat Al Numan, Tamanaah, Heish, Sanjar (Idleb Governorate), in Jabel Saman, Atareb, Daret Ezza, Aghtrin sub - districts (Aleppo), in Rural Damascus East Gouta sub – districts of Kafr Batna, Harasta, Duma, but also including At Tall, Ein Elﬁjeh, Kishweh, Nashabieh and Qatana, Dar’à sub district and Deir Ez Zor. This intervention is built on the experience, knowledge and expertise acquired by INTERSOS and SARC from previous project on NFI winterization kits distribution in proposed areas. INTERSOS intervention will have the aim to reach the most vulnerable population in need and will be adapted to respond to the concerns, needs and priorities of men, women, girls, boys, people with speciﬁc needs (PwSN) and other speciﬁc groups (i.e. age and gender). Using in kind distribution modality, INTERSOS will organize with SARC distributions activities for 2500 core and 6125 seasonal NFI. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured though implementing safe distribution lines, ensuring sustainability and equitable access to the aid provided for both male and female beneﬁciaries. The composition of the core and seasonal NFI items will be based on the minimum standards established by the NFI sector but also assessments will be conducted to customize the assistance in the form of core NFI and seasonal materials according to household size and speciﬁc needs, considering also geographical areas, availability of items and materials. INTERSOS and SARC will be conducted also a post – monitoring distribution in order to determine the effectiveness of the activity in comparison to the needs of the pre-distribution phase, identifying shortcomings in the implementation process, achievements and progress made so far.

4 - Needs

As a result of the ongoing crisis in Syria since 2011 remains an urgent need for core and seasonal NFI assistance for those displaced from their homes and/or seeking to return to their places of origin. This is especially the case for those recently displaced in Idleb and Aleppo governorates or for extremely vulnerable individuals regardless of their status (IDP, returnees, host community) who do not have access to enough ﬁnancial means to purchase basic NFI supplies. According to the latest data shared by NFI sector (Q4 report) 1,306,114 people have been reached with core NFIs and, 1,799,942 have been reached with supplemental NFIs but 3.42 million people are still in need of basic NFI, distributed among 74 districts. With regard to the governorates of Hama, Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Dar’à and Deir Ez Zor, the estimated number of people in need accordingly to HNO 2019 is: 0.57 million in Hama, 1.028 million for Idleb, 0.78 million for Aleppo, 1.4 million for Rural Damascus and 0.75 million for Dar’à and 0.9 million for Deir Ez Zor. For all the targeted districts the HNO draft for 2020 is reporting a severity scale between 4 and 5. Those needs are evident also from a recent RNA conducted by INTERSOS in the same areas, in particular for Rural Damascus and North Rural Hama,which showed a consistent need for accessing to quality NFI core and supplementary assistance. For the governorates of Idleb and Aleppo, Dar’à and Deir Ez Zor, INTERSOS didn’t conduct yet a comprehensive need assessment but is taking into consideration both secondary data shared by the relevant sectors as well as the recent development ofthe context. The PDM exercise which will follow the winter kit distribution planned to be conducted in Feb 2020 in Aleppo (650 kit), Idleb (325 kits) and Homs (650 kit) should allow to have a direct access to the need of the population in those areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

INTERSOS will distribute 2500 core NFIs and 6125 seasonal kits expected to reach 43,125 individuals/ 8625 HHs. Composition of kits will follow NFI sector standard to ensure and promote equity. Under the umbrella of the MoU signed with SARC in May 2019, INTERSOS will maintain overall coordination for NFI kits distribution activities and will work in partnership with SARC. SARC shall support in accessing to the areas of intervention (HTR and/or shifted under control of GoS) and facilitate community acceptance. Such coordination and cooperation among INTERSOS and SARC is based on those principles: consistency in provision of relief items, rapid procurement ensuring timing and fair process, assistance based on agreed vulnerability criteria (HHs composition, child-headed HHs, female-headed HHs, HHs with PwSD disable, elderly, socio – economic criteria), maintain records on the distribution process, coordination to avoid overlapping, monitoring activities of the post distribution phase. The timeline for the distribution schedule will be accordingly to working plan, with locations and quantities; kits will be stored at the supplier warehouse and transported by SARC trucks to the areas of distribution to mitigate the impact of bureaucratic impediments and security constraints. The distribution modality will be jointly decided accordingly to each specific context, and it will be done by a team composed with SARC volunteers and INTERSOS distribution staff at distribution points or door by door. INTERSOS will have its own staff (distribution officers and area/sector coordinators) and use of specific tools for monitoring (PAT, PDM reports, intermediate and final narrative and financial reports). To monitor the impact of the items and beneﬁciary satisfaction, INTERSOS will conduct a PDM exercise with SARC on 20% of the total distributions within four weeks after each distribution activity, using the PDM tool agreed at NFI sector level and PDM reports will be made available.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Prot.Risk 1: Increasing in inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members/INTERSOS will provide the standard NFI sector kits and will keep constant consultation with stakeholders and other relevant partners to ensure respect of do no harm principles/INTERSOS will analyze local context & dynamics, establishing strong links with local authorities & community leaders. Prot.Risk 2: Tension between host communities, IDPs & returnees/ INTERSOS will present clear vulnerability criteria for selection aimed at targeting the most vulnerable not based on status/INTERSOS will inform and give clear messaging about purpose of intervention and its protection mainstreaming approach/INTERSOS will made available for the recipients relevant information inclusive of feedback mechanism/PDM will be conducted. Prot.Risk 3: Abuse of authority by distribution actors/INTERSOS will conduct distributions avoiding overcrowding situations, ﬁeld team will have male and female staff/ INTERSOS will train staff on Do No Harm principles, and PSEA, child safeguarding and code of conduct.Prot.Risk 4: The risk towards the humanitarian nature of the intervention for the presence and activities of military and non-humanitarian actors. Security forces will should not be involved in distribution and beneﬁciary selection. Distributions must not inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors. Protection Risk 5: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population/INTERSOS will inform beneﬁciaries about their rights to receive assistance regardless the documentation they have/INTERSOS will protect beneﬁciaries list and ensure they have only necessary information to avoid traceability/Referral to SARC legal services will be activated based on need.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: No change in the political and security situation and GoS continue to hold control over newly liberated areas. Market systems remain relatively stable and there are no excessive ﬂuctuations bringing to devaluation of the SYP. Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions. Continued cooperation and coordination between INTERSOS and relevant local authorities and partners. Risks: mass displacement of population, access to the identiﬁed community is not granted and the level of despair and tension in the target communities is exacerbated by the non-delivery of the NFI kits. Bank regulations andrestrictions as well as constant exchange rate ﬂuctuations will have signiﬁcant impact on INTERSOS cash ﬂow and funding. Identiﬁed prequaliﬁed suppliers are not able to provide on time NFI kits or are breaching the basic procurement principles. Based on PDM, beneﬁciaries have reported concerns over the quality of items, delayed distribution of seasonal items and varying standards as well as the need for other items. Mitigation measures: INTERSOS will follow up and monitoring on the population movement, high level of information sharing with coordination mechanisms within partners and sector.INTERSOS will follow-up on the security context and prompt communicate with relevant stakeholder to reschedule distributions. Community tensions will be mitigated by ensuring participation of the population in program, clearly communicated beneﬁciary selection criteria & established complaint mechanisms. Clear procurement procedures and policies are ensured by through monitoring and ﬁnancial audits to avoid fraudulent attitude. Prequaliﬁed suppliers’ lists will be constantly updated again by INTERSOS. INTERSOS will advocate inside NFI Sector to continue working to harmonize and coordinate among partners on assistance packages, including the quality of materials through the development of technical SoP.
