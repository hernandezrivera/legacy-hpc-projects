Project Summary

The project aims at decreasing the high morbidity and mortality rate across Sudan by providing life-saving access to scarcely available services with an integrated approach covering multiple sectors. The access to free primary and secondary healthcare for 40,000 children 0-14 will be ensured through the provision of high-quality paediatric services in all EMERGENCY (EMR)’s facilities. Access to free maternal & reproductive health services for women will be guaranteed to 3,000 women living in underserved areas through in-hospital and outreach consultations. Free regular malnutrition screenings & supplementary feeding cycles will be provided to the hospitalized malnourished children in EMR’s wards. Free cardiac screenings & life-saving surgery will be ensured to patients affected by neglected heart illnesses leading to premature death such as Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD), to decrease the prevalence of chronic cardiac disease across the Country. Finally, health & nutrition education will be provided to promote positive behaviors to reduce both malnutrition and the risk of vector borne and waterborne diseases, which account for the majority of illnesses diagnosed in EMR’s Paediatric Centres.
The Centres in Port Sudan and Nyala are secondary healthcare facilities with integrated services. A total of 24.000 and 11.000 (if the centre will re-open) primary health care consultations respectively will be triaged and receive an outpatient consultation, receiving a pharmacological therapy, vaccinations, blood investigations, X-ray or blood transfusion whenever needed. Patients with medical complications will be hospitalized; those who will not meet the admission and treatment criteria will be stabilised and referred via ambulance to public facilities and feeding centres that are part of EMR’s network of collaboration with local stakeholders. In Port Sudan, a comprehensive set of SRH services such as ANC, PNC and FP for 1200 women, plus EPI services for newborns, will also be performed within the premises of the Eskandaria governmental PHC, in agreement with the local Midwifery Department.
In Mayo (or Soba Hilla area), if conditions allow PHC services will be offered to 8.000 children 0-14. In the Centre, children will be triaged and receive an outpatient consultation; those mainly affected by dehydration and respiratory tract infections will be stabilized, given free pharmacological therapy and referred to public facilities. Blood investigations & vaccinations will also be performed. All children will be screened for malnutrition and receive RUTFs and vitamin/iron supplementation if needed. SAM cases will be referred towards Stabilization Centres nearby if no further medical complications are found, and towards higher-level health facilities in case of medical complications.
Health and nutrition promotion sessions in all EMR facilities and in outreach will ensure long-lasting behavioral changes at local level. On top of this and to ensure long-term sustainability and retention, the intervention will also focus on capacity building of national health staff in all EMR facilities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As per foundation chart, EMR respects and encourages diversity, equality, inclusion and gender balance across all interventions, and is committed to ensuring access to free-of-charge healthcare in compliance with the principle of non-discrimination (both applied to medical practice as such, as well as to crosscutting policies e.g. hiring policy). This principle, which is taken into consideration during all phases of project implementation, seeks to guarantee that health assistance as a fundamental human right is “exercised without discrimination of any kind”. All international and national staff members within EMR comply with the principle, and base their day-to-day medical practice on the EQS (Equality, Quality, Social Responsibility) Model outlined in the Manifesto for a Human Rights-based Medicine (accessible on EMR’s website). Collection and analysis of Sex and Age Disaggregated Data (SADD) allows EMR staff to assess, monitor and evaluate service delivery, treatments, achievement of targets and intended outcomes, with the aim to better capture the needs of different beneficiaries and adjust programmes according to observed changes. To enhance access to healthcare services for women, female staff members are constantly recruited in various roles. Their presence ensures not only high professionalism, but also the maintenance of a culturally sensitive, safe and peaceful environment for female beneficiaries and their children to seek healthcare assistance. The access needs of persons with reduced mobility have also been taken into account, as all facilities are equipped with dedicated infrastructure such as ramps.Considering the on-going conflict EMERGENCY is constantly reiterating the humanitarian nature of the intervention.
