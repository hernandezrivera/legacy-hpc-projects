Project Summary

The project will address the needs of both IDP and Host Communities in the countryside of Idleb and Western and Northern countryside of Aleppo with an emphasis on continuity of current projects. SRD will provide integrated protection activities within health facilities, prevention and risk mitigation activities through awareness-raising sessions, in addition to response activities through case management approach (case disclosure, specialized GBV PSS, case management, referral, case follow up and close cases) and solid referral pathway. Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) will be provided in the currently functioning safe spaces to enhance the resilience of survivors and vulnerable women and girls.
SRD will provide protection, child protection, and GBV services through women and girls safe space WGSS or Community Centers in addition to community-based outreach services in rural Idleb (Termanin, Aqrabat, and Kelly communities) and Northern Aleppo (Albab, Alsalamah, and Qabasin communities). While SRD provides integrated GBV, Psycho-Social Support, and Early Childhood Development services in currently functioning SRD health facilities. Integrated protection and shelter teams are collaborated to improve living conditions through the repair of 500 damaged houses while integrating protection mechanisms to ensure accessibility, privacy, and safety for persons with disabilities and vulnerable groups. SRD provides center-based services and outreach services in the affiliated areas including community-based protection, CP and GBV structured awareness sessions through outdoor activities.
Integrating the two and streamlining outreach activities will increase access and reduce barriers for vulnerable groups. Health centers proved to be a safe and confidential access point to access GBV services and this successful model is therefore replicated and continued under this project. SRH services will include a focus on adolescents and family planning (in response to child marriage) raising awareness of relevant services available which include psychosocial support, clinical management of rape cases and providing safe spaces for women and girls.
The objectives of this project are aligned with the most crucial protection needs as indicated by the Protection Cluster, as it will increase and ensure continual access to GBV services and alleviate suffering of those requiring critical care in countryside Idleb, Western and Northern countryside Aleppo.
Qualified and trained psychosocial workers will be present in the safe spaces and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions for GBV survivors, referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. The work will include instruction on integrated case management, communication skills, community protection practices, behavior change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition, GBV survivor support, reporting techniques, cultural sensitivity, and referral pathways. Protection awareness messages will be delivered to beneficiaries through daily household visits, prioritizing messages of child labor, child marriage, and GBV. Referrals will be made for those with protection needs or other forms of support to facilities and services providers within the referral pathway to minimize the risks of vulnerability. Provision of specialized and community-based child protection services considering what shall be in the best interests of the child through case management services, integrated Emergency Response Outreach Team, structured and unstructured PSS, referrals, and addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care.

4 - Needs

The crisis environment over the past 9 years strained the capacities of communities to care and protect children of all ages. Psychosocial distress is reported in 42% of surveyed households demonstrating the toll on child and adult mental and physical wellbeing with immediate, and if not addressed, life term consequences. Separation from caregivers continues to be described as a persistent child protection issue. Events leading to family separation indicate the interconnectedness and diversity of vulnerability factors with death 24% for boys and 22% for girls, economic need 20% for boys, and child recruitment 14% for boys and 54% children with distress among top cited reasons for family separations MSNA 2019. 47% of cases were disclosed to GBV service providers and referred for urgent medical services as needed, which highlights the importance of collaborative work between GBV and health actors. In NWS yria, women reported on well-functioning safe spaces and mobile teams with psychosocial support. Protection risks have increased with the ongoing displacement, lack of shelter options and sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, specifically related to GBV, CP. Overcrowding in IDP sites and makeshift shelters and a lack of WASH facilities places W&Gs at particular risk of GBV SitRep7 OCHA. SRD conducted FGDs with W&Gs in Kelly community center. Findings showed that there is a need to target graduate women, and girls, whether a baccalaureate or university with the vocational trainings, e.g administrative courses or EnglishThe Protection Cluster Monitoring TF examined the availability of services for PWDs with a large number of informants and concluded that 93% of all KIs stated that there are no dedicated services for specific needs.SRD will need to overcome new impediments to access to provide assistance including for cases of protection concern and address the needs of, maintain capacity for emergency response.

5 - Implementation of activities

WGSSs and CCs will continue offering the following services: Case management and confidential referral to relevant service providers, vocational training and empowerment activities, SRH and GBV awareness sessions for all women and girls. Boys and girls will be provided with Early Childhood Development, structured and unstructured PSS group activities to improve the social and emotional wellbeing of a children at different ages. In order to remove physical and psychological barriers for women and girls with psychical disabilities, in coordination with the local council, An additional caseworker will be recruited with specialized services physical disabilities. Plus, elderly and disabled people will be identified and helped with IPA cash module.Case management has in-kind/cash funds to cover the needs of vulnerable beneficiaries who cannot be referred to other services. Guidance will be shared with Cluster with vulnerability criteria; documentation and compliance requirements. Un/Structured PSS activities, parenting skills, and early childhood development will be provided to children and their parents, identifying children with vulnerabilities to be referred to CP case managers. The integration of GBV PSS services with SRH care proves to be an excellent entry point to initiate care for GBV survivors, given that women and girls can more discretely access GBV services as part of visits to health professional for SRH needs or other related concerns. All the mobile staff will be trained on emergency plan and therefore ready to expand coverage in order to conduct rapid protection assessment to identify risks faced by WMBGs, providing emergency service package for newly displaced households. persons with disabilities and older persons, persons with protection concerns; particularly woman- and child-headed households or recent IDPs are served through integrated, specialized, community-based protection that is their house repaired by the shelter team.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The population displaced and specifically IDPs women and girls might face challenges in accessing SRH/GBV services due to multiple barriers on where/why to access. Referrals from and to other services might be disrupted due to the security situation. Overlapping with other SRH/GBV services might occur. Security situation and continuous air strikes can hinder the work of the WGSS, PHCs and MCs. Partners’ staff working in highly insecure environment might not be able to access counselling services. Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges in accessing the center to attend young mothers’ club sessions. Women might face challenges accessing the WGSS due to child rearing responsibilities. Community members visited by the MCs have high expectations of receiving material assistance, not only SRH awareness and discussions on disrupted services hence community might not be willing to share information and participate. Confidentiality/privacy of mobile clinics might not always be guaranteed in certain locations and this might not be a conductive environment for women and girls to share their concerns around SRH. Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development and compromised quality of trainings due to contextual constraints. Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV services. Program and field team ensures confidentiality principles are in line with global standards; data protection protocols are in place and physical space is designed to meet criteria.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mobilization activities will take place to share information with the community focusing on IDPs women and girls about the GBV services in the PHCs and WGSS. Regular meetings are conducted with other services providers to facilitate referral pathways and project staff are regularly updates on the up-to-date service providers of the surroundings communities. Regular communication with the community members and service providers will reduce the risk of duplication. One of the SRH/GBV mobile outreach clinic trained on SRH/GBV emergency response to respond rapidly to new displacements with PFA and dignity kits. Specific budget is allocated for transportation costs for the most at risk individuals. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of partner violence and other forms of domestic violence these risks might not be identified and rectified. Advocating for appropriate and adequate household rehabilitation to be provided considering privacy. We will include women, girls with disability and at-risk groups to determine such things.Designated car will be provided to facilitate the access of adolescents with disabilities to the girls’ club at the WGSS. In consultation with female community members ensure mobile tents or any other safe space that will mitigate the risks of violating confidentiality and privacy. Protection officer in charge of case management supervision. Budget and well trained SRD staff ensured for sustainable capacity building strategy that shows impacts with pre and post assessment, follow ups, mentoring, coaching initiatives and structured supervision of case managers and midwives. Non-protection Staff is sensitized around attitude in terms of sensitive service provision. Children cases with high protection concerns like recruitment will be referred to specialized actors
