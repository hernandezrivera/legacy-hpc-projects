Project Summary

This project aims to ensure that shelters in targeted camps are safe and constructed from materials that are resistant to short-term deterioration, and that the same households have access to a range of basic non-food items, including portable solar lights and LPG, necessary for dignified way of life. The project will also contribute to enhanced field-level coordination, improved capacity amongst the refugee community to improve and maintain shelters, reduced environmental impact and the evolution of the Shelter Sector strategy. In parellel to the camp-based interventions, support to host community adjacent to the camps will include shelter improvement, capacity building on Shelter DRR and the provision of solar lights.

Objectives

Objective 1 - Strengthened coordination: the appointment of catchment focal points will strengthen field-level coordination through improved communication, organisation of daily activities, and response in times of emergency. In particular, CFPs will strengthen emergency preparation, linking day-to-day camp-level shelter coordination with emergency preparedness and response activities as outlined in the 72- hour Cyclone Plan. Catchment Focal Points (CFPs) will provide a link between the revised/new CiC structure – especially with the Care and Maintenance officer – Shelter/NFI agencies, Info Hubs, and the Shelter/NFI Sector. They will focus on coordinating shelter/NFI assessments and emergency shelter assistance. Objective 2 - Diversification of Shelter/NFI Response: the provision of TSA and MTS for the refugee community, as well as tailored shelter upgrade support for the host community living adjacent to refugee camps, will improve site safety, shelter quality, and household living conditions. The piloting of alternative construction materials will contribute to the refinement of existing shelter interventions, while the treatment and supply of bamboo for both IOM and partners and the distribution of LPG will reduce the environmental impact of the shelter/NFI response. Objective 3 - Focused Engagement: The provision of voucher-based NFI support, capacity-building on shelter and disaster risk reduction, piloting of community-led interventions and tailored host community shelter support will encourage self-reliance, social stability and peaceful coexistence by increasing the Rohingya refugee population’s involvement in decisions concerning shelter/NFI assistance and providing support to the host community living adjacent to refugee camps.

Needs

Despite the positive impact on living conditions arising from implementation of the Upgrade Shelter Kit (USK), Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA) and Mid-Term Shelter (MTS) programmes, the MSNA 2019 results indicate that the shelter response must keep evolving to avoid entropy. The critical need is for all shelters to be safe, constructed from materials that are resistant to short-term deterioration and located on sites that provide protection against natural and human-made hazard. The results of the Multi-Sector MSNA 2019 showed that the refugees identify rotten bamboo, damaged shelters, leaking roofs and low-quality materials as their main issues of concern . Solutions are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to insect attack. Slope failures during the 2019 monsoon rains highlighted a need for shelter-level site improvement works not covered by the SMSD Sector remit. Such work includes slope protection, raising plinths, household drainage and access route improvement (51% of households (MSNA 2019) note access difficulties). Despite the widespread distribution of portable solar lights, 40% of households (MSNA 2019) say they have no functioning solar light. Only 20% of households possess two or more portable lights. These data highlight a need for high quality, durable portable lighting. The LPG distribution programme has been successful. 88% of households (MSNA 2019) state they exclusively cook with LPG. The need now is to reach 100% and ensure continuous refills. Households receive refills to reduce smoke in the shelter and minimise firewood collection, an activity that can harm social cohesion among adjacent communities. The need remains for training on safe cooking practice. Precarious tenure arrangements and land dispute continue to impede the provision of shelter assistance, protection response and social cohesion between the refugees and host community.

Activities or outputs

Shelter: To adhere to the principle that all shelters should be safe, constructed from materials that are resistant to short-term deterioration and located on sites that provide protection against natural and human-made hazard, IOM will implement TSA I, TSA II and MTS as follows:TSA I: Camps 8W (IOM direct 3000, World Concern 2000 via pipeline), 12 (IOM direct 5000), 14 (IOM direct 1000, HEKS 1000 via pipeline, CAID 2000 via pipeline, Uttaran 2000 via pipeline), 15 (IOM direct 1000, 6000 HEKS via pipeline), 16 (IOM direct 2000), 18 (IOM direct 500), 22 (IOM direct 3500, Uttaran 500 via pipeline) and 24 (IOM direct 5500) to ensure full coverage of all 100,000 families in the IOM AoR. TSA II (replacement of damaged materials for households that received TSA in 2019): Camps 8E (IOM direct 4800, World Concern 1300 via pipeline), 8W (IOM direct 800, World Concern 700 via pipeline), 9 (IOM direct 7100), 10 (IOM direct 6400), 12 (IOM direct 400), 14 (IOM direct 600), 16 (IOM direct 1000), 18 (IOM direct 5500), 22 (IOM direct 400) and 23 (IOM direct 2500). MTS: To be constructed in Camp 20 extension (IOM direct 2000) in coordination with SMSD and WASH. IOM will also set-up pilot studies to assess the suitability of alternative construction materials and community-led shelter interventions, as well as engage in capacity building of the refugee community on shelter and disaster risk reduction.All Shelter interventions will use borak bamboo treated at IOM’s bamboo treatment facility.NFI and household-level solar lights: To support the refugee’s health, dignity and safety, NFI assistance will be provided through a voucher system designed to strengthen the coping mechanisms of the Rohingya.FGDs will gather information on NFI needs and preferences and will involve both male and female participants.. NFI support will be provided in camps 8E (IOM direct 6100), 8W (IOM direct 6600), 9 (IOM direct 7100), 10 (IOM direct 6400), 12 (IOM direct 5300), 14 (HEKS 4000 via pipeline), 15 (HEKS 4000 via pipeline), 16 (IOM direct 4500), 18 (IOM direct 6000), 20 (IOM direct 1600), Camp 20x (IOM direct 1500), 22 (IOM direct 4300), 23 (IOM direct 2500), 24 (IOM direct 5600).To reduce GBV and general protection risks, solar lights that meet the minimum standards for quality will also be provided in camps 8E (IOM direct 6100), 8W (IOM direct 6700), 9 (IOM direct 7100), 10 (IOM direct 6400), 12 (IOM direct 5300), 13 (CARE 2000 via pipeline), 14 (6600 via pipeline), 16 (CARE 2000 via pipeline), 18 (IOM direct 6000), 20 (IOM direct 1600), 20x (IOM direct 1500), 22 (IOM direct 4300), 23 (IOM direct 2400), 24 (IOM direct 5600) and 25 (IOM direct 1500).Emergency response: IOM will support the Sector’s revised shelter focal point system by appointing catchment focal points in the 6 IOM-led catchments (9, 8W/10/18, 19/20/20x, 22, 23, 24/25). Further, IOM will continue to implement emergency response initiatives directly and via the pipeline in 18 camps (IOM AoR).Host community support: IOM will increase support to host communities close to the camps, including through shelter upgrade assistance and/or provision of household-level lighting for 2000 households. Liquid Petroleum Gas (LPG) and Safe Cooking: IOM will address the urgent cooking fuel needs of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar through the provision of LPG refills in the entire IOM AoR. Introducing alternative clean cooking fuel and technology will reduce the incessant firewood collection devastating forests in and around the camps.Across all interventions, extremely vulnerable individuals will be identified through household-level data collection and consultation with community leaders. IOM will ensure all Shelter and LPG staff are trained on GBV and PSEA. To integrate GBV into programming, IOM will actively consult both female and male refugees to seek feedback on key considerations.
