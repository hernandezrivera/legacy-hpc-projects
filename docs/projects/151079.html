Project Summary

The project will adopt a system-level approach to provide quality care and outcomes for children by focusing on gaps at the institutional, community and household and individual level. The project will also address the deep-rooted child well-being issues in Somalia at different levels which are caused by, but not limited to, weak governance mechanism and capacity in child protection, low incentives and awareness to further the child welfare agenda of key stakeholders, and high prevalence of poverty and conflict that force vulnerable families apart in search for livelihoods. Result 1 of the project will focus on supporting the government and institutions to provide quality child care by supporting the implementation of policies and guidelines, capacity enhancement and vulnerability assessment, training of officials and service providers and developing accountability framework and referral mechanisms. Under this action, WV is deliberate to ensure local ownership from the government to community level to create sustainable and concrete impacts for children. Government as a duty-bearers have an obligation to deliver and protect children's rights. Result 2 of the project focuses on tackling child protection issues at the community and household level. Strengthened child protection service provision at community level will help to complement the institutional effort under Result 1. Raising awareness in communities on any harmful practices against children and strengthening community-based child protection structure in schools and communities such as child protection clubs will reduce children’s risk of exposure to exploitation, advocate for best child protection practice and to create an enabling environment to allow possible integration of vulnerable children. Result 3 will focus on cash based assistance to vulnerable households with children at risk of violence. Income generation through unconditional cash transfers will prevent family separation as care givers search for a source of livelihoods to provide for their children's needs especially during emergencies. Cash based assistance also enables reduction of negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, child marriage, child recruitment by non state armed actors, child trafficking, school drop outs and GBV. The project will target IDPs, excluded minority clans, vulnerable host communities and children with disability.

Needs

The protracted drought in Somalia has aggravated already complex local protection concerns affecting girls, boys, women and men. Increased patterns of migration in search of food, water and safety has left populations more vulnerable to a number of risks ranging from physical attacks, gender based violence (GBV), recruitment of children and forced evictions. In addition, existing resources and services have been significantly strained creating tensions between host and displaced populations. Weaknesses in service provision particularly in displacement settings has rendered populations more susceptible to violence especially for women and girls. According to the 2017 Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS), 76% of GBV survivors are IDPS, and 99% are female. Limited access to protective shelter, health, safe learning spaces for children and unavailability of water and sanitation facilities are some of the challenges reported in displaced settings. A baseline survey by WV in Burao and Hargeisa also highlighted that the most common type of physical assault in the camps were related to domestic violence and GBV (WV, 2017). A similar WV baseline in Puntland (Eyl) also corroborates the prevalence of domestic violence, GBV and physical attacks. Research by WV also reveals that, a vast majority (99%) of women are not aware of their right issues and available services such health, legal aid, safe houses and other basic services (WV, 2016). The lack of awareness of rights and services available has made women and girls more vulnerable. GBV cases are under reported due to stigmatization and a lack of knowledge on where and how to report incidents. Women who were interviewed also pointed out their unawareness of fundamental rights under both customary and national laws. Specifically, cultural beliefs among the community were identified as a key driver in propagating gender based violence, early marriage and FGM.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is linked to strengthening government and community based child protection systems to make them resilient to the fragile context of Somalia occasioned by conflict and climatic shocks. The sustainability aspect is embedded in the project by the fact that focus is placed on building capacity of community based systems that promote children's well being. Further, the entitlement of project beneficiaries is is legally recognized since the government is a duty bearer for enforcing human rights. In addition the project is design with recognition of the rights based approach to protection.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Women, children, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific protection concerns. Therefore, integration of age, gender and diversity principles in service delivery and assistance will be promoted. Protection risks in IDP sites include widespread GBV, child recruitment, physical attacks, forced early marriages, forced evictions, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices. The project will strengthen inclusion by instituting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations i.e robust complaints and feedback mechanism for instance a toll free telephone line

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will also mainstream prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

One of the risks facing the targeted population is physical attacks and destruction of assets important for survival. Children also face a risk of forced recruitment by armed non state actors.Through advocacy, and the ongoing WV global campaign of ending violence against children, the project will disseminate messages on minimizing disproportionate and indiscriminate targeting of civilians and civilian assets vital for survival.
