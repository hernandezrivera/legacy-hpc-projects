Project Summary

Shafak will deliver a comprehensive approach to Mine Action involving Risk Education (RE), and Victim Assistance (VA). This project will be a continuation of an existing Explosive Remnants of War Risk Education ERW - RE programme in Idleb and Aleppo. Shafak proposes that RE activities continue on by existing teams.
Shafak is providing ERW-RE to children in the communities of Idleb and Aleppo. Because ERW’s are a long term problem, Shafak believes that long impact approaches for children must be sustained; the more a child is taught safe behaviours, the more likely it is that they will become ingrained in their day-to-day activity.
Under the ERW-RE part of the project, 4 teams of 5, each consisting of 2 groups of 2 and a team leader, who have already been trained by Shafak, will target locations in Idleb and Aleppo providing school-age children with RE activities, and materials to inform them about dangers of ERW. They will also provide RE material to the older generation, putting up posters about the ERW risks in the public streets and communities to increase the awareness of ERW threats. Schools and locations that were not reached in previous messaging will be the first priority.
In addition to, Risk Education will be conducted in IDP camps and host communities. Whilst the sessions will be open to all community members, the teams will be placing particular emphasis on reaching those who are most at risk of accident, including children, young men and IDPs who may not be familiar with the type of threat present in the area. The sessions will be held in both schools and community spaces. The teams will also distribute flyers and posters with Risk Education messaging to reach the wider community.
Furthermore, a “Training of Trainer” (ToT) Trainer will provide RE training to Community Focal Points (CFPs) in affected communities, and RE teams and other workers from NGOs across all humanitarian sectors, to improve the relevance, integration and reach of RE messaging across NW Syria. This information can then be used to better understand protection gaps and improve future programming. It will also be used in advocacy initiatives.
Also, the project will also enhance the resilience of people living with injuries and disabilities (PLWDs), focusing on ERW survivors, through referrals to specialized services.

4 - Needs

One in two people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards. Approximately one-third of all populated communities in Syria are estimated to be contaminated. Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families (2020 HNO). Hostilities in northeast Syria have significantly disrupted mine action activities in the area. Recent and existing contamination, increased displacement and scarce availability of services, in a context of higher risk and lower capacity of actors to safely respond, are exacerbating the severity of needs and pose the necessity for expanding scale and scope of humanitarian mine action efforts to allow for comprehensive geographic coverage and efficient sharing of expertise and resources (2020 HNO). The presence of ERW in NW Syria inhibits livelihoods, restricts access to land, basic services, humanitarian aid and causes injury or death. The problem in NW Syria is that the true extent of ERW contamination is not known. PLWDs including survivors of ERW and conflict-related injuries are disproportionately affected during situations of conflict and emergency. Aggravation of existing impairments due to environmental change, stress and limited access to services, in addition to the creation of new impairments from ongoing violence, all contribute to the burden of disability. The aim to mitigate the threat of explosive hazards through riskeducation and provide remedial support for survivors through Victim Assistance. One of the main gaps in current humanitarian response, lifesaving activities such as Victim Assistance for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard accidents, remains underfunded and under-implemented whilst population needs are growing in light of the escalation of the conflict. (Humanitarian Readiness and Response Plan, 21 February 2020) Furthermore, to ensure a life-saving protection response sustained

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through Shafak office in Syria with the collaboration of local councils and the Directorate of Education in Idleb and Aleppo. Project activities: Victim Assistance: VA component of the project will establish a case management system through referrals to specialized services by hiring 4 case workers.RE training: RE ToT Trainer will expand the delivery of ToT training currently delivered to RE Community Focal Point volunteers, existing and new RE teams from any/all NGOs, as well as internal-to-NGO RE focal points. As well as, 6 x teams (RE, VDC) will be trained directly by Shafak, this training will also include community liaison, safe behavior messaging and the use of KoBo.RE Awareness Raising Sessions: 6 x teams (RE, VDC) (4 teams in Idleb and 2 teams in Aleppo) each comprising a team leader and four team members will be deployed to target beneficiaries most at risk of ERW accidents, including IDPs, children and agricultural workers. The teams will be fully mobile, able to respond to the changing security situation and target groups. Provision of awareness materials (leaflets for families, posters for the community) to the student's families and communities designed using the experience of Shafak staff.MEAL activities: After each RE session, there will be discussion and questions about the key elements of the presentation, trying to ensure that the participants have a good understanding of the lecture content. A pre and post-test will be conducted. Shafak will also hold focus group discussions with beneficiaries both during and after project implementation. Feedback and complaints will be addressed very seriously. To demonstrate this value in practice Shafak has established a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism to affirm that our beneficiaries and communities have the right to complain. Also, CFM provides a communication channel to our communities to voice their suggestions and provide feedback on our program interventions.

Lateness and delay in implementing this capacity-building had an effect on IDPs because it takes time to do the capacity and then to implement.4. The effect of the trauma even during service providing. Mitigating Measure:1- recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA. Set-up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children2- clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity. - harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines. - coordinate with the local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination3- Training of some staff as key resource persons to act as trainers/ facilitators inside Syria4- Sustained advocacy on the impact of explosive hazards issues

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conflict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries. Shafak will maintain threat contingency plans and SOPs for local use. Field supervisors, informed by field security staff, can adjust the daily/weekly work plan and avoid areas of elevated threat. All teams deploy with mobile phone communications and a first aid kit, knowing where the nearest medical facilities are located. Partial or total suspension of the activities. If activities cannot be resumed, Shafak will assess the possibility to operate in other areas. A wide range of candidates will be interviewed and competitive salaries and contract duration will be offered. Inability to properly control or confirm the quality of training: Ensure that the training location facilitates the best possible outcomes.
