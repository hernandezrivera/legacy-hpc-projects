Project Summary

This project addresses the root causes of GBV in Emergencies through evidence based social norm change programmes; mitigates the risk of GBV occurring in camp and IDP concentration points; and provides multi-sectoral services to survivors of GBV. The three core components of the programme will be implemented in key accessible areas of Somalia in all Federal Member states and engages UNICEF's network of civil society partners who will continue to benefit from capacity building support delivered through UNICEF's local, regional, international team of GBV experts. The purpose of the project is to ensure continued expansion of lifesaving services whilst at the same time undermining the structural causes of GBV. We will aim to reach 5500 survivors of GBV; improve the safety of IDP camps and concentration points, provide access to safe houses for survivors, decreasing the likelihood of SGBV for more than 1 million displaced people; and roll out the Communities Care programme reaching 94,500 people through targeted norm change activities.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1. Provision of age, sex and culturally sensitive protection services. Activity 1.2: Training of key service providers in provision of survivor friendly GBV services (include training on the Clinical Management of Rape protocols, provision of psychological first aid, training in case management and improved policing through training of police officers) Activity 1.3: Strengthen coordinated protection responses through GBV Working Group and information sharing mechanisms among humanitarian, child protection and GBV actors Activity 1.4: Broad based dissemination of protection messages via multi media channels Activity 2.2: Conduct a safety audit in key IDP hosting sites to assess improvements, lessons learnt and recommendations from previous years Activity 2.3: Provide integrated GBV in Emergency guidelines training to humanitarian workers from WASH, Shelter, Camp Layout, Education, Nutrition and Health clusters. Activity 3.1: Scaling up of community-based protection system through Communities Care programmme to increase community-based referrals of survivors of abuse, access to safe houses for survivors, decrease in stigmatization and reduction in the vulnerability of women and children. Activity 3.2: Conduct community mobilization and sensitization campaigns that reach and engage community members through the Communities Cares Community Discussions and Action Planscommunity dialogues, house to house visits, interactive radio and SMS campaigns, publicevents and trainings are undertaken by community leaders to change harmful norms.

Mitigation Measures

Protection Risks and Mitigation Measures1.Risk of women and girls being exposed to Sexual Violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse and partners not having sufficient internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safe guarding and PSEA. Mitigating measures: The risk to survivors of further stigmatization, abuse or isolation is high even when they access professional support services. In order to mitigate against this risk all partners will undergo mandatory PSEA and Child Safeguarding ttraining and sign a code of conduct as testimony to protect beneficiaries from abuse. UNICEF will ensure partners apply fundamental principles in the delivery of services such as absolute confidentiality (in both consultation as well as record keeping); providing survivors with the opportunity to decide what is in their best interest, and involving them in the management and care process through the application of informed consent procedures. 2. Exclusion and Marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations anddecision-making processes Mitigating measures: * Conduct training on gender equalityin the targeted communities focusing on opinion leaders, Imams, women and girls. *Recruit women in decision making roles * Conduct community action research/assessment to understand causes of marginalization and community solutions toaddress them. * All beneficiary lists include a category of marginalized groups, peoplewith disabilities, the elderly, women and girls. * Use focused radio programmes andcommunity meetings to mobilise women and girls to take on leadership roles andparticipate in activities affecting their lifes.3. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area: Mitigating measures: *Develop mine risk awareness messages will be developed and disseminated duringpublic meetings, on social media and radio outlets for wide dissemination * Imams,teachers, women groups, children's clubs will be mobilized to conduct grassrootawareness campaigns to mitigate accidents. * Develop mine risk and UXO referralpathways with clear guidelines on who and where to report in case a mine or UXO issighted or an area is suspected to be mined * Conduct high level advocacy against themanufacture and use of anti personnel mines * Conduct training on humanitarian lawtargeting the military. * Conduct Mine risk awareness in local schools, and negotiatefor mine risk awareness to be included in school curriculum 4. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. * Conduct riskand safety analysis prior to implementation of any protection activities. All humanitarian workers in IDPs and host communities will receive PSEA training and GBViE to increase knowledge and understanding of mitigating measures. Staff screening and reference checks will include questions on history of any GBV and child abuse related crimes5.The risk of unsafe and unethical management of data will be mitigated by enforcing the datamanagement SOPs endorsed by the AoRs. Please provide further explanation

The entire GBV programme supported by UNICEF aims at he development of formal systems for long term identification, care and protection of people vulnerable to SGBV. The programme also acts on the informal systems that either enable or disable normative systems at the community level which are the fundamental cause of GBV. This programme aims to change thesenorms and, thereby, de-stigmatize the provision of services to survivors, as well as, create communities that are fundamentally safer and more equitable for women and girls.
