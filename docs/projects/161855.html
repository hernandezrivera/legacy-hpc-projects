Project Summary

The project aimed to improve the lives of 2000 children and 200 women beneficiaries through: establishment of child-friendly spaces (CFS) and awareness raising on child protection issues by women particularly the mothers. Key activities of the project which came into direct scrutiny by the stakeholders are: consultations with IDP leaders, religious leaders and co-identify and recruit staff and volunteers who understand local culture ad speak the local language; organize orientation and emergency training for aid workers/service providers on child protection and psychosocial support in emergency settings; conduct baseline determination of the priority child protection and psychosocial issues; identification, monitoring and responding to protection threats and psychosocial issues; provision of life skills education and psychosocial support activities to 2,000 children – with emphasis on reaching adolescent girls; establishment of safe space for children and adolescents where they can partake in age-appropriate activities, play and socialize with other children; mobilize women's participation in developing interventions that protects children, adolescents and women from exploitation and abuse in emergency situations; establish referral mechanisms in collaboration with the State Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Welfare and Child Protection Action Network (CPAN); and in view of possible limited availability of referral services, social coaching to members of the IDP community and host community will be given for them to be able to cascade basic counseling skills and basic case management to their respective communities.
However the Child Safe Space will be constructed and equipped to be used by the children.

1 - Needs

An estimated 2.7 million children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction and other grave child rights violations. At least 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been recruited, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Of those, approximatively 50 per cent are survivors of SGBV. The crisis also has caused severe psychological distress among children. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children. At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster carers and single caregivers with several children, have suffered from severe psychosocial distress and will require psychosocial and/or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care.
