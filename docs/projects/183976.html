Project Summary

The main goal of the project is to provide community support to IDPs, returnees, returnee IDPs and host communities in Central and East Darfur. The project will help strengthen the capacity of communities to manage resources and peacefully resolve conflicts, and empower them to identify and prioritize investments in their areas using a participatory and community- based approach to project identification and management. The project will contribute to longterm support for development and reintegration at the community level, peacebuilding and social cohesion in areas of return. HADC employees implemented similar projects in Ukraine, following the results of post-monitoring, community cohesion increased, when both returnees and host communities are included in return-related programs and jointly participate in the implementation of the project, realizing that they are making a common contribution to forming their own social environment.
Within the framework of the project, HADC will implement an integrated approach to the implementation of the project, creating a relationship between all components of the program at all stages of activity, which will contribute to ensuring awareness, safety, protection of rights, and access to social guarantees. The Foundation plans to implement the project in close cooperation and partnership with key stakeholders – host communities, IDPs, returnees, returnee IDPs and opinion leaders.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan is a complex mixture of internally displaced persons (IDPs), Sudanese returnee refugees and returnee IDPs. According socio-economic assessment, 2021 by International Labour Office, East Darfur hosts 289 245 IDPs, most of whom live in self-settlements or among the host community. Most IDPs fled inter-ethnic conflict between Arab and non-Arab tribes. Tensions between different community groups in East Darfur have long been heightened. As of 2021, UNHCR estimates that there are 450 117 IDPs within Central Darfur. In the past decade, there have also been increasing numbers of returns IDPs, to their areas of origin in Central Darfur. As the conflict decreased in some areas, communities that had previously been abandoned are being rebuilt. However, many of these communities lack resources or access to basic services due to years of displacement and conflict. According to the multisectoral assessment of the Danish Refugee Council, 43% of the population in Sudan in Central and East Darfur are members of the host community, 30% are IDPs and 27 % – returnees. At the same time, 47% of households are headed by women, and 14% are headed by retirees. It should be noted that 63% of IDPs believe they have less access to rights than host communities, especially access to basic services. But only 37% of IDPs intend to return to their places of origin, mainly due to intercommunal conflict or lack of services in their area of residence. There are locations where the situation is especially critical. For example, in Central Darfur one can distinguish Zalingei (according to UNHCR, 209 500 IDPs live there in 2021), where problems associated with prolonged displacement and new returns often occur, leading to intercommunal conflicts. In Eastern Darfur, one can distinguish Yassin (according to UNHCR, 34,000 IDPs and IDP-returnees live there in 2021) and Shia'ria (according to UNHCR, 37,500 IDPs live there in 2021). According to the multisectoral assessment of the Danish Refugee Council, which was published on 12 April 2021, programs in these areas are badly needed in the direction of peace-building, housing, education, WASH and protection.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Expected Result: - the living condition of IDPs and returnees in 1 settlements in Central Darfur and 2 settlements in East Darfur improved through provision of reintegration support at the community level, sustainable socioeconomic reintegration of peaceful coexistence and community development is ensured.. Within the framework of the project, 6 community projects will be implemented. 4 small-scale (with an average cost of $ 125,000) and 2 medium-sized (with an average cost of $ 350,000) multisectoral community projects with quick impact, will be focused on the needs identified by the community and localities. Depending on the identified needs of the targeted communities and localities, projects can cover a wide range of sectoral responses, for example: - reforestation, energy efficient stoves, solar systems to provide basic needs and livelihoods - rehabilitation of local vocational training / professional development centers; the creation of new vocational training centers in localities where they do not exist; provision of missing equipment to local training / continuing education centers; capacity building of local communities.After the implementation of the activities, the completed projects will be transferred to the local community or any other management structure agreed between the community and local authorities; the opening ceremony and handover of the facilities was organized.On the basis of each community project, work will be carried out to strengthen existing and establish new community based protection networks, including conflict resolution, peaceful coexisting committees, building their capacity to identify the most vulnerable persons, identify and find solutions to the problems of protecting their communities, the Foundation intends to collect and pass early warning messages.Also, within the framework of the project, it is planned to monitor protection through field missions and remotely, regularly contacting and collecting relevant information in the areas of physical security, material security, legal security, freedom of movement and residence, tracking identified problems and cases with the help of this project, other partners , PWG members, analyse and regularly prepare reports on monitoring protection, share with partners.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment.Thus, the training programs will include mandatory items that train the project beneficiaries in waste management, waste separation and disposal (including construction waste), training in cleaning their local area, careful attitude to natural resources (plants, water, land). When training beneficiaries on water chlorination, the aspect of chlorine handling and disposal will certainly be included, and when training communities to install water pumps, emphasis will be placed on how to install pumps so as not to harm the environment. In the training centers, to train opinion leaders, a separate course on environmental protection will be developed and conducted, which includes hygienic aspects, cleaning and separation of garbage, careful handling of nature, water resources, etc.In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During the implementation of the project, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. For this, communicative approaches will be used, involving transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information about the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.To submit complaints, the CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” has developed a mechanism for feedback and complaints. During the project, beneficiaries and stakeholders will have the opportunity to contact the management of the Foundation by telephone, e-mail, or through social networks. Also, at the locations of the project implementation, "feedback" boxes where you can leave requests will be installed. All complaints are brought to the attention of the management of the Foundation, and those who applied are provided with a response (if they have left their contact details). If this is a complaint, then it is communicated what measures were taken. Information about the start, implementation and completion of the project will be published on the organization's website, pages in social networks, and also posted in local media.
