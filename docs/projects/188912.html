Project Summary

Relief Agency (RA)-South Sudan is planning to integrates and implement HLP and General Protection project targeting the most affected counties in Unity and Jonglei states. The project aims to mitigate protection risks and improve safety and build resilience of the vulnerable population affected by conflict, flood, economic and climatic shocks, high prevalence of diseases outbreak and limited access to basic services. The project will provide inclusive and equal access to lifesaving protection support, legal and other protection related risks mitigation services to women, girls, men, boys and other marginalized groups such as elderly, youth and persons with disabilities. The HLP components will focus on communities to establish local committees in both groups, through collective selection of representatives to ensure good representation and inclusiveness. Relief Agency teams will mobilize community volunteers and a committee including GBV to implement community protection initiatives and actions plans. The team will also provide legal identity documentation, including nationality and the passport, displacement certificate for affected persons who cannot access basic rights and services, especially school-aged children and the old groups who have challenges applying for state exams. RA will provide both awareness raising sessions to individual and groups assistance in accessing national identity documentation and foster to improved ability to exercise HLP rights for individuals and groups affected by displacement with priority given to the most vulnerable, including female and child headed households, the elderly, individuals with physical and mental disabilities, and those who are extremely poor. RA will target conflict and flood affected areas and areas of
potential return. Relief Agency will provide awareness raising to the particular areas of potential returns in the targeting locations. Relief Agency (RA) will provide deep protection and gender analysis exercise as well as legal redress to address root causes of protection and HLP concerns and provide sustainable solutions for the displaced affected persons. Conducting static and mobile protection monitoring and assessment with project stakeholders. RA will provide PSS by the trained staff or caseworker to male and female. Conducting protection awareness raising and carrying out protection prevention messages adapted. Create platforms for establishing peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence with the community groups. Strengthening protection monitoring system with in the community. Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience with engagement of formed community groups and other stakeholders Community-based dispute resolution mechanisms created with formed community groups and relevant stakeholders. HLP awareness raising and outreach activities engaging and empowering community groups and liaison with authorities. Legal support / counselling/ assistance / case management

1 - Needs

HNO 2022 REPORT: A total of 8.9 million people, which is over 2/3 of country population are affected and in dire need of humanitarian assistant and protection. Of this, 5.6 million people are in needs of protection while 1.8 million people require HLP support. About 8.3 million people expected to experience severe food insecurity by mid of 2022. Following the 2021 data report on flooding, an estimated 835,000 people were affected and displaced country wide with 80% of them are from Unity and Jonglei states alone and expected to have not return fully this year. 328,000 people are still as refugees in the neighboring countries as asylum seekers, other 2 million remained internally displaced. 1.3 million children under the age of 5 are expected to be at risks of acute malnutrition including 676,000 pregnant and lactating women. The displacement caseloads have increased with high risks of continued diseases outbreak as elderly, persons with disabilities, children under 5 years and women are exposed to hard living conditions with limited access to basic services including restriction to information on protection lifesaving and legal services. INTER-AGENCIES ASSESSMENT REPORT-19/2/2022 lead by UNHCR in Leer county highlighted pressing issues of protection concerns. The report documented causalities of intercommunal clashes between the youth of Mayendit and Leer county in November to December 2021. The other was between the SPLA-IG led by Koch and Mayendit commissioners. These events resulted to displacement of 6,498HHs, looting and destruction of properties- (RRC Data). RELIEF AGENCY (RA)'S FIELD REPORTS-JUNE TO DECEMEBER 2021; From our field protection monitoring and protection assessments reports in the targeting areas documented issues of conflicts and violence combined with the effect of flood have deteriorated infrastructures and livelihoods resulted to high prevalence of economic shocks and vulnerabilities. Humanitarian access and services delivery has been restricted. The increased of spontaneous returns have strain the already scarce resources in the existing affected host communities. The protection concerns related to cattle raiding and revenge killings perpetuated by armed youth and gangs have caused insecurity in the regions. Other protection cases include risk of exacerbated GBV incidences like beating, harassments, domestic violence, communal conflict, child labor are confirmed among the top urgent protection and GBV concerns in Unity and Jonglei states. A concern for adverse traditional coping mechanisms include women eating less and last resorting to early marriages of girl child for dowry. The land disputes, negative political views and pervasive cultural practices created a gap for inclusion and unequal opportunity of the community members especially the marginalized groups such as vulnerable women, girls, children, youth and persons with disability. A practice of serious cultural superiority has suppressed dialogue and the seeking of truth and reconciliation process among different communities in the targeting locations. While the South Sudan revitalized peace agreement is not stable and the affected people are insecure from lack of strong protection system, women and girls continue to face and suffer from GBV, SEA and exclusion with limited access to services. Safe spaces for community protection especially Women and Girls Safe Spaces, schools and the health centers are affected due to insecurity or destroyed during the conflict or by flood. Youth are engaged in crimes because of idleness and majority of children are not in the schools. Through this project, Relief Agency (RA) will further cement on the existing support in the areas targeted and provide prevention and response interventions through participatory approach. RA will train community mobilizers, caseworkers and leaders to support in protection and HLP related activities and in coordinating project implementation.

2 - Project activities

The following activities will be provided in each of the targeting locations: Activity 1.1: Monthly Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment with male and female teams to understand protection trends for advocacy and response. The activity will target community leaders, church leaders, teachers in the schools and opinion leaders. RA will use the developed tools such as FGD, KII and Direct observations. Activity 1.2: Psychosocial Support Services with male and female teams. RA will use available or constructed centers to delivery this services. RA will provide recreational services, lifeskills sessions and livelihoods services in mean of income generating activities (IGA). Vulnerable women, girls, men and boys will be targeted. This is a coping strategies and will support the vulnerable people affected by conflict, flooding and COVID19 stresses, communal violence, survivors of GBV and among other community problems. While the community protection centers shall remain open to receive those in need of PSS, community caseworkers will be recruited to attend to the concerns reported in each center in our areas of operations.Activity 1.3: Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging adapted for low literacy and communicated through trained service providers that can handle disability diverse techniques of awareness raising. RA will observe COVID19 principles and guidelines and will therefore execute this activity accordingly. Awareness creation will be carry out using IEC materials, community mobilizations and focus group discussions with at least 8 to15 persons with face masks sitting 1meters distance. This activity will be conducted monthly to address the protection concerns collected during the protection assessment and protection monitoring.Activity 1.4: Peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence with community groups established of women and youth (male and female) including those with and without disability, to participate in social cohesion solutions. RA will provide capacity building trainings to local leaders to strengthen the local norms thatpromote peace and advocate for the right of vulnerable groups such as women, girls, disables and children. The community will be sensitized on pervasive cultural practices and integrate policies that harmonize and mitigate protection risks. The capacity building sessions will be done twice a month with only 15 leaders participants targeting all the locations.Activity 1.5: Strengthening protection monitoring system with in the community with support from community groups and local formal and informal institutions and authorities.Activity 1.6: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience with engagement of formed community groups and other stakeholders. Activity 2.1: Community-based dispute resolution mechanisms created with formed and trained community groups and relevant stakeholders and training on HLP and ADR as well.Activity 2.2: HLP awareness raising and outreach activities engaging and empowering community groups and liaison with authorities.Activity 2.3: Legal support / counselling/ assistance / case management. RA will provide legal assistance to the individuals affected either during the flooding or by conflict or as result of injustices to claim their rights to properties, land or housing. RA will support around access to legal documents/documentation either acquiring new ones or claiming for loss ones. RA will provide HLP information who lack access or with rights suppressed with empathy on most vulnerable groups such as women, girls, children and PwDs. RA will provide counseling to those affected and loss properties to avoid mental disorder. RA will also provide special support to individual in need of individualized specialized support through case management. RA will work with individual and help support them through their concerns around HLP. RA will provide legal aid to individuals in needs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The collected information from the beneficiaries will be use to evaluate the project progress and the impact. The information will further be use for wider advocacy and share widely with other stakeholders to further provide support need and ensure the protection of the community in needs. During this process, RA will consider do no harm and ensure integrity on the information provided by the beneficiaries or the community members.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RA shall mobilize community group formation with women groups including those with disabilities, men groups including those with disabilities and youth (male and female) groups including those with disabilities. They shall meet on subjects of access to justice and protection, gender equality ending disability discrimination. These platforms will function throughout the life of the project and will continue to function as volunteer groups supporting their communities beyond the life of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RA has a trained project team and have clear understanding on the PSEA internal policy. This is again roll out to first the relevant stakeholders or the local actors associated with affected community members. The key local authorities and other institutions heads or representatives such as schools, human rights activists and civil societies actors and local or traditional leaders etc are targeted and will be train on the PSEA. The PSEA policy will be integrated in the implementation of the project activities and the beneficiaries will be aware in-order to protect themselves.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In consultation with these community groups a community building (church, school etc) will be selected to enhance capacity for improved adult literacy and access to skills for improved livelihoods. (Cash can be used here for small business establishment based on a vulnerability criterion decided among the groups). RA will use interviews, groups discussions or complaint desks for beneficiaries to share information. While the literacy rate is low in the local area, beneficiaries are encourage to use local languages to share their feedbacks.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Relief agency Protection department will identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programs. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities’ leaders including the payams administrators to ensure locations are safe for distribution. During the distributions, RA will conduct sensitizations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in long distance and in mixed groups and during daylight hours. The program teams will ensures gender balance during staffing and ensures that all staff are aware about the protection and gender related risk. The CRM will be used to identify protection cases and will be directed to the Protection department. The elderly who will be at the distribution side will be assisted by the project volunteers . The safeguarding policies of Relief Agency require all staff and any one engaged in its programs to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitized on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Programs manager will ensure that all the staff were trained on GBV and sensitize the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways. Relief Agency (RA) will integrates protection principles such as Do No Harm while implementing this project. The persons with disabilities will be consider and assist by the project volunteers during the the distributions and registration.
