Project Summary

"To address the identified needs based on JMSNA-21 for Rohingya refugees mostly required the WASH infrastructure repair and maintenance, and rehabilitation of existing infrastructure rather than new construction. Promoting their hygiene practices including behavioural changes with gender-sensitive hygiene promotion round off the year of the NRC WASH program and contingency stock is available to support emergency response.
NRC have a presence at camp-7 with WASH activities as well as a host community that enforces to extend and continues NRC services with quality delivery to address crisis-affected people. NRC have a close relationship with the camp focal point, area lead agency and SMS agencies including close collaboration with CiC and RRRC for getting recommendation and approval. NRC will work with the WASH infrastructure especially sanitation facilities construction and rehabilitation in camp-7 and will provide support based on needs and gaps where applicable with close coordination with the camp wash focal and CIC office. Provide support with water supply, sanitation and Hygiene promotion and awareness campaign for community people's behavioural changes with good practices in the host community. Awareness sessions will be conducted through local staff or trained volunteers and WASH sector guidelines, modality, and approach NRC will follow. Formation of the community will be formed consisting of 15 people per group segregated by males and females close to the HHs. They will be introduced to how properly they should take their own safety measures from water-born, vector-born and water-washed borne diseases. People will be sensitised to water safety measures, personal and family hygiene, practices on proper hand washing and the use of hygienic latrines. They will also be aware of food and environment hygiene, AWD, and Covid-19 prevention. WASH sector-developed IEC materials will be distributed among the BNF. The composition of WASH kits will be distributed periodically to encourage them for changing their practices. NRC will ensure BNF meaning participation in decision-making, plot selection and community report building. In the host community NRC will do close collaborations with UNO, DPHE, PIO, chairman and host WASH focal and other stakeholders.
Minimum requirement of ISCG WASH Sector standards, NRC will conduct this intervention following the latest Covid-19 guidelines without any distributions. NRC have a CFM team for receiving community complaints and feedback for program quality improvement and quality service delivery. A PDM will be conducted for comparing the stratus with the baseline. Outcome minoring will let NRC the impactful result and BNF satisfaction service from NRC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Water supply and sanitation facilities are the core and continuous needs in camps as all the structure are made on temporary materials that need to continuous rehabilitation, R&M and new construction as well. Compared to households persons with disabilities, vulnerable groups and higher proportions of gender needs reported: lack of water supply and sanitation facilities including not functioning, being unclean, being too far, difficult to reach for females and feeling unsafe due to lack of gender segregation.However, the main drivers of WASH gaps/needs were found to be:• Water: 12% of FDMS and 26% of HC reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs.• Sanitation: Only 8% of faecal sludge treatment plants meet standards while 7% of sanitation infrastructure requires major maintenance and 51 FDMNs on average share bathing facilities due to a lack of space• Hygiene: Only 66% of women and girls access MHM kits as per standards. Regarding outbreaks, cases of scabies and dengue have respectively been multiplied by three and by twelve in 2022 compared to 2021• Waste: Only 51% of refugees are disposing of waste in designated areas resulting in 27% of households reporting garbage near their shelters.Some of the major challenges of the WASH sector remain financial gaps (only 47% of JRP requests in 2021), inflation (+35% on the soap), poorly functional infrastructures and response mostly due to lack of space. To address/mitigate the gaps HP sessions will assist the program more effectively and sustain change and habituate with hygienic life.

"Rationalization aims to ensure that all Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. NRC will work closely with the CiC office, site management, and protection focal points, camp and area focal alone with other relevant stakeholders so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs, in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. NRC programs are predicted to the stable operations and improving the relationship and social cohesion among refuges, host and government. In addition, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable to understand the WASH ground needs and gaps, improve access to facilities, consider the protection risks and vulnerability. NRC will do prior consultation with sector, twig and ISCG for initiative activities in align with the approved modalities and guideline with ensure minimum and basic requirements to address BNF critical needs based on needs and gaps. NRC will do follow the sector objective, guidelines and direction that adhere to NRC policies and minimum standards while serving the BNF. NRC have the established CFM desk where BNF can share their complaints and feedback for improving the program. NRC always focus on BNF real time needs and BNF oriented service to quality delivery with a transparent and equitable manner with adapted and integrated approach. BNF engagement and participation are ensured for decision making while initiative the activities in terms of location, contribution and promote ownership. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in WASH and experience gained in Bangladesh context in WASH response on these thematic areas."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

NRC always do believe the integrated program is more impactful and ensure inclusiveness. As a humanitarian organization and Considering Protection Mainstreaming principles – a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment NRC will provide real time needs based support with ensuring BNF participations and equal &qualitative service to BNF.

3.2 - Gender

NRC always engage and consider the gender sensitivity in all programs and activities and reports will include sex, age and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). Safe and Inclusive (SIP) programming principles and confidentiality will be promoted throughout the project implementation. To mitigate risks of delivering inequitable assistances or reinforce pre-existing inequalities, NRC will ensure intervention is community led with meaningful participation of women in the decision making as well as involve men, LGBTQ groups for gender balance. NRC will employ creative ways to ensure community structures have equal gender representatives, while avoiding practices that. Gender equality will be mainstreamed and each of the activities will include sensitization on the abandonment of GBV and promotion of women and girls’ rights.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

NRC operate the program with safeguarding concern and avoid child labours and concern to sex and age disaggregation, gender and disability marker. NRC targeted the individuals with wide range of criteria. These includes the individuals of Adult Females (18-59 years), Adult Males (18-59 years), and Adult Females (above 59 years), Adult Males (above 59 years), Unknown Sex and Age and Differently abled persons.

3.4 - Diversity

Vulnerable refuges and host are at risk of protection concern, gender (all gender) and age discrimination, person with special WASH needs (PWSWN) along with persons with disabilities, religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in accessing services. NRC will ensure access to equal service and BNF meaningful participations with gender and age sensitivity, separate que in wash kits distribution, protection concern and special attention for mostly vulnerable people with quality if service delivery.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

NRC always transparent with its service delivery and accountability also part of inclusive program that NRC will do by community leaders/BNF orientation on the response and program modality, ensure BNF participation and promote into community decision making process, identify Vulnerable HHs with a NRC technical assessment and community feedback, Provide kits and sensitised the key massages on behavioural with proper knowledge transfer. During the WASH infrastructure rehabilitation NRC will engaged the BNF and ensure their participation and feedback alone with CiC office and wash focal concern for BNF oriented service delivery. Community people are open to share their ideas and complaints through CFM hotline number and drop box in camps that are always given a satisfactory answer to them.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project has number of DRM elements to address the needs of population affected by climate-driven disasters which includes, emergency WASH/hygiene kits distribution, affected infrastructure rehabilitation, raised awareness through session and campaign and will create community leadership for key messages sensitisation. NRC also will do some early action with the information that will help the community to be prepared for monsoon or disaster through the community volunteers. Also, number of capacity building training will be conducted for emergency response roster and community volunteers as a partner organisational readiness to respond to an emergency. An awareness building campaign on disaster preparedness and management will be organised through celebrating national and international DRM days.
