Project Summary

Guided throughout Protection and Do no harm Principles, multiyear and multisectoral approaches, SIF proposed interventions for 2023 will directly contribute to the Food Security and Agriculture Sector’s priorities and objectives 2 and 3 by delivering comprehensive sustainable dignified long-term solutions.
Syria is facing continuous degradation of its agriculture production and contraction of wheat harvest. The precipitated drop in agriculture and food production has directly contributed to the increase of food and nutrition insecurity in the country, where 15 million of community members is hungry and malnutrition continuously worsening (HNO, 2023).
SIF multiyear interventions are stretching over 4 governorates identified according to the severity of the needs (sector severity scale 3 and 4) high presence of food insecurity and sector priority to focus on underserved and underfunded locations, their level of accessibility, and SIF’s technical, social assessments and expertise in Aleppo, Hama, Homs and Dar'a.
In line with the results of consultations with the local communities and relevant authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, the proposed activities will contribute to building the resilience of 121,595 community members through:
(1) Providing 355 agriculture kits
(2) Rehabilitating 2 irrigation canal sites
(3) Rehabilitating the drainage system of 1 Irrigation canal site
(4) Rehabilitating 3 bakeries
Throughout the project cycle, SIF team will be guided by the accountability indicators, and participatory approach will be implemented through community based structured dialogues with the groups of different ages, gender and backgrounds, particularly those with special needs on FSA issues to ensure that any protection concerns are detected, addressed and resolved.
Projects monitoring and evaluation are ensured by SIF technical teams composed (not only) by the engineers and MEAL teams with regular field visit prior, during and post implementation to furthermore to evaluate the impact of the intervention throughout its cycle.
SIF will minimize protection risk of vulnerable population, and will facilitate a safe and dignified return and reintegration of IDPs and returnees along with creating suitable, safe living conditions, job opportunities for vulnerable communities in cohesion to our multisectoral programmes in Education, Protection, ERL, Shelter & Wash. SIF takes into account local capacities and encourage the contractors in recruiting workers from local communities. Accountability to affected population will be realized through strengthening community-based complaint mechanisms, improved participatory assessments and enhanced outreach through SIF MEAL and technical teams.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with various stakeholders on the ground for an effective response (line Ministries, NGOs, contractors, SARC, Clusters, local committees, refugee, IDP/returnee and host community leaders) in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services.
SIF observes the sector guidelines and donor requirements. SIF is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles based on which SIF’s internal policies and procedures have been developed to guide project implementation. SIF will capitalize on its extensive expertise in implementing FSA projects in the targeted locations.

3 - Needs

In 2023, Syria entered its twelfth year of protracted complex humanitarian crisis, with communities continuing to face violence, displacement, socioeconomic deprivation, as well as more recent pandemic-induced health hazards. Food security situation in Syria has steadily worsened over the past years, and currently 68% of the Syrian population is food insecure (HNO 2023). The latest escalation in food and nutrition insecurity is indicative of a population increasingly vulnerable, with many having exhausted their capacity to cope due to a range of factors, including subsequent reduction of government subsidies, degradation of livelihoods, low level of the Euphrates river (affecting electricity), drought outlook an erosion of purchasing power. The following needs were identified based on SIF assessments conducted in the last trimester of 2022 aiming to define our intervention for 2023, which focused on resilience-building to strengthen the Syrian agricultural sector at the individual, household and community levels, by improving infrastructure that serves agriculture and provide agriculture inputs to farmers to cultivate crops and manage their farmlands. Particularly, SIF has identified the following:- Rehabilitation of 3 bakeries. - Rehabilitation of 2 irrigation canals, rehabilitation of 5000 l.m. of drainage system of irrigation canal (indispensable to irrigate fields), and distribution of 355 agriculture kits tailored to the needs of each farmer and seasonal crops.

4 - Implementation of activities

SIF has an established coordination mechanism with the local authorities and in good standing with the local community which will further facilitate access and implementation of the proposed interventions. SIF will coordinate with the SARC, MoWR, GOLD, and local authorities for approvals to implements activities in the targeted locations. SIF will rely on contractors to rehabilitate the irrigation sub-canals, bakeries, drainage, as well as to provide the agriculture inputs. The contractors’ selection will be based on SIF’s Procurements/SOPs to ensure transparent & accountable selection process. SIF technical team is composed of skilled experienced engineers, supervising the contractor teams ensuring the quality delivery of planned works. Contractors provide period guarantee period ensuring sustainability. Qualified workers from local communities are hired for the implementation of projects activity. Selection of farmers will be based on SIF pre-set and objective criteria which align with the Sector specifications. SIF commits to provide FSA sector tools such as consolidated 5W, FTS and response snapshot on regular basis which will help to avoid duplication and inform on monitoring and needs. SIF will put in place a robust and independent MEAL system for the intervention aiming at identifying trends, strengths and areas of improvement. SIF MEAL team will use internal tools (PMP) to track output and outcome indicators.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if assistance is provided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure uniform and transparent beneficiary selections procedures which are agreed among the sector members and government authorities and are clearly communicated to the community. (2) Ensure that comprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation and communication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) Ensure standardize assistance package and guideline that are followed by the sector. Risk 2: Risk of gender based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) while accessing the infrastructures. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: (1) conduct safety audit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to Shelter infrastructures sites. (2) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the accessibility of infrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives in respect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and men should be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in Shelter Assistance (5) ensuring locks on each door to provide privacy to girls and women and reduce risk of sexual abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation of beneficiaries in return of promise for assistance. Mitigation measures include the following: (1) SIF ensures that specific PSEA indicators are included in the monitoring framework. (2) Vetting process is conducted during the recruitment of a candidate at application and reference stages. (3) The code of conduct, which includes the strict prohibition of SEA, is signed by everyone working for and with SIF, including staff, SIF volunteers and contractors. After the selection of the contractors, as the same time of the signature of the purchase order, the contractor signs the SIF Code of conduct which is then attached as an annex to the contract/agreement. (4) SIF employees are trained on PSEA and a PSEA focal point is assigned in all governorates targeted by SIF. Awareness sessions of the community members on PSEA are conducted every year. Sessions includes the definition of the what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing. (5) SIF community-based complaint mechanisms is accessible to affected populations in all response locations. (6) A process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of allegations of SEA has been developed by SIF.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Not getting/delay in getting approvals from local authorities Likelihood: Low Impact: High. Mitigation: SIF establishes a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities Risk 2: Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation Likelihood: High Impact: High. Mitigation: 1) Any changes in the economic conjuncture and the official exchange rate may require revisions in coordination with donors 2) In case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly Risk 3: Security situation deteriorates and effects the implementation of the activities. Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: 1) Regular monitor 3) Ensure preparedness of SIF through regular briefings and updates on any change in the situation. Risk 5: Fraud and/or corruption, aid diversion and non-respect of accountability from any stakeholders Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation: 1) Apply and monitor SIF financial/logistics and programmatic procedures; 2) conduct a transparent and participatory beneficiary verification exercise; 3) ensure segregation of duties with regular checks Risk 5: Spread of COVID: Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: 1) strictly follow COVID prevention measures. 2) PPE are available for use 3) in case of detection of symptoms, immediate isolation and testing are performed. Risk 6: Shortage of fuel Likelihood: Medium Impact: High, Mitigation: 1) monitoring of the situation 2) advance planning for transportation 3) Coordinate with Logistic cluster.
