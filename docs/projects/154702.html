Project Summary

The project proposed aims to provide immediate emergency livelihood assistance to vulnerable conflict affected returnees in Krikuk (Hawija) and Ninewa (Telafar) Governorate to enable them meet their basic needs through income generation activities. The project will target 550 IDPs households in these Governorates with target of 30% women through provision of asset replacement grants to enable target population for restart of their income generation activities).

Needs

Since the start of ISIS infiltration in 2014 and resultant crisis has led to displacement of millions across many areas of Iraq. Although in 2017 GOI declared the liberation of ISIS occupied areas and return process slowly started, however due to lack of basic services, scarce income generation opportunities, damaged infrastructure, lost productive assets and security concerns in areas of origin have made it difficult for this displaced population to achieve the return ambition. By the end of October, more than 4 million displaced people returned home. Despite the scale of overall returnees, the rate of return has slowed in recent months and shows sign of levelling out. According to OCHA, as of October 31, 2018, more than 1.9 million people remain displaced, 50% of whom have been displaced for more than three years (https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/timeline_of_displacement_and_returns_31_oct._2018.pdf). In terms of IDPs by governorate of displacement, Ninewa is ranked top with a figure of 584,582 IDPs as of October 31, 2018 (http://iraqdtm.iom.int/IDPsML.aspx). The question posed are, why are these IDPs are not returning to their areas of origin? And are they advancing to a durable solution or they are at the risk of more protracted displacement? (OCHA). The more prominent and convincing answer to these question is the obvious lack of basic services, inability meet their basic needs, and absence of employment opportunities. Secondary displacement is another big concern where even the return population are not able to resettle and have to cope with another displacement journey. Across entire Iraq, out of displacement population living out of camps, 36% percent are in need of livelihood assistance. The absence of this assistance is compelling the population into adopting negative coping strategies. The MCNA conducted by REACH in September 2018 revealed that 62% of IDPs are buying food on credit or borrowed money, 50% spending savings, 44% selling household property, and 40% reducing expenditures on non-food items. The same assessment found 62% of households with a total monthly income less than their monthly expenditure. The relying of debts is seen high among IDPs and primary reasons reported for taking debts shelter (e.g. rent and utilities), food, health care, and purchasing productive assets (http://www.reachresourcecentre.info/system/files/resource-documents/reach_irq_factsheet_mcna_idp_out_of_camp_sept2018_0.pdf). The IDPs who are out of camp reported to remain in the current location in next 12 months, i.e. 65%. The reason for this non-return in near future relates mostly to above mentioned challenges at their place of origin. According to emergency livelihood cluster 2019 PIN-HNO data, Ninewa is on top with IDPs seeking employment or some type of income source, where 45% of IDPs out of camp are in need of livelihood support. In total, 1.3 people are in need of livelihood emergency livelihood assistance of which IDPs accounts for 3,99,536 Providing immediate income generation support to these segment of the vulnerable population is identified as one of urgent need. Absence of this support can lead to worsening their living conditions and can raise to another humanitarian crisis.

Activities

Project objective: Provide immediate access to income to support highly vulnerable returnees and host communities to recover their livelihoods and strengthen resilience. Output: # of vulnerable returnees and host population that have emergency asset replacement grants to restore income generating activities In alignment with the strategy objective of HRP 2019, ‘Increase access to basic services: for the most vulnerable people and ensure responsible transition for prolonged humanitarian needs through durable solutions’ Oxfam proposed activities will contribute toward the strategic objective 1 by providing mostly vulnerable IDPs out of camp immediate access to productive assets to enable them earn income to meet their basic needs. This proposed activity will also be meaningful toward responsible transition through durable solutions by building a link and base for humanitarian and long term recovery efforts. As per the emergency livelihood objective for 2019, which states, ‘’provide immediate access to income support to highly vulnerable displaced persons living in camps, temporary, formal and informal settlements in order to assist and facilitate safe return and strengthen resilience’’, Oxfam planned intervention is based on the indicator 2 of the emergency livelihood cluster, i.e. vulnerable displaced persons have emergency assets recovery grants to restore income generation activities. Oxfam under this proposed project intends to implement emergency asset replacement support, cash/in-kind, for the most vulnerable conflict affected returnees and host community members in Ninewa, Telafar and Kirkuk, Hawija. The proposed activities, i.e. emergency asset recovery grant will restore the livelihood options and ultimately contribute toward regaining of immediate income generating opportunities for the most vulnerable affected segment of the population. Through this, IDPs will be able to restart their income generation activity and protect their already depleting households and productive assets with protection from adopted negative coping strategies. Similarly, emergency asset recovery grant will enable the vulnerable households to replace their lost productive assets & contribute positively in protecting any further deterioration of existing livelihoods options. People unable to meet their basic expenses relying on selling their productive assets & using other damaging coping strategies which is strongly harming their future livelihoods. Providing this support at this stage is the right time which can save this population to ultimately fall into another humanitarian crisis. The project beneficiaries will use these grants to purchase small and quick productive items like, tri-cycles and fuel to sell small marketable items as street hawkers, purchase small livestock; chicken, sheep, goats, or fodder for existing livestock; TV, AC, refrigerators, plumbing/electric repair tool kits etc. While doing a cost/benefit analysis, the cost per beneficiary is relatively small compared to the benefit of the support. This support acts as a trigger to restart income generating of target population building on their existing capacity and any available resources. The unutilized resources will be made functional and productive, for example, if a person has a technical skill but does not have tool kit or equipment, that person can get benefit from this support and start earning income. This can also lead to multiple benefits where restart of income activity can open opportunities for target population in area of return. Further, it ensures that the targeted beneficiaries are able to sustain themselves for longer term, as opposed to being dependent on hand outs. Lastly, this also plays a big role in restoring dignity for the recipients. In order to implement the proposed intervention, Oxfam will conduct following activities. 1. Conduct geo-profiling in target districts to identify key priority areas with high concentration of returnees population.2. Conduct baseline assessment in project targeted areas. 3. Conduct rapid market assessment in targeted areas to decide on transfer modality and transfer mechanisms. (At first level, all secondary sources will be considered). 4. Conduct household vulnerability assessment in selected districts of Ninewa and Kirkuk Governorate.5. Identification and registration of most vulnerable families (returnees, host communities) for emergency assets replacement assistance.6. Work with selected beneficiaries on selection of type of assets. 7. Disbursement of emergency asset replacement grants or in-kind support for identified families.8. Conduct post distribution monitoring.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. The proposed project is covering only returnees and host communities so there can be risk of increased tensions between IDPs and other segment of the target population. The conflict has impacted all segment of Iraqi population and all targeted areas have IDP communities in protracted displacement living in poor condition and need of assistance. 2. Political and security situation does not allow access to beneficiaries and to the geographical area selected for implementation. This risk includes direct exposure / danger from the conflict. 3. Oxfam creates additional vulnerabilities for women, due to family or community backlash once they start earning money and traditional gender roles and responsibilities are challenged.

Mitigation Measures

Project aligns with Oxfam Iraq country strategy goal 3 “Protecting the vulnerable conflict-affected civilians and their rights: women, girls, boys and men are safer, better protected and have their rights upheld through a combination of safe programming, advocacy and campaigning, and protection programming”. Oxfam across all project areas have protection team supporting protection mainstreaming across its all programming and implement stand-alone protection initiatives. Oxfam response team in Ninewa and Kirkuk closely works with protection team to refer protection cases that come across during project implementation for further follow up and referrals. To incorporate the principles of do no harm in programming, livelihood team together with protection assess and identify the protection concerns of the affected population, consult community members and develop joint processes to ensure safe and fair access for women, disabled and elderly. This allows Oxfam to collaborate with family members to assist those at risk and have a reporting system in place in case participants felt their safety is threatened due to receiving humanitarian assistance such as domestic violence, increased risk of theft etc. Families which include people with chronic illness, lactating/pregnant women, elderly at risk, person with disabilities and individual with any other localized vulnerability receive higher score during need assessment subsequently increasing the opportunity to receive humanitarian assistance. Most vulnerable being at the forefront of protection Oxfam provides accommodating spaces at distribution sites and expedited service, as not to inflict undue burden for vulnerable population. For this project, Oxfam will ensure aforementioned protection protocols are adequately followed and will further provide protection session attached to livelihood sessions to enhance the self-protection capacities of participants and assist them to claim their rights. Oxfam hotline number will be provided to affected population to raise their protection concerns and seek necessary advice from Oxfam protection team. Below are some immediate remedies to identified protection risks:1. Oxfam will work closely with different stakeholders including government entities and local leaders from the host population to ensure that project does not cause any tension among different set of population groups, especially IDPs and host communities. Project committees will be formed consists of members both from IDPs and host communities which can facilitate the relationship between IDPs and host.2. Oxfam is currently operating at Security Level 2 in KRI and 3 in GoI (out of 5) controlled areas in Ninewa. Oxfam has adopted a country-specific Security Management Plan, which includes an evacuation plan and outlines key risks, mitigating actions and operating procedures. The plan is regularly updated according to changes in the security situation. Key risks identified in the plan include: Road traffic accidents and petty crime; Sexual harassment of staff, particularly females; Terrorism (injury/death/damage to assets from IEDs); and UXO/ERW (injury/death/damage to assets)Should a change in the security situation in the area prevent it from conducting direct implementation, Oxfam will adapt activities and follow-up to be led by local partners in areasOxfam will work closely in partnership with local partners and other stakeholders, and with advice from Oxfam’s gender team and protection team. Assets delivery can build resilience and empower women however they can also increase vulnerability to risks, including gender based violence. Women and men will be consulted regarding women’s safe participation in all project activities, and a conflict-sensitive and ‘do no harm’ approach will be followed. A gender risk analysis will be developed by Oxfam’s gender team at the start of the project, and regularly updated throughout implementation.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Oxfam has strong principles and protocols in working together with affected population in all stages of project implementation (project designing, planning, monitoring and evaluation). Particularly for this project, a standard accountability and transparency mechanism comprising of series of activities such as community sensitisation/community mobilisation, formation of project support committee, community feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) will be established/enhanced to ensure quality and continuous community involvement in project design, project implementation, decision making and maximising representation of women and marginalised segment of population. Further, sensitisation events prior to disbursement of asset replacement grants will enhance women participation in project and group dynamics and provide women opportunity to connect with market. At the beginning of project, information on processes and criteria of beneficiary selection will be circulated through community sensitisation events (covering at least 70% of population) to ensure informed decisions and choices, are made by affected population and dialogues with affected population are facilitated. The beneficiary selection criteria will be refined jointly with the communities to ensure the relevance and acceptance of targeting. A gender balanced project support committee (PSC) will be formed to guide beneficiary identification, oversee and guide project implementation process and take part in the activities of the project. Further, establishing effective CFRM will ensure community feedback and complaints are received, addressed and streamlined. CFRM will have three system components comprising of a) providing information to communities and beneficiaries, b) receiving feedback and documenting and c) responding to feedback. While setting up CFRM cultural norms, accessibility (in person, Oxfam hotline, IDP call centre), safety and confidentiality will be considered, for an example sensitive complains will directly go to CFRM focal person from MEAL team, protection concerns will be referred to protection team and beneficiaries will have flexibility to register complains and feedback anonymously. The views of affected populations will also be consolidated to improve relevance in programming through various means such as Oxfam hotline, UNOPS IDP call centre, post distribution monitoring (PDM) reports and end line results. Baseline, end line and PDMs will also be conducted to measure the impact of project interventions on lives of beneficiaries served. Program Quality Visits will be undertaken on a bi-monthly basis to ensure quality standards are met. Suggestion boxes will be installed in project location to receive feedback from targeted audiences. The feedback received from various sources will be collected, analysed and informed to adopt any changes in programming. Besides, Oxfam will closely work with UNOPS call centre to address the complaints and requests received through referrals. Lastly, Oxfam will work together with protection actors, local authorities, community leaders and other concerned stakeholders and ensure humanitarian safeguarding principles are followed and no harm is made to socioeconomic, environmental and cultural aspects of society.
