Project Summary

The impacts of the economic crisis and food insecurity have created a domino effect on the malnutrition rate, particularly among under-five children and pregnant and lactating women. The proposed project is aimed at improving the well-being of conflict-affected households in the neediest households in the proposed localities, enhancing community access to integrated nutrition services
Under this action, Relief Internation(RI) proposes implementing emergency primary nutrition care for 50,382 displaced and the vulnerable host communities in Zamzam IDP camp in El-Fasher, Mellit, Al-Malha, Sara-Umra, Kekbkabiya, Al-Tawisha and Kalamindo localities located in North Darfur; and in Abujabra, Abusinidra, El-Fardous, and Bahr al-Arab localities in East Darfur; and Damazine, Roseries, Kurmuk, Geizan, and Bau localities in Blue Nile State.
RI’s approach includes an Integrated Targeted Supplementary Feeding (TSFP) for 34,384 moderately malnourished children 6-59 months and 6,860 PLWs, and 9,138 Outpatient Therapeutic Feeding (OTP) for severely malnourished children of 6-59 months; and Food-based prevention of moderate acute malnutrition as preventative care among PLWs and under two-year children who are likely to rapidly drift into acute malnutrition. To ensure sustainability, RI also proposes the implementation of Infant and Young Child Feeding practice(IYCF).
The approach proposed ensures services are adapted to the needs of vulnerable groups mainly IDPs and vulnerable host communities in localities with high needs of malnutrition. Relief International (RI) appreciates that many cases of malnutrition are due to chronic limited food insecurity and livelihood options, poor Wash services, high morbidities, and poor IYCF practices and will ensure collaboration between health and nutrition sectors to support access to quality primary health care services; and through its WASH sector, RI will support the target communities to increase hygiene awareness through campaigns.

1 - Needs

Years of conflict, recurrent climatic shocks, and d COVID-19 pandemic marked by high inflation of goods and services continue to affect the lives and livelihoods of many Sudanese. The situation is worsening and now over 9.3 million people are facing severe hunger, in a country with already high malnutrition rates. According to HRP 2020, approximately 2.7 million children suffer from malnutrition including 522,084 children under age 5 that are severely malnourished. With the surging inflation, and annual rates of 212 percent in September 2020, more and more people are unable to meet their basic needs, as high inflation continues to erode families’ purchasing power, thereby creating food insecurity. An average local food basket takes up at least 75 percent of household income (UNOCHA, October 2020). The 2018 S3M (FMOH) assessment identified numerous factors that cause malnutrition in North Darfur. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate according to MUAC results in El Fasher and Mellit stands at 15.58% and 20.49% respectively, whereas SAM stands at 3.18% and 4.38% respectively (Draft 2018 S3M II). Overall, North Darfur still presents dire needs of malnutrition with 16.1% GAM and 2.95% SAM. The S3M II in Abujabra and Bahr El Arab localities in East Darfur shows GAM rates of 8.87% in Abujabra and Bahr El Arab Localities in East Darfur, although there was an influx of South Sudanese refugees since the S3M, who now reside within these localities and do not have access to lifesaving servicesThe key aggravating factors contributing to malnutrition are limited food intake, poor WASH services, poor IYCF practices, and poor dietary diversity. Without intervention, the health and nutrition situation will deteriorate, particularly for children under 5years and PLW. A comprehensive response is needed to address disease outbreaks, poor hygiene and infant and young child feeding practices, lack of water and sanitation facilities, and limited access to diverse foods.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Through this project, RI will continue to respond to crises with an integrated nutrition package with basic primary nutrition services that include SC, TSFP, OTP, and FBMAM for the vulnerable children under 5 years of age, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Three additional project components include: (1) community health promotion and IYCF, (2) Micro-nutrient supplementation, and (3) skills transfer to service delivery providers, which are aimed to ensure sustainability and long-term capacity for preventing and addressing acute malnutrition at all levels, from the members of vulnerable communities to the state-level ministry of health personnel in the three states. Furthermore, the proposed intervention will mitigate the secondary impact of the COVID19 pandemic on sustaining essential WASH and protection services, including gender-based violence responses within its nutrition intervention. This project will mainly have two key outputs including: Output 1: Increased and maintained recovery rate of cure of malnourished individuals to over 75%, Death rate to below 5%, & Default rate to below 15% (Minimum Sphere standards) for boys, girls admitted in the OTPs in the targeted localities.Output 1.2: Enhanced knowledge to staff on CMAM and communities through the promotion of IYCF, Kitchen garden, NIPP cycles, and Hygiene awareness. To achieve these outputs, RI will undertake activities includes:• Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW• Refurbishment of SC/OTP/TSFP nutrition facilities.• Provision of RUTF/RUSF• Support routine screening and Mass MUAC screening campaigns for active case finding, through various community mobilization techniques (Outreach workers, Community-owned resource person, Caretaker SAM mothers).• Referral service for SAM with complication case;• Micronutrient supplementation• Establish mother support group for the promotion of IYCF• Conduct training for nutrition workers, community volunteers on CMAM, IYCF, etc.• Conduct routine health education sessions on various topics on infection prevention and control to individuals (men, women, boys, and girls, as well as on mother’s role to support community health and hygiene, and CoVID‐19, floods, and diseases outbreak preparedness and responses‐related topics.• Conduct SMART survey in Abujbra locality• Training of staff and communities on NIPP• Formation of NIPP cycles within the community• Distribution of hygiene kits for SAM Household• Conduct IYCF awareness campaigns• Promotion of Kitchen gardens among the household of recovered SAM cases

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI’s programming takes into consideration that in situations of conflict-related displacement, women and girls, as well as men and boys, are often subject to heightened risks of GBV. The legacy and consequences of violent conflict can also increase intra-family violence due to a number of factors: individual and social trauma; changes in gender roles and in notions of masculinity; generalized impunity and breakdown of the rule of law; and increased tensions and frustration caused by economic stress, challenging living conditions, and the lack of opportunities. RI takes these gender dynamics into account during the design of its programs. RI ensures that all interventions are aligned with all applicable international standards, including but not limited to the GBV Guiding Principles and the Child Protection Minimum Standards. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the region in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries, including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy. Promoting equitable access to nutrition services will ensure that the project reaches the most vulnerable members of the targeted communities. Nutrition services focus on CU5 and PLWs, which are a subset of the population requiring protection mainstreaming across every activity due to their heightened vulnerability in situations of protracted conflict. This is particularly true in Sudan where very high levels of GBV are prevalent. I will actively encourage the recruitment of female ICVs and Nutrition staff but accepts that it is difficult to recruit a gender-balanced team in the states due to social and cultural norms surrounding female employment. Lead mothers in the mother-to-mother support groups will go some way to addressing this as sensitive messaging will be able to be disseminated directly by them. During home visits, ICVs, CHWs, and Lead mothers will be required to work in pairs and as per RI safeguarding standards, no individual handling of children is allowed. RI will establish separate consultation areas for women and girls, where they will be offered privacy by way of screens, and at nutrition sites, PLW will be accompanied during screenings. When referrals of women and girls are made, RI will ensure they have a safe way of reaching the referral service. If not, RI will offer an ICV to accompany them.RI also will ensure communities are reporting their complaints and feedback through existing accountability channels including hotlines, suggestion boxes.

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries, including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy.Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices, complementarity in complaint responses such as referrals of GBV cases; and strengthening capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedbacks to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and, enhance program recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle, etc. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program designing and adjustment, budget allocations, and programming in the different sectors.
