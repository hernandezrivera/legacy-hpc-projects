Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian needs overview 2021 of Somalia, 1.4 Million people are need for humanitarian assistance, 1.1 million of whom are non-displaced persons where more than 300k school aged children live in the IDP camps. An estimated 893,000 people have been displaced in Somalia between January and August 2020, 633,000 food insecurity, 177,000 conflict and 71,000 by drought. Galkayo and Galdogob districts of Mudug region and Adado and Abudwaq districts of Galgadud region are among the areas hardly hit by multiple shocks including conflicts, drought, floods and dessert locus compounded with Covid-19 pandemic.
According to the Joint multi-cluster needs assessment(JMCNA) report issued in September 2018, Somalia has one of the world’s lowest school enrolment rates as a result of a weakened education infrastructures and systems due to decades of neglect, conflicts and recurring national disasters such as the drought that culminated in widespread population displacement and the paralysis of social services including Education. Despite the political progress and the ongoing endeavors to strengthen government institutions, the Ministry of Education (MoE) of Somalia continues to lack the capacity to deliver basic education services for IDPs, children living in areas with ongoing conflict and other groups living under the most challenging circumstances
According to the 2021 HNO of Somalia, a total of 81,018 Children living in Adado and Abudwaq districts of Galgadud region; and Galkayo and Galdogob districts of Mudug region are in need for humanitarian assistance including emergence education for school age children (Adado-PIN 5,832, Abudwaq-PIN 6,252, Galkayo-PIN 54,308, Galdogob-PIN 14,626). Addressing these needs and ensuring all children are enrolled in a safe and protective learning environment with a good quality of teaching is an urgent and pressing need to ensure safety and continuity of education. The proposed project will address access and retention barriers to education for children affected by crises and emergencies in the above mentioned four districts Galgadud Mudug regions of Somalia.
Though this project, Mercy Corps will provide direct emergency education support to 6000 (45% girls) displaced and non-displaced school aged children in 20 schools within the target districts with the consideration of 10% disability inclusion as per the cluster guidelines. The project will address the negative effects of hunger through school feeding during school days in the target schools to retain children in school and boost their ability to concentrate during class lessons. In addition, the project seeks to support emergency water supply in targeted schools through the installation of water systems and provision of access to gender segregated sanitation facilities to create a safe, conducive and protective learning environment for children. The project will integrate hygiene and COVID-19 awareness sessions into the project activities through hygiene trainings, distribution of facemasks and hand washing soaps. Similarly, the project will undertake the installation of temporary learning spaces and rehabilitation of existing school classrooms and other essential facilities to improve the learning environment. Moreover, the project will be intentional in integrating child protection measures in its intervention strategy through training of teachers and CECs on Child protection, SGBV and Psychosocial support. The trained teachers and CECs will in turn provide child protection awareness sessions to their respective school children. In addition, Mercy Corps will design school’s classrooms, temporary learning spaces, latrines and other school essential facilities in a way that provides equal opportunities for able and non-able children, to ensure the facilities provide equal access to every child and allows everyone receives similar experiences.

Project Activities

Mercy Corps will align its strategy with that of the cluster on two strategic approaches including retention of crisis-affected school children and increased access to education for vulnerable displaced children and children in host communities. The project focuses on schools as an entry point for life-saving assistance to vulnerable children and is integrating the provision of WASH and protection services at the school level. The project will further pay increased attention to school-aged children living with disabilities and response interventions will be designed to enhance their access and inclusion to education. Where and when possible, the project will prioritize the most sustainable solutions and engage with development actors to bridge the humanitarian-development divide. This includes activities to strengthen the capacity of partners and MoE staff in response planning and coordination. The project response is aligned with the MoE Education Sector Strategic Plans and is aiming to complement the efforts of the government to strengthen the capacity of the education system to respond to the impact of the crisis. Mitigation Measures

1a. Protection of civilians: Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area• Identify safe residential locations and avoid placing facilities near military installations and conflict frontlines.• Consider safety risks associated with landmines, explosive remnants of war (ERW), busy roads, exposed wires and dangerous materials on the ground. • Situate play areas in clearly visible and safe locations • Train teachers in first aid (including psychological first aid) and that first aid kits are available/equipped. • Classes should take place in daylight hours giving children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes • Consult women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents to understand their needs and preferences for school location, design and education services. • Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response • Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and hold discussions with groups of children that could face added difficulties. • Collect and use data dis aggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and priorities the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups (refer to Vulnerable Categories guidance note).4d. Protection mainstreaming: Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms• Establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning schools to advocate against attacks on schools, child recruitment by armed groups or armed forces and military use of schools • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct • Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training. • Enhance the capacity of teachers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.
