Project Summary

Due to the conflict in Sudan since the 15th April, 2023, humanitarian needs have increased dramatically across all sectors. Repercussions of economic collapse, fueled by disrupted supply chains, mass displacement, and the destruction of critical infrastructure have triggered a surge in the prices of food, rent, and basic commodities. The humanitarian landscape has witnessed a 57% increase in the number of people in need (OCHA), with 37% of the population in a state of severe food insecurity (IPC). With 65% of displaced people currently residing in host communities (IOM-DTM), sharp increases in rental costs are a growing concern, as well as the ability for IDPs living in collective gathering sites to meet key non-food item (NFI) and WASH needs. In response to these significant and evolving needs, Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) is a priority modality of assistance. Market assessment findings indicate functioning markets in over 95% of surveyed areas (REACH), MPCA offers a significant advantage in providing greater flexibility for recipients to address their specific and evolving needs and affected populations have themselves identified MPCA as a key priority. The value of the MPCA modality within the Sudanese context is also underscored by the security landscape, as humanitarian access to areas with the highest needs is at best unstable and often fully restricted. As such, remote cash transfers offer strong potential to reach vulnerable populations not directly accessible to humanitarian actors.
This project aims to continue Acted's efforts to offer timely, impactful, and flexible assistance to conflict-affected populations through MPCA, in complementarity with its multisectoral operations. As such, MPCA will constitute a core element of Acted’s multisectoral mobile CCCM assistance package to displaced individuals and in-need host communities. Where Acted does not have direct programmatic access, such as Al Jazeera and Khartoum, MPCA will be leveraged as a primary modality of remote assistance to conflict-affected communities. This will be implemented in close collaboration with local NGOs and CBOs with on-the-ground access to the targeted communities, to enhance follow-up, beneficiary engagement, local community participation, and activity monitoring. Beneficiary identification will be conducted alongside with local communities, other INGO actors (notably the Cash Consortium of Sudan, of which Acted is a member), and the relevant clusters, to ensure MPCA support reaches the most in-need individuals whilst avoiding duplication of assistance. Acted will prioritise blanket provision of support within specific localities level, as informed by multiple data sources including but not limited to the HNO Severity Ranking and People in Need, as well as internal rapid needs assessments. Blanket targeting thus prioritises conflict sensitivity and a “do no harm” approach, by mitigating tensions within communities that arise due to targeting based on individual status. Acted is partnering with multiple Financial Service Providers (FSPs) to maximise sustained coverage, according to the security situation and viability of different cash transfer modalities across the targeted areas. Modalities of cash transfer vary according to security, access, and the needs of affected individuals within the targeted localities. Where access is possible, direct cash distribution to individual households, in partnership with local stakeholders and community representatives (in particular, representatives of people with specific needs), will be implemented. Prior to the finalization of direct distribution arrangements, a gender and safety assessment will be conducted for each distribution to ensure targeted households have safe and equal access to MPCA support. In all cases, Acted’s MEAL staff will conduct beneficiary verification and post-distribution monitoring to ensure that MPCA sufficiently reaches and meets the needs of the targeted households.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographicgroups and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which isembedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach.Preparation Phase: - Before the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to addressprotection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women,PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs). Program Implementation Phase: - Actedmainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and distributions. - waiting time and safety during travel to distributions will be assessed through a gender and safety assessment conducted by a designated focal point within Acted’s field staff teams. Accountability: - Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposedintervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design. Human resources: - Acted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency StandingCommittee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of theProgram Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to coordinationthrough a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. - For the intervention, Acted will set up a gender- balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment, improve protectionmainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, Acted will promote an equal gender participation.

4 - AAP & PSEA

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Acted has an experienced and independent MEAL unit which will establish an Acted Feedback Mechanism (AFM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints, and give feedback in a secure way, and receive responses in a timely manner. A dedicated member of staff will be responsible for managing the AFM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback safely and confidentially. Dedicated member of staffs responsible for managing the AFM will be permanently based in the Area office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during the implementation of the project, target communities will be sensitised on the AFM and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. The AFM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries and build trust between Acted and the community, paying specific attention to gender-related concerns and improve the quality of programs, whilst aiming to mitigate and/or prevent any adverse social, environmental, or health risks.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through the Acted Feedback Mechanism (AFM). Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment: - Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. - Training program related to safeguarding issues. - Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7,000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish the Acted Feedback Mechanism (AFM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated member of staff will be responsible to manage the AFM in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. The AFM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaints investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
