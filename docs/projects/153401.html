Project Summary

Yirol East county is located in the southern part of former Lakes State. The county has seven Payams with an estimated population of 112,0931. The population of Yirol East County practice farming, livestock keeping and fishing for their livelihood and land dispute and cattle raiding. The IPC of February 2018 projected that between May and July 2018, Yirol East would be in emergency phase (IPC level 4. In March and April 2018 there has been a high influx of IDPs (reportedly to be around 28,000 people) in Yirol East County. Their original home area is in Maper county (part of Rumbek North). The IDPs were displaced as a result of boarder and Land disputes. From several payams in Rumbek North County, including Alor, Madol, Malueth, Maper, Meen and Wurieng whose Properties were also destroyed to the extend you can even observe some undressed women and children. In Yei Central Equatoria State an inter-agency team compressing of eleven humanitarian agencies visited Mugwo County, about 23 kilometers south of Yei town to assess the situation of large influx of people that fled conflicts in Kenyira and surrounding area of Mugwo county in October 2018. Two UN agencies and Nine NGOs participated in the assessment. They include UN OCHA, IOM, TDH, ACROSS, AAH-I, CEFORD, UMCOR, IPCS, SSDO, CAHO and ARD-African. According to assessment report Shelter is critical gap with most of the population sleeping in the open, under trees and a few under makeshift shelter made of palm thatches that barely prevent water from entering within when it rains. The few that were met had corrugated roof on their home are forced to accommodate some others without homes, forcing an average family of between ten and fifteen to share a single room. It was reported that IDPs are mostly women & children families have no utensils and those with saucepan use it to cook their cassavas/yams and put them on leaves when they are ready in order to use the saucepan for preparing their sauce. Women particularly made passionate appeal for kitchen sets and shelter materials. Other outstanding items required are carpets, blankets, mosquito nets
Distribution of tarpaulin/plastic sheets and non-food items—kitchen sets, carpets, blankets and mosquito nets amongst others. People have returned to their pre-war homesteads following multiple displacement and will need construction tools to settle in. In Pibor Jonglei the main driver of the crisis is cattle raiding. Cattle are central of all community livelihoods but also to their social and cultural systems. The proliferation of guns, the commercialization of cattle and rising bride prices made cattle raiding more violent and more lucrative which increased the insecurity among communities in the area. The current incident mainly affected to two Payam as Lokoromach, Marpoz, Kambolok, Kermeth and Gei of Likuangole County. This has led to massive displacement of about 17,000 populations in different locations of two Payams as Lokormach, Marpoz and Kermeth under Lokormach Payam and Kerimeth, under Gei Payam.The community chiefs/leaders within the community reported that Cattle raiding is a typical incident in this location but this incident was different than the past due to volume of size cattle raiding.

Needs

Plan International has ongoing humanitarian projects in education, food assistance and livelihoods, nutrition and child protection.The project primary focus is to support vulnerable communities and ensure that the most vulnerable persons are the Centre of humanitarian response to the population. The IDPs fled their home area after all their houses were burnt according the assessment done on the 26th July – 4th August 2018 in Eastern Lakes and early October in Yei Central Equatoria 2018. They fled with their complete families and their cattle with only some basic items. The host population is sharing their resources and items with the IDPs who are living together with them., these items shared are shelters. Some old plastic sheets and blankets were given to the IDPs by individuals from the host population. The IDPs who are living in the cattle camps have access to less NFI items as they don’t live with the hosts and have no utensils for cooking and are using empty gallons /containers that were used to store cooking oil which also has got some challenges with taste and smell of food, they fabricated mosquito nets from pieces of cloth, they are sleeping on leaves collected from coconut tree, some of them made beds out of wooden poles and grass. The children mentioned they had a very hard time when it was raining very hard and they had to sleep outside. From the FGD the IDPs are mentioning that their most urgent need (besides food) is plastic sheets so they can build a proper shelter. The project will assist communities in desperate needs of NFI and shelter materials to provide immediate lifesaving support and improve their lives to cope with recurrent shock and build resilience. In order to have effective and efficient response to the needs, Plan will continue with its current mobile response in Central Equatoria and Lakes to reach many people who are in need of NFI and emergency shelter. This will include need assessments, mapping of communities, coordination meetings, mobilization of resources and community awareness and responding to the immediate needs of the population through a well-coordinated cluster approach. The project will significantly contribute to addressing the shelter and NFI needs of 12,118 individuals (2,020HHs, 6 individuals per HH) of the most vulnerable IDPs and affected residents. The NFI and Emergency Shelter project will support the most in need families and individuals thereby providing reasonable shelter by using locally available materials such as poles and bamboos combined with plastic sheet and NFI kits. The project will consider a consultative and collective approach with humanitarian and local actors in the design of vulnerability criteria to the identification and selection of the project beneficiaries. Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Plan international is keen to make the community and people affected by crisis participate in decisions that affect them. From the onset of the project, Plan will institute community engagement, dialogue and make them understand and own the project, its objectives and outputs/outcomes. Plan International will ensure that a mechanism is put in place for the beneficiaries to provide feedback and complaints and whenever it is possible these complaints and/or feedback are addressed. A feedback and complaint mechanism will be strengthened for the community to inform Plan International what they think about the project, what they like and their reservations and how they would like the project to improve. This will be achieved by establishing a complaint desk during our distribution and other activities. Committees will be set in all the target location to hear from the community members about the prospects and improvement of the project. Plan International has built relationship with communities in Yei Eastern Equatoria and Lakes State over the years. With its presence in Yei and Lakes, Plan will engage the communities routinely using its feedback mechanism framework to adjust activities based on the feedback from the communities and incorporate them into the programming.80"% of displaced population served with shelter and/or NFI reporting that assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible and participatory manner" (target: 80%) During the project cycle, the project team will work together with represented community focal persons and this group will also assist the community outreach volunteers in mobilization and quick dissemination of information and help clarify any misunderstandings that may arise because of the information gap. Committees and community members will be sensitized about the type and amount of items to be distributed for each beneficiary ahead of the distribution process.
