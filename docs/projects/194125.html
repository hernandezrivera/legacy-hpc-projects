Project Summary

ADRA will use it's extensive expertise in the area, and in the region to carry out Shelter programing that meets both emergency and protracted needs of vulnerable communities. The main goal is to provide conflict and disaster affected individuals in West Darfur, Blue and White Nile and Khartoum with lifesaving assistance. Currently, ADRA has projects in 5 states Khartoum, White Nile, Blue Nile, Central Darfur and West Darfur. All of them have faced hazard events such as flooding and intercommunal conflicts in different areas. This crisis directly affects the community, without exception of gender or age, triggering a dangerous and uncertain environment for the affected people, who suddenly loose their houses, jobs and, in some cases, their lives. ADRA looks for recovering their dignity through this project in terms of S/ NFI, complementing other WASH, FLS and Protection projects that have been developing in same areas.
Our purpose is to assist IDPs and returnees improving their currently emergency shelter through Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA). Additionally, we plan to provide shelter kits and NFI kits, for new IDPs, as we have been doing in other opportunities.
ADRA will be working in different localities of White Nile (Kosti, El Jabalian, El Salam, Rabak, and Tendalti), Blue Nile (all localities) and West Darfur (El Geneina, Jebel Moon & Kerinik). The emergency shelter kits will be provided to 5,000 households, benefiting more than 25,000 individuals. With the same number of beneficiaries, we are going to work for the NFI kits, benefiting 5,000 households as well. For those IDPs who needs to improve their emergency shelter, we plan to assist 2,000 households, benefiting around 10,000 individuals. Out of the all beneficiaries, ADRA aims at targeting 2000 HH that need to improve their emergency shelter will be assisted with cash for shelter grants .

1 - Needs

Shelter and NFI needs in Sudan continue to grow amidst an extremely stressed and complex operational environment. While political issues fuel tensions and polarization throughout the country, competition for land and resources add to the mix as one of the key drivers of the conflict. Environmental degradation and drought continue to cause shifts in migration patterns, pushing many nomadic groups to transition from pastoralism to the semi-sedentary lifestyle, forcibly occupying land, and property of others in the process. Seasonal flooding continues to affect communities across Sudan causing devastation to housing, infrastructure, services, agriculture, livestock, and various other livelihood assets (food stocks, NFIs, etc.).Only 16% of the population live in adequate dwellings, i.e., permanent shelters that provide sufficient levels of privacy, security, and protection from exposure. About 24% of people live in substandard shelter types, while the rest (60%) live in semi-permanent shelters. However, as much as 75% of HH reported their current shelters are damaged, with 22% reporting severe damage which translates to compromised structural safety or total structural collapse. Similarly, 85% of the population experiences issues with their shelter, with 25% experiencing 3 or more issues at the same time. This indicates that even families with access to more durable shelter options struggle with precarious living conditions and crumbling housing. The most prevalent shelter issue is leaking (76%) which is consistent with most reported damage types: cracked roof (54%) and partial roof collapse (18%). Consequently, household items reported as most needed are plastic sheeting (80%) and mosquito nets (73%) followed by torches/solar lamps (71%), blankets (65%), jerry cans (48%), kitchen sets (47%) and mattresses/sleeping mats (43%). In terms of other shelter issues reported, one fifth of the population experiences issues with flooding, inability to lock their shelter and lack of privacy.As a consequence of these crises, families live in poor humanitarian conditions where protection risks are heightened as a result of loss of shelter and property. In both contexts, affected people require tens/shelter kits as an emergency need. ADRA will respond to the emerging need of those affected by current flooding or conflict through a community-led response, which meets the immediate needs of households affected by one of these crises. ADRA will provide shelter materials and NFI, as well as pilot cash assistance especially for urban vulnerable households and returnees to help in rehabilitating their shelters and rental subsidies.

2 - Activities/Outputs

To respond to the emerging crisis, ADRA will carry out activities that aim at providing immediate assistance to the disaster affected households through an rapid and tailored approach: 1. Rehabilitation of communal shelters: This will be through a integrated response, especially with the WASH and protection sector. And will include the rehabilitation of doors, ceilings, rooms and other facilities. Especially for sites that are hosting a large number of IDPs. 2. Provision of Cash for shelter. ADRA has already started providing cash for shelter in White Nile State. This will be of two kinds, where urban displaced communities will be provided with rental subsidies, as well as shelter items based on a market assessment determining the availability of the items. 3. Distribution of Shelter-related and NFIs / Emergency Shelter Kits (ESK): In collaboration with local authorities in the target villages and with community committees, the ADRA team will select locations for the distribution. The distribution points will be selected based on their proximity to the targeted community as well as their security. ADRA will also ensure that distribution times and sites are suitable to the most vulnerable members of the community, particularly for females and disabled individuals.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ADRA Sudan will follow environment preservation approaches, providing environmentally friendly, durable, and locally sourced shelter solutions. The materials and construction methodology will be defined, selected, and adopted to prevent over-exploitation, pollution, and degradation of environmental conditions. The distribution of shelter materials will involve eco-friendly materials such as bamboo, reed mats, grass mats, with steel pillars for construction and rehabilitation of shelters. Bamboo, grass, and reed are growing in White Nile River. They are encouraged to be removed by the forest department because they are hindering boating and fishing activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this project, ADRA will maximize the positive impacts of shelter and NFI programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights. ADRA will build on our existing relationships with communities and build rapport with new communities, to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable in reaching out to them if and when issues arise to provide support to those who may not have any way to report issues; particularly surrounding GBV. ADRA uses its knowledge of the context in the project area, as well as consultations with local staff, in the design of gender-sensitive and tailored approaches. Both male and female community members will be consulted during the design of the project through focus groups as well as needs. During distributions, separate queues for women will be established, and females will be consulted on the most appropriate time and place for distributions. Tailored awareness methods will aim to be inclusive of different segments in the community. In order to successfully deliver the services in the area, ADRA will ensure that channels of communication continue to be open, regular, and accessible throughout the program cycle.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The feedback and complaint mechanism, will be used by ADRA as one of the main channels to counter fraud and aid diversion. In the event of families being assessed the Shelter/ NFI assistant and found to be lacking legal documents, cases will be referred to UNHCR and other protection partners. In the event of families having lost IDs (including IDs giving them access to basic service provision), cases will be referred to the respective issuing agencies. The current project design and geographic selection are coordinated closely with the cluster on the camp level and the block level with all partners involved. Multiple meetings will take place prior to the implementation of the action with all active partners in the field to avoid duplication with other programs, such as UNHCR Shelter/NFI activities. ADRA Sudan is an active member of the Cash Working Group (CWG) at the national level ensuring regular representation in meetings. Moreover, ADRA will strengthen the coordination of cash and voucher programming in the camps and ensure that standard operational procedures are in place for all partners and that minimum standards endorsed by the CWG are followed to avoid confusion and tension in camp communities.
