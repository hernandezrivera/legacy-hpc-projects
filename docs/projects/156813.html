Project Summary

The project aims at supporting people inside East Ghota with essential health care services
Activities will be implemented in 3 static centers in East Ghota (Saqba, Hamouria, Ein Tarma) which are under the sub district Kafar batna, AlTamayoz has already rented those 3 centers and they are ready to provide primary health care services
Planned activities will be in line with sector objective No. 1 (Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need.)
The main activities will be:
Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, and referral.
30000 patients (men, women, boys, girls) will benefit from medical consultations
20000 persons will benefit from mental health consultations
100 persons will benefit from trauma consultations
700 physical rehabilitation sessions will be provided to people in need
100 vaginal deliveries and 100 caesarian deliveries will be supported
500 patient will be supported with referrals for specialized treatment ( between levels of care )
Al Tamayoz will be responsible of deploying well qualified staff to assure the qualitative implementation.

Needs

Throughout 2018, health needs in Syria continued to be exacerbated by access constraints, damage to health facilities, and insufficient human resources. Many areas that witnessed escalation in hostilities and changes in control are suffering from disruption of critical health services, access constraints, and an increased need for humanitarian interventions. The health system struggles to respond to trauma and rehabilitation needs. High numbers of trauma-related cases continue to be reported across Syria. Females make up 72 per cent of people in need of health assistance in Syria, out of which 5.2 million women of reproductive age are in need of Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescence (RMNCH+A) services. Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. Thirty-seven per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41 per cent of Syria’s population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs); and at least 15 per cent of the population requires mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS), but the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after seven years of conflict is inadequate to cope with existing needs. (HNO draft 2019)Access and referral to specialist health services are similarly affected. This includes dialysis, and cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation. At least 15 per cent of the population of Syria is disabled. Forty-five per cent of people injured by the conflict in Syria are likely to face a permanent impairment and require specialist support which will far outlast the hostilities. (HNO draft 2019)According to HRP 2019 draft version, there are ( 31247) people in need for health care services in Kafar batna, Rural Damascus. And according to Al Tamayoz observation in this area from June 2018 till January 2019, there is lack of free of charge health care services, There are no DoH centers in Hamourim, EinTarm, and Kafar batna, with one center in Saqb that doesn’t have specialists and has no medicines. Saqba is now overcrowded with people because its buildings are less affected, so many families moved to it from nearby locations to spend the cold winter months, consequently, the need for medicines is becoming higher especially for children who are suffering from respiratory diseases due to the usage of wood as a source of heat. Elderly percentage is high, with an increased need for chronic diseases medications (hypertension, diabetes, rheumatoid…). Many people need referral because of trauma injury and referral for surgical operations, in addition to the services of laboratory tests, X-Ray, and incubation for new born children.The escalation of violence has led to increasing war related psychological trauma for children and their families. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area (Physical Rehabilitation Working Group, 2018).It is critical to ensure that health services are sufficiently linked to Child Protection interventions including the integration of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and other specialized protection services. It is increasingly critical to expand access to psychosocial support and recreational activities for vulnerable children, particularly in areas of high severity of need. (HRP 2019 zero draft). Parents and their children suffer from stress and psychosocial disorders due to the protracted nature of the crisis and are in need for MHPSS services.

Implementation of activities

The project aims at supporting people inside East Ghota with essential health care services Activities will be implemented in 3 static centers in East Ghota (Saqba, Hamouria, Ein Tarma) which are under the sub district Kafar batna, AlTamayoz has already rented those 3 centers and they are ready to provide primary health care servicesPlanned activities will be in line with sector objective No. 1 (Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need.)Activities will include: providing medical consultations in Pediatrics, internal medicine, gynecology, in addition to providing essential medicinesproviding mental health consultation, including individual and group counsellingsupport trauma consultationssupport people with special needs by physical rehabilitation sessionssupport referrals to specialized services (MRI, CT scan, laboratory test, surgical operations,..) through a well monitored mechanism to ensure the quality and efficacy of the service. Cases will be referred to public and private health facilities in Damascus noting that AlTamayoz has very special price offers that ensure the cost effectiveness.
