Project Summary

The project seeks to provide emergency and lifesaving nutrition services to vulnerable drought-affected populations living in Balcad district of Middle Shabelle. The intervention will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) together with children under the age of 5 years among host communities, IDPs and returnees in Balcad district where we are the primary rationalized partner, Wocca will operate one fixed outpatient therapeutic center and 3 mobile out- patient therapeutic programme teams targeting six drought-affected villages/settlements in Balcad district. The static facility will be established in Muki-dheere settlement with the highest population among the targeted locations while the three outreach teams will serve 5 locations namely; Kurshaale, Mareerey, Bulo sheikh, Bananey and Bulo Doonka villages. WOCCA will engage UNICEF, who will support the project by facilitating training of the project staff ,community health workers and hygiene promoters on nutrition screening, case identification and Integrated management of acute malnutrition(IMAM).The trained staff shall be deployed to the targeted villages, mobile and static OTPs where they will screen children under 5 and PLWs. Children under 5 with SAM conditions and no medical complications will be enrolled to the static and mobile OTPs for treatment while the cases with medical complications will be referred by the mobile clinics and CHWs to the SC in Waberi star village managed by WOCCA for nutrition rehabilitation. Children under 5 and PLWs with MAM conditions will be referred to TSFP center managed by SHARDO. The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements to pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of 5years through the static and mobile facilities.UNICEF has been supporting WOCCA with nutrition supplies and technical guidance on previous nutrition projects within the targeted locations and currently supporting our SHF funded project running in Daynille and Kaxda camps. Therefore, for this project WOCCA will also engage with UNICEF to provide nutrition supplies and technical support for management of SAM cases .

Needs

Malnutrition is one of the most persistent humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners between June and July 2018 (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2018 Post Gu Technical Release - 02 Sep 2018) indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia has continued to improve due to a combination of improved food security conditions, reduced outbreak of diseases and sustained humanitarian interventions. However, high level of acute malnutrition tends to persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes. There is no improvement in the overall current acute malnutrition prevalence compared to 2017/18 Deyr (median GAM of 13.8%). GAM prevalence is Critical (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed. Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is Critical (≥4-5.6). This affected population needs nutrition services to enhance accessibility of quality nutrition services in the integrated management of acute malnutrition. These include children under 5 (boys/girls) who are severely malnourished as well as the Moderately malnourished pregnant and lactating women. Psychological trauma, poor nutritional and physical health experienced due to loss of livelihoods and insecurity, limit the capacity of child care. PLW are also disproportionately affected by under nutrition due to increased physiological needs. In addition, as caretakers, women take extra activities to support household food security where male heads are absent often leading to disruptions in infant young child feeding and reduced care practices. For this reason, measures to prevent and treat malnutrition related morbidity and mortality cases will be implemented. In order to reduce the levels of malnutrition WOCCA will address cases among children under 5 and PLW, through systematic treatment inform of ready to use therapeutic foods, nutrient supplements as well as Basic Nutrition Service Package.

Activities

- Community mobilization and sensitization. - Community screening for malnutrition and referral to the appropriate component (i.e. Outpatient Therapeutic feeding Programme (OTP) or Stabilization Centre (SC) targeting children under five boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)- Treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children 0-59 month through Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (Outpatient care) and/or Stabilisation Centre (Inpatient care)done through distributions of Ready to Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTFs) and routine medicines; medical and nutritional monitoring of the patient- Treatment of moderately malnourished pregnant and lactating women through referral to targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP)- Monthly awareness events within the community to key persons (government leaders, village elders, religious leaders and women), on malnutrition causes, the misconceptions about nutrition and prevention mechanisms - Training on effective acute Malnutrition management for project staff and community health workers.- Provision of De-worming tablets and vitamin A supplements to boys& girls.- provision of Multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women and Iron supplementation for pregnant and lactating women

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

IDP's in Middle Shabelle have been displaced the past 2 to 5 years due to drought, clan conflict and floods in the area. Recent reconciliation meetings have opened the doors for most people to return to their villages of origin thus making the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions/resilience. In addition, Wocca working with DDG also supports processes to make traditional conflict resolution mechanisms more representative and capable of solving civil disputes and aligning with the formal justice sector in South-Central Somalia. Wocca with the approval of the middle shabelle region plans to uplift the Balcad stabilization center to an MCH which in addition to offering the normal nutrition services will also provide primary health care. systems strengthening will lead to resilient nutrition and health interventions able to withstand shocks in the future which this project aims to achieve through capacity development of the MoH and local structures like the community health committees and CHWs resulting to longer term interventions anchored at community systems. Sustainability/handover process is built into the project that will ensure smooth phase out process leading to the MoH and the local community taking over completely by end of project implementation. The entitlement of beneficiaries to services/utilities will be legally recognized.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs displaced by the floods, drought and the ongoing conflict in the targeted regions face risk of exclusion from accessing basic humanitarian services at the camps. Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs and minorities from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries, discrimination from host communities, lack of access to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement, IDPs and minorities lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. Women and child care organization will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Using participatory beneficiary targeting and selection approaches cognizant of age, diversity, disability, vulnerability and gender-specific needs, to ensure the most vulnerable and minority groups are targeted 2. Recruiting individuals from both the host, minorities and IDP populations in the implementation of the project in order to benefit the local economy as well as to ensure a non-discriminatory recruitment process (Do no harm) 3. Conducting sensitization meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles, more over the project has proposed mobile OTPs or outreach teams which will take services directly to the people

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The primary targets for the project are pregnant and lactating women (PLW) who are women by definition, they are disproportionately affected by under nutrition due to increased physiological needs. As caretakers, women take extra activities to support household food security, where male heads are absent often leading to disruptions in infant young child feeding and reduced care practices for children under five years of age who are by definition boys and girls. Women and girls in Somalia face exclusion, they are at increased risk of violence incase of civil unrest and may be unable to access assistance or make their needs known. They form a major part of the beneficiaries, to prevent risks of exclusion, Wocca abides the virtues of Accountability to Affected populations (AAP); WOCCA will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders During the sensitization meetings, the scope of the project, beneficiary type and selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement are discussed and agreed. Wocca will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries. Wocca will adhere to these principles of “do no harm” at all times throughout the project cycle by involving all categories of the community

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project location can be easily accessed by the local and international organizations and no incidents have been reported recently. Wocca has been operating in the project area over 10 years thus, the relationship between wocca and the community in the district is very strong, this has enabled wocca to implement many projects in the sectors of WASH, Nutrition and food security, this shows that wocca has cordial relationship with the communities in the regions where it intervenes, Wocca enjoys full access from all corners of the district including the government , civil societies and beneficiaries. we have main office in Mogadishu and Johwar, which allows easily to access the targeted locationsAs a matter of policy, Wocca ensures that conflict-affected people access emergency services by engaging clan elders, religious leaders and local authorities to gain access, community acceptance, enhance program awareness, protection of staff and to avert possible security risks. WOCCA employs participatory approaches by engaging IDPs, host communities and community leaders in order to avoid any unintended conflicts within the larger community. Women and child care organization also adheres to the principles of "Do no harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries. WOCCA is also working with DDG to support processes like traditional conflict resolution mechanisms that are more representative and capable of solving civil disputes and aligning with the formal justice sector in South-Central Somalia.
