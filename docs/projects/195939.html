Project Summary

DRC will deploy 2 highly mobile Multi-Task Teams (MTTs), to respond to urgent threats to physical safety, dignity, freedom of movement and access to humanitarian assistance within communities. Additionally, DRC will deploy 5 Teams focused on delivering Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to conflict-affected communities to further mitigate risks of explosive hazards. The proposed project aims at:
• Providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs of the most vulnerable (including women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of ERW in conflict) in hard-to-reach areas.
• Enhancing prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience
• Enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations
Tasks that will support the humanitarian response in the country will be prioritized through the following key activities:
• Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Technical Survey (TS) – NTS and will be conducted to target and prioritize clearance according to humanitarian need. Surveys will be conducted using a mixture of key informant interviews and household questionnaires held with the local population and authorities. NTS will determine the likely locations and density of ERW contamination in target locations, whilst assessing the impact of these hazards on vulnerable populations in any given location. TS (using detectors) will be used upon discovery of an area with a high concentration of explosive hazards.
• Manual Mine Clearance (MMC) / Battle Area Clearance (BAC) - the MTTs will be able to act as a MMC / BAC capacity, performing static clearance activities across larger, clearly defined areas (identified through the above data collection activities and technical survey as appropriate).
• Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) – once NTS has determined the nature of the threat, technical capacities within the teams will be deployed to remove and destroy identified hazards.
• Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) – all teams will contain a capacity to deliver risk messaging, toraise awareness about how to recognize dangerous items. as the risk education will emphasize safe practices that should be followed, and high risk behaviour that should be avoided in relation to mines and explosive remnants of war.

1 - Needs

The Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) presents the strongest opportunity for peace and post-conflict recovery in several years. However, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian needs. In 2022 an estimated 8.3 million people million individuals are in need of protection assistance and decades of conflict have left South Sudan heavily contaminated by landmines, munitions, and unexploded ordnance (UXO). According to The UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS) records, all known contamination currently amounts to an area of 16,125,908m2. According to Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) data from October 2022, there are 448 hazardous areas (HAs) identified across the country including 10,142,191m2 of land covered by minefields, 3,073,604m2 of mined roads, and 5,983,717m2 of land contaminated with cluster munitions and other ERW. Much of this contamination straddles the primary return routes for refugees in neighboring countries. While the overall security environment has improved in South Sudan, the situation remains volatile. Localized ethnic conflict and cattle raiding remains prevalent. To ensure that it can reach areas most in need, DRC will implement a roving response capacity which will rapidly deploy to areas where ERW poses a grave protection risk to communities. In close collaboration with the DRC Protection team, the DRC Mine Action team will target displaced and conflict affected populations through mapping and prioritisation systems that are designed to target the most vulnerable. Furthermore, the existing DRC infrastructure in Aburoc, Bentiu, Mayom, Juba, Magwi, Maban, Malakal, Akobo and Wau will enable DRC to rapidly deploy to priority locations identified by the Protection Cluster.

2 - Project activities

The overall goal of this project is to prevent and mitigate the physical risks posed by landmines and ERW through clearance and enhance preparedness and resilience through EORE messaging. The proposed intervention directly supports Protection Cluster Objective 1 by reducing vulnerabilities of women, men, girls and boys at risk of mortality through clearance. The intervention also supports Protection Cluster Objective 2 by increasing awareness of safe behaviors through EORE, thereby enhancing community level resilience against explosive hazards. Targeting considerations will also be adopted with the aim of supporting durable solutions aims under Cluster Objective 3, targeting clearance activities towards areas where the removal of landmines and ERW can support local integration, returns and relocation by easing strains on land access and use. To support the above objectives, DRC proposes to deploy 3 clearance teams focused on either enabling recovery or improving humanitarian access, depending on needs at any given time. 5 EORE teams will complement clearance responses as necessary, by targeting surrounding locations that have not yet received a clearance response. It should be noted that the locations included in the targeting below outline possible areas of intervention, rather than a list of every location to be targeted under the project. Exact target locations will be determined based on needs at the time of prioritization within the project period, with a focus on enabling the delivery of other sector activities supported through the HRP wherever possible. Combined, the teams will undertake the following activities: 1. Ensure the provision of critical assistance to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and / or priority geographical areas. 1.1: Carry out desktop research and engage with other stakeholders to identify population affected by conflict and by landmines or ERW, including restrictions on access to humanitarian assets or service delivery. 1.2: Conduct Non-Technical Survey (NTS) in severity areas 4 and 5 to inform clearance, EORE and overall humanitarian response. 1.3: Survey infrastructure and transit routes through NTS to facilitate the timely, effective and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance by other stakeholders. 1.4: Conduct meetings with humanitarian agencies to prioritise locations in terms of humanitarian access / service delivery, including warehouses, compounds, distribution sites and/or schools. 1.5: Conduct refresher training for DRC staff on NTS, technical survey / TS (using detectors) and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognizing the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age-specific daily routines and movement patterns. 1.6: Conduct (mobile) Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) / (static) Battle Area Clearance (BAC) / Manual Mine Clearance (MMC) to remove and destroy dangerous items. 2. Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. 2.1. Conduct EORE targeting at-risk women, men, boys and girls within displaced and host communities to promote greater awareness and safer practices in relation to landmine and explosive remnants of war 2.2: Conduct refresher training for DRC staff on EORE delivery, including key messaging, participatory learning methods and engagement strategies aimed at securing participation across different age and gender groups. DRC will monitor gender and age specific attitudes towards explosive hazards, with the ability to develop tailored risk reduction strategies where required. 3. Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other populations. DRC will retain the capacity to work in both static and highly mobile team formats.• Conduct desk research and survey in areas of return or displacement in severity areas 2, 3, 4, and 5 through NTS.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where men, women, boys, and girls are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities, which is achieved through sharing information, engagement with different segments of the community, and ensuring effective complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM). DRC operates several types of CFM to ensure that there are no barriers for different community members to access them. Examples of CRMs include information desks, telephone hotline, and household surveys. In the event that any challenges are identified by the community in the implementation of the project, DRC seeks to adapt the intervention where possible. Very young children, such as those five years and under, excluded from influencing the project due to communication challenges and to ensure adherence to protection and safeguarding principles.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DRC has IEC materials available in multiple formats that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaints mechanisms. DRC conducts regular awareness-raising with communities on the presence of the complaints and feedback mechanisms. As a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), DRC fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. Fundamental to all DRC activities is adherence to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM), achieved through engagement with the various community members and existing institutions. Through formal and informal interactions with representative groups and other community members, beneficiaries are empowered to inform the type of activities and services provided by DRC and ensure their implementation is relevant, effective, and responsive to the needs of displaced populations DRC engages different community representatives across all the project locations, including women, men, youth, elderly PWD, community leaders, and community-based committees. DRC ensures information reached and is received by all affected population through different mediums such as community leaders, community mobilizers, women and youth groups. DRC information desks are accessible and inclusive of everyone, either in-person or one-on-one meeting with DRC field staff on monitoring visits. To strengthen its accountability to the affected population, DRC establishes complaint and feedback mechanisms to provide community members and other stakeholders a platform to provide feedback on the activities. The exact complaint and feedback mechanisms will depend on the target location and activities being implemented but examples of complaint and feedback mechanisms DRC has established includes complaint and feedback desks at key project sites, hosting of focus group discussions to receive feedback from beneficiaries and installation of complaints and feedback boxes at static protection desks. Beneficiaries will be informed on the feedback mechanisms during community engagement activities, at the beginning of the intervention and throughout the project. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC will conducts regular awareness raising campaigns with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as provide information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats. DRC staff were trained and informed on the DRC Code of Conduct and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DRC has a zero-tolerance for cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. This has promoted staff accountability and transparency to persons of concerns in service delivery. DRC provide regular refresher PSEA training to the staff and to the community it serves, to ensure their knowledge of the appropriate channel of communications and reporting if encountered with a potential case. Under this project, DRC will have IEC materials stationed at strategic locations such as the information desk, community centers which include contact information on PSEA reporting mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC adopts an age and gender diversity (AGD) mainstreaming approach across all its programmes, meaning that it gives full consideration to AGD in the design, implementation, and monitoring of its activities. For example, under the project DRC’s Community Liaison capacity engages community leaders, religious leaders, women, men, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities on targeting considerations. To achieve this, existing representative structures are drawn on wherever possible (if initial survey deems that the community considers them to be legitimate). Further to this, DRC’s Community Liaison Coordinator visits each task site during prioritisation to determine additional vulnerable groups that may not be represented by traditional community structures. In addition, DRC engages with members from all segments of communities to solicit their feedback on the project to ensure that activities are tailored and meet the needs of different groups. For instance, when carrying out a training or awareness raising session, DRC obtains feedback from men, women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities to select an appropriate time. DRC also considers physical access issues for those with disabilities when selecting an activity site.
