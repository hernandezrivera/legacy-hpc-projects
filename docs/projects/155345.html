Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to reduce the number of cholera cases in 2019 by reinforcing coordination, epidemiological and community surveillance; improving laboratory capacities; improving cholera case management and infection prevention and control at institutional level; and through the use of reactive vaccination to reduce the risk of transmission. All these activities in support of the Ministry of Public Health and Population’s National Plan for the Elimination of Cholera 2019-2022.

Needs

In October 2010, the emergence of cholera in a naïve population of Haiti resulted in a complex humanitarian situation following the earthquake in January 2010. Cholera spread rapidly and with a high case fatality. As of mid-October 2018, a total of over 819,000 cases and 9,700 deaths due to cholera have been reported. Since the peak of the epidemic in 2011, an overall decrease in the annual number of cases has been observed. In 2017, 13,681 suspected cases represent the lowest annual number of cases reported to date and 2018 is on track to record an even lower number (3,402 cases as of 20 October 2018). However, there are cholera hotspots in the country, which facilitate the transmission of the disease, and are known to have recurrent outbreaks. These hotspots are primarily located in the departments of Artibonite, Centre, Ouest and Nord Ouest. As per the Global Roadmap to 2030 by the Global Task Force on Cholera Control (GTFCC), a targeted multi sectoral approach focusing on cholera hotspots, in conjunction with early detection, rapid response, case management, reactive vaccination, and effective coordination are essential to achieve cholera elimination. If efforts are not strengthened to continue the decreasing trend in suspected cholera cases, a significant recrudescence of cases may occur nationally due to the persistence of cholera hotspots. The Haitian Ministry of Public Health and Population (MSPP) and DINEPA (National Water and Sanitation Directorate) have set up a long term multisector National Plan for the Elimination of Cholera, aiming for cholera elimination for 2022. Currently, most Departmental Health Directorates’ are supported by data collected at institutional level to understand the dynamics of cholera transmission. However, in an emergency context, it is crucial to use individual data (line-listings) with information on the homes to identify cholera hotspots and vulnerable populations more precisely. This will allow to establish targeted interventions and reduce transmission. PAHO/WHO supported the MSPP with assistant epidemiologists working in 8 departments, but further efforts are needed to achieve the goal of better data collection. The community surveillance mechanism (SEBAC) done by Community Health Agents (ASCPs) is also an important element of cholera surveillance to detect outbreaks in vulnerable areas, more precisely in isolated areas prone to large outbreaks due to late detection and required travel time for interventions. Laboratory testing is crucial to differentiate cholera suspected cases from other diarrheal diseases. Improving this will guarantee more direct and appropriate interventions. Due to the efforts done by “labo-moto” nurses (in charge of collecting and transporting cholera samples to laboratories) the percentage of cholera suspected cases tested increased from less than 20% in 2016, to 80% in 2018. In order to obtain 100% of cholera suspected cases tested, “labo-moto nurses” work must continue. The use of oral cholera vaccine (OCV) is an integral part of cholera emergency response to reduce the risk of spread of the disease. Two doses can offer protection for up to 3 years with an effectiveness of 76%. Financial resources are necessary for the provision of vaccines and the logistics for these reactive campaigns. Regarding the management of cholera cases, although the number of cases and deaths has decreased significantly in 2018, the capacities of cholera treatment facilities (both integrated and non-integrated) were frequently surpassed, requiring the support of partners for the provision of medicines, supplies, human resources, etc. WaSH and structural aspects of these facilities are often neglected, requiring rehabilitations and resources to guarantee infection prevention and control. Furthermore, in isolated areas with lack of access to a health institution, temporary cholera treatment facilities are required to be able to provide care and avoid deaths.

Activities or outputs

1- To strengthen epidemiological surveillance and laboratory capacity to detect and test every suspected case of choleraEpidemiological surveillance and laboratory testing capacity need to be improved in order to detect and test every suspected case of cholera in an accurate and timely manner. This requires the training of health personnel to clinically diagnose cases according to the case definition determined by the MSPP and training of the OSEs in the immediate notification of cases seen. An adequate supply of laboratory supplies to sample and test every case, and a reliable network for the timely transport of samples to laboratories are needed. PAHO/WHO will continue funding nurses/ laboratory technicians in five identified departments to ensure the quality of the sampling and transportation process to laboratories. Additionally, an information management system needs to be implemented in order for laboratory test results to be integrated with epidemiological data to better orient case management and the response in the field. PAHO/WHO will support the training of ASCPs for community surveillance for the rapid detection of cholera, particularly in isolated communities. 2- To ensure proper case management of cholera cases to reduce case fatality rate and to prevent nosocomial infectionsAppropriate case management, through the rehydration (orally or intravenously) and hygiene protocole must be followed by antibiotic treatment (within the first 72 hours) to ensure the survival of the patient. Health professionals in health centers need refresher training in order to clinically diagnose cases and to take appropriate actions. CTDAs need to be integrated into MSPP health facilities. Additional support will also be provided to health directorates in the event of an epidemic through other implementing partners by putting in place a comprehensive and integrated response to the CDTAs concerned. This support will include: Provision of additional human resources (doctors, nurses, hygienists, support staff) during the outbreak; completing small rehabilitation works in CTDAs; install temporary care structures when there are outbreaks and where there is no structure. Proper IPC measures in health centers decreases the risk of transmission to health care workers and other patients. This requires the sufficient availability of medical supplies, case management kits, personal protective equipment (PPE). Provide WASH materials and inputs for cholera management in all departmental CDAIs, purchasing and delivering cholera inputs and WASH materials for all departmental CDAIs; supporting local health authorities in the monitoring and delivery of inputs and materials; and pre-positioning contingency stock in case of epidemic in hard-to-reach areas 3- To implement and strengthen rapid response activities, with contact tracing, chemoprophylaxis, active case research and reactive vaccination in small geographical areas with outbreaks or persistence of cases (‘hotspots’) in order to reduce transmission and to prevent the expansion of the outbreakPAHO/WHO provides support to the rapid response mechanism of the MSPP by working on the investigation of cases and analysis of data and by providing medical and WASH supplies to departmental health directorates. Cholera outbreak investigations and response, need to fall under global WHO strategies, as the all-hazard early warning system for unusual events, the Health Emergency Response Team; and the Emergency Medical Teams (EMT). OCV reactive campaigns, as integral parts of a comprehensive emergency response to cholera, will be completed, targeting small areas with cholera outbreaks or areas with cholera persistence (‘hotspots’) in Artibonite, Centre and Ouest. This project aims to support the operational costs (transportation and in situ training of vaccination teams, cold chain, data management materials, among others). Activities will be planned with and supporting the MSPP.
