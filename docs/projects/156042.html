Project Summary

Management coordination is essential to enable UNRWA to deliver a timely and effective humanitarian assistance. The project consists of three main components: ‎management and monitoring of humanitarian response, safety and security, and repair and maintenance of UNRWA premises in the new accessible areas. One of ‎UNRWA’s main priorities in 2019 is to resume its service in the new accessible areas to better respond to the needs of Palestine Refugees and ensure capacity in place ‎to provide the service particularly in light of the spontaneous returns of IDPs and Refugees to camps and gathering areas. In order to ensure effective delivery of ‎service as well as support of operations in the logistic and administrative fields, deployment of local and international staff based in the target locations is a must. ‎
Considering the scale of the humanitarian needs, UNRWA will continue to require dedicated technical staff to manage, support and monitor multi-sectorial activities as ‎well as emergency operations in Syria. International dedicated experts (e.g. humanitarian officers, monitoring and evaluation specialists, donor relations officers) will ‎continue to be deployed at the central office to increase programme quality and neutrality. Adequate staffing is essential for UNRWA to implement its operations, ‎especially with regard to direct implementation, as well as to ensure an efficient coordination with all stakeholders and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine ‎refugees.‎
UNRWA has made significant investments in the security and safety of its staff, assets and facilities security procedures. UNRWA requires funding to maintain as well ‎as strengthen the existing facilities. The main investments in this regard will be, procurement of communication devices such as VHF radios, maintenance of vehicles, ‎purchase of personal protection equipment, and uniforms for security staff. UNRWA will also continue to conduct safety and security as well as fire safety trainings for ‎staff in Syria. Additionally, UNRWA will continue installing surveillance systems in the main office and do the necessary security repairs to ensure that UNRWA assets ‎and premises are safeguarded. These investments are mandatory as they constitute a prerequisite for UNRWA to continue operating in Syria, especially in high risk ‎areas, and delivering vital assistance to affected Palestine refugees
Needs assessment carried out by UNRWA through 2018, particularly in new accessible areas, indicated that most of UNRWA facilities were either partially or fully ‎damaged. UNRWA developed area specific implementation plans based on the assessment mission reports. UNRWA facilities have been extensively damaged in the ‎past 7 years due to the effects of the crisis, making it more difficult for Palestine refugees to receive humanitarian assistance, access basic services, and enjoy a ‎measure of protection. Repair and maintenance of UNRWA facilities in the newly accessible areas will remain critical in 2019 to contribute to the provision of ‎humanitarian assistance, protective environments and access to basic services for 445,000 affected Palestine refugees.‎

Needs

Eight years of conflict in Syria continue to have dramatic consequences for the people living in the country, and Palestine refugees in particular. Violence, economic ‎disruption and a contracting job market as well as a decrease in provision of public services and social security programmes have created and compounded ‎vulnerabilities within the population. In the absence of a durable political settlement, intense and widespread hostilities have resulted in deaths and injuries, ‎continued internal displacement, large-scale migration to other countries, lost livelihoods, mounting humanitarian needs and access restrictions hindering the ‎emergency response, including in UNRWA areas of operations.‎ In 2019, UNRWA estimates that 445,000 Palestine refugees remain in the country out of 560,000 individuals registered with the Agency in Syria. Displacement, loss of ‎assets and livelihood and high levels of inflation have contributed to the increased vulnerability of the Palestine refugee population. Many families have lost their main ‎breadwinner to involvement in, or repercussions of the conflict, whether through death, incapacitation, detention or departure to another country in order to seek ‎protection and asylum. Almost 60 per cent of Palestine refugees have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict and, according to UNRWA ‎estimations, more than 180,000 have seen their homes destroyed or severely damaged due to hostilities. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously ‎home to more than 30 per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, are almost completely destroyed. A vulnerability assessment conducted by UNRWA in ‎‎2018,[3] estimated that 90 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than USD 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash ‎assistance. As a result, it is expected that a large majority of the Palestine refugee population will continue to rely on UNRWA humanitarian assistance and services to ‎meet their most basic needs in the years to come. ‎ Although vulnerability remains high across Palestine refugees in Syria, funding challenges are limiting the Agency’s ability to continue to provide the same levels of ‎humanitarian assistance and service as in past years. In 2018, UNRWA was confronted with an unprecedented financial crisis and faced major challenges in securing ‎the resources needed to provide humanitarian assistance to Palestine refugees in Syria. As of December 2018, UNRWA had only received 22% of its total ‎requirements against the Syria portion of the Emergency Appeal.‎‎ ‎

Implementation of activities

The main activities of the project are:‎‎1.‎ Recruitment of local as well as international staff to ensure adequate support and monitoring of operations and services. ‎‎2.‎ Procurement of security and safety equipment items‎3.‎ Security enhancement of UNRWA’s facilities ‎‎4.‎ Building the capacity of staff on safety and security including SSAFE training.‎‎5.‎ Electrical repairs and equipment for UNRWA premises and monthly general maintenance‎6.‎ Maintenance/repair and reconstruction of UNRWA premises in newly accessible gathering areas and camps ‎‎ ‎

Protection Risk Analysis

‎-‎ UNRWA staff coordinating the humanitarian response face protection risks when travelling to distribution points or UNRWA installations across the country. Risks are considerably higher in newly accessible areas. ‎o Likelihood: medium ‎o Impact: medium‎-‎ Explosive remnants of war (ERW) are recognized as a protection risk for UNRWA staff across the country, particularly in newly accessible areas. ‎o Likelihood: lowo Impact: high‎-‎ UNRWA staff, particularly female, are vulnerable to SH and SA. Vulnerability has increased during conflict and emergency. ‎o Likelihood: medium ‎o Impact: mediumMitigating measures: ‎‎-‎ UNRWA conducts thorough security assessments in coordination with partners. Before missions to high risk areas, thorough mission risk assessments and security briefings are conducted. UNRWA coordinates operations with UNDSS. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA has been focusing on training of staff (roll-out of SSAFE training to all area staff; Women Security Awareness Training (WSAT) for female staff. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA has prioritized awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to these risk related to ERW.‎‎-‎ UNRWA has considerably increased its focus on PSEA/PSH. Key frontline staff will be trained and trainings/awareness raising will continue to be a priority. ‎‎-‎ UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a regular basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to ‎optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. ‎

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Improved access with localized conflict in certain areas of the country: Overall, humanitarian access is expected to improve with fewer Palestine refugees in hard-to-reach areas (13,500 as of end October 2018). At the same time, hostilities may continue in certain areas of the country. Should safe access resume for civilians in areas like Yarmouk and Dera’a camps, the Agency will focus on resuming its basic education services in these areas. Most Palestine Refugees in Syria will remain displaced: It is anticipated that the levels of displacement will remain high among Palestine refugees, as the housing stock in Yarmouk unofficial camp suffered significant destruction during the conflict, most recently in May 2018. The majority of the 160,000 former residents of Yarmouk are therefore expected to remain in displacement, often paying high rents, which further increases their vulnerability. A similar situation is expected in Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps which have sustained major destruction; these three camps used to provide housing for more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria. Increase in spontaneous returns as more areas become accessible: As new areas become accessible to civilians, as observed for Sbeineh and Khan Eshieh camps in 2018, additional small scale spontaneous returns are anticipated, provided that safe access is granted for civilians and basic public services (water, electricity) are restored by the authorities. UNRWA will therefore seek to resume its education services in defined locations where feasible, to ensure that Palestine refugee school children are served. While a small number of spontaneous returns of PRS (Palestine refugees from Syria) from outside Syria were observed in 2017 and 2018 (about 2,000) and are expected to continue, most returns are expected to involve Palestine refugees displaced inside Syria. UNRWA continue to raise sufficient funds to sustain its operations in Syria: UNRWA will continue to mobilize resources and to advocate for the needs of Palestine refugees among donors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders. UNRWA will need an estimated US$ 220 million to respond to the emergency needs (health, education, protection, humanitarian assistance, WASH) of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria in 2019. UNRWA will mitigate the risk of limited funding by prioritizing key projects and interventions, and focusing on the needs of the most vulnerable.
