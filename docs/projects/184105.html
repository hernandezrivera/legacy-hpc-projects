1 - Needs

An estimated 120,000 people displaced since 2020 in Darfur with trends indicatingcontinued displacement as armed violence continues in Jebel Marra. Market inflation andeconomic challenges has significant impact on the Sudanese people, increasing theirvulnerability to risks. In 2021 HNO, estimated over 5 million will encounter acute food andlivelihood insecurity in Sudan based on review and analysis of various food securitiesinformation reports IPC 2020. Lack of adequate income and their low diversity, lack ofagricultural extension activities, lack of improved seeds, insecticides, pesticideschemicals, lack of mobility tools for extensionists, lack of the fruits seedlings propagationnurseries, and lack of improved vegetable and fruits demonstration plots are the commonchallenges. In Feb, UPO conducted a survey and found the most common constraintsfaced by the communities and the local authorities are lack of sufficient on farm seedproduction, poor agricultural production methods, lack of skilled human resource capacityat the state, locality and community levels, and lack of skills particularly among theyouths. Women and youth also reported insufficient income-generating sources UPO hasconducted a rapid assessment in IDPs camps and most affected rural areas with newlyIDPs and host communities in July 2021 where UPO team has interviewed newly IDPs andmost vulnerable returnees with involvement of men, women, boys and girls through directgroup discussion especially with those who newly displaced and affected and armedinter-tribal conflicts between the periods of January –Oct 2020. More than 24,000 newlydisplaced and conflict affected individuals including women, men, boys and girls wereidentified to be in urgent needs for food security and livelihood coping mechanisms.Another 7,600 most conflict affected and returnees' populations (women, men, boys andgirls) in ED ty were assessed to be in needs agricultural inputs, extension services, trainingon clay stoves and other substantial livelihood opportunities to help them possess newincome opportunity. UPO has identified limited opportunities for households to generateincome and produce food, with agricultural inputs and livelihood activities. In UPO,community interviews 60% of households in Darfur indicated they do not have sufficientfood. The majority of respondents cited a lack of seed, and 78% indicated they have notbeen able to store their own seed (UPO rapid assessment in Feb). Community membersidentified insufficient land, due nomadic occupation, requiring improved methods tomaximize output and diversity their livelihoods. Animal health is also poor, particularlyamongst IDPs and host households, with only 5.6% indicating good animal health. UPOFSL intervention will focus protecting animals' health, provide quality declared droughttolerant and early maturing seed to enable their own production and ensure self-reliance
