Project Summary

The Project aims to provide integrated adequate, safe and clean water; appropriate sanitation and hygiene promotion activities in six counties of Upper Nile state. Relief International (RI) proposes a 12 month project focusing on working with target communities to improve access to portable water, appropriate sanitation and context relevant hygiene promotion services. The project, which will target refugees, returnees, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities, will be implemented in Maban, Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang and Baliet counties of Upper Nile state. The Program goal is to reduce morbidity and mortality due to WASH related diseases in the targeted 6 counties through integrated programming with Nutrition, protection and health. The project will built on the ongoing projects RI has been doing in the target counties including WASH, Nutrition, Food security and Livelihoods, protection and health. The project objectives are aligned to the 4 WASH cluster objectives namely:
1. Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition
2. Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.
3. WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation.
4. Community member’s exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure.
RI will rehabilitate boreholes, upgrade existing hand-pumps with solar power system and distribute water filters or household water treatment products. The project will support establishment of water reticulation systems, community led climatic and gender sensitive sanitation infrastructure, strengthening community management of WASH, environmental health awareness, social behavior change centered health and hygiene promotion, waste management and distribution of WASH NFIs as appropriate. In terms of infrastructure, RI will target both community and essential institutions without appropriate WASH infrastructure including health institutions, markets, nutrition centers, civic centers and others.
RI will build on its current work on Gender based violence, and mainstreaming of protection to ensure children and women have access to appropriate and relevant WASH services, and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services. In all the project locations, RI will assess protection concerns related to women and girls accessing WASH services and take robust action to mitigate the risk of exposing women and girls. Thus WASH technologies, messaging and choices on place and time will consider protection principles of do no harm.
RI will integrate WASH activities with nutrition to maximize the impact and reduce excessive morbidity and mortality of children under five. Households with children under 5, pregnant and breastfeeding women with Acute Malnutrition (SAM/MAM) will be targeted for basic climatic sensitive sanitation and WASH NFIs. In addition, RI will improve access to safe water and sanitation facilities to nutrition and health facilities. RI will also support communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rates with hygiene promotion and nutrition messaging. The hygiene promotion will be aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrheal, cholera and vector borne. RI will work with protection partners, community leaders and religious leaders to identify people with disabilities, elders, marginalized and People Living with HIV (PLWHIV).
Across the project cycle, the project will emphasize on localization principles, specifically partnering with local organisations and target communities. RI will endeavor to empower communities from within, through local partnerships, nurturing resilience and ownership for sustainable change. The project will promote community-led approaches and system of accountabilities.

1. Needs

The WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Upper Nile state. Displacements associated with insecurity, severe flooding and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes in areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced. According to the WASH cluster HNRP 2025 severity mapping and targeting, the WASH sector needs and gaps in Nasir falls under priority 1, while the WASH sector needs and gaps for Maban, Longechuk, Maiut, Ulang and Baliet falls under priority 2. According to the December 2024 to March 2025 IPC analysis, 296,000 people form the six counties (Mabn, Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang and Ballet) are in Crisis (IPC3) while another 118000 people are classified as emergency (IPC4). Similarly, according to the first acute malnutrition mapping (October 2024-March 2025), Ballet falls under IPC 4 while others five counties falls under IPC 3. However, the situation is getting worse from April- June 2025. All five counties will be falling under IPC 5. This indicates that intervention of integrating WASH activities is crucial to reduce the severity clarification from 3 to 2 since absence of WASH services is one of the aggravating factors which contributes for acute malnutrition. The already dire situation in the counties has been exacerbated by Sudan Crisis and Flooding which has destroyed WASH infrastructure across counties. According to UNHCR 855,000 returnees and refugees crossed into South Sudan giving high pressure on the little WASH Infrastructure available. Maban alone holds 230,000 refugees who are living in four camps, and the numbers are continuing to surge as more Sudanese and third nationalities fleeing the Sudan Crisis are being settled. Furthermore the recurrent flooding in the six target counties has destroyed the little of the WASH infrastructure available. According to the various Rapid Needs Assessments done in the three counties (Ulang and Longechuk) still poor access to secure sources of water, boreholes are in adequate, whilst many existing are not functioning. Most households have no solid and liquid waste management mechanisms. Most houses were observed unclean, with excessive grass due to the rainy season whilst community members were unaware of the risks posed by poor environmental and sanitation conditions.According to Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) 2024 data, most counties have very limited improved water sources with Nasir and Ulang having coverage of 37.7%, 38% and 47% respectively. All five targeted counties have less than 35% of the households having improved water sources at premises with Ulang, Baliet and Longechuk having less than 12%. Latrine coverage and handwashing facilities in the communities is very low, and open defecation practices is very high public health risks. In Ulang, Longechuk, and Maban over 82% practice open defecation. In all targeted counties, Sanitation coverage is very low, with only 10% of the households having access to improved sanitation facilities in Nasir, Maban and Ulang. Public institutions like clinics, hospitals, markets and schools have not been spared from acute shortage of water and proper sanitation. The communities lack access to WASH NFIs, dignity kits and awareness on safe hygiene practices. In terms of handwashing, less than 10% of the households in all the targeted counties use soap. Diarrheal diseases are prevalent across all age groups, primarily due to poor water supply, hygiene, and sanitation. Diarrheal diseases prevalence is over 16% in most of the counties with Baliet among the highest having 20.7%.

2. Project activities

The following activities will be implemented; 2.1. Upgrading of hand-pumps to solar powered water supply system and rehabilitation of water hand pumps repairs targeting the Payams with lowest coverage. During rehabilitation in flood prone areas, the aprons of the water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. Safe and adequate drinking water will be provided at nutrition facilities level for patients, caregivers, and staff. RI will also supports communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rates through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. 2.2. Establishment and training of Water management committee (WMC). The training will include specific modules on operation and maintenance of water sources and material support the WMC with pump maintenance tool kits. RI will ensure that at least 50% of committee members are women.
