Project Summary

Syria is one of the largest protection crises of our times. Hundreds of thousands of civilians have been killed, and armed battles continue to carry out violations of human rights, with women and children particularly vulnerable to exploitation - including breaches of international humanitarian law. Since the beginning of the crisis in 2011, Syria has witnessed significant challenges in the humanitarian and security situation across the country, intensification in the targeting of civilian infrastructure.
Rural Damascus has been severely affected by the war for several years, resulting in losing all their goods and chattels. After defeating several armed groups hundreds of families decided to return to their own towns to restart a new life with their relatives and friends. The returnees could find housing possibilities, but they are in lack of any income resources.
The project aims to support IDPs in the hosting communities and returnees in their own towns with providing vocational training sessions. Assisting IDPs would help them either starting with self-employment or finding job to alleviate the financial burdens on the hosting communities or giving them the opportunity to return with a some life skills to even start a new life in their own locations. Accordingly, the project for IDPS and returnees would have a positive impact on individuals and societies in many aspects
The objective of this project is to enable 800 IDPs, returnees and host communities’ members (male and female), to have an honorable source of income to help their families and other society war affected members.
The program will create economic opportunities through vocational training.
Beneficiaries will receive life skills and management experience during the program so that they will be able to start their own small business by the end of the program.
Tool kits are to be distributed for all of beneficiaries by the end of the program.

Needs

Since the year of 2011, about 6.3 million people who have left their homes are still in Syria, they are called internally displaced persons (IDPs).The available data from the surveys that (UNHCR) conducted show that the majority of these people is living a miserable life of poverty, with no source of living, due to several reasons such as:- Conflict / fighting.- Lack of economic movement.- Economic inflation.Some of the results of this displacement are:- Lack of income.- People lost their assets.- Lack of education and vocational skills.An estimated of 11.400 persons are registered in GOPA-DERD centers asking to enroll them in vocational training programs for different professions. According to UNHCR, about 1.250.000 IDPs were registered in all of Rural Damascus areas (estimated by the end of 2018). Among these IDPs, 14.600 persons are existed in the project targeted area 'Arbin'. The project will target 800 persons in Arbin to help them acquire new Vocational knowledge. Beneficiaries and courses will be chosen based on the needs assessment process. *In this proposal, we present a solution to help some of the internally displaced families in Rural Damascus by imparting vocational training skills to at least one member of the family

Implementation of activities

The implementation process of this project will include at first a social evaluation to select 800 beneficiaries from Rural Damascus based on the following beneficiaries selection criteria: - Families/individuals that have been unemployed up to 12 months.- Youth (17-24 years old).- Families/Individuals having PwD member(s) or injured member(s).- Female-headed families.- Families/Individuals who did not receive any kind of assistance- Female who experienced sexual abuse or violence. The vocational courses will be identified based on the beneficiaries needs such as: Sewing, Hair dressing, Mobile maintenance, Cooking…A public invitation to tender will be announced in order to choose qualified training centers and to find the best offers to provide the needed tool-kits.The average time frame for each course is about 140 hours. During the program implementation, a monitoring process will be done by the staff as follows: - Weekly visit to the targeted areas.- Trainers’ performance evaluation.- Follow up with the mentors to check on progress of the beneficiaries’ professional level.The beneficiaries will receive some life skills and project management courses after completing the theoretical and practical training. By the end of the training courses, the staff will be in charge of the tool kits distribution process.After 6 months of the program close-out, the staff will follow up with the beneficiaries to evaluate the impact of the program on them and the hosting community.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Possible sexual abuse during the courses. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Staff and volunteers will conduct awareness sessions with the beneficiaries, besides applying daily supervision to prevent this kind of abuse.-Protection Risk: Slight possibility of gender-based violence, as the program considers the diversity of gender and age. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High.Mitigation measures: Ensure that the team is well trained on basics of GBV.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Risk of drop-outs (Low risk). Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: Provide a day care service for the children so that the mothers can attend the courses with them and provide transportation by GOPA logistic department. - Risk of unavailability of qualified vocational training centers in the targeted areas (Low risk). Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ by keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress. - Risk of delay caused by the unstable security in the country (Low risk). Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: Perform conflict analysis before planning the response.
