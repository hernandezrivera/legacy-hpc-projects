Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving ES/NFIs as well as building resilience through cash based programming to 35,000 (20,000 females and 15,000 Males) conflict affected IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities in Upper Nile State. The target population are based in Upper Nile counties that has experience a cycle of violence that has made it difficult to access for a long time. The same conflict has displaced many of the target population to either neigbouring settlement areas, POCs or neighbouring countries and are now returning to settle back in their original homes and are in need of immediate ES/NFI support and or resilience support. The host communities in these areas are getting it hard to cope with the number of returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. SSUDA proposes to intervene in three areas or objectives: save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys; Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services; and support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks. This will be done through: providing access to life saving NFIs, providing access to emergency shelter using in-kind support as well as cash based program. SSUDA will focus largely on increasing cash based projects based on cash feasibility assessment report that is to be done in Upper Nile this year 2018 that will indicate which counties has active markets and advise on cash modalities to be used. Apart from this, SSUDA will mean-stream protection including GBV and HLP, consultations with the affected populations, working closely with other clusters, and other SSUDA implemented projects, monitoring of activities, and provision of skills, as well as providing messages of protection and health. SSUDA has experience in working in Upper Nile State and is implementing other projects such as education, communication for development, child protection, food security among others in the region that will help in providing not only synergy to this project but also information and critical understanding of the context and dynamics of the counties, knowledge of the local leadership and prospected beneficiaries, such as a 3-year EU funded Pro-Act resilience project for food security, livelihoods and WASH in Fashoda, Malakal, and Manyo, Education project in Panyikang and Manyo, Communication for Development (C4D) project in Ulang, Nasir, Panyikang, Longochuk, and Maiwut, and Child Protection in Ulang both supported by UNICEF. SSUDA is the ES/NFI state co-focal point in Upper Nile with an office in Malakal, and therefore about 6% of the project costs will go into overhead costs of SSUDA to improve its presence in the State and region.

Needs

According to the Humanitarian Bulletin (9th May 2018), around 25,000 people who fled renewed fighting and the government offensive on the West Bank of the River Nile in April traveled to Aburoc, with many subsequently continuing on to Sudan. At the same time, there were returnees going back to Baliet County who had been displaced as IDPs in Melut County that needs urgent response. Lately in September 2018, there were huge displacements in Maiwut where serious fighting was taking place with the government offensive in Pagak with most of the displaced moving towards Ethiopia in payams such as Jekow. According to EASAT quoting Radio Tamazuj (July 12th, 2018), UN officials said about 5,000 civilians have been forced to seek refuge in neighboring Ethiopia due to escalating clashes near Pagak in Upper Nile.Most parts of Upper Nile State is still volatile. According to Humanitarian Overview Report 2018, continued fighting in Upper Nile, including on the western bank of the River Nile, Renk, Mathiang, Longochuk and Pagak, has caused tens of thousands of people to flee their homes, with some moving to Sudan and Ethiopia. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Crisis Overview 2018 by SIDA, there are multiple challenges to humanitarian access in the country due to a number of reasons, including conflict and violence, poor infrastructure, flooding, criminality and bureaucratic impediments and restrictions by government and other conflict parties. In Greater Upper Nile, many displaced people are hiding in remote areas, swamps and on islands which makes it difficult 6 to reach them with assistance. Counties affected include Longochuk, Nassir, Maiwut, Panyikang, Manyo with existence of soldiers from both the government and opposition. UNOCHA in their April bulletin reports thousands of civilians displaced from Tonga and surrounding villages in Panyikang County, and Kodok and villages to its south in Fashoda County and lower Manyo particularly in Kaka and Adhidhuoi, in Upper Nile following fighting in the two locations. According to Humanitarian Snapshot (June 2017), the refugee outflow continued, with more than 3,400 people seeking refuge in Sudan between 1 and 15 June following fighting between armed actors, including in Manyo and Renk counties in Upper Nile. The displaced fear to go back to their villages and are expected to stay in Fashoda for quite long due to heavy presence and control in the area. This has and will continue to pose serious humanitarian demand in Fashoda. This displacement was at the same time witnessed in Panyikang particularly around Tonga and lower Manyo (Kaka, Adhidhuoi areas) fighting in the West bank.Because of the recent peace agreement, most of the areas such as Panyikang, Fashoda, Manyo, Longochuk, Maiwut, and parts of Nasir in Upper Nile that were not served by humanitarian organizations due to accessibility challenges have very huge caseloads as accessibility is slowly returning. In addition to this, the same areas are receiving many returnees from IDPs settlement areas, including POCs and from neighboring countries that they sought refuge during the war. The few host community bears the burden when returnees are coming back with their resources depleted. Most of the returnees are coming back empty handed without anything. This emergency support will be supplemented by resilience building such as a combined cash based intervention, with this hope of a peace agreement. In this project, SSUDA will use at least 2 staff per intervention with an estimated 3 interventions in Upper Nile to uphold steady presence as a static partner. Among the 6 field staff SSUDA will engage, 1 of them will be a roving protection specialist. Activities will be designed in a way that they address some needs of other sectors/clusters such as protection, livelihood, WASH etc. with multi-sector survival kit particularly in hard-to-reach areas where most of SSUDA's interventions are based.

Activities

1. Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys. This objective will have the following activities: • Conduct gender and age segregated rapid needs assessment in target locations to determine the needs, number and priorities. • Carry out beneficiary’s identification and verification with consideration of women and female-headed households.• Conduct awareness campaign to inform the community of impending distribution include locations, dates, and time to ensure all beneficiaries are informed.• Conduct identification, orientation and hiring of community volunteers to assist in distribution.• Conduct distribution of ES/NFIs materials to the beneficiaries• Conduct Rapid Monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring assessment for feedback purposes2. Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services. This objective will have the following activities: • Conduct gender and age segregated rapid needs assessment in target locations to determine the needs, number and priorities. • Carry out beneficiary’s identification and verification with consideration of women and female-headed households.• Conduct awareness campaign to inform the community of impending distribution include locations, dates, and time to ensure all beneficiaries are informed.• Conduct identification, orientation and hiring of community volunteers to assist in distribution.• Conduct distribution of ES/NFIs materials to the beneficiaries• Conduct Rapid Monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring assessment3. Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks. This objective will have the following activities: • Conduct needs and market assessments to determine the voucher type, amount and disbursement mechanism to determine the conflict implications and market feasibility of doing a cash intervention.• Identification/registration of project beneficiaries and local traders for cash intervention• Conduct Rapid Monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring assessment for feedback purposes• Develop and distribute cash vouchers and other cash modalities to beneficiaries of cash intervention.• Procure local shelter materials• Construct shelters to the identified beneficiaries.. Conduct skills training to identified beneficiaries to be able to cope with survival needs

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

SSUDA will ensure participation of every constituency of the affected and targeted population at every level of project implementation and ensure their feedback is taken into account at decision making. SSUDA will work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee nominated by the target beneficiaries that is composed majorly of beneficiaries. This committee will be meeting with the project implementation team as frequent as possible and the issues agreed during the meetings will be shared back to all the beneficiaries. This is the committee that will also help with the ideas of the assistance needed, apart from the assessment that will be conducted and committee will last for the duration of the project. Three days to distribution, SSUDA will engage volunteers with megaphones to announce what will be distributed, distribution locations, dates and time to the beneficiaries. A rapid assessment and or post-distribution monitoring will be conducted by SSUDA and feedback shared with the beneficiaries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SSUDA will provide tailor made information to specific groups during implementation of the project. Information around SGBV for example will be shared with female gender during focus group discussion s to understand the dynamics of SGBV with the specific area and the information given with the protected by SSUDA team. This massages will be shared during assessments, before distribution and after distribution during PDMs. During distribution, SSUDA will ensure that there are massages around protection, health such as HIV/AIDS, Ebola, cholera among others in collaboration with other clusters.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection is one of the most important aspects considering the emphasis given to it by ES/NFI cluster during the HRP development process. SSUDA is mainstreaming protection in its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls receive protection when they are providing information during assessment and also during distribution of items. SSUDA will employ a protection advisor for this project to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. SSUDA will also ensure that the distribution sites are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Our actions will address both basic needs including NFIs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. We will also pay special attention to people living with disabilities, children, child-headed and female-headed households. SSUDA is an active member of Protection Cluster where information and issues related to protection are shared, discussed and addressed. SSUDA will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor the distribution process.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SSUDA has experience working in most if not all counties of Upper Nile implementing different projects. SSUDA's consultations with communities begins early before the project inception and usually target directly those affected by the conflict without community 'gatekeepers'to find out what are the real needs. So far, 3 consultative meetings have been held. Because of the deeper engagement and the added tasks needed, SSUDA will hire more national and local staff to work on this project to understand the local context as well as language for deeper engagements and consultations. SSUDA will explain to "gatekeepers" as they have done before on the reason why they dealt much directly with the beneficiaries. SSUDA will ensure local staff are not put into decision-making position during assessment, registration and distribution to avoid any bias that might come from any influence whatsoever as all these levels were strictly monitored by national and international staff. SSUDA will not only ensure that the needs are identified but also reduced conflict that would arise between beneficiary groups over the distribution of items. SSUDA will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor the distribution process that will be meeting regularly to plan and implement.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

SSUDA will hold consultative meetings with every constituency of beneficiaries such as women, men, boys and girls separately to get their feedback before distribution of items on the best way this can be done so as to avoid local "gatekepers" from such decision making and also ensure protection issues raised are addressed before the distribution. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by SSUDA will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. SSUDA will also develop mechanisms of real, unbiased feedback from beneficiaries to SSUDA and to the cluster through, rapid monitoring, post distribution monitoring and reporting in small cluster meetings at County level with other agencies horizontally and vertically to the national cluster. SSUDA will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor the distribution process.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SSUDA will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries in all stages of this project cycle. This action will address key life saving threats identified during needs assessment including women, men and children. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner (also including host communities) and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women. SSUDA has experience and network in all the targeted locations and this project will create a deeper engagement between the organisation and the targeted community we serve. SSUDA will ensure that the community volunteers and local staff they engage during the project phase including assessment and distribution are well trained on protection issues including gender-based violence and signed a code of conduct before being hired. The community and beneficiaries will be engaged in monthly monitoring of the project which will then include rapid monitoring, and post distribution monitoring after the end of the project for feedback purposes.
