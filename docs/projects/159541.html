Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to address acute malnutrition among vulnerable groups through an integrated community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) that combines prevention and and lifesaving treatment measures in Pibor County. The project will target 38,927 children aged 6-59 months for malnutrition screening, with a target of 5,368 for SAM. In terms of nutrition preventive activities the project will target 30,634 children 6-59 months for Vitamin A supplementation. The project will further target 73,107 primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months for MIYCN counselling. Early prepositioning of nutrition supplies will be undertaken to prevent rupture of essential supplies in all OTP sites.
Key components of the project include; (i) the promotion of equitable access to nutrition specific preventive service delivery; (ii) screening and treatment of acute malnutrition cases in children U5 and PLW; (iii) advocacy and coordination to enable integration of nutrition sensitive services with WASH, food security, health and protection services and (iii) information collection and analysis to strengthen evidence based nutrition response in the County(iv) capacity building to Ministry of Health to establish Nutrition System within County Health Department (CHD).

Efforts will be put in place to station all nutrition services within existing health systems and where health systems do not exist, strong collaboration will be made with health partners to offer health mobile clinics at all nutrition sites. Integrated nutrition services will involve the health sector for treatment of childhood illnesses specifically malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea as well as preventive health services such as immunization, growth monitoring and deworming. Growth monitoring will be done for children aged 0-59 months to monitor growth faltering and action taken timely. WASH activities will be integrated in all health and nutrition sites which includes provision of safe water, hygiene promotion activities and sanitation including distribution of hygiene kits to families of children with SAM and MAM through collaboration with WASH partners. FSL will be integrated in all health and nutrition sites and demonstration gardens will be established for training of primary caregivers to gain knowledge and skills in establishing home gardens in living places. MSGS will be supported to carry out cooking demos with primary caregivers of children aged 6-23 months to develop competency in complementary food preparations and feeding to improve MIYCN indicators. All health and nutrition sites will be made child friendly through provision of Baby Friendly Spaces (BFS) to offer Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities including equipping them with toys. Early detection and treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases will be through screening and admission to the OTP program while addressing underlying causes of acute malnutrition by counseling on appropriate childcare practices, improving sanitation practices and provision of micronutrient supplementation and deworming.
Gender mainstreaming in nutrition program will be enhanced to ensure more females are recruited in the program to implement child care activities especially in MIYCN. Accountability to Affected Populations will be enhanced though Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM). Community members will be consulted before implementation to help in designing feedback mechanism that is culturally sensitive to the context.
In order to monitor and identify evolving needs and facilitate timely nutrition responses, the project will strengthen its monitoring and reporting to the cluster and support nutrition surveillance activities, FSNMS and conduct pre-harvest SMART survey.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is expected to persist with over 6.35 million people amounting to 54% of the population currently facing severe acute food insecurity with 10,000 in the catastrophe level. The highest percentage of those in need are concentrated in Jonglei State where nearly 1.18 million people making up 61.6% of the population are affected. In addition, 1.3 million Children under 5 years old and over 350,000 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition with a high burden of these from several states including Jonglei. A convergence of factors, including severe food insecurity, lack of health services and inadequate water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services with assessments showing that only 40% of nutrition treatment sites have access to safe water, has resulted in continued high malnutrition amongst children and women in the country. In Pibor County 154,000 people making up 75% of the population are in crisis and emergency levels of food insecurity and require urgent lifesaving humanitarian assistance to prevent extreme outcomes. Pibor County is one of the least understood, complex and hard to reach areas of South Sudan both due to security concerns as well as poor road infrastructure which become completely inaccessible during the rainy season. The majority of its inhabitants are primarily nomadic pastoralist communities who rely on livestock for their livelihood. Poor feeding practices, poor quality and limited food diversity as well as seasonal increase in morbidity due to malaria and other diseases has compounded its malnutrition problem. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to further deteriorate within the coming year due to the early onset of lean season, as most households are expected to deplete their household food stocks. The nutrition cluster estimates that currently, among people in need of nutrition services 77% are from the non-displaced host community. Pibor County is one of the counties with majority of non-displaced populations; and if at all there is displacement it is within the county as populations move for safety due to floods or in search of pasture and water for livestock. According to FSNMS Round 24, the GAM rates in Jonglei State (24.2%) among children aged 0-59 months has crossed emergency threshold of 15%. The results further revealed high diseases prevalence among children below 5 years old (40%) in Jonglei State; sub-optimal young child feeding practices with only 20% children aged 6-23 months received diversified foods, 7% received Minimal Acceptable Diet (MAD). Analysis of HRP nutrition targets indicates that Jonglei State covers (17%) of Under 5 SAM, (21%) of Under 5 MAM, (18%) of PLW MAM, (28%) of MIYCN and (10%) of VAS coverage in South Sudan for 2020. On the other hand, Pibor County covers (12%) of Under 5 SAM, (11%) of Under 5 MAM, (21%) of PLW MAM, (23%) of MIYCN and (10%) of VAS coverage in Jonglei State. MSF-B assessment report dated 21st October 2019 highlights the need for organizations to mobilize resources to mitigate the impact of rising flood levels in affected locations, and to ensure adequate attention is given to Pibor. With entire populations now cut off from health care and assistance, there is an anticipated rise in acute watery diarrhea, malaria and other communicable diseases. JAM in collaboration with ACF SURGE carried out MUAC screening among (507) children aged 6-59 months among Pibor Population on 24th October 2019 and found GAM rates (8.9%) with SAM (3.2%); the situation will escalate considering increasing floods. All indications point to the critical need in Pibor with a worsening humanitarian situation, with the population constantly changing due to displacement. An integrated nutrition intervention that encompasses Health, WASH, FSL, Protection, infrastructure rehabilitation and intensive community mobilization continues to be imperative.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In case of influx of displaced persons, policies and procedures are in place through UNHCR for assessment and registration of displaced persons followed by relevant support. The project will be implemented in close partnership with the government and community institutions and hence all the services and assistance provided by the project will be recognized by the respective local stakeholders. During stakeholder consultation and community sensitization, the project will provide clear information on the specific services and entitlements so as to build common understanding and facilitate the stage for local accountability.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Displacements is likely to be internal and systems are in place for inclusion of displaced persons under UNHCR/UNOCHA and government systems through RRC. Rapid Response Fund (RRF) is available for implementing partners to access for support in case of influx of displaced persons. In case of influx in the county, the project will prioritize displaced populations and ensure their rights are considered and are integrated in project implementation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Nutrition services are part of the Health System; The program will support CHD at all levels to initiate and strengthen Nutrition System at the MoH level. Nutrition project cycle (needs identification, implementation, monitoring and evaluation) will involve CHD to ensure the ministry is aware of the project and majority of personnel will be recruited locally; training and capacity building for nutrition personnel will be done using in country training protocols and transfer of skills will be emphasized to support long term interventions. CHD will be supported to carry out joint supervisory and monitoring in all OTP sites to help familiarize themselves with the nutrition system. JAM will strengthen community actors such as Mother Support groups, Father Support Groups, School teachers, youths and Boma chiefs including Health and Nutrition committee members to participate, get involved and make supportive decisions in nutrition activities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Nutrition is part and parcel of South Sudan Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and its objectives and implementation are embedded in the Country Health Policy. The project will build capacities of constituencies, strengthen health and nutrition system, improve infrastructure and work in collaboration with other sectors to address determinants of health. South Sudan Health Policy, 2016-2026 aims at improving service delivery to its constituencies through Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS). The main policy objectives include: Strengthening health service organization and infrastructure development for effective and equitable delivery of BPHNS and universal coverage; Strengthening leadership and management of health system and increase health system resources for improved health sector performance and Strengthening partnership for healthcare delivery and health care systems development. Hence the proposed nutrition project is aligned to South Sudan Health System Policy of 2016-2026 and will work closely with Pibor CHD to contribute to policy implementation.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Following the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, the security situation has relatively improved in many areas. The latest IPC report indicated that as a result of reduced insecurity in the country, access to livelihoods and market is gradually improving. The improving protection environment has also led to better humanitarian access further contributing to the increased access to food, nutrition and livelihood support, as well as service delivery. With the ongoing positive progress in the peace process, the security situation is anticipated to improve further in the coming year; creating conducive environment for humanitarian interventions to promote resilience focused long term development measures.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

A phase-over of responsibility to the County Health department through partnership is envisaged in the implementation and monitoring. A shared responsibility and participatory process with other stakeholders and CHD is seen as instrumental in ensuring sustainability. The feedback and lesson learnt from the participants will be an essential step toward sustainability. The activities will be linked to on - going JAM nutrition activities in Pibor whereby community component is already linked with community members, the Mother Support Groups (MSGs), Father Support Groups etc. Moreover, JAM in collaboration with UNICEF will train health facility staff and other nutrition workers including Mother Support Group members in both CMAM and MIYCN. The knowledge and skills imparted to local staff and SMOH staff will remain in place for continuity of the program. Nutrition (OTP) sites currently managed by JAM will be equipped with relevant supplies and equipment for continuity of the program.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Majority of the population that the project will be working with will be women, girls and the youth. At project inception the views of these groups will be actively sought in order to design a response that is friendly and culturally appropriate to the context. The project implementation mainly targets women and adolescents with leaders who will be empowered to serve as role models for continued behaviour change and project sustainability. The project will promote MIYCN through mother support groups, father support groups and adolescents in schools as well as women of reproductive age. There is an existential risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to and location of OTP sites. The project will scale up community outreach though Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) who will conduct door to door campaigns on MIYCN and other malnutrition prevention activities. All personnel will be trained on appropriate handling of GBV cases including referrals.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed project has mechanisms by which target community members can voice their needs and preferences and demand inclusive processes. The project will facilitate community feedback mechanisms including setting up of feedback boxes at all OTP service centres, AAP contact person/Payam Supervisor to gather community views and concerns regarding project services and procedures. Community members will be encouraged to give feedback on various aspect of the project including quality of service, access to information, OTP sites accessibility and time taken to be served, adequate privacy, adequate infrastructure, attitude of nutrition personnel, availability of supplies as well as on issues related to sexual abuses. The complaints and feedback mechanism gives community a safe and effective avenue to express their views and find solutions for issues that affect their interest. The project will ensure all information communicated to the target community is appropriate and tailored to local specific circumstances. The information will be delivered in local languages and will be culturally and gender sensitive. Printed information will take into account the high level of illiteracy rate and will be presented in pictorial forms.
