Project Summary

UNFPA & its partners will focus on quality life-saving SRH interventions targeting 250,000 vulnerable, marginalized Lebanese & migrant women, men, boys & adolescent girls. They will receive support in SRH information & services including for medical consultations, RH drugs & contraceptives. Migrants will be reached through UNFPA implementing partners Amel, Caritas including in the shelter of the latest. Hidden women and those subject to GBV, Persons with disabilities will be reached including through mobile teams. An estimated 3000 health care-providers & front line/ outreach workers will receive capacity development on SRH & GBV in addition to COVID19 in pregnancy. Interventions are endorsed by MOPH & partners. UNFPA proposed interventions are as follows:
A-Promoting SRH service delivery by
-Enhancing quality of maternal care through a series of training sessions to 1250 health care providers at the primary health care level on the revised RH services delivery guidelines namely for the ANC/PNC package.
-Procurement of RH drugs and commodities to 120,000 men, women, boys and adolescent girls accessing MOPH PHC network & dispensaries located in remote & poor areas in order to avail RH commodities to the increasing number of vulnerable Lebanese and migrants accessing these heath facilities. Based on data by the MOPH PHC Dept,. the no. of beneficiaries accessing health services at the PHC is constantly increasing, namely, female beneficiaries.
-Community mobilization and awareness raising on health care with focus on SRH through engagement of 80 midwives & outreach workers who will promote health seeking behavior among 80,000 women and adolescent girls. The outreach workers will receive series of training on health topics. The outreach workers will ensure referral of beneficiaries to the PHCs for further care as needed. Also adolescent girls and PLW identified at risk of poor nutrition will be referred for nutritional counseling and support. A media campaign about promotion of health care & availability of health services including for SRH will be conducted. Migrants will be reached through peer to peer approach, shelters and services run by UNFPA partners Amel and Caritas who have experience with this target population.
-Provision of SRH Services including medical consultation, midwifery care and subsidized tests for the most vulnerable women including women with high risk pregnancies, GBV survivors, etc.

B-Enhancing COVID19 response especially for PLWs by
-Establishing a team of specialist trained on the protocol for case management of severe COVID19 pregnant cases who would provide 1) on-site support for COVID19 infected pregnant women; 2) remote support for the management of COVID19 infected pregnant women; 3) facilitate referral to facilities providing specialized care/ intensive care for COVID19 infected pregnant women.
-Engaging midwives to ensure 1) awareness raising about COVID19 and pregnancy including for IPC and Vaccination 2) identification and support to COVID19 infected pregnant women including home care visits & referral to further care as needed 3) linkages with the specialist response team to ensure early response as needed, support transfer/ transportation of cases to health care facilities as needed
-Conducting capacity development for 500 health care providers on updates on COVID19.
-Training of 50 health care providers on management of COVID19 case management as per developed national protocols.
C-Mainstreaming protection for the most vulnerable women and adolescent girls through
-Capacity development on GBV safe identification and referral to 1250 health care providers from the MOPH PHC network to ensure their understanding of GBV and promote referral to further care as needed.
- Integration of GBV services in SRH through referral or within the same site.
-Provision of Dignity kits to 25,000 women and girls combined with the awareness raising on SRH and GBV.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of women & girls of different age, nationalities, cultural background, sexual & gender orientation. Needs of women & girls with disabilities will be factored in. Safety audits & community consultations will be held. Key messages about what constitutes PSEA and how to report allegations will be mainstreamed. Monitoring activities will include a section on protection risks & PSEA. UNFPA health partners will be supported to ensure a properly functional mechanism for feedback& complaints that allows beneficiaries to report their concerns/inputs. UNFPA will ensure that all health staff & front line workers engaged, have completed their PSEA Training. UNFPA will ensure close follow up & monitoring through1) regular follow up with the implementing partners, 2) field visits 3) review of data making sure it is disaggregated by age, gender, and disability. UNFPA will also ensure capacity development of partners on monitoring and reporting.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNFPA operates according to the Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (Leadership and governance, Transparency, Feedback and complaints, Participation, Design, monitoring and evaluation). Women and girls are key actors in their own protection, and it is critical that they are active partners in identifying protection risks and solutions. UNFPA will facilitate their active involvement in the design, implementation and monitoring of this Action, by consulting with women and girls on GBV risks and constraints to their participation in and access to aid delivery, GBV and SRH services. UNFPA will also work with women and girls to identify those who are most marginalized and at high risk, and design approaches to ensure their participation. UNFPA will engage with women, girls, boys, and men from the affected communities to explain protection activities, facilitate community acceptance and address barriers to women's and girls' attendance.
