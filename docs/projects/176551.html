Project Summary

The proposed project will provide timely lifesaving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable under 5 years children, Pregnant and lactating women and increased access to maternal, infant and young child nutrition knowledge to nurture behaviour change in Aweil North and Canal Pigi Counties.
The project will target 21,800 beneficiaries in the two Counties with Management of MAM and SAM: This
selection is based on the latest IPC data Jan to March 2021 for Aweil North which shows the population in severe and extreme levels of food insecurity as 44,200 and 43,200 respectively. Children brought to the nutrition centres will be assessed using MUAC and weight for height and admitted onto the relevant program depending on meeting the admission criteria. Those diagnosed with SAM without medical complications will be admitted to the OTP and given weekly rations of Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) as well as routine medications in accordance with CMAM protocols. OTP sessions will be conducted on a weekly basis targeting children 6-59 months with SAM without medical complications. All children discharged as cured from OTP will be referred to TSFP according to national protocols and treated as per the MAM cases. Moderate acute malnutrition will be diagnosed through the same methods as SAM and will be treated through targeted supplementary feeding programs (TSFP), which will operate out of SAADO nutrition centres and outreach sites. Children found to be SAM with complications will be referred to MSF new Fangak stabilization center for inpatient management. TSFP supplies will be provided by the World Food Program (WFP) and RUTF will be provided by UNICEF through Nilehope FLAs and PCAs.
SAADO aims to strengthen Maternal Infant and young child nutrition knowledge and nurture optimal feeding practices for the first 1000 days and beyond. Community engagement in promotion and advocacy of breastfeeding through information dissemination and campaigns. Individual and group counselling will be conducted to women with specialized MIYCN identified need and a group woman with optimal feeding practices challenges. Trained MIYCN counsellor will be recruited and trained on the counselling techniques to support vulnerable caregivers identify challenges and draw resolutions together.
The lead mothers will be responsible for engaging 200 support groups members in discussion and providing basic health and nutrition education in an interactive participatory manner, as well as taking an active role in the community and kitchen gardens. Through this fund, SAADO aims to continue encouraging MTMSGs in Aweil north, Aweil south and canal/pigi counties in the use of ecologically-appropriate technologies for sustainable vegetable production. Through a participatory community approach, members of the MTMSG and Men advocates will be supported to establish a keyhole garden to cultivate micronutrient-rich vegetables either in their kitchen garden or small farms. To improve access to food security and its diversity, the mother-to-mother support groups will be supported with vegetable seeds and provided with technical support to establish and maintain nutrition gardens. Gunny bag gardening is an economical way to grow vegetable as it utilizes air space above the physical limits of the farm thereby maximizing production where land and water is limited. When vegetables are grown in the bags, there is almost no weed control, and pest control is easily done traditionally. The purpose of the cooking demonstration is to improve and diversify family feeding practices and food intake, with a specific focus on improving the nutritional quality of families’ and young children’s diets thus contribute to food and nutrition secure community.

1 - Needs

Besides the lapse of two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan is likely to continue to worsen in 2021, driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence, further aggravated by subsequent shocks. Continued macroeconomic crisis, the low preparedness capacity of national and state authorities, COVID-19 pandemic, relatively lower oil prices, and consecutive years of flooding are expected to further exacerbate existing vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of people, while pushing more people downward spiral into crisis and reliance on humanitarian assistance. Despite many interventions in place northern Bahr el ghazel and Jonglei states suffered a great deal given that they experienced additional catastrophes. According to IPC 3 report, between October to November 2020, an estimated 481,000 people faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 330,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 151,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in northern Bar el ghazel. The key drivers of food insecurity include the seasonal depletion of own stocks, particularly for households whose crop production was negatively affected by climatic shocks; increased market dependence at a time when food prices are expected to rise further; the unpredictable security situation along the border with Sudan that is likely to impact on trade; the ongoing economic crisis that is likely to continue eroding household purchasing power in a highly market-dependent State; migration of livestock away from homesteads that will lead to decreased access to livestock products; and COVID-19 measures that are likely to have an impact on the supply chain systems and affect market functionality. Households will increasingly rely on petty trade and selling of natural resources for income, as well as consuming of wild foods.In the corridors of south pigi in Jonglei state, the in October and November 2020, an estimated 1.38 million people (69.8% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 724,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 648,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in Jonglei state. COVID-19 pandemic, floods in Jonglei and heightened inter-communal conflict were shocks that tremendously impacted access to nutrition services in the country. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM admission criteria for children have exacerbated access to services alongside other factors such as heightened inter-communal conflict and insecurity, worsening economic crises, flooding, among others.Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, anthropometric assessment was suspended in 2020 in line with the global guidance. Therefore, no recent anthropometric data was available to inform the October 2020 IPC AMN analysis. However, in reference to nutrition cluster assessment canal pigi and Aweil north counties have proxy GAM rate is 11.9% and 24.3% according to SMART survey 2020 which calls for emergency nutrition interventions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will utilize the established beneficiary communication and accountability (BCA) mechanisms and project management committees (PMC’s) to provide information to the beneficiaries as well as address issues around prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Whenever such issues are raised SAADO’s protection staff will link the survivors with protection implementing partners as part of strengthening the referral pathways. The project management committees will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow beneficiaries to provide feedback and anonymously report grievances and complaints relatedto PSEA. The PMCs will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints. The project management committees will be trained to follow SAADO’s PSEA policy standards and guidance for reporting and investigation and any other requirements as per national law.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations withdifferent age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SAADO’s proposed project will establish Beneficiary Communication and Accountability (BCA) mechanisms for communicating information relating to the project, as well as soliciting feedback, grievances and complaints from beneficiaries and will take into consideration issues such as traditional decision-making structures, access to communications, local culture and literacy. SAADO’s commitment to accountability will be clearly demonstrated in ensuring that the project beneficiaries in the targeted counties have a genuine voice in the assessments, design and decision-making process that inform the priorities, modalities and trajectories of this proposed project. In addition, aspart of their orientation training and continual development, the project personnel receive instruction on why AAP is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area SAADO will ensure that communication with the community and key stakeholders is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and nonbeneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.
