Project Summary

WFP is implementing a nutrition response frame with stronger focus towards prevention of malnutrition. The programme focuses on managing and preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months including those children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition-MAM), children aged 24-59 months suffering from MAM and PBWGs in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, and East North west and south west regions. The programme activities include: 1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment; 2. Management of modarete acute malnutrition in children between the ages of 6-59months, 3: Promotion of infant and young child feeding practices 4; Distribution of specialized nutritious food on cash transfers to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant and lactating women and girls(PLWGs),5.Social and behavioral change communication in favor of good nutrition and health practices 6 .Promotion of the use of the supplementary feeding platforms for provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etc. WFP intends to provide Nutrition assistance to 29227 nutritionally vulnerable persons on the North west and South west regions in 2024

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

It is estimated that in 2024, more than 230,700 people including children under five and pregnant and lactating women will need nutrition services in the North-West, South-West, West and Littoral regions. Up to 48,800 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in these regions including 11,600 with severe acute malnutrition. Populations living in remote areas, IDPs in urban/peri-urban areas, and households/communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). The nutrition situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1 per cent and 7.1 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively. Analysis from Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners from July to December 2021, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 1.5 and SAM prevalence of 0.4 per cent in the North-West and the South-West regions. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition status in the two regions as there could be pockets of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified. Prior to the crisis, the North-West and South-West regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36 per cent and 28 per cent in the North-West and the South-West respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5 per cent for the North-West and 58.3 per cent for South-West, of exclusive breast-feeding rates. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8 per cent in the North-West and 20.7 per cent in the South-West. Approximately 40 per cent (44.3 per cent in the North-West and 41.9 per cent in the South-West) of children aged 6 to 59 months were reported to have anemia and with the ongoing humanitarian crisis, it is very likely that the poor situation of MDD has worsened.WFP’s Programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000 days of life. The Programme targets all children aged 6-23months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in a food-insecure household. With a package of activities including, distribution of specialized nutritious food, screening and referral of malnourished cases to relevant programmes for treatment and social behavior change communication in favor of appropriate IYCF practices in emergency. WFP in 2022 intends to target about 30,000 beneficiaries in the NWSW regions
