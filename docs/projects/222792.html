Project Summary

The SAWAB project aims to enhance educational opportunities for Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar by creating an inclusive, safe, and supportive learning environment. The project involves the operation of twenty-three established learning centers across five camps: Camp 7 (Block B-8), Camp 8E (Block B-67), Camp 9 (Block C-14), Camp 18 (Block E-4), and Camp 19 (Blocks B-11 and D-1). Additionally, there are plans to establish five more learning centers in Camp 14 (Block E). The project targets a total of 1,130 students, including 310 in Camp 7, 50 in Camp 8E, 200 in Camp 9, 250 in Camp 18, 170 in Camp 19, and 150 in Camp 14. This initiative specifically includes marginalized groups such as learners with disabilities and LGBTQI individuals. The target demographics consist of 980 existing children in Kindergarten (KG) and Grades 1 to 5, along with 150 new enrollments in Grades 6 through 10.
Key initiatives include comprehensive teacher training and professional development aligned with the Myanmar Curriculum, ensuring educators have the necessary skills for quality instruction. Training will cover topics such as gender-based violence (GBV) protection, child protection, hygiene management, inclusion of persons with disabilities, and mental health awareness. By focusing on teacher professional growth, the project aims to improve student learning outcomes and foster a supportive educational atmosphere.
Regular student assessments will monitor progress and identify areas needing additional support. Educational materials will be distributed to enhance the learning experience, ensuring students have access to necessary resources. Additionally, school feeding programs supported by WFP will promote well-being and encourage regular attendance.
Safeguarding is a critical component, with designated focal points for GBV and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). A comprehensive safeguarding policy will be enforced to ensure the safety of all participants, including regular risk assessments and staff training on safeguarding principles.
Community engagement will be integral, involving parents and caregivers in the educational process. Workshops and training sessions will enhance parents’ understanding of effective parenting and supporting their children’s education, strengthening community-school connections.
The project will emphasize inclusive education by conducting individual education planning for learners with disabilities and providing tailored home-based education support. Accessible teaching materials will ensure all students can participate fully, and inclusive sports and recreational activities will promote physical well-being and social integration.
Awareness sessions will prepare individuals, especially those with special needs, for emergencies, highlighting safe routes and evacuation points. The project will involve children and youth with disabilities in child rights committees and community-based structures to promote their rights and participation.
In summary, the SAWAB project is committed to fostering an enriching educational environment for 1,130 Rohingya learners in Kindergarten and Grades 1 through 10 across Camps 7, 8E, 9, 14, 18, and 19. This will be achieved with the support of 62 skilled and qualified teachers and facilitators, working collaboratively with all education stakeholders. Through a comprehensive approach focused on quality education, safeguarding, community engagement, and inclusivity, the project aims to empower students and their families while promoting harmony within the Cox’s Bazar communities. The total project budget is set at $ 270,581 for 2025, ensuring sustainable support for these initiatives and lasting positive impact.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The project is developed in response to identified needs within the education sector in Cox’s Bazar, as highlighted in several key assessments and reports. The educational landscape in Cox's Bazar faces significant challenges for Rohingya refugee children. The need for improved educational access and quality is evident from various assessments, including the Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA) 2024 and the Education Sector Year-End Assessment 2024.Over 84,000 learners participated in the Year-End Assessment, highlighting a critical need for educational facilities and resources. With 1,030 students targeted for enrollment across camps, including Camp 7, Camp 8E, Camp 9, Camp 14, Camp 18, and Camp 19, substantial barriers remain, particularly for marginalized groups. Many children, including those with disabilities and girls, face obstacles to attending school, necessitating efforts to create safe and inclusive learning environments.Language proficiency is a significant concern, particularly in Burmese. Assessments show that 17% of students require additional support in Burmese, while only 3% need assistance in English. This disparity indicates an urgent need for enhanced Burmese language instruction to improve comprehension of educational materials. Targeted teacher training can help address this gap and boost overall academic performance.Mathematics and science are particularly challenging subjects, with 30% and 31% of students respectively needing extra support. Students scored an average of 36.32% in mathematics, with grade 3 students scoring the lowest at 32.9%. In science, many learners struggle to meet expected competencies, highlighting the need for additional resources, practical learning experiences, and specialized training for teachers.Gender considerations are critical, as data show that boys outperform girls across all subjects. While 88% of boys and 86% of girls met grade-level competencies, the marginal gap indicates that girls require additional academic support, especially in mathematics and science. Implementing gender-sensitive teaching strategies and targeted interventions can help bridge this gap and create a more equitable learning environment.The assessments underscore a pressing need for ongoing Teacher Professional Development (TPD) programs. Continuous training in effective teaching strategies, particularly in mathematics and science, along with gender-sensitive approaches, is crucial. Well-trained teachers are essential for addressing subject-specific gaps and providing necessary support for all learners.The lack of interim assessments presents a challenge in identifying and addressing learning gaps. Currently, students undergo only one year-end evaluation. Implementing mid-year summative assessments is essential to monitor progress and provide timely interventions for those at risk of falling behind.Safety remains a significant barrier to attendance, particularly for adolescent girls. Although 93% of learners feel safe traveling to and from school, 7% report safety concerns, including fears of abduction and threats from armed groups. Addressing these safety issues through improved security measures and safe routes to schools is vital for increasing attendance and retention rates.The identified needs in the Cox's Bazar education sector reflect urgent requirements for improved access, quality education, and safety for all learners. By addressing these needs through targeted interventions and comprehensive training programs, we can create a more inclusive and effective educational environment that supports the diverse needs of Rohingya and host community children. Insights from the ISNA and the Education Sector Year-End Assessment provide a crucial foundation for developing this project, ensuring responsiveness to the real challenges faced by these communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project aligns with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note on protection mainstreaming by integrating protective elements throughout its design to safeguard vulnerable populations, especially children, adolescents, and individuals with special needs among Rohingya refugees and host communities. This supports a zero-tolerance policy for exploitation and abuse.Robust safeguarding procedures, including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Rights and Safeguarding, are fundamental. Comprehensive training for staff and key stakeholders fosters a culture of vigilance and responsibility. Strategies include thorough background checks, reference verifications, and Codes of Conduct (CoCs) that comply with safeguarding recommendations. Contracts with partners and subcontractors explicitly incorporate safeguarding practices.The project implements risk assessments and maintains a structured risk register to identify and mitigate potential risks proactively. Multiple reporting channels—such as supervisors, safeguarding focal points, helplines, and whistleblowing processes—ensure compliance. Comprehensive case management and response mechanisms are in place to efficiently handle reported incidents while adhering to protocols.SAWAB’s commitment to protection mainstreaming ensures that the safety, rights, and well-being of the most vulnerable remain central throughout all activities. This includes integrating protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity in humanitarian efforts, focusing on four guiding principles: prioritizing safety and dignity; ensuring meaningful access; fostering accountability; and promoting participation and empowerment.Additionally, the project encompasses the eight core components of the Protection Mainstreaming Framework: analyzing the protection context, identifying specific needs, sharing information about rights, engaging communities, and providing safe feedback mechanisms for timely responses.

2.3 - Age & Disability

SAWAB's project demonstrates a commitment to addressing Age and Disability issues, in line with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note. It aims to target at least 3% of persons with disabilities (PWD), with the goal of increasing this to 12% when feasible.The initiative includes sector-specific training on inclusive programming for staff and volunteers and developing accessible communication materials. Accessibility audits will be conducted in facilities to ensure they meet the needs of all users. Targeted programming will focus on PWD, older persons, and other vulnerable groups. Individual education planning sessions will cater to learners with disabilities, complemented by the purchase or development of accessible teaching materials.SAWAB will offer porter services for PWD and the elderly, ensuring priority queuing and door-to-door service for women and girls with disabilities. The project will provide accessible information regarding early warning and emergency preparedness, engaging PWD in disaster risk management activities, including camp disaster committees.Inclusive sports and recreational activities for all children will be organized, along with awareness sessions on preparedness and safe routes for individuals with special needs, including the elderly and PWD.The project emphasizes inclusivity by enrolling at least 3% of learners with disabilities and from the LGBTQI community in all educational facilities. Adherence to safeguarding and PSEA policies, in collaboration with stakeholders, ensures a secure environment, particularly for children.SAWAB's efforts in health and hygiene, PSEA, and mental health support aim to empower communities with disabilities and those from the LGBTQI spectrum. This comprehensive approach guarantees that the needs of various age groups and individuals with disabilities are recognized and addressed, aligning effectively with the guidelines in the 2025 JRP Guidance Note.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is a commitment by humanitarian actors to responsibly address the needs of those they assist. SAWAB’s project emphasizes AAP through actions aligned with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note. The project draws on findings from participatory assessments and analyses, such as the 2024 ISNA. Community involvement is key in evaluation methodologies, with results shared in accessible formats. Annual consultations will inform project evaluations, and beneficiary selection processes will be transparent, engaging affected populations in prioritization. SAWAB will address common issues by translating response plans into accessible formats for communities, holding meetings in each camp to explain these plans. A coordinated strategy will communicate information on delivered and upcoming assistance. Regular monitoring of the affected population’s perceptions will occur through Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) and surveys. Community participation will be emphasized throughout the project, with timely updates on service changes based on feedback. Dialogues will ensure information is clear and accessible through various channels. All materials will be available in English, Bangla, Burmese, and Rohingya-English transliteration. Inclusive practices will address the needs of all demographic groups, especially women, older persons, persons with disabilities (PWD), and youth. SAWAB commits to information dissemination to keep stakeholders informed about its mission and accountability commitments, fostering open communication. Transparent feedback mechanisms will be established to enable swift issue resolution. The project focuses on continual learning and adaptation, integrating lessons learned to better serve affected populations. Overall, SAWAB promotes transparency, inclusive participation, responsive feedback, and adaptive learning, ensuring accountability to those impacted by the project.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

SAWAB will engage actively in the PSEA Network to minimize risks of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and ensure effective responses to incidents. All SEA allegations will be reported to the UN Resident Coordinator, with proper investigations and actions taken as per established procedures.SAWAB will assess SEA risks regularly, considering local contexts and the vulnerabilities of affected populations, addressing identified risks with support from the PSEA Network. The organization will involve communities, especially vulnerable groups, in designing prevention and reporting mechanisms. PSEA awareness materials will be displayed prominently to inform beneficiaries about reporting options.In projects using Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM), SAWAB will train all staff to handle SEA disclosures safely, ensuring accessible reporting channels. PSEA activities will be integrated into project plans, aligning with PSEA Network priorities.The PSEA focal point will oversee PSEA integration and coordinate with the PSEA Network. The organization’s code of conduct will include PSEA principles, prohibiting SEA for all contractors.SAWAB will provide mandatory PSEA training, including regular refreshers, for all project staff and related personnel. The project lead will promote an environment that prevents SEA, making PSEA a regular agenda item in project coordination meetings.Through these initiatives, SAWAB will align with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note on PSEA mainstreaming, ensuring engagement, comprehensive training, visibility for reporting, culturally sensitive feedback mechanisms, adherence to procedures, and holistic protection for all learners, especially vulnerable groups, to reduce the risk of abuse.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The SAWAB project is committed to incorporating climate change adaptation and mitigation strategies to enhance resilience among Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar. To achieve effective climate change adaptation, the project will adopt a comprehensive approach involving several key activities.SAWAB will develop a Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) plan that outlines strategies to minimize the impact of disasters, such as floods and storms, which are increasingly frequent due to climate change. This plan will include risk assessments, identifying vulnerable populations, and implementing measures to reduce community risks.SAWAB will also create a contingency plan detailing actions to be taken before, during, and after a disaster. This proactive strategy ensures resources are allocated efficiently and that the community is prepared to respond quickly to emergencies, minimizing potential harm.Additionally, SAWAB will establish a communication tree to facilitate effective coordination during disasters. This system will ensure that all community members, especially vulnerable groups, receive timely and accurate information. The project will collaborate with the Camp Disaster Management Committee to streamline emergency preparedness and response.Furthermore, SAWAB will strengthen information-sharing and decision-making processes to enhance humanitarian-disaster risk reduction linkages. By integrating Climate Action through Disaster Risk Management (DRM) and anticipatory actions, the project will prepare for immediate disasters while anticipating future climate-related challenges.SAWAB will design learning facilities with disaster-resilient features to serve as shelters during emergencies. This approach aligns with the ISCG’s Multi-Hazard Response Plan and the National Plan for Disaster Management (2021–2025), ultimately enhancing community resilience. Through these strategies, the SAWAB project will play a vital role in building a safer future for Rohingya refugees.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, SAWAB’s project incorporates a comprehensive Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) framework, including a business continuity plan and emergency response strategies. SAWAB prioritizes strong coordination with government entities, the EPR Working Group, and operational partners to ensure efficiency and avoid duplication of efforts. The project is disaster risk-informed, with clear adherence to existing reporting mechanisms, enhancing EPR effectiveness. Gender inclusion is central to EPR efforts, promoting diverse representation in disaster response roles. These measures collectively strengthen SAWAB’s capacity to address emergencies swiftly and sustainably.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 15-18, particularly focusing on their educational needs up to Grade 10. This includes implementing sector-specific tips to tailor educational interventions to young people's priorities. Participatory approaches are integrated into program design, implementation, and monitoring, allowing youth to voice their opinions and assert their agency in the educational process. Referral pathways among partners will be established to enhance collaboration and support for youth, ensuring they receive the necessary resources for their education. Moreover, staff and volunteers working directly with adolescents receive specialized training to effectively communicate with this age group, ensuring that interactions are appropriate and engaging. By prioritizing educational engagement for youth through Grade 10, the project fosters an inclusive environment that empowers young people to take an active role in their learning and future opportunities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the SAWAB project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by providing Teacher and Facilitator Professional Development Training, which equips educators with skills in curriculum alignment, safeguarding, inclusion, mental health support, and hygiene education. This training helps create a safe, inclusive learning environment and empowers teachers to take on leadership roles within the community. The project also involves refugees in decision-making and offers training in emergency preparedness, gender-based violence (GBV) awareness, and social cohesion. By integrating disaster risk-informed approaches and supporting camp management committees with refugee representation, SAWAB strengthens both individual and community resilience and fosters community-led preparedness.
