Project Summary

UNICEF will provide women, men, girls, and boys affected by multiple shocks and hazards in South Sudan with critical water, sanitation, and hygiene services, items, and facilities. The objective of this project is to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services for conflict affected people, vulnerable host communities and people in disease prone (high GAM famine and outbreak related) areas including protection related actions to mitigate GBV, strengthening of communities’ resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks. This emergency WASH project will comprise of situation assessments, preparedness, transitional programs, frontline emergency WASH response, strengthening of partners’ capacity, and cluster coordination including sector information management. UNICEF will deliver emergency lifesaving support by providing access to WASH services as per Sphere and WASH Cluster standards.
The project will focus on addressing the following:
- Effective Cluster coordination mechanism in the country
- Provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees, including transitional programming
- WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention with a minimum package of interventions.
- WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level (i.e. Ebola, Cholera, etc.)
- While implementing projects a special attention will be given to mitigate WASH-related GBV.
The project will also support expansion of partnerships with local and international NGOs and CBOs in highly affected areas to ensure basic services are provided and long-term capacity built. The activities will be implemented in complementarity with other sectors such as Health, Nutrition, Protection and where possible Education projects including disaster risk reduction (DRR) focusing on strengthening community coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

WASH access throughout South Sudan remains critically low with 64 percent of households either lacking access to an improved water source within 30 minutes or reporting protection concerns to access these sources and 73 percent of the country practicing open defecation. A conflict sensitivity analysis done by UNICEF in 2020 concluded that water represents a key driver of conflict across the locations of the study in a variety of ways with water-related conflict mirroring the fluidity of wider conflict patterns IDP sites still remain without durable solutions for WASH services with long-term financial requirements for emergency services and no current closure plan for enduring sites with 300,000 people in the previous protection of civilian (POC) sites. Poor access to WASH services contributes to the high levels of food insecurity with an overlap of WASH Cluster priority counties with critical needs and areas reporting high GAM rates. Water-borne diseases, including diarrhea, contribute to negative nutritional outcomes and contribute to the risk of famine. In addition to WASH infrastructure needs in nutrition treatment centers, mothers and caretakers of malnourished children require access to hygiene information and non-food items to practice safe hygiene in support of nutrition improvements. The WASH Cluster reports a spike in the number of cases of acute watery diarrhea and acute bloody diarrhea in 2020 with 593,000 reported cases by October 2020 with anticipated increases in 2021 due to multiple humanitarian shocks in 2020. With sixteen percent of individuals reporting safety concerns when fetching water women and girls remain the most vulnerable to incidents of gender based violence due to limited WASH access. Inclusive WASH infrastructure remains a gap for the 15 percent of households with a family member living with a disability.

2 - Project activities

Integrate WASH response with Nutrition/ (Food Security) through WASH service provision in nutrition facilities (WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion to SAM children) and access to safe water to facilities in IPC 4/5 locations. Provide sustained water to communities showing high GAM prevalence and poor access to water. Integrate WASH response with Health by providing sustained water services to communities showing high prevalence of WASH-related diseases Provide access to basic services through rapid assistance in all counties and populations affected by a sudden- or rapid-onset disaster mainly with temporary services, but also if applicable semi-permanent solutions. Mitigate Gender-Based Violence by conducting safety audits at site level (or use protection mainstreaming tip sheet) to identify GBV concerns and implement mitigation measures. Build WASH resilience by providing permanent access to water and sanitation services for IDPs, returnees, hosts and chronically affected by floods and (pre-) famine conditions

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Vouchers for water and kiosk systems are in line with the current market for water sales. Voucher programs for NFIs are only applicable where markets are functioning and the target items are available.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Voucher programs will be included for water payments in former POC sites where sustainable solutions for water supply are implemented as a transitional method to train and adapt kiosk attendants and develop a connection of monetary value on water provision. In areas where markets are functioning and hygiene items are available locally UNICEF aims to incorporate supply provision through vouchers as applicable.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All UNICEF partners are assessed for PSEA processes prior to agreements to mitigate the risk of SEA. UNICEF provides training for partners to develop PSEA action plans which are context specific for the target beneficiaries, program activities, and operational specifics.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Individual consultations with vulnerable members of affected populations will be used to inform programming, implementation, and monitoring. Minimum targets are set for all programs for inclusion of women in community leadership roles, including but not limited to water committees and project management teams._

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A central component of all WASH programs includes the engagement of women and men in community water committees to empower ownership of facilities. Voucher programs will also include physical complaint desks with information to communities and feedback loops to providers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As the lead agency for the WASH Cluster UNICEF will continue to support the collection, dissemination, and technical assistance for partners assessing partner feedback to adapt overall WASH Cluster programming. This will include UNICEF implemented programs in addition to the technical support provided to the broader cluster.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

UNICEF is prioritizing underserved populations in IPC 3,4, and 5 levels through provision of durable water supply solutions and behavior change promotion for sanitation to increase access to facilities and services that will allow households to implement safe hygiene practices to improve nutritional outcomes of target populations. In addition to this UNICEF will support nutrition centers with critical water supply and sanitation facilities and provide hygiene information and items to patients to enable patients to improve their nutritional status.
