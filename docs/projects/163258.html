1 - Needs

Libya is contaminated with mines, cluster munitions and other explosive remnants of war (ERW) including Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs), from historical conflict, the uprising in 2011 and intermittent armed violence in the years that followed (especially 2014 and 2019). Explosive hazards affect the daily security of civilians, in particular children, risking life and limb. An estimated 448,000 people are in need of mine action assistance as a result of widespread contamination from explosive hazards, particularly in urban areas. According to the 2019 MSNA, “nine percent of returnees and eight percent of IDPs reported the presence of explosive hazards in their neighbourhood. Nearly 3.5 percent of all households reported at least one member of the household either killed or injured by explosive hazards”. There is national data available on the levels of contamination in Libya from mines and ERW (latest report from LibMac has been shared on October 24th 2019, but the figures have obviously increased since, especially in Tripoli area). Priority areas include Tripoli and the surrounding areas, Tawergha, Sirte, Gharyan Sebha, Muzruq, Benghazi and Dernah for the removal of the most significant threats.In all areas subjected to recent fighting, a comprehensive mine action response is required to increase the safety of local and returning populations through education on the risks posed by explosive hazards, the survey, clearance and destruction of identified unexploded ordnance as well as victim assistance (VA). Specific focus will be made on strengthening capacities of clearance and RE staff to enable rapid response. Contamination also impacts on the reconstruction of damaged infrastructure and the access of humanitarian personnel to areas of past and recent conflict. All mine action projects conducted in Libya will also work in support of the LibMAC in enhancing data collection to enable targeted deployment of teams in priority areas.

2 - Activities

- Deployment of Community Liaison Officer;- Identification of agents of change to disseminate key awareness messages (community leaders/teachers, medical staffs, etc.);- Set up of a referral mechanism and facilitate telephone hotline for community reporting of suspected hazardous areas to HI in collaboration with LibMAC;- Delivery of direct and indirect Risk Education sessions by HI teams;- ERW NTS conducted in areas with suspected contamination;- Training and preparation for deployment of Multi-task clearance teams;- Non-Technical and Technical Survey and Clearance (removal and destruction of ERW if situation allows) conducted in suspected and confirmed hazardous areas;- Reporting to the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA);-Stock positioning; -Referrals to rehabilitation and MHPSS structures.
