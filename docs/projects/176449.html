Project Summary

This 12 months’ project plan is to provide safe, protective, and equalised participation and learning outcomes across gender and disability for 18,355 (10,830 girls) children and youth children aged 3-17 through the provision of school facilities, quality delivery of curriculum, safe teacher conduct and community participation in 52 primary & secondary schools; 5 County Education Departments (CEDs) maintaining teacher quality built on continuous professional development and teacher evaluation systems for 468 (154 female) primary & secondary school teachers; 468 PTA members (154 female) and 50 (10 female) local education leaders developing education service delivery & improvement plans to reduce the risk to fail in the long- term in Ayod, Duk & Bor South Counties of Jonglei State and Ulang & Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State, South Sudan.
Key project activities include enrolling 18,355 (51% girls) children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers; establishing 20 temporary learning spaces and classrooms; rehabilitating 20 temporary learning spaces and classrooms; providing 5,513 (2,811 girls) children aged 3-5 children with ECD kits; establishing 30 gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion; rehabilitating 20 gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion; procuring and supplying 130 sets of Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to students 1,985 (1,171 girls) for ECD; 14,057 (8,294 girls) for primary; and 662 (232 girls for secondary); procuring and supplying 130 sets of sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to teachers- 201 (70 female) for Ayod; 56 (19 female) for Duk; 66 (23 female) for Bor South; 98 (34 female) for Ulang; and 47 (16 female) for Longechuk); providing emergency-affected 18,355 (51%) children with 50 quality education recreational materials; training 1, 200 (400 female) teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching-learning process for emergency-affected children; training and update 1, 200 teachers and 50,116 learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms; training 1, 200 (400 female) teachers and 50 (10 female) education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition; training 1, 170 (520 female) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures; and organizing 10 back to learning campaigns in Ayod, Duk, Bor South, Ulang & Longechuk Counties (2 each).
There will be 24 schools in Ayod County targeting enrollment of 8,448 (4,007 girls) children aged 3-17 years; supporting 201 teachers (70 female), 201 (70 female) members of 50 PTAs & 10 (2 female) local education leaders. In Duk County, 6 schools will enroll about 2,183 (1,580 girls) children; support 56 (19 female) teachers, 56 (17 female) PTA members, and 10 (2 female) local education leaders. In Bor South 7 schools will provide access to 2,244 children (1,854 girls); support 66 (23 female) teachers, 66 (20 female) PTA members & 10 (2 female) local education leaders. Ulang County will have 10 schools with 3,620 (1,878 girls) children; 98 (34 female) teachers, 98 (31 female) PTA members, and 10 (2 female) local education leaders. Longechuk will have 5 schools as well but with about 1,860 (835 girls); 47 (16 female) teachers, 47 (13 female) PTA members, and 10 (2 female) local education leaders.

1 - Needs

October 2020 -July 2021 IPC indicates the effects of Covid-19, food insecurity levels, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods as key drivers in exacerbating an already poor economic outlook with 8.3 million people in need in South Sudan comprising of 54% or 4.3 million children (0-17 years). Ayod, Duk, and Bor South Counties of Jonglei State and Ulang & Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State now have an extra burden; with many schools in the Counties affected and damaged by the floods and violence which has been recurrent and some of them occupied by IDPs. This has delayed the re-opening of schools in many areas. Access to education as well as protection and legal services is limited and much of the service infrastructure was damaged, destroyed or closed in 2020. A CMD-led inter-agency IRNA in Ayod County in August 2020 found out that the floods had affected upwards of 17,000 people in Wau, Mogok, Pajiek and with women and children bearing the brunt. Schools were found to be occupied by the displaced population as well as livestock. Most Teaching and Learning materials were completely damaged and Learning infrastructure including Temporary learning Spaces and latrines had collapsed. In Bor South, CMD carried out a baseline assessment in September 2020 to ascertain school needs for education in an emergency (EiE) in 15 schools. Only 3 schools out of 15 had minimum WASH facilities. The situation is not different in Ulang and Longechuk Counties (Upper Nile State) where CMD conducted a rapid school-based WASH assessment in October and established that 4, 033 children (43% girls) in 10 schools of Ulang and 3, 740 children (40% girls) in 7 schools of Longechuk lack adequate TLS and WASH structures for schools to reopen. In the same vein a rapid school-based WASH assessment for Ayod in February 2021 also pointed out that 12 schools are in dire need of WASH facilities for them to be able to reopen in April 2021 for 3, 570 (40% girls). CMD basic assessments on Children with Disabilities (CWDs), School Safety Audit and GBV Risk Mitigation in School Settings in Duk County in October 2020 found out that of the forms of disabilities existing in schools, 60% of the disabled children are physically disabled; 20% said the children are blind while the smallest proportion of 15% of the teachers said children are deaf. Findings from an on-site safety audit for 5 schools in the County revealed that 17% of the students interviewed had been harassed in some way through being hit or bullied threatened with 10% saying they had seen a student with a gun on school grounds; 7% said they have seen a student with a knife on school grounds. 20% said someone has been mean to them because of their gender or disability, and 30% said they were not safe at their schools. The findings are also shared by a GBV risk mitigation survey in Duk County where all learners interviewed noted that their schools do not have fences and controlled access points with 40% saying they sometimes feel unsafe. On GBV awareness, prevention, and response, 50% of the teachers said they have never been trained on GBV prevention and response in school settings. CMD’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2021, therefore, seeks to increase equitable access to, safe and protective and participation of 50,116 vulnerable children and youth aged 3-17 years (24,556 boys & 25,560 girls) in Ayod, Duk & Bor South Counties of Jonglei State and Ulang and Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State improve quality of teaching and learning; bridge the gender gap in access to education; improve access to quality education for 4, 164 (2, 478 girls) persons with disabilities; and improve management of education service delivery in Ayod, Duk and Bor South Counties of Jonglei State and Ulang & Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State. This target targets 18,355 of Education Cluster’s 2021 Target of 805,951.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will Include GBV mitigation as part of staff training & conduct. The project will Identify and minimize the risk of harm to children and adults from staff. In order to provide beneficiaries with information about PSEA, all training will incorporate elements of PSEA, indicating the engagement levels of the project staff with the community, reporting avenues available, their rights to benefit without being compromised, and more. The beneficiaries will be reminded that CMD staff interactions with children or with adult beneficiaries, or with personal data about such persons, will comply with the attached CMD’s Safeguarding Behaviour Protocols. - Any incidents of harm or risk of harm to children or to adult beneficiaries will be reported immediately through channels that will be explained and demonstrated to the community. - Beneficiaries will be reminded of all issues related to PSEA including reported cases being treated with utmost confidentiality; investigated and action taken. All actions taken will be discussed with the complainant after a specified time (feedback).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CMD will use the following Information sharing mechanisms: monthly focus group discussions and community meetings to share information; weekly or bi-monthly announcements through notice boards; quarterly (where feasible) printing and distribution of leaflets and posters; and monthly child participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from children about the support they receive or the spaces they attend (using simple child participatory tools, such as the H-assessment, to generate useful information for quick decision-making). The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through: - Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, students, youth out of school and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess child protection knowledge, attitudes and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with participatory assessment. Bi-annual public meetings will be organized to gain beneficiaries’ feedback in a structured format, enabling different stakeholders to discuss and exchange views on specific topics, often adopting a face-to-face format which allows for brainstorming and testing ideas. In addition, in-depth interviews (face-to-face or virtual interviews) with individual stakeholders will be used to get a sense of stakeholders’ perspectives; and key informants/stakeholder assessments will used occasionally to explore an issue in depth and permit related issues to be introduced into the discussion. Interviews with key informants possessing particular knowledge of an issue can be especially useful. CMD will use its internal tools of checklists (pre-prepared lists of items to assess numerous activities rapidly, suitable for self-assessments, classroom observations and audit purposes); activity logs ( records kept by teachers/administrators of specific activities such as instructional practices, learner-teacher interactions, learner behavior and assessment of classroom layouts; and infrastructure audits ( assessments of the levels of education infrastructure, access and availability) to keep track of its information-sharing strategy. For complaint and feedback mechanisms, CMD will use focus group discussions/community meetings; help desks; suggestion boxes (including information on how the boxes will be handled); and child-friendly feedback mechanism (use of H-assessment, body mapping, etc.). CMD’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will provide continuous information and feedback on the quality of services provided, gaps and ways forward to strengthen the response. It will also be part of monitoring exercises in schools/TLSs and lead consultation meetings at school-level. Complaint desks with a focal point shall be made known to all stakeholders in Poktap, Ageer, Pagaak, Dornyang and Ayueldit Payams of Duk County to voice their concerns and the desk focal points shall make field visits at intervals to hear from the beneficiaries. During these visits women and girls shall be given priority to air out their views in complete confidentiality. CMD will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. This mechanism will follow CMD guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including CFM boxes in schools and safe spaces, as well as through static helpdesks. CMD’s MEAL Team will identify risks, classify any high-risk activities, and document steps being taken to reduce or remove these risks for schoolchildren; develop and implement an action plan to ensure the child’s/children’s safety. Child-friendly information will be made available to ensure children understand about their safety and protection, and feel comfortable about speaking to someone if they have any concerns.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CMD will develop mitigation measures for potential protection risks associated with implementing this plan. In the event of closure of schools and disruption of learning as a result of social distancing guidelines as a measure to contain the spread of COVID 19, CMD will mobilize and sensitize the school community and stakeholders (teachers, students, parents’ committees) about COVID 19 disease – this includes the use of megaphones, house to house visits maintaining the MoH standard of physical distance; develop, print and distribute appropriate IEC materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures; identify volunteers/teachers to support home-based learning and catch-up classes in small groups (ensuring social distance standards); and train volunteers/teachers on distance learning and related methods. In the event of finding difficulties for women to participate in the intervention, CMD will conduct community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and the importance of engaging women and girls. Where safety in schools (and traveling to and from school) for girls, other vulnerable groups is the issue, CMD will include school safety in training for CEDs and PTAs; provision of toilets in schools and/or establish community support for school participation, for example by encouraging community mechanisms that arrange to accompany groups of children to school if routes are considered unsafe. Where some schools may resist the admission of over-aged/stigmatized children (disabilities being a case in point), we shall work with teachers, PTAs, and communities to overcome negative cultural attitudes towards education for marginalized groups; and also maximize existing school infrastructure to meet population needs by using double, girls-only or other appropriate shifts. We shall assess both protection benefits and risks when designing and implementing a component on influencers when working with boys and men may create a backlash against adolescent girls and women or when target influencers abuse their power and children’s trust and engage in behavior that calls into question their commitment to gender equality and GBV prevention. Procedures for staff recruitment will contain appropriate child protection measures, including, wherever possible, the provision of background information and references.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Throughout the project, CMD will establish complaints/feedback help desks/help persons disaggregated by gender, people with specific needs, debilitated, old, and marginalized groups to gather daily and routine complaints. If the concerns can be addressed on the ground level, the feedback will be given in realtime, if it requires some bureaucratic procedures, the referral persons/powers will be engaged confidentially to attain amicable solutions. Moreover, there will be constant awareness campaigns to the public on PSEA and also capacity building capacity-building to enable different actors (e.g help desks/contact people and referral persons) to understand their roles and meet their responsibilities with regard to protection. CMD staff involved in the project will also undergo several PSEA refresher courses to spearhead the PSEA and other protection-related issues.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

CMD will focus on reducing risks and strengthening protective factors for children and youth, particularly girls, affected by conflict and internal displacement as well as from vulnerable host communities. The Project will mainstream effective female inclusion in all stages of project design and implementation. Particular attention will be given to tailoring activities that respect cultural norms and traditions for female beneficiaries. The Project will involve women, girls, and other at-risk groups in all aspects of programming while being duly cautious where their inclusion poses a potential security risk or increases their risk of GBV. Whenever feasible, data will be disaggregated by gender to track project progress and impact.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Targeting will be gender and AAP conscious. This project will benefit the most vulnerable; Child-headed, Elderly and Female-headed households under IPC 3 and 4 suffering from food insecurities and malnutrition. Protection will be mainstreamed in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender-Based Violence and ensure the Do No Harm principle is followed during beneficiary targeting and provision of support.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project has integrated Health, WASH, and Nutrition within the planned locations and will leverage existing services through coordination with other partners to manage MAM and SAM children with medical complications.
