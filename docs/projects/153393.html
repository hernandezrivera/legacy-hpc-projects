Project Summary

Hope for Children and Women Foundation (HCAWFO), through this project, will respond to assessed, prioritized, unmet health needs or gaps in primary health care service provision, to reduce morbidity and mortality of vulnerable girls, boys, women and men in conflict affected and other vulnerable in South Sudan. This project aims to improve access to quality preventative and curative primary health care services, including reproductive health. With the underlying vulnerabilities of the affected population, high maternal and under 5 mortality rates, this project will focus on improving access of quality lifesaving services to pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years. This project will provide essential primary health care services to IDPs, returnees and the host community in Magwi, Lafon and Torit Counties where the health system has been dysfunctional as a result of the conflict.
The project will provide HCAWFO’s health mobile response capacity to act as “Emergency Responders” in an emergency location following a needs assessment, as well as to scale-up this first response together with other partners. HCAWFO’s health emergency response team forms part of a well-established multisector emergency response team that has been responding to acute emergencies Eastern Equatoria State for more than 4 years. HCAWFO deploys health personnel and lifesaving assistance at short notice, to assess or respond to needs triggered by acute health emergencies in the South Sudan. HCAWFO's mobile teams propose to respond to the needs of communities affected by disease outbreak or other public health emergencies and to affected communities or impacted by the ongoing conflict.
This project also aims to support the State Ministry of Health, County Health Departments and other relevant authorities in emergency response capacity, training local male and female health workers to respond to health emergencies and providing training in disease surveillance, outbreak response, case management, reporting systems, and awareness of various health gender needs based on current disease trends.

Needs

Hope for Children and Women Foundation (HCAWFO) considers provision of essential and emergency health care services to displaced or vulnerable host populations as a significant need for the upcoming 12 months of 2019. The situation in Magwi, Lafon and Torit Counties, clearly show reduced capacity of the County Health Department (CHD), displacements of the people and destruction of facilities such as the PHCUs/PHCCs, this has crippled the health infrastructure and affected health service delivery. There are no proper referral systems from villages to the Counties put in place and inadequate health workers at the PHCUs. The primary health care coverage is far below the Minimum Sphere Standards. These areas are characterised by seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and also malaria is endemic posing high challenge to health systems which is fragile and disrupted. Displaced populations face the most complex challenges in accessing health care services. Children <5 years are one of the most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases owing to poor nutrition and low levels of Immunization coverage and immunity. The coverage in 2017 and 2018 for all vaccine preventable diseases remain under 50%, as a consequence 42% of the children <1year are at risk of measles. Women of reproductive age face serious health risks as they have inadequate access to skilled personnel during pregnancy and childbirth. Sexual and gender-based violence is rampant recoding high figures of 4,310 in 2016 and 5,732 in 2017 respectively with survivors lacking access to adequate services. Cross cutting issues like HIV/AIDS, TB, Mental health, disabilities and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. The large presence of military personnel, displaced population and high number of female headed households increases the risk of SGBV in Magwi, Lafon and Torit County. In Magwi County, intense conflict in 2016 caused disruption to all health and nutrition services. Destruction of health facilities and widespread looting of essential PHC drugs and health supplies as well as displacement of health care workers caused a crisis in health care access for vulnerable groups, particularly women and children. The presence of multiple risk factors over a prolonged period such as lack of food, displacement contributing to increased incidence of malaria and AWD, widespread SGBV and a high number of trauma wounds including gunshots, makes the population in Magwi County among the most at risk and severely affected within Eastern Equatoria State. This project will be implemented in other counties prioritized within Eastern Equatoria state, depending on needs assessments of new health emergencies. Where primary health care is supported, reducing the maternal mortality rate is a programme priority. Through this project, HCAWFO will respond to disease outbreaks within the one priority state. When suspected outbreaks are reported, HCAWFO will prioritize responses in locations with multiple underlying vulnerabilities, such as displacement, malnutrition and limited healthcare access.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

HCAWFO will provide public information to the beneficiaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and health facility based awareness and health promotion activities. HCAWFO will consult with local authorities, community leaders, CHD and health staff throughout the project implementation regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Emergency Rapid Team’s assessments include key informant interviews and focus group discussions amongst community groups. In static facilities, HCAWFO will use annual household surveys to assess programme coverage and post exit interviews are used at the facility level for monitoring the quality of health service provision. Every staff member who joins HCAWFO will get orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign upon it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the National and International Staff Guidelines. In addition, HCAWFO will conduct consultative meetings with the community to identify their needs.
