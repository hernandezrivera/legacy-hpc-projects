Project Summary

WVSR with its partners will be operating in Idlib (Harim district), Aleppo (Afrin, Jarablus, Azaz and Jebel Saman districts), Raqqa (Tel Abyad) and Al Hasakeh (Ras al Ain), Deir-ez-Zor- Al Mayadin, supporting 13 PHCs, one mobile clinic and 3 ambulances. This will include support with salaries, operating costs (electricity, fuel for generators, water supply, equipment, medical supplies – pharmaceuticals and consumables) and transport.
It is worth mentioning that WVSR will be implementing planned activities through its implementing partners(IPs). All planned activities are coordinated with relevant stakeholders and coordination platforms. For instance, in NES, IDA is the implementing partner, IDA is registered in AANES and member of NES HWG. Same in NCS, both WVSR and the implementing partner SAMS have access to NCS, planned activities are coordinated with relevant stakeholders there. WVSR has contracted to a consultant agency to conduct MSNA in NWS, NCS, and NES, final assessment reports will be shared with relevant stakeholders sooner once finalized.
WVSR’s health program will align programs to implement the EHSP with partners for primary health services, ensuring local communities receive the best possible care. This is contingent on adequate funding, staff capacity, supply chain and pipeline continuity, and security that does not result in damage or closure of services. WVSR and partners will be supported to adapt to changing circumstances to continue to offer services. WVSR will work with hold regular meetings with donors, local governorate health authorities, partners (on a monthly basis or as needed) and other actors to meet these basic needs in conjunction with the health cluster, of which they will actively participate.
Essential lifesaving primary health care according to the cluster guidelines, this will include child health, reproductive health SGBV prevention and response. Treatment of communicable and non-communicable disease including basic mental health services. All health programming will take into consideration COVID-19 prevention and IPC measures in all health facilities. WV with partners will support 13 PHC, and 1 MMU and 3 ambulances with free medical treatment. Where the health facility is unable to meet the needs of the patient, referrals will be provided with follow up, and in Tel Abyad & Ras al Ain will support an ambulatory service for patient transfer and referrals during medical emergencies. All health programs will focus on contributing to responding to the COVID-19 pandemic with provision of PPE, infection prevention and control (IPC) protocols in health facilities.
WVI will contribute to collective efforts of other actors responding to cholera outbreak in Syria as per Syria AWD/Cholera Response Plan and under the lead of WHO and cholera taskforce in NWS.
Capacity building of health staff and community health workers linked to health facilities' on topics such as prevention and management of communicable disease (CD), non-communicable (NCD) diseases, IPC, reproductive health (RH) including family planning, SGBV, and STI management, based on capacity needs.
Community health outreach with trained CHW, social workers and SGBV specialists to support home- based care, outbreak prevention, immunization awareness, PSS, SRHR and SGBV awareness and response.
Support to EWARNS-Covid-19, covering salaries, transport and operations in the 4 labs (Jarablus, Idleb, Afrin, Tal Abiad) that can manage PCR tests within NWS and NCS for all COVID-19 related surveillance and conducting contact tracing. This project will have the support of WHO for technical guidance and, a reference lab in Turkey to ensure accuracy of testing protocols.
Monthly reporting from health facilities in conjunction with the technical, MEAL and accountability teams, assessing outputs, reach and acceptability, with third party monitoring on a 6-monthly basis. It includes feedback from questionnaires and FII, patients, community leaders.

3 - Needs

More than 15.3 million people are in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023, a 3.2 million increase from 2022. The increase in 2023 PIN is attributed to the recurring disease outbreaks, the change in the methodology to be more proactive rather reactive and focus more on services availability, deteriorating socio-economic status of the communities, which resulted in increased displacement and worsened determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes.Health care workers need to be trained and equipped to implement integrated and essential health service packages and provide gender-sensitive and comprehensive services. As a result, based on the health sector severity analysis, the number of sub-districts with severe, extreme and catastrophic health needs (severity of 3-5) increased from 181 in 2022 to 223 sub-districts in 2023. Chronic shortages of health care workers in North Syria have disrupted the functioning of and access to essential services, particularly reproductive and maternal health. Shortfalls of safe essential medicines and limited services availability and access remain challenging. According to MSNA conducted by WVSR in North Syria, supporting provision of primary health care services, pharmaceuticals, strengthening surveillance system and health information system, and incentives for health providers are the key priorities.

4 - Implementation of activities

WVSR and partners will work with local communities to ensure appropriate health services are available that meet community standards. This includes operating primary health care centers with free services to all people in the local catchment area. The centers will negotiate with other providers to offer referrals for services they cannot offer. All staff will be hired locally so that the training and support will benefit the whole community. They will also be trained in safeguarding, PSEA, and accountability. Community outreach by locally trained CHW, social workers, MHPSS specialists, and nutrition officers to households and through community meetings to provide information, training, advice and referrals. This will be done culturally sensitively using male and female facilitators. WV will support the partners in building their capacity in project management, financial management, and reporting and grant management etc.. the EWARN system will be run and managed by ACU our implementing partner. They will provide reporting back to WVSR, WHO, and the donor of all activities. Monthly reporting from health facilities on patient services, staffing, medication consumption etc. in conjunction with the technical, MEAL and accountability teams, assessing outputs, reach, and acceptability, with third-party monitoring on a 6-monthly basis. WVSR field team will be responsible for conducting regular field monitoring site visits and providing supportive supervision.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

WVSR will undertake contextual and security risk assessments to decide on location of service provision, including safe access. WV will engage with local authorities to ensure safety of community, medical staff, services and commodities. Through community engagement WV will manage expectations and the effect if health services may need to be curtailed. They will build relationships with neighboring services and set up referral mechanisms to enable communities to access services if needed. Mitigation of Protection risks for children, SGBV, adolescent pregnancies, barriers for minorities and those with disabilities to access services. WV will develop clear communication to manage community expectations including updated service mapping, operating hours, roles of staff. For safety of health workers, WV security focal points will engage with WHO the cluster and community to ensure that health staff are informed of changes in context. WV will report any attacks on health workers to ensure that all cases are tracked. The program will also focus on prevention and response to GBV to mitigate stigmatization, family resistance to girls’ mobility, and lack of trust in services through community education, and referral f survivors for any services they may wish to access. Staff will be trained in clinical management of rape, and how to counsel survivors.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA. And it is committed to ensure that all people –women, girls, boys and men –are treated with dignity and respect, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. All staff will be trained in their responsibilities related to PSEA. WVSR has mechanisms in place to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers. including policies that all staff sign, character checks of all new staff, and policy review with partners. WVSR will train first line workers, assign field focal points with partners. WV field and national focal points will raise the awareness in the community of their rights, and how they should expect to be treated with special focus on child friendly material. WV will provide regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with clusters and the PSEA network led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists by WV field staff and is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. WVSR has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents. WVSR also has an internal online platform called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely anonymous reporting with impartial response.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There is a risk of health worker shortage as staff may leave Syria due to deteriorating of social and economic conditions, which will impact the ability to maintain health services. This is a medium risk and WVSR will provide approved salary and benefits for staff, and PSS services as they need. Increased cases of Covid-19 or other outbreaks may result with health staff absence to treat patients. The project will monitor staffing and plan to ensure adequately trained staff are available or provide referrals and where necessary assistance for patients to attend another facility. All staff will be encouraged and supported to get vaccinated and provided appropriate PPE with refresher training on prevention and treatment. There is a moderate risk of attacks on health workers, and the project security staff will monitor the context and provide regular updates and training to ensure that health staff are aware of security risks and how to mitigate them. Health facilities will have evacuation or hibernation facilities in place. Stockouts of medical supplies are a moderate risk and the project has approved suppliers of quality medications and will keep at least 1 month buffer stock to reduce the risk of stockouts. The project will liaise with donors and WHO about contingency stocks. Overwhelmed health system with cholera outbreak is a medium risk and WVSR will keep close coordination with WHO and other actors to prioritize RCCE, WASH, and supporting cholera treatment centers & units.
