Project Summary

This project will seek to improve access to lifesaving WASH services to conflict affected children and adults in Syria. Aligned with Objectives 1, 2 and 4 of the Humanitarian Response Plan, this project will address WASH needs in a comprehensive and integrated manner by providing a range of services along-side Child Protection, Education and/or Nutrition activities. The purpose is to provide assistance to vulnerable individuals that are impacted by more than a decade of conflict, COVID-19, and climate change, with the goal of reducing the risk of communicable and water-borne diseases, and improving learning environments for children.
Implementation will take place in North East Syria and North West Syria, and will in large part be a continuation of existing programming. Community acceptance will be prioritised, and a conflict sensitive approach will be taken to reduce the risk of unintended consequences. Activities will be informed through action plans developed together with the community, and may include rehabilitation of communal infrastructure, including at institutional levels, distribution of hygiene items, hygiene promotion, and basic service provision of WASH services such as water trucking, solid waste management, desludging and operations and maintenance of WASH facilities in camps / settlements.
The sustainability of the project will be ensured through systems strengthening of the technical departments, or where conditions allow, WASH committees. Children youth, women and people living with disabilities will play important roles in the need identification and design stages.

4 - Needs

Water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) needs in across Syria have deteriorated severely, with 2 million fewer people accessing water through a network compared to one year earlier (HNO 2022, draft). Access to clean water is an interconnected and complex challenge due to low efficiency of conflict affected infrastructure, reduced rainfall, lack of transboundary water resource management policies, and lack of stable electricity supply. The dire economic situation is having negative impacts on internally displaced and host communities, and 13.2 million persons, including 3.6 million persons living with disabilities, are in need of WASH assistance (HNO 2022, draft). Paying for services such as solid waste management, and buying soap and menstrual hygiene items to enable safe hygiene behavior is extremely challenging as a result of the Syrian Pound devaluation, which has lost more than 700% of its value since November 2020 (LSE: Mapping the depreciation of the Syrian Lira, 2021), and is further demonstrated by the 43% of people in need that are considered to fall under severity score of 4 – meaning they have to modify their behavior, including hygiene practices due to a lack of water and cannot meet sanitation and or hygiene needs (HNO 2022 draft; WASH Severity Needs). Ultimately this limits the likelihood of following guidance related to COVID-19 infection prevention and control.

5 - Implementation of activities

Integrated and participatory activities will include rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure in institutions and at communal levels in urban and rural areas. The response plan will include installation and or rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure – this include ensuring access to safe and accessible basic WASH services in temporary learning spaces and child friendly spaces in NES and NWS. In NES, the camp level response will be balanced with rehabilitation of key water and sewerage infrastructure in communities, and will seek to take an area-based approach by complementing, and therefore improving the impact, of Nutrition, Child Protection, Education and Early Recovery activities. Through needs assessments and engagement with the relevant authorities and coordination mechanisms present in the area of operation, activity outcomes will be relevant and timely. The implementation modality will be largely through contractors, and where enabling conditions allow, through cash for work (note that this is captured under ERL submission). Hygiene promotion and distribution of hygiene consumables such as soap will seek to reduce the risk of spreading communicable diseases and will be implemented by relying on methodologies such as Wash’Em. Participatory approach such as child-to-child sessions will be relied on to improve the effectiveness.Emergency water trucking will also be provided to vulnerable households, when needed, in coordination with the WASH WG.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff. Risk 2: Gender insensitive contents varieties; of kits with inconsistent items. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: Harmonize and standardize gender sensitive hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use; hygiene promotion in consultation with women and girls. Risk 3: Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7; Access to toilets not available for all. Likelihood: high, impact: high. Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Risk 4: Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organization, its partners or contractors. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ laborer's; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for SC to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: SC has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to SC and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Due to the dynamic context in NES there is a high likelihood of losing access to certain locations where assistance is most needed. The impact will be high, and mitigation measures include recruiting as much as possible from the same communities and camps that activities are implemented, and building capacity of the staff to ensure business continuity. Resulting from the volatility of the local currency, and border crossing regulations, there is a medium likelihood that supply chains relied on for materials and commodities are disrupted, increasing prices to reduce organisational capacity inter terms of response capacity. Market assessments will be used to identify sudden increases in the construction materials and equipment that is needed to implement WASH activities.Displacement trends are difficult to track, the likelihood that families will continue to be displaced in out of camp locations is high. The impact is that vulnerable children and families are not able to sustain themselves in communities and revert to IDP camps – where service provision is already limited. To this end, the response will focus on an area-based approach to balance in and out of camp response. While the situation in terms of extremist attacks or incursion have been calm, compared to 2019, there could be increase in activity that result in sudden displacements. Impact: existing resources are not easily mobilised for emergency needs. Mitigation: continued analysis and scenario planning.
