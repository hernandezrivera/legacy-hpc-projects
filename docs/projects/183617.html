Project Summary

In the West Bank (WB), including East Jerusalem, Palestinians continue to face a coercive environment due to Israeli policies and practices. Restrictions and discrimination applied limit the population’s access to basic services and to meet their basic needs, and was exacerbated by the covid-19 pandemic. OCHA estimates that in the WB, 36% of the population is in need of multisectoral services, including 21% in humanitarian needs, corresponding at 0.75M people. The deteriorated situation has also significant effects on the psychosocial well-being of the people. It is estimated that 145,000 children are in need of protection and MHPSS services in WB (MSNA, 2021). During the Tdh’s RNA done in June 2021 following the May’s escalation, the main health concern expressed by respondents was mental health, presented as stress, a psychosocial or well-being issue of importance not just in terms of current presenting symptoms and conditions, but in terms of future psychological and physical health. GBV is also an important issue, impacting more women and girls than boys and men, and having implication on their mental health. According to the MSNA 2021, in WB, 366,000 people are in need of GBV services. Persons with disabilities suffer also from mild to moderate and severe mental health problems, poor integration in the society, stigmas, and lack of accessible infrastructures/services. The Protection Cluster has also identiﬁed providing MHPSS services, case management as key interventions needed in the WB. This action is a response to such needs and it is consistent with HRP's second Objective of Protection Cluster which is Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conﬂict-related violence. The proposed activities include awareness on MHPSS aspect and provision of MHPSS services, including case management, MHPSS services. Awareness-raising activities focusing on reducing the prevalence of GBV and MHPSS services, including case management for GBV survivors are also included. Overall, 9,148 persons will benefit from the project (2,515 girls, 2,180 boys, including 94 children with disabilities, 3,076 women, and 1,377 men). Of the targeted children 560 will benefit from PFA and 280 from MHPSS services. A total of 60 identified children will benefit from case management. Community and family members and caregivers will be also included, through awareness activities, PFA and joint child-parent sessions. The GBV issue is also tackled under this Action, with 4,000 community members receiving awareness-raising sessions for persons at risk of GBV, and MHPSS support to 180 GBV survivors through support group, cooking classes and sport activities. Case management and dignity kits will also be provided to 100 GBV survivors. Capacity building through training on related aspects of this Action will also be provided to partners' staff, volunteers and community leaders. The provided protection services will be timely and of good quality protection responses that are tailored according to children's needs and the best interest of boys and girls, equally, and to GBV survivors. The total allocated budget for this project is USD 400,000. FGD were conducted with the targeted beneﬁciaries, from different age groups and gender, including children and seniors. They indicated the high needs in protection services, and they were involved in the design of the project, and proposed activities. During the implementation, beneﬁciaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. To closely monitor and follow up on the results and achievements of the project, a MEAL plan will be developed at the start of the Action with the participation of the Partner, including indicators, assessments, and means of veriﬁcation tools deﬁned in the logical framework. The Project team will coordinate with the relevant authorities, child protection actors, the Protection Cluster, GBV-sub-cluster, and CP-AoR.

1 - Needs

In the West Bank (WB), including East Jerusalem (EJ), Palestinians continue to face a coercive environment due to Israeli policies and practices. Restrictions and discriminations applied limit the population’s access to basic services and to meet their basic needs. The Covid-19 pandemic, started in March 2020, and the related measures, have seriously affected the Palestinians, especially the refugees living in overcrowded conditions. The resulted decrease of livelihood opportunities have added more challenges for the population in WB. OCHA estimates that in the WB, 36% of the population is in need of multisectoral services, including 21% in humanitarian needs, corresponding at 0.75M people. Since April 2021, tensions have raised between Palestinians and Israeli forces leading to the May hostilities, triggered by access restriction to public spaces in the Old City, and the threatening evacuation of 4 families from the neighbourhood of Sheikh Jarrah which sparked 10 days of protests resulting in 27 casualties, 7,516 injured people, and 798 detentions, according to OCHA (West Bank: Escalation of Violence 13 April - 21 May 2021). Clashes continue in the WB in a regular basis, and other tensions remain in the territory. The number of Israeli Security Forces operations and of incidents with settlers since May has reached an unprecedented level in 2021 resulting, based on OCHA’s data, in 72 fatalities including 13 boys and 5 women, 13,391 injured people, including 697 children, until the end of Sept. 12% of households in the WB have adopted coping strategies in the last month to avoid threats or violent/destructive acts, such as withholding children or adult family member from travel (MSNA, 2021). The economic impact is also important as out of the 674 structures demolished in 2021 (until 30/09), 233 were for agricultural use and 107 for other livelihood activities. The area C is especially affected with 79% of the demolished structures. Displacements, linked to those destructions, add also further difficulties for the displaced people. A total of 959 people were displaced consecutively to the destructions, from January to Sept. 2021 according to OCHA’s data, impacting 7,555 people. In its Economic Developments in the Palestinian Territories report (Nov. 2020), the World Bank planned an increase of the poverty rate from 14% to 30%. In the WB, due to covid-19 pandemic, 68% of the households report that their monthly income has decreased (MSNA, 2021). This reinforces the food security and livelihood issue with 40% of the households who are food insecure or vulnerable to food insecurity (HNO 2021). Education is also challenging in WB, access to education facilities, or need to contribute to the household’s livelihood or frequent children arrests contribute to a children drop out from school. In East Jerusalem, 33% of students do not complete 12 years of schooling (HNO 2021), and 8% of the households in the WB report having at least one person under the age of 18 working (MSNA, 2021).In addition to the impact on life, health and economic, the last escalation, and its consequences as well as the implications of living under occupation, had also significant effects on the psychosocial well-being of the people. In the WB, and 20% of households report that at least one member was showing signs of psychosocial distress in the 30 days before data collection (MSNA, 2021). According to the RNA conducted by Tdh in WB and EJ in May 2021 amidst the crisis, EJ is among the areas with the highest MHPSS needs as reported by the key informants' interviews, and that women and girls were mostly affected. It is estimated that 145,000 children are in need of protection and MHPSS services in WB (MSNA, 2021). During the Tdh’s RNA, the main health concern expressed by respondents was mental health, presented as stress, a psychosocial or well-being issue of importance not just in terms of current presenting symptoms and conditions, but in terms of future psychological and physical health. Children and youth are especially at risk, facing arbitrary arrest and risk of violence. According to OCHA, over 335 children were detained/arrested in East Jerusalem, including 170 between 7 May until 31 July 2021. Women are as well severely impacted by this situation, dealing with the pressure of the households, and limited access to services. Furthermore, GBV impact more women and girls than boys and men, having implication on their mental health. According to the MSNA 2021, in WB, 366,000 people are in need of GBV services. Based on UNFPA’s assessment of MHPSS interventions in Aug. 2021, 22% of married Palestinian women have been subjected to violence by their husbands in the WB. While there is a higher prevalence of mental health disorders for girls and women in the oPt than for boys and men, the numbers from the governmental sector for 2019 and 2020 show that almost 60% of mental health service users are male (MoH, 2020). The stigma around mental health is still higher for women and threatens their status in society. Only 1% of GBV survivors requested for assistance in 2019 (UNFPA, 2021). Additionally, women often require the permission of family members to access mental health services, which is not easily granted due to stigmatization and fear for the reputation of women. Thus awareness on MHPSS to decrease stigmas, safe spaces and confidentiality are essentials. Persons with disabilities suffer also from mild to moderate and severe mental health problems, poor integration in the society, stigmas, and lack of accessible infrastructures/services. All these factors illustrate the importance of providing children and youth with integrated protection services, including structured MHPSS and child protection (CP) case management. During the conducted focus group discussions, the high needs for protection services were also indicated. During the implementation, beneﬁciaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Awareness on MHPSS available services that aims to decrease stigmas, and to provide information on how to contact and benefit from MHPSS services. In total, 4,000 persons will be targeted. Different approaches and methodologies will be used to reach the different groups across the society, especially targeting youth, women, vulnerable groups, like “Engage” methodology. This methodology, resulting from MHPSS structured support that will be given to children, will be used to raise awareness and advocate on youth issues and rights and it will be facilitated by youths and adolescents within their communities through youth-led initiatives. In total, 4,000 persons will be targeted.Activity 2: Provision of psychological first aid (PFA) sessions to 560 children and adolescents (280 girls and 280 boys) and 280 caregivers (200 women and 80 men). Sessions will be age, gender and diversity friendly. During the activities, children and adolescents will have the opportunity to connect, network, experiment, acquire skills, develop a sense of worth and hope, critical to prevent negative coping mechanisms, such as disruptive behaviors, and to avoid the development of signs of psychosocial distress, including the resorting to violence and isolation. Caregivers will also be able to connect and exchange experience and learn how to support their children to address their fears and traumas. Activity 3: Provision of non-structured and structured MHPSS to 280 children (140 girls, 140 boys), a specific attention will be on the access to MHPSS services by children with disabilities. The MHPSS support will follow the IASC guidelines, and use different methodologies developed by Tdh or other specialized organizations, depending on the needs and age of the targeted groups, like: Move on and Engage, which addresses 5 aspects of mental wellbeing and youth and adolescents' resilience: i)safety and security, ii) relationship and connection, iii) roles, responsibilities and identities, iv) justice and rights, v) hope and meaning. Participants will develop psychosocial skills to positively cope with exposure to risk to their protection and wellbeing (MOVE ON) and then they will ‘ENGAGE’ in adolescents/youth-led initiatives to practice their skills and lead adolescents/youth-led protection mechanisms; Or for children from 4 to 14, the Movement, Games, Sport, and Creativity (MGSC) methodology which uses sport, games and creativity as a psychosocial tool to ultimately develop the personal and social skills of vulnerable children, and therefore improve their resilience and psychosocial wellbeing. Activities are age, gender and diversity friendly, and segregated across gender and age breakdowns, when relevant. Children and young people will be engaged in a protective environment that promotes their dignity, health and self-respect. And support will be given as per the best interest of the child principle, respecting the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS). Children will be referred to other protection service providers if needed.Activity 4: Conduction of joint child-parent activities for 100 families (85 women, 65 men). These activities will be provided to enhance the protective environment of the children, improving the communication inside the family and a better understanding of each other. These families will also benefit from the PFA sessions. Activity 5: Provision of comprehensive case management for 60 identified children in need. These children are identified through activities conducted by Tdh and its partner. Children will be referred if needed to other MHPSS specialized service providers.Activity 6: A total of 19 Partners' project staff will be trained on these MHPSS methodologies and on the safe identification of signs of mild psychosocial distress (for example behavioral change) and in the identification of patterns of exposure to protection risks affecting children (girls and boys). Activity 7: Help the helpers sessions will be organized on a quarterly basis with to 22 partner’s staff, as these helpers need to evacuate the pressure and stress they face every day dealing with persons in psychological distress.Activity 8: Awareness-raising for 4,000 persons at risk of GBV and more broadly awareness on women rights and PSEA, aiming at tackle the GBV issue, and inform on available services. Different methodologies will be used to raise awareness taken into account the different targeting groups, like campaigns combatting gender-based violence facilitated by youths and adolescents within their communities and through social media platforms. Activity 9: Provision of structured support group sessions for 180 GBV survivors (especially women and girls at risk of violence) in WB. Sessions aim at increasing their understanding and knowledge of GBV issues, including how to cope with GBV risks, access to available services and protect themselves. Additionally complimentary activities like sport or cooking will be also organized to reduce stigma.Activity 10: Provision of case management for 100 identified GBV survivors during the structured support group. Referral pathways will be also done in case of a specialized support is required, and dignity kit will be distributed.Activity 11: Provision of training to 120 partners' staff, CBOs and community leaders in WB on procedural guidelines of access to justice standard for GBV cases in formal and community justice spheresActivity 12: Training will be provided to 30 persons from partners’ staff and volunteers on PSEA and child safeguarding and protection mainstreaming. The training will be based on national and Tdh PSEA material. The training will be conducted by either Tdh expert by an external expert.All activities will be done respecting the preventive Covid-19 measures to avoid any contamination: small groups, hand washing, hydro-alcoholic gel, face masks... A reminder of the protective measures will be done at the beginning of each activities.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

During the project implementation, Tdh and partners will guarantee with the targeted CBOs that project beneficiaries’ feedback, suggestions and recommendations are encouraged and collected, to ensure activities remain relevant and beneficial. At the beginning of project implementation, complaint and feedback mechanism will be explain to beneficiaries. Different ways will be available such as complaint/feedback boxes in different locations where the activities take place, a phone number, a Whatsapp number and an email address will be widely disseminated to the targeted populations. This will ensure that persons with limited mobility due to physical disability, COVID 19 movement restrictions, or any other reason, have equal access to feedback mechanisms. Based on the beneficiaries’ feedback in its design, to make it more child friendly, gender sensitive and inclusive, adaptations will be made. Complaints will be tracked and logged by dedicated and trained focal point in a confidential manner. All complaints will be handled efficiently and confidentially by Tdh accountability team, and lessons learned are generated regularly based on complaints received. Complaints and feedback channels are assessed on a periodic basic to remain relevant and accessible to all the affected population. Beneficiaries will also be engaged, including children, in the action's regular monitoring through feedback sessions and project activities, as well as regular satisfaction surveys. Tdh has a very comprehensive Child Safeguarding policy (CSP), PSEA policy, Code of Conduct and confidential mechanisms in place for reporting and response. Every Tdh staff, partner and volunteers will be trained on the CSP, PSEA and the Code of Conduct and sign the policies. Satisfaction surveys are a part of the service delivery mechanism of the proposed project and periodical consultative meetings with beneficiaries will be organized to create a safe environment for them to express their feedback.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project design has considered the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries as a first priority, given the very specific group it covers children and adults particularly and directly affected by conflict related violence and/or affected by GBV. Indeed, the project has been conceptualized according to the Do No Harm framework tailoring services according to identified needs of beneficiaries, taking into account the context, and will have in place all the mitigation measures to ensure beneficiaries' safety. Specific methodologies within the service provision delivery have been integrated to avoid re-victimization of children and survivors, including capacity building, support activities and awareness to families, to community members and to child protection workers. All staff involved in the proposed project, including partner organization’s staff, will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Package (CSP, PSEA, DSMW and GCC) to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm is resulting from Tdh’s activities and staff. Additionally, preventive and responsive protection mechanisms will be also applied following a multidimensional holistic approach. It will also include awareness-raising and community-based integration activities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of the equity, under this project, boys, girls, women and men will equally participate and benefit from the proposed activities according to their assessed needs. Tdh will also provide equal access to services to all identified children and survivors, including persons with disabilities but also taking into account the gender differences. The project will also target equally all people of concern without discrimination. The project will also use an AGD lens, to collect and analyze disaggregated data, and ensure that Tdh G&D Marker is implemented and is taken into account in the MEAL plans in compliance with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines. Gender balanced participation will be specifically monitored through M&E diversity, gender and age disaggregated tools and indicators in order to quickly identify possible participation and protection concerns related to activities and take consequent measures to promote safe and dignified access. Dedicated spaces and moments will allow free expression, participation and the possibility to provide feedbacks. The partner align with Tdh Gender and Disability policy that informs the program in all its stages together with the IASC guidelines on inclusion of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian Action. In accordance with Tdh Gender and disability policy, the proposed action sits at level three of the G&D scale, namely Gender and Diversity responsive. Specifically, the implemented approaches aims at proactively overcome existing biases, discriminations, barriers, taking into account different needs, opportunities, interests for the targeted population in the program design. The partners will ensure that systematic inclusive needs assessment and needs observation is done, taking into consideration the voice and needs of people with disabilities and people with specific needs. The capacity building package on child protection, safeguarding PSEA mainstreams inclusion to ensure appropriate capacities are developed within the targeted communities and among community-based workers. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are also designed to be inclusive across gender, age and diversity. Priority will be given to children, caregivers, survivors marginalized and discriminated, disabled in accessing services and participate meaningfully to the proposed activities. Activity locations will be assessed against inclusivity and accessibility standards and remedial actions will be taken to enhance compliance with Tdh policies and IASC guidelines.
