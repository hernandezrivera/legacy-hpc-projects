Project Summary

The program seeks to respond to the prolonged humanitarian crisis affecting the Palestinian population under occupation, by contributing to the protection of their rights and dignity in accordance with IHL and IHRL, and with a cross-cutting focus on gender and community participation. The program is to be implemented by the Action Group (Protection Cluster Partners) for Organised Local Response, a group of civil society organisations that seeks to promote the localisation of humanitarian response, and to respond to the multiple needs of Palestinian communities in an organised manner, by building on the respective sectoral and geographical strengths of each of its members. The program as a whole is multi-sectoral, and includes actions linked to protection, livelihoods, WASH, education, mental health and advocacy.
Despite the fact that the program address many sectors, it has been divided in sectoral projects to ease the management of such a comprehensive program. In this sense, the present project include only the protection-cluster-related activities even though the whole program is in line with the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster as it i) seeks to increase the respect for the IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as ii) to put in place protection mechanisms -as the CPC or the provision of protection services- to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence such as settler violence, property damage and confiscation, blockade and restrictions and violations against children.
The population targeted under the program is highly vulnerable to IHL/IHRL violations due to the presence of their communities primarily in Area C, and to their proximity to settlements, military bases and Israeli checkpoints. The program pays special attention to the different vulnerabilities, needs and capacities of women, men, girls and boys. Of those groups, specific targets are children affected by the conflict, women affected by GBV and vulnerable victims of conflict-related violence such us land workers and youth,

Needs

In addition to the HNO needs assessment, the current program is based on two assessments carried out by the Protection Cluster Partners in 11 communities in the governorates of Salfit and Qalqiliya in 2017 and 2018. These human rights-based, integrated and participatory assessments found that the protection of the rights of the target population in accordance with IHRL and IHL is compromised by several factors: The main protection problems identified are: - Confiscation and isolation of land and harassment of settlers and IDF: The evaluation pointed that agricultural land and resources are being abandoned progressively, and with this the appropriation of the land by the settlers and Israeli authorities and the risk of confiscation is increasing. In addition, families are losing the ability to continue with their traditional agricultural and livestock activities. The western communities are more affected by the illegal wall of segregation and the Israeli zone of separation, leaving isolated lands to the other side, being extremely difficult their access- Lack of accountability, isolation from HR offenders and lack of legal support: The incidents related to the settlers cause the population to have to take long and costly legal actions to protect their property and themselves. Lacking resources and finding little support, despite there being a few actors that provide effective support, the main response strategy of families is to monitor these violations, often being deficient and unable to provide necessary evidence for procedures legal and defense- Lack of protection and support services for GBV survivors: There are some service providers but physical, economic and cultural problems to access them, such as lack of time for women -for multiple tasks- or lack of transportation. While other protection-related problems that falls into other sectors are: - Cultural norms and frustration and stress exacerbated by occupation (Gender): The GBV has its causes in the unjust relations of power in the Palestinian society itself. The occupation contributes to aggravate the GBV by increasing the frustration of many male heads of family who end up turning their frustration into violence against the family. - Restriction of movements imposed by Israel: Mobility is limited by the multiple controls, barriers, checkpoints, settlements, etc., which prevents the population from accessing basic services such as education or health or work on their land.- Limited access to mental health services and psycho-social support (MHPSS): The main barriers to accessing MHPSS are stigma, lack of available services, economic access, lack of qualified personnel and the impossibility of leading a normal life without a psychological balance.- Social stigma regarding mental illness: The main barrier to accessing support services is social stigma. Likewise, there are many members of the community who do not recognize that there is violence against women, boys and girls.

Activities

One of the relevant part of the program is related to the provision of services for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups, in a sustainable way (responsive and remedial) involving the community and legal support:- Awareness sessions with teachers and parents- Creation of self-help groups of women victims of GBV - Group therapy for men ex detainees and/or with symptoms of psychosocial stress - Group therapy for women victims of GBV and/or with symptoms of psychosocial stress - Legal assistance (GBV…) - Workshops with teachers to develop their knowledge and skills in dealing with students who show symptoms of psychosocial stress The communities are also engaged with this pillar of the project in the design of the Community Protection Response Plans and relate tool with the following activities:- Participatory workshops for the incorporation of the perspective of women and for the inclusion of child protection approach in the Community Protection and Response Plans- Central workshops to collectively identify the risks and priorities that should be included in the Community Protection and Response Plans, from the perspective of women and from the child protection perspective- Design / update of the report format to document and report on violations of IHL / IDHR with gender approach and with child protection approachPSCCW is responsible for the implementation of all the activities mentioned and they could be asked for they experience, methodologies, capacities and any other detail regarding any activity. They are engaged with the Protection cluster as well as with the GBV working group and it is expected to increase their participation in the cluster through this program. They have hired recently one person in Salfit area for the implementation and monitoring of the programThe reinforcement of the Community Protection Groups (CPC) are the core protective mechanism for the communities. There is long experience in the creation, expansion and strengthening of these groups which are formed by members of the community such as leaders, members of the council, teachers, etc. and always with an equal participation of women. Their role in the protection program is related to two advocacy campaigns in West Bank:- Advocacy National Campaign - Campaign for the right of the Palestinian People / Documentation and dissemination of cases of violations of IHL / IHRL by the CPC- Advocacy National Campaign - Campaign on the Right of Women to Housing, Land and Property (VTP)Another leg of the advocacy strategy embrace the monitoring of IHL and IHRL violations and the legal aid for the victims. The activities are the following:- Accompaniment of victims to file complaints with the Israeli police and coordinate access to police stations and make complaints to the IDF- Register of testimonies of victims affected by crimes against property, violent crimes, land confiscation or environmental damage committed by Israeli settlers / military- Registration of the cases through Geographic Information Systems (GIS) by type of incident and geographic location, and with data disaggregated by gender- Use of data from registered cases to inform advocacy activities- Collection of information in the communities and adjacent areas with reference to the state of the land, planned plans and orders issued, and identification of threats and action plans- Creation of action plan of steps to prevent threats of home demolitions or forcible transfer (objection to plans, promotion of legal cases, submission of expert opinions in legal cases, etc.)- Mapping of vulnerable lands due to confiscation risk in the face of land grabbing by the Israeli authorities- Advocacy campaign of social incidence on the settlements directed to holders of responsibilities- Monitoring and updating of data on Israeli settlements and outposts as a violation of IHL / IHRL- Presentations, workshops and tours in the WB
