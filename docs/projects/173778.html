Project Summary

The provision of educational support and enrolment of vulnerable boys, girls into formal and informal schools cannot be over emphasized in Borno State North East, Nigeria. In the current era we live, terrorism has become a threat that poses a challenge on education, physical and mental well-being of children. Since 2009, the Boko Haram group has waged a brutal campaign of violence in the north east of Nigeria targeting schools by killing, maimed, abduction, kidnapping and forceful early marriage of children. This has resulted in large scale loss of life, destruction of schools, property, and poor mental health has severely disrupted economic activities and day-to-day life this resulted in closing down of some schools and making the children to be vulnerable and could not access education.
An estimated 1.01 million people are displaced in Borno around 40 percent (0.8 million) of that population is comprised of school aged children between three and seventeen years displaced. While the number of displaced persons and returnees increased compared to the previous year, the total number of children and education personnel in need decreased from 2.9 million for 2018 to 2.2 million for 2019 based on education sector estimates (EiEWG HNO, 2019). Recent data on the key education indices in the NE still indicate a dire situation in education with Borno recording the brunt effect of the crises Children and teachers in all three affected states are in dire need of psycho-social support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. There is an urgent need to engage the local
communities in supporting education as a longer-term strategy for recovery and peace building.

2 - Activities

The Big Smile Foundation will carry out the following activities during the projectimplementation: • Train teachers on psycho-social support and life skills to cope with thenegative effects of the COVID19 • Empower communities through school Board managementcommittees (SBMC) to protect education and assist in the establishment and maintenance ofchild-friendly school environments • Support parents and guardians with cash-basedprogrammes to increase enrollment and retention rates • Provide sanitary kits toadolescent girls to encourage them to stay in school • Provide recreational materials toschool-going children and support peace/sports clubs in schools • Provide early childhooddevelopment materials to preprimary school-aged children • Empower teachers and the School BoardManagement Committee (SBMC) members with knowledge on referral mechanisms for issuesrelated to GBV and child protection
