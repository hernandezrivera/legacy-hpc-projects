Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to the reconstruction and rehabilitation of basic
social infrastructure through:
The goal of the project is to improve water supply and sanitation infrastructure of IDP camps in Azaz Region and azaz center, Northern Syria through providing mobile water supply and sewage system and emergency repairs for sanitation and sewage systems, water supplies and electric generators of camps.
The expected outcome will be improved water sanitation in IDP camps in Azaz region. The importance of water supply and sanitation in emergencies is to prevent diseases and infections due to inadequate sanitation and water supply. The main purpose of improving water supplies and sanitation is to allow and establish conditions for the right to life with dignity.
The rehabilitation of large water networks, such as water stations and boreholes, and the construction of new water systems for areas without access to installed water facilities are among the most important needs in the region. The current need in this regard has been determined by discussing with member of the local council. The rehabilitation of common sewage networks will prevent hygiene problems in the region. The local council is requesting urgent support in this regard. Another important need in the region is the establishment of garbage cans and solid waste management campaigns, including support for municipal garbage collection trucks. In this regard, the necessary steps are planned by using the needs analysis provided by the field team. In addition to the rehabilitation of water wells in the region, hygiene promotion campaigns will be required, including the distribution of hygiene kits. In this regard, it is planned to increase awareness of the use of clean water by organizing joint campaigns with the local council, which is our project stakeholder.

3 - Needs

Azaz drinking water supply is provided by the Midenki dam. The pumping and maintenance of the water lines of this dam needs to be done urgently. In addition, sewage repair and maintenance for the Al Haramein, Ahlusham, Al Noor and Al Mukawama camps in the Azaz region within the borders of Aleppo province are among urgent issues. It seems that people living in these camps will live in this region for a long time, so according to the information we have received from the Syrian task force and the local council November 1-5, these are among the most urgent issues that need addressing.Our target beneficiaries in this project are; people living in camps, hence all age groups. In these camps, where widows and orphans are high, women and children constitute 68% of the total population. The fact that the need for clean water continues to be met in these containers will also prevent the spread of diseases within the camps. Since the sewer and water line maintenance has not yet been carried out, the rainfall that increases with winter & spring months increases the risk of overflow in the camp. With this project, we will also prevent waterborne diseases.Especially for the prevention of cholera disease, the implementation of this project is more important and timely. Necessary preliminary negotiations were held with the Azaz local council & AFAD Azaz coordinators for the implementation of this project. We have a protocol to carry out these studies and activities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

68% of targeted population are women and children which results in majority households who have female or child under 18 years of old head of household because the males in the household died, had to go fight or look for better economic opportunities elsewhere. In the camps especially, there are a number of persons living with disabilities & many cases of social violence & further protection risks due to high number of orphans in the camps & their exploitation by political actors, competition for resources sometimes leads to distributed items getting taken from those in need and for sale in another area. To mitigate these protection risks, IBC analyses the field. All records of support provided within the scope of the protocol we signed with local council, AFAD, Syrian Task Force department will be shared transparently with donor. In addition to information obtained, we will ensure that the support offered by the project reaches the target beneficiaries through periodic analyses and household visits in the field. We will make sure that the support offered in the project reaches all people in need mostly through KII, FGD, snowball effect & raising protection risks among community. Otherwise, we will make the necessary interventions & inform project stakeholders at every step. We'll support female staff & consult female beneficiaries in the field in project cycle & ensure all staff receive training in GBV risks related to WASH & cross sector coordination incl. health referrals.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sixty-eight percent of the total population is women and children under 18 years old, with many children and female heads of household especially in the camps. Child and early forced marriage, as well as abuse from unequal power balance between ''givers'' and ''receivers'' of project support. To combat this unequal balance of power, IBC follows its own PSEA, whistleblower, and complaints and response mechanism SOPs which were created in line with IASC standards. In the project planning stage, IBC consults with community members and stakeholders on best modes of communication in order to effectively articulate our commitment to PSEA and what our strategy is based on for the targeted communities. After consultation, IBC will incorporate the input of beneficiaries and stakeholders when starting the project, and ensure that everyone involved with the project is knowledgeable about IBC code of conduct and other relevant SOPs- with information freely available, as well as how the CRM system will work to be as inclusive as possible. This includes training and awareness raising sessions for both project staff and stakeholders (incl. beneficiaries) on how to engage with CRM in ways relevant and effective for everyone involved with the project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security and access are the two main issues that may be beyond the project's control. These can be affected by violence, political shocks, or epidemics. Öncüpınar gate will be used for transportation to the WASH sites. If emergencies and violence arise/break out, the steps to be followed by all employees will be implemented in accordance with the IBC safety/security procedures booklet. The safety of all employees will be considered a priority issue. Our WASH activities will continue to actively use the cluster coordination mechanism for most complimentary & non-duplicated services.IBC obeys sphere and host state authorities' standards for a response during its implementation of such projects worldwide. The same applies to the proposed project in Syria.IBC has protocols to organize activities with the camp management, AFAD, Syria Task Force, and Local Council, assisting our team during the assessment, implementation, and recording of the activities.IBC has conducted needs analysis at 2 different times so far and conducted focus group interviews with nearly 200 people. In these interviews and in the needs analysis prepared, it has been seen that the people living in the region are seriously concerned about access to clean water. Assessments on the ground in 2022 show that cholera emitted from polluted water, food, and sewage causes severe diarrhea and dehydration. If left untreated, it can result in death. This project prepared by IBC has critical importance.
