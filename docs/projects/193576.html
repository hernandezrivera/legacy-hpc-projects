Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to contribute to the reduction of WASH vulnerability in marginalized communities through improving access to equitable, sustainable, and safely managed WASH services in institutions with a particular focus on vulnerable Schools and Health clinics in the governorates of Hebron, Bethlehem, Jericho, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin, and Tubas.
The proposed intervention responds to the 2023 HRP and to WASH Cluster’s Objective 1 and, in particular, Objective 2. It was also designed based on the WASH Cluster’s 2023 Humanitarian Needs Analysis which highlighted access to WASH services in institutions (schools & health care facilities) as a main priority area of intervention. The intervention is also in line with SDG 6 “Ensure access to water and sanitation for all” & SDG 3 “Universal Health Coverage”.
The intervention will achieve its aim through two components: first, its tailored approach to enhancing WASH infrastructure and resources in vulnerable schools and HCFs and second, through enhancing target group’s responsive hygiene practices and awareness. The intervention is designed to ensure equitable and dignified access for most vulnerable community groups with a particular focus on PWDs, children and females across 47 communities in the West Bank.
MA’AN and WeWorld-GVC are proposing to improve WASH facilities and capacities in 47 vulnerable WASH institutions benefiting in total 22,030 direct beneficiaries (7623 boys, 6367 girls, 3746 men, 3578 women, 248 elder men, 310 elder women - of which 392 are PWDs) in the West bank, mainly in Area C (51%), and Area A/B (49%). Through:
i) Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools in coordination with & according to MoE standards, targeting 30 schools (1 in Jericho Governorate, 4 in Jerusalem, 3 in Bethlehem, 14 in Hebron, 2 in Nablus, 4 in Jenin, 1 in Ramallah, and 1 in Tubas).
ii) Conducting hygiene awareness campaigns in targeted schools in partnership with MoE health units to promote hygiene and sanitation practices with gender & protection mainstreaming.
iii) The provision of hygiene and cleaning kits for the 30 targeted schools.
iv) Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in health clinics/HCFs, targeting 17 vulnerable HCFs (2 in Jericho Governorate, 5 in Jerusalem, 2 in Hebron, 2 in Jenin, 5 in Ramallah, and 1 in Tubas).
v) The provision of hygiene and cleaning kits for the 17 targeted HCFs.
vi) Conducting trainings for clinics’ staff on promotion of hygiene practices to patients and protection guidelines including PSEA.
vii) developing awareness raising products (video, factsheets, reports) regarding access restrictions to WASH in Area C
All proposed activities will ensure protection principles on safety, dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.
This intervention has been proposed based on coordinated efforts to complement current WASH aid actors’ proposed and ongoing interventions to ensure comprehensive contribution to the reduction in overall WASH vulnerability amongst marginalized and prioritized communities. The proposed intervention also complements another initiative submitted by WW-GVC (with MA’AN as an implementing partner).
The project has been coordinated and endorsed by the MoH, MoE and the WASH Cluster. It is in line with the MoH Strategic Plan (2021-25). Proposed activities and target locations were selected upon WASH Cluster’s eligible interventions for HRP 2023 and based on the cluster’s community & institutions vulnerability assessments.
MA’AN and WW-GVC will ensure that proposed interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of WASH vulnerability, increasing the resilience of the communities & institutions, especially in Area C, including PSEA promotion and messaging. Moreover, the project will enhance the protection of the communities & institutions, supporting preparedness and promotion of best hygiene practices that would contribute to combating disease outbreaks.

1 - Needs

The fragmented Palestinian territorial sovereignty has many ramifications including inability to enforce the rule of law, undertake physical and socio-economic development planning, construct residential and public service facilities, or provide adequate level of public services and infrastructure including WASH. The pervasive poverty and unemployment are compounded and caused by occupation regimes restrictions which further hinder the PA’s ability to invest in WASH infrastructures. One of the main factors contributing to WASH accessibility vulnerability at the national level (Areas A,B &C) is the existence of inadequate and deteriorated WASH infrastructures. Improving access to WASH services is an urgent need to ensure protection and dignity of patients, outpatients, health workers and students of the most vulnerable HCFs and schools of the West Bank. Improved WASH services in HCFs are considered as necessary inputs to be prioritized for achieving all global and national health goals, especially those linked to Universal Health Coverage (SDG 3) and Access to Clean Water and Sanitation (SDG 6). Effective sanitation, hygiene, and infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are one of the five objectives of the WHO Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance. In schools, the need is growing for availability of safe and functional WASH facilities with a minimum level of hygiene standards especially during operations within the pandemic. Since the outbreak of COVID-19, the MoE rolled out safe schools COVID protocols and prioritized WASH in schools’ national standards which many schools with high WASH vulnerability are struggling to operationalize in light of limited resources thus further exacerbating their WASH and Health vulnerability. During 2021, the WASH cluster directly and through partners/members, MoE and the MoH staff conducted several WASH vulnerability assessments including the WB&GS Community WASH vulnerability mapping, WASH vulnerability in HCFs where 638 clinics in the WB were mapped, and WASH vulnerabilities in schools where 847 schools in the WB including East Jerusalem where mapped. The assessments revealed as presented in the WASH 2022 HNO that with regards to institutions in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, at least 129 schools and 164 HCF units suffer from insufficient WASH facilities (primarily toilets, drinking and handwashing stations) to meet the standards set up by MoE & MoH. In Oct 2021, the WASH cluster team, MA’AN, and Oxfam held a response coordination meeting with MoE where the WASH vulnerability mapping in schools was presented and main indicators for consideration from the mapping to identify prioritized WASH vulnerable schools were determined. Further, the MoE briefed on proposed WASH national standards in schools (1 latrine for each class, handwashing station for each 2 latrines, 1 drinking tap for each 50 students, at least 1 PWD latrine in each school (AGD segregation if mix), 1 latrine for each 20 staff (AGD segregation if mix). Upon coordination with the WASH cluster and others, MA’AN and WW-GVC have conducted a rapid assessment in Sep-Oct 2022 pursuant to aforementioned mappings. The assessment of HCFs revealed that a significant number of HCFs lack adequate gender segregated and/or staff vs patient latrines resulting in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices. At least 19 HFCs have no adaptable toilets for PWDs, 25 need urgent WASH facilities rehabilitations due to substandard unmaintained structure. Majority of assessed HCFs have regular access to latrine cleaning materials and tools but often face shortages and could benefit from hygiene kits, particularly HCFs with inpatient services who have increased consumption. Majority of HCFs further reported rarely or never receiving capacity building or training on hygiene awareness and practices. Regarding schools, many schools need replacement of drinking fountains from rustable steel structures to standardized water coolers to encourage students’ water consumption during hot days. Their WASH facilities do not meet minimum standards including due to the lack of appropriate flooring causing sinking of installed toilet or lack thereof, the lack of water drainage piping and network causing pooling of water, lack of existing access paths for students, and staff especially PWDs, rusted sewage pipes causing clogging and insufficient capacity of sewage septic tanks. Additionally, there is a lack of privacy due to substandard partitions thus deterring students in particular females from use, disproportionate number of stalls to students & staff, damaged door frames, need for shading to address various weather conditions (hot temperatures in summer and rain in the winter) and any related fixes of window frames, and water gutters. Based on the above MA’AN and WW-GVC have identified 30 schools, and 17 HCFs demonstrating high WASH vulnerability in the following intervention target localities: Jericho Governorate (Ein ad Duyuk al Fauqa, Fasayil, Marj Naja), Jerusalem Governorate (Abu Dis, Al Eizariya, Ar Ram & Dahiyat al Bareed, Arab al Jahilin - al Jabal, As Sawahira ash Sharqiya, Beit Anan, Beit Ijza, Khan al Ahmar - Ab al Helu), Hebron Governorate (Yatta, Adh Dhahiriya, Deir Samit, Al Karmil, At Tuwani, Zif, Beit ‘Awwa, As Samu’, Khirbet Ghuwein al Fauqa, Khirbet Zanuta, Wadi ar Rim, Al Heila, Beit Kahil, Al Buweib, Beit ar Roush ar Rush al Fauqa), Bethlehem Governorate (Za’tara, Jurat ash Sham’a, Al Khadr), Nablus Governorate (Qabalan, Odala), Jenin Governorate (Jenin, Jalbun, Beit Qad, Kafr Dan, Deir Abu Da’if, Umm at Tut), Tubas Governorate (Kardala, Al ‘Aqaba), and Ramallah Governorate (Wadi as Seeq, Deir as Sudan, Al Lubban al Gharbi, Al Midya, Umm Safa, Deir ‘Ammar).

2 - Activities

The proposed activities have been coordinated with the MoH, MoE, Local Authorities, and major stakeholders active in the WASH sector on the target communities to avoid overlapping. At the onset of the project, MA’AN and WW-GVC will lead coordination meetings to map out implementation plan in target locations and explore special regulatory considerations for program implementation in the 47 target localities in the West Bank. The proposed intervention will be implemented in mutual partnership between both organizations based on complementarity of expertise where MA’AN will lead components related to schools and coordination with MoE and WW-GVC will lead components related to HCFs and coordination with the MoH. Furthermore, both organizations have reach across the targeted localities and employ local staff who will be able to monitor political risks daily. Before any activity when relevant, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community will be conducted. Specific SOPs are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks which will allow reducing the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment of Area C, enhancing the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries, to guarantee safety, dignity and avoid harming (do no harm) the affected population. Therefore, the intervention will be committed to ensuring gender sensitiveness and inclusiveness on the facilities design for all, including girls and boys with special needs and/or disabilities. In accordance, the design of the sanitation facilities at schools and HFCs level will ensure privacy for women and children with the provision of dignified space with good physical and sound insulation and will be adapted to special needs for PWDs. Proposed Key activities: Activity 1 – Need assessments: at the beginning of the intervention, together with the stakeholders and Local Authorities, a need assessment will be carried out to verify any change of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. Activity 2 – Improvement of access to WASH services in institutions: 2.1 The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in coordination with and accordance to MoE standards, targeting 30 schools in the West Bank in the Governorates of Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron, Ramallah, Bethlehem, Nablus, Jenin, Tubas. MA’AN will coordinate with the MoE and its relevant health and infrastructure units at the directorates level to conduct an individual technical infrastructure needs assessment of each of the 30 targeted schools WASH facilities. PWDs accessibility and female students’ privacy will be prioritized for schools lacking thereof. As the needs from one school to another differ, some works might entail construction of new bathrooms in order to accommodate standards of 20 students per stall or existing non segregated staff vs students’ latrines. As per MA’AN’s standards operations in rehabilitation of schools for the past years, BoQs of materials and works will be detailed in accordance with MoE’s standardized design for adequate WASH facilities in schools and shared with the MoE for approval. The works will be conducted through contractors and supervised by MA’AN’s civil engineers in cooperation with MoE infrastructure units. 2.2 The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HFCs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system), targeting 17 vulnerable Health care facilities located in the Governorates of Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron, Ramallah, Jenin, Tubas. WW-GVC will coordinate with MoH and its relevant directorates for the construction and rehabilitation of handwashing stations, latrines and sewer systems in the 17 HCFs based on individual technical assessments. All the works, especially regarding the design of the bathrooms/latrines, will take into consideration the gender needs and separation, equipping of toilets to meet the specific needs of women, staff (men and women), patients, and companions as well as the special needs of PWDs and children, in order to decrease the risk of GBV cases and to ensure safety and dignity to all. Moreover, it will include the rehabilitation/construction of new handwashing stations and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall-mounted dispensers, supporting the prevention of the spread of infectious diseases and will be implemented in accordance with the National WASH standards for HCFs. Similarly, works will be conducted through service provision modality. 2.3 Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns in targeted 30 schools in partnership with MoE health units to promote hygiene and sanitation practices with gender and protection mainstreaming. 2.4 The provision of hygiene and cleaning kits for the 17 targeted HCFs and 30 schools to ensure proper segregation of waste in bins, and availability of cleaning materials and tools for both staff, inpatients and outpatients. 2.5 conduct trainings for HCFs staff on promotion of hygiene practices to patients and protection guidelines including PSEA. 3. develop awareness raising products (video, factsheets, reports) regarding access restrictions to WASH for communities in Area C. All proposed activities will ensure protection principles on safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.

3 - Indicators

MA’AN and WW-GVC will perform ongoing monitoring, combining both quantitative and qualitative data, then systematized through a series of outputs. Accordingly, the monitoring implies a participatory approach that includes focus group discussions and individual interviews (with men, women, girls, and boys, with and without disability) to assess whether the identified activities consider the needs and dynamics of the beneficiaries, analyzing the different effect of the initiative on different age and gender groups. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative impact of the initiative will be addressed through focus groups, interviews, and surveys collecting the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, boys, girls, elderlies). Overall monitoring will be done by a MEAL officer, who will also lead reporting cases received in the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in accordance with MA’ANs complaint systems and procedures. The Field engineers will work and cooperate with the MEAL department in ensuring that the indicators are being met and ensuring that the best strategies are being used. MA’AN’s Project Manager will be responsible for overall monitoring, evaluation and reporting and will lead the reporting to MA’AN and GVC Focal point in the WASH Cluster. The indicators and sources of verification will be the base for collecting data and observation on the ground by the project coordinator. In the event targets are not being met, immediate action will be agreed upon and taken to reach the targets. Monitoring will be shared with relevant stakeholders including the project committee which will be established at the onset of the project and will include representatives from MA’AN, WW-GVC, MoE, MoH and WASH cluster, and representatives from communities. All information will be shared with the donor, especially any lessons learned. The tools that will be used are baseline survey (at the start) end line survey (at project’s closing) as well as interviews, field visits, general observation and field visit reports. Photos will also be taken to document the project. Monitoring activities will be done throughout the project cycle to ensure proper implementation and targets are being met to achieve results. Special attention will be given for conducting interviews and data collection with women beneficiaries through female team members. The intervention will be supported by compliant CRM accessible to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries through several channels. The CRM will ensure that all feedback and complaints are recorded, responded to and handled according to their sensitivity and beneficiaries will be sensitized on its function, process and usage, and will be encouraged to rely on it without fear of any adverse actions. CRMs are customized under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a confidential and secure way duly reported to MA’AN’s management. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Based on the classification of feedback or concern, feedback would be used to adapt project implementation taking into consideration needs of target groups (especially women & PWDs). Beneficiaries’ satisfaction will be continuously monitored by MA’AN’s team which regularly visits and interviews beneficiaries. The team will report any issues brought to its attention to project management team. MA’AN’s female MEAL officer will also oversee and ensure transparency in the confidential Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), duly reported to MA’AN’s management when relevant, and then properly addressed. MA’AN establishes a CRM under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a confidential and secure way. As many communication means as relevant are made available to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities. Schools and HCFs staff will be sensitized by the project team on the mechanism, and how to access it, and information is clearly displayed at project sites. Complaints are recorded in an area-level register under restricted access. Under project supervision, project committees will then inform and encourage project beneficiaries to share feedback on the provided assistance, potential encountered issues and/or remaining needs to be addressed. Depending on the nature and severity of the cases received under the CRM, several different action pathways may be activated. All feedback received through CRM or collected through field visits will be taken seriously and dealt with according to MA’AN’s SoPs. Moreover, MA’AN will provide targeted beneficiaries with the PSEA hotline number and explain that it can be used for complaints involving inappropriate behaviors, sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse, where all correspondences will be addressed with the utmost confidentiality & seriousness.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

The project implementation is coordinated directly with the MoE and MoH, and thus creating another channel for provision of feedback on the planned works in the project. MA’AN’s local coordinators will also be present in the field for daily supervision and coordination of implementation in schools and HCFs thus being another accessible channel to relay feedback. Further the contacts info of MA'ANs MEAL persons (CRM focal points) will be provided to schools and HCs at onset of the project during orientation meeting and staff of schools will be informed of the project compliant/feedback channels and CRM that is accessible to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries through several channels. The CRM will ensure that all complaints are recorded, responded to and handled according to their sensitivity and Beneficiaries. The MEAL persons from both organizations will further conduct at least 3 visits to each of the Schools and HCs during implementation and relay any feedback or concerns in order to inform project implementation or to deal with complaints in accordance with their type and classification and standard procedures.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MA'AN and WW-GVC CRM systems have various tools to accommodate different groups and circumstances and any attempts of bypassing submission of concerns, complaints and feedback. Furthermore, due to the nature of the intervention, MoE and MoH staff can relay concerns and feedback directly. For Sensitive matters related to protection and PSEA concerns, orientations on PSEA will be conducted at the onset of the project with details of CRM channels per type of incidents. The entire project cycle including - need assessments, designed strategies, proposed interventions, set indicators, outcome measuring procedures – takes into account the needs of the most affected groups such as women, children, PWDs, and elderly, in order to achieve gender and age-sensitive relief through responsive assistance that meets the material and protection needs of specific vulnerable groups, ensuring adequate & equitable participation & access to humanitarian goods and services, and eventually preventing/mitigating potential negative effects of the intervention on different gender/age groups. At the onset of the project, beneficiaries of all population groups will be informed about the CRM in place and modalities of accessing it. MA’AN will also consult beneficiaries on the best place to install a complaints box which would guarantee safe access to all. A CRM brochure guide will be distributed during meetings, installed in accessible public places in a community. The brochure will explain the different channels: in-person, at any time during the field visits of the staff of the two organizations, through email, or remotely through the dedicated hotline. The latter will ensure an anonymous and discrete means to file a complaint, ensuring that all population groups may access it. A sticker will also be put on the kits distributed with a phone/mobile number or email address, to receive any complaint from the beneficiaries.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as MA’AN and WW-GVC are guided by protection principles, considering safety, dignity, privacy, meaningful access, participation &accountability in all stages of the project. Addressing the needs of community clusters and involving community members as leaders throughout the project’s implementation through project committees, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk and enforce protection standards for the “do no harm” policy. Community stakeholders including governorate offices and local directorates of MoE and MoH will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. The security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MA’AN and WW-GVC to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented. Furthermore, MA’AN and WW-GVC’s local coordinators and continued presence in the communities along with the targeted institutions’ staff will be regularly reporting on any risks to safety and security during implementation to mitigate time and effectiveness impact. To ensure safety and security and protection of existing services of institutions during implementation and in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN and WW-GVC ensure that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C does not affect the arial view of structure dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders thus no construction work is done, only rehabilitation work will be conducted. Also, in order not to impact the operations and services provided by such institutions, infrastructure work is coordinated to be conducted after school time, and for HCFs as much as possible when after work hours. MA’AN and WW-GVC will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during weekends) in coordinated times with community stakeholders to assess the security situation. MA’AN and WW-GVC mobilize/employ local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur and be aware of any problem with ICA or Israeli Military Forces in the communities in real time. And when possible, in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines, MA’AN procures from local sources. In addition, MA’AN has adopted several safeguarding policies such as PSEA and Child Protection which are mainstreamed throughout its scope of work. Furthermore, MA’AN staff follow strict health, hygiene, & safety measures (that were adopted during the spread of COVID-19) in order to mitigate the risk of harm being done to targeted beneficiaries and community members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed response will be implemented in coordination and partnership with WASH sector stakeholders at the national level with little or no direct provision of input on the HH or individual level. Within its core design, the proposed response is designed to ensure equal and impartial access to aid as it focuses on institutions (schools and HCs). Intended impact of the project will hence be impartial in aid accessibility to groups based on age, gender and disability while the design of institutional construction and rehabilitations works are rooted within addressing the needs of vulnerable groups. The planned proposed interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWD to safe water and sanitation. MA'AN and WW-GVC respect a non-discrimination approach through ensuring the equality in the access of the outcomes and benefits of the project.It is important to note that MA’AN Development Center adheres to the codes of conduct and standards that have been developed by the international humanitarian community. MA’AN has set standards of behavior in humanitarian settings seeking to maintain the high standards of independence, effectiveness, and impact. MA’AN recognizes its obligation to provide humanitarian assistance wherever it is needed. We strive to alleviate human suffering amongst those least able to withstand the stress caused by disaster. We base the provision of relief aid upon a thorough assessment of the needs of the disaster victims and the local capacities already in place to meet those needs. We endeavor to respect the culture, structures and customs of the communities we are working in. We do not tie the promise, delivery or distribution of assistance to the embracing or acceptance of a particular political or religious creed. Additionally, in order to protect our independence, we seek to avoid dependence upon a single funding source. We place a high priority on the proper co-ordination of our emergency responses, and we make sure to engage and build local capacities while doing so. Intended beneficiaries are always involved in the design, management and implementation of the assistance we provide. We also endeavor to minimize the negative impact of humanitarian assistance, seeking to avoid long-term beneficiary dependence upon external aid. Most importantly, we hold ourselves accountable to both those we seek to assist and those from whom we accept resources.
