Project Summary

In line with Cluster guidance, the objective of this response is to save and sustain lives by providing timely, targeted, and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. PIN plans to provide different shelter and NFI kits to meet the priorities and different needs in North-East Syria, notably distributions of Sealing-off (SoK) and emergency shelter kits, kitchen kits Winterization kits/cash. PIN will also contribute to sector SNFI sector objective - Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure through repair/rehabilitation of housing and related community/public infrastructure. PIN’s response will be grounded in needs assessment data, contextual understanding, and scenario planning. PIN aims to ensure safe, dignified housing to affected populations, protecting them from environmental and protection risks and allowing them to cover basic needs and restore livelihoods. PIN will continue activities started in previous years, where needs are still observed in host and IDP communities, and will remain flexible to the needs presented through 2022-23. PIN is moving towards a settlements-based approach, with the aim of ensuring affected communities have access to critical community infrastructure as well as shelter provision. At the same time, PIN retains the capacity to respond to sudden-onset displacements. In all sub-sectors, PIN will maintain flexible programming to respond to changes in operating context and support longer-term more sustainable services; with multi-sectoral integration. PIN will target locations based on needs assessment data, highlighting the most critical needs, vulnerabilities, and gaps. In areas where security, access, and market functionality permit, and needs assessments indicate cash-based approaches would provide a more appropriate response, PIN may shift assistance to value vouchers to assist the targeted population. Value vouchers for winterization will only be provided in cases where needs assessments show that NFI/winterization items are priority needs to mitigate the risk of these being used for other purposes. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups through employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet the distinct needs, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at all levels – from cross-border to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, the overall shelter severity increased across Syria, an increase from 2019 by 20%t to 5.65 M people. Of this, around 70% are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city, and camps in NES. With the ongoing displacement, concomitant lack of shelter options, and the sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, protection risks have increased, specifically related to GBV, child protection, and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in the IDP sites and makeshift shelters, in addition to a lack of WASH facilities, will put women and girls at particular risk for GBV. Assessments overwhelmingly demonstrate that households resort to negative coping strategies to meet basic needs while preparing for winter.Based on updates from the SNFI cluster, 1.7M people live in 1,400 unplanned sites and camps. The majority (80%) of people living in IDP sites in NWS are women and children. 38,000 people were reported to live with specific needs, 2.2 million people need shelter assistance, and more than 750,000 live in tents and shelters. PIN will provide winterization cash grants to support vulnerable food insecure HH to mitigate the deterioration of food security during winter and utilize the cash assistance to fulfill winterization needs without negative coping strategies to meet the NFI winterization they need.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN's proposed response is to save and sustain lives by providing timely and appropriate shelter assistance. PIN also aims to support more sustainable housing solutions through improving housing and related community infrastructure. PIN plans to conduct the following activities: Sealing-off kits (SoK) and emergency shelter kits will be provided to HH in informal settlements, collective centers, and damaged returnees' houses; emergency shelter kit to address acute shelter needs; new arrival & kitchen kits, winterization kits/cash to IDP and returnees in camps, informal settlements, and housing units during the winter of 2022-2023. Selected beneficiaries will be supported with light and medium repairs to their homes in a continuation of private shelter rehabilitation assistance, providing a durable solution to the chronic shelter needs of the population and reducing dependency on shelters of last resort. The work is done partly through local contractors and partly through cash-for-rehabilitations, where the affected population is supported through conditional cash and technical assistance.PIN will distribute 12,600 winterization cash grants to the most vulnerable HH in NWS; targeting winter-affected camps. The cash value will be determined in coordination with CBR-TWG, SNFI cluster, and humanitarian organizations to cover HH winterization expenses for 3 months. A value of approximately $150 to support winterization needs is estimated to meet fuel, NFIs, and clothing needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021 carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feed the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1) MM: needs-based targeting criteria communicated with communities and LCs; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion PwDs; FGDs with community representatives, communication channels for CFRM, 2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of packages across partners, assistance to host communities and IDPs.3) R:PSEA, 3)MM: Staff training; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process for PSEA cases, Signs posted in project sites stating free assistance. 4) R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe,4)MM: distribution procedures inline with Sphere, collaboration with security teams for timing and locations, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered areas, seating if possible; Different queues for men/women, PIN home deliveries assistance to beneficiaries with mobility difficulties; kits are grouped to lighter boxes for easy transportation. 5)R: Shelter infrastructure is not accessible to all/safe, 5)MM: Health facilities are rehabilitated to be safe for people with disabilities (platforms, ramps).

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to other areas; and/or switch to remote management, through local partners. (A2/R2) staff, offices are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, temporarily suspends activities; investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention ;(A3/R3): LCs/others collaborate inline with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access WG) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted from transferring goods, PIN suspends activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift funds to Cash program.
