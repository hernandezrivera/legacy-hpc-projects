Project Summary

This T-ICSP is in line with SDG 2 and 17 and aims to contribute to the following strategic outcomes: - Crisis-affected populations, including refugees and IDPs, in targeted areas are able to meet their basic food and nutrition needs during and in the aftermath of crises.
- Food insecure school-age children, in targeted regions have access to adequate and nutritious food all year-round.
- Vulnerable groups including children, pregnant women and adolescent girls in targeted areas have enhanced nutritional status all year-round.
- Food insecure populations and communities exposed to climate shocks in targeted areas have more resilient livelihoods and sustainable food systems all year-round.
- National institutions have strengthened capacities to manage food-security, nutrition, and social protection and resilience building programmes by 2030.
- Humanitarian and development partners have access to common services to access and operate in targeted areas throughout the year.

Needs

• The national SMART survey of 2016 indicated that the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Niger was 10.3%, which is slightly above the 10% serious threshold. GAM rates among children aged 6 to 23 months stood at 17.4%, well above the emergency threshold (15%) compared to children aged 24-59 months (6.2%). • Analysis by region at the national level showed a higher prevalence of malnutrition in rural areas with a rate of 10.5% against 8.5% in urban areas. The disparities between regions indicated that the situation in four (4) regions, Maradi (13.3%), Agadez (2.8%), Diffa (11.4%) and Zinder (10.9%) maintained a prevalence of acute malnutrition above the national rate. The rate of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), was estimated at 1.9% remaining close to the critical threshold of 2%. Chronic malnutrition remains above the 40 percent "critical" threshold with 42.2% at national level. The Nutrition Cluster estimated that some 1,746,220 people require nutritional assistance, including 380,166 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM), including 59,557 cases with medical complications. It should be noted that recent publications have highlighted a methodological problem regarding the estimation of needs for SAM in Niger; these methodological problems, which for the time being follow single international recommendations for all the countries of the world, would tend to give largely underestimated results. Some 922,656 children aged 6 to 59 months (boys and girls) suffering from moderate acute malnutrition, 303,757 pregnant women with an upper arm circumference of less than 210 mm, and 77424 accompanying persons in the CRENIs will need appropriate nutritional supplementation.
