1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The refugee response in Mozambique aims at strengthening the protection environment and enjoyment of rights by refugees and asylum-seekers; advocating for their inclusion into national systems and services; ensuring their meaningful engagement in decision making that affects their lives; providing life-saving humanitarian assistance where needed and supporting access to durable solutions. For 2025, over 25,000 refugees and asylum seekers are projected to reside in Mozambique with the majority (46 per cent) living in the province of Nampula (including 31 per cent in Maratane refugee settlement), 20 per cent in Maputo city and 19 per cent in Maputo province. The remainder live in eight other provinces (around three per cent each in Cabo Delgado, Tete and Zambezia, two per cent in Manica and Sofala, one per cent in Gaza and Niassa, and around 0,5 per cent in Inhambane and Niassa). Among them, 39 per cent are women, and 61 per cent per cent are men. Around 27 per cent per cent are 17 years of age or younger, 70 per cent are between 18 and 59, and two per cent are above 60. The majority originate from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (39%), Burundi (36%), Rwanda, (15%) ,Somalia (9%) and 1% of other nationalities. More than 2,400 persons with specific needs are registered in Mozambique, including single parents, unaccompanied and separated children, children at risk, women at risk, older persons at risk, persons with disabilities, and persons with serious medical conditions.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The 2025 refugee project will target the following areas: Maratane Refugee Settlement, Nampula, Maputo, Cabo Delgado, Tete, Zambezia, Manica, Sofala, Gaza, Inhambane and Niassa provinces. The following summarizes the key activities: - The Government will be supported to ensure the national legal and policy framework is aligned with international conventions and enhance inclusion into national systems, as well as in the implementation of the out-of-camp policy for Maratane. - INAR will continue to undertake biometric registration of refugees and asylum-seekers as well as the registration of their children born in Mozambique and the issuance of ID cards. - Access to civil documentation and legal aid will be supported, including in support to access to nationality. - Gender-based violence survivors and women at risk will have safe and adequate access to quality services to respond to their needs. - Risk of violence, exploitation and abuse of children and adolescents, including those on the move, will be identified and addressed, as part of the regional Route-Based approach regional strategy. UNHCR prioritizes the protection and rights of children, adolescents, and youth through psychosocial support and tailored programmes addressing their specific needs.- Lifesaving assistance will be made available to the most vulnerable refugees and asylum-seekers, including, nutrition, shelter and CRIs. - Access to basic services such as health, WASH, and education will be enhanced. - Disaster risk reduction and emergency preparedness will be strengthened through engagement with the government and communities.-The allocation of land for refugees and asylum seekers residing in Maratane Settlement will be prioritized to enhance food security and promote sustainable local integration. This initiative aims to empower refugees and asylum seekers to engage in agricultural activities, enabling self-reliance and reducing dependency on external food aid.-Efforts will be made to strengthen social cohesion between the refugee population and the host community. Collaborative programs and dialogue platforms will be implemented to foster mutual understanding and cooperation, particularly concerning land use and farming practices. - Community self-management structures will be strengthened.-Design and implementation of context-specific livelihoods programmes, based on comprehensive assessments of skills, assets, and market opportunities, while strengthening agricultural sector and social and solidarity-based economies. This will be supported by the call to action (C2A) which is a global initiative rolled out in Mozambique which directs UNHCR and WFP to ensure the necessary humanitarian assistance is available and targeted to those in need while identifying and moving forward concrete actions to expand food security, nutrition, self-reliance and inclusion into development and government programmes.-The transitioning of Maratane from a humanitarian-dependent settlement to a climate-resilient, locally integrated village where refugees and host communities thrive.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Through strengthened community-based protection, and comprehensive complaints and feedback mechanisms the identification of needs and protection incidents among refugees and asylum-seekers will be undertaken, contributing to a better understanding of protection trends and informing the response. Through AAP approaches, which involve regular community engagement, UNHCR ensures people are at the center of all interventions, providing feedback before, during, and after, mitigating the risk of acting in a way that causes harm or is not in the best interest of the population. UNHCR will work with the refugee community to enhance their participation and inclusion at all levels. In this regard, UNHCR will strengthen the existing two-ways dialogue with all categories of the affected population, including women, girls, men and boys. The following mechanisms are in place and will be strengthened: individual protection counselling sessions, field monitoring visits, monthly and thematic meetings with community leadership structures, protection helpline, complaint boxes and participatory assessment using an AGD approach. UNHCR is also a member of Linha Verde 1458, the interagency humanitarian hotline managed by WFP and has increased its financial contribution to the hotline while sitting in its steering committee. UNHCR will continue to manage the protection referrals from the hotline. Protection desks in Maratane and Nampula city will continue to operate on a weekly basis to provide counselling to refugees and asylum seekers with heightened protection risks. In coordination with Instituto de Comunicacao Social-Sede (ICS) and WFP, the Maratane Community Radio will be supported and strengthened to disseminate information to the refugees and host community. Community-based protection empowers communities to claim their rights with dignity, fostering collaboration between refugees, asylum seekers and humanitarian actors to address protection risks and develop effective responses. UNHCR will support government and civil society partners in adopting an age, gender and diversity approach, preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring accountability to the affected people, and improving communication with communities to promote safety, inclusion and meaningful participation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNHCR and partner agencies maintain a regular presence in Maratane and focus group discussions are conducted monthly. Community engagement with refugees and host communities is at the center of planning and decision-making to build protection strategies and strengthen social cohesion between communities and foster local integration. Through a rights- and community-based approach, community participation is ensured in decision making that affect their lives. For urban refugees and asylum-seekers, protection counselling sessions and monthly meetings are held to identify protection risks and strengthen participation and feedback. Partners engaged in the response are guided by the concept of ‘putting people first’ and drawing on the rich range of experiences, capacities and aspiration of refugees and asylum-seekers with recognition of age-gender diversity mainstreaming. UNHCR is committed to prioritizing persons with specific needs through various interventions and activities, such as the provision of mobility aids and assistive devices. Additionally, UNHCR will continue to deliver community-based rehabilitation services aimed at improving the quality of life for persons with disabilities, including children, youth, and adolescents. These efforts are designed to promote independence, foster self-reliance, and enhance their active participation within their communities. UNHCR is committed to supporting community self-management structures. By fostering skill development and providing necessary resources, these measures aim to empower community leadership, promote resilience, and ensure timely and efficient service delivery within the refugee and asylum-seeker population.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR engages and consults people with specific needs, including people with disabilities, through an age, gender, diversity (AGD) approach in order to identify the specific barriers they face and ensure disability inclusion in the provision of life-saving assistance, including in making referrals to specialized services as needed. By applying an AGD approach in all Protection activities, including for instance reception and individual interviews, but also, for example, support to the authorities on registration and access to civil documentation, UNHCR aims to ensure that all affected populations, including those with specific needs such as persons with disabilities, enjoy their rights on an equal footing and participate meaningfully in decisions that affect their lives, families and communities.People with disabilities are given a distinct voice in UNHCR’s participatory assessments and field monitoring, ensuring they have a say over decisions that affect their lives.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Disasters linked to natural hazards, including the impacts of climate, are drivers of displacement. In Mozambique, refugees and asylum seekers reside in locations prone to flooding droughts and cyclones. Hence, UNHCR refugee response integrates environment considerations, including climate resilient shelter construction, latrines improvement plan to phase out pit-latrines and protect the aquifer from contamination by human waste, waste management and community hygiene and promotion and environment protection programmes. UNHCR is also actively involved in the contingency planning to mitigate the impact of natural disasters, in line with INGD disaster preparedness and response plan as approved by the government, advocating for the inclusion of refugees and asylum-seekers in any government-led contingency planning and early warning measures. UNHCR is also involved in developing the capacity of the government authorities through legal advice and guidance at national and regional levels to enhance the protection of the rights of people displaced in the context of disasters and climate change. Finally, over recent years, UNHCR has advanced climate action at Maratane through key initiatives, such as integrating refugees into local disaster risk management committees and introducing durable shelter models using sustainable materials. These efforts have saved lives, safeguarded property during extreme weather, and mitigated environmental degradation. Moving forward, and working with relevant stakeholders, including the government, UNHCR aims to transform Maratane from a humanitarian-dependent settlement to a climate-resilient, locally integrated village where refugees and host communities thrive.
