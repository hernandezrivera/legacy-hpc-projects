Project Summary

The purpose of this intervention is to provide support to children and youth with disabilities who are out of school or at risk of dropping out due to the economic crisis. The crisis has resulted in families, especially the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant households, having to adjust their coping strategies related to their livelihoods. The intervention aims to enhance the academic and non-academic competencies of these children and youth, by providing them with the necessary support to expand access and retention in education. The project will employ door-to-door outreach activities to reach a target group of 3000 children and their caregivers. This approach will help identify children who are out of school or at risk of dropping out. Considering that at least 2 children are expected to be identified in each household, the outreach activities have the potential to reach at least 6400 children. Out of this group, approximately 3000 children will require referral to public formal education or Multiple Flexible Pathways to ensure they can access quality education. The project will prioritize support for highly vulnerable households, including Lebanese(60%), Palestinian(35%), and migrant populations(5%) in North, BML and Bekaa regions, respecting a 50-50 gender ratio for students, but expected to reach more female parents during outreach. All other nationalities will be referred to complementary projects conducted by HI and its partners.
HI will conduct assessments to identify the needs and barriers to education for children and youth with disabilities. The project will also provide awareness sessions to caregivers and children to increase awareness about the importance of education and reduce dropout rates. Additionally, based on findings from a previous OCHA project, HI will distribute educational technologies and assistive devices to children with disabilities to ensure they have the necessary tools to succeed in school and are able to retain their access to education. The project will also refer and follow up on the children to complementary services to enable their retention in education.
In addition, the project will identify at least two formal and informal education centers/facilities in the four areas of intervention and develop MoUs with these centers. HI will provide structured psychosocial support activities to at least 3000 children and youth, and refer and follow up on 500 children with additional needs to support/improve their education engagement. The project aims to expand access and retention in education for children and youth with disabilities and enhance their academic and non-academic competencies.
To ensure protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion across all project activities, HI will sensitize staff and centers on HI's policies (PSEA, AAP, risk identification) and DGA marker. The project will implement AAP throughout all phases, through continuous assessments at the field level. Beneficiaries will receive fundamental protection messages through outreach efforts, and they will be informed about all HI FCM channels (complaint phone line, complaint email, complaint boxes, HI staff, FGDs) to ensure that all groups are aware of how to express feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

HI will implement a project complementary to its UNICEF program to support out of school children and/or in learning students, specifically with disabilities to remain in schools. Under outcome 1 (SO1), HI will provide Children, youth and their caregivers with the necessary support to expand access and retention in education. Activities will include 1) Implementation of door to door outreach activities to identify out of school children or children at risk to be out of schools. 2) Assess the needs of the identified 3000 children and their barriers to education, 3) Provide outreached households awareness sessions 4) Provide the children with educational technologies and assistive devices for children with disabilities. 5) Refer and follow up the children to complementary services to enable their retention in educationUnder outcome 2 (SO2), Children in learning spaces will have enhanced academic and non-academic competencies. Activities will include 1) Identify at least 2 formal and informal education centers/ facilities in the 4 areas of intervention. Target; 8 2) Develop MoUs with education centers. 3) Provide structured PSS activities to at least 3000 children and youth 4) Refer and follow up on 500 children with additional needs (health, educational technologies, formal education, assistive devices …) to support/ improve their education engagement.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As part of its implementation strategy, HI mainstreams protection in all its projects and activities, which allows to focus on providing access to the most vulnerable, including persons with disabilities, and ensure that activities are safe and dignified. Project activities are designed to include all critical vulnerable individuals within the Lebanese, Palestinian and migrant population. All HI and partner staff have been sensitized on HI’s policies (PSEA, AAP, risk identification) and DGA (Disability, Gender and Age) marker to recognize all vulnerabilities, while Leaflets, posters conveying Safeguarding messages are displayed in accessible areas for all.HI will conduct tailored activities based on previous and continuing beneficiary consultations to ensure that their needs are promoted during all phases of the response.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAP is ensured throughout all phases of project design, implementation, and evaluation. The project design is based on continuous assessments at field level that analyze the changing needs of affected populations and the relevance of ongoing and potential activities. The project will ensure that beneficiaries and stakeholders have access to project information via posters, brochures, and the existing coordination working groups. The project will ensure that all HI and partner staff receive PSEA and FCM training/refresher. Beneficiaries will get fundamental protection messages through outreach efforts, and they will be informed about all HI FCM channels (complaint phone line, complaint email, complaint boxes, HI staff, FGDs) to ensure that all groups are aware of how to express feedback or complaints in a safe and confidential manner. A complete MEAL plan, including an indicator matrix, will be established, and systematic monitoring will take place to oversee activity execution.
