Project Summary

The proposed project aims to cover the health needs of most vulnerable conflict, flood and insecurity affected women, men, boys, and girls in the target locations by providing basic lifesaving curative and preventive health services as well as scale-up emergency response (EWARN), emergency trauma, care and case management. Moreover, the action will capacitate the frontline health care workers on Somalia treatment guideline, proper medical waste management, basic and comprehensive emergency maternal obstetric and neonatal care as well as clinical care for sexual assault survivors. The project also in cooperates GBV component including clinical management of rape (CMR) and referral for mental health and psychosocial support services with 1,307 GBV survivors planned to be supported with a prompt response within 72 hours . Facilities not offering clinical management of rape to the survivors will ensure referrals are made as necessary. The proposed action will also incorporate response to disease outbreaks including COVID-19 pandemic through provision of platforms for COVID-19 vaccination roll out at the target facilities. Overall, the project envisages to reach 243576 individuals (119,352 M and 124,224 F) including 8,522 people living with disabilities for a period of 12 months. The project will provide primary and secondary health services in 7 PHUs, 20 health centers, 2 referral health centers and 2 hospitals in 6 regions (Bakool, Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Bay, Gedo and Nugaal). To expand coverage the project will ensure service provision for people living in hard to reach areas through deployment of 10 mobile teams to provide primary health services including clinical management of rape for SGBV survivors. To bolster accountability to affected population, joint supportive supervision with the respective state MOH and the district health management teams will be undertaken on a quarterly basis. Additionally, the health facility committee and community-level health service provision and referrals through CHWs will be strengthened in the project target location with a full representation of key community stakeholders including people living with disabilities. Routine mental health screening at the outpatient will be conducted at facility level on a daily basis and cases identified will receive treatment and essential medicines for mental health will be included in the list of medical supplies for the project. The project will be aligned with MoH strategies (e.g. Health Sector Strategic Plan III, revised EPHS guideline), and information systems (e.g. DHIS2, ReportHUB and HeRAMs). The project will also strengthen referral linkages at the district level by establishing referral pathways and linkages among the different levels of care. Moreover, project beneficiaries will be linked with other interventions (Nutrition, WASH and FSL) supported by Action Against Hunger in the same locations in order to provide a holistic package to the target beneficiaries for optimal impact.

1. - Projects activities

The proposed project will support provision of primary and secondary health care, including OPD consultation, Inpatient services, maternity, Immunization and mental health services through the fixed facilities and mobile teams in the target locations. Under-five children will be immunized from vaccine-preventable diseases with follow-up mechanisms put in place for successful completion of required vaccination as per the country immunization schedule including TT vaccine for WCBA attending the supported facilities. Action Against Hunger will through this project improve quality MNCH preventive and curative care services at health facility and community levels targeting children, adolescents and women. This will include provision of Antenatal care services including tetanus toxoid vaccination, intermittent preventive treatment for malaria in pregnancy, use of treated bed nets (ITN), iron and folate supplementation. In addition, there will be treatment of anemia, STIs, deworming, screening for preeclampsia with urine protein dipstick and BP and pregnancy monitoring including early detection of high-risk pregnancies and close follow up of high-risk pregnancies. Moreover, skilled Birth health personnel within supported health facilities will conduct institutional deliveries. Continuous OJT and theoretical training will be provided to the skilled birth attendant to better manage pre-eclampsia and other obstetric complications. The supported facilities will carry out basic and comprehensive emergency maternal obstetric and neonatal care services in line with the EPHS guideline at the different levels. Other services provided will include prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV and referral of emergency cases that require specialized services. Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM) will be continued in this project to achieve higher treatment coverage and to deliver high-quality care to sick children at community level in far-flung and hard to reach villages especially in Bakool region. Treatment will focus on three major causes of child mortality: Diarrhoea, malaria and childhood pneumonia. Linkages will be made with health centers and other fixed facilities for CHWs to refer patients needing skilled care services. Community health workers will be provided with a treatment register that they will use to record all the treated cases and referral forms for any cases that require review by the clinicians. Community health workers will also be trained on identifying complicated ICCM conditions and they will recommend referral for advanced clinical management. Community health workers will also sensitize the community to ensure that all sick children are reported to the CHWs for community case management or referral based on the condition. Action Against Hunger will equip the supported health facilities to meet the required standards for clinical management of rape services as well as mapping, training health care workers and expanding coverage through mobile teams. Additionally, Multi-sectoral response will be undertaken during implementation by setting up referral linkages for psychosocial, legal and security aspects of GBV response. Survivors will be supported with a prompt response within 72 hours. Action Against Hunger will continue to sustain adequate IPC measures taking standard precautions for all patients, hand and respiratory hygiene, use of appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), injection safety practices, safe waste management, proper linens, environmental cleaning and sterilization of patient-care equipment. Action Against Hunger will collaborate with local leaders, community health workers, and community health committees to organize community dialogues/meetings to discuss and identify barriers to care-seeking and locally appropriate strategies for overcoming these barriers to increase demand and utilization of health services in target locations.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: Action Against Hunger have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. Action Against Hunger already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to Action Against Hunger regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards). Action Against Hunger will provide medical care within 72 hours for the SGBV cases reported and will further refer survivors who need additional support such as psychosocial care to the GBV partners. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking inter community conflict over access to resources: Action Against Hunger will engage District local authorities, Health facility committees and Village structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.
