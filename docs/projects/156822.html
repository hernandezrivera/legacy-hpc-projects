Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter assistance to IDPs and returnees in Syria, contributing to resilience of the population. The project activities will take place across Rural Damascus, Homs, Aleppo, Der Ezzor, Quneitra and Daraa Governorates.
SAFE project is designed to respond to two shelter objectives:
Objective 1: provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support, targeting approximately 5,282 individuals.
Objective 2: Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure targeting approximately 2,285 individuals.

Needs

More than 7 years of crisis in Syria has resulted in over 4.7 million in need of shelter support. The conflict contributed to the lack of basic shelter resulting in displaced and returning families having to live in damaged, unfinished or otherwise inadequate shelters and urgently require durable shelter solutions. While all Syrian communities have been affected by the crisis, newly displaced populations are often the most severely impacted. Displacements continue to take place in areas like Deir-ez-Zor requiring emergency response and many IDPs remain in need of emergency shelter in Tel Refaat in Aleppo. In Tel Refaat, displaced families live in unfinished buildings that have no protection from external elements and no internal facilities. Throughout 2018, many hard-to-reach and besieged areas have become newly accessible allowing increase in families able and/or willing to return to their places of origin. While relieving the burden on host communities, many IDPs have however returned home to find dire living conditions with limited or no access to basic services, destroyed homes, high infrastructural damage and few livelihood opportunities. Between August and October 2018, over 400,000 returns took place (OCHA 2018) and many more are expected to happen in 2019. Based on ADRA’s multi-sectoral needs assessment across the six targeted governorates (Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, Daraa, Quneitra, Deir-Ezzor), high priority needs (of young children, children, adults and elderly) were identified in the sector of shelter amongst the other sectors. Many families that are unable to return to their homes as they are unsuitable for living. Returnee families find their apartments with infrastructural damages, without doors and windows, no WASH and kitchen facilities and without electricity and protection. While some are able or forced to rent, other live in inadequate shelters with lack of necessary bathing or cooking facilities, inadequate space, a lack of privacy, a lack of heating or a lack of insulation. More importantly, the most vulnerable groups such as female headed households, families with elderly and disabled (male and female) and young boys and girls, require urgent intervention for shelter. Privacy, adequate space and sufficient in-house WASH facilities are desperately required for dignity of the households, especially for females, young girls and boys, teenagers and young adults disabled and elderly as they tend to spend more time in the house. As female tend to spend more time in houses looking after their children and elderly family members, the requirements for safety and cooking and WASH facilities is especially important to respond to their needs. Male tend to be out of the house during the day trying to provide for their families. Currently, in Rural Damascus, ADRA identified more than 3,000 damaged houses (in Hajiira, East Ghouta, Sbeineh and Zakiya) that require rehabilitation as well as infrastructures that require rehabilitation to allow people to return. Homs Governorate has 0.68 million people in acute need, ADRA identified locations such as Al Bayada and Al Husn as well as in Rural Homs, Palmyra as priority to provide adequate and durable housing to returnees. Aleppo governorate is the second highest in population of people in need, 2.25 million, and the needs for assistance for returnees in Aleppo remain high, requiring the provision of the support to the people living in substandard houses in areas such as Al Ansari Mashhad and Rural Aleppo. Following ADRA’s shelter interventions in 2018, and the feedback collected from the beneficiaries, the needs of the elderly, disabled and pregnant lactating women must be addressed separately according to each individual case. ADRA learnt that in general, families with elderly and disabled male and female requests western toilets (instead of eastern style), latrine chairs and ramps.

Implementation of activities

Sector objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter supportActivity 1.1: Rehabilitation / upgrading of life saving and life sustaining shelter spaces in collective shelters and unfinished buildings. Sector Objective 2: Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing.Activity 2.1: Support to sustainability repair / rehabilitate housing using durable shelter solutions. ADRA will coordinate with the local municipalities, Governorates and Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) to identify the specific needs of each area. The response could include rehabilitation of collective shelters, light shelter rehabilitation, private shelter upgrade and repair of damaged houses. The most vulnerable families will be prioritized as per the shelter sector guidelines, such as female headed households, families with infant boys and girls, families with elderly and / or disabled men and women and families with large number of individuals. Only lightly damaged apartments will be rehabilitated under the Objective 2. Under both objectives, 1 and 2, the needs of males and females will be considered ensuring gender segregated latrines with lockable doors and sufficient lightning are rehabilitated. Additionally, ramps to access shelter and specially designed toilets and showers for disabled, elderly and mothers with infants will be rehabilitated. HLP issues, if identified, will be referred to the local Civil Registries. Once the prioritization is completed, the experienced ADRA male and female engineers will carry out the technical assessment to identify the needs of the shelters in consultation with the owners and beneficiaries in order to bring the living spaces to SPHERE and the shelter sector standards. At this stage, males, females, boys and girls, elderly and disabled beneficiaries will be consulted for their priority needs for rehabilitation and any special requests will be taken into consideration, including building ramps and installing western toilets. The Bill of Quantities (BoQ) will be prepared. Once all approvals are obtained, the tender document will be prepared. Selection criteria include quality of submitted samples, past experience in similar projects, delivery deadline and price. ADRA team will closely monitor the progress, ensuring the contractor’s compliance with the contract, technical specifications and guidelines. Special attention will be paid to female, children (young boys and girls and teenagers) as well as elderly, disabled and male protection and access to ensure no risks are imposed during implementation. At the completion of the intervention, the apartments/buildings/shelters will be handed over to the beneficiaries or the municipality in charge. In cases of collective shelters, ADRA, where possible will carry out post rehabilitation monitoring visits to ensure sustainability and when needed maintain the facilities through light intervention (such as replace taps or unblock sewerage systems, replace burned bulbs etc). ADRA has a number of emergency funding reserved should sudden emergencies occur. According to ADRA’s assessments, the needs for private shelter upgrade have been identified in Tel Refaat, Aleppo, for the IDPs from Afrin, which are not able or willing to find housing at the established IDP camps. Throughout the rehabilitation, the ADRA team, comprised of men and women, will ensure their availability to accept any feedback from the beneficiaries and respond accordingly. Additionally, ADRA has a well-functioning complaint mechanism distributing a free of charge hotline number where people can call and complain or provide their feedback. Their feedback is recorded and if any technical follow-up actions are required, the responsible individuals are contacted to ensure that the issue was addressed.

Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA takes protection very seriously and has active Protection policy which was recently reviewed and endorsed. When implementing shelter activities, ADRA adheres to the humanitarian principle of ‘Do No Harm’ and pays attention to protection risks that can occur. While there are many risks that can occur, one of the main protection risks are unclear beneficiary selection criteria and unfair shelter response, which can lead to tensions between households and communities. The likelihood of this risk is high, however the impact is medium. As a mitigation method, ADRA will ensure to follow the shelter sector guideline for selection of beneficiaries and ensure that the local municipalities are also aware of the criteria. For monitoring purposes, ADRA will have officers and the MEAL team monitoring the intervention as well as monitoring field visits from the top management. Another protection risk that can occur is health and safety for beneficiaries during rehabilitation, while building materials are being installed. The likelihood of this risk is high and the impact is also high. As a mitigating measure, ADRA regularly monitors the implementation and ensured contractors comply with the health and safety measures. All, male, female, boys and girls in the household are made aware of the time when the intervention will take place. Phased intervention, such as doors and windows installed simultaneously, WASH facilities rehabilitated at the same time are implemented. Additionally, all ADRA staff have been trained on first aid and have first aid box available when necessary. ADRA has a two-way fully functioning complaint mechanism that allows boys, girls, men and women to raise complaints. The ADRA staff are trained on accepting complaints in the field and it is ensured that a male and a female staff are available for beneficiaries to communicate with the person of their choice. Additionally, anonymous complaints can be raised through the hotline number that is shared with all beneficiaries and is answered by a specifically dedicated complaints officer. This officer is also responsible for follow-up on the complaints to ensure they have been responded to and the people were satisfied with the response and how it was dealt with. All ADRA officers are trained on protection mainstreaming to ensure it is incorporated at every stage of the response.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Potential risks and assumptions that can occur during the project life are as follows:Risks and assumptions: - Access is available to the target population. To mitigate this risk, ADRA will regularly monitor the situations in the areas of intervention and pause visits when necessary.- Qualified suppliers and contractors are available and gain the necessary approvals to access the area. To mitigate this risk ADRA will ensure to advertise in the local newspapers to reach the local contractors. In case of unavailability, ADRA has a list of potential contractors with good history record who will be contacted. This will be in line with ADRA’s procurement policy. - Qualified staff are available. To mitigate this risk, ADRA will ensure that capacity building is in place for all employees. - Necessary permissions are available. To mitigate this risk, ADRA will regularly follow-up with the relevant ministries to obtain the approvals. In cases approvals are not obtained, other locations will be selected.
