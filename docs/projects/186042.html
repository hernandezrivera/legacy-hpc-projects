Project Summary

Since April 2018, IOM has operated and managed a total of 9 humanitarian hubs composed of a base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and 8 field hubs located in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2 hubs) and Damasak. The humanitarian hubs provide humanitarian actors the ability to work with safe and secure accommodation, office space and meeting/training facilities, enabling them to stay and deliver interventions necessary to saving and improving the lives of hundreds of thousands of Nigerians affected by conflict. The field hubs also host the internet connectivity service of the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS). These humanitarian hubs help sustain the access of humanitarian responders to the population affected by the conflict, enabling them to coordinate and provide better assistance to the affected population. Demand for these services has increased, and IOM has accommodated additional space at hub facilities. The humanitarian base camp in Maiduguri now has a total capacity of 153 beds while each additional field hub has a 20-bed capacity. Banki Hub added an extension of 72 beds, when the international community needed them. All of the hubs have office space and training/meeting facilities available to humanitarian actors. Under the proposed project, IOM will aim to support the operation and maintenance of eight humanitarian field hubs and the base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof).
Through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), IOM will respond to Inter-sectorial needs Assessment based on Joint Inter-sectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF) requirements, including having 120 Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), data collection at a household level in 60 Local Government Areas (LGAs) for a total of 8,700 Households (HHs), monitoring of needs on a quarterly basis, Rapid needs Assessment (ad-hoc) upon request by Inter-sector Working Groups (ISWG) and Inter-sector Coordination Groups (ISCG) – for a crisis requiring significant needs assessment. This will be done through regular meetings with ISCG / Assessment Analysis Working Group (AAWG) partners and Individual follow-up to ensure adequate Joint Inter-sectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF) / Inter-sectorial needs assessment methodology and forms. The Geographic priorities are BAY States of Nigeria– Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states (60 LGAs).

1 - Needs

The North-East region of Nigeria experienced violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) since 2015 resulting in massive displacement of residents who fled from the areas affected by the violence. Several humanitarian agencies responded to the resulting humanitarian crisis but were met with several challenges. One of the major challenges faced by the humanitarian agencies was the availability of safe accommodation and office facilities for their teams. In response to the need, IOM is operating and maintaning humanitarian hubs in Maiduguri and within accessible parts of Borno state. Currently, IOM operates a main hub in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and a total of eight field hubs in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2 hubs) and Damasak in deep field locations. Despite the establishment of the eight deep field hubs, the continued insecurity in these areas still poses a major challenge to humanitarian presence. In 2021, two locations, Damsak and Dikwa, were temporarily shut down after attacks from armed groups. Both locations suffered from infrastructure damage, and with funding from various donors IOM rebuilt the hubs. IOM then worked with UNDSS to identify security enhancements that will improve the security facilities of the hubs based on specific threats experienced. These improvements are underway and will be finalized in early 2022 as part of an augmentation of priority 1 categorization. Priority 2 needs are also critical, such as the installation of solar power systems in the hubs lessen the dependence of the hubs on diesel for power generation and to lower the operating costs of the hubs. IOM utilizes a cost-recovery mechanism which supports up to 30% of the total cost of operating the 9 hubs. Additional funding is needed to cover the budget gap in operating the 9 humanitarian hubs, to keep their facilities in good running condition, ensuring their continued operation, and completing Priority 2 solar power in the hubs. DTM programme provides support to the Government and humanitarian partners by establishing a comprehensive system that collects, analyses, and disseminates data on IDPs and returnees to ensure timely and practical assistance to the affected populations. In each round of DTM assessments, IOM, National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) and the Nigerian Red Cross Society (NRCS) staff collate data in the field, including displacement baseline data at Local Government Area (LGA) and ward-levels. More so, carrying out detailed assessments in displacement sites, such as camps and collective centres, and in locations where IDPs reside among host communities. Since its establishment in Nigeria in August 2014, IOM’s DTM team has grown with well-trained and experienced DTM enumerators, a strong information management structure supporting robust data processing and analysis. DTM’s field presence is based on dynamic and responsive mechanisms, capable of scaling up activities in a short space of time. Partners rely heavily on DTM products for planning, decision making, service provision, and accountability to the affected population. The project aims to gather critical data to provide a comprehensive understanding of the needs and vulnerabilities of crisis-affected people in BAY states of North-East Nigeria and inform the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2022, ISCG and AAWG for more coordinated humanitarian assistance.

2 - Activities

The base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) has a total bed capacity of 153 beds, while the field hubs each have a capacity of 20 beds, except for the second hub in Banki which has a capacity of 72 beds. In addition to providing accommodation, all hubs provide office spaces and meeting/training facilities for humanitarian agencies. In collaboration with the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS), the field hubs also provide internet connectivity to humanitarian workers who are not residents of the field hubs. Activities for 2022 include the following:• Complete the implementation and augmentation of the priority 1 security facilities requirement of the hubs, IOM received funding to cover the implementation of all the priority 1 items in the list of security enhancements needed by the hubs. IOM shall continue the implementation of the security facilities enhancement of the hubs and complete it within the second quarter of 2022. • Reopen both Dikwa and Damasak hubs early in the first quarter of 2022.• Complete solar power installations in Banki, improve the solar capacity of the safe rooms, and bring solar power to all remaining hubs, depending on donor funding. This will contribute to the sustainability of the operation of the hubs. • Keep the facilities of all the 9 hubs properly maintained and available for the use of humanitarian workers. The facilities and equipment of the hubs are at least 3 years old. They have been constantly used since the hubs started operating. For example, we are improving the solar capacity of safe rooms/bunkers with new batteries and inverters with greater capacity and will last longer, since the technology has improved. Other items, like furniture and equipment need replacing in some instances. DTM:a) Quarterly Needs Monitoring through the Inter-Sector Needs Assessment with sub-activities of the Joint Inter-sectoral Analysis Framework JIAF / Inter-sectorial needs Assessment, b) Needs Monitoring c) Rapids Assessments (Ad-hoc). The JIAF is a methodologically new approach to analyzing multiple needs of populations in crisis. The international humanitarian community introduced it into the Humanitarian Programme Cycle in 2020. The JIAF offers a standard method, process and tools to conduct a “people-centred” holistic needs analysis.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM is committed to gender mainstreaming, promoting gender equality and prioritizing the needs and concerns of vulnerable groups. IOM has worked to strengthen its institutional policies and commitments on gender and protection within its crisis operations worldwide by developing the Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises (GBViC Framework). Addressing GBV is an issue of institutional accountability and at the core of IOM’s mandate. Central to the framework is the need to ensure that the safety, dignity and well-being of all crisis-affected persons, especially women and girls, and their equitable access to services are prioritized, integrated and coordinated across all IOM crisis operations. IOM is thereof committed to the Call to Action on Protection from GBV. IOM will contribute to promoting gender equality by including both men and women in all components of the project. IOM also provides Protection against sexual exploitation and abuses (PSEA) trainings across various locations (namely Monguno, Damasak, Ngala, Bama, Banki and Banki extension, Gwoza, and Dikwa) for all IOM staff hired locally. These trainings are scheduled and organized in 2-hour slot per location, depending on staff availability and workload. The humanitarian hubs has trained staff in all humanitarian hubs through an orientation/ workshop of PSEA to heighten their awareness on PSEA and the reporting mechanisms in place. In addition, IOM observes gender equality in recruiting staff. In the humanitarian hubs operations, accommodation rooms are assigned to guests taking into consideration the gender.Sanitary facilities in all the hubs have been designated for female and male and marked accordingly. The hubs assign sanitary facilities that are easily accessible to female guests and staff. All the hubs facilities, including the bunkers, are suitable for the use of both female and male. IOM is guided by its gender equality policy, institutional framework, and tools for addressing GBV risks and applies the IASC GBV guidelines to ensure that measures are taken to mitigate risks of GBV and gender-specific needs are addressed throughout all projects activities. IOM will ensure that its project activities benefit women and men, girls and boys, and be delivered sustainable and equitable. IOM consists of male and female staff members, which is an essential component and allows IOM to reach females and males across the population, ensures that women’s and girls’ participation is promoted, and further provides a better ground for monitoring the impact of interventions. IOM will keep the progress to sufficiently include gender in needs assessment, outputs and activities focusing on addressing gender inequality through targeting gender and/ or focusing on gender issues to contribute to gender equality significantly. IOM’s primary strategic planning documents include at least one high-level result on gender equality, where programmatic initiatives consistently include gender equality and the empowerment of women results. Our proposals, working documents, reports etc., apply systematic use of sex-disaggregated data. IOM has been contributing to UN SWAP for gender equality and UNDAF pillars indicated under the joint working plan among UN agencies to ensure gender equality and women empowerment. IOM keeps ensuring that staff at all levels take the online mandatory Gender training and become aware of IOM’s gender equality policy to be considered in the workplace and IOM’s programming. In addition, we will continue the gender training to ensure our staffs’ understanding of GBV and/or SEA issues is enhanced. Regarding Assessment, monitoring and evaluation of gender-specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will continue engaging with community structures on the ground in different locations during missions and establish or support communication channels with them where possible.
