Project Summary

GOALPrime intends to target a total of 1200 individuals: out of which 1160 (60% girls and 40% boys)
are children (learners age 3-17) including those living with disabilities in schools, camps, and host
communities. The adult target will be 20 (50% women and 50% men) teachers who will be trained on
improved teaching and learning approaches including PSS & conflict-sensitive education. Also, 20
SBMC members (8 men, 8 women, 2 boys & 2 girls) will be trained on safe schools, conflict-sensitive
education and support to learners. A target of 5% of the overall individuals to be reached will be persons
living with a disability.
500 out of the total learner target will be Out of School Children (OOSCs) who will be identified in
consultation with the community people and enrolled in the age-appropriate Accelerated Education
Programme (AEP). Profiled OOSCs after the successful AEPs will be mainstreamed into formal schools
in collaboration with LEAs, SAME & SUBEB. The 1160 learners targeted will be supported with age and
class-appropriate learning materials; the 20 teachers targeted will be supported with class-appropriate
teaching materials, while the 250 out-of-school children will be supported with Back-to-school enrolment
kits.
Moreso, caregivers of targeted learners and OOSCs will be reached with positive parenting sessions for
those who are able to come to the schools for the session while those who are unable to attend the
sessions will be visited by the GPON CVTs (door-2-door). Targeted adolescent girls in school will be
reached with Menstrual hygiene management life-skills sessions after which Menstrual Hygiene
Management Kits will be distributed. The Pads in the kit will be reusable sanitary pads.
GOALPrime education team will work with child protection colleagues to identify OOSCs using the
community-based PSS as an entry point and in collaboration with the Community Based Child
Protection Committees (CBCPCs) and Local Education Authorities (LEAs) in Kala-Balge and Monguno
LGAs to identify and profile Out of School Children (OOSCs) for enrolment during the termly back-to-
school campaigns. GOALPrime will support the renovation of 3 dilapidated TLS in target LGAs and also
provide target schools with 3-seater classroom desks. GPON will also coordinate effectively with the
LEAs at the LGA level for effective EiE service delivery to learners in target LGAs.
All approaches and activities will follow the COVID-19 Prevention and Control measures as provided by
World Health Organization (WHO), State Ministry of Health (SMoH), and the Nigerian Centre for Disease
Control (NCDC). GOALPrime will also integrate child-focused risk communication topics of COVID-19
into school club and Inter-House activity topics and themes. GOALPrime will also work with partners of
other sectors across target locations and encourage their participation in enrolment drives and support
to caregivers of learners. GOALPrime will be sensitive to the protection of the environment and ensure
that our approaches and tools do no harm to the environment and the beneficiaries. GOALPrime will
leverage this project to raise awareness of Climate change among learners accessing target schools
and TLS’.
GOALPrime, through her NHF-supported Humanity Studio, will also support EIEWG with the designs of
the sectors’ referral pathways, newsletters, advocacy notes, and other relevant resource materials.

1 - Needs

Yes, the trend of recent activities of the Non-State Armed Groups (NASG) suggests that the major Humanitarian crises in the Northeastern states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states) will persist in 2022. What started as a localized insurgency more than twelve years ago has turned into a regional armed conflict with no end in sight and severely affecting millions of people, subjecting them to displacement (newor continued), impoverishment, and the threat of violence. The humanitarian crises in Northeast Nigeria remain one of the largest and most severe in the world today and the effects of the insurgency in 2022 are the greatest with 8.4 million people in need. The 2022 Humanitarian needs overview has projected at least An estimated 1.9 million children need education services. The rapid assessment by GOALPrime isnMMC, Jere, Damboa Monguno, Kala-bulge Mafa, Gujba, Damaturu, Geidam, Yola south, and Girei has also validated the increasing education needs in these LGAs of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe.The impact of the crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without learning opportunities. This, in turn, increases their vulnerability. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO), an estimated 900,000 children have limited access to education services. In Adamawa State, only 30% of schools have sufficient learning materials, with lower proportions in Borno(26%) and Yobe (25%). Only 47% of schools in Borno have adequate furniture, 32% in Yobe, and 26% in Adamawa.The delivery of vital education services to the most affected and vulnerable people in hard-to-reach areas like Kala-Balge, Pulka LGAs in Borno state and Gujba LGA in Yobe state is hampered by access constraints caused by frequent attacks and clashes targeting civilians and school facilities. Only 29% of schools in the BAY states have teachers with the minimum level of teaching qualification, while in 36% of schools, half or less of their teaching workforce has the minimum required teaching qualification. In Kala-Balge, Pulka, and Gubja LGA,access to education remains a huge challenge notably due to the lack of financial means, and the increased population due to new displacement, ongoing relocations have worsened the pressure on schools already overcrowded.Furthermore, the NEDS report reveals the extent of the damage caused by decades of development neglect combined with the region's ongoing humanitarian crisis, exacerbating the fragility. As a result, the conflict has weakened an already fragile education system and driven more children out of school. This has increased children's exposure to violence, psychosocial stress, economic exploitation, armed group recruitment, and early marriage for girls as a family coping mechanism. Education intervention that is equitable, inclusive, and of high quality is urgently needed to turn years of underdevelopment, poverty, violence, and radicalization into self-empowerment and a brighter socioeconomic future for the region.To contribute to the strengthening of the education system, access to inclusive quality education services, and protect schools from attacks, GOALPrime, through this action will implement quality basic education and alternative learning support, renovation of classrooms and toilets, as well as provide capacity-building for SBMCs, and teachers on MHPSS, PSS, accelerated education programs, positiveparenting, safe school activities, child protection, and conflict-sensitive education services in the target LGAs of Borno State.

2 - Activities

-Community entry engagement with relevant education authorities and community stakeholders.-In consultation with community stakeholders, identify formal schools needing education support vis-à-vis learning/scholastic materials, teaching materials, learners’ desks, chairs, etc.-produce and provide learners and teaching materials (books, pens/ pencils, and erasers; whiteboards; packs of chalk/markers; and sporting equipment eg., balls, volleyball nets, skipping rope, etc) to support teachers and learners in target schools.-Monitor to ensure learning and teaching materials provided to teachers and learners are effectively utilized.-Carry out life skill sessions for 100 adolescent girls in schools with a focus on Menstrual Hygiene Management. 100 MHM Kit will be distributed to the 100 adolescent girls on completion of the life-skills sessions. The life-skills sessions will be supported by the trained CVTs.-Produce and distribute 100 MHM kits accessing life skill sessions.-Renovation of dilapidated Temporary Learning Spaces and Formal schools in target LGAs-Identify and profile 500 out-of-school children (OOSCs) in camps and host communities of target LGAs,-Enrol 500 out of school children (OOSCs) in formal schools in target LGAs-Carry-out back to school campaigns and enrollment drive campaigns in camps and communities of target LGAs.-Carry out positive parenting meetings for 100 parents/caregivers of out of school children (OOSCs).-Identify teachers without PSS trainings in consultation with both the LGA and State Education authorities.-Train teachers from schools targeted for Back-to-school campaigns on MHPSS, accelerated education programs, and conflict-sensitive education -including topics on GBV, SEA & CP.-Engage trained teachers & CVTs to carry out accelerated education programme for profiled OOSCs prior to transition to formal schools in collaboration with SAME, LEAs and SUBEB.-Produce and distribute 250 enrolment kits to out of school children (OOSC) on enrolment into formal schools in target LGAs.-Carry out routine dialogue sessions with learners to understand education needs in their respective schools.-Conduct alternative learning sessions for adolescents boys and girls needing alternative learning support identified in camps and host communities.-Train and continuously mentor ten Community Volunteer Teachers (CVTs) on effective delivery of alternative education in target LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By integrating every strategy with the protection principles, GOALPrime will mainstream protection (GBV & PSEA) throughout the executionof this project and advance meaningful access, safety, and dignity of both beneficiaries and of employers and volunteers. Safety, decency,and avoiding any kind of harm to both beneficiaries and employees are priorities for GOALPrime. Additionally, GOALPrime will also make sure that the target population has equitable access to the service, free from impediments and prejudice, in accordance with their needs.GOALPrime will pay attention to people and groups who could be especially vulnerable or have a hard time obtaining help and resources,including those with disabilities and GBV survivors who have been identified. GOALPrime will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation efforts to reduceor decrease GBV risk to beneficiaries.GOALPrime will work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will alignwith the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk incompliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle.Also, GOALPrime will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All staff,volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given PSEA orientation. They will befamiliarized with GOALPrime's policy on PSEA, and they MUST express their unwavering commitment by signing GOALPrime's ZeroTolerance to SEA Code of Conduct. Also, GOALPrime will also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately,they occur. GOALPrime will also ensure that the identity of the reporter(s) is kept very confidential. When convenient, GOALPrime willencourage that reports are made on anonymous grounds.Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GOALPrime will ensure a systematized engagement with affectedpopulations including confidential complaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency levels. Asrecommended by IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified inter-agency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-basedcomplaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA.GOALPrime will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexualfavors as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate complaints mechanisms, and support to set up in the community if SEAoccurs. Furthermore, GOALPrime will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective and that there is inter-agencycoordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is as broad and straightforward as possible for potentialcomplainants.GOALPrime will also be intentional about the environment and safety of the communities where we implement by ensuring that ourapproaches and tools does not contribute to the degradation of the environment. In addition, GOALPrime will also integrate climate changeand provide routine sensitization of the project beneficiaries on climate change prevention. GOALPrime shall mainstream CP, GBV & SEAinto all training contents as well as approaches

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will be consolidating its established community-based project accountability mechanism through this project to improve community stakeholders and project beneficiary participation both in the project decision-making and implementation. This project was designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and children based on lessons learnt from our previous implementation in thetarget LGAs.GPON uses a people-centred feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach includes, forming an LGAlevel project committee that will include all community groups' representative and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process. GPON has also made provision for anonymous feedbacks complaint/feedback boxes,SMSs and direct calls to the Compliance and Accountability team. GPON project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the established community-based project committee to discuss the projectdeliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. Also, the LGA project committee will work with the project team to define project beneficiaries’ vulnerability based on the context of the LGA, agree on vulnerability criteria andprovide a recommended approach on how the project can be implemented to better reach and serve the project targeted groups.Also, the project team will conduct quarterly review meetings with the community-based project committee at the LGA level to inform how each quarter can consolidate with the previous quarter's achievement to improve the quality of services rendered.
