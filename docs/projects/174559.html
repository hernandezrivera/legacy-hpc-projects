Project Summary

Assessments carried out by IOM reported that more than 542,158 people have been displaced in North West and South West regions as of October 2019. The crisis increasingly has a negative impact on livelihoods, trade, markets and access to land. The total population in Phase 3 (Crisis) or above is estimated to 2,7 million people and the proportion is much larger in the NW and SW regions than the rest of the country, respectively with 22% and 21% of the population estimated in acute food insecurity in the current situation and 40% and 31% of their population suffering from moderate or severe food insecurity (Cadre Harmonise, December 2020 and ENSAN, September 2020). The most reported negative coping mechanisms relate to economic and food security vulnerabilities equally applied (Protection Risk Assessment (PRA) conducted by IRC/WFP in NW, June 2020). Furthermore, 20% women-headed households in the NWSW are more prone to food insecurity than men-households and 17.5% of households in the NW are extremely economically vulnerable (EFSA, 2020). About 20% of households in the NWSW have used extreme negative coping strategies while 32.9% and 29.5% of households have resulted to emergency coping strategies, respectively in the NW and the SW (EFSA, 2020). Other risks faced by the IDPs and host community include threats and rights violations, including when accessing humanitarian assistance, when receiving assistance or after distributions of assistance, abduction, or attacks from NSAGs, theft, among others (PRA, June 2020). The multiple lockdowns and ghost towns imposed by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) threaten the security of the IDPs and compromise any opportunities for livelihoods. The risks to contamination to and impact of Covid-19 pandemic have been evaluated higher in urban and peri-urban areas than in rural areas, with inability for IDPs to access protection kits (face masks, etc.). The Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) project will target 7500 beneficiaries in the NW region, Mezam and Momo divisions, of whom 85% IDPs relocated from very insecure areas and 15% from host community, and 55% women versus 45% men, with priority to households with a presence of special needs persons (pregnant and nursing mothers and girls, people chronically ill, people with disabilities and the elderly) but also to households more exposed to protection risks (abuse and sexual exploitation, survival sex, women and children-headed households). Results from EFSA show that households with one or more special needs persons are more prone to food insecurity as their needs put an additional strain on household budgets in order to be able to cover increased healthcare expenditures and increased food expenditures necessary to meet their nutritional needs. The three priority sectors to be included in WFP’s MPC assistance will be food security, nutrition and protection. WFP will provide a monthly entitlement equivalent of US$ 15.49 per capita per month for six months. In addition to the 50% monthly food rations, the MPC value is made up of costs for nutritious food items using local market prices in the NW, equivalent to US$ 12.08$, and a top up plus US$ 3.41$ for Covid-19 protection kits (face masks, soap, etc.), for phone charging, transport to/from markets, health centres and cooking ernegy. Providing cash assistance for food security and nutrition can promote additional dignity as compared with in-kind food assistance and can reduce the risks to exploitative labour, early marriage, and sale of food assistance to get cash for other lifesaving needs. The protection component can enable them to pay for transportation, reducing time exposure moving to resort to negative coping strategies including survival sex, child recruitment an in dangerous areas. Protection principles will be mainstreamed (non-discrimination, participation, meaningful access, risk reduction, and safety and dignity).

1 - Besoins / Needs

(a) MPC Monthly transfers. 7,500 beneficiaries, pregnant and nursing women and girls and persons with special needs will receive monthly protection MPC and enabled to buy nutrition foods, a top up on 50% the General Food Assistance for nutritious food (USD 12.08), plus USD 3.41 protection package. Prior to each monthly transfers, beneficiaries and partners will be consulted and sensitized on opportune MPC transfers to ensure meaningful access and to mitigate any risks that might result from beneficiary movement to/from transaction points or marketplaces. In Covid-19 context, Social Behavior Change Communication will be mainstreamed to ensure the project is covid-19 sensitive, beneficiaries and stakeholders are aware of symptoms, contamination and spread modes, and abide with containment measures. WFP M&E team will support Cooperating Partners (CPs) monitoring to ensure beneficiaries can easily access cash entitlements, retailers have enough stocks and sell at market prices, beneficiaries can get appropriate support and are able to provide feedback through established Accountability to Affected Population mechanisms. (b) Protection mainstreaming. The project will ensure that activities are carried out in safe, dignified and reduced risk environment considering Do-No-Harm (DNH), inclusiveness of access for all person and all activities are gender, age and capabilities sensitive while ensuring participation and AAP. The project will apply the principle of protection centrality by identifying potential targeted beneficiaries at risks, why and how they are at risk with regards to specific vulnerabilities and risks of different beneficiaries’ categories. A protection risk assessment (PRA), protection baseline and DNH assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project and negative coping mechanisms identified to ensure that protection risks and mitigation measures are identified and protection is mainstreamed across project’s phases to reduce risks faced by targeted vulnerable populations. An action plan proposing mitigation measures will be developed to address risks and the barriers. Recommendations will be drawn on how cash interventions can reduce protection risks mitigation measures and ensure that the assistance can reduces direct safety concerns. Information, education and communication materials will be designed, contextualized and adapted to the findings of the assessments and end users for use and dissemination during awareness and sensitization campaigns, and posted at transaction and food distribution points. CPs will be trained on protection mainstreaming and action plan, and on the use of IEC materials. Regular protection monitoring as well as a mid-term and end-line protection risks and DNH assessments will be conducted. The overall objective will be to ensure that the project is protection-sensitive in the context of NWSW crisis, impact is maximized, risks are reduced and no harm caused to targeted beneficiaries. (c) Supporting and reinforcing AAP and feedback mechanisms. Partners will leverage on WFP’s existing accountability to AAP. CPs and community mobilizers will be trained on protection mainstreaming principles and APP mechanisms, including DNH, so that they operate community complaints systems and support persons with special needs at community level throughout the project cycle. Community complaints committees will be established at quarter level and managed by community mobilizers; helpdesks will be set up at respective nearest Food Distribution Point (FDP) and managed by CP staff, and WFP toll free line will be communicated to beneficiary affected populations can call to submit complaints or to provide feedback on the project implementation. A helpdesk will be operated on ah-hoc basis during MPC transfers to ensure maximum of beneficiaries can receive adequate support during payout pics. The operation of APP will strictly comply with data protection and privacy.
