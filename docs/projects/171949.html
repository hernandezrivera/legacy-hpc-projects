Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to enable the targeted categories of beneficiaries to cover their basic needs in food and non-food items, including shelter and utilities cost thus contributing to their access to decent living. This project will cover the basic needs of beneficiaries living mainly in the hard-to-reach frontline zones from among the most vulnerable categories. To ensure access of the targeted population groups to cash and markets, PIN will employ multiple options of financial service providers, including the home delivery of cash grants to the most vulnerable beneficiaries. This option is especially relevant to reduce the risk of contracting COVID-19 for older beneficiaries. In addition, the Ukrposhta and Oschadbank have mobile brigades that visit locations with no stationary departments of these FSP twice a month. In very exceptional cases, PIN will employ the option of targeted personal delivery of cash grant to beneficiaries. Throughout these actions, PIN will maintain a robust Community Feedback and Accountability Mechanism though its community-based approach and engagement with women, girls, boys, men, elderly and people with disabilities to raise their voices; enhanced consultations and testing of participation and social accountability mechanisms; feedback and complaints hotline, response, referrals and analysis for further adjustment of the assistance.

1 - Needs

The on-going conflict; high numbers of IDPs; many thousands caught in the conflict zone; significant disruption of basic services; a lack of means to cover essential costs have cultivated a significant humanitarian crisis. The continued correlation between food insecurity, vulnerability and socio-economic trends lead to increased levels of poverty. Most products are available on markets, so prices remain the largest barrier for vulnerable populations to access basic and winterization goods. In addition to the above factors, the sudden rapid spread of the Covid-19 pandemic only exacerbated the rather low standard of living of the population. As a result of ongoing and protracted conflict, the front-line hard-to-reach communities without basic services are the most exposed to the potential effects of the pandemic, have the insufficient ability and recourses to cope with crises situation. Protection risks related to shelling and mines, food insecurity, lack of communication means and social networks, damage to property and barriers to accessing basic services are more pronounced for populations living in rural areas within 5km of the LoC. 40% of elderly do not have access to basic services such as transports, healthcare and shops needed for dignity items and other reasons. Considering the fact that the majority of residents of the frontline zones still cannot independently cover their basic needs for food, hygiene items, medicine and heating, and resort to negative coping strategies, there is a need to implement the Multipurpose Cash assistance.2019 Humanitarian Need Overview of Ukraine (HNO) and MYHRP show that 30% of those directly affected by the conflict are women and men aged 60+. Due to the conflict’s lasting impact on structures of support services and healthcare, in addition to disrupting income sources, elderly women and men are disproportionately affected: Poverty amongst the elderly remains high (21.7%) due to the low monetary value of pension available to them (around 50 EUR per month). 9.12% of them reported having an official disability status, which means that they are often in need of personalised assistance (hygiene kits, adult diapers and assistive products). The situation can be worse for those without a recognised status for social benefit eligibility, but with impairments (due to non-recognised disability or chronical disease) and compounded vulnerabilities affecting their capacity to survive and live in dignity on a daily basis.(97.85%, at least 1 significant functional impairment, HAI, 2020). The selection of cash beneficiaries will build on the cumulative vulnerability approach (i.e. analysis of quantitative and qualitative vulnerability data, economic conditions and aggravating factors especially under the COVID-19 context) as recommended by the CWG. This will be done through thorough needs assessments, including first an updated localised context analysis to identify the most vulnerable (isolated and underserved) communities, then inclusive needs assessment. On this basis, precise criteria for eligibility to cash assistance will be designed. The objective will be to target the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men based on thorough analysis and assessment of plural, cumulative vulnerabilities beyond the usual social policy categorisations. The objective will also be to ensure that beneficiary engagement and selection does not cause harm, create additional threats, increase discrimination or add to people’s vulnerabilities. PIN will include nuances of gender, age and diversity to ensure proper targeting. For instance, care dependency whether official or not, will be integrated to ensure gender-sensitive criteria. To ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable due to disability or chronic disease or similar health issues, whether they are or not recognised as social benefit recipients, PIN will enhance the application of Washington Group approach.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Objective 1: Activity 1 Provision of timely MPC transfers The proposed intervention will support 2500 beneficiaries with multipurpose cash transfers to bridge the gap between income and minimum subsistence level for three months. This project will cover the basic needs of beneficiaries living mainly in the hard-to-reach frontline zones from among the most vulnerable categories. The proposed assistance will provide additional income to selected beneficiaries enabling them to afford basic commodities that they would otherwise lack, while at the same time freeing their personal incomes to be spent on other goods and services restricted by the MPC modality. PIN will identify communities at the initial stage of implementation in close coordination with communities, local authorities, social services and active humanitarian partners, using community-based approach.PIN will use different approaches to deliver cash transfers. For people with disabilities or limited mobility this assistance will by provided by address with aid of Ukrposhta`s staff. The MPC delivery will be based on 3 rounds, which will follow the structure below:1) January – March (1000 beneficiaries) covering winter preparedness and as an assistance to mitigate the impact of the winter.2) April – June (1000 beneficiaries) covering the recovery period during spring, as a response to increased prices on the market as well as exhausted agricultural resources after winter;3) July – September (500 beneficiaries) as a general vulnerability response during summer and especially supporting self-sufficient measures on food security; Monitoring and evaluation: Using the Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) methodology PIN will conduct the design, analysis and implementation of PDM to track beneficiary use of MPC as well as beneficiary satisfaction with assessment, selection and distribution processes and generally with MPC as an aid modality. The M&E teams of PIN will ensure the applicability, accuracy and efficiency of the activities implemented over the course of the project and in the post intervention/post distribution period. The following M&E processes are sought:Beneficiary Verification: Beneficiaries will be cross-referenced with other humanitarian actors to avoid any risk of overlap. PIN will verify most vulnerable beneficiaries with community members, with due respect of confidentiality and protection mainstreaming. Monitoring: Regular monitoring activities to be conducted by both management and M&E staff with random beneficiary interviews. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), baselines and endline CSI: M&E Units of PIN will carry out PDM surveys reviewing beneficiary experiences on the whole aid process.Accountability and Complaints Mechanism: This includes clear communication of criteria to target the most vulnerable, as well as protection mainstreaming and protection risk mitigation. Pre-distribution information sessions with beneficiaries will be organized in particular on how to use the dedicated hotline and e-mail address that have been established by PIN. A complaints database will be maintained, where all complaints will be categorized on the basis of urgency, relevance and sensitivity/seriousness. PIN will enable women, men, girls and boys as well as elderly personsand diverse person (IDP, host, disabled etc) to play an active role in identifying specific needs, in decision-making processes over the best response to provide to such needs, and in expressing their views, concerns, feedback, opinions and perception in terms of safety, protection, dignity, access and participation throughout the programme implementation. Based on needs, active members and local groups of communities will support PIN in coordination with local administration to support beneficiaries, participate and support project design on the ground, and facilitate assessment and the implementation/distribution and monitoring processes.
