1 - Identified Needs

Three years into the influx, the total population of FDMNs and HCs in need of assistance is projected to be more than 1.3 million (Health Sector PIN-JRP2021). The sudden influx had negative effects on the host communities in the form of increased cost of living, loss of employment opportunity, exploitation of local natural resources. This situation created a degree of tension among the host community, the aid agencies and the refugee population. Thus, a need for improving similar services in the host communities is acknowledged and incorporated in to the JRP. The extremely congested living condition has also worsened the hygiene conditions in the camp. The seasonal monsoon brings kiosks to both communities in the form of outbreaks and water-borne diseases. Limited availability of Health care/Nutrition seems to affect women and children including pregnant and lactating mothers in both communities. Especially, general knowledge and services related to sexual reproductive health need to be addressed. As the preparation of JRP 2021 for Rohingya Humanitarian Crisis in underway, the needs for health assistance are still very high. According to the JRP, while improving access to the medical facilities, adolescent health would be set as a priority. It further calls for strengthening sexual and reproductive health, maternal and new-born health care and access to safe, voluntary family planning. Pregnant mothers chose the comfort of home deliver partly due to poor physical access to a maternal & child health services, lack of female delivery staff and cultural/ customs differences. Treatment for non-communicable disease (NCD), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services and specialized services such as eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare etc. along with community outreach are in need of further improvement. There is also a call for strengthening host community facilities.PWJ acknowledges the importance of local-knowledge plays in a successful project implementation and thus it partnered with successful local organizations such as Dhaka Community Hospital Trust and Community Initiative Society. As per the Core Humanitarian Standard commitments 3, 4, 5 and 6, all of which promotes partnership and proper communication with the beneficiaries and other players in the field, PWJ/DCHT team coordinates and closely collaborate its activities in the field with local authorities as well as the health sector. During their daily interaction in the field, our field staff gather all kinds of information and opinions from the beneficiaries and incorporate them into project implementation, where and when it is feasible; for example, as basic as how to engage local communities. A suggestions and complaints mechanism through service evaluation box is in practice. PWJ and DCHT always make sure the participation of beneficiaries and their voices are used as the base for our need assessments and surveys. Target for such research/ study is usually identified by consulting and cooperating with the local government and camp authorities. The survey results are then shared with other partners through the health sector coordination group. Plus PWJ/DCHT team keeps a constant communication with the Maji’s/block leaders and CiC to address any shortcoming and complaints from the community. Our team also visits the local government community centers at the host communities, regularly, to share and seek information related to maternal and child care.As Health sector identify the need for SRH and specialized services such as treatment of NCD their gap analysis also shows a shortage of PHC in the camp In line with JRP 2020, our project will provide the much-needed basic medical services with Maternal and child health, special care for NCD and create awareness of health and hygiene through an all-inclusive approach where not only the FDMNs but also the host communities will be beneficiaries.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1. Provide essential health care services 7 days a week, covering young children, adolescent boys and girls, adults and elderly. Regular consultation service and care for NCD patients with proper medication. Activity 2. Provide a comprehensive Family Planning awareness and services to male and female of reproductive age with provision of contraceptive methods according to each beneficiary’s choice.Activity 3. Awareness activities on maternal and child health in refugee camps and 2 host communities, including the benefits of facility delivery, importance of the number of ANC and PNC visits. Beneficiaries of female/ male and pregnant women & their husbands are targeted separately. In addition, at the clinic, pregnant women and infants will be vaccinated according to the guidelines of the Ministry of Health of Bangladesh.Activity 4. Implement health and hygiene awareness and hygiene activities at refugee camps and host communities with the help of CHW, targeting the young male/female, children at schools (HC)/learning centers (Camps) and mother.Activity 5. Organising or conducting relevant training/workshop for field staff to improve capacity. Medical specialists (doctors, nurses, midwives, etc.) will be dispatched to field to provide training on medical knowledge and skills that are needed locally. Staff, especially for CHWs and TBAs, would be send to various training program to develop skills. The training target will be selected from the staff according to the training content.Activity 6. Data collection / analysis / monitoring: Daily record taking of each beneficiaries’ data related to diagnosis, prescription and analysing it on a weekly and monthly basis. The final report is shared with the health sector and GOB as required/necessary.
