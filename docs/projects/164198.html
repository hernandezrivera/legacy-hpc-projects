Project Summary

The projects intends to provide a multi-faceted response in an integrated and coherent way. Firstly, by stimulating local production through provision of agricultural inputs, targeted farmers will be able to restart and/or increase production, increase their income and improve the availability in the market. Secondly, by supporting livestock herders with inputs they will be able to maintain their current asset base while increasing income. This will also support availability of local dairy products and animal based proteins in the market. Thirdly supporting creating short term income and labour opportunities with fixed-term wages. Finally rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure to protect and maintain community productive assets (e.g: irrigation channels, mills, bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads and silos). Shafak's proposal is designed to supporting farmers and livestock herders needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and HCs. The project comprises the following components:
- Providing agriculture inputs (winter crops, summer crops, , olive value chain, pistachio value chain and forage crops).
- Purchasing wheat.
- Providing fodder, vaccines and treatment.
- Rehabilitating value chain related infrastructure.
- Providing short-term employment opportunities through Cash-for-Work activities.
ER1: 13,500 Targeted households are able to resume agricultural activities, increase household income and improve consumption through: i) provision of agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; olive harvesting and pruning and ii) improving/restoring agricultural infrastructure (for example irrigation / drainage), IV) Cash for harvest, V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat.
ER2: 7,000 Targeted households are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through: i) provision of livestock inputs (500 kg of fodder per beneficiary 3,000 beneficiaries) ; ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (4 campaigns, 200,000 head per campaign owned by 4,000 beneficiaries)
ER3: 500 Targeted households stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community related value chain infrastructure , disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest and vol control and olive trees harvesting .
ER4: Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Mills, bakeries, storage, fodder mixers, feeder roads and silos.

4 - Needs

Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Food security exists when all people at all times have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food to meet their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life.” Some 70 per cent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 per cent of their expenditure. More than 37 per cent of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 per cent of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Almost a decade into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 90 per cent of an unskilled laborer’s average monthly salary and between 50 and 80 percent of a public service employee’s average monthly salary. Wheat production estimated to be at 2.2 million tons remains lower than the pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tons (2002-2011) and that of the domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tons. About 216,000 hactares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees, vegetables and grazing areas (17 percent of national areas planted) were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, hailstorms, high temperatures and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust. In Northwest Syria, vaccination of 2.4 million smallholder chickens against Newcastle Disease and of smallholder almost 18,000 dairy cattle against Lumpy Skin Disease has prevented a resurgence of these two devastating livestock epidemics in 2019.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community identification; beneficiary selection and verification; market assessment; provision of agricultural inputs and improvement of agricultural irrigation and extension services; provision of livestock inputs and support to extension services; improvement of community infrastructure through the implementation of Cash-for-Work activities. Cash-for-Work activities enabling the provision of temporary labour opportunities focusing on the rehabilitation of related infrastructure and community identified projects; asset protection; provision of inputs to small businesses; provide extension sessions and demonstration fields visits; regular monitoring; identification and prioritization of community needs; sharing of information, promotion of joint analysis of vulnerable population needs and gaps, efficacy of livelihoods programming including advocacy initiatives to respond to needs and gaps by other humanitarian actors.
