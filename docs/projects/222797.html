Project Summary

Gender based violence incidences in South Sudan is very high and can be attributed to the prolonged period of violence in the country that occurred in 2013 and 2016. The country still experiences sporadic fighting intercommunal. This left many women and girls seriously traumatized. In the country a study conducted by UNFPA shows that South Sudan has the highest number of gender based violence with 41% of the women having experienced violence at least once in live time and the study shows raising cases of intimate partner violence currently standing at 49%. (UNFPA 2020).
Cases of early and forced marriage continue to be extreamly high in the country and the study further shows that that Eastern Equatoria (39.1%), Jonglei (39.1%) and Central Equatoria (38.6%) report the highest rates of physical violence in the country. Similarly, the study documented a high prevalence of child marriage (34.6%, nationally) with Lakes and Jonglei states accounting for the high rates of child marriage in the country - 60.7% and 40.9%, respectively.
Women and girls in the country lack safe spaces due to the limited number of women and girls friendly spaces where they can get trauma healing services and other live skills that reduces their vulnerability to violence. Referral pathways for women and girls to have access to services are limited and not well defined and thus increasing the vulnerability to violence.
All these protection concerns calls for efforts to reduce the cases of GBV in the communities and also raise awareness in the communities on the need to reduce this vice with male involvement in reducing toxic masculinity and improve positive masculinity having male champions for the reduction of gender based violence.
Women Agency for Resilience and Transformation (WART) will improve the protection situation in the country through provision of psychosocial support to the victims of GBV. The organization will have Psychosocial support desks to ensure that women and girls who need support are given the support in good time.
The organization will build and equip disability inclusive women and girls friendly spaces that will support victims of gender based violence and also provide them with skills needed to improve their livelihood. There will be awareness rising on the importance of reducing GBV that will be done in the community.
The organization will establish referral pathways for victims of GBV to ensure there is immediate access to legal aid, medical services and where possible other key services like psychosocial support and activities to build resilience and trauma recovery.
WART will also focus on activities that greatly reduce exposure to GBV like making of fuel efficient stoves to ensure that women and girls frequency of going to look for firewood and other energy sources is greatly reduced thus reducing the exposure time to risks associated with gender based violence.
WART will conduct trainings to the case workers and other actors on GBV mainstreaming in health, WASH and other programmes so that safety audits can be conducted in construction of key social amenities in the communities. Distribution of dignity kits to women and girls will be done to also support them and empower them on efforts to reduce GBV.
This project will be implemented in Aweil East, Aweil North, Rubkona, Bor South, Ayod and Duk Counties Sudan targeting a total of 2500 (500 girls, 500 boys 1000 women and 500 men).
WART will submit 5W reports on monthly basis to the cluster and will also submit financial status through the financial tracking system.

1. Needs

The conflict in South Sudan exposed many to the risks of gender-based violence. Cases of gender-based violence in the country are on the increase and according to a study by UNFPA, south Sudan ranks second on cases of gender based violence in East Africa with over 63% of the population reporting to have experienced some form of violence.The country is currently experiencing influx of returnees from Sudan with current data showing that more than 700000 people have crossed over to South Sudan in search of safety. The displaced populations are in dire need of protection services as majority of them are women and girls. They also lack safe spaces due to congested displaced persons camps.The country is also experiencing severe flooding that has affected over 1.4 million and displaced many more in over 22 counties. According to flood snapshot Nov 2024, Aweil east has over 91000 people who have also been affected by the current flooding. This comes with increased vulnerabilities to protection threats especially to women and girls.According to a coordinated IRNA conducted in Ayod County , there is evidence of sexual assault, gender based violence, The assesment shows that reported cases of sexual assault on girls and children. Distances to the water points are long and the water points are not protected thus exposing women and girls to protection threats. The IRNA also showed cases of forced marriage that make young girls drop out of school. (Ayod IRNA Sep 2024)Similar findings were reported in another IRNA conducted in Duk and Aweil North Counties where WART actively participated. Lack of dignity Kit and clear referral pathways for victims of gender based violence is a major concern to the communities and continue to expose young girls, children and women to serious gender based violence threats. Intimate partner violence is also a great concern in families where women and children are exposed to violence by partners and their guardians.The IPC report from NOV 2024 shows high levels of food insecurity. This exposes women and girls to the risk of sexual exploitation in in exchange of food during food distribution and other key humanitarian assistance. WART will address this needs by provision of integrated GBV services to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations have access to GBV prevention activities and have access to referral pathways where they can seek support when exposed to gender based violence.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of survivor-centered psycho social support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery for victims of gender-based violence.2. Distribution of multipurpose cash vouchers to victims of gender-based violence.3. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support including mental health services and Psychosocial support.4. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support that including legal aid and case management of rape. 5. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment 6. Conduct dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation 7. Conduct community awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle 8. Conducting safety audits in for construction of sanitation facilities and other facilities to minimize gender-based violence. 9. Training humanitarian workers on GBV prevention and mainstreaming protection in their programming. 10. Establishment of referral pathways to legal and health services and also supporting victims with psycho social support.
