Project Summary

It is estimated that there are 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. Successive years of drought exacerbated by a prolonged conflict has resulted in an enduring humanitarian crisis that demands urgent life-saving interventions. The protracted nature of this crisis requires a strategy that looks beyond the severe immediate needs of the displaced population, and instead focusses on a long-term strategy; incorporating durable solutions into the immediate response. With this project IOM will ensure the provision of camp management and camp coordination services to men, women, boys and girls in displacement sites in the districts of Baidoa, Kismayo and Doolow, ensuring a focus on those most vulnerable and inclusivity, and that the most urgent needs of the Internal Displaced Persons (IDPs) are met, their voices heard, setting the foundation for the achievement of durable solutions.
Latest estimations of the displacement context in Somalia indicate that around 2.3 million people have settled in over 2,400 IDP sites (CCCM Cluster HRP 2022 Guidance note). Most of these sites are small, informal, and scattered settlements in urban or peri-urban areas. The majority of them are positioned on private land meaning that IDPs are at constant risk of eviction, which further exacerbates their already vulnerable situation, that results in stress and fear at the very real risk of further displacement.
Baidoa hosts the largest number of IDPs after Mogadishu with more than 430,991 displaced persons living in 548 sites (CCCM site verification exercise April 2021), a 10% rise on figures in 2020. In 2019 government authorities allocated public land for IDPs which has enabled IOM and other humanitarian/development partners to relocate 2,009 vulnerable families at risk of eviction. These families have now received legal land tenure documentation, are no longer seen as IDPs, and their area no longer and IDP, but a village north of the city. This is an example of linking traditional CCCM activities with an approach tailored towards the achievement of durable solutions. As more public land is becomes available, IOM plans to coordinate with local authorities and other actors to support further families to relocate.
In Kismayo, 66,051 IDPs are hosted in 146 sites (CCCM site verification exercise May 2021). The vast majority of these sites are on privately owned land, for which IDPs have to pay a fee in order to live there. Nonetheless families live in constant fear as rental agreements either don’t exist or are verbal, meaning that evictions are commonplace with families forced to move at short notice. Most of these IDP sites are overcrowded and lack basic infrastructure; living conditions are poor with overstretched services and shelters often made of locally available materials such as plastic sheets and worn-out clothes.
Doolow is currently hosting around 67,271 IDPs (CCCM site verification exercise May 2021) in two, large formal IDP sites located on public land. These two sites, named Kabasa and Qansaxley are internally divided into 112 separate blocks. The high population density in these sites puts pressure on the provision of adequate services, in particular shelter needs and the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) system. The subsequent concentration of service provision makes service monitoring and coordination key aspects of CCCM related needs. In addition, community mobilization and the development of representative governance structures are continually needed to order to facilitate strong dialogue between service providers, affected populations, and local authorities, as well as to enhance accountability towards the affected population.

1. - Projects activities

IOM will conduct site verifications on a quarterly basis, map the services available in sites, and monitor service delivery in sites quality. IOM will conduct regular site safety audits to identify areas of high protection reported risk and implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites for all population groups. The living conditions of displaced people will be improved by supporting community-led site maintenance assessments, committees, and activities to ensure the upkeep and safety of sites’ the physical living environment In coordination with the local authorities, and protection actors, and HLP actors, IOM will identify and develop new land for the relocation of displaced people and in order to facilitate decongestion activities, thus ensuring minimum living standards for IDPs. Community self-management and access to information for displaced populations will be strengthened by supporting the establishment (where not already in existence) and strengthening of diverse and inclusive governance structures. IOM will provide capacity building to existing governance structures on CCCM and related principles to Camp Management Committees, local authorities, and humanitarian partners. Safe and furnished community spaces for Camp Management committees to host meetings, trainings, events etc will be provided and IOM will operate a timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms - accessible at help desks in community centers through outreach teams or via a toll-free hotline.
