Project Summary

Children in Somalia have grown up under the stress of conflict, displacement, family separation and fear, compounded by a lack of access to basic services, including education, and food insecurity. The effect of protracted violence and conflict has also eroded social structures in Somali society and will continue to have a profound impact both on the psycho-social health of individuals, and on long-term prospects for peace, development and social justice for the whole society. BTSC believes and highly prioritizes to ensure Gender Inclusion as a key asset to ensure provision of equal educational opportunities to the girls and boys living in Burhakaba regardless of race, sex, and/or ethnicity. Education and Child Protection are deeply interconnected. Education is a pivotal right for the development of girls and boys and child protection is a condition to guarantee their well-being. Education requires an appropriate and inclusive environment to protect all children and youth regardless of race, sex, and/or ethnicity. The provision of quality and relevant education that promotes child protection means empowering children and youth to build their own holistic life plans – grounded in their culture, their interests, and their needs – as a way to concretely pursue their goals and reach their potential.
