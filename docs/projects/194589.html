Project Summary

This response plan aims to increase access of essential emergency lifesaving health care services including maternal, child, adolescent health provision through mobile and static health facilities (i.e. PHCC &PHCU) to vulnerable populations of Akobo & Twic County of Jonglei and Warrap State. Approximately, 29,747 (Men-8728 Women-9629, Boys- 5472 and Girls -5918) vulnerable hosts, IDPs and Returnees in Akobo & Twic County are targeted with a budget of 800,000US$ translating to 26.9$ as CPB. The focus will be on provision of basic essential health services as per the national standards centered on the context epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. This will ensure prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable population such as women, children as well as people with special needs. The response aligns with Health Cluster Strategic objectives as follows; SO1: Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to meet their basic needs. SO2: Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. SO3: Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions to displacement with respect to their rights. Specifically, the response will deploy, 3 mobile health clinics as follows: -2 Mobile Clinic in Akobo East and West while 1 in Twic East and 4 Static health facilities (2 PHCC and 2 PHCU both in Akobo East, West & Twic East). This will witness reduced excess morbidity and mortality by ensuring timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations while enhancing resilience and promotion of humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
The response will engage the communities and existing development health programs along with the CHD in the planning and organization of emergency health services to be entrenched in the cluster coordination mechanism both at national and sub national level to enable other partners and authorities support in the implementation, monitoring and review the response activities.
The response will also deploy qualified health workers to improve quality health care services through capacity building of CHDs and health workers, Procurement of medical supplies, use of EWARS and weekly IDSR system, use of the HMIS & other internally improvised tools to ensure the quality & consistency of data while obtaining beneficiaries. The implementation of this response will ensure that Complaints Feedback/Response Mechanism desks are set up to enable project beneficiaries provide feedback. As such lockable CRFM box will be installed in strategic places such as CIDO offices, 4 static
health facility’s and 3 mobile clinics within Akobo East & West to facilitate
Communication and Community Engagement as adequate information sharing and communication is enhanced through individual house-house interviews, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), FGDs, Community consultation meetings, engagement of civil groups, local authorities and other partners active on the ground. The feedback from the CFMs will help to improve programming and ensure positive adjustment on health service delivery. Therefore, consideration of this response will optimize CIDO’s 8 years’ experience
to provide humanitarian services.

1 - Needs

Akobo and Twic counties continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions largely due to localized conflicts, flooding and severe acute food insecurity. In Akobo, acute malnutrition persists (GAM rate of 25.24 % much far away from WHO threshold of 15%GAM, (FSNMS round 26)) while in Twic, the influx of IDPs due to violence further constrained access to lifesaving services. In both counties, access and quality of health are poor and critical protection concerns coupled with WASH needs for the most vulnerable children, women, and PWD remain a subject while climatic shocks that results in dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding as experienced during the year 2020-2021, severely impacting on people’s livelihoods, hygiene & health services, water, sanitation as protection concerns recorded high with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. The worst, health system and infrastructure within are among the poorest in the world. Poorly equipped health facilities, staffed with a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. Increasing reliance on inadequately trained/ low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare which also takes them approximately a day to access services (HNO2022SSD). CIDO facility mapping exercise revealed that in the 2 counties 60% of health units are non-functional, and as this prevail, the referral system within the county's is compromised forcing affected populations to walk for an average of 2-6 hours for the case of Akobo and a day for Twic in search of health care services while EPI coverage hampered as affirmed by UNICEF report that Penta 3 coverage remains at 49% leading to spontaneous disease outbreaks increasing the vulnerability of children under 5 years across the country whereas Incidences of malaria, ARI and AWD among other preventable diseases within the response catchment areas are still prevalent contributing to the highest morbidity in country (IDSR bulletin WK8 2022). Lack of access to safe clean drinking water as observed, contributes to poor health conditions with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrheal diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E with water vector-borne diseases such as malaria with likelihood to increase. Access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate as notes WHO citing an approximate of 2.5 million cases, emphasizing the risk of mental health burden in the affected population. Attacks on health care continue to negatively impact the provision of and access to health services. Likewise, findings from the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 indicate that despite mental health and psychosocial issues reportedly being common across the locations and population groups (host communities, IDPs, and returnees), access to services is limited. When services were mentioned to exist, they were mentioned to primarily include community support mechanisms (mentioned in interviews with host communities and IDPs). In a few interviews with returnees, use of traditional healers was mentioned as a coping mechanism in places with limited access to mental health and psychosocial services (HNO 2023). The 2022 – 2023 health cluster target indicates that 146,831 men, women, boys & girls from Host, IDPs and Returnees of Akobo County alone are in dire need of health services while 124, 382 are PIN for Twic County, as such, this response therefore, targets 28% & 27% of Health cluster target in Akobo and Twic county respectively which includes 8728 Men, 9629 Women, 5472 Boys and 5918 Girls amongst which 400 are people with disability. Approval of this appeal will greatly complement on the existing health service delivery within response location hence enhancing reduction on the number of people in need of humanitarian as well as morbidity and mortality by scaling up health care services to the vulnerable populations in bid to save life.

2 - Project activities

The following activities will be undertaken through the response period: - Conduct Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities Ensure community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM Conduct IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities Carry out Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (2 PHCC and 2PHCU) and 3 mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected populationConducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

An integral part of this project delivery will be participatory Learning and Continuous Improvement. CIDO will organize regular program performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries and partners to assess progress towards objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback, identified risks and risk management strategies during implementation. At the end of the project CIDO will carry out a project evaluation and document the process used to achieve project outputs and outcomes. The report will also be shared with the beneficiaries

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CIDO has a functional policy on PSEA. All staffs will be obligated to sign against the policy in respect to the 6 core principles of PSEA. The respective project staff leading this project will be guided by the standard operating Procedures (SOP) of PSEA and where applicable will use the community based complaint Mechanism to address cases of SEA. CIDO will ensure that PSEA IEC materials are used and are visible in all designated areas of service delivery. In collaboration with other partners and recognition that CIDO is an active protection partner, efforts will be consolidated to conduct capacity building of front line workers on PSEA and extend applicability of SOP to other NGOs at grassroots level

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO’s policies and Culture Prompts Senior management to organize proper orientation sessions to all employees on PSEA issues. As such, before engagement with the beneficiaries the team is well informed on the PSEA consequences and code of conduct is signed by each staff as part of the contractual obligation. The staff is also obligated to organize awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, women leaders, community leaders, law enforcement agents and every stakeholder in the counties on what this project considers as sexual exploitation, channels of reporting and the available resources within the organization.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CIDO will put necessary measures to ensure that project beneficiaries are protected not only from external threats that impedes them from service acquisition but also from data mismanagement. This will involve ensuring that the available data does not trickle in the wrong audience by keeping confidential information well and educating health workers on importance of confidentiality. Referral pathways will be created for survivors of SGBV and CIDO will ensure that they access health services without impediments in to the health facilities. To ensure that beneficiaries access feed back and complaint mechanisms, CIDO will use existing structures such as health committees to address accountability issues. Suggestion boxes will be placed in the health facilities to ensure that the beneficiaries can easily access feedback and complaint mechanisms. This will also include having a dedicated line for receiving feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries. CIDO will work hand in hand with protection partners to conduct safety audit at every health facility to determine protection issues that might jeopardies service delivery and provide recommendation on addressing identified risks. In the initial stages of response, CIDO will engage all stakeholders in consultation meetings which will enable joint mapping of the locations and identification of gaps, this will also help to trim expectations of various stakeholders, identify security hotspot areas alongside land issues that may have a bearing on the response, build consensus and buy in of the project by the community while addressing unpredicted risks and threats to the response. The project staffs will also be taken through PSEA policies and obligated to sign code of conduct
