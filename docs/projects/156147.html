Needs

Since the beginning of the crisis in March 2011, conflict has caused 5.5 million Syrians to flee to neighbouring countries and further abroad. Within Syria, 6.1 million people, of whom almost 2 million are school-aged children, have been internally displaced. The volatile security and access situation within the country impacts children and their ability to access education. Education, as well as other social services, have been limited by the destruction of infrastructure, displacement, injuries, loss of life and resulting distress. Furthermore, the ability to provide adequate response is hindered by the limited information on humanitarian needs and vulnerability in the education sector. The Education Need Assessment conducted in 2018 shows that 8% of children aged 6-11 and 25% of children aged 12-17 did not attend school in government held areas sub-districts in Syria mostly due to the need to work or help family, lack of learning materials, and poor quality of teaching. The most urgent educational needs in sub-districts were ensuring safety and security for children and teachers, additional classrooms, and provision of teaching supplies and kits.

Implementation of activities

COOPI conducted assessment in Aleppo and East Ghouta where COOPI has Protection and FSL activities. Taking advantage on COOPI team presence in the area assessment for CP, Education have been conducted. COOPI plans to conduct an integrated intervention including:• Outreach to out of school children• Back to learning campaign• Facilitation of Self Learning Programme (training of facilitators and delivery of the sessions)• PSS activities• Recreational activities• Assessment and referral of children to the formal education pathwayCOOPI has a valid registration with SARC, which supports with the procedures for granting access to the project areas and the activities in the field. SARC will liaise with Ministry of Education who will support the identification of a suitable space to be used as a static facility for the delivery of basic education services in the project areas. Self-Learning facilitators and recreational activators will be recruited to conduct the self-learning and recreational activities, in cooperation with the field team also for outreach activities (especially awareness back to learning campaigns). They will support with the identification of beneficiaries in need, they will provide psychological first aid and referrals to identify child protection cases. Child Protection case workers will be able to use the static facility to follow the cases, provide counselling, and support families at risk of adopting negative coping strategies.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk 1: Unintentional injury in disaster zones, risks can include damaged infrastructure (roofs and walls collapsing) in the building where activities are delivered.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measure: 1. Liaise with the community and with SARC to ensure the space is located in a suitable place and it is compliant with the minimum standards of safety.2. Consider mobility patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing a static facility or a mobile service provision or combine both; 3. Conduct safety audit to ensure safe access (route and location) by children.4. Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.5. Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered).6. Select staff for all activities (especially outreach volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile7. Consider the provision of transport costs for the beneficiaries who have difficulties in reaching the education space. Protection risk 2: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and servicesLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation Measures:1. Establish a specific complaint and feedback mechanism.2. Thorough background check to be conducted for staff conducting activities with children including check of criminal record.3. Provide training to staff on Code of Conduct and ensure newly recruited staff receives and understands the Code of Conducts and minimum standards for Protection. Protection Risk 3: Social Workers might encounter survivors of gender-based violence or people needed specific psychosocial support, who might require special medical attention and could face consequences for having reached out to help and face stigma.Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigation measure: 1. Refer survivors and children with serious mental illness to relevant actors operating in the area.2. Ensure the space can provide comfort and confidentiality, in the spirit of the ‘Do not Harm’ principle. Protection Risk 5: The referrals might not be successful as other humanitarian actors might not have the capacity to assist additional beneficiaries, which would prevent the beneficiaries from accessing the needed support. Likelihood: Low. Impact: HighMitigation Measure: Constantly liaise with other humanitarian actors in the areas through the Protection sector, to ensure coordination on referral pathways.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

COOPI through daily coordination with SARC insured a direct access to the project's location. COOPI secured authorisations from the Syrian stakeholders to send daily its staff and volunteers. COOPI also monitors the situation through a coordination mechanisms with other INGOs, UNDSS as well as ICRC and Clusters.
