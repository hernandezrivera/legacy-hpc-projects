Project Summary

NRC will focus on provision of alternative education opportunities to students ages 6 to 24. This will include both literacy and numeracy and vocational education classes. This will include provision of training for teachers on effective pedagogies, classroom and NRC’s Better Learning Program, which prepares teachers to provide psychosocial support to students and assist them in developing strategies for improved resilience. Training for teachers and other school staff will also include child safebguardingand PSEA to enable school staff to create a protective environment and this training will be followed by mentoring to ensure that skills learned are applied in the classroom and school to benefit students. NRC will work with SUBEB and SAME to identify government schools which can host alternative education classes and will also construct temporary learning centers where existing spaces are too from beneficiaries and these will be provided with full WASH facilities and enhanced hygiene to reduce the risk of COVID-19.The project will distribute teaching and learning kits to ensure that both teachers and children are prepared with the materials they need for effective learning and adolescent girls will receive dignity kits to ensure that they are comfortable attending classes when menstruating. School based management committees will be established and trained to mobilize communities to support education and interact with parents on school attendance and participation as well as assist in monitoring the quality of education in the school. After school recreation activities will provide learners with additional opportunities to learn, develop supportive social relationships with peers and build a cohesive school community. NRC will extend the benefits of these supportive activities through the development of connections between alternative education facilities and nearby government schools. This will include school visits for non formal learners and their parents so that they are familiar with the location and environment of the school and will feel comfortable transitioning when the time comes. It will also include discussions between school based management committees from non formal learning classes and those at government focus schools to identify strategies to facilitate the transition of learners currently attending alternative education classes. These practical activities will be complemented by discussions with education authorities at LGA and state level to ensure that there are mechanisms to support transition and that schools and government education administrations are+−Leaflet prepared for non formal education students transitioning to formal education. As part of its commitment strengthening existing government education activities NRC will carry out rehabilitation and equipment projects in government schools to ensure that they offer conducive learning environments for current and formal students. To ensure that the school’s physical project will carry out rehabilitation or construction and equipment of classrooms and other school facilities based on a structural needs assessment and concerns identified by the school administration, teachers, school based management councils and the students themselves, and this process will be supported byNRC’s shelter department. The structural needs assessment will pay particular attention to the availability of gender appropriate WASH facilities so that all students have access to required hygiene facilities and that girls feel comfortable and safe accessing latrines, a process which will be supported by NRC’s WASH department. Additionally, adolescent girls will receive dignity kits to ensure that they are comfortable attending school during their periods.

1 - Needs

While primary education is free and compulsory in Nigeria, only 53% of primary age children in the northeast of Nigeria are enrolled, with the attendance rate in camps falling to 25%. The enrolment rate for girls is particularly low, with only 47% of girls ages 6-12enrolled in formal education as the result of cultural barriers and protection concerns. IDP and returnee families face significant challenges to participation in formal education as the result of multiple displacements and the impact of COVID-19 on already fragile and uncertain sources of family income. Surveys indicate that families consider education associated costs such as school materials and uniforms a critical barrier to education participation. Government schools are struggling to provide an environment which is safe for children and conducive for learning. Teachers are often unqualified or poorly qualified and unable to cope with the challenges of teaching large classes of students of different ages and abilities. The lack of teaching and learning materials and textbooks compounds already challenging teaching and learning conditions. Further, many IDP and returnee children have experienced significant stress, trauma and loss as the result of displacement, vulnerability and difficult economic conditions and teachers are not well prepared to provide the support needed. Many schools lack furniture, and classrooms are often bleak, unwelcoming and unsafe. Schools have limited or poorly maintained WASH facilities and cannot provide the facilities for basic hygienic requirements for students.These conditions can discourage school enrolment and attendance and may leave struggling learners behind and at increased risk of dropping out. For children who have dropped out or who have never attended school there are NGO provided emergency or alternative learning opportunities. However, the lack of effective mechanisms to help learners participating in those opportunities transition to formal learning and negative community perceptions of formal education means that few learners progress beyond those opportunities. Additionally, the lack of functional connections between alternative learning opportunities and government schools means that school are poorly prepared to welcome alternative education graduates. The COVID-19 pandemic has had a disastrous effect on access to education. Schools closed in March and while the national government offered free access to online learning platforms, the lack of devices andinternet access was a major barrier to the use of e-learning, and few children have been accessed radio learning opportunities. The national Ministry of Education has identified safe back to school requirements, and non formal education activities provided by NRC and other INGOs have been adapted to accommodate COVID prevention practices, but theEducation in Emergencies Working Group reports that many government schools are unable to meet requirements and will need considerable assistance. For midterm, it is possible that the economic impact of COVID-19 on already vulnerable families may lead to a reduction in the number of children who are able to participate in formal or non formal classes because of reduced family circumstances. Borno State government has stated its intentions to remove IDPs and returnees currently living in formal or non formal camps to resettlement areas on the fringes of cities or to their home communities during 2021. The timeline for this proposed resettlement or return is unclear, but large scale movement of the IDPs and returnee population would require significant adaptation of program activities which assume a generally stable population in a generally stable location.Further if return areas identified are in insecure locations, provision of education services to the returnee population would require a shift in the modality of providing education.

2 - Activities

Education activities will include non formal literacy and numeracy education for out of school children ages from 6 to 14 with a focus on literacy, numeracy instruction as well as psychosocial support through the first level of NRC’s Better Learning Program with the potential to adapt and expand in 2022. Recreational activities will be scaled and adapted to allow for social distancing and reduce the risk of COVID-19 prevention. As possible, activities will be hosted in existing temporary learning spaces or formal school buildings to reduce expenditure on structures and reduce the ecological impact of construction. These spaces will be rehabilitated to ensure that they provide a conducive, safe and hygienic learning environment. However, where no appropriate structures are available within a safe distance from learners’ homes, new learning space locations with will be identified in consultation with communities and government authorities and complementary WASH facilities provided or rehabilitated. This strategy includes provision of training for school-based management committees (SBMCs) and parent teacher associations (PTAs) to create a supportive and enabling environment for learning activities in focus communities. Training focus on community mobilization, school management and social audits to identify barriers to participation in formal or non formal learning activities. The Education Program will pilot provision of microgrants to PTAs to address barriers to enrolment and attendance in communities and build parental and community capacity to identify and solve problems. Vocational and non formal learning facilitators will be trained on effective pedagogies, prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA) and COVID-19 awareness and prevention so that they can provide a spectrum of support to learners. As part of the goal of creating pathways for learners to return to formal education, this strategy will also develop connections between non formal education classes and nearby formal school partners, where learners may continue their education. This will include a variety of support to ensure that nonformal education participants and their parents are connected with formal school partners; school visits to orient parents and children with the formal school and joint meeting of formal and nonformal school PTAs to identify common concerns and strategies to encourage learners to continue their education. Further, to create a conducive learning environment in the formal school partners, the Education Programme will engage in joint training for formal school partner teachers and PTAs with nonformal learning teachers and PTAs and minor rehabilitation and repairs. Further, as described above, the Program will provide microgrants to parent teacher committees and school based management committees to identify and address barriers to education and solutions identified will be used to inform project and program design and shared with other education providers and communities.For learners ages 15-24, the Education Program will provide vocational skills training identified by a labour market assessment to ensure alignment with real economic activities taking place in the community. This training will also be enhanced by YouthEmployment Programme activities to develop life and leadership skills within beneciary communities, startup kits and apprenticeships. Both nonformal and vocational activities will include COVID-19 awareness and prevention to ensure that students and their families are safe. Activities for young learners, adolescents and adults will include provision of teaching and learning kits as well as enhanced hygiene and dignity kits for girls to remove barriers to participation. Learning activities will include childcare services to ensure that young mothers can participate.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will benefit, IDP, returnee and host community students, their parents, school based management committees and school staff and will begin with consultative processes including all of these groups to ensure that proposed project design is acceptable. Additionally, consultation and feedback mechanisms will be provided during the life of the project to identify concerns and gather feedback and recommendations from beneficiary groups so that project design continues to be acceptable to communities and students. Adaptive mechanisms will be applied so that children, mothers and female community members’ voices are represented and this will include development of age appropriate feedback mechanisms and gender segregated discussions. Conflict sensitive education principles have been applied during the design of this project so that all activities encourage peaceful co-existence between host and IDP and returnee communities. activities will benefit all children and will be carefully designed to focus on cooperative rather than competitive activities to build positive relationships between students. Additionally, after school activities will include storytellers and musicians from a variety of ethnicities and both host and IDP and returnee communities to positively showcase the traditions of different cultures.Distribution of dignity kits and uniforms will benefit vulnerable children from all communities to eliminate resentment and school based management committees will include representatives of the different groups attending the school. NRC’s existing child protection and safeguarding measures will guide the project; NRC’s child safeguarding policy is annexed to the staff code of conduct and includes a commitment to not abuse or exploit children, take or share personal contact details with children, avoid being alone with a child and listening to and treat children equally and is supported by required online training. Further all teachers will participate in child safeguarding and protection, prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation and positive discipline training to ensure that classrooms are safe and teachers prepared to respond to concerns about the welfare of their students.
