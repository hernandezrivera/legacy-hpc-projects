Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Middle Shebelle, Gedo and Bay regions. ARD is currently implementing health project at same of these regions and this project will be complementary with ongoing project to cover nutrition needs of target vulnerable communities including women, girl’s boys. The project will provide an integrated response to acute malnutrition targeting 17,225 beneficiaries including 6249 boys, 6521 girls, and 4,455 pregnant and lactating women.
The planned interventions include identification and treatment of acute malnutrition, identification and referral of complicated cases and increased prevention activities including mass screening, deworming, micro nutrient supplementation, Infant Young Child Feeding and counseling, community education and sensitization) This will be achieved through four fixed OTP sites with three mobile teams. The capacity of 60 staff will be trained on integrated management of acute malnutrition while 30 staff will be trained on infant and young feeding.

Needs

FSNAU-FEWS NET Technical Release, January 29, 2018 states that 2018 Gu(April-June) rains are forecast to be below average. As a result, food and income sources are expected to remain below normal in most areas throughout the projection period. Livestock production and reproduction remain lower than normal due to low livestock herd sizes and low conception rates during previous seasons. Poor pasture and water conditions still persist in some pastoral livelihood zones in northern and central Somalia and parts of Gedo region. In these areas, further deterioration of pasture and water is expected during the dry Jilaal (January-March) season. Most areas of Somalia are currently Stressed (IPC Phase 2) or in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Somalia faces a fifth consecutive below-average season. Climate forecasts from the International Research Institute for Climate and Society (IRI) at Columbia University indicate a 40 percent chance of below normal 2018 Gu rains in most parts of Somalia (50 % in parts of Lower Shabelle and M/Shabelle regions). As a result, pasture and water availability, crop cultivation, livestock reproduction, access to agricultural employment, and water and food prices are expected to be adversely impacted. In addition, in the absence of continued large-scale assistance, food security is expected to deteriorate through June 2018, as food and income sources decline further. Over 2.7 million people will face Crisis or Emergency (IPC Phases 3 and 4) between now and June 2018. In total, 5.4 million people across Somalia face acute food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support is needed for approximately 301 000 acutely malnourished children, including 48 000 who are severely malnourished. It is expected that over 2.2 million people will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 496 000 people will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) between February and June 2018 and they need life-saving humanitarian assistance and livelihood protection support.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

ARD will work to build the leadership capacity of district and regional state health systems and will target Government key health managers, health facility staff, community members as well as patients accessing services so as to empower them to solve problems at their level and build relationships and social networks and work with the Government in order to build a resilient and responsive health system infrastructure. ARD will use the model of everyday resilience building in the health system of Government through a combination of absorptive, adaptive and transformative strategies that health authorities, staff and community members can use to adopt the health system in responding to strains and shocks. This will help in producing a strengthened and more resourceful resilient health system infrastructure. ARD will train the health facility staffs, patients and community members on how to deal with sudden shocks to the health system such as outbreak of diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Cholera, and Measles and so on.
