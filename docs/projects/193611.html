Project Summary

The project was designed and built in reference to the secondary data provided by the Palestinian government, WASH cluster vulnerability mapping that PARC contributed in and according to validation visits and a concrete need assessment that has been conducted by PARC recently where women actively participated in the assessment.
The project can be summarized by two main activities, the first activity is related to drinking water in Bethlehem governorate south WB and the second main activity is related to adequate sewerage in Jenin governorate north WB. The following is a brief summary for each activity:

1- The installation of new drinking water distribution networks (6 km) and HH connections (600) in two of Bethlehem communities (Nahhalin and Al Khader). These communities and according to the mapping that was conducted by the WASH cluster in cooperation with the partners showed that those two communities deeply need this kind of intervention. This is due to the low water consumption per capita in these communities. In both communities, Nahhalin and Al Khader, the average drinking water consumption is 30-50 liter per capita per day which is far below the average defined by WHO which is 100 liter per capita per day. Moreover, The high prices of trucked water is an increasing burden on the residents' shoulders especially in summer time when the weather becomes dry and harsh, in such times, one cubic meter of drinking water approximately costs around 20 NIS. Another important activity in this section, is the connection of 600 HHs (400 in Nahhalin and 200 in Al Khader) to the newly installed water network, this will guarantee the provision of high quality, safe and affordable water resource. In this case, it is expected according to PARC experience that the average consumption of liters per capita per day will increase to reach 75 instead of 30-50. In addition the cost per cubic meter will decrease to be around 5 NIS. This is an increase of 100% in consumption and decrease of 75% in cost for the local residents. All these developed elements will be reflected on the livelihood conditions of the targeted communities including health and hygiene.
2- The installation of new sewer pipelines of Anza village south of Jenin, this activity includes the installation of 1km of sewer pipelines including all needed manholes and accessories. This can be considered as an extension to the existing sewer network that was conducted by PARC 8 years ago.
According to PARC need assessment, only 70% of Anza's population are connected to the existing sewer network. In this intervention, 50 additional households will be connected to the network. This will reduce the harming environmental impacts of cesspits and increase the amount of treated wastewater of Anza wastewater treatment plant that is being used widely in summer for irrigation and discharged in Wadis safely in winter as treated wastewater up to the Palestinian standards. To conclude, the intervention in Anza will target installation/ extension of 1km of sewer pipelines with all needed accessories and facilities in addition to connecting 50 HHs to the newly installed sewer network. This will provide the sewer services adequately and directly to 50 HHs (approximately 250 capita) and will provide access to sanitation services to future extension projects, expanding landscape and growing population.
PARC believes that the developed and reliable WASH infrastructures (Water and Sanitation), will be reflected on the quality of livelihoods of the local residents in the targeted communities more than anything else. It is highly important to mention that the quality services deeply enhance the resilience of the residents of these communities and decrease the chances of internal migrations to the cities which is directly reflected on a more sustainable, diverse, food secured and stable community in Palestine.

1 - Needs

PARC worked in three dimensions to address the needs of the targeted communities:1- The first dimension, PARC referred to the secondary data that was provided by the Palestinian government, especially the data released by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA). Additional coordination was done with PWA to agree on the targeted locations as a priority. Written approval was provided by e-mail from PWA side approving these communities.2- The second dimension, according to the WASH cluster vulnerability mapping that was conducted previously this year with the contribution of PARC and the other partners, the preliminary data was analyzed and showed that the targeted communities are in real need for the proposed interventions of this project. This is obvious from the row data that can be found in the mentioned mapping. This was noticed from the low consumption and the high prices of water. In addition to this, coordination between partners was guaranteed through the pre-submission sheet that was shared across all partners to ensure no or least duplication in choosing communities.3- The third dimension, PARC conducted a thorough need assessment on sites, this was conducted through a series of site visits to the targeted areas including visiting the HHs that are in need in Nahhalin, Al Khader and Anza. During these visits, there were many meetings with village councils and the related stakeholders. This was a survey and a validation act from PARC side. Referring to the previous acts, Nahhalin and Alkhader were chosen for drinking water interventions due to the following needs and circumstances:1- Most of the HHs that are not connected to a water network in these communities lie in Area C, these HHs are frequently exposed to settler violence and attacks. Moreover, they are frequently exposed to the civil administration's house destruction notifications and decisions, they are located in areas very close to settlements due to the lack of lands for building and expansion. For example, the targeted area in Nahhalin is very close to "Beitar Elit" settlement which is one of the biggest settlements in the WB with population of approximately 40,000, the settlement keeps cutting the village's lands and decrease the possibility for the villagers to expand.2- The average consumption of water per capita per day in these communities (30 - 50) is far below the safe average defined by the WHO which is 100 LPCPD. Thus, there is a big gap between the safe consumption and the actual consumption in these community. For example, the majority of Al Khader village HHs are connected to the existing water network so the consumption of water in these HHs is noticed to be higher than the HHs that are not connected which are targeted by the proposed interventions. It is expected that the consumption will duplicate (from 30-50 to 75 LPCPD) and the cost per cubic meter will decrease by 75% (from 20 NIS to 5 NIS).3- The average price for the cubic meter of water that is provided through a water distribution network is about 5 NIS all over WB, while the trucked cubic meter of water that is used widely in the targeted areas is about 20 NIS, this is a 300% more. This creates additional burden on the budget of these HHs who are mostly poor, leads them to use less water as it is expensive and rare especially in summer or both. The decrease in water consumption is obviously reflected on their livelihood, green areas, health and hygiene especially for PWDs, elderly and children, newly born diseases, women and girls personal hygiene, and many other aspects.4- During harsh weather and especially in summer times the water supply becomes rare and not continuous, even by trucking, this makes the lives of Nahhalin and Al Khader residents full of challenges and difficulties. Most likely, they can arrange for drinking but also they are forced to quit many important activities that affects the quality of their livelihoods and their hygiene.5- Most of the HHs that are targeted by the proposed interventions are financially poor with limited income and resources. Some of them are women headed, some of them are unemployed and many of them lack the sufficient resources to improve their conditions and enhance their livelihoods even inside their own houses. This makes the interventions vital for them and with significant impact. Anza was chosen for sanitation interventions for the following reason:1- Anza is one of the very few villages across WB that has an access to an operating, modern and efficient wastewater treatment plant. This plant is treating Anza's wastewater and producing treated water that is being 100% used in agriculture activities during summer season and being discharged to wadies in winter season in a safe way according to the Palestinian standards through periodic sampling process. This makes Anza a suitable candidate for receiving a sanitation intervention.2- 70% of Anza's HHs are connected to the existing sewer network that was installed by PARC in 2014, the network covered 95% of the HHs then. Due to the growth of Anza's population and due to rural expansion, the No. of HHs increased and the covered HHs with sanitation services become 70%. The proposed intervention will ensure that most of Anza's HHs will be connected to the sewer network.3- The cost of installing the sewer networks are among the most expensive infrastructure works ( it needs approx. 100,000$ to install 1km). Anza is a small village with population of 2,200 capita who could not afford the high cost of the network expansion.4- Anza is a mountain village, this makes the sewerage essential for the environment and for the daily life. The project is designed in a participatory and flexible way , and since water related issues mainly affect women they were engaged in the assessment and during implementation special attention will be given to track the level of women satisfaction and improvements in their livelihoods through indicators and MEAL plan.

2 - Activities

WASH facilities development is representing an important issue for many actors; Availability , accessibility and affordability concern the villages councils, the HHs and mainly women and related ministries and agencies All these interests are reflected on the planned intervention. - The project will start by the first activity which is announcing the project in the targeted communities publicly, thus all the people in the targeted communities will be informed about the main activities of the project. A local committee from the community itself including the local village council and the public figures will be formulated for the project. This committee will help PARC in choosing the most vulnerable HHs and help in solving any social conflict. Each HH from the targeted areas has the right to apply and to be a beneficiary of the project. People will have the right to complain, suggest and have their own concerns, this will be done through a hot line, a complaint box and a complaint e-mail. - The technical engineering activities will take place in the targeted communities as the following:1- Site Mapping and surveying: After choosing the beneficiaries, there will be a development for engineering drawings by engineers from each village council and PARC engineers, the channels for the pipelines will be defined. The activity includes preparing the maps needed for the contractor and the municipality; for the contractor to define the locations of pipelines, house connections, manholes and to achieve the proposed works according to BOQ and engineers' guidelines. For the municipality to define the pathways of pipelines, the real locations of the connections for future expansion. Moreover, in this activity there will be initial vision about obstacles that may occur on site and the best possible way to get over them by the municipality during implementation 2- Developing BOQ, related needed details, maps and documents: In this activity PARC in cooperation with PWA and local village council will work to develop the BOQ needed for the contract of implementation of the water distribution network and the pipelines. These needed documents will be developed by specialized professional engineers. All mentioned documents will be achieved according to the Palestinian standards that will be provided by PWA and according to the technical manual developed by WASH cluster. 3- Tendering, rewarding and site visit: The tendering process will take place after that, the preparing of the BOQ for each community, publishing the tenders, evaluating the offers, awarding and contracting. 4- The supervision on the contractor's works by the engineers of both PARC and the local village council to ensure daily coordination, quality of works, receiving any complaints from the local community, dealing with any technical challenges and other tasks in daily works. These works include:A- Site handing overB- Site preparation and mobilizationC- The provision of the needed materialD- Excavation worksE- BackfillingF- House connectionsG- TestingH- Reinstatement 5- Ensuring the adequate implementation of HH connection to the networks (both for the drinking water networks and the sewer network). 6- The handing over process including the testing processes, the quality assurance, guaranteeing the sustainability, finalizing the engineering works, ensuring maintenance guarantees and handing the new assets to the local village councils. 7- Final reporting and indicator verification: In this stage of the project. In this important stage PARC will ensure the following:A- Justify financially and technically the whole project activities by drafting a full financial and technical reports with all needed supporting documents including the sources of verification for the output indicator(s).B- PARC will organize a field visit for OCHA and PWA to visit the site after implementation to see the impact on the local residents through naked eye. - 3 sessions per community (9 in total) will be carried out by PARC staff to improve people awareness in the field of rationing the water use at HH level for women, water born diseases and hygiene dimensions designed mainly for women. - In addition to that 3 sessions per community (9 in total) will be carried out by PARC staff in the field of running and maintaining supported infrastructure, proper storage and transfer of drinking water targeting municipality technicians, wastewater treatment operators and consumers in general where women will take part of these awareness sessions. - Formulation of three Community Protection Committees (CPCs), prepare them and give them the needed trainings. These CPCs are responsible for assuring the safety and protection procedures in the targeted communities, during and after the implementation of the project. These committees will be trained in details on how to deal with any protection incidents that take place while and after the implementation of the project, including how to contact the responsible sides like OCHA and others. These CPCs are being formed in approximately all PARC's projects and they proved their worth in many cases and places. The previous activities were summarized briefly, full details and breakdowns will be elaborated in the full proposal, including the exact lengths and diameters of pipelines in different locations. All the pipelines and their fittings that will be used will be according to the Palestinian standards PS.421 and PS.425 by the PWA and in line with the WASH technical manual which was developed by the WASH cluster including PARC and the other partners.

3 - Indicators

PARC believes that the role of the MEAL department starts from the design phase of the project. The MEAL officer gets involved in the design in order to build a comprehensive understanding to the impact, overall objective of the project. Which will be reflected in a MEAL plan relevant to the proposed activities. PARC’s MEAL officer works at two level, first the process (transparency, accountability & equality and access to information) and secondly, the activity implementation. At activity implementation level; the proposed activities, results and objectives will be achieved fully with high level of effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability in order to achieve the overall objective of the project. M&E plan will be updated for the project and followed by the MEAL officer hand on hand with project coordinator. This matrix will track all the project activities matching them with project indicators. Quarter reports and plans will be achieved supported with site visits and meeting with beneficiaries, in addition to the daily follow up through PARC’s field supervisors which will be documented and monitored by the MEAL officer. At the start of the project, a project manual contains the necessary information and forms for the implementation of the project activities will be developed including: beneficiary selection and scoring system, purchasing and imbursement procedures, and closing of services, monitoring system, documentation system and the process of updating according to reality on ground. In this project the MEAL officer in collaboration with project team members will supervise all monitoring activities and reporting of the proposed project. The MEAL officer will be responsible to document and develop tools that measures the progress and level of achievements of each activity and how it pours into the project indicators outputs and outcomes. To sum up, responsible person will work to develop a comprehensive M&E plan at the beginning of the project with set timelines, activities, indicators and the methodologies of monitoring each individual activity. In addition to preparing and conducting a baseline and end line with support to measure overall impact of the project. The importance of these visits is to ensure proper implementation, measure the progress, document change at beneficiaries’ lives, collect success stories, lessons learned and communication with local community to measure level of satisfaction. According to the variety of activities and variety of targeted communities, there will be variety in indicators and sub indicators, these indicators can be divided to the following parts:1- Indicators related to the improvement in the access to sufficient, safe and affordable water supply on the community and HH levels. This will be measured in reference to the baseline study that will be performed to measure the exact numbers of HHs and disaggregated individuals who improved their access to safe, sufficient and affordable drinking water in regular manner. Moreover, a sub indicator for measuring the level of satisfaction for the households will be conducted through a questionnaire after the implementation. "main indicator is # of people benefiting from improved access to safe drinking and domestic water services", "# of meter length of water pipeline, network installed/rehabilitated" 2- Indicators related to the quality, consumption and price of water supplied after the intervention to measure the impact of the project on HH level, these indicators will have a target to be reached based on the findings on the baseline study. An end-line study will be done to measure the impact of the project. "# of people benefiting from improved access to safe drinking and domestic water services" " % of water consumption increase by Liter per capita per day" "% of cubic meter price decrease" 3- indicators related to the improvement of sanitation services on the community HH level. This will be measured in reference to the baseline study that will be performed to measure the exact numbers of HHs and disaggregated individuals who improved their access to better sanitation services. Moreover, a sub indicator for measuring the level of satisfaction for the households will be conducted through a questionnaire after the implementation. "main indicators are # of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation services or connected to sewer network", "# of meter length of sewer network pipelines installed/rehabilitated" 4- Indicators related to the improved awareness in WASH topics and especially on drinking water consumption, management, storage and other related issues. That is the improvement in the level of awareness achieved through the local communities in subjects related to the methods of water and management, water conservation and pollution of water (KAP). There will be also a sub indicator related to the number of the local community individuals who participated in the awareness campaigns and in the training sessions. # of awareness products developed 5- indicator will measure the progress in protection activities related to the creation of the protection group in each targeted community and risk mapping. In addition, another sub indicator will be added to measure the improved ability for the protection group members in dealing with protection incidents and communication with organizations related to the protection field such as OCHA, protection cluster and others. # of awareness products developed.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a well-articulated feedback and response mechanism (FRM) that is put in action from the design phase until closing. FRM at PARC is ensured with respect to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). The CHS adopted by PARC will ensure to provide a safe/dignified space for targeted population to express their feedback and complaints. In June 2021, PARC updated its accountability policies (information sharing, complaints and feedback and participation) and the Safeguarding policy hand on hand with Accountability specialist from Christian Aid. Accordingly, PARC consulted with local communities to identify the most appropriate channels suitable for them. Hence, a dedicated email (Complaints@pal-arc.org), complaint boxes distributed across the governorates, in addition to the MEAL Officer phone number (Whats App). These three channels will be utilized to receive feedback and complaints. Then, based on PARC accountability policies the complaint shall be registered into a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked and documented through the MEAL documentation system. Additionally, all project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer within the project MEAL framework. With reference to CHS, complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. Field staff will announce the availability of the three feedback mechanisms for the local communities and ensure that any complaint or feedback shall be addressed will be taken seriously including any sensitive complaints. Any complaints received shall be protected in accordance to data protection protocols adopted by PARC. Feedback mechanisms will also be promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs). In regards to safeguarding complaints (SEA), PARC has a well-articulated policy with clear standard data protection procedures and will take any sensitive complaint seriously when proven correct no tolerance policy shall be applied.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm" as seen to be part of ensuring accountability towards the local communities used and monitored during any intervention implemented by PARC including participation, information sharing, enabling complaints and "do no harm”, environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project interventions and implementation strategies adheres to the social, political, and economic context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the conducted participatory needs assessment and PARC do-no-harm approaches. In regards to social sensitivity, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and will ensure that in all project phases the announcements and meetings will take place in a wide, easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join and share their needs. For the political side, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting the local community to document the Israeli violations. In addition to that a group of the targeted community will follow up to document all Israeli violations and report back to PARC’s team, other relevant acting organizations and OCHA field office. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyze potential risks that the Project team will take into consideration while progressing/implementing the project. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation of the project. The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) including women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, nondiscrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches are well integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, local governmental bodies and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs, including the participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the locality to ensure optimal outreach, transparency and ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC’s team will be responsible in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear gender sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team will develop a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable (Children, PwDs, elderly, women headed HH… etc). An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at beneficiary and community levels. Weekly meetings will be held between the municipality, project local committee, community members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. PARC will share any human rights-based violations to relevant clusters.
