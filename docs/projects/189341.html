Project Summary


This project is a food security and livelihood support intervention with Strengthening of coping mechanisms and skills in agricultural productions amongst 16,605 households high food insecure household in Tonj north County (Warrap State and Awiel East of Northern bahr el Ghazal State, the immediate objectives is to support vulnerable households with direct crops kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits and tools so as to enable them to have access to food through their own production. The intervention will contribute to the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster objective 2 “Protect and rehabilitate livelihoods for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition”, directly linked to the Strategic Response Plan (SRP) objective “Improve self-reliance and coping capacities of people in need by protecting, restoring and promoting their livelihoods”. The FSL Cluster strategy in line with 2022 HRP to scale up existing interventions with support to frontline actors with a presence on the ground to respond in a timely manner to prioritized Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) counties. To deliver the required results, the key program strategy is to implement the direct distributions of crop kits, vegetable kits, fishing kits and tools to boost household food production and sustaining food security. The distribution of FSL emergency livelihoods kits will complemented by Strengthen coping mechanisms and skills in agricultural production through training of farmers groups and fishermen groups on improved agronomics, vegetable utilization and preparation, post-harvest handling while fishermen received training on sustainable fishing, hygienic handling and preservation of fish in order to reduce post-harvest loses. Post distribution, post planting and post harvesting assessment will be conducted.
To ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) a committee will be formed to address issues that may affect the beneficiaries negatively during the project implementation. The project will target mainly vulnerable returns, flood affected persons, IDPs, and the host population for the main cropping season, second season and dry season responses; a total of 16,605 HHS will be supported from Tonj north and Awiel East Counties of Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states. 2022, urgent pre-positioning is necessary ahead of the main farming season. The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sectoral analysis identified an increase in the severity and extent of food insecurity through the use of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System Plus (FSNMS+) outcome indicator data, FSL assessment reports (including over 100 pieces of evidence. The RWDSS with SSHF support will prioritize immediate and short-term term lifesaving activities (essentially our famine prevention and response actions) and the shift towards building resilience: absorptive and adaptive capacities especially through training, been a very strong and active partner of the food security and livelihood cluster at national and state levels and have participated in several joint assessment missions in its areas of operation. RWDSS has record experience working with FAO ELRP activities and More than 6 years working with WFP in implementing purchase for progress project, GFD, FFA, School feeding programs and Cash vouchers. This solid experience of RWDSS both in term of context, high impact previous implemented projects and active presences will give opportunity to implement this project with quality results among the affected population.

1 - Needs

The proposed project will be implemented in Tonj North County and Awiel East Counties. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May – July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The main drivers are: (1) covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence and (2 household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal, on 18th of January 2022, Awiel East have experienced repeated attacks by suspected Misseriya tribesmen killed at least 24 people displacing thousands others into neighboring communities. The frequent violence prompted the state government to close the western border with Sudan indefinitely until the dispute is resolved. Conflict there has existed for years amongst the two ethnic groups of Dinka Malual, Rezigat and the Misseriya nomads. The Bordering Community of Bahr el Ghazal region has clashed with the Misseriya tribesmen for years. Fighting there has been associated to wrangle over grazing land, water and pasture for animals. Several peace conferences between the Dinka Malual and Misseriya have been held in the past, however conflicts have continued despite the peace efforts. This has resulted to increasingly high level of vulnerability, especially people loosed their lives, food items, houses and livestock were burnt down. meanwhile nearly 2620 households (HHs) were displace to the nearest villages of Warnyiel, Majok yinthiou,War-agany, Riang-awei, Angoot, and other places seeking for safety. In Tonj North County Throughout 2021, insecurity and flooding have simultaneously driven large-scale displacement and prevented very vulnerable populations from undertaking displacement journeys to produce their own food or access food and services. Tensions with neighboring communities in Unity State and Lakes State have been high. It was reported that IDPs have been robbed and killed. Barriers to movement have been further compounded by flooding which prevented many households from cultivating. Harvested food stocks are expected to be exhausted as early as November/December 2021 and thus households are likely to continue to experience atypically severe, widespread and prolonged food consumption gaps unless humanitarian access improves in the near term. Widespread insecurity has reportedly resulted in cattle raiding and access to livestock has been atypically low throughout the 2021 lean season, during which reliance on cattle used to be generally highest. Access to cattle is unlikely to improve in the near to medium term, leaving highly vulnerable groups without access to livestock products and cash. Findings suggest that community-level coping capacity has been negatively affected by the erosion of livelihoods and a consequent lack of assets redistribution. Mass displacement to populated areas has placed substantial pressure on host community reserves. With the assistance from SSHF, RWDSS will during this intervention target households experiencing food insecurity with a quick impact intervention that will have immediate effect in addressing the food needs of the community through their own effort. The fishing intervention proposed for instance will be done in areas where people depend on fishing as main source of survival. Vegetable supports are quick and short maturing that can provide food in less than 6 weeks. Besides been quick maturing, it terms of seasonality these are the activities that are only appropriate at this time that could sustain ably addresses the food need.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The situation in South Sudan is politically and economically very dynamic, with some risks remaining constant and some new ones arising. The security situation in some of the Payams of Tonj East throughout 2021 has been fluid with active internal conflicts among the cattle keepers and tribal tension .Crimes along Tonj East Road has been on the rise due to the weakening economy and locally generated conflicts. Other risks are linked to economic crisis; varying casual labor rates in field among agencies operating in Tonj East. To mitigate the risks in all project locations, RWDSS conducted a safety audit to map existing and potential risks posed to staffs, project supplies and beneficiaries. Safety audit findings have been used to project staffs to inform their movements and advocate for protection through local authorities. Also, RWDSS FSL teams continue to follows all the procedures and systems to provide security to its compounds (staffs and assets).RWDSS has procedures for travels and field trips; screening the locations regarding security and local dynamics. Maintaining close relationship with local communities, as well as informing local authorities and other stakeholders about the protection concerns has ensured activities are performed in a relatively safe environment in the field. Further, RWDSS liaises closely with the NGO forum, NGO partners SFP, UNDSS and UNOCHA on information gathering and evacuation of staffs. During planning, RWDSS has engaged Local authorities and other representatives of the beneficiary community (including a balanced representation of vulnerable groups) to avoid perceptions of bias. RWDSS has also included vulnerable host communities as beneficiaries to minimize tension over assistance provided. Also, RWDSS protection officer has supported in-depth protection analysis using the protection risk manuals developed by International Mercy Corps. Finally, the protection officer visited distribution sites to advise the project team on adjustments, if needed, based on safety audits. RWDSS staff members and enumerators in the field were made to understand and sign the Code of Conduct and Child Protection Policy prior to commencing project activities. These policies are made available for the beneficiaries in a form that is widely understood by the community. Entitlements were clearly visible at the distribution points and these were communicated to the community. On distribution days, the staff created separate queues for vulnerable groups (such as the elderly, people with special needs, pregnant and lactating mothers and child-headed households) and were served first. Carriers were considered for people with special needs and the chronically ill, especially those who had limited movement capacity.
