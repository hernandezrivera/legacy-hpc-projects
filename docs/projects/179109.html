Project Summary

In line with the Shelter and NFI cluster guidance, SECD is planning to sustain and save the dignity and lives of the targeted people by implementing timely, needs-based, and effective interventions
the people in north-west Syria are living in a very diﬃcult situation, the area experienced one of the biggest displacement waves, more than 700,000 people left their houses in search of a safer place to save the lives of the women, children, elderly, PWD, and other families members
through these interventions, SECD is planning to support 35500 People ( 7675 Men, 9010 Women, 9219 Boy, and 9596 Girl) based on in-depth need assessment, with focusing on responding to the critical needs of people with considering the access, the functionality of the markets and the community engagement approach by consulting the people in need about the proposed interventions
According to HNO 2020 "In the north-west, there are critical levels of overcrowding putting signiﬁcant pressure on overburdened communities, with few if any remaining shelter options available." the IDPs cannot ﬁnd a shelter to live in, and if it is available HNO 2020 mentioned that "Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west and north-east, a signiﬁcant proportion of whom are in improvised shelters which will need replacement/ repair in the next year.9 In addition, some communities in 23 sub-districts in Aleppo, Hassakeh, , Idleb and Rural Damascus OR Al Tal, Tal Rafat, Dana, Sabe Byar and al Hol and Sabe Byar10 are overburdened as they host more IDPs than resident populations with limited shelter and hosting capacity,"
SECD is planning to build its response plan based on the following interventions:
1- Undertake the rehabilitation of 4 damaged, unﬁnished buildings, collective centers, and 920 houses
the shelter intervention will be based on the following aspects:
- Ensuring proper HLP situation for the targeted shelters
- Prioritize the cost-effective interventions
- Ensuring the participation of the beneﬁciaries in all the intervention stages
2- Ensure better infrastructure in the self-settled camps and the informal camps in order to respond to the repeated flood risks every winter, all the targeted camps will be selected based on the priority list which is shared by the SNFI and the CCCM clusters after confirming the needs by SECD's MEAL staff
Moreover, the plan is considering the obvious need to support the IDPs with NFI assistance which is still a big gap in the humanitarian response, HNO 2020 indicated that " many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inﬂation, and low wages, while populations routinely report needing for items outside the core NFI packages. There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly thousands of people are returning to their homes after the current relative calm in the situation in their areas, the return movements have increased to some areas and an estimated 64 percent of communities are underserved. This is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs during harsh weather with fuel and power sources remaining in high demand"
SECD is committed to supporting the people in need especially the new IDPs by distributing 1000 core NFI kits 500 winterization kits including stoves, fuel, and blankets in addition to the distribution of 180000 litter of fuel in the winter

4 - Needs

Since 2011, waves of conflict, political and economic instability in Syria caused severe deterioration in the standard of living on the community and individual levels and the escalation of this conflict since 2019 has worsened these conditions. Damage to housing and infrastructure has been observed across NWS, mostly on the communities and areas which are classified as safer area. According to HNO 2020 "Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west and north-east, a significant proportion of whom are in improvised shelters which will need replacement/ repair in the next year". Lack of appropriate shelter and NFIs including clothing, bedding, and hygiene items negatively affects all population groups regardless of their age (children, young adults, vulnerable adults, and elderly) and gender (men, women, boys, and girls) worsening their wellbeing, with negative impacts on health, protection, and access to basic services. The dire economic situation, limited access to livelihood opportunities increases the difficulty of making rent payments and can consequently increase the risk of eviction. Shelter represents 50% of domestic expenditures for most households. According to SECD multi-sectorial need assessment, The IDPs face a combination of liquidity constraints and a shortage of housing, which has led to very high rental costs. Those living in public and informal settings, experience lack of access to basic services, poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions, overcrowding, and a lack of privacy. Informal settlements are often places of physical insecurity due to crime and other forms of violence. In displacement areas, thousands of people without adequate shelter are living in damage

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse during the shelter rehabilitation: All SECD staff are receiving regular training on the code of conduct and PSEA policy and they signed on these policies, The contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration with a specific clause on PSEA with an emphasis on mitigation measures and sanctions in case of abuse by an employee, Beneficiaries will receive information on the CRM and the expected behavior of SECD staff and its contractors with an emphasis on how to report any SEA incidents. A woman staff is present during all visits to women and child led-householdsIn SECD as an active member within PSEA Network, All of the core staff trained on PSEA, we ensure that all new staff and the key people within our project will train on PSEA and knowing very well how they can deal with the PSEA case if that happened, the Turkey and Syria SECD PSEA focal point monitor all activity and lead the PSEA campaigns within the communities, SECD file staff posted PSEA brochures contain the hotlines and how the people can make the complaints, all step of these process deal with the strictest confidential way.SECD management ensures female representation within the program staff; and provides support for them to assume leadership positions, also ensure participation of women in decision-making processes in the camps. And Include women and girls in beneficiary satisfaction surveys.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Threats to health, GBV, PSEA and safety and security of staff, and beneﬁciaries linked to conﬂict or criminal activities: SECD has dedicated Safety and Security staff, both at coordination and ﬁled level, to ensure a close context follow-up and the correct implementation of SECD Security policy and standards. All SECD staff receive proper security and safety training. regular meeting with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent recruitment policy and criteria for selection of beneﬁciaries, clear red lines identiﬁed while negotiating with local authorities, careful liaison work. Scale-up internal procurement to guarantee access to supplies. Adaptation of programs. Advocacy efforts. Fraud/Corruption/Bribery: Enforcing SECD’s policy of zero tolerance of corruption through the continuous strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the “whistle-blowing” policy, corruption risk mapping, -Changes in territorial control: good understanding of context, contextual analysis, Security Plans and procedures, contingency planning, community acceptance and good relationship with local authorities
