Project Summary

Juba has become a place of refuge to those fleeing from their war torn counties. The focus has been IDP camps previously supported by the UN. Evidence suggests that child, early and forced marriage has increased during the conflict as families use it as a coping mechanism to alleviate economic pressures, clear debts, buy protection or settle disputes. With the disruption of routine activities, change of environment, dropping out of school and missing out on friends, distress increases among both boys and girls. There is a need for psychosocial support using creative structured age and sex appropriate activities. This project will alleviate the impact of the conflict on children through the provision of targeted psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification services. It targets children and caregivers with an aim of rebuilding ability to reconnect and cope positively.
The project will:
1. Provide structured community-based psychosocial support activities: The conflict has severely affected the psychosocial wellbeing and mental health of children, with some witnessing brutal murders. Additionally, they and their caregivers daily experience difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods and recreation. In this project, MTT will administer psychosocial support to vulnerable children—including Psychological First Aid and other forms of non-formal psychosocial support as they reintegrate into communities. CFSs will be made to coalesce into communities through community based child protection networks (CBCPNs).
2. Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members based on existing tools: Conflict-affected and displaced children risk becoming involved in the worst forms of child labour such as child tracking, hazardous work or begging in the streets. Through training foster caregivers, MTT will increase the level of children care and protection. This is in realization of the fact that children outside parental-care are at more risk of exploitation. The capacity of communities will be increased to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns through CBCPNs and existing referral pathways.
3. Support community-based child protection committees to engage in child protection activities: The project has considered gender and age in planning and implementation. Boys and girls with special needs are targeted. The project will provide life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys who will be supported to organize peer support activities with children. Women and men will be duly represented in all decision-making organs to provide avenues for maximum input from all groups who will be encouraged to participate in every component of the project with the dynamics of the gender roles in mind.
4. Capacity building of non-child protection workers on CPiE: In collaboration with education and health clusters, capacity built of health workers and teachers with emphasis on girl-child education and provision of dignity kits. Feedback mechanisms are embedded for project flexibility. Nearby primary schools will be identified as partners with whom to mainstream Child Protection and psycho-social support in schools through capacity building events while observing the: "do no harm"

1 - Needs

The last project in Juba MTT was able to access 5,941 to age-appropriate, effective, multi-sectoral CPiE responses in Juba IDPs Camp and Urban Juba. The current project targets 5,000 people with children in Juba in Juba IDP camps and Per-Urban Juba, Juba (Central Equatoria) who require FTR, PSS and comprehensive Case Management. Children in conflict in the law need legal assistance and community rehabilitation. They will receive additional education about, unexploded ordinances of war, prevention of separation and psychosocial support through public awareness sessions and community discussions (focus group discussion). The Project will deliver critical children protection in emergency services including Family Tracing Reunification (FTR) Packages. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be integrated in the project through definition of process, reporting and referral to GBV Sub cluster. Psycho-social support will target boys, girls, women and men thereby provide a platform for a multi-sector response to the affected populations. Targeted will be boys and girls within various age groups (0-5, 6-12, 13-17) with each age groups with specific needs. Adolescent girls and boys need to learn life skills and resilience in the situation in which they find themselves. This is mainly through peer support. For sustainability, skills will be transferred to community leaders and volunteers on basic case management cycle, children interviewing skills, safeguarding, tracing and CBPSS and Psychological First Aid. Within the IDP camps, skills will be enhanced, re-organized and strengthened for community based support and referral networks. There is need for case workers and CBCPN member(s) to do daily awareness raising on prevention and response to Child Protection issues and provision of general psychosocial services to men and women, boys and girls. Care givers and other community members need to learn parenting skills based on existing tools, mainly prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and handling children with disabilities (CWD) There is need to identify and document children with protection concerns, support girls and boys in vulnerable situations through case management and referrals to specialized services. MTT will continue to train CP staff on Child Protection case management and Family tracing and reunification of UASC. Schools need to mainstream Child Protection and psychosocial support through capacity building events; paying special attention to PSEA, and handling CWD. The project will provide training and mentoring to care givers, child protection volunteers and staff on child protection approaches. Non-child protection workers, especially teachers and health workers will be trained on CPiE and modern mentoring methods. Adolescents needs for life skills and resilience programming through organized peer support will be addressed. For sustainability, capacity building of community-based and government officers on child protection structures on CPiE needs to be done. Feedback mechanism will be integrated from the community for project monitoring and evaluation.

Activities Activity1: Identify and document children with protection concerns, paying special attention to boys and girls likely to suffer sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and children with disabilities Activity2: Support Girls and Boys in vulnerable situations, paying special attention to children likely to suffer sexual exploitation and abuse, and children with disabilities through case management and referrals to specialized services Activity3: Raise awareness on prevention and response to Child Protection issues, including PSEA, and handling CWD, build capacity of community-based and government officers on child protection structures on CPiE, PSEA, and handling CWD Activity4: Train and re-train CP staff, teachers, parents and other community members on Child Protection, case management and PSEA Activity5: Train and re-train CP staff, teachers, parents and other community members on Child Protection, case management and PSEA Activity5: Provide targeted girls and boys and youth in humanitarian situations with psychosocial support, using child friendly spaces, schools and other community spaces Activity6: Provide Family tracing and reunification services to unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) Activity7: Obtain feedback on project through discussions with religious and local leaders, age groups and focal group discussions. This will be incorporated back into the project to enrich content during the implementation.
