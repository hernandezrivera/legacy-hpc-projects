Project Summary

An estimated 2.6 million people are displaced in Somalia (2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview). The displaced population’s coping mechanisms have been affected over successive years of drought, floods and conflict, leaving them vulnerable to shocks and inhibiting a sustainable return to places of origin. Of the total displaced population, 2,341,411 million people are living in IDP sites with the most severe needs. (2021 CCCM Cluster PiN) An estimated 351,211 (15%) of them are persons with various disabilities while 47% of the displaced are women. IDP sites lack dignified living conditions, sufficient access to basic services, land tenure and adequate resources to sustain their populations.
A large proportion of humanitarian aid in Somalia targets IDP sites and camp-like settings which accommodate some of the most vulnerable populations in the country. The coordination of site-level activities in addition to general service monitoring and site management support largely relies on CCCM partner expertise. Consequentially, given the sheer volume of IDP sites in Somalia (over 2,400 IDP sites), there is a pertinent need to support in the coordination of CCCM actors addressing priority needs within districts/regions of the country. The CCCM cluster actively works at ensuring that partners are delivering services in a coordinated manner that fosters a high quality of service. Moreover, the cluster plays a vital role in developing the capacity of government, humanitarian and development actors aimed at exploring sustainable solutions for IDPs living in camp-like settings. Lastly, the cluster's role in inter-cluster coordination is imperative through highlighting, analyzing and raising key trends in displacement patterns, service quality at the site-level and accountability to affected population data.

Project Activities

This HRP project aims at maintaining national and sub-national cluster coordination responsibilities with IOM’s continued co-chair role. Under this project, IOM will provide 1 national CCCM Cluster Coordinator, 1 national CCCM Cluster Information Management Officer in addition to 3 sub-national focal points (Kismayo, Gedo and Baidoa). The CCCM cluster will continue to play an active role at the national level and via the ICCG advocating for improved living conditions in IDP sites and strategic fundraising decisions allowing for partners to target IDP sites and populations articulated within the sectors HRP. Furthermore, the CCCM cluster will strive to improve both intra-and inter-sector coordination at the sub-national level through the technical support of cluster focal points in nine geographic regions within the country. These initiatives will link with durable solutions programming when possible, aspiring for the achievements of durable solutions. Through the CCCM cluster team, partners will be supported with a range of high quality programmatic tools which will be revised and updated on an needs basis. Additionally, capacity building support will occur targeting both CCCM partners, local authorities and other humanitarian/development partners with the aim at enriching the understanding of CCCM principles, capacity to implement following these principles, and establishing a clear exit plan for the sector. Under these activities, the cluster will promote localization measures to strengthen participation of national partners. The information management officer will assist in compiling data to form baseline data for functional and verified IDP sites, in addition to actively contributing data for the establishment an overall IDP population within Somalia. Furthermore, the information management officer will create information products that visualize some of the important data categories captured by partners such as needs/gap analysis, complaints feedback mechanism trends and site verification updates.

Project Indicators

This CCCM cluster project has the intentions of supporting all CCCM partners operating within the sectors cluster system. As the CCCM cluster, the success of the sector will be predicated on achieving the following indicator results: # of sites with established CCCM mechanisms, # of people benefiting from site improvement projects and # of sites with information on availability of services and functioning Camp Management Committees (CMC) structures. The successes of indicator achievements will allow for partners to provide critical data and community support which can serve as the foundation for durable solutions programming.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risks related to PSEA/GBV: Through the CCCM cluster complaints feedback mechanism system, the cluster has incorporated guidance from the PSEA taskforce on how to make confidential and structured referrals based on the country’s standards. PSEA trainings for partners have occurred in 2020, and will continue to occur as refresher trainings during 2021 to mitigate the risk of agencies being unable to combat such issues via their internal mechanisms. 2: Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. The CCCM cluster will attempt to collect information regarding partner agency data sharing protocols, and will look to establish a standard for data management that can be followed by CCCM partners. Aspects of this activity’s aim have already been streamlined through the complaints feedback mechanisms with additional work required to ensure that there’s a harmonized standard for data sharing procedures followed by the sector. 3:Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. The CCCM cluster, via its partners, will aim to create self-governing structures at the IDP site-level that are educated on equitable humanitarian service delivery. The objective is to ensure that Camp Management Committees (CMCs) are able to interface directly with service providers and provide feedback related to any insufficient beneficiary targeting. Such issues can also be filed through the site-level complaints feedback mechanism. 4. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area.Through integrating HLP and eviction mitigation activities into CCCM programming such as monthly eviction risk data collection, HLP/conflict resolution trainings for CMC members and referral mechanisms in case an eviction notice has been posted, the CCCM cluster is consciously attempting to provide support to the IDP communities. Moreover, the cluster intends to supply information management/data support to HLP partners to ensure that targeted eviction prevention and mitigation work can occur in high-risk sites. The cluster will seek to mitigate evictions by working with protection partners, local authorities, landlords and communities to find alternative solutions. The cluster will advocate for sustained humanitarian responses for populations displaced by conflictin the instances that durable solutions cannot be found.

Please provide further explanation

The CCCM cluster recognizes the need for coordination and strategic complementarity between humanitarian partners, local authorities and development stakeholders to ensure that humanitarian programming links relief, recovery and development. IOM, as CCCM cluster co lead, will continue working closely with development actors at both national and regional levels to ensure that emergency projects support long-term solutions for both displaced and host communities. In particular, the cluster will create synergies with programming related to the Somalia Durable Solutions Working Group, as well as related consortiums, that aim to complement the humanitarian response with resilience programmes and long-term actions that target IDPs as well as hostcommunities. The cluster will work closely with relevant authorities and community governance structures to ensure that affected populations are informed about options for durable solutions and receive support to attain their plans.
