Project Summary

Cadre Harmonisé” results conducted in Cameroon in October 2021 showed that 1,154,661 people in the North-West and South-West regions are food and nutritionally insecure (crisis to emergency phases) that is, 755,959 and 398,702 people in the North West and South West regions respectively. With 21% increase in food insecure estimates compared to October 2020. 9 Divisions out of 13 are classified as phase 3, Donga Mantung, Bui, Kupé Manenguba and Fako divisions are all classified in phase 2. With continuous humanitarian aid, anticipated peace and next agricultural season running good, it is expected that the number of people suffering from food insecurity would have decreased by March 2022 farming season but remains very worrying. Moreover, the current conflict has plunged the North West and South West regions into a drastic slowdown of economic activities in general, and agro pastoral production in particular
Due to the multiple killings, theft of livestock, destruction of crops and kidnappings perpetrated in rural and urban areas, and with all the trauma associated, many rural workers have left their homelands in search of more peaceful space. The massive shifts in productive workforce has implications not only for the loss of jobs for rural workers, but also on the scarcity of local agro-pastoral products thus an increase in food insecurity and malnutrition of these families. In the current global logic of achieving the sustainable development goals, appropriate humanitarian actions are to be taken into consideration in order to facilitate access to productive assets, preserve livelihoods, combat malnutrition, protect those affected and keep the interest and voice of vulnerable people and to achieve the highest standards of the human rights, especially for women and children.
The unexpected outbreak of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus has spread rapidly across the world, devastating lives and livelihoods. In March 6, 2020 the first case of COVID19 pandemic in Cameroon was declared. As of November 24, 2021, the number of cases had increased to more than 100 000, with 1795 deaths, indicating an exponential growth in the number of cases. The full impact of the virus on food security is not yet fully known. However, it is clear that the direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 is already having significant negative effects on food supply chains, from producers to processors, marketers, transporters and consumers (FAO, 2020). With the COVID 19 pandemic, it is anticipated that nearly seven million more children across the world will suffer from malnutrition, many in Africa (UNICEF, 2020). This might worsen the situation of communities affected by crisis who are already food insecure.
The proposed one-year intervention will contribute to improving food and nutrition security and reducing the vulnerability of crisis-affected IDPs and host communities in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon, especially women, men and children, through the provision of agricultural support. Specifically, the intervention aimed at (i) increase access and utilization of diverse and nutritious vegetables, (ii) increase access and consumption of eggs, (iii) increase access and utilization of mushrooms, (iv) increase access and utilization of snails.

1 - Besoins / Needs

A) Main outcomes :1) 80% of the beneficiary households increase their consumption of vegetable, eggs, mushrooms, and snails in the targeted communities; 2) 80% of the beneficiary households increase their access to vegetable, egg, mushroom, and snail in the communities;3) At least 70% of the beneficiary households improve their livelihoods from selling their production surplus B) Key outputs :1) 3500HHs received seeds of the targeted vegetable and training in vegetable production and 3500 tons of leafy vegetable and 10 000 tons of tomatoes and watermelon are produced;2) 3500HHs received breeding and construction material and training in egg production and 35 000 eggs are harvested per day in the targeted communities; 3) 3500HHs received breeding and construction materials and training in snail production; 4) 3500HHs received mycelium and construction materials and training in oyster mushroom production; 5) 150 local or community-based monitoring personnel mobilized and trained on beneficiaries monitoring;6) 90% of beneficiary households sensitized on COVID 19 preventive measures;7) 90% of beneficiary households sensitized on the importance of consuming vegetables, mushrooms and snails; 8) 10% of the total targeted beneficiary will be persons with disabilities; C) Main activities1) Implementing market gardening made of rapid growth crops: providing seeds of targeted crops (leafy vegetables, tomatoes, okro, watermelon), training in vegetable production and follow up; a two days training workshop will be organized for partners who will then conduct cascade training to direct beneficiaries. Trainings will involve presentations and practical hands-on experience on vegetable production practices. Seeds of targeted vegetables and small field equipment will be distributed to the direct beneficiaries to allow them establish their own garden.2) Implementing sustainable poultry production units: providing veterinary products, and breeding and construction materials, and training in egg production and follow-up; a two days training workshop will be organized for partners who will then conduct cascade training to direct beneficiaries. Trainings will involve presentations and practical hands-on experience on poultry production practices. Breeding and construction materials will be distributed to the direct beneficiaries to allow them establish their own poultry production units. 3) Implementing sustainable organic oyster mushroom production units: providing mycelium, construction materials, and training oyster mushroom production and follow-up; a two days training workshop will be organized for partners who will then conduct cascade training direct beneficiaries. Trainings will involve presentations and practical hands-on experience on mushrooms production practices. Breeding and construction materials will be distributed to the direct beneficiaries to allow them establish their own organic oyster mushroom production units. 4) Implementing sustainable snail production units: providing breeding and construction materials, training in snails production and follow-up ; a two days training workshop will be organized for partners who will then conduct cascade training to direct beneficiaries. Trainings will involve presentations and practical hands-on experience on snails’ production practices. Breeding and construction materials will be distributed the direct beneficiaries to allow them establish their own snails production units5) Promoting the consumption of nutritious leafy vegetables, mushrooms and snails to improve nutrition of the targeted communities; Awareness raising on the importance of vegetables, eggs, mushrooms and snail as part of a balanced die 6) Coordinating, monitoring and evaluating: strengthening of food security cluster 7) Sensitization of beneficiaries households on COVID 19 prevention measures
