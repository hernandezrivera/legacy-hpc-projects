Project Summary

Under this proposed Emergency WASH Interventions for 2024, The Rescue Mission intends to support the people affected by conflict, natural disaster and displacement in emergency context and static as long-term support for the IDPs, host community and people to return, resettle and reintegrate with host community. The interventions are designed to address the emergency WASH needs through rapid response mechanism (RRT) and static interventions which will contribute to improve health, nutrition, education, Livelihood and food security, shelter and protection outcomes and durable solutions. The proposed interventions are to reduce prevalence of malnutrition contributing to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality, provide access to basic WASH services in underserved and hard to reach locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facilities, TERM will respond to the Sudan crisis, flooding and disease outbreak in the priority 2 counties through Rapid Respond Mechanism (RRT) with static interventions in priority 1 counties. The project will seek to enhance supply of sustainable access to safe and clean water (15l/p/d), dignified, inclusive and adequate access to sanitation services and opportunities aimed influencing social behaviour and norms in relation to personal, household, communal and institutional hygiene.
The proposed intervention will reach a target population of 22,222 vulnerable Individuals (Host 8400, IDP’s 6000, Returnees 7822, People with disabilities 250). The activities will include, Construction of new water points; rehabilitation of water points, Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities at Health/Nutrition facilities, Schools and at the community levels; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing; hygiene promotion education through door to door visits, participatory hygiene sessions in public places for effective behavioural change; distribution of hygiene kit to SAM children WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable.
Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of reproductive age group accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions. TERM will ensure ownership and sustainability of the response through involvement of the targeted community throughout the life cycle of the intervention. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, through accountability to affected populations (AAP) , working with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities (PWD), pregnant and lactating women (PLW) incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. WASH structures and infrastructure in the flood prone areas, contributing to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties.
To enhance and ensure sustainability and proper usage of the facilities, TERM will deliberate on comprehensive training to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs), Schools and other institutional staff. Solid waste management training will also be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns.

1. Needs

South Sudan has been experiencing and continues to face deteriorating humanitarian situation, The situation further attributed and worsened due to multiple shocks and unaddressed root causes of need of endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, according to the WASH cluster 2023 Humanitarian Need Overview sectorial analysis, total of 6.15 million people needs access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene facilities, the conditions are worse in Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The main WASH cluster objectives and strategies for 2023-2024 is to Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WASH severity Counties (WSC) 4 and 5 counties.An IRNA assessment reports of Ulang and Nasir Counties (Upper Nile state) on the 20 and 23/8/2023 indicated that WASH facilities in the assessed areas are limited, open defecation has been observed due to absence of latrine facilities, 85% of the host community, IDPs and returnees are defecating in the bush. Assessed locations have poor excreta disposal observed for children and nearby bush for open defecation witnessed). Most of the population fetches water directly from the Sobat River and directly from the Nile in Nasir due to limited boreholes, WASH NFIS including soap jerricans were limited at the household level, The main water sources for domestic use are Sobat & Gilo rivers, nearby swamps and from over flooded hand pumps (Bad taste and water color changes witnessed/smelled for all assessed hand pumps), River/swamps are the available sources of water for 80% of the population which may results in to water born disease outbreak The HNO (Humanitarian Needs overview) 2023, further indicated 42 percent of the population reported do not have access improved water sources to meet their household needs countrywide and 45 percent of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, 49% of the populations / households are relying on surface and unimproved water such as river, canal, stagnant water as their main water sources for drinking and secondary usages,. Water storage and water collection containers are limited and only 16% of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and /or bucket to safely store water. Protection to access the water points is also another concern. According to HNO 2023, the rampant open defecation is further creating public health risks especially in areas where people rely on and using surface water, Sanitation facilities across south Sudan are uncommon access to improved sanitation is below 10% in 56 counties with most extreme percentage in Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states; there has been some cultural barriers to use the constructed latrines is also another challenge to improve people sanitation practices. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. In Upper Nile and Jonglei, access to clean and safe water is below 28%, with over 72% of the water points requiring at least 30 minutes of walking for an average family. This not only raises the likelihood of protection related issues for women and girls, but further encourages populations to rely on nearby open surface water points that are unsafe. Poor WASH standards also affect school retention of adolescent girls, and contribute to GBV, SEA and food insecurity.
