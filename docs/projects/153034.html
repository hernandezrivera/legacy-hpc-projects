Project Summary

The project will provide lifesaving GBV services and prevention of GBV in Nyal, Koch, and Ganyiel targeting 47, 107 (30, 255F, 16,852M) IDPs and host community. In Nyal and Ganyiel, the proposed project will target 38, 225 (16,288 women and 13,937 male) members of the crisis affected population. IRC will promote access to lifesaving services through comprehensive case management, referrals, and individual and group psychosocial support services in women and girls’ friendly spaces (WGFS). IRC will utilize two existing women and girls’ friendly spaces in Nyal and establish three women and girls friendly spaces in Ganyiel for the provision of psychosocial support services, skills building activities, recreational and life skills activities for adolescent girls, group information sessions, and provision of social cohesion and support networks for women and girls. To promote resilience and enhance psycho-social support, women will be consulted and supported with, unconditional cash assistance to improve access to food and basic needs including hygiene and sanitary items and reduce vulnerability for female and child headed households, elderly, women with disability and those that who are chronically ill. The cash will enhance household purchasing power and economic capacities of beneficiaries to access food commodities of their choice and basic needs from the local market. Discussions will be held on power dynamics to mitigate risk factors that may come with cash based support to women for petty business.
In Koch County, Unity State, the proposed project will support 8,882 (5,967 female and 2,915 male) direct conflict affected beneficiaries through the provision of case management and psychosocial support services in an adequate and timely manner through the daily operation of the women and girls’ friendly spaces and through mobile services to Ngony, Patit and Bieh. IRC’s trained GBV Response Officers and Caseworkers will be based in the women and girls’ friendly spaces located in Pachol, Koat, Bargok and Mirmir as well as in Buaw with regular travel to support mobile outreach services in Ngony, Patit and Bieh, and link up with the health partners and other service providers for provision of clinical management of rape services. The IRC will work to mitigate and respond to the needs of women, girls, men and boys affected by conflict by reducing GBV risks through frequent joint community-based safety audit assessments, address risks identified, and strengthen community based support mechanisms, creation of safe and meaningful access to relevant services, and advocacy with other humanitarian actors on GBV mainstreaming. The IRC will also provide material support such as dignity kits and survival kits to the most vulnerable women and girls in Koch and Nyal.
Within the women and girls’ safe spaces, specialized, age-appropriate, group-based support is available to women and girls who fear to come forward to access available GBV services. Women will also be supported with cash for petty business to promote resilience and psychosocial support.
To mitigate barriers such as fear, stigma, and a lack of information on health consequences of sexual violence, which prevent GBV survivors from seeking help, the IRC will implement both basic and more tailored psychosocial support interventions including GBV information sessions, as well as specialized group psychosocial support sessions with a focus on supporting survivors’ healing process from traumatic events. These structured sessions are designed to provide emotional support to survivors, and equip women and girls with improved coping mechanisms, self-esteem, resilience and social support networks. The IRC will also provide tailored psychosocial support activities for adolescent girls to mitigate their vulnerability to multiple forms of GBV, and offer support systems and access to information. This will be pivotal in reducing women and girls’ isolation, and boosting their self-confidence.

Needs

Southern Unity State has been plundered with numerous attacks in the last few months causing displacement of the community. The recent IPC report also indicates that some locations in Southern Unity are at IPC phase 5 - famine. Panyijiar County continues to experience significant food insecurity and acute malnutrition which remains a major public health emergency. Severe food insecurity continues to affect the population as a whole, with internally displaced persons (IDPs) being the most vulnerable. Persistent insecurity and armed conflict have disrupted livelihood activities, affected market functionality and limited physical access to markets especially for traders risking their lives to bring food commodities from neighboring areas. The worsening economic crisis has resulted in high food prices, local currency devaluation and hyperinflation, which in turn eroded household purchasing power. A recent needs assessment conducted by IRC from 15 – 16 August 2018 in Koch County, and reports from the IRC general protection and a safety audit assessment conducted in Nyal, Panyijiar County, show that multiple displacements have continually exposed women and girls to rape and other forms of violence this is exacerbated by major gaps in GBV services in the three locations. Women and girls were exposed to sexual violence by armed groups aggravated by brutality and theft of their belongings. Risk of violence against women and girls remains high for women going to collect food, water and firewood due to the unpredictable security and the consistent presence of armed actors in the surrounding areas. Women reported having developed secondary GBV complications as a result of untimely response to GBV incidents due to poor access to GBV services. GBV Information Management Systems (GBVIMS) trend analysis indicates that rape is an increasing threat and sexual exploitation is becoming more acute as the conflict continues. The increasing protection needs far outweigh the available and limited resources for GBV programming. . Finding money and other resources to take care of themselves and their families is a significant challenge. Women and girls reported being beaten, looted, raped, abducted and tortured when going out to collect firewood. There is a lack of secure or private bathing facilities. There is also a lack of assistance for women’s groups that can help provide networks for mutual support and protection. Based on women and girls’ focus group feedback, there is no available women and girls’ safe space with a counselling room that can provide a safe and private environment for people to receive compassionate assistance in Koch and Ganyiel. No legal counselling services are available to advise survivors of their legal rights and remedies and the process for criminal proceedings. There is only a traditional court system existing in the area with limited and/or no clear procedures to promote dignity and confidentiality for the survivors. The increasing trend in sexual violence, the recent scale of violence and the important gaps identified justify the prioritization of sectoral support for GBV prevention and response in the HRP allocation. The areas identified in this proposal require a robust scale of interventions to ensure violence prevention and service availability.

Activities

• Establish women and girls safe spaces in Koch (3) and Ganyiel (3);• Provide support for women and girls safe spaces (2 in Nyal and 3 temporary structures in Koch) to host and support psychosocial support (PSS) activities;• Conduct focus group discussions, safety audits and community mapping assessments with women and adolescent girls to identify GBV risk prevalence in the community• Hold coordination/advocacy meetings with humanitarian actors to ensure needs for women and girls identified are prioritized by other sectors such as WASH, NFI and shelter• Provide women and girls’ cash assistance for risk mitigation support materials to cover basic needs for example (including dignity kits, second hand clothes, and cooking stoves). • Provide age appropriate case management/psychosocial support services and referral services through a holistic and coordinated approach for GBV survivors, women and girls• Conduct case management review meetings and follow up bilateral meetings with service providers • Update and establish functional, safe and immediate referral pathways for GBV survivors and update them regularly based on changing contexts• Ensure GBV survivors access the women and girls safe spaces and receive appropriate comprehensive care according to their needs, including referral for medical and legal protection (where available) and support costs (transport, hospital/laboratory fees, pharmaceuticals)• Provide training to case managers at local NGO’s/CBO’s to enable them to provide quality case management and psychosocial support services to GBV survivors;• Train local women’s groups on provision of psychological first aid and referrals;• Train local service providers on GBV, referral pathway and GBV guidelines;• Identify and train community outreach teams of staff/volunteers• Conduct sensitization and training sessions on gender and GBV related topics targeting religious leaders, traditional leaders, women’s leaders, local authorities and parties to the conflict• Establish a feedback mechanism for provision of services by IRC, including distribution of risk mitigation materials and PSS services. This will include client satisfaction surveys, quarterly listening sessions with women and girls, and quarterly community group discussions• Support women with cash for petty business to promote resilience and psychosocial support
