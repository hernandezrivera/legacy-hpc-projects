Project Summary

Under this Proposal Fazaa will build upon its experience of working in Education sector in Syria since September 2013, to provide safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for 4553 crisis-affected children and youth ;and to support 192 staff in Northwest Syria.
The proposed project will provide access to formal and non- formal education to the most at risk children in Idlib Governorate (Ariha and Al Ma’ara districts ) , targeting 4,553 children from (5-17) years – 2,195F and 2,358m- and 192 staff -101 f and 91 m- . The action aims at reintegrating dropout boys and girls into formal Education pathways. Vulnerable children including adolescents girls and children living with disabilities will be prioritized in this action 50 children with disabilities will be targeted).
The project will focus on achieving three specific goals (in line with the Education Cluster goals):
Education Objective 1: Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth including those with disabilities (aged 5-17 years)
Education Objective 2: Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5-17 years) within a protective environment.
Education Objective 3: Strengthen the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence based education response.
Access strategies will include small-scale rehabilitation of damaged schools and establishment of other safe temporary/alternative learning spaces through a gender-sensitive approach. Fazaa will also focus on improving the access to quality education services for IDPs in informal camps ( 688 children : 314f , 374m and 35 staff : 9f , 26m ) and within communities ( 3,865 children: 1,881f , 1,984m and 157 staff: 92f , 65m ); and ensure that these services are sufficiently linked to Child Protection interventions including the integration of psychosocial support and recreational activities for vulnerable children, particularly in areas of high severity of need because both teachers and students suffer from stress and psychosocial disorders due to the protracted nature of the crisis; and teachers require support to address their own and their students’ psychosocial support needs; and prioritizes strengthening the capacity of the education system to deliver and sustain education investments; and establishes school-based governance and accountability mechanisms to support school operations.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year has forced 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. As for Idleb governorate, 853,292 school-aged children and 17,777 teachers are in need of education assistance, as indicated in 2019 HNO. Children and youth accessing formal and non-formal education are not experiencing quality teaching being significantly affected by the shortage of qualified teachers, shortage of incentives coverage for teachers and the educational personnel, shortage of education advocacy by the community. There are growing concerns that the toll of the crisis in its many forms is preventing children from attaining foundation literacy and numeracy skills as well as those skills relevant to cognitive, social and economic empowerment. Having been exposed to the psychological trauma of hostilities, displacement and family upheaval generated by the crisis, many children and teachers are affected by psychological trauma. The vulnerability of school-age children with no access to learning opportunities has increased over the almost eight years of the crisis, putting children at higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violation. As mentioned in ACU report on school camps in the North of Syria, " One of the main difficulties encountered by students in obtaining a proper education at schools in camps was the shortage of educational materials, books and stationery."Ma'ret An Nu'man sub-district is hosting 60,958 IPDs and has 41,525 returnees. A for informal camps, there are 34 informal camps in the sub-district with approximately 23,000 IDPs - 58,584 children and 1220 teachers in need.Ariha sub-district is hosting 34,719 IPDs and 427 returnees. As for informal camps, there are 2 informal camps with approximately 2700 IDPs in the sub-district. The sub-district has 46,413 children and 967 teachers in need. On the community level, as per HNO 2019, there’s 3023 school-age children in need in Orm Eljoz,1887 in Kafrlata and 1292 in Motaram and 752 in Kafr Najd.Fazaa will support 3 informal camps (2 in Ma’ara and 1 in Ariha) reaching 688 children ( 314f, 374m ) and supporting 35 staff( 9f, 26m) in need ; will also support 8 formal schools in Ariha reaching 3465 children ( 1681 f, 1784 m ) and 157 staff( 92f, 65m) in need in Ariha. Additionally, 400 youths (200m, 200f) will have access to life skills trainings.In the needs assessment process, Fazaa communicated with the Education Assembly in Ariha district to reflect on the educational needs in this area and Hama Education Directorate to name the unsupported informal camps in Ma'ra. Consequently, Fazaa assessed the needs of 8 schools and 1 informal camp named by the local council of Ariha and 2 informal irregular camps in Al Ma'ra. Fazaa assessed the schools taking into consideration the highlighted needs in 2019 Education HNO and HRP. Fazaa also conducted 126 perception surveys with the community ( 64 children 32F, 32 M , 56 adults 21F, 35M), and interviews with education focal points in the local councils in Ariha and Ma'ra. As Fazaa has an ongoing ECW project in Ariha and Al Ma're, the findings of the monthly reports (4 Ws, narrative reports, M&E reports, coaches reports) have been taken into consideration into the needs assessment process. For a better coordination and no duplication, Fazaa communicated with Big Heart Organization that also supports some schools in Ariha.The targeted schools eed partial rehabilitation, improvement or constructing gender-sensitive WASH facilities. The assessment also showed shortage of textbooks and learning materials -approximately 56% of the children don’t have textbooks while children in camps don’t have textbooks at all, very poor learning and teaching environment, and very poor access for children with disability.

Implementation of activities

Objective 1: Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years).● Fazaa will coordinate with Idleb Directorate, Education Assembly and local councils to provide formal education to 8 formal schools in Ariha sub-district ( Ariha,Motaram, Orm Eljoz,Kafr Najd and Kafrlata ); and non-formal education (remedial classes, ALP and BLN) in the same 8 formal schools in Ariha sub district and 3 camp schools in informal camps (IDPs camps) in 2 communities :Ariha (Motaram) and Al Ma'ra ( Ma'arrat An Nu'man - Aldan).Will conduct joint 6 back-to-learning campaigns. The campaigns will be conducted on the community level for better outreach. Fazaa will activate the role of community in this campaign by establishing Parents- Teachers Association that will participate in the BTL campaign in the selected locations.● Establish tented schools in 2 camps and provide rehabilitation and WASH support in 8 formal schools and a school building in a camp. Provide fuel for 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools.● Provide education supplies and teaching materials for teachers and children, in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools.● Provide vulnerable children with transportation to school: children with disabilities and children living away from the school and are at protection risk to come to school on foot and alone, in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools. Objective 2: Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5-17 years) within a protective environment● will implement a teacher’s support system. In this system, Fazaa will build the capacities of teacher coaches who in turn will train, supervise and support teachers to promote a safe, friendly, inclusive and participatory learning environment for children. It will include trainings such as the INEE, teachers in crisis context, positive discipline, learning difficulties and disabilities, child centred learning and teachers’ follow up and support, in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools.● Teachers and education personnel will be paid as per the salary scale that is being developed by Fazaa according to the existing stipends in the area.● Will provide recreational activities in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools..● Will provide a life skills program for the youth ( 12-17 years) in a life skills centre providing languages and computer courses; life skills and citizenship workshops that enhance their resilience such as communication skills, leaning management skills, time management skills, personal interaction skills and peace education. ● Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools. ● Provide children and teachers with school-based psychosocial support activities through facilitators who will receive special trainings on PSS, in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools.● Provide textbooks to children : Fazaa will participate in providing textbooks in formal settings by 50% ● Provide children with supplementary learning materials in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools. ● Provide children with recreational materials in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools. Objective 3: Strengthen the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence based education response · Build the capacity of education actors( Education Assembly and local councils) in Ariha and Al Ma're sub-districts on EiE and INEE MS, and on advocacy of the needs and rights of children in crisis contexts. · Support or establish school-based governance and accountability mechanisms to support school operations by establishing Parents- Teachers Association consists of : parents, teachers, educational personnel and students, in 8 formal schools and 3 camp schools.
