Project Summary

The impact of climate change, communal violence, flooding, economic crisis, insecurity and displacement across the country has severely disrupted the education system and impacted the school aged children. The adolescent boys and young men being vulnerable and most affected are the adolescent girls and exposed to increasing protection risks and are subjected to early forced marriage as well as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
Education cluster estimated 3.4 million girls and boys between 3-17 years of age as in need of education and over 42,000 teaching personnel needed to support provision of education to these learners.
Approximate of 54% girls and boys between ages 3- 17 years in conflict- and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2023 to 2024 including teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. The learners in need being approximately 857,000 children 3-17 years old; 53% % girls, 4,330 teachers and 4,330 SMC members and PTAs in areas affected by conflict, displacement or facing acute food insecurity levels including the project HRP2024 focused counties are supposed to be reached with various education services.
The closure of these schools due to the mentioned drivers not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affected and hindered children’s access to essential basic needs and services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services.
The current estimated number of out of school children is 3.4 million with child protection cases increasing as well as the number of pregnant learners increasing in some schools. The schools are the key institutions to identify and refer children requiring protective services and case management to mitigate gender base violence threats.
Most schools in the project focused counties has no strong referral mechanism in place and Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In 2024 HRP project focused counties Wau, Aweil South and Tonj North active conflict and recurring communal violence led to school closures
Floods displaced learner’s boys and girls as well as occupation of schools by communities during floods and WASH facilities in schools destroyed. In adequate learning spaces and school supplies. Most schools do having full learning cycle in terms of all classes. Schools are located in far distances from the community making learners not to access them.
By mid-2023, a total of 251,613 which is 47% female learners were reached through access to education, provision of school supplies and other services while about 1,609 teachers 22% female learners benefited from trainings such as pedagogy and subject specific pedagogy, life-skills and curriculum and materials, ECD trainings, and Approximately, 3,162 32% female PTA/SMC from among the community education committees and local education officials were trained in school management.
The HRP 2024 education project targeting 3003 children ages 3 to 17 boys and girls as beneficiaries dis aggregated by gender and age girls 801, boys 776, youth women learners 750 and youth men learners 676 including those with disabilities where most of schools in the focused counties were destroyed, damaged, closed and children being forced out of school based on different drivers.
The project will prevent and address Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, with a PSEA communication strategy and appropriate complaints mechanism. The HRP 2024 project will have ZERO tolerance on protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse based on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy.
The project will ensure that girls and boys learners including people with disabilities have access to feedback and complain mechanisms so that correct action can address specific protection and assistance needs.

1. Needs

Climate change impact, conflict, economic shocks and flooding has affected schools and damaged school infrastructure which made schools inaccessible especially for the most disadvantaged. Due to the floods in prone areas and conflict affected s counties such Aweil South County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal states and Tonj North in Warrap state disrupted education infrastructure especially the WASH facilities at schools affecting reproductive age school girls who are more likely than boys to be excluded from education.The increasing climate change, food insecurity, economic shocks and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education teachers and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. These barriers include lack of regular teacher salaries, damaged or non-existent school infrastructure, learners’ inability to pay costs associated with schools, hunger, significant household responsibilities and marriage and pregnancy. Teachers and members of school management dis aggregated by gender committees are in need of humanitarian assistance. Lack of trained PTA and SMC to manage school development plans as well as mobilizing learners to go to schools is among the priority needs affecting learning. Lack of qualified education authorities at state and county levels to enforce learning outcomes in schools, Lack of trained teachers in schools which is a priority need for effective learning.By mid-2023, a total of 251,613 which is 47% female learners were reached through access to education, provision of school supplies and other services while about 1,609 teachers 22% female learners benefited from trainings such as pedagogy and subject specific pedagogy, life-skills and curriculum and materials, ECD trainings, and Approximately, 3,162 32% female PTA/SMC from among the community education committees and local education officials were trained in school management.School going children girls and boys between 3 and 17 years of age in conflict and crisis affected areas including the project focused counties Wau, Aweil South and Tonj North in western and Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap states are estimated to not have access to per-school, primary and secondary education in 2024.Aid Support Community Organization has its geographical presence in Western and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states implementing education program in Wau, Jur River and Aweil South Counties.

2. Project activities

The project will scale up the response in the focused counties to meet the priority needs of targeting 3000 beneficiaries male and female learners, the vulnerable host Communities, returnees, and displaced children by conflicts mostly constitute of the number of school aged children boys and girls including adolescents aged 3-17 with access to education in emergency as well as build their resilience and the number of preschool children aged 3-6 with access to play and early childhood education shall be given priority. The project will consider and navigate the activities across all three sect-oral objectives for 2024 targeting 3000 children ages 3 and 17 boys and girls as beneficiaries where most of schools in the focused counties were destroyed, damaged, closed and children being forced out of school based on different drivers. Objective one: Improve equitable access to safe and. protective education services for 857,000 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth girls and boys.The following are the activities that project will work on under cluster objective one: • The project will ensure to establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms• Rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms.• Establish gender segregated latrines, water points in schools.• Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials especially hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc. and PPE for students and teachers in schools. Objective two: Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the well-being of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments.• The project will provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education.• Training and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including child protection as well as Gender Base Violence, health and nutrition.• . Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents. Objective three .Strengthen or enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency on learners, teachers, and communities.• . Train SMCs, PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psycho social support PSS and Disaster Risk Reduction climate change.• . Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication IEC materials i.e. posters, leaflets.• Support conducting back to learning campaigns• Support technical and institutional capacities of national actors, including finance, procurement, HR, MEAL, governance and other areas.
