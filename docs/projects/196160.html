Project Summary

CARE proposes an integrated approach to gender-based violence and sexual and reproductive health that will reach 33856 people living in Camps 11, 12, 14, 15, 16 and Jaliapalong Host Community in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The project activities are informed by CARE’s current rapid gender analysis and operational expertise and are adapted to meet the differing needs of men, women, boys and adolescent girls, person with disabilities and gender diverse group. CARE will provide GBV response and prevention services through 9 WGSSs and a multipurpose center. Women and girls will be provided with GBV case management, PSS, recreational activities including resilience building and empowerment programming in WGSSs. Whereas through multipurpose center men and gender diverse populations will have access to GBV services. Further, in coordination and collaboration with WASH team access to MHM kits will be ensured and for better livelihood opportunities GBV team will coordinate and collaborate with DRR, WASH and SMSD team and sectors. GBV services will also be provided through CARE’s health posts in camp 12,13 and 14. Similarly, in WGSSs and multipurpose centers basic SRH information and services will also be provided. Referrals will be updated and used in coordination with protection sector. Additionally, through community mobilization women, men, boys and girls, PWD and gender diverse population will be reached with proven strategies, to address negative social norms that fuel GBV among the population. CARE will work with Community Outreach Groups (COG), men’s group and adolescent girls, women, transgender group to improve their access to information and change service seeking behavior. Further, CARE will integrate Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) approach to identify the barriers for women’s participation in decision making process. To avoid backlash, CARE will conduct awareness sessions with men, community leaders (Majhi and Imams) and wider community. CARE will coordinate with CiC, protection actors, community leaders to promote social cohesion while implementing the project.
CARE as protection and GBV lead in camp 16 will conduct protection risk assessments, update referral pathway, build capacity of PERU members and protection and GBV actors. In any kind of rapid emergencies, CARE will mobilize PERU team and ensure protection services immediately. CARE and partner staff will be trained in GBV basics, GBV minimum standards, case management, PSS/PFA, referral and PSEA. GBV safety audit will be conducted and program will be informed on the emerging protection and GBV concerns. Further, to reduce the GBV risk and identify and refer GBV cases safely and confidential manner WASH, SMSD, DRR and SRH team will be trained on GBV PG.
CARE will continue assessing how well the program is meeting quality and standards, accountability commitments as well as the adaptation of programs based on learning. A systematic process will be followed to capture learning and reflections. Programming will be implemented, informed and driven by current sex, age and disability-disaggregated data, ensuring activities meet the differentiated needs of direct and indirect beneficiaries. Based on the trends identified, the programmatic emphasis will be placed on offering services, support, and resilience building tools for women girls, men, boys, PWD and gender diverse population. Activities will be undertaken in accordance with protection policies and will address the unique needs of vulnerable populations. CARE will continue its strong coordination with GBV SS, GBV IMS, Case Management working group and other relevant working groups to ensure harmonized, quality and collaborative GBV services. Collaborative lobby and advocacy will be conducted at different levels in coordination with GBV SS to improve provision of GBV services and transform discriminatory gender norms.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

August 25th 2022 marks five years since the massive displacement of 742000 Rohingya refugees to Cox’s Bazar. The refugee population has increased to 913660 where more than half of the population are women. According to a study by REACH,2018 minimum 12% of Rohingya refugees in camps and host communities are people with disabilities. Over the last five years, the needs of Rohingya refugees and host communities have changed from emergencies to protracted needs. However, the complexities and challenges in ensuring the availability, accessibility and acceptability of the GBV services has not reduced. Both host communities and Rohingya refugee camps the GBV and protection concerns are increasing affected by hazardous events. According to the inter-agency Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) reports, intimate partner violence (IPV) has been identified as the overwhelmingly dominant form of gender-based violence reported in Cox’s Bazar. Average of reported IPV incidents in 2022 recorded around 85%. The increase in percentage of reported incidents in survivors’ residences (92%). Because most of the women and girls have less access to information, coupled with insufficient provision and institutional barriers, they often choose not to seek aid when faced GBV or any type of abuse. Inadequate GBV outreach activities and social stigma result in under-reporting of sexual abuse, and IPV cases. The report illustrates the need to strengthen the security and safety of these locations through awareness and prevention mechanisms. According to Inter Agency research report 2020 by UNHCR, CARE & Action Aid, Rohingya girls and young women are particularly vulnerable to child marriage, sexual abuse and trafficking. Women and girls, particularly those living with disabilities, and Hijras, are vulnerable to physical, psychological and sexual harassment and abuse. Due to refugees’ heavy reliance on aid, there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers in the camps and situation will get worst with significant reduction on aid. Women and girls in polygamous marriage situations are particularly at risk of experiencing IPV. Married girls are at higher risk of IPV than their unmarried counterparts in the camps. The movement of teenage girls in the camp is also constrained. After puberty, adolescent girls' access to education and life skills activities is constrained due to their fear of experiencing sexual abuse and human trafficking. Women and girls are also facing GBV while accessing family planning methods due to deep rooted gender inequality and religious believes. Further, men said there is a lack of PSS services for men (CARE’s Rapid Gender analysis, 2022). Person with disabilities and gender diverse populations are still marginalized. Restricted access to the majority of services and stigmatization leaves gender diverse population more open to abuse and exploitation (FGD with transgender group). Lack or limited service provisions combined with less diversified services is another reason GBV survivors are not seeking support which is contributing in normalization of the gender-based violence. There is a gap in understanding the concepts of protection and gender among some key humanitarian actors, including a lack of regular and systematized sensitization on rights-based and refugee rights issues. Most of the service providers including Government agencies being male dominated and gap in understanding the life-saving protection/GBV services and not considered as one of the life-saving humanitarian services creates delays in Government approval process-ultimately challenging the safe and dignified access to GBV services. Overall coordination on gender-specific protection issues in the response still requires improvement.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Currently, CARE is providing GBV prevention and response services through 12 WGSSs in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Six out of 12 WGSSs are being implemented by local partner Mukti Cox's Bazar. Remaining 6 WGSSs which CARE is directly implementing in camp 16 and host communities Jaliapalong and Haldia Palong. 2 WGSSs in Haldia Palong will be discontinued due to a fund gap. Aligning with GBV SS's rationalization, CARE will transform 1 WGSS in camp 16 into a multipurpose centre to increase access of men and LGBTIQ population to GBV services. Further, in collaboration with SRH team, CARE will provide SRH services through its WGSSs and GBV services through CARE's health posts. Regarding MHM/dignity kits, CARE will coordinate with WASH team and ensure the kits for GBV survivors. In terms of predictability and continuity, CARE has secured funding till June 2023 for 6 WGSSs operational under a local partner. For the remaining 3 centers there is a plan to apply for CARE Germany fund.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) in all interventions, CARE Bangladesh has established and incorporated a robust Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in all humanitarian projects of Cox’s Bazar. This system consists of various solicited and unsolicited channels to receive information, recommendation, feedback, opinion, question, complaint, or a request from beneficiaries, stakeholders, staff, volunteers, government agencies, other organizations, etc. CARE has ensured that all affected community, regardless of sex, age, disability, race, caste, or color, has equal access to the safe and responsive CFRM. The CFRM team responds to almost all problems received at the earliest possible time based on the type and priority of the feedback. The Accountability Focal persons shares CFRM reports with different donors and projects in ad hoc or specific time intervals like weekly, monthly, quarterly. CARE has its own Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for CFRM.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will aim to support the search and rescue/DRR volunteers, GBV/Protection workers in the camps including individuals and communities affected by sudden hazardous events, disasters in relation to their mental health and well-being. It is evident that mental health and psychosocial impacts of emergencies and climate change is a vital part of humanitarian operations program as individual may react in diverse ways, from relatively transient and minor stress reactions to significant distress and psychosocial impairment. Additionally, social stigma can lead further stress, therefore awareness raising sessions will incorporate the DRR, climate change related messages. Capacity building of CARE and partner staff on minimum first aid requirements and waste management in coordination with WASH and DRR team will be conducted. In solidarity with the Government’s plastic bag free campaign, CARE will promote cloth bag in this project interventions.
