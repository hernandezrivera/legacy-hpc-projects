Project Summary

In this project, Violet aims to provide lifesaving health and medical interventions in Idleb governorate through the following health facilities:;
G070036-Aljamiaa, G0705035 Mohambel,G0705052 KafarYahmoul,
Through the above mentioned health facilities and services, the community will have access to primary health services and referral service comprise:
- Ensuring Access to Essential Primary Health Care Services
- Integrated Health Service Delivery
- Capacity building
To contribute to the stationary aspects of the project, a mobile network of community health and community outreach workers will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities to deliver key messages to community members with special focus on pregnant and lactating women, communicable disease and non-communicable disease and air borne diseases. With this component the project aims to increase the community awareness of the COVID 19 and Cholera symptoms, means of transmission, and prevention measures including hygiene promotion (HP) messaging in northwest Syria.
Throughout the life of the project, Violet will insure proper coordination with the Health Cluster and WHO and the all relevant TWG to be sustained mainly in determining the needs and locations of the proposed activities, which will contribute effectively to the success of the project and properly implementing it. Also coordination with other specialized centers and PHCs operating in the area supported by other NNGOs to insure proper referral for relevant cases will minimize risks related to patient lives.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protracted emergency characterized by over ten years of ongoing hostilities and the resulting long-term consequences, more than 15.3 million of population in Syria, approximately a 3.2 M increase from last year, are estimated to be in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023. According to HNO 2022: 14.6 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelters and with limited access to basic services. In NWS, 4,6 M population (2,8 IDPs out of them 1,8 M are living in camps) out of the total population there are 4,1 M in need and 3,1 M are food insecure people. HNAP recorded 19,545 new internal displacements in north-west Syria in September – the highest record so far in 2022 - largely driven by the deteriorating economy The first cholera case in north-west Syria was confirmed on 19 September. The outbreak is spreading fast and as of the 4th of October, 13,059 suspected and 900 confirmed cholera cases including 60 associated deaths (CFR 0.46%), have been reported from 13 of 14 governorates in Syria. These additional crises continue to overstretch the already weak health system and have direct consequences on vulnerable communities and patients, increasing morbidities and mortalities.

4 - Implementation of activities

All supported health facilities will provide free of charge preventive and curative services. In the supported PHCs, VO will ensure providing primary health services aligned with WHO essential Primary Health Care Package: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS and nutrition. Supported facilities will receive payment of salaries for staff (medical and non-medical), monthly operational costs, professional training and monitoring, provision of pharmaceuticals, and other medical and non-medical commodities (including equipment, medicines, and consumables). MEAL activities include a) daily visits to the facilities and observation notes, b) patient satisfaction surveys, c) AAP, d) monitor number of visitors/patients, e) conducting verification checks and f) exit interviews with patients. The team will prepare the IPTT, observation reports and means of verification. Plus uphold the AAP mechanism and learning.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excluded Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection communicated through the AAP team. Service mapping to inform about alternative services. 2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from services or facilities. Likelihood: Low; Impact: High Information sharing, services and facilities are suitable for at-risk groups, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion. 3) Risk of GBV to/from/in overcrowded health facilities Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Selection of suitable locations and facilities, referral of special needs cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, provide child care services, suitable service times, guarded facilities. 4) At-risk group do not receive needed services Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Coordination with sectors to avoid duplication, collaborate with protection and GBV teams to ensure access, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection. 5) Risk of COVID-19 Upholding WHO recommended measures, provision of relevant information to BNFs, increased GBV response to mitigate domestic violence as result of economic pressure on the family.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded facilities and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation if needed. Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Staff infected with COVID-19 do not exceed 2% at all times. - Community acceptance for this project continues. - Current conflict doesn't affect program activities. - Border crossing from Turkey is maintained. - Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line. Risks: Unstable security situation: VO regularly monitors the local context, and employs security focal points. VO staff complete mandatory security trainings. Lack of the health services quality: VO has a health technical team who will be responsible on following up the quality of the health services provided in the health facilities as well as link the staff with the updated international guidelines and protocols. Moreover, a training plan for the staff will be set in coordination with health facilities teams and the staff needs. Complaints mechanism is already set in the health facilities and the patients can raise any issue regarding the quality of the health services and will registered and directed to the relevant department to deal with. the Cross-border resolution is not renewed: VO will advocate to the importance of the cross border operations to the people in NWS and will shed the lights on the humanitarians need that still exist in the are
