Project Summary

To address the identified gaps, the project will improve immunization coverage by supporting the outreach and supplementary immunization services to remote areas and communities including migrants and IDPs; conduct regular nationally representative household surveys to confirm reported vaccination coverage levels; capacity building and staff training on the newly updated guidelines for immunization, delivery, and Vaccine management; supporting cold chain management especially at the district and peripheral levels and maintain regular and timely procurement of vaccines on Comprehensive multiyear plan (cMYP).

1 - Needs

Despite the challenges and difficulties that the country is undergoing the national immunization program of Libya has developed capacities. This has been evident by the high vaccination coverage rates as well as the number of facilities offering the immunization services. According to the official report Libya has been declared polio-free since 1991, and no cases of tetanus were reported since 1993. The country has a national compulsory vaccination program since 1972 and currently included several vaccines in its vaccination programs. Based on official and administrative data sources the national vaccination coverage rates is above 95%, however, there was no nationally representative household survey within the last 7years to confirm this report. The large size of the country coupled with the uneven distribution of its population is posing a challenge to the national vaccination program. Additionally, the internal displacement together with illegal immigration is becoming a great challenge to maintain satisfactory vaccination program and a delivery system which might increase the possibility of outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases such as measles. Furthermore, a low vaccine stockpile caused by the lengthy procurement process, irregular electricity, and power sources that might affect the storage of certain vaccines, absence of immunization guidelines at immunization facilities and shortage of trained staff are hindering the overall readiness of the primary healthcare facilities for immunization.
