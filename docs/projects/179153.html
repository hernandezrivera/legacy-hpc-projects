Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria since 2013, Oxfam continues to work with local authorities in responding to the vulnerable populations’ WASH needs in districts/subdistricts of Aleppo, Hama, Idlib, Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Quinetra and Deir Ezzor, based on assessments, severity of needs and in response to further emerging needs in NES and NWS. The subdistricts that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by conflict and require WASH support. Based on HNO, the subdistricts targeted by OXFAM for education interventions, 12% has 5 PiN severity score, 53% has scored 4 and 35% has scored 3. OXFAM conducted assessment of schools’ WASH conditions in 2020 to date covering over 80 schools. To address the catastrophic education conditions, the project is designed and aligned to the Education sector’s objectives, 1) Availability and access: increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth, 2) Quality: enhanced quality of formal and informal education for children and youth, and 3) Resilient systems: education systems and communities are better able to support a timely, coordinated and evidenced-based education response.
OXFAM intend to meet specific lifesaving and resilience needs around water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in schools. The response is focused on children from the formal and non-formal primary and secondary education; in new returns, displaced and host communities; affected by COVID-19, economic issues with persisting WASH needs in schools and communities concurrently. It is gender and protection mainstreamed, overarched by community engagement and child centered approach suitable to access and security challenges; implementation engages school authorities and trusted community structures with strong collaboration with other actors. The target locations proposed are according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of school children populations in need, adapted to the evolving ground context, access and people's movements.
The type of interventions strongly aligns with the education sector’s SO3 which captures the sector efforts to respond and assist in restoring WASH services for schools. Accurate disaggregation of population data will be collected from relevant ministries and actors. Oxfam has a unique MOUs with several ministries gaining trust, strong relations and approval of interventions. Coordination meetings at the Ministry of Education allow insight and agreement for selection of prioritized possible activities and discussion of operational challenges. This project’s close alignment with the HRP in terms of outcomes, activities and methodology is a reflection of Oxfam’s close engagement with the Sector, particularly through strategic discussions and information sharing throughout 2019 and 2021

4 - Needs

According to HNO, 2.45 million children were out of schools in 2020 and 1.6 million were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and at risk of dropping out, is almost to increase; 94% of all school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme, or catastrophic education conditions; an increase to last year’s 86 per cent. The compounding impact of the 2020 situation results to students either behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out. Students face health risks while attending schools exacerbated by COVID-19. It is difficult to ensure physical distancing or ventilation in overcrowded schools, together with a limited availability of operational WASH facilities.Oxfam assessments to over 80 schools in five governorates bared most schools were heavily damaged by war, high rates of IDP students against existing and functional WASH facilities, inaccessible to disabled students, insufficient taps for drinking and handwashing, toilets are not gender separated; sewage, solid waste system, water pipes and tanks are broken and non-functioning. COVID-19 safety measures are ineffectively complied having no soap and water for children for handwashing and no disinfection materials to keep the school in hygienic condition. Many schools cannot be considered safe spaces to teach and learn, continues to be compromised by health risks, unsafe, lack of privacy and access to WASH facilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

OXFAM’s education humanitarian and resilience response will implement the following activities in schools: 1) Availability and access: 1.7e: support to rehabilitate gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities and services in schools & learning spaces; 1.7g: provide solar power systems to learning spaces and WASH facilities; 2) Quality: Provision of Education WASH supplies: 1.8b: provision of PPEs, hygiene and disinfection materials; 3) 2.6a: Awareness raising on health and hygiene for students, teachers and education personnel, and Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs) or parent engagement initiatives supported or established.In close coordination with the WASH, Education and Protection sectors, the target communities are selected based on extent of damage in the WASH facilities, high school children population and vulnerability, poor coping mechanisms, affected with COVID-19 and other prevalent and emerging diseases, returnees and affected residents and IDPs. Oxfam will establish baseline, monitoring &end line studies, during &post monitoring, evaluation, children and wider community satisfaction and participation in decision making on selection of schools, and ensuring voices of girls and boys those marginalized are heard. Oxfam will aim to target communities with large voluntary returnees, addressing social cohesion. OXFAM will submit report to the sector’s 4W monthly in line with the indicators monitoring of input, output and limited extent of outcome focused; including other practical information to report monthly. OXFAM will regularly monitor the outcomes and impacts of interventions.All project data will be desegregated to gender, disability, age, geographical and community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

OXFAM's humanitarian programming has established various tools for conducting risk analysis during planning and monitoring: Oxfam Minimum Standards for Gender in Emergencies, Gender&Age Monitoring tool,Humanitarian Gender Action Plan are reinforced with capacity building &compliance for staff and volunteers. Activities include: multisector and multi-level rapid gender analysis to inform our programming and advocacy, Gender and GBV/Child Protection training, developing partnerships and strengthening coordination with the Protection Sector including Child Protection and GBV Sub-sectors. Oxfam engages with specialized agencies (UNHCR, UNICEF and UNFPA and their local partners) to gain better insight on protection risks as well as define Oxfam’s role in supporting referral pathways. Oxfam integrates conflict sensitivity analysis in selecting activities and locations, updates guidelines and gives awareness training to staff and volunteers including Child Protection, Safeguarding (PSEA) and fraud, protection risks and vulnerabilities. Oxfam engages communities in a conflict sensitive manner, cautious not to exacerbate tensions, particularly among women-led initiatives, wherever possible.Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate to its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle and staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming, risk assessments for prevention, mitigation and management measures with emphasis on addressing sexual exploitation and abuse, aid, diversion, durable solutions & conflict sensitivity.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk:Tension develop between communities recipient of WASH in schools infrastructure rehabilitation &communities who will not be reached by these interventions. Probability:High. Mitigation: Selection of schools with least or compromised access to WASH services, are using the resources of/consultations withthe neighboring communities;explaining the selection criteria,highlighting how the intervention will ease extraction from their water resources&improve sewage functions;&coordination with WASH actors, local authorities & community leaders. Risk: Parents and students may resent girls' participation in the hygiene and menstrual promotion sessions for going against expectations or norms that menstrual is taboo. Probability:High.Mitigation:Ensure sessions contents &methods are consulted to parents, students and school authorities,organise parallel gender discussions with them;develop allies among parents,male members of the school&community to help in consultations concerning the activity; ensure that sessions are separate male&female & held in private school venues.Risk: Increase risks of GBV if toilets are not separated or have no locks on the doors & windows.Probability:High.Mitigation:Consult with girls on location&design of toilets; ensure locks on the door and windows; &separate male & female toilets. Risk:Unstable inflation&exchange rates,complicates spending&expenditures in SYP.Risk:High.Mitigation: Explore options used by other actors for this challenge,pay vendors in tranches to keep suppliers interest&that neither Oxfam nor the supplier is affected by the exchange rate fluctuations.
