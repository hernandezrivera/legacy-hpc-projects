Project Summary

Multiple shocks continue to exacerbate the WASH situation of vulnerable communities in Greater Tonj. Poor WASH services contribute to increased malnutrition especially amongst under 5s, PLWs, as well as food insecurity, disease burden, protection issues which affect the well being and standard of living.
The project aims at providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases targeting the returnees, host community and IDPs. Interventions will include safe water provision through water rehabilitation eg; hand pumps or provision of household water filters and water purification tablets. Others shall include provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure including latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management. Hygiene promotion will be prioritized through hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions like nutrition centers. Proposed interventions shall also include capacity-building of community pump mechanics, water-user committees, community hygiene promoters, and distribution of WASH NFIs soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, and PUR among others.
The project will integrate WASH in Nutrition to provide caretakers of SAM/MAM children at OTP/SCs with famine prevention kits. Due to the area being disease prone, a WASH in health response is planned for outbreak prevention and response as well as risk reduction. The project will also train nutrition and Health workers on WASH minimum package for SAM and MAM Admissions.

1 - Needs

More than half of the populations in Greater Tonj lack access to safe clean water, due to the long conflict, displacement, destruction of infrastructures, and health, education and WASH facilities, with women and girls being the most affected by lack of access to water and sanitation. Less than 29% of the Greater Tonj population has access to a borehole, tap stand or a water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns, while the remaining have to walk long distance to access safe water or rely on surface water/unprotected water sources (HNO 2020). More than 90% practice open defecation due to lack of access or cultural behaviors, while only 13% of the population has access to WASH items. The increased of displaced population, both static and in transit, has exerted pressure on already limited resources among the host communities hence limiting the coping abilities to withstand the shocks. WASH and health needs are high in Greater Tonj especially pastoral areas, where poor access to latrines, safe boreholes and health facilities increase the risk of waterborne diseases. Widespread open defecation poses a high health risks, particularly considering cholera hotspots. More so, Greater Tonj has a global acute malnutrition rate of 16.7%, indicating a critical need to provide WASH services at OTP/TSFP centers and provision of famine prevention packages to SAM/MAM households. There are few operational boreholes in rural areas, destroyed by the conflict/not properly maintained, as well as perceived lack of access to safe water points This has caused reduced per-capita water consumption (under 10 L/person/day), increased use of unprotected water sources, increased risk of waterborne diseases, increased time for water collection at the expense of other essential tasks, increased conflict over communal resources, and higher GBV risk (abduction of young girls and boys, forceful marriages, killing, rape). Most displaced households in Greater Tonj are in dire need of basic hygiene kits (including water storage containers, water purifiers, soap, etc), due to displacement, looting and deterioration of items, lack of access to the markets and little purchasing power. Hygiene practices, and especially hand-washing at critical moments are reportedly low across the counties. This increases risk of cholera and EVD outbreaks and highlights the need of spreading preventive hygiene messages and establishing hand-washing facilities. High rates of open defecation in the road sides and bushes poses a great health risk and a security concern to women, girls and the community at large, while lack of MHM Kits for women and adolescent girls of reproductive age (limited by lack of functional markets or purchasing power by these marginalized groups), pose sanitation and protection concerns that need an immediate remedial action. The critical health needs, insufficient food access, high cases of SAM and GAM, and poor WASH services may contribute high levels of morbidity and mortality if not responded rapidly. Inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased SAM cases. Lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices contributes to poor feeding habits of children leading to malnutrition. Data from IOM, OCHA and partners revealed that displaced persons are settled in areas without clean water or improved sanitation, and have little opportunity to practice proper hygiene in what turns out to be flooded areas for most of the year.

2 - Project activities

• Rehabilitation/repair and development of new water points. Rehabilitation will focused on the water points that are most beneficial to the community in terms of access. This will be consulted with women in FGD. Consideration will also be given to water points at institutions – such as health/nutrition sites. Locally recruited pump mechanics will be trained and water quality analysis will be conducted. NuSS will focus on the inclusion of women in the mechanical training, with the target of at least 30%. In order to ensure the proper maintenance of water points, water user committee will be established and 60% of the committee members will be women.• Provision of solar lamps for HH to reduce GBV risks related to WASH. Provision of solar lamps, dignity kits and soap will be targeting HH most at risk of GBV. Targeting will be done jointly with GBV partners and may include GBV survivors. Targeted beneficiaries will include IDPs, mothers to SAM screened children, among others will be provided with vouchers for soap which will cater for three months’ supply of soap. • Distribution of WASH NFIs. NuSS will rely on core pipeline for the items to be distributed. The targeting criteria will be defined and eligibility criteria for identifying beneficiaries will be used. • Protection training and awareness raising will be provided to community hygiene promoters, pump mechanics water user committee, women groups and general community leaders. • Community hygiene promotion activities through house to house visits, sessions, campaigns will be carried out at community and institutions like schools, health and nutrition centers. • Conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practices (KAP) baseline survey, to understand access, privacy needs, dignity, cultural practices, disease transmission, and capacity of the community to develop relevant coping strategies. • Training of 50 nutrition team members in 5 OTP/TSFP sites on minimum WASH standards.• Distribution of household famine prevention packages to caretakers of SAM/MAM children• Rehabilitation of 5 boreholes at OTP/TSFP sites to provide water for appetite tests and domestic use.• Rehabilitation of 5 gender sensitive latrines, and construction of other 5, including provision of 10 hand-washing facilities, at the OTP/TSFP.• Trainings (2) of WASH staff on PHAST/CHAST and Child to Child (C2C) approaches for hygiene promotion.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Project Staff will inform IDPs and Host communities through community gathering about PSEA and Nurture South Sudan’s strong commitment to its PSEA policy. In the gathering, staff will put major emphasis on dissemination of key information such as “humanitarian assistance is provided for free; no one should pay or establish sexual relationship with project staff to receive assistance or get employ”. Nurture South Sudan will also encourage project beneficiaries to report any case of, or relating to PSEA in practice to senior staff or through community leader to Nurture South Sudan as well as to staff of UN member organization in reach.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Nurture South Sudan in its community engagement action plan considers working with locals who speak native’s language and live with target communities. The plan of having community members on board as project staff enables the organization to communicate effectively with beneficiaries in local dialect through translation which encourage and ease beneficiary communication needs. From project inception through to implementation, Nurture South Sudan also organize separate tailored meetings for women and girls, men and boys to capture their specific needs. This approach of holding separate meeting with each group enable particular group (either being males or females) to interact freely with project team as an interest group with specific demand to be met by the project. Nurture South Sudan will invest in outreach activities which are meant to encourage participation of beneficiary with mobility challenge like person living with disability, bedridden sick elderly and person with critical illness, first time pregnant and lactating women who face challenge in balancing household chores and participation in community engagement meetings. All of the cited groups benefit from outreach activities that alternatively engage and involve them in emergency FSL activities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure an in-built feedback mechanism. These Feedback mechanisms will be shared with the vulnerable community members during inception meetings. Other informal channels like; periodic community meetings- with key informant interviews and Periodic focus group discussions with the vulnerable community members to seek their views and concerns. NuSS also uses the regular post-distribution monitoring (PDM) which provides additional opportunities for collecting feedback from the vulnerable community members. Through awareness raising to the vulnerable community members, they are requested to use office visits to raise their questions and issues with staff. At the office level, the program manager is responsible and is the focal person for handling complaints, while at the community level, project committees will be trained on how to report suspected cases and complaints to project managers in each location of implementation. Project managers, community mobilizers at the field level will be focal persons for report issues of concern.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Nurture South Sudan will document beneficiaries’ feedback and study them during weekly activities review meeting to learn and address any challenge that has potential to lessen project results. The outcome of weekly activities review & beneficiary feedback will inform future planning and adaptation of revised approach to meet dignified need of project beneficiaries.
