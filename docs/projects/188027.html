Project Summary

In 2022, protection issues remained widespread, with 97% of covered communities reporting the occurrence of at least one protection concern for one or more population groups. Despite a steady increase of protection interventions, responses and services throughout the NWS, the magnitude of protection needs still outstrip response capacities. Although protection issues like child labour preventing school attendance, child recruitment, domestic violence, early marriage, economic exploitation, explosive hazards, family separation, harassment, housing, land and property issues, kidnapping, lack/loss of civil documentation, sexual harassment and sexual violence are still witnessed in NWS. As majority of the assessments have been reflecting that the protection issues are more prevalent in camp settlements and rural communities. In camps settings, a variety of protection services is still needed to address acute and complex protection needs of all population groups. WHH is planning to provide special needs funds (SNF) support to compliment other sectoral intervention which does not require complex protection risks but focuses more on the vulnerability criteria. It aims to mitigate the impacts of war/conflict, forced displacement, and poverty on the most vulnerable people by providing support to cover their urgent and special needs through services, in-kind assistance, and financial assistance to meet their lifesaving needs when there is when their needs are not met through regular programming or the individual cannot pay due to the economic hardship and social disruption caused by war and forced displacement. 4 - Needs

According to Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment Report, prepared by NWS Protection Cluster, the movements restrictions are very high (58%); lack of documentation (20%); child marriage (34%); followed by applying some coping mechanisms like relying on humanitarian assistance, local/community support, restricting movement of women and girls, begging, engaging in illegal activities. At the camp level, the usage of coping mechanisms are higher than other areas due to economic downturn. Therefore, SNF assistance will be provided to individuals who - who are using negative coping strategies (children dropping out of school to work, early marriage, child labour, begging and involving illegal activities) and cannot be covered by the regular programming. The vulnerability of the people in Afrin is still ongoing as almost one out two people are IDPs. WHH in evaluating a case mainly takes into consideration the risk that the case is facing and the individual’s vulnerabilities and capacities as well as the impact that the provision of the SNF can have on the life of the assisted individual.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches offundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuseof those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values.It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassmentof our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations.Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.
