Project Summary

This is a project aimed at improving households food access and utility in Western Bahr el Gazal, parts of Warrap and Aweil through immediate food assistance to the vulnerable households; emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support ; and enhanced household resilience toward food insecurity via dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. 1 - Needs

According to the August 2019 South Sudan IPC analysis, food security has slightly improved during the 2019 lean season compared to the same time last year due to improved security conditions, which have permitted greater household movement and access to fish, wild foods, and markets. The proportion of the population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the May-August lean season declined from 59 percent in 2018 to 54 percent in 2019. Although food security has slightly improved, high levels of acute malnutrition have endured. Nutrition data collected during the 24th round of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS R24) classified global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ) in 58 counties as ’Serious’ (GAM WHZ 10.0-14.9 percent) or ’Critical’ (GAM WHZ 15.0-29.9 percent), which are indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4), respectively. Based on a rapid assessment of Juba markets conducted by FEWS NET in late July and early August, relative improvements in security have served to open major trade routes and this is increasing both domestic and regional commodity flows. In August, the retail price of white sorghum in Wau was 128 percent higher than in August 2018 and 326 percent higher than the five-year average. This is driven by informal taxes and continued fluctuations in the U.S. Dollar (USD) to South Sudanese Pound (SSP) exchange rate. In former Western Bahr el Ghazal State, an estimated 440,000 people (68% of the State population) are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse, up from the 280,000 people (54% of the State population) in August 2018. In the former Warrap State, a decrease in insecurity resulted in a noticeable decline in the number of people experiencing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity compared to August 2018. However, the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity remains similar to August 2018. In former Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, the food security situation has remained largely the same with a slight increase (54% in August 2018 to 59% in August 2019) in the proportion of households experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This project will target 15,000 vulnerable households in Jur River county of Western Bahr el Gazal, Gogrial West county of Warrap state and Aweil West of Northern Bhar el Ghazal. The project will focus on assisting the beneficiaries to improve their nutrition and food security through crop and vegetable production, improve hygiene, economic empowerment, access of water for irrigation and domestic use and buildan environment of peaceful coexistence in the community. These interventions will be implemented in strict adherence to and in line with FSL cluster strategic objectives that include; a) Provide food assistance, b) Enhance emergency food production and c) Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs. The beneficiaries will be identified as per the cluster strategy of ‘targeting based on vulnerability’ and not blanket geographic targeting to support livelihood protection and restoration. The proposed interventions are also in line FSL cluster 2020 objectives. To ensure that gender market principles are adhered to, all targeted beneficiaries shall be dis aggregated by; gender, age and disability.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will provide a high quality, accountable and dignified response, which takes account of the needs and capacities of children, women, men and vulnerable groups. World Concern has adopted the seven (7) Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations, which means that World Concern meaningfully engages affected people at every key stage of their work (assessment, design, implementation and monitoring). To ensure this is achieved Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) committees shall be formed and trained to handle feedback, engage with beneficiaries and offer localized solutions. The community will be engaged in selecting project the demonstration sites, training centres and community projects and in monitoring and evaluation process. The AAP committee will receive any complaint from the community and engage with the community based volunteers who shall address the issues and escalate appropriately. The project will provide poster in local languages with key messages. During distributions information regarding the beneficiary entitlements will be displayed and beneficiaries explained before the start of the activity and lockable suggestion boxes will be provided and the key maintained by the community liaison office. An information desk will we established in the office and a dedicated telephone line dedicated to receive any feedback from the community. The line will be handled a dedicated community liaison officer who is not part of the implementing team. The liaison officer will receive the suggestion boxes every week and any other feedback and record in a feedback book and follow through to ensure what needs to be addressed feedback is provided. The feedback shall be updated and reviewed by manage.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

This project has components that will promoted the use of improved cooking technologies that World Concern has introduced in Western Bahr el Gazal and hope upscale through this project into other location. this will significantly reduce the amount of firewood and charcoal used hence help in environmental protection.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project will seek to have durable solution to displacement in this project. These activities shall be guided by second objective of “Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan of 2017” that state;“Given the comparative advantage of all stakeholders involved, the framework is conceived as tool to: a) consolidate peace through integrated humanitarian and development support; b) strengthen fast track planning and implementation of relief and reintegration activities; c) guide the prioritization of resources and return areas; and d) facilitate dialogue between Government and relevant stakeholders, including IDPs, local communities, civil society, humanitarian and development actors, UNMISS, and the international community.”To minimize disturbances due to conflict The project will initiate a cohesion committee in the community that involve different actors in the community. The committee members will include women, men, boys, girls, children, community chiefs, and the county administration to discuss about peace and inclusivity and peaceful coexistence. The committee will be trained and together with WCDO identify the triggers of insecurity within the community and come up with possible solutions to the sources of conflicts. Throughout the project cycle the committee shall hold meetings atleast once every months to evaluate action points towards peaceful coexistence and for accountability on agreed actions.
