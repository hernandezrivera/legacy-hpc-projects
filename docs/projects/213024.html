Project Summary

Needs: according to the HNO 2024, over 14.9 million in Syria are estimated to be in need for primary and secondary health care assistance, out of which 5.1 million displaced persons as per the HNO 2024
Also after the devastating 6th February 2023 earthquake out of the total 585 health facilities assessed by the health cluster in NWS, 15% (97) health facilities were reported damaged [23 fully damaged and 71 partially damaged],which affects the access to health services for the population of NWS. Additionally, 532.595 live birth expected and 4.1 million women of reproductive age require health care assistance to access essential reproductive health services, including antenatal care, safe deliveries, postnatal care, family planning, and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care.
Districts classified as severe (3) & and extreme (4) in the health sector severity have increased from 89 and 113 in 2023 to 122 and 118 in 2024, respectively.
Intervention and Implimentation of activities: In this project, Violet aims to provide lifesaving health and medical interventions in Idleb and Aleppo governorates through 1 CEmONC facility named as "G0704002 ! Ain Al Beda FH" hospital in Ain Albida, nutrition rapid response teams distributed in five locations in addition to an ambulance network into which 4 ambulances are linked in two locations across both governorates to ensure access to quality lifesaving and live
sustaining health and nutrition services.
Through the above mentioned health facility and services, the community will have access to primary and secondary health services comprise:
1) comprehensive sexual and reproductive health
2) pediatric care service out and inpatient.
3) referral network operating on 2 different locations in Idleb and Aleppo Govs
4) nutrition rapid response team distributed in five location across both governorates
5) technical training for health staff including CHWs
Through the hospital at the heart of the project, Violet will work to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality through provision of ANC, safe delivery, early initiation of breastfeeding, and PNC. To uphold health standards, Violet will make sure that all the
midwives and nurses are trained on IYCF concepts and best practices with special focus on early initiation of breastfeeding and skin-to-skin contact, including within the first hour after delivery. To contribute to the stationary aspects of the project, a mobile network of community
health and community outreach workers will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities to deliver key messages to community members with special focus on pregnant and lactating women, communicable disease and non
communicable disease and air borne diseases. The community work is complemented by an ambulance network led through two
emergency centres. The currently 4 ambulances are referring patients from their accommodations to health facilities as well as between health facilities. The referral networks supported by Violet is working and will continue to work in coordination and under the umbrella of the local referral networks for both governorate (Idleb and Aleppo referral networks). Also, the nutrition rapid response team will focus on screening, follow ups, treating MAM and SAM cases and counseling sessions at community level, and referrals the complicated cases to the hospital and other stabilization center in the area of operation. Throughout the life of the project, Violet will insure proper coordination with the Health Cluster and WHO and the SRH TWG and other working groups to be sustained mainly in determining the needs and locations of the proposed activities, which will contribute effectively to the success of the project and properly implementing it. Also coordination with other hospital operating in the area supported by other NNGOs to insure proper referral for relevant cases will minimize risks related to patient lives

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk analysis :1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excluded Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection communicated through the AAP team. Service mapping to inform about alternative services. 2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from services or facilities. Likelihood: Low; Impact: High Information sharing, services and facilities are suitable for at-risk groups, conduct sessions to inform about requesting special assistance for PWD/elderly/pregnant, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to/from/in overcrowded health facilities Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High selection of suitable locations and facilities, referral of special needs cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, provide child care services, suitable service times, guarded facilities.PSEA Strategy: Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b)integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c)appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d)support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasize on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded facilities and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation if needed.Risk, assumption and mitigation measure:Assumptions: -Community acceptance for this project continues. - Current conflict doesn't affect program activities. - Border crossing from Turkey is maintained. - Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line. Risks: Unstable security situation: VO regularly monitors the local context, and employs security focal points. VO staff complete mandatory security training . Lack of the health services quality: VO has a health technical team who will be responsible on following up the quality of the health services provided in the health facilities as well as link the staff with the updated international guidelines and protocols.
