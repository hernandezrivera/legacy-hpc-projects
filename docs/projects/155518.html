Project Summary

The proposed project will help to ensure the protection of the most vulnerable children ( girls and boys) victims of exploitation and violence such as separated and unaccompanied children, orphans, children victims of disabilities, children with serious or chronic diseases, children associated with armed forces or army groups, children in the worst forms of work and other children at risk and victims abuse and neglect such as the one in need of birth certificate including new born, in situation of early/forced marriage. Children who do not go schools/school dropout. All of those children will received case management and referral services according to their identified needs. In coordination other humanitarian such as ICRC, UNICEF, UNHCR, Identification, Documentation, Tracing and Reintegration (IDTR) services will be provide for separated children. The proposed project will also focus on the production of birth certificate under regular and late procedures for boys and girls in Mayo Sava and Logone and chari division. Identified vulnerable children and children in exam class will be targeted by civil documentation activities in order to avoid the risk of statelessness and to facilitate children access to social services such as Education and Health. Male and female, girls and boys, traditional and religious leaders, president of woman and youth groups of Logone et Chari and Mayo Sava where the highest number of IDPs, off site refugees and returnee are registered will be involved on protection committees in order to make child protection activities sustainable and to reinforce their appropriation by communities. These committees will contribute under the leadership of Minas social centers to the promotion of right of children via sensitization session, identification and referral of vulnerable children. Government technical services and communities leaders will be at the center of all of those strategy and protection mechanism.

Needs

The protection response in the Divisions of Mayo Tsanaga, Logone & Chari and Mayo Sava is largely insufficient with relatively weak capacities of state-run protection facilities. The 3 divisions have 6 social centers in total. In Logone & Chari, there are 3 social centers with 4 staff. In Mayo Tsanaga, there are only 2 social centers with 5 staffs while in Mayo Sava, there are 4 staff. The 13 existing staff are not necessarily trained on case management and psycho-social support. This number is largely insufficient to cover the estimated 260 635 vulnerable (HNO 2019) children in need of protection services within the 3 divisions. 65% of IDPs in Far North are under 18 years old (DTM round 15 - 2018). The displacement of people due to insecurity has negatively impacted the children's ability to have a "normal life" as children. Girls and boys separated from their parents or guardians and other vulnerable children facing a greater risk of economic exploitation and other forms of violence and abuse. According to the Delegation of social affairs of Mayo Tsanaga, from January to October 2018 about 2865 cases of child abuses have been registered including early and force marriage, child exploitation, child labor. Children also continue to face on birth certificate issues prohibiting their studies. Indeed, about 73 100 pupils are concerned among whom 4576 of Class 6 are exposed to risk of school dropout because of the absence of birth certificate. They are 10 247 pupils for Mayo Tsanaga while this number is 4354 for Mayo Sava (regional delegation of MINEDUB). 12 out of 16 classrooms were destroyed in the district of Amchide. 3000 children of school age are identified by the community in Amchidé. 3 schools including a high school are closed in Limani. (UNICEF- Maroua sub -office humanitarian needs assessments report – May 2018). Only IMC provides Child Protection services in these two localities. 260 unaccompanied and separated children with 100 girls and 160 boys as well as 37 children (18 boys and 19 girls) associated with army groups were supported, between June to December 2019, in these localities. Among these children aged between 3 and 17, only 70 were enrolled to school (60 in Limani and 10 in Amchidee). The need is immense and the risks of abuse on boys and girls are extremely high. The lack of civil documentation due to the loss of birth certificates during the population displacement or the non-declaration of children at birth exposes girls and boys to abuses, statelessness and worst forms of work including recruitment into armed groups or forces, sexual exploitation or hazardous work. Due to traditional beliefs and mindset that prevail in Far North in general and in target areas in particular, suspicion and mistrust characterize opinion makers resulting in timid involvement of community leaders in child protection mechanisms. This puts in question sustainability and ownership of different interventions being implemented despite the inevitable transition towards development promoted nowadays. Appropriate approach is needed to address those cultural obstacles and lay a path for continuity of efforts and gains obtained so far beyond humanitarian aid.
