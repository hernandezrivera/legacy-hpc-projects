Project Summary

Through comprehensive CCCM response and services, IOM will improve living standards, strengthen accountable life-saving service delivery and provide a path for durable solutions for conflict affected IDPs in sites, site-like settings and in host communities in the Northern provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa. In response to the ongoing, complex response in the region, CCCM services need to to be scaled up through on-site and mobile approaches, supporting IDPs and partners through service monitoring, community engagement and sustainable site-planning and development to ensure minimum standards are met and displaced populations are living in dignified conditions as the context evolves. CCCM will play a great role in maintenance of site infrastructure through more intense community engagement to counter vandalism and continued site upgrades. Rapid response to newly displaced populations will be facilitated by coordinated reception centre management. CCCM response and services will ensure the integration of a strong gender equality perspective.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IOM CCCM plans to target 253, 926 people (116, 803 males, 137 123 females and 38, 086 People living with disabilities ) in need through a full CCCM response includingsite planning and improvement, community engagement, information management,coordination of services, and capacity building. With the ongoing displacement in thenorthern regions of Mozambique and the evolving context, displaced populations remainin dire need due to heavy congestion in the temporary sites exposing Internally DisplacedPersons (IDPs) to further risks. Displaced persons living outside of sites are equallyvulnerable as host communities are overwhelmed and traditional social protection nets arenot able to meet the needs of these many displaced people. With its expertise in siteplanning and development, IOM has led several site assessments across government identifiedpriority areas to establish service areas with improved living conditions fordisplaced communities. IOM will continue to expand its capacity to develop sites thatmeet improved standards while also capacitating local governance structures to ensure aprincipled response. In 2021, through meaningful community engagement and communityparticipation, inclusive governance structures in sites and site-like settings have increased in theirinclusivity through women and disability inclusion committees engaging with leadership,equipping these structures to support the community and service providers while ensuring adaptedprotection and social cohesion. Complaint and Feedback mechanisms which have been set up since 2021 in line with protection principles and regular capacity building of governance structures,partners, and government counterparts will continue to be expanded to cover new sites. In hard-to reach areas or in areas of new displacement, mobile teams will provide coordinationsupport and advocacy for improved services as well as support reception management ofnew arrivals. IOM CCCM will activate the Urban Displacement Out of Camps (UDOC)approach in specific districts and areas where there are concentrations of IDPs living inthe host community. IOM will expand CCCM coverage to newly accessible areas. As CCCM Cluster lead agency, IOM will also take on the responsibilityof CCCM provider of last resort. IOM will engage in regular CCCM partner and serviceprovider meetings to ensure response consistency across sites and between actors,bringing these meetings to district and site level as appropriate. CCCM cluster will engage with the GWG to implement a strong gender equality perspective to the CCCM response.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Output 1: Strengthen the role of women, youth and People With Disability in community governance structures in relocation and temporary sites through community engagement and capacity building. Ensure AGD mainstreaming across camp governance structures. All IDP representatives actively participating in site governance structures will undergo Protection/GBV/PSEA training. Output 2: Continue to expand the implementation of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM). Enhance access to CFM for populations who have mobility challenges, different rates of literacy and disabilities. Ensure all site residents have the information they need in an adapted way to safely access services and make informed decisions regarding their displacement.Provide updated demographic and site information to partners on a regular basis.Provide site level coordination to ensure predictable and effective service provision in sites.Ensure inclusion of IDPs living outside sites in the response through Urban Displacement and Out of Camp approach.Facilitate multi sector first line assistance for newly displaced populations through enhanced reception center management.Output 3: Coordinate and carry out site planning with government and humanitarian partners to identify viable land for new sites and agricultural land;Expand capacity building trainings with government technicians on site planning and demarcation. Implement site infrastructure. Ensure that damaged shelters are fixed, drainage is in place and functional, implementation of accessibility works for PWDs, women and men. These should ensure safety and be a physically, socially and culturally appropriate for residents, women and men. Provide training and ongoing technical support including on-the-job supervision to CCCM partners for camp management, site planning, information management, community engagement and registration.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In order to ensure none of the CCCM project activities and modalities contribute tofurther discrimination of certain marginalized groups, a gender responsive assessment will be conducted todetermine the dynamics that exist in the community including factors exacerbatingmarginalization and gender roles and dynamics these affecting conflict. CCCM team will promote meaningful representation of marginalized communities and discriminated groupsin decision-making and community governance structures (i.e. Community DisabledCommittee; Proportional Representation among Block Leaders; bilateral consultationwith leaders of newly arrived communities). Through CwC, Camp Management willensure marginalized groups are reached with all public adapted awareness messages,information on project activities and concerns regarding access to services. With theaccessible and inclusive complaints and feedback mechanism most marginalized groups will beable to address issues of exclusion/lack of information, and lack of representation.Prior to any CCCM intervention in new sites, Camp Management teams will regularlyconsult with different actors including community leaders, state and local authorities including women,OCHA and partners regarding camp administration and land usage to ensure that thedisplaced population will not be forcibly evicted from the site. Where returns women and men areaffected by housing, land and property (HLP) issues, Camp Management will link themwith HLP actors for assistance.CCCM will participate in inter-agency safety audits to identify gender-based violence (GBV) risks and address related issues. In addition, Camp Management will collaborate with protection and security actors for the development of community based protection measures sensitive to gender, age and diversity that enhance the safety ofwomen and girls reducing their exposure to sexual violence including proper siteplanning, infrastructure, and site care and maintenance interventions to reduce GBVrisk (i.e. Solar lighting, elimination of dead-end corridors, better designed publicspaces) and engaging with women’s governance structures, representatives, and IDPsdirectly to identify and address safety concerns. Through the GBV referral pathwaysCamp Management team will be able to refer GBV cases to GBV actors for assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

IOM will use CCCM Cluster tools to conduct gender responsive rapid assessments engaging with women and men in different age groups from the target population to ensure that the project is in line with their differentiated needs. Through the project implementation, inclusive and adapted Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and CBCMs in sites and site-like areas, and the reinforcement of the national Linha Verde contact number, will be used to ensure that feedback is considered as the project progresses. In addition to this, the creation of community committees inclusive of women and strengthening of preexisting leadership structures both inclusive of women will provide an additional means for the affected population to hold IOM accountable. Camp Management will promote the meaningful representation and participation of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, certain sub groups of women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses in community structures and CCCM forums to raise issues of exclusion, stigmatization and access constraints to services. Messages and meetings minutes will be context specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity translated and shared via notice boards keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. The project contains a PSEA training element to ensure that IOM and committees receive PSEA training and are aware of reporting structures, including Linha Verde. Adapted SEA information will be provided through posters and audio messages in public spaces and engagement through the community committees and townhall meetings as required.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Baseline sex and age disaggregated data for the project is disaggregated to show disability. IOM will work within the project to ensure that people living with disabilities are meaningfully represented in community structures, such as through a Disability Inclusion Committee. The project will use assessments to understand the main barriers to inclusion of PWDs and understand how or support can help to overcome these. IOM will work with the Disability Inclusion working group to in development of IEC materials that are related to PWDs. In site design, IOM CCCM team account for structural elements to support accessibility such as bridges, ramps and advocate for adaptations in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities. IOM will provide mobile gender balanced teams that will conduct household visits to capture the concerns PWDs through CFM. IOM teams will provide response to PWDs through household visits on issues raised through CFM. Information sharing by CCCM teams will take into consideration access to information by persons with literacy challenges and PWDs who have mobility, visual or auditory challenges.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Site planning is crucial to reduce the risk of creating a negative impact on the environment. Through thorough assessment of the area prior to implementation, the site planning teams will be able to adjust for environmental degradation of resources such as water sources and trees. In addition, the interventions will use locally sourced materials wherever possible to reduce the transportation requirements and to ensure that materials are appropriate for the context. Labour based interventions inclusive of women will be prioritized reducing use of fuel and increasing community meaningful participation by the affected populations in the sites. Community Engagement throughout the project will ensure community meaningful buy-in to environmentally friendly practices through community committees.
