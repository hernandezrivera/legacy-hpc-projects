Project Summary

This project implementing in Alhol and Roj camps, Shadadeh community in addition to detention centers in NES, this project will focus on Strengthen the preparedness of social welfare providers, caregivers and CP community mechanisms to support the (re)-integration of CAAC and IDPs and Addressing CP, education and psychosocial needs of CAAC and IDPs , through this intervention, the NGO will Support the psychosocial needs of mothers and caregivers, Mitigate the health and psychosocial risks of children in Roj camp and Alhol camp, provide NFE for boys and girls, including those with disabilities aged 3 to 15 covering basic education and pre-school education, provide social emotional learning sessions, where boys and girls develop important skills that support them inside and outside classrooms, in addition to GBV prevention and risk mitigation services, in this regard three CFS in Alhol cmap (annex) will provide protection activities to children and their caregivers, tow CFS IN Roj camp -extension and main - in addition to women friendly spaces (1 in main and I in extension) with PSS activities , Arabic lessons and first aid training- IP assistance for female adults above 18, while CM services will provided in two ICC in Hol camp and annexes and Shehadeh community, while children and their caregivers in detention centers (Houry, Halat, Alaya and Halat women prison) will receive youth resilience program and PSS services, and regarding education activities in Hol camp annexes 1,3 this project will support (2TLS,2ECCD ) in addition to 2 secondary school and 2 CFS in ph-4 and ph-7 and 3 ECCD , in addition to 1TLS and 1 ECCD in PH-7 will supported starting from 1st of April 2024,while in Roj camp a TLS and ECCD will established in in addition to vocational training for women, finally this project will provide emergency response for any expected Turkish incursion through NES.
Need assessment :There are currently over 32,000 children held in Roj and Al Hol camps, and nearly 1,000 held in detention Centres, including in Al-Hasakeh, Derek, Al-Raqqa, Qamishli and Shadadeh. Many have been detained for years, with no legal basis for their detention and no clear pathway for reintegration in the future . Children in detention facilities face multiple risks, including psychosocial needs, overcrowded and unhygienic conditions, often with various types of sicknesses and wounds with very limited access to treatment. They also lack opportunities for self-development and in some settings even the most basic access to recreation.-Child labour also impacts adolescents’ ability to attend school - children of 11 years and above in Al Hol often work to support their families whilst young girls are left to take care of young siblings in tents. Lack of support for adolescents has consistently been raised during focus group discussions (FGDs) that the NGO conducted with caregivers and children during internal monitoring and third-party monitoring in the main camp.
Al Hol hosts 13,802 HHs for a total of 49,831 individuals. Over 63% of the population is below the age of 18 and 46% below the age of 12 and insecurity and COVID 19 rapidly exacerbated the pre-existing challenges. Al Hol continues to be subject to high levels of violence, murder, and social tensions, which disproportionately affect children, limiting their ability to access education and protection services. This is predicted to have severe psychological and emotional effects with lasting risks to the health and development of children. It was estimated in late 2022 that only 47% of children accessed education, in facilities that do not provide certification and the NGO remains among very few actors providing education services in Al Hol.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NA Continued: Women, children, and adolescents in Roj Camp are from more than 40 different countries. Overall, the camp houses around 773 HHs comprising of 2,502 people, approximately two-thirds of whom are children under the age of 18. Protection and PSS services have been consistently reported as gaps, particularly in the extension area. Almost 30% of school-aged children are not attending school in Roj Camp (30% of girls and 25% of boys) and only 50% of children between 3 and 5 have received early childhood education. Children's physical, cognitive and social development is jeopardized by the deprivations they live and the risk of negative coping mechanisms, like early marriage and child labour, and risks of recruitment to armed groups and sexual exploitation are high. Halat, Houry, and Alaya detention Centres hold 18 girls and 169 boys, as of June 2023. The centres are characterised by limited access to psychosocial services, education, material and human resources. For all the children in the detention facilities, there is no access to legal services, and concerns regarding proper nutrition and access to regular and diverse food options remain. Based on the interagency assessments, urgent need for fresh food, recreational/creative materials as well as gaps in education and PSS need to be addressed as priorities. MEAL: The MEAL capacity and depth of data collection will vary significantly between the target locations due to the access limitations. However, where possible the (MEAL) team will track progress on project indicators monthly, with all data disaggregated by sex and age and where possible, along with disability status. And, where appropriate, will collect data through online databases, to ensure a higher quality of collection and analysis of data; paper-based data collection will be used only if most appropriate for the context (both in terms of sensitivity using online data collection). In addition, also there is like a planning for lessons learned workshops to better tailor programming based on best practices and lessons learned. Protection risks: Boys and girls, including those with disabilities, who have experienced and report protection concerns such as exploitation, neglect and abuse, including GBV, will be provided with age- and gender- appropriate services to aid their recovery and reduce impact of the violations on their wellbeing and development. Education and CP integration: the project will target the benifited children OF project ED and CP activities in all project location which mean same childern who recieve education services will be targted for CP intervention. Coordination : the NGO has taken on leadership in various roles within the cluster system, with Education Working Group (WG) Coordinators based in North East and North West Syria. The NGO actively participates in the Protection WG, the Child Protection WG the GBV Task Force, MHPSS TWG, the CP Case Management Task Force, and the CAAC Task Force.
