Project Summary

Unicef stated that the crisis in Syria has taken a devastating toll on education; leaving over 7,000 schools damaged or destroyed and about 2 million children out of school. Many of these children are the most vulnerable, including those recently displaced by insecurity. According to HNO, an estimated 6.9 million people (97 per cent children) need humanitarian education assistance. The intervention aims to fill the gaps in education in northwest Syria. School-age children dropping out of schools, as 28% of school-age children have never entered schools due to lack of schools, educational equipment, and the inability of displaced people and vulnerable families to bear Education costs, girls, women and children are at risk due to the lack of protection means in school buildings, as well as children with disabilities suffer from access to education services, as most schools are not equipped with tools and aids for people with disabilities (such as bathrooms, toilets, etc.), and there are no incentives for teachers, as around 5,165 teachers work on a voluntary basis in 565 schools in Idlib Governorate.
The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. Many students continue to learn in unfavorable and/or unsafe learning environments. There is one practical classroom for every 178 school-age children in Idlib, and more than a third of schools work in shifts with triple and quadruple shifts in Idlib to address overcrowding and distance.
The purpose of the project is to increase school enrollment rates, maintain the retention of children in schools, provide them with the means to ensure the continuity of their education, improve the quality of education, and strengthen the educational system through the implementation of the following activities:
 provision incentives to teachers and education personnel (male/female)
 provision of school supplies to children (3-17 years, girls/ boys), School/ teachers (female/male)
 provision personal protective equipment to (3-17 years, girls/boys, female/male)
 provision teaching materials to teachers/facilitators (male/female)
 provision school maintenance and repair, building gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities.
 provision solar power systems.
 provide school feeding materials for students (3-17 years, girls/boys).
The activities will target areas where there are large gaps, according to the cluster reports. The following areas where the number of PIN not reached in 2021 as following:
Idlib:

To facilitate access to education services for people with disabilities, the following activities will be implemented:
- Building latrines for people with disabilities, and rehabilitating the demolished ones
- Building ramps at school entrances.
- Installing armrests on stairs to help people with disabilities
- Providing Assistance tools for people with disabilities (glasses, wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, etc.)

4 - Needs

Eleven years or crisis affected the education sector greatly, many schools have stopped for several reasons, partially or completely destroyed due to the bombing. Schools are located in areas classified as high-risk, population displacement. The use of schools as a shelter and housing for the displaced, the lack of educational equipment, the lack of trained teachers, the inability of the parents to bear the costs of education, etc.), Idlib Education Directorate says that there are 18 destroyed schools and close to the lines of engagement, 76 occupied schools before the displaced, access to 177 schools was also lost at the beginning of 2020 due to bombing. According to HNO, ninety-four per cent of all school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions mainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb Governorates, an increase to last year’s 86 per cent. The education system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. Many students continue to learn in unfavorable and/or unsafe learning environments. Directorate of Education in Idlib states that 57% of the latrines cells are out of work and need maintenance.As for feeding programs, education equipment and stationery, 98% of the schools did not have meals available for students, and 64% of the schools did not distribute stationery to students. 23% of schools suffer from the lack of heaters and their accessories, and 90% of schools are not covered with fuel for heating.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented according to the following methodology:- Conducting a comprehensive needs assessment for the educational sector in northwestern Syria to identify the gaps and work areas in coordination with the Education Cluster.- Coordination with local authorities and organizations operating in the area to avoid repeat beneficiaries.- Determining the needs and launching the purchase operations in accordance with the procurement policy of Islamic Relief and in accordance with the policy of the donor.- Choosing the target schools in coordination with the education directorates and local authorities.- Carrying out recruitment operations in coordination and partnership with the directorates of education- Monitoring and evaluation all activities since project design to close the project. - Submitting periodic reports to donors of education cluster and documenting all activities in accordance with the documentation policy approved by Islamic Relief and agreed upon with the donor.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The protection risk analysis are listed below: 1- Exposing students, girls and women to sexual exploitation and harassmentProbability: mediumMitigating measure: awareness sessions on the dangers of harassment and exploitation will be provided on how to inform the competent authorities, and sharing means of communication with the beneficiaries. Sign the Code of Conduct for Protection by School and International Relations Officers Resources Required: Qualified trainer, Communications, Awareness posters, Referral aids, Code of Conduct, Acceptable.2- The inability of women and girls to reach schools because of customs and traditions. Probability: MediumMitigating measure: awareness sessions for the local community will be provided. Required resources: Qualified trainer, awareness posters.3- Inability of people with disabilities to access education.Probability: Medium Mitigating measure: Building ramps at school entrances, providing aids (glasses, headphones, crutches, etc.) Psychological support for integration with healthy students. Required resources: Budget, specialist awareness, case management, and referral. 4- Lack of privacy in schools by women, girls and children. Probability: MediumMitigating measure: building separate toilets according to gender, segregation between males and females, and awareness sessions. Resources required: budget, qualified trainer, awareness posters, case management, and referral.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IR guarantees protections against PSEA risks in the education sector through:Beneficiaries (boys, girls, males, females) are at risk of harassment, exploitation, or abuse from project staff, teachers, school management, local authorities, or students among themselves. Mitigating Measures:- Staff sign the Code of Conduct, conduct PSEA training for staff, educate beneficiaries, and share means of communication and reporting.- School management and teachers sign the Code of Conduct, and conduct PSEA risk training.- Emphasis on protection issues from PSEA in all MoU signed with the Directorate of Education.- Conducting awareness-raising for the beneficiaries, sharing awareness posters in the places where the project activities are implemented.- Sharing the means of communication and reporting PSEA cases (phone number, WhatsApp, Skype, complaint box, etc.), while ensuring confidentiality and efficient case management. An employee who is a focal point in each school is responsible for following up on PSEA and GBV cases and referring them to the case management centers.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks, assumptions and mitigation measures are listed below: 1. Spread of COVID-19 among students, schools staff, and field teams. IR staff will take the following measures, organize an awareness session, fix awareness posters at schools, use hand sanitizer, and face masks.2. No access to the project location. In coordination with all relevant stakeholders, IR can change the project location if access to the planned location becomes impossible. 3. Closure of the Turkish border will hinder the access of some material and staff, IR will look for another alternative to get the material from the Syrian market or coordinate with the Turkish authority to deliver the items through the humanitarian border. 4. Interference of armed groups in the project areas ( In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, IR will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.) 5. Risk of children dropping out of formal education ( Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program; meeting with parents to encourage them to send their children to school.)6. Market fluctuation changes in prices. Prior to the start of implementation, a market assessment will be conducted in order to see what is available and what is not, and establish contingency plans.
