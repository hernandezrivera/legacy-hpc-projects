Project Summary

Bonyan designed its inclusive education interventions to meets the actual education needs in Syria and comply with the strategic Objectives of both the HRP and Education Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance to assessment findings and key indicators. Bonyan realizes that the priority for Education intervention should respond to the education needs of children and youth in those areas facing the highest severity of needs.
Likewise, Bonyan 2019 Education program will focus to bring children back to formal education pathways through variety of interventions ensuring that they have access to lifelong learning. The response prioritizes vulnerable children including adolescents/youth, girls and boys living with disabilities, especially in hard-to-reach locations targeting total of 50,000 children (29,875boys/20,125girls, 5-17 years old) with strong coordination with relative authorities (Turkish MONE, LC, ED) in Aleppo, Idleb, and Hama.
***** Obj.1: Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years).
Starting with back-to-learning campaigns targeting children with their parents to raise awareness of importance of education, address parents concerns, and outreach for the established education services including distribution of School kits for both students, teacher, and schools including hygiene kit and recreational kits. Total target for this activity is 44,000 children (22,220boys/21,780girls, 5-17 years old) and their parents and surrounded communities.
Distributing supportive items plans which includes Schools’ kits, teachers’ kits, students’ kits.
Schools and desk maintenance activity will be conducted in order to expand the capacity of the educational facilities to host more children, provide fair services for both females and males, and children with disabilities and provide better education, targeting 50 schools~26,250 children and 5,000 desks~15,000 children.
Providing operating costs for 25 formal education school targeting 14,250 children (6,000boys/8,250girls, 5-17 years old). non-formal education is tailored by age and needs in order to fulfill the gaps for each targeted age and gender, 15 NFE centers will target 9,000 children (4,300boys/4,700girls, 8-12 years old) to provide literacy and numeracy along with PSS sessions. And 10 NFE centers will target 3,000 children (1,500boys/1,500girls, 12-15 years old) to provide Remedial program. Both FE and NFE centers will be provided with equipment, student and teachers kits, learning materials, operational consumables, and heating during winter season.
As education is one of the very basic children’s rights Bonyan is planning to provide access to education for children with disabilities by providing transportation services and improve school environment by provide targeted assistance within the schools in order to encourage these children to continue their education and integrate them with their peers.
***** Obj.2: Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5-17 years) within a protective environment
Building the capacity of 1,072 volunteering teachers and educational actors on child-centered, gender-sensitive inclusive education, codes of conduct and anti-bullying psychosocial support and referral mechanisms, EiE, and INEE. Targeted training will be conducted for the in-school protection supervisors, protection coordinators in order to improve project quality and compliance with protection principals and improve the already established referral system within the schools.
In the operated schools Bonyan will provide incentives for the teachers and education actors along with teaching resources and supplementary kits during the whole academic year. And will integrate PSS (I Deal curriculum), Children’s rights promotion and life skills and citizenship programs within school activities.

Needs

The overall scale and severity of humanitarian needs in Syria remain extensive. 11.7 million people are considered to be in need of humanitarian assistance, and the crises left no one untouched, it continues to impact population groups differently. Some segments of the population are likely to be more acutely affected than others due to their sex, age and disability status and exposure to specific risk factors. while an agreement between the Russia and Turkey in Sep2018 led to a significant reduction of conflict in the northwest De-Escalation Zone, including a near cessation of airstrikes. However, a reduction in the level of hostilities does not translate to the absence of humanitarian needs, given the long-term impact of conflict on individual, households and communities. HNO2019 conclude that: *** Across Syria, 2.1M children, are out-of-school and a further 1.3M children are at risk of dropping out*** More than 1/3 of schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education Displaced children are more likely to be out-of-school and the dropout rate is higher in displaced communities. Idleb and Aleppo was already limited education services prior to recent displacement influx. With the addition of over 1.9 million displaced school aged children, education capacity has been nearly exhausted, with widespread implications on the ability of children to access quality education. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area. After years of crisis, the number of school aged children who have been out of school for more than five years is on the rise resulting in complex learning needs; Furthermore, 1,266,284 persons are in need in the targeted area Edlib, Aleppo and Hama. Bonyan assessment in the targeted locations showed that:*** the highest number of operated schools are located in Idleb city reaching 153 schools. In the other hand, the highest number of suspended schools is 18 in the region of Al Bab. We also found that Jisr-Ash-Shugur the lowest region in terms of schools’ number.*** the need of teaching materials is the highest priority in most of the assessed schools followed by providing incentives, and school rehabilitation*** More than 40% of the out-of-school children in Syria are between 15 and 17 years of age and have different learning n, and livelihoods needs. The boys are forced to leave schools to make a living as a breadwinner or join the armed actions, the girls are mainly to help in house-keeping with some cases related to early marriage ****** Needs gender analysis4.7% of the IDP households are female headed, among IDP children between 0-19 years, 48.7% are male and 46.4% are female. Each of these groups have had a unique experience of the crisis and are often exposed to different risks and vulnerabilities. Given the disruption generated by the crisis, with regard to large-scale displacement and family separation, traditional gender roles have changed. Women becoming the primary family breadwinners because of the absence of male family members. Women and girls as well as persons with disabilities face distinct protection risks, including gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse.The lack of education has a differential impact on boys and girls, who are facing complex barriers include a wide range of protection and socio-economic issues, extending beyond the education sector including the prevalence of child labor, child marriage, lack of IDs and education documentation, lack of safety, the cumulative psycho-social effects of the protracted crisis and extensive displacement, putting children at higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations. The intensification of hostilities in some areas has negatively impacted thousands of children who lost access to education opportunities.
