Project Summary

As Syria enters its thirteenth year of crisis, the 438,000 Palestine refugees face socio-economic challenges, heightening their vulnerability. Addressing their needs is crucial for UNRWA, focusing on food assistance amid the deepening crisis. Of these refugees, 60% have experienced displacement, enduring the impact of broader macro-economic and social conditions, including growing scarcity of food and financial resources.
The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) underscores the severity of the ongoing political, economic, and humanitarian crises in Syria, placing an additional burden on Palestine refugees, an already vulnerable group. An October 2023 post-distribution monitoring (PDM) survey highlighted distressing trends: 92% of households resorted to borrowing food or seeking help, 64% had to cut daily meals due to financial constraints, and 40% adopted emergency livelihood strategies, including begging (4%) and selling challenging-to-recover assets (36%). These indicators underscore the diminishing ability of refugee households to cope with increasing poverty.
The vulnerability of Palestine refugees deepened in February 2023 with earthquakes compounding their challenges. UNRWA health records revealed a nearly doubled number of patients accessing Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) in earthquake-affected areas from February to June 2023, emphasizing elevated trauma levels. The earthquake displaced an additional 1,511 households, adding to the 40% already in protracted displacement due to the Syrian conflict.
In-kind food assistance remains a crucial intervention to safeguard the resilience of the most vulnerable Palestine refugees. UNRWA's 2024 plan adopts a more targeted approach, focusing on providing in-kind food assistance to 155,500 of the most vulnerable groups. This selection process adheres to the Agency's two-tier targeting approach, prioritizing female-headed households, families led by individuals with disabilities, families led by older persons, and unaccompanied minors or orphans. The food ration for one person includes essential items like flour, rice, pasta, and sardines…etc, procured internationally or locally, depending on price competitiveness and timely availability. UNRWA's distribution strategy ensures efficient delivery through its distribution centers in targeted governorates.
Monitoring and Evaluation are assured through regular field visits, feedback from implementing staff, and the dedicated Area Support Team and SFO-based Programme Support Team. The UNRWA PSO's Monitoring and Evaluation system closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects. The Multipurpose Cash (MPC) component, with a reduced transfer value, complements the in-kind food assistance program, recognizing potential fund unavailability. UNRWA's commitment extends to mobilizing resources for the four planned distribution rounds, one each quarter, contingent on fund availability. The comprehensive approach integrates not only food assistance but also multipurpose cash assistance and protection interventions, ensuring a coordinated effort across different sectors. The in-kind food assistance program stands out as a crucial form of UNRWA's emergency support, stabilizing food security for Palestine refugees in Syria, reducing humanitarian needs, and mitigating escalating levels of food insecurity.

Focus on community engagement and participation, and feedback is responded to. Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV particularly at locations with high numbers of beneﬁciaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: 1. Vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities prioritized to reduce their waiting time. 2. Protection Alignment Reviews conducted on a biennial basis to get feedback from staff and the community on how to improve services and how to optimize compliance with the main protection principles. Risk: Exposure to Explosive Ordnance while accessing services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. Facilitator and focal point training of staff including psychosocial counsellors and teachers and widespread awareness-raising activities with children to increase their knowledge and reduce their exposure to these risks. 2. Community members in higher-risk areas are prioritized for EORE sessions. 3. Advocacy for rubble removal around UNRWA installations.PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN Clear Check recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
