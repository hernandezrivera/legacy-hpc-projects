Project Summary

Zimbabwe is currently facing periodical food insecurity and shocks that are mainly caused by climatic shocks and poor performance of the economy. The situation has affected both rural and urban-peri urban areas according to Zimbabwe Vulnerability Committee (ZimVAC) report of 2019 projections for rural and urban food security that about 7.7 million people are food insecure in both rural and urban areas. It is estimated by ZimVAC urban food assessment of 2019 that 2.2 million people in urban areas are currently food insecure. About 32.8% were estimated to be food insecure in Bikita and Zaka urban areas. The situation is likely to worsen as the peak lean season period is progressing from January to March 2020. Food insecure populations in peri-urban areas of Bikita and Zaka are likely to continue exposed to food insecurity risks and increasingly engaged in negative coping strategies because of the geographical locations of the areas in natural region iv and v where food production is a challenge under dryland agriculture. Agriculture related livelihoods options are currently hindered by climatic related shocks. The surrounding districts of Buhera, Gutu, Masvingo, Chiredzi, Chivi and Mwenezi are currently in cereal deficit. This implies that staple cereal supplies by farmers from surrounding communities into Bikita and Zaka urban areas have been limited as there are no sources nearby the districts.
It is against this background that Christian Care is appealing to assist 2,400 acute food insecure women, men, boys and girls with monthly food baskets through emergency mobile cash transfers to save lives and enhancing and ensuring targeted people are able to meet immediate food and nutritional requirements during the period from January to April 2020.
The project will target beneficiaries in IPC 3 and 4 through community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. The community will define food insecurity indicators of own choice that matches with IPC 3 and 4 characteristics. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed. Verifications of beneficiaries will be conducted through purposive and random sampling to reduce inclusion and exclusion errors.
Since 2009, the organisation has implemented various food security and livelihoods programmes in the two districts funded by Canadian Foodgrains Bank (CFGB), Act for Peace of Australia and the United Church of Canada. In 2019, the organisation assisted Tropical Cyclone Idai victims in the districts with in-kind food from May to July 2019. The organisation has current running, Food Security and Livelihoods program being implemented in Zaka district. The program is funded by Act for Peace of Australia and has a duration of 12 months from the 1st July 2019 to 30th of June 2020. The organisation will took advantage of its presence in the district to synergize previous efforts with the proposed response to maximize the benefits of humanitarian response to affected people.

1 - Activities

The project will provide monthly food baskets through mobile cash-based transfers targeting 2,400 most acute food insecure women, men, girls and boys including people living with disabilities, chronical illnesses, child headed and the widowed. Each beneficiary of the project will receive a monthly entitlement in via mobile ecocash equaivalent to US$13.2. A household with 5 members will receive ecocash equivalent to US$66 using the prevailing bank rate by the time of project roll out. The project will also conduct the weekly market assessment and monthly post distribution monitorings. The market assessment will track the changes in prices and availability of cereals and ither basic commodities on the market in response to the project. The post distribution monitoring will be undertaken every month 2 weeks after the food distribution to determine the outcomes of the cash distributions. The project will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. Christian Care will attend all coordination meetings that will be organised by government departments, other partners and the food security cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through determining the percentage of women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed who received emergency food assistance through mobile cash-based transfers. The success of the project will be determined through comparisons of the baseline and endline situations. The monthly post distributions monitoring will inform the success of the project on monthly bases by tracking on the outcomes of the project to the targeted households. Baseline and endline data will be collected using a combination of qualitative and quantitative techniques consisting of focus group discussions with communities, key informant interviews with line government ministries, household questionnaires and review of relevant food security documents prior to the start of activities to establish benchmarks. The post distribution monitoring will be undertaken 2 weeks after the cash distributions to gather information on outputs and outcomes of the project as food consumption scores, coping strategy index, food consumption patterns, and food utilization trends through focus group discussions.
