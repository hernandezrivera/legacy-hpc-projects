Project Summary

The aim of the project is to ensure the survival of the most vulnerable people affected by the conflict through multi-sectoral emergency aid and to make them more resilient to further external shocks. The project area includes the remaining areas outside the control of the Syrian regime in the north-west of the country (Idlib, Aleppo), where around 1 million people were recently displaced or again internally displaced. The target group of the project are internally displaced persons as well as residents of the host communities. In order to achieve the goal, we use a mix of different measures and dosage forms. For newly internally displaced people, WHH will provide emergency assistance in the food security, WASH and shelter/NFI sectors, both in-kind and in the form of vouchers, to alleviate the first most urgent needs. WASH activities will include: i) to supply and install water tanks for latrines, distribute cleaning materials and perform removal of digesters ii) supply water trucking to the camps and iii) hygiene sensitization and training of the population in camps by the Hygiene Promotion Team. Under FSL, food e-vouchers and bread will be distributed in several locations and people who have already arrived or are residents of host communities will receive agricultural vouchers to make them less dependent on acute emergency aid and thus reduce negative coping strategies. Particular attention will also be paid to the area of humanitarian protection. Under SNFI, the construction work/light rehabilitation of shared/single accommodations will take place and winterization assistance will be provided to beneficiaries in the form of winterization cash. The protection component of the program will be further broadened in 2023, adopting a community approach and increasing the availability of specialized services. Under ERL, a clothes production workshop will be set up. 20 women will be provided with a training on sewing and later produce different sizes of pajamas for 6 months, during which they will receive 1$ per day (for max. 5 hrs/day and average monthly income of 100$). These clothes will be distributed the ones in need to be defined at a later stage. As per the last component, nutrition activities will be implemented by a local partner, PAC, specializing on this field. PAC’s intervention will focus more on awareness raising, prevention and counselling, coupled with the delivery of nutrition cash vouchers (for PLW and caregivers of infants) in the Idleb region, It will focus on most vulnerable women of reproductive age and children who are under the risk of malnutrition and have a lack of access to the food diversity in North-West Syria (NWS). Accordingly, it aims to enhance Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW)’s access to Nutrition, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Services and Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) Supportive Services in Idleb Governorate and Jebal Saman district of Aleppo Governorate. This will be achieved through providing IYCF counselling to the caregivers and PLWs through either OTP center or RRTs. PLWs and caregivers will be also supported with 3 months of food voucher which will provide them with fresh foods to mitigate their malnutrition risks.

3 - Needs

The scale and complexity of humanitarian needs in Syria remain deeply concerning and are expected to increase in the coming period amid the heightened risk that the UN cross-border resolution will not be renewed beyond Jan. 2023 leaving millions of people without access to humanitarian aid. The eco. situation further exacerbates tensions among the population.The prices are increasingly high and there are shortages in essential goods and services, eroded employment opportunities, and deterioration of household coping mechanisms. As of Nov. 2022, out of 4.6m people in NWS, 4.1m are in need and 3.1m are food insecure. 2.8 million are IDPs of whom almost 60% are living in camps and informal settings. As per HNO 2023, around 70% of Syrian families are food insecure and access to clean and regular water supply remains to be a major need in the region, especially with the increase of water-borne diseases. According to the SMART survey conducted by PAC in June 2021, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19% to 24.5% between May 2019 and June 2021 in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) increased from 0.9% to 4.7% over the same period. Among pregnant and lactating women, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across Northwest Syria is 12.4%. The findings of 2022 were similar to 2021. Aleppo and Idleb are two of the most-impacted regions across Syria across several sectors.

4 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented through 2 modalities: self-implementation and IPs. As per the former, the activities will be carried out through field team based in Syria and project team based in Türkiye through cross-border. As per the latter, the implementing partners will be monitored by WHH throughout the projects. WHH strives to align and adjust its activities in accordance and coordination with Cluster recommendations as an active member of all relevant clusters. The activities will be introduced to all stakeholders prior to the implementation. The activities will be multisectorally under: FSL (food vouchers, agriculture vouchers for homegardening and farmers, bread distribution), ERL (clothes production), Nutrition (nutritional awareness, training, counselling and nutrition cash vouchers) by PAC, emergency response (winterization voucher assistance and fuel support to schools), WASH (water trucking, latrine cleaning and water tanks, hygiene promotion) and shelter (tent repairs) by Shafak and Protection (CBP committees and GP). As per its design, monitoring and evaluation phases, WHH ensures to include vulnerable groups (youth,PwDs, PLW, women, etc) through FGDs, setting up accessible and proper facilities for its distribution/redemption points, workshops, etc. and through CBP committees. WHH will collect its data disaggregated by AGD to shape decision making, empower women through equal opportunities and carry out protection mainstreaming assessments.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches of fundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuse of those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values. It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassment of our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations. Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1)Turkish authorities prevent implementation partners from operating across the border into Syria. M1)WHH ensures good relations with the Government of Turkiye through legal compliance and appropriate coordination with the relevant authorities. R2)Border closed due to acts of war. M2) Measures are partly carried out by vouchers, so that in the event of a border closure, only in-kind measures with procurement outside Syria would be affected. Furthermore, with the existence of PTT and a procurement team in both Syria and Gazinatep, procurement inside SYR is an option in order to be able to continue the project. R3)Strong fluctuations of the SYP and TRY price in relation to EUR/USD. M3)Procurement within the TUR, including lines in the contract to allow price negotiations with the suppliers, and budget realignment when necessary. R4)Project funds are stolen from armed groups in Syria, or armed groups force the IP to hand over parts of the aid to them. M4) As an active members of AWG, coordinating on aid diversion, available robust partner monitoring framework and requesting the support of local authorities. R5) Military actions of the civil war parties force the IPs to cease their activities. M5) existence of appropriate protocols and shifting to other loctions where programming is possible R6) Finding well-trained IYCF workers and maintaining easy access to target beneficiaries M6) high level of community engagement and existing network of qualified staff to provide mentoring
