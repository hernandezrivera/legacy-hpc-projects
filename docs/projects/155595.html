Project Summary

This project aims to promote the rights of CAR refugees to identity, Housing Land and Property (HLP). NRC will work with refugees and host communities for social cohesion. Support will be provided refugees by both NRC Cameroon and NRC CAR for durable solutions such as local integration and voluntary return. Civil documentation will be provided through legal assistance and HLP related issues will be addressed to enable them have secure access to land to livelihood activities. This is a 4 year project that will start in the second semester of 2019.

Needs

Since 2013, the conflict in CAR has continuously forced people to flee and resettle in many neighbourhood countries, including Cameroon. Boys, girls, women and men were obliged to leave and abandoned their houses and farms during the violence perpetrated by armed groups. Cameroon hosts nearly 236,700 Central African refugees residing in several sites and villages in the country's regions. Those in Eastern are around 149,300 people, (source HNO 2017-2010)These populations face various problems like limited access to basic and social’s services, access to land, freedom of movement, lack of ID, lack of birth certificate for children. Forced displacement resulted in loss HLP rights and limited legal identity rights of individuals and affected communities. Followings are details about HLP and Legal identity rights challenges Access to land for agriculture or housing issue is a real challenge faced by CAR refugees in the East region of Cameroon. Some refugees received plots, allocated by the local communities’ leaders or thanks to humanitarian actors help. In Many cases, allocated parcels had limited value (poor soils) and were not adapted for cultures. Then refugees still struggling to get food or other sustainable resources. Local communities don’t allow refugees to plant crops like cassava. They assume that this culture will enforce land occupation by refugees. Such restriction limits access of CAR refugees to livelihood. Land tenure: host community have some local agreement on land use (contract) with refugees, about how to share their field harvest. These contracts are not fully respected by land owners, who require 33% of the field production. Refugees interest are generally injured in this kind of agreement. They lost part of their farm production Land management and risk of conflicts. Some threats of eviction were reported regarding land affected to refugees by host communities. During harvest period or after discovering that the allocated contains some minerals, the owners claim to get their land back. These kind of claims lead to conflict and violence. Local authorities and humanitarian actors in the Kadei department are not familiar with dealing HLP issues in displacement. To prevent new conflicts, facilitate integration and prepare communities for durable solutions, it is important built the capacity of stakeholders on HLP rights Legal Identity Rights: Most of refugees do not have national ID card delivered by their country of origin. These documents were destroyed or lost during displacement. This lack of document affects their freedom of movement and will later hinder their capacity to claim their houses and land left behind in CAR. Most of children of Central African refugees born in CAR do not have any documents either because they have never been registered, or they were lost or destroyed during displacement. Only those enrolled in exam class, received a document facilitated by UNHCR ""tenant lieu d’acte de naissance"" . This document allows pupils to take the examination. They are not a substitute for the birth certificate. Civil status documents should be provided for these children in a perspective of durable solution. ID and HLP : The Central African refugees living in the East region have not had the culture of ID documentation since they were in their home country. They vast majority (around 80%) are without civil documentation and national ID. (source NRC RNA Feb 2018). In the context of return and durable solutions these documents are a prerequisite to confirm their nationality, identity and to facilitate HLP's claims.
