Project Summary

In Gaza Strip, poverty and vulnerability are major barriers that prevent the fulfillment of children’s rights. These structural weaknesses are exacerbated sharply by humanitarian shocks such as the May 2021 escalation in Gaza, which deeply impacted children. Over 40,000 children lived in households whose homes were damaged or destroyed as a consequence of the May 2021 escalation. The impact on their homes and families is likely to push already vulnerable households deeper into poverty.
As such, UNICEF State of Palestine will deliver a Humanitarian Cash Transfer programme to provide child-sensitive cash support alongside strengthening the shock responsiveness of the national social protection system within the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). This programme, working along the humanitarian-development nexus, targets 9,000 children and adolescents (0-18 years old), who are members of poor and vulnerable households affected by the recent crisis, with cash support over 18 months. Under this project, UNICEF seeks humanitarian funding to support 6,000 children with a three-month child cash grant (monthly transfers), while complementary funding will be sought for a longer-term response.
This social assistance will enable affected children and their families to access goods (food and non-food items) and essential services (health, WASH, education, nutrition, psychosocial support, etc.) locally. By responding to this emergency in support of the national social protection system, UNICEF intends to support the coverage of the immediate needs and contribute to accelerating the recovery phase and building resilience of the affected populations.
Efforts will also be concentrated on supporting the execution of the overall shock responsiveness social protection capacity building plan and leveraging the lessons learned from the immediate response. Moreover, the component on Shock Responsive Social Protection of this project will also be undertaken in conjunction with the funding under the EU/ ILO project for ‘Strengthening nexus coherence and responsiveness in the Palestinian social protection sector’ which is planned to be implemented by ILO, UNICEF, and Oxfam.

1 - Needs

According to the Rapid Disaster Needs Assessment (RDNA) losses resulting from the May 2021 escalation in the Gaza Strip in the social protection sector were estimated at US$40 – 50 million. The escalation impacted households, livelihoods, and safety nets for the most vulnerable. Across the Gaza Strip, there were 17,343 houses which were damaged or destroyed as a result of the escalation. There were over 40,000 children who lived in the families who lost their homes, and around 600 children in the Gaza Strip were injured as a result of the escalation. These families were identified through self-registration with the authorities in Gaza, and the Ministry of Social Development has verified that many of these families whose homes received direct damage during the escalation were living below the national poverty line even before the escalation Residents are at risk of falling deeper into poverty as a result of the conflict damage, incurring debts, and being unable to meet their families’ basic needs. Poverty rates were high in Gaza before the escalation, and economic conditions have further worsened, with over 120,000 people losing their income sources as a result of the direct damage from the escalation to economic establishments, loss of assets, death of family members who were the main breadwinners, and a reduction of job opportunities. This complex humanitarian crisis coupled with structural needs will contribute to further worsening the socio-economic conditions of the affected population, especially the most vulnerable groups: women-headed households, persons with disabilities, and those already living below the poverty line. In addition to the economic slowdown resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic, the growing needs resulting from the escalation will exacerbate the constraints already faced by the existing National Cash Transfer Programme (NCTP), which was not designed to address critical humanitarian needs and which has faced critical funding shortfalls in 2021. This programme targets households whose homes were damaged or destroyed in the May 2021 conflict in Gaza. By working with the Ministry of Social Development UNICEF has access to data on the affected households and can work with the Social Workers to access further information on these households. This allows UNICEF to work with the MOSD to collect the data required for the proxy means calculation and to accurately establish the poverty level of each targeted household. Through this UNICEF can target the most poor and vulnerable households.

2 - Activities

Child-sensitive cash will provide income support to meet the basic needs of children and adolescents who are members of poor and vulnerable households affected by the May 2021 escalation, and connect shock-affected families to complementary services such as protection services, case management and referral pathways. The cash support will be implemented in line with the National Cash Transfer Programme (NCTP) of the Ministry of Social Development (MOSD). This programme will also be linked to beneficiary awareness and behavioral change on child rights and well-being such as vaccination and the violence prevention.The programme will support 9,000 poor and vulnerable children in families whose homes were damaged or destroyed in the May 2021 escalation, with nine monthly Child Cash Grant payments (following humanitarian payments made in 2021). These families will be supported alongside the MOSD as a humanitarian cash top-up as a complementary programme for those who are beneficiaries of the NCTP, or as a humanitarian cash transfer for non-NCTP beneficiaries. Payments will be made through the MOSD where possible, or through a Financial Service Provider if required.As a child-sensitive cash intervention, the calculation of the transfer value will consider the number of children per targeted household in line with the transfer value recommended by the Cash Working Group (CWG), linked to the 2021 Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) calculation exercise. In the humanitarian response phase, all targeted households with one child will receive a monthly amount of US$ 108 which represents 54 per cent of the transfer value linked to the Emergency Minimum Expenditure Basket (EMEB) or survival MEB. Each household with more than one child will receive an additional amount of US$ 70 for each extra child for the same duration, and the total transfer amount will not exceed US$ 324 for four or more children, which represents 100 per cent of the transfer value linked to the 2021 MEB calculation. In the subsequent phase, the transfer value will be reduced to align with the value of the National Cash Transfer Programme. Depending on vulnerability level, these different amounts should enable the shock-affected households to cover critical child-focused needs such as nutritious food, core relief items, drinking water, sanitation, shelter, education, protection, life-saving medical care, transportation, and communication. These payments will be made for nine months, with the period from January to March falling into the humanitarian response phase of the Child Cash Grant, and the period from April to September a recovery phase where the transfer value per household will be reduced.UNICEF will work through both the Ministry of Social Development's Social Workers and a local NGO to undertake household visits to undertake protection monitoring ensure that the cash does not Do Harm, and to undertake any follow up required with households. The local NGO will also undertake financial management coaching for the targeted families, which will include a component of building the families' awareness on the need to support the vulnerable children in their households.The local NGO will also follow up with all targeted households to pass messages on key child protection issues including on the prevention of Violence Against Children. Where the NGO identifies any cases of vulnerable children, or children in need of support such as Psychosocial Support they will be referred through the existing Child Protection referral protocols either to the Family Centres or to the Ministry of Social Development.The programme will develop dedicated monitoring and accountability processes, including distribution and post-distribution monitoring of the implementation and effects of the programme on the target population, as well as a behavioral change strategy supported by the RapidPro (an open-source SMS software) community feedback mechanism. UNICEF promotes a people-centered approach across its sectoral and multisectoral interventions which aims to ensure Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and create an enabling environment for them to raise concerns freely and receive an appropriate response. Through the Core Commitments for Children (CCCs), UNICEF is committed to putting in place the preventive measures to uphold the rights of children affected by humanitarian crises, including preventing gender-based violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and sexual assault and transactional sex. Thus, a complaint and feedback mechanism will be put in place in collaboration with an international NGO, utilizing the most appropriate and accessible communication channels. This will offer the opportunity for the beneficiaries to share information related to the response, flag issues or concerns, and report any misconduct which may affect programme quality. UNICEF will work to ensure that the complaints are managed in a timely manner while respecting confidentiality. Moreover, according to UNICEF’s approach to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA); any harm related to the receipt of cash in the household; any grievances relating to fraud, sexual exploitation, abuse of power; or a beneficiary data breach will be immediately escalated through relevant fast track procedures. UNICEF staff and all partners will comply with this procedure, cooperate, and take appropriate action, ensuring confidentiality while handling beneficiaries’ complaints.

3 - Indicators

The cash programming will be supported by Payment Verification and Post Distribution Monitoring with the targeted households. The Payment Verification will assure that the households received the cash in a safe and dignified way. While the Post-Distribution Monitoring will follow up on a series of outcome indicators to asses the quality of the programming that it met the needs of the targeted households, to measure how they used the the cash provided and to assess any changes to the lives of the households which received the cash. UNICEF will also support the establishment of a two-way SMS based system for the Payment Verification through the RapidPro platform. This platform allow UNICEF to initiate communication (flows) with beneficiaries, or for beneficiaries to initiate when they require. Overall, UNICEF will use the below indicator to measure the project success:• # households reached with humanitarian cash transfersWithin these households UNICEF will also specifically measure:• # of children (boys and girls) living in households reached with humanitarian cash transfers• # of female-headed households reached with humanitarian cash transfers• # of persons with disabilities living in households reached with humanitarian cash transfers. UNICEF will work with other cash actors to measure the impact of this programme as a contribution to the nexus elements of Social Protection system building. This will contribute to the learning on the overlap between humanitarian and development cash programming, particularly for the expansion of child-focused cash programming in emergencies. UNICEF will feed all learning on this humanitarian cash top up back into the national social protection systems to build the shock-responsive capacity of the National Cash Transfer Programme within the Ministry of Social Development.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Beneficiaries will receive multi-purpose cash assistance either through the National Cash Transfer Programme as a top-up, or through a self-managed SMS. Through either modality, the beneficiary will have control over how they access their cash. The provision of unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash provides a dignified response modality.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project targets households who self-registered with the national authorities following the May 2021 Gaza escalation as having their homes damaged or destroyed. This project targets vulnerable households with children. The targeting of the specific households has been undertaken with the Ministry of Social Development and ensures that households targeted are living under the nationally defined poverty line as measured against the Proxy Means Testing Formula.
