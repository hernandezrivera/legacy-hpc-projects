Project Summary

The Integrated Response for Resilience Programme aims at providing a comprehensive response through Early Recovery support in 80 communities impacted by the crisis.
Communities, particularly those most dramatically affected – and under additional stress because of IDPs returns and other displacements - need assistance in reinforcing their resilience to address these shocks as well as the consequences of the protracted crisis, and to address their needs on seriously depleted infrastructures and service capacities.
The essence of the Programme is about providing Syrian communities with – still limited - support to regain control over their basic needs (livelihoods, access to essential services) and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance. Prioritization of locations, is the outcome of a context analysis and needs assessment conducted on more than 5,000 Syrian communities, based on the following three criteria:
1) Hard to Reach Areas
2) Areas of major IDPs presence and IDPs returns
3) Highest scoring in the Severity Scale
The integrated response consists of the following components: strengthening of social services-related capacities (access and coverage), livelihoods creation and rural recovery, PWDs support, social cohesion/community safety, reducing gender inequality and youth marginality, women empowerment, tensions reduction and rehabilitation of basic infrastructure and public services.
The program is built in a modular manner both in terms of geographical and sectoral/thematic interventions and foresees the allotment of average 1 Million US$ per community. Hence, donors willing to support this approach will be able to pre-select certain location(s) and/or area(s) of intervention of their preference, within the 200 prioritized communities.
The programme relates directly to the sector objectives:
1. Living conditions
2. Livelihoods
3. Access to social services
4. Access to productive and market infrastructure
5. Social protection
6. Strengthening of local public services delivery capacity
7. Reinforcing opportunities and capacities for civic engagement

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has depleted the capacity at all levels to address basic needs, resulting in the following situation:1. Poverty, at 13% before the crisis, now stands at around 85%, 2. Unemployment rate is estimated to stand at 53 per cent, reaching as high as 75 per cent among youth (up to 24 years of age, while a sizeable reduction in the size of Syria’s current workforce, which stands at 51 per cent of precrisis levels is witnessed. 3. In the absence of livelihood opportunities, close to 20% of Syrians have been forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms, including child labor; early marriage of their young children; and participation in the violence sector,4. 2020 Severity Analysis on issues that affect people’s resilience most (leaving aside equally critical issues related to governance and participation) such as jobs, access to resources, social services, availability of markets, water and electricity, damage to buildings; etc. put the severity scale in prioritized communities at around 60% average, with a large number of the 281 sub-districts in Syria scoring even much higher levels of severity,5. A 2019 assessment on disability among adults in western Aleppo, Idleb and Ar-Raqqa showed an average of 45 per cent of surveyed persons injured during the Syria crisis are expected to sustain a permanent impairment (e.g. amputation, spinal cord injury, brain injury).6. 2018 figures also show that about 1.4 million Internally Displaced Persons have returned to their communities. IDPs returns decreased in 2019 although still around half million people made it to their communities of origin.The elements displayed above, show how needs have evolved on the ground and across lines of control. They clearly indicate that also the humanitarian-resilience response should evolve towards more (early) recovery in order to be effective in alleviating the suffering of PiN and reducing their resorting to negative coping mechanisms.

5 - Implementation of activities

In communities selected for this programme, a mechanism for consultation and participation will be established as one of the first programme actions. This will be preceded or accompanied by a conflict-sensitive analysis, to ensure a full understanding of the issues to be taken into account when designing programme priorities and implementation options, so that conflict threats are not exacerbated, and community cohesion is reinforced. As such, there will be a strong underpinning of ‘Do No Harm’ and ‘Do Good’ considerations. This will ensure that priorities supported under the programme will be identified and prioritized transparently and with the participation of the communities or through their representatives. Spaces of participation thus created/reinforced, will be linked to all areas of activities, particularly social cohesion, throughout implementation. As to project implementation, options will be prioritized that meet the following objectives: one, of drawing as much as possible on local – and preferably community-based – implementation arrangements, as far as they are inclusive (e.g. community groups; faith-based organizations; parents associations; farmer groups; youth clubs), and, two, draw as much as possible on local labor (with priority of recruitment among the most vulnerable) so as to enhance the overall positive impact, in particular in terms of livelihoods, on those prioritized participants. An effort will also be made to encourage the participation of local informal and small businesses, to ensure the programme also provides a rapid (even if small) boost to local economic recovery. Finally, solid collaboration will be pursued with other UN Agencies and with non-governmental organizations engaged in the same geographic and/or thematic areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Assumptions and risks-Key stakeholders allow UNDP and partners to apply minimum targeting criteria outlined in this proposal, ensuring impartial and needs-driven service delivery. Should UNDP and/or partners be unable to apply its procedures, activities may be delayed or frozen until needed permissions are negotiated.-Areas of operation remain safe for staff and beneficiaries to access. Should life-threatening risks (such as ERW contamination, outbreak of active conflict, huge risk in V/PIED incidents, heavy IAF affecting local area) occur, activities may be frozen to safeguard lives. There is a risk that, for example, intensified conflict triggered by resurgence of ISIL remnants following coalition forces withdrawal could affect the operating environment in Deir-ez-Zor. -Local communities actively participate to all phases of the initiative and vulnerable groups are positively included in the community-based mechanisms. Contingency/mitigation measures-Red line is inability to apply minimum targeting and do-no-harm procedures to ensure a principled response. Should restrictions compromise ability to do this, UNDP would as a first measure open negotiations with stakeholders; and as a last resort liaise with consortium partners and ECHO modify or cancel planned activities. -Withdrawal from a target area due to safety concerns could trigger a shift of activities to other target areas, or freeze of activities before re-assessment and re-entry into an area after the situation stabilizes. -If a major devaluation of local currency continued to occur, consequences in terms of operational capacity of partners and contractors would be bered by UNDP using direct implementation modality. -The timeframe is adapted to ensure a proper mobilization at community level and sensitization on the participatory approach. Activities in every target area will start only after the acceptance by local authorities and civil society representatives of the proposed methodology.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Resources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension; Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response;2. Ensure that an updated needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent;3. Make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, area reached equally;4. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms;5. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the communities concerned;6. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’, by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation;7. Make sure some process of receiving complaints and of responses to complaints is put in place;8. Have a plan B in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities. Risk 2: Escalation of the security situation; Mitigation measures: 1. Focus on responses to the benefit of the entire community rather than individual beneficiaries (e.g. rehabilitation of social or community infrastructure, rather than e.g. individual housing support);2. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment.
