Project Summary

The proposed intervention is focused on the promotion of livestock re-stocking and management to promote ruminant value chain and to support smallholder farmers affected by the insurgency to increase Crop production through capacity building and empowerment with improve seeds and Agro-inputs to enhance Agricultural productivity and profitability for both dry and wet season farmers in the project targeted locations.
The intervention will also involve family Business Schemed (livelihoods activities) that will focus on beneficiary training and empowerment on entrepreneurship development, village saving and leading schemed, market access and Agricultural productive chain analysis. Beneficiaries will be supported with financial linkage services to enable them to access additional funds for the expansion and sustainability of their Agribusiness Business. The activities will also include training on small business management and record keeping. IDS will set up Electronic Marketing Platform to support smallholder farmers to access the global market for their farm produce to maximize profit. A demonstration farm will be established to provide hands-on skills innovative, including climate-smart agricultural practices and food and cash crops seed multiplication centre.
IDS has line-up plan to develop strategies and actions to broaden the range of possible options for alternative energy systems, prioritizing those with the least negative safety, health, and environmental implications for beneficiaries. Livelihood and empowerment opportunities will be assessed, including the possibilities for local stove production, training centres and shops run by the IDPs, Returnees and Host Community Population community to enable access to fuel-efficient technologies and renewable energy at the household level.
PURPOSE: To boost Agricultural Productivity for Food security, employment opportunities, income generation and promotion of fuel-efficient technologies for cooking and productive uses among households affected insurgency.

1 - Needs

The prolonged Boko Haram insurgency in Borno State and other northeastern states of Nigeria has led to heightened levels of displacement and food insecurity. The conflict continues to affect the lives and livelihoods of millions of people. Almost 3 million people are facing hunger/extreme hunger. While humanitarian access is improving, most displaced families still rely on vulnerable host communities for basic needs, including food and nutrition. According to the recent report by Early Warm Early Action (EWEA)/FAO in January 2020; these heightened levels of displacement have put already impoverished host communities under extreme pressure, leading to increased exposure to food insecurity and malnutrition. Looting and fear of attacks have prevented many farmers from working in their fields and venturing into other small and medium-scale enterprises (SMEs), leading to the loss of harvests and productive assets, and extremely reduced purchasing power. According to the October 2023 CH, a total of 3.3 million (19% of the population) and for the projected lean season period (June to August 2023), the number of people in crisis to worse phases (Phase 3-5) of food and nutrition insecurity will increase to 4.4 million (25.5% of the population) if no consistent humanitarian assistance is put in place. The IPC for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) analysis conducted in August, and November 2023 projected that Nearly 1.53 million children aged 0-59 months in the Northeast will likely continue to suffer from acute malnutrition from May 2023 – April 2024. The acutely malnourished children aged 0-59 months include 512,00 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases and 1.01 million Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases in the Northeast. In addition, nearly 207,000 pregnant and lactating women in the Northeast suffered and will likely continue to suffer from acute malnutrition from May 2023 – April 2024. Globally, nearly 3 billion people rely on traditional biomass, such as fuel wood, charcoal, or animal waste, as sources of fuel for cooking and heating. In many African countries, more than 80% of the population cook their meals using traditional biomass; by 2030, it is anticipated that 1 billion people in Africa will depend on traditional biomass to meet their energy needs. Vulnerable populations – including refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and the communities hosting them – often have severely constrained access to fuel and energy. Refugee and IDP camps are frequently established in fragile, sparsely forested ecosystems in which both host and displaced populations depend on the scarce natural resources found in areas surrounding the camps. The influx of displaced people and their fuel needs places great pressure on surrounding forests and woodlands. This is often a source of tension between host and displaced communities, which increases the risk of women and children being harassed and assaulted while searching for fuel wood. Furthermore, collecting fuel wood takes time away from school attendance, income-generating activities, childcare, and leisure and can reduce the effectiveness of other humanitarian and development programs targeting women and children. The diversification of livelihood activities and sources of energy – as well as the introduction and promotion of energy-efficient technologies – helps to build resilience in households and communities, allowing people to cope with and recover faster from natural or man-made shocks.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IDS will be responsible for the collection of feedback and reports directly to the Project Director/Focal person. Reports can be made through IDS GBV Helpline: 08172702242 or info@ids.org.ng, The phone number and email address that receives and responds to inquiries, complaints, feedback and reports of safeguarding issues. This phone number and email address are managed by the trained staff to maintain confidentiality and promote the rights of the complainant. IDS have a complaint and feedback box in all areas of intervention where individuals, community members and beneficiaries can anonymously drop their complaints, feedback or report any safeguarding concerns. The complaint and feedback mechanism focal persons manage the boxes at the community level while the gender officer manages them at the organizational level, IDS protection manager, gender officer and complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) focal persons in communities of intervention are trained to receive, handle and manage reported safeguarding cases, these staff are also trained to recognize and respond to safeguarding issues. All reports are treated as soon as possible efficiently and in confidentiality. Reports are assessed by the protection manager then case management for the survivor (if need be), the investigation is launched, and the outcome of the investigation is used to make decisions and also used to inform the disciplinary action to be taken against perpetrators. Information on the investigation is shared on a need-to-know basis and with the consent of the complainant. The Email and complaint box mechanisms can be used for anonymity, the whistle-blowing policy (procedures) protects people with serious concerns. Depending on the magnitude of the case IDS makes referrals for survivor to have Justice.
