Project Summary

Conflict-affected populations struggle to gain access to humanitarian assistance and essential services in an inclusive and equal manner. Vulnerabilities associated with gender, age, disability, have been heightened by the conflict and are generally exacerbated by proximity to conflict areas. Persons with disabilities are highly affected by the crisis and have a right to receive services on an equal bases with others. Children with disabilities women with disabilities, and older persons at high risk of exclusion,
In order to leave no one behind, humanitarian actions need to take widespread discrimination on the grounds of Disability, Gender and Age (DGA) into account, and engage in removing barriers to equal opportunities. Reduce vulnerability and improve access of persons with disabilities, mainly women with disabilities and older persons. Despite the adoption of IASC 2019 guidelines, several barriers continue to negatively affect persons with disabilities capacity to enjoy their rights.
.The organization will work to enable persons with disabilities to participate on an equal basis with others, on accessing services through provision of community approach, technical guidance for actors and reasonable accommodation for individuals (individualized supports). On one hand the INGO action focuses on providing guidance on how to include persons with disabilities mainly women within humanitarian assistance and to overcome risks of increasing vulnerability., while on other hand working to empower persons with disabilities to know their rights, having the confidence and the right tools needed to play a key role within programming cycle
The INGO is conducting regular analysis of barriers faced by persons with disabilities. The analysis finding raised: i)lack of understanding of the identification process , ii) lack of clarity among key actors and humanitarian actors regarding the persons with disabilities human rights; iii) lack of involvement in the program design, provision and evaluation of services provision and in the delivery of the humanitarian assistance; iiv) lack of human, operational and financial resources to provide inclusive and accessible services and assistance; iv) negative beliefs, attitudes and practices limiting their participation in social life at the community and family level.
The INGO acknowledges, among other factors generating inequalities and that need to be considered, that:
·Disability, gender and age as socially constructed norms can be observed in any human group,
·Disability, gender and age are key factors to understanding different people’s capacities, needs, and exposure to risks.
·The impact of inclusion is not only through the direct targets, we rely on increasing the understanding of disability, gender and age vulnerability risks, within communities, humanitarian actors, clusters, working groups and persons with disabilities themselves which will lead to include PWD, older persons and women with disabilities in social, cultural and socio economic activities. Moreover, empowerment and support persons with disabilities to participate through the project cycle will allow providing good example and set an example of good practices for, clusters and working groups who will be targeted within this project. Trained persons with disabilities will target wider communities, to improve inclusion principles.
Another reflect is the increase of % of persons with disabilities through actor's projects. For example for partners who will be trained, we are aiming to increase the % of PWD among their projects up to 30% as some of our partners they only target between 10%-15% only.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO report People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, with a disproportionate impact on female HH, older persons without family support, PWDs and children, older persons and PWDs face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by displacement where families/community support networks have broken down or are overstretched. They face stigma, social isolation, are at increased risk of separation from their families, care providers, and dependent on the assistive products which support their independence is limited. According to HNAP report, nearly (30%) aged 12 and above have disabilities. Economic deprivation and the COVID-19 pandemic, have spurred displacement and exposed PWDs to increased barriers to socioeconomic participation and accessing services. Barriers negatively impact mental, physical health and wellbeing, attainment of rights on an equal basis with others. For children, barriers significantly limit ability to learn and develop along with their peers. PWDs face challenges enjoying an adequate standard of living and leading independent lives in Syria. Evidence based data have been reported by the INGO through barriers assessment, conducted in 2021, shows that persons with disabilities experience reduced independence in their daily activities due to Physical, attitudinal such as bullying, Institutional barriers, such as the lack of inclusive humanitarian programming.

5 - Implementation of activities

The INGO aim to enhance inclusion of PWD through mutually supportive programming to increase participation, independence and access to services. Through the implementation of twin-track approach, the intervention will combine a package of activities to 1) empowerPWD by providing tailored support at individual level based on technical assessment of needs of persons based on their GAD persons will be targeted through community outreach approach. 2) Build inclusive and accessible living environment at the community level by removing attitudinal, institutional and physical barriers taking into consideration GAD. Tailored support to PWD, local community’s representatives, humanitarian actors and services providers to promote inclusive environment and improve inclusion and accessibility, in line with HNO/HRP 22-23. The activities: roll out IASC guideline, create guidance for actors, awareness sessions; tailored training and define barriers, facilitator factors and existing tools to implement advocacy and awareness; trainings of humanitarian actors, and key stakeholders to improve PWD access to services, WASH facilities, basic needs and livelihood opportunities; Facilitate systematic inclusion of diverse voices and special attention to women, children, youth, elderly, PWD needs. As for M&E, the project is regularly monitored through several approaches such as documentation of success stories, technical assessment, MOUs signed, training plan, KAP surveys and beneficiaries feedback.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Protection of field staff and partners, INGO keep low visibility to protect identities of staff. In case of an attack on activities locations, activates its DoC policy and provide field staff with psychological support to prevent burn out. • INGO applies PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. Also raises awareness on its policy on PSEA and complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. Part of inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to staff and its partners to highlight vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities, person of older age and girls and women (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has protection task force, to ensure protection mainstreaming is optimized. • Staff regularly trained on how to interact, communicate and engage with persons with disabilities. • Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data.• Working through implementing partners: before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. Reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place.• Collecting regular feedback from persons with disabilities about level of satisfaction, success stories and good practices is ongoing during all project cycle.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The INGO expresses its determination to combat the sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment of children and vulnerable adults. And implements measures to reduce risks on programs. Firstly, PSEAH and Child Protection policies and Code of Conduct are in place and PSEAH training and other safeguarding policies for staff and partners are mandatory; all new employees submit background checks. And accompanying family members, trustees, consultants, interns, volunteers are informed of PSEAH policy. Secondly, a whistle blower system and complaint mechanism are in place and open to all complaints incl. PSEA allegations. All beneficiaries receive information sessions at facility and community level with support of the PSEA focal per hub as well as complaint numbers and process. Every allegation is monitored by the country Manager. The complaint officer follows received issues closely and addresses them accordingly. • To address culturally sensitive and risks with GBV and CP, INGO will ensure gender balance of staff, with the aim of increasing number of female staff. •Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a change in rules and regulations


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted. •Lack of acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with stakeholders, community and staff. INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and ensure to keep close coordination with local authorities to maintain positive relations with community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure satisfaction of the community. •Loss of funding is mitigated by the INGO’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding and adapting programs accordingly.•The eventual spread-out of COVID-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this threat, the INGO and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging including DGA; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster •Logistic and fund transfer issues and supply chain interruption, mitigated by assessing transfer partners and enhancing coordination with INGOs and prepare contingency stock. •Mismanagement for cash related issue is mitigated: 1) individual monitoring of beneficiaries, 2) FRM and anti-fraud corruption policy applied.
