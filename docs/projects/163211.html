Project Summary

This project aims to promote and improve psychosocial support & well-being for children affected by the conflict through appropriate education services. The component will integrate gender issues and sensitization to gender-based violence, through teacher training, promotion of equal and safe education for girls, boys, IDPs, or host communities. To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The projects result will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents, boys and girls.

Besoins / Needs

Access to education opportunities: The OCHA situation report revealed that about 90% children have been out of school for nearly three years with 80% of schools closed or burnt. Children forced to flee to bush areas have been left without access to any form of education. More than 4,482 schools (80%) in NW/SW Cameroon have been forced to Close. More than 201,000 school aged children directly affected by the war . The barriers to access to education and reason for increasing out of school children, primarily, include (a) the presence of armed groups and military personal with security threat, (b) closure of or non-functional schools and learning spaces, (c) lack of teachers due to insecurity, and (d) paying school fees .Need of Psychological support for affected children: Children in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon have been exposed to traumatic incidents such as witnessing violence from military and/or NSAGs, destruction of homes and villages, torture and killings, and mass displacement. More than 90% of children are now out of school and are therefore completely exposed to a variety of severe crisis-related protection risks including sexual exploitation and abuse, gender-based violence, harassment and recruitment by armed forces or NSAGs, arbitrary arrest, early marriage, pregnancy and child labour. Children require urgent support to manage their emotions, understand the normal reactions they are having to an abnormal situation, and improve their psychosocial well-being through play-based learning and positive social interactions with peers and adult role models. A sense of normalcy and structure needs to be established before children will be emotionally and socially ready to re-start their learning. In addition, it’s also important to strengthen linkages with GBV actors by increasing teacher’s capacity on safe and ethical referral.Need for alternative education: There are few flexible, alternative education opportunities that accommodate those who do not fit into the formal system. There is no data reflecting the extent to which protection risks, security issues, and gender-based violence (GBV) affects education; however according to the education assessment conduct by NRC, cultural practices in the Far North area exacerbate child marriage, especially girls.
