Project Summary

* All areas targeted by the project has severity above 4 as per the Education severity scale.
*Average cost per beneficiary is around 200 usd per child including 2 NFE cycles, school rehabilitation, school materials, cash for education and protection related activities.
* 5 % of targeted beneficiaries have disabilities.
* Total funding FCA received in 2020 339,174.78
* Total estimated funding FCA received so far in 2021 2,143,623.91
* In 2020 FCA Syria Country Programme has reached 35 575 (direct) beneficiaries (18 292 women, 17 283 men)
The project is part of the FCA work in Hama that is partially funded by SHF/OCHA and ECHO grants.
From April 2017 to May 2018, the Syrian government forces gradually advanced in the northern parts of Hama, which led to the changes of control in those areas. From September 2019, with the improvement in the security situation, the government allowed displaced people to return to their homes. Though most of the towns are severely damaged and the basic services are not available, many families left areas they sought refuge in and returned to their homes. Though the MoE has made some efforts to open classes in the existing schools for returning students, most of the schools are not prepared to receive students - they do not have WASH facilities, doors, windows, fences, or teaching tools and equipment.
FCA has conducted its assessment in five areas of Hama Governorate in January 2021 and found that the number of out of school children in the 5 targeted communities is about 1358 (MoE, Municipalities). The assessment also included the conditions of ﬁve schools in the areas that witnessed a large displacement movement: Karnaz, As-Saan, Kawkab, Halfaya, and Bredage. Based on the information from the local authorities in the targeted locations, the large number of families did not enroll their children in schools, because children of absence of schools, overcrowded schools and children are working to support the families and depend on negative coping mechanism such as early marriage.
The aim of the proposed Action is to improve access to a safe and inclusive education for out of school children and children at risk of dropping out in Hama. The project will be implemented in 5 schools and will serve the communities surrounding those schools. To improve access to quality education, the project offers a systematic package of the activities that tackle various barriers to education - absence of functional schools or their poor physical conditions, absence of dignified WASH facilities, poverty, years of missed education, long distance to schools, poor quality of education, and protection issues. In 5 schools, 62 classrooms and 17 WASH facilities will be rehabilitated, thus creating a safe learning environment. A Cash for Education intervention will promote education of children who are engaged in child labour or are at risk of early marriage. The project will last for 12 months and will include 2 cycles of NFE classes that will allow children that have been out of education to catch up to 2 academic years of education and create conditions for return to formal education. FCA will organize trainings for teachers in EiE modules and TiCC package that include pedagogy skills + practical skills for teachers and include a skills in dealing with the students and positive discipline and classroom management , PSS as well as general student centered pedagogy to increase their professionalism and prepare them for the NFE classes. Protection events, PSS and referral of severe protection cases to specialized services will be also organized through building the capacity of schools. We expect that majority of OOSC children receiving NFE will enroll in formal education.
The project builds on FCA experience in EiE in Syria and particularly on its latest work on EiE in Hama.

4 - Needs

The war in Syria continues to have a profound impact on people across the country. The education sector is impacted by low availability of education services, few functional school, shortage of teachers and especially qualified teachers. The impact of the COVID19 and the rapid devaluation of the Syrian Pound are putting additional strain on the population and local administrations. most of the schools are not prepared to receive students. FCA has conducted its assessment in five areas of Hama Governorate in January 2021 and found that the number of out of school children in the 5 targeted communities is about 1358 (MoE, Municipalities) and even bigger number is at risk of dropping out due to inadequate knowledge for the level a child is enrolled in. The assessment also included the conditions of 5 schools in the areas that witnessed a large displacement movement: Karnaz, As-Saan, Kawkab, Halfaya, and Bredage. The shortage of functional schools and their distance from the homes is one of the main reasons for students to drop out of schools. Based on the information from the local authorities in the targeted locations, the large number of families did not enrol their children in schools, because children are working to support the families and depend on negative coping mechanism such as early marriage. The estimated number of students in the mentioned 5 schools is about 1966 students, schools are:1. Azahraa school /Halfaya/Muhardeh (Severy Scale 4).2. Karnaz Nuhdatheh school/Karnaz (SS 4).3. North Kawkab school/Suran (SS 4).4. As-saan southern school (SS 4).5. Braidage school/Asuqaylabiyah (SS 3).The five schools are in a very poor condition and all of them experience lack of teachers.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1: Insufficiently safe learning facilities.To mitigate the risk the project will invest in school rehabilitation including classrooms, fence and WASH facilities. All works will be supervised and monitored by technical personnel. The NFE classes will only start after the schools have been rehabilitated and are safe to accept the students. RISK 2: COVID19 FCA will take into account COVID19 restrictions and risks, ensuring that both staff and beneficiaries are not being exposed to additional threats as a result of the project. FCA is aware of and will follow the relevant authority’s Covid-19 rules. Learning facilities will have Covid-19 safety protocols in place that include identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health center. RISK 3: Beneficiary and other information is not sufficiently protected. FCA has clear Duty of Care policy that clarifies the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff, volunteers and associated personnel such as facilitators, teachers and other education personnel. FCA adheres to data protection rules. RISK 4: Staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they are serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children.Project staff will be aware and adhere to FCA's policies and procedures to prevent from sexual abuse and exploitation. Through protection events, the awareness of communities will be raised.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Operational Risks related to implementation and approvals. M1: MoU with the MoE has been signed on Jan 13, 2021. It creates background for smooth cooperation with DoE in Hama and Idleb. R2: Delays for national staff to access selected areas of interventions. Access restrictions on international staff. M2: In case FCA is not able to reach the target population, and related delay in start-up and implementation of activities a closer and more systematic coordination and engagement with SARC and MoE will take place. R3: Risk of insecurity, conflict and violence. M3: FCA will continue regularly to update security standard operating procedures and provide refresher training for all staff. In the event that the conflict breaks out, security contingency plans already in place will be followed. Evacuation to safer areas will be evoked when deemed necessary. In case of inaccessibility as a result of insecurity, activities will be moved to safer areas while trying follow beneficiaries to their areas of refuge. R4: High inflation rate. M4: In case the price of goods or services will increase, FCA will inform the donor on the difficulties to follow the agreed plan and proposes revisions. FCA will closely monitor the exchange rate of SYP and will make sure that impact of the exchange rate on the project activities is minimal. Joint action through DINGO. R5: Transfer of funds to Syria. M5: Request donors to send funds directly to Syria. Consultations through DINGO. R6. Increased returnees/IDP caseload leading to potential conflict. M6: Given the current context in the area, the probability of this risk materializing is medium and FCA is aware of estimated returnees numbers. FCA will observe the Displacement Tracking Matrix
