Project Summary

This project aims to deliver life-saving humanitarian aid and support to the most vulnerable populations in Malakal, Renk, Fangak, and Pibor. Covering 4,800 households (approximately 29,486 individuals), the initiative will integrate multi-sectoral assistance including monthly Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), sectoral cash assistance, and essential non-food items through a restricted commodity voucher system. These interventions are designed to address immediate survival needs while promoting resilience and recovery for flood-displaced persons, returnees, and host communities.
The MPCA will provide financial resources to families for essential expenses, distributed every three months across the year, supplemented by sectoral cash for urgent shelter, WASH, and livelihood needs. Activities include needs assessments, community engagement, monitoring, and capacity building. This project integrates a robust monitoring and evaluation system to ensure transparency, track outcomes, and improve programming.
To achieve lasting impact, the initiative prioritizes protection, inclusion, and capacity building for marginalized groups, including women, children, and individuals with disabilities. Partnerships with local leaders, traders, and protection agencies will strengthen accountability and ensure equitable access.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan has escalated, with floods, displacement, and conflict leaving millions in need of urgent assistance. In Upper Nile and Jonglei States, vulnerable host communities, returnees, and internally displaced persons face acute challenges, including food insecurity, lack of shelter, limited access to basic services, and exposure to protection risks. Over 29,486 individuals across Malakal, Renk, Fangak, and Pibor urgently require multi-sectoral assistance to survive and rebuild their lives. The proposed intervention addresses critical gaps identified through comprehensive needs assessments, leveraging cash-based modalities to enable rapid and flexible support. These communities struggle with limited economic opportunities, deteriorating living conditions, and heightened vulnerability due to displacement. The provision of MPCA and sectoral cash will ensure households meet urgent needs, including food, medical care, and education, while restoring their dignity and autonomy. Given the rising risks of gender-based violence, protection concerns, and social exclusion, the project integrates tailored solutions to mitigate harm and promote inclusion. Without immediate intervention, these populations face increased morbidity, mortality, and loss of livelihoods. This project is an essential step toward building resilience and fostering sustainable recovery for South Sudan's most at-risk populations.

2. Project activities

• Need assessment and verification of beneficiary:At the initial stage of the project, HDC will coordinate and collaborate with cash actors on the ground to conduct multi-sectoral needs assessments and risk assessments in Malakal, Renk, Fangak, and Pibor to understand the context. The reports will be shared with the Cash Working Group for approval. During the assessment, the team will conduct in-depth evaluations through household interviews, focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and direct observations.During the registration process, the HDC team will work closely with community leaders, and the Community Selection Committee (CSC) will be used to pre-register households that meet the selection criteria. Final verification with tokens will be done later. Before registration, HDC will engage community members, including women, men, persons with disabilities, and the elderly, to explain the responses.• Provision of unconditional/multi-purpose cash assistance:During the project period, the Humanitarian & Development Consortium (HDC) will respond to 29,486 individuals (4,800 households) in the Upper Nile State counties of Malakal, Renk, Fangak, and Pibor in Jonglei State through a cash and voucher assistance modality known as Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance, which is the most feasible and quickest modality to disburse assistance to returnees, those displaced by floods, and the most vulnerable host communities. Therefore, aid will reach 1,000 households (6,095 individuals) in Malakal, 1,800 families (11,046 individuals) in Renk, 1,000 households (6,238 individuals) in Fangak, and 1,000 households (6,107 individuals) in Pibor to ensure a quality response, the HDC Monitoring and Evaluation team has developed tools to track achievements and changes throughout implementation. The data collected will help make informed decisions. Given the limited banking services in the areas of Malakal, Renk, Fangak, and Pibor, financial service providers will be used to implement a direct cash mechanism for transferring assistance to beneficiaries. During the response, the distribution plan will be shared with beneficiaries through community leaders. This includes a unique code created for each household to differentiate it from others, as well as the location of the site, date of disbursement, number of households to be served, and the amount to be given per household. Team members and the monitoring team will conduct exit interviews• Provision of Sectoral cash through restricted voucher Assistance:During the project period, HDC will implement a one-time sectoral cash assistance program aimed at providing support for shelter, non-food items, WASH supplies, food security and livelihood (FSL) tools, and individual protection through a restricted commodity voucher system. The value of these vouchers will be based on the needs of each household, determined from a set value adopted from various clusters. Eligible items for redemption will include plastic sheets, blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, rubber ropes, wooden poles, bamboo, solar lamps, kanga, buckets, washing soap, jerry cans, seeds, garden tools, fishing gear, crutches, walking sticks, and wheelchairs.• Repaid Monitoring and Post-Distribution Monitoring Exercise: HDC has been implementing cash and Voucher assistance since 2017 in different locations and under different donors such as UNHCR, Norwegian People Aid, Care International, Help Age International, and with support from South Sudan Humanitarian Funds (SSHF). Establishing a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system led an experienced team with support from different clusters. The team will perform a longitudinal survey with beneficiaries in Malakal, Renk, Pibor and Fangak conducting household interviews, focus group discussions, key informant interviews and direct observation to track change over time, which is crucial for assessing household resilience.
