Needs

The humanitarian situation in Northern Somalia in 2018 remains fragile due to residual impact of drought, floods, Cyclone Sagar, conflict and displacements. Although the risk of famine has reduced, humanitarian needs remain high, with extreme water and pasture shortages reported in rainfall deficit areas of Somalia such as Baidoa, Bari, Sool and Banadir. With only a third of the population having access to improved sanitation, poor hygiene practices (including frequent open defecation) are prevalent, contributing to incidences of water-borne diseases. Consequently, there is an urgent need to improve WASH service delivery at district level. In relation to this Islamic relief Somalia will implement a WASH cluster-specific intervention aiming to 1) Provide reliable and sustained access to sufficient safe water, based on identified strategic water points; 2) Establish an appropriate system for the management and maintenance of facilities, with different groups contributing equitably; and 3) Enhance community sanitation and hygiene awareness, improve access of safe water through establishments of water piping system in the IDPs and construction of latrines. IR is proposing a WASH intervention response that addresses the multi-dimensional needs of children and their families’ living in Banadir, Danynile, Qardho, Caynabo and Baidoa IDPs camps. IRS has noted significant increase in the number of people living in these camps as the destitute families who lost their livelihood move to these camps. The increasing number of people living in these IDPs raised a hygiene and sanitation concerns. IRS Somalia conducted primary needs assessment in July and August 2018 that targeted camps 10 IDPs in Banadir, Danynile, Qardho, Caynabo and Baidoa IDPs camps where the majority of drought and conflict displaced community are living it was reported that 60% of the IDPS families interviewed in IDP camps reported that they have less access to water and they buy water from water vendors.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In IR project areas, the IDPs live within or adjacent to the host community which is ensuring durable solutions. Targeting of project locations & beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that are marginalized due to family & clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. IR will consider the safety & protection of women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons & marginalized persons in designing & construction of WASH facilities to preserve safety, dignity & to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements & preferences for location of WASH facilities. To ensure continued access of beneficiaries to the services provided, IR will conduct assessment to establish nature of ownership of the land where IDPs, returnees or host communities reside and where water points are located before intervention. Rehabilitation of construction of WASH facilities will be undertaken if and only if the communities own the sites where the water facilities are located to ensure that they legally own the services/utilities. In locations where there is no security of tenure for the land, temporary solutions are provided as negotiation with authorities take place to ensure IDPs absorb shocks. The project will be linked to government/community systems. IR undertake need assessment with inputs from local authorities to ensure community prioritized needs are addressed, and that the intervention will be supported by the local authorities in the long run. Sustainable access to safe water will be delivered with the installation of boreholes, equipped with elevated water tanks, distribution systems with household connections when possible and hybrid energization systems. Sanitation facilities provided solar lighting and hand washing stations. The communities are also trained on safe use of the facilities through community hygiene promoters. To enhance the project’s sustainability, established or existing water management committees will be trained on operation and maintenance of the new/rehabilitated water assets, which will be handed over to them after project completion. WMC will also be trained on leadership; on water treatment; on book keeping; and will be provided with borehole operation and maintenance tool kit to undertake minor repairs. Sanitation facilities will be handed over to WASH committees who take care of them to ensure they are safely used. IR will also use Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) and Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) for behavioral change. The design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation of all activities will be guided by the application of a gender perspective and an understanding of the root causes of gender inequalities in the Somalia.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IR will target internally displaced people & the surrounding host communities. IDPs have poor access to basic services including WASH services. This exposes women & young girls to sex & gender-based violence. IDP & host communities who are members of weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities & protection threats including economic exploitation, gender-based violence & discrimination. Targeting of project locations & beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that are marginalized due to family & clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. IR will consider the safety & protection of women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons & marginalized persons in designing & construction of WASH facilities to preserve safety, dignity & to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements & preferences for location of WASH facilities. IR will collect data disaggregated by gender & age, which will be used for assessing specific needs of women & other disadvantaged groups. To address the identified challenges, WASH, NFIs & hygiene kits will be given directly to women which will promote a greater role for women in decision-making within targeted households. IR will distribute hygiene kits with appropriate size for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden & without being dependent on others to carry it for them; & will ensure that the hygiene promotion services are respectful & inclusive of cultural & religious practice & there is a proportionate number of female hygiene promoters. IR will share information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures & processes that affect them to ensure transparency & accountability to the beneficiaries. IR will set up accessible, confidential feedback mechanisms for suggestions & complaints.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Key risks include but not limited to lack of WASH facilities that are gender sensitive to the needs of women, girls, boys & men within reasonable distance. For instance, water points, that are in distant places expose women and girls to SGBV. Lack of engagement beneficiaries in decisions that affect their lives, risks exclusion. The target beneficiaries are sometimes treated as recipient of the service without consultation and engaging them in the decision making from the design of an action. Lack of involvement in decision making regarding the delivery of services, especially for women and men from marginalized and vulnerable sections of the community is prevalent. To address the identified challenges, WASH, NFIs and hygiene kits will be given directly to women to promote a greater role for women in decision-making within targeted households. Women, girls, boys, men, and people with disabilities will be consulted on the most appropriate collection points for water trucking and rehabilitation of essential water points based on needs and priorities, which will reduce the amount of time women spend collecting water. This will allow enough time for women to engage in other roles and will promote health seeking behavior by women and under age five, positive nutrition practices. It will also reduce women’s and girls’ exposure to related SGBV risks. Moreover, construction of latrines will ensure that the beneficiaries, especially women, girls, and people with disabilities have access to safe places to protect their dignity and reduce their exposure to risks. IR will also distribute hygiene kits to the most vulnerable people, prioritizing women and girls. IR will distribute hygiene kits with appropriate size for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden & without being dependent on others to carry it for them. IR will set up a complaints response mechanism that will enable women to register complaints privately by phone.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In the WASH sector, the careful selection of sites for water access points is important as it can be a potential flash-point. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff among. During consultative meetings with communities/programme beneficiaries around WASH services and construction, all efforts will be also made to include all relevant groups/clans and ensure all are given opportunity to provide their views. IR applies Do No Harm and conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at all levels. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. IR staffs are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Moreover, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential and consent will be sought if any information such as pictures are to be shared. IR will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.
