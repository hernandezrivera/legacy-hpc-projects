Project Summary

According HNO 2024; over 14.9M Syrians are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary care health assistance. Less than 65% of hospitals are fully functional while 152 of 270 sub-districts (home to 11.76M) are dramatically underserved and suffering from the compounded threat of below-minimum standards for hospital beds, healthcare workers, and functional PHCs per 10,000 pop. A devastating 7.8 magnitude earthquake hit N. Syria in Feb 2023 and further disrupted the health system. Within WHO Syria’s area of operations, 18 hospitals in Aleppo, Hama, Latakia & Tartous governorates sustained damages. At the same time, severe socioeconomic decline has further accelerated health worker migration and hindered patients’ ability to afford not only private care where public services are unavailable, but also transportation costs. Interrupted regimens for NCDs have also increased morbidity and severity of disease, leaving more patients vulnerable to catastrophic effects of the crisis.
In 2024, WHO SYRIA will:
- Procure lifesaving supplies, kits, hemodialysis, cancer care & equipment
- Conduct capacity building on specialized care, IPC, pharmacovigilance & GMP, community IPC, first aid & referral pathway
- Partner with NGOs to support referral services
- Expand neonatal resuscitation
- Strengthen HIS
- Monitor quality & improve service delivery, incl. supervisory visits and meetings
- Rehabilitate SHC/THC infrastructure
- Conduct assessments & surveys
- Develop, print and disseminate guidelines / other materials
In NWS, 13 years of crisis and the devastating earthquake have disrupted and overwhelmed the fragile health system. The EQ led to more than 15k deaths & injuries in NWS and provision of SHC/THC services, including TB/ HIV services, dialysis, and Early Essential Newborn Care, was severely disrupted and left heavily dependent on donors' funding, especially in the absence of any governance structure, which created additional burden on the health system and the continuity of lifesaving services.
In NWS, WHO aims to ensure and maintain the accessibility and utilization of SHC services, while following the Continuity of Operations shift to iNGOs and focusing on key PH functions within SHC and emergency preparedness & response. WHO plans to
-Continue TWG coordination/leadership: service mapping & prioritization, needs & capacity need assessment, health strategies & annual workplan. Provide technical support, monitoring & info-sharing.
-Ensure availability of essential medicines & medical supplies to support SHC service delivery.
-Develop a comprehensive capacity-building strategy to address training requirements of the SHC workforce, incl. dialysis, Early Essential Newborn Care, Thalassemia, Psychiatry & TB/HIV.
-Integrate vertical programs (TB & HIV)
-Build the dialysis service quality improvement program, focusing on technical support, capacity building &monitoring service quality.
-Maintain operations of 16 dialysis centers in NWS via advocacy for resources or filling operational gaps whenever possible.
-Mobilize resources for comprehensive TB/HIV services and last resort coverage in case of funding gaps.
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
