Project Summary

VSFG intends to improve access to food security and nutritional status of conflict affected communities, IDPS and returnees in newly accessible areas of Blue Nile and South Kordofan States - in total 122,500 people - through:
-Provision of agricultural support in the form of assorted vegetable and pulses seeds and extension training on good agricultural practices.
-Establishment and rehabilitation of slaughter slabs, provision of inputs and training.
-Voucher-based treatment program: VSF Germany will use voucher based animal health service delivery system as a strategy to create access to veterinary services for 3,000 vulnerable HH in the project area - a subset of the above mentioned 20,000 HH. This modality is chosen to address targeting vulnerable HHs through distribution of voucher only for selected benefiting HHs, it also involves livestock owners and gives them a choice in the treatment of their animals, this modality is also used as a strong monitoring mechanism to trance the number of animals treated and activity of CAHWs. Targeted vulnerable families will be selected by an emergency animal health intervention committee made up of community leaders/representatives, local authorities and beneficiary community. The selected HHs will then be given vouchers worth up to 8 USD per household in order to contract the services of their respective CAHWs and to pay for the services obtained if any of their animals falls sick from common livestock diseases and also to receive treatment with de-wormers and acaricides to strengthen the health of their core stock. The livestock owner has a choice according to his/her needs and this guarantees the owner makes a choice and no CAHW can decide on his own what drugs will be given to the farmer, and no influential person can divert the use of veterinary drugs for his benefit. The voucher guarantees that the family identified as a beneficiary has the purchasing power with the voucher. And the CAHW needs the voucher to account for the received drugs
- Provision and capacity building on fuel efficient stoves (FES) and Safe Access to Firewood and Alternative Energy (SAFE)
- Protecting key livestock assets which contribute to the food security and income generation of households through provision of veterinary services, restocking and supplementary animal feed.
-Breeding goats Restocking programs: This activity will be undertaken to support targeted beneficiaries to rebuild their assets and contribute to the food security of targeted HHs. In this project a total of 1,200 dairy goats will be procured from the local market through contracting existing community based income generation groups, then they will be distributed to 300 households in the four intervention localities. Beneficiaries will be targeted with particular emphasis on female-headed household, household with malnourished children and households who have lost their livestock.
- Increasing household income through supporting women groups in food processing (fish processing, cheese making and ago-food preservation, techniques etc.) and cash transfer programing in order to cover immediate needs such as food and other basic services.
-Cash transfer modality is chosen to assist extremely vulnerable HHs to access their basic needs including food and other commodities and services. This modality is flexible, it also empowers beneficiaries to make their own choices, preferences and to prioritize.Unconditional multipurpose cash transfer to vulnerable HHs to enable them to procure particularly food items while giving them choice and flexibility to spend some of the cash on NFIs, health or education; depending on their more pressing needs. Only heads of selected vulnerable households will be registered in beneficiaries’ registry indicating name, village and contact of beneficiary. There are a key criteria for targeting unconditional cash transfer beneficiaries. Markets are assessed and the amount of transfer determined accordingly.

Needs

This project locations include newly accessible areas in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States. Habila, Dalami and Abassiya Localities will be targeted in South Kordofan, while Gissan, Bau and Roseries Localities will be targeted in Blue Nile. The project sites include IDPs settlements, return villages and IDPs who reside with host communities in their villages to address the needs of both.The project is designed as an integrated program to address the interwoven needs across the following sectors: Food security and WASH, with smaller components also contributing to improved nutrition and interventions with animal health focus contributing to improved food security and livelihood. The proposed activities are meeting the needs pertaining to these sectors and are expected to bring the desired change in regard to the following needs: In 2017 some areas of South Kordofan were affected by drought and increase of cereal and non-cereal prices due to low production and high cost of transportation. This has reduced poor households’ access to food from the markets. In the current season, rainfall continued to be below average in some parts - including the target areas - of South Kordofan, leading to delayed crop establishment (FEWS NET Key Message Update). It has been indicated that in Habila Locality there are approximately 27,000 IDPs returnees and residents, in Dalami Locality there are 28,625 IDPs, returnees and residents and in Abassiya Locality there are 32,502 IDPs, returnees and residents who are in need for interventions to improve their food security. In Habila and Dalami 11 villages were assessed; the villages are inhabited by 15,990 returnees, 12,600 residents and 4,500 IDPs, 91% of the people practice agriculture as their main livelihood, although IDPs don’t have sustainable access to arable lands. There is need to increase the awareness of farmers and agro-pastoralists in good cultural practices, particularly pest control and to support extension services to improve crop and animal production performances. Veterinary centres in the three localities need to be supported to carry out animal treatment and expand animal vaccination to avoid cross-border disease transmission. Recently, there has been Lumpy Skin Disease, Sheep Pox, Haemorrhagic Septicaemia and Peste des Petits Ruminants outbreaks in the state. The water resources in the assessed villages include one water yard, 12 hafirs of which 8 need rehabilitation and 46 pumps of which 24 need rehabilitation.In Blue Nile, households are suffering from food insecurity, almost over 90% of the assessed households have no food stocks in their homes, they totally depend on local markets to secure their food, as well as the market survey conducted shows critical increase in commodities prices including basic food which is beyond the purchasing capacity of poor households. Dietary diversification is very poor among respondents, almost 80% of them are completely dependent on cereals.

Activities

Rehabilitation of Roseries Slaughter Slab that provides meat for domestic consumption of all populations in the Roseries locality including IDPs.Training of 30 butchers on meat handling hygiene, the 5-days training will focus on improved hygiene practices in slaughter of livestock, meat processing and marketing stages.Provision of agricultural support in the form of assorted cereals, legumes, vegetable and pulses seeds to 3000 HHs. This will target IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities in the project areas. Priority would be given to women headed HHs, farmer HHs with more than 6 children, elderly. Extension training on vegetable gardening will be provided to 40 farmer HHs to act as village extension agents Support mass livestock vaccination campaigns: VSFG will support the local government livestock vaccination campaigns across the targeted localities for 20,000 HHs (100,000 beneficiaries) from IDPs, returnees, host communities and pastoralists.Voucher-based veterinary service training for government staff, CAHWs and beneficiaries. VSFG will use voucher based animal health service delivery system as a strategy to create access to veterinary services for 3,000 vulnerable HHs in the project areas - a subset of the above mentioned 20,000 HH. This modality is chosen to address targeting vulnerable HHs through distribution of voucher only for selected benefiting HHs, it also involves livestock owners and gives them a choice in the treatment of their animals Restocking with lactating goats to 600 vulnerable HHs (3,000 Beneficiaries) in the 2 states.Provision of concentrated animal feed for restocked goats for the period of three months. Provision of unconditional cash for 600 extremely vulnerable HHs for 3 months on the bases of 75 SDG per day.Provision of capacity building training on cash based programming to government staffs and community.Provision of training on fuel efficient stoves (FES) and Safe Access to Firewood and Alternative Energy (SAFE).

Impact on environment/natural resources

VSF-G will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. It will be taken into consideration that the transfer of resources (project inputs and services including NFIs, cash and agricultural commodities) into a resource-scarce environment can trigger conflict in the communities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas, and creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of: (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc. (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (ii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers/tensions and connectors/local capacities for peace. VSF-G will work to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build on connectors that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. Through its implementation approaches and measures, VSF-G will ensure that no negative impact should result from this project including: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, inclusion of diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, or youth in project activities and leadership structures.
