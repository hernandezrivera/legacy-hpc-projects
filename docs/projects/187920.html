Project Summary

The project aims to provide the most urgent life-saving humanitarian assistance through integrated intervention, including winterization interventions and NFIs, and better-dignified shelter solutions in NW Syria for the most vulnerable people in NW Syria.
The main accomplishment for the proposed interventions is to enhance the situation for the current shelter to improve the living conditions for those IDP's living in the informal settlements' self-settled sites and enhance the privacy for the targeted beneficiaries
to ensure their protection and safety as well. This achievement can be achieved by conducting the following activities:
a. Establishing dignified shelter units that would contribute to the elevation and promotion of the living conditions of IDPs./ (Target: 2,500 individuals / 500 HHs/ $2,000 per shelter unit)
b. Provision/Replacement for the worn tents in the IDPs' informal settlements.(Target: 1,500 individuals / 300 HHs / $350per tent)
c. Construction of community infrastructures in camps (drainage, ground preparation/leveling of the site, etc.) (Target: 7,500 individuals / 1500 HHs / 10 camps/ $134 per HHs)
d. Provision for seasonal assistance such as stove, heating fuel in-kind. (Target: 10,000 individuals / 2000 HHs / in-kind olive-pit fuel with Stoves, $150 per HHs)
e. Provision for NFI kits includes essential items to the IDP's such as kitchen kits.(Target: 10,000 individuals / 2000 HHs / in-kind standard NFIs kits, $170 per NFI kit)

4 - Needs

According to the (HNO 2022/MSNA 2021) for Syria estimates, 13.4 million people require humanitarian assistance, of which the highest number (2.7 million) are located in Aleppo governorate. An estimated 6.7 million IDPs are in need, many of whom live in protracted displacement, while years of conflict have increased demand amongst host community members. COVID-19 has further undermined the ability of conflict-affected host and IDP households to meet basic needs. According to the MSNA 2021, fewer households live in damagedapartments/buildings (20%) compared to the MSNA 2020 (28%). However, families reportedly living in last resort sites more than doubled from about 3% (MSNA 2020) to about 7% (MSNA 2021).Over 2 million people are internally displaced in various locations in NWS, and many of the people continue to need assistance in a shelter. Given the protracted conflict, annual tent replacement is no longer viable. The Shelter/NFI Cluster estimates about 1.7 million people live in 1,400 in unplanned sites that are unsafe and prone to devastation during the flooding season. Deterioration of the HH’s ability to meet basic needs is observed across all population groups. Current low wages reduced productivity, hyperinflation, and a general loss of employment in specific sectors all indicate a loss of purchasing power manifested by insufficiency of available income to cover expenditures related to basic needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

According to the type of the mentioned intervention activities (Provision/Replacement for the worn tents, infrastructures, Provision for seasonal assistance, construction dignified shelter units), The deep needs evaluation will be started by conducting a RNA for IDP’s Sites which are located in the targeted area which Rahma Worldwide has undertaken to identify the gaps and needs for the response taking into consideration the dynamic changes in the humanitarian context with keeping the flexibility of tuning the intervention according to the need. While the project will be targeting the most vulnerable people in camps, the project, once is approved, will be started by coordination with the SNFI Cluster to avoid any potential overlap with the other active humanitarian actors. The guidelines which are developed by the clusters and the SoP for the related programs which are developed by Rahma Worldwide will be taken into consideration during the design of the activities. The most vulnerable IDP’s sites will be identifed using the vulnerability criteria, which is recommended by the SNFI, along with the developed selection criteria by Rahma Worldwide. After finalizing the identification for the IDP’s sites, a technical evaluation will be conducted for the targeted areas to prepare the BoQ’s taking into consideration the PSEA, GBV, AAP to ensure the community engagement especially for the Women, PwD’s, Children and elderly benefciaries.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Risks: (No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place - Mixed distribution lines for males and females - All distribution staff are males - No shared clear information about timing and content of distribution -Taxation on the assistance )Rahma Worldwide will train all project staff on its protection policy, including the child safeguarding policy and the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse policy. Rahma Worldwide also will raise awareness about PSEA, in coordination with PSEA Network. - Rahma Worldwide will mainstream PSEA policy including training of Rahma Worldwide`s personnel in addition to signing Rahma Worldwide protection policy and COC by all of Rahma Worldwide personnel, volunteers, partners, suppliers, services providers, and all relevant persons. - Activating the complaint mechanism including protection incidents and penalty procedures. RWW will ensure separate lines for males and females in all distribution/redemption processes and ensure gender balance in the hiring process and clear information about timing, contents, and procedures of the distribution with emphasizing (this assistance is Free). - Rahma Worldwide will assign a PSEA focal point to ensure the implementation of all protection principles, prevent any abuse, and conduct the protection training and awareness-raising sessions related to PSEA and CSG topics, with coordination and supervising of Rahma Worldwide Protection Program.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlapping, losing some locations due to other NGOs intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) - coordinated with all the stakeholder during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - Involved the camps management committees,LCs,KIs in the process in case of any conflict - Rahma Worldwide will be monitoring all updates on the ground, and if we noticed that any of the beneficiaries are displaced, we will change the selected beneficiaries and choose other beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the same location. 4: Increased number of cases infected with COVID - Training Rahma Worldwide teams about best practices, including social distance, sterilization and wearing face masks. - Providing teams with PPE kits (gloves, sterilization and face masks). - Rahma Worldwide distribution teams will organize beneficiaries and make the social distance. 5- Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the eld staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding sexual exploitation and abuse, and investigation if necessary (hotline)
