Project Summary

South Sudan is currently facing a multitude of crises, including subnational and inter-communal violence, unprecedented floods, an economic crisis, and widespread crime. These factors are the primary causes of the humanitarian needs in South Sudan, as identified in the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2024. The objective of the project is to analyse and develop strategies to enhance community resilience in South Sudan, specifically in the states and counties of Jonglei (Pibor Administrative Area), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil East & South), and Warrap (Tonj North) This will be achieved by addressing the challenges posed by food insecurity resulting from climatic conditions and inter-communal violence. The project is focused on addressing the needs of individuals who have returned to their home country, refugees, individuals who have been internally displaced, and the communities that are hosting them. Activities refer to a set of actions or tasks that are performed to achieve a specific goal or Deliver food aid to mitigate the risk of famine and enhance food intake, nutritional variety, and adaptive mechanisms for populations at high vulnerability levels (classified as IPC 4 and 5). A total of 58284 recipients are involved in improving and maintaining emergency food production by utilising supplementary inputs for vegetable and crop livelihoods, engaging in fishing activities, and raising livestock. A total of 58284 households and individuals are beneficiaries of agricultural inputs, vegetables, and fishing kits. A total of 8 kitchen gardens have been established at various nutrition facilities, educational institutions, and residential households in targeted counties (Aweil East, Aweil South, Pibor and Tonj North). Minimise reliance on food and agricultural inputs to enhance households' resilience in coping with shocks and adapting to changing circumstances in targeted counties (Aweil East, Aweil South, Pibor and Tonj North). HAC South Sudan leads the South Sudan Joint Response (2024-2026), with implementation in Tonj North and Pibor. In partnership with UNICEF, we implement an Education in Emergencies project in Wau County and an Early Childhood Development (ECD) project in Pibor. We also have a privately funded ECD project in Pibor, where we include an element on food security and livelihoods (FSL) through kitchen gardening. HAC South Sudan has previous experience implementing the Participatory Integrated Plan (PIP), a sustainable livelihoods approach adapted to emergency conditions, working with value chains and economic empowerment for women and training people in climate-smart agriculture through ReGrow. In 2023, 14.7% of our international budget went towards FSL activities.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is worsening due to a combination of factors, including conflict, food insecurity, displacement, disease outbreaks, climate-related stocks such as flooding, and economic instability. The arrival of returnees and refugees fleeing conflict in Sudan is further straining the country’s fragile infrastructure and resources. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) from September to November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people—47% of the population analysed—are expected to face IPC Phase 3 or higher (crisis levels of food insecurity or worse). Of these, around 1.71 million will be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), while 41,000 will experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe), indicating extreme food shortages. During the harvest and post-harvest season from December 2024 to March 2025, it is projected that about 6.1 million people (45% of the population analysed) will remain in IPC Phase 3 or above, with 1.71 million in IPC Phase 4 and 31,000 returnees facing IPC Phase 5. As the lean season begins in April and extends through July 2025, food security is expected to deteriorate further, affecting an estimated 7.69 million people (57% of the population analysed). This includes 2.53 million in IPC Phase 4 and 63,000 projected to be in IPC Phase 5, representing catastrophic levels of food insecurity (IPC, 2024). Populations facing the most severe food insecurity are concentrated in areas heavily impacted by recurring climate shocks, such as floods and droughts, compounded by the ongoing economic crisis marked by currency devaluation and soaring food prices. Additionally, conflict and insecurity—including the spillover from the conflict in Sudan—have led to widespread displacement, diminished agricultural output, and reduced access to humanitarian aid. Critical issues that cut across all forms of assistance include the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), addressing gender-based violence (GBV), and promoting appropriate conduct and accountability standards. HAC South Sudan and other humanitarian staff, volunteers, visitors, and suppliers will be oriented on ‘common topics from the registration to the distribution phases. All visitors and suppliers (e.g., transport services) will also be oriented on PSEA, expected behaviours, and HAC South Sudan policies before being deployed to the field. The Food Security and Protection Officer will be responsible for ensuring that awareness sessions will be conducted and monitored for compliance. The feedback and complaint mechanisms will be widely explained and publicized. Each site will undergo a safety audit led by the Gender and Child Protection Officer. It is important to evaluate site compliance with key security issues, including location security establishments (army barracks), gender participation, child protection, and inclusiveness of access for all vulnerable groups. Concurrent monitoring for continuous compliance will also be done as part of the distribution accounting process. The beneficiary verification process will be conducted during and after the beneficiary’s selection and registration to determine the targeting efficiency, check inclusion and exclusion errors, and identify “ghost” beneficiaries and inflated households’ sizes.

2. Project activities

1. Training of 24 groups on FFS (consisting of 1000 farmers) Through the AEW, the project will form/revive and support 12 farmers groups consisting of 500 farmers applying the farmer field school method in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap and Jonglei States. 2. Establish seed multiplication and demonstration training plots (24): The project will promote the multiplication of locally adapted seeds by building the capacity of farmers on modern seed production and handling skills. 3. Training of 04 framer’s groups on quality seed and seedling production: Local farmers largely plant materials through their stock, social networks, and local markets. 4. Training 300 farmers in post-harvest handling: To enhance standards, HAC will organize training of AEAs as Training of Trainers (ToT) on USAID’s Post-Harvest Handling Training Manual for South Sudan before delivering training to participating farmers on timely harvesting and post-harvest handling practices like threshing, drying, sorting, grading, and storage to minimize post-harvest losses while ensuring high-quality final products are available for sale. 5. Provision of farming tools: During the first and second sessions of the project, 300 farmers in all the locations will be supported with farming tools (sickles, hoes, pangas)6. Strengthen the marketing information system and sharing through CMCs: Through the project, the capacity of CMCs will be strengthened to conduct routine agricultural market surveys and mapping to identify agricultural market opportunities and constraints, main actors and interrelationships, current and expected demand for goods, as well as policy initiatives to strengthen the business environment 7. Rain farmers/beneficiaries on agroprocessing (produce mills, value addition, fish processing, and preservation)—based on roadmaps/pathways for agroprocessing opportunities for Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Jonglie, agroprocessing and value addition have largely remained an untapped and unexplored value chain area.8. Establish community drying yards/storage facilities: To ensure product quality and promote collective marketing committee initiatives, the project will work on a need basis (where production evidence can be provided) with producers to identify critical community needs and establish community storage and bulking facilities. 9. Establish a kitchen garden at nutrition facilities, schools, or households10. Facilitate comprehensive livelihoods and market assessment. 11. Training of farmers and extension workers refresher training: HAC shall offer additional refresher training to model farmers and extension workers. The refresher training shall be conducted once every year. 12. Food value addition: HAC has trained a group of 30 women in food value addition in the past year. HAC will train 300 more women in value addition. HAC will also build on its prior expertise in training women to preserve okra and fish.13. Distribution of fish gear, seeds, and farming tools. The proposed activities will help farmers obtain crop seeds, vegetable seeds, and farming tools, making them more productive for populations severely hit by the health crisis.14. Accountability to the affected person (AAP): collection of complaints and feedback from the community using news tools developed by Help a Child Global.
