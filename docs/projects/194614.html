Project Summary

UNH is a dual mandated organization that provides development & emergency response. Based on our experience in emergency response, developed this project following consultative meetings with the respective state MOHs, CHDs, local authority, and affected population including elderly and People with disability. UNH has operation presence in Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria. Warrap, Jonglei, Greater Pibor and Upper Nile States providing essential health services.
UNH’s HRP for 2023 targets Magwi, Duk, Pibor, Tonj North & Twic targeting Host communities, IDPs and returnees. These counties except Magwi are inhabited mainly by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, interclan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility and humanitarian crisis. Magwi inhabited by farmers, experiencing intercommunal violence since February 2022 between cattle herders from other states & the indigenous, has displaced over 17,000 (NGO Forum daily security update March3,2022)
Health needs of Magwi, Duk, Pibor, Tonj North & Twic enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, infection prevention/control measures, food insecurity, flood, COVID-19 pandemic and threat of disease outbreaks, poor EPI coverage, limited health facility functionality, stockouts of essential medicines/supplies.
November 2022 IPC puts Duk/Magwi in IPC 3; Pibor/Twic/Tonj North in IPC 4 with small proportion of population in IPC 5 in Pibor for period October - November 2022, Magwi/Duk projected to be in IPC3, Pibor/Twic/Tonj North in IPC 4 from December - March 2023; Magwi in IPC 3 from April - July 2023 while Pibor/Twic/Tonj North/Duk in IPC 4. Coupled conflicts, contracted economy, effect of COVID-19 and now EVD, will drive malnutrition rate and health indicators in these counties will deteriorate if no emergency health services supported
In 2023, UNH will support PHCCs/PHCUs & mobile clinics to provide emergency and essential primary healthcare services to the vulnerable population. Will implement following activities which are in line with health cluster specific objectives and will contribute to the HRP objectives.
• Implement emergency preparedness, contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases including COVID-19/EVD.
• Provide EPI services.
• Provide BeMONC services.
• Provide life-saving essential health services including management of non-communicable diseases.
• Integrate & provide MHPSS/SGBV services.
• Strengthen IPC in our health facilities.
• Conduct community consultative meetings & strengthen feedback mechanism.
• Provide technical/operational assistance to HFs through supportive supervision.
• Provide community health services using BHI and strengthen referral system.
• Provide services for people with disability.
• Conduct community sensitization, disseminate IEC materials include those for COVID-19/EVD.
• Participate in RRM.
• Coordinate with core pipeline managers to provide emergency kits.
• Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.
• Provide management for SAM children with medical complication.
• Support Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care.
Direct project beneficiaries 70,000 (7858 male U5, 6667 female U5, 11643 male 5-17 years; 11793 female 5-17 years; 12627 male 18-60 years, 13823 female 18-60 years; 2240 male >60 years, 3349 females > 60 years). Out this, 6,580 are People with Disabilities (1833 male under 17, 1735 female under 17, 1187 male 18-60 years, 1299 female 18-60 years; 211 males >60 years, 315 females > 60 years).
To implement those activities, UNH needs 2,000,000 USD in 2023, aligned with health cluster’s strategy on cost per beneficiaries. Will work with other health, Nutrition/WASH/FSL/Protection partners to enhance synergies, will engage CHD & development health partners in each county to take over at the end of the project to ensure sustainability.

including outbreak detection, outbreak investigations and case management for selected diseases including COVID-19.• Provide Immunization at mobile and static facilities against vaccine preventable diseases and strengthen the capacity of health workers on EPI (cold chain management and handling of vaccines) and COVID-19 vaccination. • Build the capacity of health workers on BeMONC and provide BeMONC services in our primary health centres. • Provide life-saving essential quality health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through our static health facilities and mobile clinics and build the capacity of health workers on provision of lifesaving essential health care.• Integrate and provide MHPSS services in all our health facilities. • Integrate and provide services for SGBV including CMR in all our health facilities and enhance referral to safe houses where necessary. • Strengthen IPC in our health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities.• Conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected populations and strengthen feedback mechanism to ensure beneficiaries provide feedback on the project.• Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services.• Provide community health services using Boma Health Initiatives and strengthen the linkage with facility-based services. • Provide services for people with disability.• Conduct community sensitization and disseminate IEC materials include those for COVID-19.• Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2023. • Coordinate with core pipeline managers to provide emergency kits (WHO Interagency emergency kits, UNFPA Reproductive Health Commodities including Rape kits and cold chain equipment including vaccines from UNICEF) to support our mobile and static facilities.• Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.• Conduct MUAC screening for children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women to identify MAM and SAM cases and refer to TSFP and OTP centres respectively. • Provide management for SAM children with medical complication in our primary healthcare centres.• Support the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA) on Health Care and provide regular report on the attacks. • Support and submit timely, accurate and complete reports through EWARS, IDSR and 5Ws. • Coordinate the health response with Health Clusters, MoH and all levels, CHDs, partners including UN agencies, national and international NGOs to enhance synergies.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We have accountability and feedback mechanism in place, will ensure the system is robust, confidential and safe. Will raise awareness and inform the vulnerable population (people disabilities, illiterate, women and girls including the elderly) to know their rights and entitlements. We will encourage these vulnerable groups of people to provide feedback through Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), the RRC has representatives at all levels from State to Boma levels, they will be informed to collect and share feedback from the vulnerable groups.We will engage and encourage the most vulnerable persons to provide feedback through their chiefs, community/religious/youths/women leaders and associations of people with disabilities. Today most of the illiterate and other group of most vulnerable people have mobile phones where telephone networks available, will share with them the hotline for providing feedback and will encourage them to use their local language which will be recorded and interpreted in case the receiver does not understand the language. We will ensure that the receiver calls back to get details feedback since the most vulnerable may not have adequate airtime units.We will also encourage the most vulnerable to provide feedback through the members of household they trust who may have access to UNH provided means of collecting feedback.Our mobile team will be trained and task with the responsibilities of collecting feedback from the most vulnerable since they will be reaching to the hard-to-reach areas and providing services to the most vulnerable who may have no access to the fixed health facilities.We will always encourage the participation of the most vulnerable people in the monthly and quarterly review meetings so that they provide feedback.

Accountability to affected populations is one of UNH’s key commitments under the core humanitarian standards, and we are therefore committed to ensuring that affected populations are meaningfully involved in projects and have the ability to give feedback and make decisions about anything affecting them.UNH had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups and minority groups- elderly and people with disabilities) of Pibor, Duk, Tonj North, Twic, and Magwi, needs of each group of people incorporated into this proposal, no activity will be carried out in an isolation/fragmented manner as the proposed project aims to adopt a comprehensive approach based on participation of all stakeholders. The copy of this proposal has been shared with our focal persons in each county who discussed with the County Health Department (CHDs), County Relied/Rehabilitation Commissions (RRCs) and the affected persons, their feedbacks incorporated into this final version hence county authorities and the affected populations are involved in the designing of this project.UNH will launch the project in each county where State MOH, CHD, local authorities at Payam/Boma levels, community/religious/youths’ leaders, affected populations including elderly and people with disabilities and all stakeholders will be actively involved, project documents will be shared and meeting will be conducted with community leaders and affected populations, they will be informed about the project. They will actively participate in the implementation phase, joint monitoring and evaluation, monthly/quarterly review/feedback meetings will be conducted where affected persons give feedback on the quality of the service delivery. 80% of our local staffs implementing UNH ongoing project in targeted counties are from the affected population and will continue with our philosophy of empowering the affected populations hence will recruit most of the staffs for this emergency response locally. 45% of our staffs are females and will encourage and recruit more women at all level of positions in this project. We will continue to capacitate the local staffs and be actively involved in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of this project and will continue to build their capacity through supportive supervisions, mentorship and refresher trainings. UNH will strengthen the existing and form additional local management and community committees (members from the affected population), capacitate them to monitor the project.We have accountability and feedback mechanism in place, will ensure the system is robust, confidential and safe. Will raise awareness and inform the beneficiaries including people with disabilities to know their rights and entitlements. Have suggestion boxes in all operation areas and an email address and dedicated feedback hotline allowing for anonymous feedback, while the illiterate and people with disabilities can provide feedback through their community/youth/religious leaders, chiefs and county relief and rehabilitation commissions, also during monthly/quarterly feedback meetings. Such feedback is monitored by our M&E focal points, feedbacks received are always analysed and use to improve our strategies on service delivery and other cross-cutting issues.UNH has a code of conduct, all staffs have been oriented on the it and humanitarian principle of “Do No Harm”, copies given to them, all have signed it. New recruits will be oriented on this code of conduct, will be refreshed quarterly, will instil our core values to all staffs so that they provide holistic care to the beneficiaries while respecting the community system, principle of Do No Harm and Global humanitarian principles. The beneficiaries will be encouraged to provide feedback on the behaviours of our staffs, feedback will be analysed and respond to it accordingly.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

United Networks for Health has Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy which guides UNH employees including its board of trustees, consultants, volunteers, collaborators, suppliers, sub-contractors and anyone who has signed an agreement with UNH. We have trained our staffs on PSEA and all signed the policy in addition to UNH code of conduct, new recruits will be oriented. We have and will print more banners, posters and flyers on PSEA which will be placed in the offices, health facilities, mobile posts and given to literate population accessing our services to get information on PSEA.We have integrated information on PSEA on our routine health education conducted at the health facilities and communities. We will strengthen the system to ensure that our beneficiaries are well informed about UNH policy on PSEA. People with disabilities and elderly will be reached through mobile services where they will be informed on PSEA.We will include a session on PSEA during monthly and quarterly review meetings where affected population/beneficiaries including their leaders- youths/religious/women/community/chiefs are always invited and will be requested to disseminate the information on PSEA to the communities.We will orient the local management and community committees (members from the affected population) on PSEA to ensure dissemination of PSEA to the communities.UNH will collaborate with protection actors in our areas of operation to ensure beneficiaries are reached with key information on PSEA. We will ensure that the beneficiaries are well informed that all the humanitarian assistance UNH provides are free and the services are offered equitable with no discrimination and favouritism, no exchange of services for sexual purposes. And reporting channels for SEA will be widely disseminated ensuring that the system is safe and confidential.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project has been designed following consultative meetings with the communities and their leaders, people with disabilities/their associations and elderly also included. The project will be launched and the beneficiaries will be informed so that they know the project to enable them provide sound feedbacks.Feedbacks provided by the beneficiaries will be analysed to guide the project implementation. Feedbacks can be positive showing the satisfaction by the beneficiaries in such a scenario the team will be encouraged to continue with implementation ensuring all in line with the planned activities and workplan. In scenario of negative feedbacks, the management will quickly do further analysis and if it is within the scope of the project then will reprogram the implementation plan internally to ensure improvement in performance to achieve the desired deliverables within the set timeline, will also increase the level of monitoring to keep the project on track. In case the feedback analysis shows the demands of the beneficiaries are beyond the scope of work stipulated in the project document, then further consultation will be conducted swiftly with the beneficiaries (women/men, girls/boys, people with disability of different age groups and elderly) and the leaders at different levels (state to Boma levels) to understand the deviation of the needs of the affected population. If the needs are paramount and beyond our scope of work then will contact our donors and request for project revision with clear justification and evidence from the beneficiaries. This will help to ensure that resources are channelled to address the humanitarian needs of the affected populations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The mitigate risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). UNH will integrate basic messages on GBV, child protection, eviction and explosion hazards in our awareness messages which will be conducted at the fixed and mobile health facilities as well community. We will collaborate and work closely with protection and mine action partners on the ground to ensure synergies and enhance referral. We will also work with other health partners and refer survivors of SGBV/GBV to their facilities where we don’t provide comprehensive care- CMR (at mobile and PHCU levels).South Sudan has diversity of cultures where in some areas the most vulnerable especially people with disabilities, elderly, women, girls and boys are excluded in decision making and accessing humanitarian assistance. We normally conduct assessment to identify and map out the most vulnerable groups in our project areas, we do mobilization and also involve the chiefs, community /religious/women leaders and association of people with disabilities to identify and invite the marginalised groups for consultative meetings ensuring that their needs are taken into consideration while developing the proposal and are engage throughout the project cycle. People with disabilities, elderly and those in hard-to-reach areas will be reached through mobile team who will ensure their needs are identified and they participate in decision making for this project. The project will be launched and the state, county and Payam authorities and the affected populations will be informed about the objectives and targets of the project so that all including marginalised groups are well informed about the project. We will provide humanitarian assistance to the marginalised groups without discrimination and those with limited access to the services will be reached through mobile services. There are instances where county authority would push an emergency responder to response in the areas of their interest (villages where they come from) not based on the needs. To mitigate such external interference, we will brief and share the project documents with the state and county authorities and let them know the target beneficiaries. The authorities and the communities will be sensitized about the project and project target beneficiaries. Our team being South Sudanese understand the context well and based on UNH code of conduct will work with the local authorities transparently to reach to the target beneficiaries. Any external forces interfering with the beneficiary’s selection will be reported and action taken appropriately. Land disputes and insecurity caused by it is not uncommon in our project areas. Such disputes normally lead to insecurity affecting delivery of essential basic services, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure. In Magwi, there is high risk of land disputes as most of the returnees will find their land occupied and getting them back may lead to disputes. People of Magwi are farmers and will have problem with pastoralists who are crazing their cattle in the farmland. We will work closely with the peace and reconciliation commission at county level who will able to detect early warning signs of such disputes and resolves the problems amicably before escalating. The inhabitants of Pibor, Duk, Tonj North and Twic are mainly pastoralists, land disputes normally arise over the grazing land. In all these counties we will work closely with community leaders and county peace commission to detect early warning signs and respond to it appropriately. UNH is non-political organization, does not take sides, will continue to work closely with community leaders, state/county authority, UNOCHA and UNDSS to ensure safety of humanitarian workers and access to provide lifesaving services.

UNH will work closely with the state, county and community leaders in flood prone counties to determine early warning signs for the flooding and develop flood contingency plan in line with state and national Health Custer flood contingency plan to enhance timely response to the flooding.UNH will support community initiative in construction of Dykes to minimize population displacement and destruction of infrastructure and livelihoods.UNH will advocate for and be part of the forum for discussion of flooding at national, state and county level and uses it past experience on response to flood to contribute towards planning to reduce displacement due to flooding.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

UNH team providing health services are guided by UNH code of conduct and PSEA policies and humanitarian principles of Do No Harm. We response and manage all those at risks- Children and women and GBV survivors without discrimination ensuring all are treated equally with respect and dignity as we provide holistic care looking as a person as whole. We will work and collaborate with protection partners and all the development and emergency health partners in our project locations. Will integrate basic information on child protection, general protection and GBV/SGBV in our routine health awareness conducted at the facilities, mobile posts and communities, will also print and put posters and banners with information on protection and referral pathways at the facilities and public places, this will ensure that those at risks and GBV survivors are timely referred to protection partners and to health facilities for medical care and mental health psychosocial services. We will strengthen referral pathways between communities and health facilities/protection partners and between protection actors and health partners to enhance referral of GBV survivors from the communities to the health facilities for medical intervention and to protection partners for management of protection risks including safe houses where necessary and the protection actors taking lead in the legal aspect of the services provided. This collaboration and enhanced referral system will lead to timely response to and increase access to quality services for GBV/SGBV survivors management. UNH will capacitate its and CHD staffs on management of SGBV survivors through refresher training and on job training and mentorship.We will orient the community/youth/religious/women leaders, chiefs and association of people with disabilities on risks, mitigation measures and referral pathways to reduce stigma hindering reporting cases of GBV/SGBV and enhance early referral of the GBV survivors for protection services.We will conduct radio talk shows to create awareness on child protection and GBV, also work with schools to create similar awareness in schools and market places in our areas of operation

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

UNH will integrate basic components of nutrition, food security and livelihoods into its programming and ensuring that information on nutrition, food security, coping mechanisms and resilience shared with the target populations. UNH will work closely with nutrition and Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) partners to lobby and support General Food Distribution (GFD) through World Food Programme to the target populations and provision of school feeding assistance to school girls and boys. We will encourage the population to engage in farming, fishing, livestock keeping and micro-business projects with support from Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) to improve their livelihoods.Work with WASH partner to enhance hygiene, sanitation and provision of clean drinking water to the target population to minimize risk of waterborne diseases.Work closely with community leaders and involve community in preparedness and early warning signs for inter-communal violence, floods which are the main contributing factors for the food insecurity in the conflict and flood affected areas.We will conduct screening for sign of acute malnutrition among children under 5 to ensure early detection and management. This helps to minimize progression of MAM children to SAM and SAM getting medical complications. Will also manage SAM children with medical complications in our health facilities to reduce mortality and ensuring good treatment outcome. We will integrate information of Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) on routine health education and promotion conducted at the fixed and mobile health facilities. Will work closely with nutrition partners and strengthen referral systems to ensure that MAM and SAM children identified in UNH facilities are referred timely to nutrition partners to get the required services, also SAM children with medical complications are referred to UNH facilities for proper diagnosis and management.
