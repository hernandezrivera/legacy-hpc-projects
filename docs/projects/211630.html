Project Summary

Syria continues to be one of the most complex protracted conflict humanitarian crises globally, exposed to high levels of ongoing conflict, and prone to natural disasters and epidemic outbreaks. These factors have forced an increase of 1.4M of people in need (PIN) since 2023, totaling 16.7M (HNO, 2024) people in need in Syria. Given the myriad needs and vulnerabilities, Concern proposed this action across Northwest Syria (NWS). Concern has proposed intervention areas in line with the severity of the needs identified by Humanitarian Needs Overview where there continues to be lack of, or challenges for NGOs to provide assistance in Afrin and Azaz Districts. Concern will work with local partners to comprehensively address gaps in the response, ensuring the inclusion of diverse beneficiaries, including girls, children with disabilities, and marginalized communities.
The intervention will involve implementing small business grants support for persons with disabilities (individuals who were injured during the earthquake), each of the selected beneficiaries will receive business cash grants of up to an average of $800 to start or rebuild their own business. Based on a labor market assessment conducted by Concern and Implementing Partners, the field team will provide the selected beneficiaries with mentoring and coaching during a two months minimum period (no TVET will be provided before delivering the grant), or once the business is running properly, subject to ongoing monitoring. The beneficiaries will be selected through self-targeting mechanism and will receive the cash grant on three instalments, the cash will be transferred through PTT directly to the beneficiary’s PTT account and with get the support needed for transportation from and to the PTT branch in the targeted area. The small business grant value for Syria is according to the Syria ERL Cluster recommendation, still the estimated value in the budget reflect the average grant value taking into consideration the deferent needs for each single project.
Concern will conduct FGDs with beneficiaries to gather feedback on the current activities and suggest ways in which they could be improved. The insights gathered from these discussions are then used to guide the implementation of future activities, ensuring that they are tailored to the specific needs of the population. Throughout the implementation of these activities, Concern and its partners will be present in the field to offer support and assistance to beneficiaries. This may include providing direct services or referring beneficiaries to other actors who can provide the necessary support. By being present in the field and working closely with beneficiaries, Concern and its partners are better able to understand the challenges faced by the population and ensure that their activities are responsive to their needs. During all project phases, Concern and Implementing Partners will consider the AAP measures, mainstreaming of protection, and PSEA. The project has a risk analysis and mitigation matrix. Along with all activities, monitoring, and evaluation processes will be carried out. At the end of the project, a meeting will be made for the project staff and key stakeholders to evaluate the project results and register the lessons learned and the best practices that have been followed to achieve project results.
So far, Concern was able to secure 50% of the proposed budget covering 100 beneficiaries in Afrin district, and the beneficiaries will be reported to the respected clusters (FSL and ERL) based on their business type. Rural businesses (agriculture, livestock, food processing, and linked livelihoods) will be reported to FSL cluster and the remaining business types will be reported to ERL Cluster.
