Project Summary

Green Hill (GH) aims to establish a Health Post (HP) in Camp 1W to provide preventive, promotive and curative services as well as simple diagnostic investigations and access to tertiary referral facilities. The HP will abide by national and humanitarian standards included in the Sphere Handbook and technical guidance of the Health Sector. Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) will be integrated into the HP to improve accessibility and acceptability of care and delivering prevention programs.
The semi-permanent HP facility will operate 6 days/week for 6 hours/day. The HP will serve 10,000 beneficiaries. HP will be staffed with a clinic/SRH coordinator, 2 medical doctors, midwife, nurse, pharmacist, dispenser, data collector, and support staff. Services will include: curative treatment, SRH services, child/adolescent health, GBV referrals for case management, nutrition services for children, communicable diseases/non-communicable diseases, MHPSS, emergency referrals, laboratory, and pharmacy.
In camps 1W and 4, we have 40 trained volunteers providing health education and awareness, referrals with emphasis on SRH services, maternal mortality surveillance/verbal autopsy surveillance, and routine EPI referrals. We will continue capacity building of the same CHVs in both camps in 2021.
To prevent overcrowdedness at ITC and SARI facilities, GH will continue to deploy medical professionals and volunteers to support community-based surveillance for mild and moderate cases of COVID-19 and ensure linkages with quarantine, ITCs, and health facilities.
Patient information at HP will be managed through DHIS-2 and EWARS. Ongoing patient feedback will also be collected to measure quality and patient satisfaction with services. GH will coordinate with the Health Sector, government authorities (Civil Surgeon, CIC, site management, RRRC, UNO, and DC office), other sectors (WASH, Nutrition, GBV, Child Protection, Food Security), and working groups (MHPSS, SRHR, CHWG).

1 - Identified Needs

For 2021, the Health Sector prioritizes the implementation of the minimum essential service package for the FDMN and host community population. According to the Health Facility Gap Analysis from September 2020, Camp 1W has 2 HP serving 38,032 people. Although 1 Primary Health Center (PHC) is under construction, there is still a need for an additional HP to adequately cover the remainder of the population. The HP will aim to meet the specific gaps identified in the HS needs analysis:SRH services: Access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal, and newborn health services is a major concern. July-August 2020 MSNA indicates that 49% of pregnant women were enrolled in an antenatal care (ANC) program. According to the CHWG, facility-based deliveries in Camp 1W have dropped below 40% in Q3 from over 50% in Q1 in 2020. Additionally, data published in April 2020 demonstrate that ANC visits dropped by 36.7%, and between January and June 2020, institutional deliveries decreased by 23%. Community feedback demonstrates that the husbands or in-laws often make decisions for pregnant women. GH will emphasize including the household decision makers in the door-to-door awareness sessions through CHVs. Family planning needs remain unaddressed, particularly for the most vulnerable populations. GBV services: Services for GBV are not yet offered at all facilities; less than 50% of primary health care centers offer GBV medical services. Protection services and linkages with health facilities need strengthening.Immunization campaigns: Immunization awareness and education needs to be expanded and surveillance needs to be maintained. COVID-19 restriction and access, prevention measures regarding social distancing, and lack of PPE for CHVs prevented block-level outreach, resulting in a dramatic drop in immunization. HS updates in July 2020 state that the dropout from Penta 1 to Penta 3 was 76%. EPI follow-up by CHVs has decreased due to being overburdened with additional responsibilities of addressing COVID-19 related issues (awareness sessions and community-based surveillance).NCD and specialized services: Availability of NCD treatment and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remain limited. The existing CHV network will be integrated into the HP. CHVs will be engaged in:Health promotion and health education: In the past, there were outbreaks of diphtheria, cholera, measles, and chicken pox. Early health promotion messaging will help to prevent these outbreaks. Awareness messaging on vaccination, hygiene, nutrition, waste and water management, clean drinking water, etc. Raise awareness of SRH among youth and adult male and female population: Health literacy regarding SRH remains low. Awareness messaging on significance of ANC and PNC check-ups, family planning, vitamin supplements and vaccinations, importance of facility-based births.COVID-19 awareness: Rumors and misconceptions regarding COVID-19 continue to affect health seeking behavior of the FDMN community. GH will continue awareness messaging, COVID-19 contact tracing, and referrals for cases and contacts.EMS training and services: CHVs will be equipped and trained to stabilize and carry patients to an ambulance or TomTom for a timely hospital referral at any time of day or night. A block-level CHV will take phone calls to provide immediate household-level support for pregnant women to support accompanied referral. GH’s CHV network in Camp 1W has emphasized the challenges they face in mobilizing the community in accessing services because of the lack of health facilities in the catchment area assigned by the CHWG and requested for a HP in Camp 1W. All health services will meet the sector minimum service package for HPs.

2 - Activities or outputs

A1: Designing and constructing HP facility based on age, gender, and disability-focused design, guidelines established by the Sphere Handbook, the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for Primary healthcare facilities in the FDMN camps.A2: CHVs will make routine bi-weekly household visits within their catchment area assigned by CHWG.A3: Training: Sexual and reproductive health, basic first aid/CPR, nurtition, immunization, communicable diseases, neonatal care/breastfeeding, data collection, mental health and psychosocial support services, COVID-19 prevention, camp health facilities/referral processes, protection, gender-based violence and rights, child marriage, human trafficking. Training staff on clinical protocols and case management, standard operating procedures, security and safety, and codes of conduct.A4: Health Education: Sexual and reproductive health, nutrition, Immunization, communicable disease, neonatal care/ breastfeeding. CHV will follow up with all pregnant women in catchment area and link the pregnant women to non-clinical traditional birth attendent (TBA) for accompanied referral. CHVs will collaborate with non-clinical TBAs by providing necessary trainings, integrating them into the health service network and the referral pathway.A5: Heatlh service delivery: prevention, childhood vaccine/preventable diseases, reporoductive, maternal and newborn healthcare, injury and trauma care, mental health care, care of non-communicable diseases, palliative care, diagnosis and case management, health information, emergency services, services for GBV survivors including clinical management of rape and referrals if needed. CHVs will perform Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) on all children between 6 months and 59 months and provide referral services if needed.A6: Health workforce: surveillance and outbreak detection and early response, management of newborn and childhood illnesses, sexual violence and clinical management of rape/ referrals.A7: COVID-19: COVID-19 awareness, preventive measure, school health trainings for teachers regarding COVID-19. Disseminate sector-approved risk communication messages regarding COVID-19/ GBV/camp updates.A8: CHVs will engage in community mobilization for COVID-19 vaccine (when available).A9: Mobilizing and sensitizing the community to address health needs by accessing healthcare services at the HP.A10: Linking beneficiaries to advanced healthcare services based on need.A11: CHVs will distribute hygiene materials at household levels based on guidance from CHWG.A12: Implementing IPC guidelines (on standard precautions, transmission-based precautions and clinical aseptic techniques), having an IPC team in each setting, and training healthcare workforce.A13: Appropriate supply, management and use of medicines, diagnostics material and technology.A14: Health information and analysis and data collection.
