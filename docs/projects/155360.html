Project Summary

GOAL has done tremendous work in delivering sustainable WASH activities in Tongogara Refugees Camp (TRC), which is home to 13,043 refugees, approximately amalgamated into 4,333 households. Most of the refugees and asylum seekers are from DRC which constitutes about 72% of the population, Mozambicans 11%, Rwandese 7%, Burundians 7% and the remaining 3% is made up of people from Angola, Botswana, Congo, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya, Lebanon, Mali, Somalia, South Africa and Sudan. Tongogara Refugee Camp throughout the year 2018 continued to have an upsurge in the populations of Persons of Concern (POCs) and the recent influx of refugees in TRC is causing a strain on resources, especially on WASH services with resources being surpassed by the demand.
In 2017 GOAL was nominated to take over WASH activities in the camp and since then, it has managed to ease potable water challenges and improve sanitary conditions for the refugees in the camp. GOAL has successfully created communal structures that ensure smooth delivery of community-based health, hygiene education and promotions to influence behaviour change through adoption of positive WASH practices among the persons of concern.
GOAL seeks to further enhance service delivery in WASH activities to ensure POCs have access to quality potable water for household consumption, increased access to proper sanitation facilities, promotion of behaviour change through adoption of positive WASH practices and strengthening localised communal WASH structures so that TRC community becomes proactive and responsive to WASH related challenges and hazards.
GOAL will target households that have no access to proper sanitation facilities and will seek to strengthen water point management committees and equip pump minders with requisite skills that ensure there is adequate localised response to frequent pump breakdowns.
Additional boreholes will be drilled to enhance supplementary water supply in between rationing intervals in the recently developed section 9.Existing supplementary boreholes shall be targeted for rehabilitation to enhance their useful life. These boreholes of late have been going through a series of frequent breakdowns from excessive use thanks to power cuts which have affected water delivery from the other 5 main power driven water sources serving the whole camp. Additional water storage buckets will be given to the POCs to enhance safe storage of water.

Needs

A WASH assessment conducted in 2016 indicated that due to the absence of WASH facilities, open defecation was prevalent. To date, GOAL has constructed 1,141 toilets and is currently adding more 210 toilets in section 9 which had serious challenges with open defecation. Approximately more than 2000 households have imminent need of latrines creating a huge deficit on the aspirations of the project objective to ensure POCs have increased access to proper sanitation facilities. The project in 2018 provided 15 skip bins which were placed at various public spaces with high human traffic and frequency, this has created extra need for dump sites to ensure the litter collected in the bins is safely disposed. GOAL increased water distribution in the camp during 2018. Three new pumps were installed in the pump station to boost water pumping capacity to make them 5. The main water line from section 8 to section 7 was upgraded, replacing the 63mm pipe line with a 93mm pipe. This enabled additional water tanks to be erected in Section 8 and section 7; 20,000 litre tanks in section 8 and 10,000 litre tank in section 7. A new water pipeline was constructed in Section 4 enabling piped water supply to this section, along with 12 water outlets in Section 4. Water rationing continued during 2018, even though water discharging times were increased from 30 minutes per section per day in January 2017, to 2 hours per section per day by November 2018.To complement this positive development there is need to provide containers to families to ensure they stock enough water to meet their daily use. Power outages have become a daily phenomenon consequently putting immense pressure on the 11 existing bush pump boreholes. The down time frequency for the past 2 months has extremely increased, hence the need to rehabilitate the boreholes so that their useful life is further enhanced. Section 9 has no boreholes to provide supplementary water in between the rationing intervals and POC in the section have to travel longer distances to other sections if they miss their scheduled slots. 11 Pump minders who were trained need further refresher training to bolster their acquired skills so that they can proficiently and effectively attend to these breakdowns without outsourcing expertise and skills from outside the camp thus consolidating localised knowledge. GOAL has facilitated the formation of Water points committees to ensure there is community based management of water points to date 44 water points have been established. The water points have mobilised communities to create water funds that facilitates and enables communities to pay for maintenance and repairs on their own. Water rationing comes with a fair share of conflicts as refugees collect water at the water points and some may degenerate into intense conflicts. Conflict management therefore becomes key and imperative to ensure there is harmony and order at the water points.

Activities

1. Drilling of 2 new boreholes in Section 9This is a new section of the camp where construction of 180 new houses is taking place. There is a possibility that this new location will expand, hence the need to drill 2 new boreholes fitted with the B-type Bush pump to provide backup in case of power-driven pumps breakdown and also provide additional capacity during the water rationing intervals. 2. Rehabilitation of existing water points 11 existing functional boreholes will be rehabilitated. Rehabilitation will cover mechanical faults, head works and fencing around each of the water points. The rehabilitation of water points will reduce pressure on the existing water points thereby increasing their useful life. There are a lot of stray animals in the camp (pigs, goats and cattle) and fencing will cordon the water points from stray livestock and will reduce chances of contamination from animal waste. 3. Strengthening management of existing water pointsStrengthening management of existing water points will include establishing water point management committees where they are not in existence and provision of training on managing the water points.14 water points committees shall be trained from the selected 14 boreholes. The training will cover issues related to scheduling of access to water points to avoid conflict. The committees will also ensure proper handling of water pumps and creating a fund for maintenance as well as supervising the work of the local pump minders. 4. Training of pump mindersFor effective operation and maintenance there must be designated people responsible for operations and maintenance who can articulate or demonstrate procedures followed to operate and maintain the facilities. There is also a need for appropriate tools for the water system to be operational and in good working order. One Community Pump Minder (CPMs) per section selected by people of concern will be trained to undertake repairs of the boreholes when necessary. Communities will provide incentives (in cash or kind) to the CPMs for any work done. A total of 14 CPMs will be trained. The CPMs will undertake simple maintenance on the pumps and inform the GOAL in the event of a serious malfunction. 4. Sanitation To date under previous interventions, GOAL has constructed 917 BVIPs in the camp, but there are still some households in need of latrines as we escalate efforts towards making TRC a zero open defecation zone. Goal will construct additional 50 new BVIPs toilets in section 9 where some households are still practicing open defecationGOAL will also increase the number of communal latrines at the designated flood evacuation site from 2 to 4 to keep abreast with populations that are ever increasing, where refugees are evacuated in the event of floods. Flood scares have become an annual phenomenon in TRC owing to changing weather patterns and its proximity to Save River.6. HygieneCommunal dump sites shall be constructed in each of the 9 sections of the camp where POCs will dump their litter collected from households and the skip bins that have been provided at public spaces.7. Safe Water StorageHouseholds store water in containers of various designs, materials and sizes. Many People of Concern obtain or buy previously-used containers for water storage because they are cheaper. However, sometimes these containers have held poisonous substances such as pesticides. In most cases the water is not protected from subsequent contamination during use. Contamination occurs because water collection and storage containers are open, uncovered or poorly covered. Unhygienic methods are used to dispense water from household storage containers and containers are not protected against contamination introduced by vectors (flies, cockroaches, rodents, etc.). The program will support 4,333 households with 2 water storage containers with lids per household to facilitate improved household water management and storage of sufficient quantities of water.
