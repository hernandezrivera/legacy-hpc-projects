Project Summary

According to the latest reports from FSL cluster there are an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,283 displaced people living in last resort IDP sites/camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities.
The project aims to meet the food needs and as an urgent humanitarian response in five random camps in the rural of Idleb province through distributing 1044 food kits in every month for 1044 families for six months sequentially. The beneficiaries in this intervention will be 100% from IDPs in these camps including the new displaced from Northern Homs, South Syria, Damascus and other areas in order to contribute to strengthening their ability to tolerate the situation through providing food assistance that meets their basic needs. The kits will provide the families with 2100 Calories which is equal to 100% per day as per Sphere standards. The project location will be as the following:
Idleb- Harim- Dana- Kafr Deryan. (in four camps)
Idleb- Harim- Qourqeena- Barisha (AL-Salam camp)
This intervention will target the families which meet the selection criteria in the same time we are going to focus on the families that have not received any food assistant during the last three months. The kit weight approximately 83 Kg. Therefore, we are going to distribute the food kits in the distribution centres in these camps. In addition to the kits will be procured inside Syria in order to support the local markets.

Needs

The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 Due to the crisis has affected all people in most cities and villages in Syria, but some populations and locations have been more acutely affected than others. The most severe needs are seen in areas of conflict with large numbers of IDPs. According to the Global Humanitarian Overview 2019 (GNO),13.1 million people need humanitarian assistance, 11.2 people are being targeted and 2.5 million of the estimated 13.1 million in need are facing acute needs at catastrophic, critical or severe levels. The HRP 2019 identifies some population groups considered particularly vulnerable and who are likely to face multiple lifesaving needs and heightened protection risk. Displacement patterns across Syria remains extremely fluid. Many people have been displaced multiple times. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,283 displaced people living in last resort IDP sites/camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines. The geographic focus of new displacements has shifted as the situation on the ground has evolved. New displacements have been most frequent in northwest of Syria such as Idleb Governorate. In addition to, large numbers of people were also displaced from Hama and Aleppo governorate of which many now residing in Idleb governorate which hosts over 1.1 million of IDPs. During last time where, 214,000 people were displaced, mainly in the northwest. Further, an estimated 53,000 people were newly displaced to Harim district in Idleb governorate due to renewed the conflict. Harim is a Syrian city within the Idlib Governorate. It has an altitude of 160 meters and a population of 21,934. Harim is located on the border of Turkey, 55 km west of Aleppo. Number of IDPs has been increased recently due to the new influx of displaced people which estimated by 28000 individuals in addition to Harim is considered a safety area and close to Turkey. The main triggers of food insecurity in Syria remain insecurity, financial and physical access to food, and depletion of livelihoods. This puts the vulnerable population at an increased risk of contracting communicable diseases at the time where approximately all the incoming IDPs are facing acute food insecurity and as needing immediate food assistance to live. last year the number of displaced people at risk of food insecurity has increased, clearly indicating further depletion of livelihoods assets and opportunities throughout the country.On 20 / 01 / 2019 MASRRAT conducted many visits and needs assessment in Harim district in Idleb in five random camps after the influx of new IDPs the results showed that ,these camp did not receive any food support from more 8 months and Some of undernutrition cases and general shortage of basic food items were observed ,the field team in coordination with the responsible about these camps had selected 1044 families from the most vulnerable people and according to selection criteria (family consist of five members or more - Prioritizing families headed by disabled, elders, or women - Beneficiaries who have not received any food support since 3 months ago.

Implementation of activities

- Monthly food assistance for the assessed food insecure people through appropriate modalities.- The project will start on 27 Feb 2019, project duration will be seven months (The first month will include preparations, procurement and contracting and Six months will include distributions), the staff will be recruited directly to carry out the project implementation. The project will provide the assistance that meets food security of 1044 vulnerable families. The Kit will provide the family with 2100 Calories which is equal to 100% per day as per Sphere standards. The targeted locations will focus on five camps in Idleb / Harim district. Priority to these locations have been given because they have not been assisted by any organization during the last eight months under the same intervention. At the beginning of the implementation of the project MASRRAT's team will sign Memorandum of Understanding (MOUs) with the local councils in the area. The process of engaging the Local Councils will start with advertising about the project in the communities of intervention, explaining the mechanism of selection, and the number of potential beneficiaries under this project. Advertisements of application will be published and will include selection priorities. Applications will include questions about basic personal information of the families. Data collection of the beneficiaries will be conducted at the beginning of the project (in the preparation month), and this process usually has no risks or any threats to beneficiaries. The data will be verified based on the criteria of selection (IDPs didn’t receive food assistance from three months ago / Households headed by disabled, elders, or women). Delivery of food Kits will be after receiving a receipt card and according to distribution lists. Beneficiaries will sign on the distribution lists, and they will be verified through their IDs. The project will follow the procurement policy of MASRRAT, as the consumptions (Food Items) will be procured from Syria. The components of the Kits will be as the following: Short-grain Rice (10Kg)-Bulgur Wheat (5Kg)-Red Lentils (5Kg)-flour (5Kg)-white beans (4Kg) hummus (5Kg)-Sugar(10Kg)-Olive Oil (3Kg)-Vegetable Oil (3Kg)-Vegetable ghee (3Kg)-Spaghetti (4 box)-Iodized Salt (1Kg)-Tomato Paste (3Kg)-jam (2Kg)-Dates (2Kg)-canned fish (160 G/5 pieces)-canned olives (2Kg)-canned beats (400 G/3 pieces)-luncheon (380 G/4 pieces)-Milk (4L). The basket weight approximately 83 Kg as total. In the beginning of the project, the procurement committee will announce about a tender in the local markets after that the offers will be received from the vendors during the selected period of tender. The committee will review the specifications and quality of the offers in order to determine the best quality and ensuring cost-effectiveness. The appropriate vendor will be contracted according to the conditions in the tender and will be responsible to the good delivery of the food items to the distribution points for six consecutive months and the quantity of each distribution will be sufficient for one month. We are going to stick a paper on the food basket included the components of the Kit. MASRRAT will coordinate with the I/NGOs and local councils in the area in order to assure the accuracy of implementation and to avoid any duplication with other humanitarian actors in the area. The distribution will be through MASRRAT field team (100% IDPs) in the targeted locations in the distribution centres. A complaints’ mechanism will be set up, together with beneficiary satisfaction surveys. The complaints and the feedback system will be received through special phone number, email, mobile boxes, and sharing complaints directly with M&E field staff in the center. MEAL Team will monthly record; problems, complaints and received recommendations from the beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis

The targeted location of the project is considered relatively safe as there haven't been any kind of conflict during this time. MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions, starting with obtaining approvals from the local councils to move freely on the ground, strictly observing security and safety regulations and preventing the beneficiaries to work if the airstrikes are in the air. The following consideration will be considered with regard to protection:• Prioritization for safety, dignity, and ‘do no harm’ principles will be concerns in this program; this project has been designed to prevent and mitigate any negative unintentional effects of intervention that might physically jeopardize people (like violence, offence, coercion, and environmental risks), or psychologically threat them (like disrespect, lack of privacy, and disengagement). • Equal and unbiased access to assistance and services: Assistance and services will be provided basing on the needs with no discrimination.• the risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers is considered an average indicator in the project because there is a confidential complaint mechanism, Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA or GBV complaints and conduct trainings on this subject for the new staff. • There will be no sensitivity in the community during the implementation of the project due to the project will target all the IDPs in these camps and according to the established criteria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:• Increase in the needs: a new influx of IDPs into these camps in the targeted locations (mitigation: collaborate with donors/INGOs to cover any additional needs)• Lack of food materials in the region (Mitigation: purchase the materials from other nearby areas)• Increase in food prices during implementation the project (Mitigation: fix the prices with suppliers during the project period) • Deterioration of the security situation in the region (Mitigation: Postponing the distributions or changing distribution locations)Assumptions:• There will be permanent access to the project implementation area and the targeted beneficiaries.• There will be Cooperation with the local councils and other humanitarian agencies in the implementation of the project through sharing the data and selection of beneficiaries and others.• collaboration of the targeted families and stakeholders with the team and their participation in the implementation of the project.
