Project Summary

The project seeks to increase access to, and utilisation of integrated health care services, strengthening local ownership and governance of the health delivery system at county level through addressing both the supply side and demand side barriers to services delivery. Health care services will be provided to the communities through both static and mobile outreach services. This will be achieved through expanding the reach of essential primary health care, clinical emergency response services such as BEmONC, referral of SGBV, Clinical Management of Rape, reproductive health services including adolescent reproductive health and FP, screening at Community level for MAM and SAM and Management of Acute Malnutrition at the facility level The project will strengthen existing programmes like EPI and IMNCI and others as outlined in the Basic Package for Health and nutrition Services through refresher training, provision of Job Aids and tools, supportive supervision and mentorship of Health workers developing health worker clinical competencies and skills. To improve vaccination of children, the project will also work with Boma Health workers (BHWs) to mobilise care givers of children under the age of one to take children for vaccinations at the health facility and at outreach points and to refer children with danger signs for further management at health facilities. BHWs will also follow up on Vaccination defaulters and through this network of BHWs, the project seeks to improve surveillance and referral for epidemic prone diseases. To raise awareness, promote identification of people with mental health problems and create demand for mental health services, the project will implement Low Intensity Mental Health Interventions such as Psychoeducation, Community Messaging and awareness use community health structures to Identify people with mental health problems and refer them to the health facility and community support systems. The project will also seek to improve the screening and referral of people with disabilities using the Washington group of questions at every point of contact with health service provider.
The Target population includes:
• Health workers (community based(BHWs) and Facility based to ensure continuum of care from the community to the health facility and vice versa) through capacity building for BEmONC, HBB, Essential care for every baby, IMNCI , CMR and Qaulity of Care
• Children under the age of 5 for IMNCI, Screening , referral and management of SAM
• Women of child bearing age including pregnant women for ANC, vaccination, IPTp with fansider and distribution of ITNs and promoting institutional deliveries, At hospital level and selected PHCC screening for HIV and provision of PMTCT services
• Men, Women and boys and girls with disabilities and Mental health problems
• Women, and children exposed to SGBV
• Adolescent girls and boys out of school for peer education and life skills

1 - Needs

The health situation in Northern Bahr el Ghazal remains fragile and this can be attributed to severe food insecurity contributing to poor diets, high poverty, poor access to health services, some conflict events and insecurity notably in Aweil Center, Aroyo and Mabok Akot Communities in July, high morbidity related to epidemic prone diseases such as Measles that affected both Aweil south and Aweil Centre in the months of April and May 2019 and poor WASH practices. Aweil Centre, Aweil North and Aweil South are counties supported by HPF and Medair was providing emergency response and Nutrition interventions in Aweil Centre while IRC was providing Nutrition interventions in Aweil South. HIO was supporting in Aweil North. Despite having support from these partners, the coverage and utilisation rates of essential services such as FP, Immunization, institutional deliveries and OPD consultations at some health facilities are still low owing to geographical, cultural, financial and health system barriers. Aweil Centre has a total of 17 Health Facilities and HPF3 Project is supporting 3 Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs) and 4 Primary Health Care Centres (PHCCs) and 1 Aweil State Hospital. The remaining 9 facilities (Bau PHCU, Umora PHCU, Akuangkar PHCU, Jelwan PHCU, Awulic PHCU, Nyinalel PHCU, Nyalath PHCU, Chamanguei PHCU, Lulic PHCU are Government supported. Aweil South has 12 Health Failities and only 9 are supported, Among the 12 in Aweil south Health facilities are 2 PHCC but only one is supported by HPF. All the health facilities not supported by HPF have interrupted services due to lack of staff, drugs and commodities leaving communities with limited or no access to health services. HPF Budgetary limitations have affected access to vaccination services in both counties as only one vaccinator per health facility receives incentives making it difficult to provide both static and outreach vaccinations. All the 3 Counties Aweil South, Aweil North and Aweil Centre have been hard hit by floods. Aweil North sits in the lowlands, with many areas subject to seasonal flooding of the Lol River. Excessive flooding has contributed to limited accessibility to health facilities for example Wuncum PHCU, Ayai PHCU and Nyieth. Curative consultation dropped from 7,929 in August 2019 to 7,381 in September in Aweil south against a monthly target of 8,056. The drop in consultations can be attributed to limited accessibility due to flooding and movement of affected communities to higher ground outside the catchment area of the supported facilities. This calls for implementation of an innovative project to scale up access to and utilization of health services by communities that are disadvantaged by various factors.

2 - Activities

The Project approach to increase access to, and utilisation of, health facility-based services will address both supply side and demand side barriers to service delivery. Supply side improvements will include: (1) developing health worker clinical competencies and skills to provide the basic package of health and nutrition services; trainings on BEmONC, HBB and kangaroo care,IMNCI, PMTCT,SGBV and Clinical Management of Rape, IYCN and including IPC as part o every training. (2) strengthening health service provision : OPD Consultations for common diseases, basic preventive nutrition services through maternal infant and young child nutrition Screening and referral targeting women of reproductive age, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in Aweil Center as per nutrition cluster guidelines, management of SAM and MAM, strengthening utilization of health facilities by pregnant women for ANC, delivery, Postnatal care and family planning: Screening for Mental illness and disability and referral to higher levels of care and support, strengthening routine diseases surveillance, Support to Mass drug administration and NIDs. Provision of EPI services Vitamin A supplementation after every 6 months, and IMNCI as Part of BHI at community level, Strengthen provision of CMR services respond to epidemics and disease outbreaks. Provision o mobile and outreach health services(3). Supervision and mentorship of health workers for service quality improvement and monitoring attendance of health workers at posts; (3) health facility maintenance; and (4) ensuring availability of essential medicines and equipment. (4) Demand side interventions to support increased utilisation of services will include community engagement, health education and promotion at community level, and improving referral to health facilities through theBoma Health Initiative. As part of our approach we will offer patient-centred services that are sensitive to local customs, community needs, geographical and social factors, and protect the basic rights of individuals, their dignity, privacy and respect(5) The project will also support for procurement of supplies, last mile delivery of commodities to HFs and BHTs, inventory management and drug stock reporting, prepositioning of drug stocks during the rainy season, and collaboration with other health partners to avail supplies and streamline distribution systems under the CHD as the accountable lead.(7) Management of human resources for health: We will work with CHDs to ensure that all CHD team members and health workers have their job descriptions reviewed and updated based on national guidelines and recruit critical staff at HFs in agreement with CHDs. Any new recruitment will follow South Sudan Human Resources for Health guidelines. We will collaborate with CHDs to ensure a review of the data in the human resource information system (HRIS), to ensure integrity of the system. We also will collaborate with SMoH and CHD to reconcile HRIS with South Sudan Electronic Pay Roll System and ensure CHDs regularly review the pay roll for its integrity(8) Governance and leadership at county level-support CHDs in turn on their micro-resilience planning and mitigation, which will feed into the overall state emergency preparedness response plan, Support CHD to Develop Micro Plans for EPI and hold programme progress review meetings and quarterly planning with partners. Support the CHDs to develop and maintain an asset register of equipment, vehicles (including ambulances) and health facility buildings, routinely check their state, and have plans in place for their maintenance. Support minor renovations and provision of Wash facilities at health facilities

The Project will put in place mechanism to ensure that the rights of beneficiaries are recognized and protected. The Project will ensure that all health workers are are trained aware of the patient`s rights through education of the Health workers and the beneficiaries about their rights. 6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

To ensure that the project takes into consideration the voices and perspectives of the affected population, the project will work with and involve the host communities throughout the project cycle from project design to end of project evaluation. During county planning, we will work with CHDs to take the lead in identifying communities, groups or individuals that may be vulnerable to exclusion from services, their locations, the types of exclusion they experience, and how this exclusion is perpetuated or reinforced. We will develop actions with CHDs, and in consultation with communities, on how to organise services in a way that includes all vulnerable groups. We will train project staff Following this, we will work with CHDs to set up committees at county (with participationof women and disabled people in the committee), Boma, and village level, linked to HFs, to support the review of GESI issues, and propose strategies and actions to improve gender equity, prevent exclusion, and mitigate violence against women and girls. We will work with BHCs and BHWs to engage local leaders, to raise awareness about the project, its purpose and target to ensure understanding by the communities and informed participation in project activities. We will remain mutually accountable on responsibilities and conduct quarterly performance reviews including budget reviews with CHDs. We will collect feedback (quarterly) from beneficiaries, HF staff and CHDs to understand their perspective on our implementation, and to enable issues to be highlighted (including positives) on the running of interventions. For monitoring purposes, this feedback will be documented with clear responses on the concerns raised and as required adapt programme strategies and approaches to fit the changing context. Remain impartial, engaging with all project stakeholders and beneficiaries in an inclusive way that is non-partisan and non-discriminatory. Recruit project staff in a transparent and fair way.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Through working with the SMOH, CHDs, Government facility health workers and BHWs to deliver the Government approved Basic Package of Health and Nutrition services (BPHNS, the project will ensure that the health services provided are compliant with Government policies and guidelines. We will ensure clarity and alignment of the CHD team on their responsibility in achieving national priorities and delivering BPHNS. We will collaborate with partners to update existing stakeholder mapping, and jointly develop a county plan. To improve engagement, coordination and transparency related to health service delivery, we will encourage CHDs to sign MoUs with partners supporting health services, outlining roles and responsibilities, and for them to share information so it can be incorporated into budgets and workplans. This will also support them in asserting their leadership role in managing service delivery. We will facilitate the CHD to conduct support supervision of services at health facility and community level, and quarterly review meetings to collectively assess performance against plans and identify challenges and management actions to improve service deliveryAt Community level, we will ensure CHDs take a leading role on community engagement with communities as true partners in health. Working with the CHDs we will re-invigorate the working of the health facility committees and BHC as advocates for better services at HFs. They will also act as community pressure group to monitor and report on health worker attendance at HFs. We will involve the Payam health officers and Paramount chiefs in oversight of the BHI services, and we will share monthly feedback with them on community health data and health facility service performance information from HMIS to promote uptake of key services, interventions and on social behavioural change

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project shall be guided by the National Health policy 2016-2026, Health Sector Development Plan 2015/2016-2019/2020, According to the National Health Policy 2016 -2026. The national objectives of these documents emphasize the need to: 1: To strengthen health service organisation and infrastructure development for effective and equitable delivery of the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services, and Universal Health Coverage.2: To strengthen leadership and management of the health system and increase health system resources for improved health sector performance.3: To strengthen partnerships for healthcare delivery and health systems development. This project focuses on increasing the utilisation and quality of health services to vulnerable populations with emphasis on maternal and child health to reduce excess morbidity and mortality from epidemic prone diseases. It also seeks to promote and scale scale up health promotion and protection interventions so as to empower communities to take charge of their health by doing so it seeks to increase access to services for SGBV and mental health disorders. Through Training of health workers, improving Capacity of CHDs to govern and manage the county health service delivery system, the projects seeks to scale up access to integrated quality health care services by vulnerable populations. All the project objectives and population targeting is aligned to the national objectives stated above

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will work with local authorities and CHDs to jointly map and analyse the potential consequences of common shocks and drivers of vulnerability such as: (1) floods, drought and food chain shortages (2) conflict from within or surrounding areas and its consequences on IDPs livelihood, SGBV and health. We will support the the local authorities and CHD to lead the engagement of partners in WASH, nutrition (UNICEF, WFP), health (WHO) and NGOs to develop an integrated risk management plan that includes longer-term durable solutions efforts for displacement-affected communities (IDPs, refugees, returnees and hosts). This crosssector plan will anticipate risks, identify ways of militating against threats, and show the needs and resource gaps for implementing mitigation actions. Together with CHDs and partners we will develop Conflict sensitivity approach: We will draw on guidance and tools from the Conflict Sensitivity Resource Facility-South Sudan (CSRF-SS) to do an in-depth analysis of historical, cultural, political and economic drivers of conflict in consultation with the county leadership. This analysis will inform (1) how our actions during implementation may potentially further conflict, their likely negative and positive consequences, and (2) how best we should adapt our programme approaches to avoid exacerbating tensions and conflict. Some of the potential areas/topics that may cause conflict when implementing the project include: (1) defining and setting of roles and responsibilities between the project and CHDs, including tracking of project budget expenditure; (2) recruitment of project staff, health facility workers supported by the project and community BHI staff (3) procurement of goods and services; 4. how services are delivered and distributed to communities including displaced populations.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure sustainability, our approach to empower CHDs will be to offer technical assistance and support for: (1) planning based on local needs and linked to use of health information; (2) coordination and management of heath activities based on a one-county plan model i.e. One Plan, One Budget and One Monitoring and Evaluation system; (3) Support Supervision to improve health worker skills and oversight for health services; (4) reporting of health information, improving data quality, data analysis and use of health information for monitoring of service delivery performance and planning; and (5) medical supply chain management and the use of logistical information for forecasting of commodity needs and planning. We will strengthen CHD managerial and technical capacity by running the project as part of Aweil-Centre and South CHD, with co-location of our consortium project staff to work side by side with the CHD offering long-term technical assistance. This will facilitate collaboration between the CHD and the project , and allow our staff to continuously mentor, coach and do on-the-job training for CHDs. To improve access to and service quality of the basic health care package, we will ensure CHDs take a leading role on community engagement with communities as true partners in health. Working with the CHDs we will re-invigorate the working of the health facility committees and BHC as advocates for better services at HFs. They will also act as community pressure group to monitor and report on health worker attendance at HFs. We will involve the Payam health officers and Paramount chiefs in oversight of the BHI services, and we will share monthly feedback with them on community health data and health facility service performance information from HMIS to promote uptake of key services, interventions and on social behavioural change. By Doing this, the CHD and local authorities are capacitated to continue providing services to all populations
