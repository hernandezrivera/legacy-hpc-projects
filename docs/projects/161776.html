Project Summary

Plan International Nigeria will implement a 12-month Nutrition project intervention focusing on strengthening capacity of management of acute malnutrition and prevention of malnutrition in areas were receiving new influx of IDPs, returnee and Host Community in BAY states. The project plans to provide treatment for a total of 14,557 uncomplicated SAM children in OTP for 5 LGAs in Borno and one Adamawa state and 2011 complicated SAM children 5 LGAs for Borno, and one Adamawa state. At project start-up, Plan International Nigeria will prioritize access to life-saving services for community mobilization, complicated, and uncomplicated SAM children in the targeted LGAs of Borno and Adamawa state. We will strengthen capacity of government service providers including community capacity to prevent and respond to the treatment and as well as prevention of under nutrition and will work closely with communities and partners working the existing camps with the consultation of CCCM sector.
Plan International will hire and train a team of CMAM Officers, CMAM assistants, SC nurse, SC Doctors and community nutrition mobilizers who will be deployed at the community level to provide anthropometric measurement for all children under five and provide necessary treatment for SAM children.
Plan International will integrate this project using our mobile nutrition outreach approach to reach out to new influx IDPs and mobile population in the LGA. The mobile nutrition outreach operates from a pick-up truck and compose of government health worker, CMAM/ Nutrition Officer, Community Mobilization Officer, psychosocial counselling officer and CMAM assistant who provide a package of services including identification of SAM, treatment of uncomplicated SAM, psychosocial counselling, and referral of complicated SAM case. The project will strengthen accountability to beneficiaries through the establishment and strengthening of a community based complaints and feedback mechanism that will allow women, girls, men, and boys safely report concerns of the emergency nutrition response in their respective community.
Plan International Nigeria is proposing an emergency nutrition response in Chibok, Mafa, Biu, Hawul, Kaga and Konduga Local Government area in Borno Stata, and Michaka LGA from Adamawa state and IDP camps. Thus, the proposed activities will strengthen the management of acute malnutrition through CMAM approach by establishing new mobile outreach OTP sites for IDP, providing technical and logistical support, supporting regular community mobilization and house to house MUAC screening and strengthening the supportive supervision. The project will focus on supporting the most vulnerable population groups, specifically children between under five-year age.
In order to address the most urgent needs of the LGAs, plan international proposes to implement treatment of SAM at mobile outreach OTP sites for uncomplicated SAM and SC for complicated SAM at hospital and community outreach for identification and referral of SAM cases and also Maternal and infant young child nutrition (MIYCN).

1 - Needs

According to the Nigeria 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy, a total of 7.1 million people including 2.3 million girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men are in need of humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria as an effect of the crisis in the past ten years. The on-going conflict in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states continues to undermine social stability and economic development due to the scale of displacement, disrupted livelihoods and diminished agricultural production, and has an especially severe impact on the food security and nutrition situation of women, children and vulnerable groups. The October 2019 Joint Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (JNAFSS, 2019) conducted in BAY States revealed that the overall GAM and SAM rates in Borno state were at 8.1 % and 0.9% respectively. In the same survey, in central Borno (Mafa, Konduga and Kaga) were assessed GAM and SAM rates by weight- for height survey revealed rates of 10.1% and 0.5% respectively. Similarly, in southern Borno LGAs (Chibok,Biu, and Hawul) assessed GAM and SAM rates by a weight-for-height survey were at 9.4% and 1.5% respectively. A July 2019 Save the Children survey for central Borno (Kaga and Konduga) showed the GAM and SAM rates at 21.1% and 5.5% respectively. The overall acute malnutrition rate across the Borno state is alarming, with the GAM rates reaching emergency levels which needs immediate humanitarian assistance. This situation is further aggravated by the rainy season and the increase in malaria, AWD and cholera, and by protection concerns related to new displacement following ongoing military operations. Malnutrition in children under-five is a major cause of morbidity and mortality. If not treated in a timely manner, the likelihood of death in severe acute malnourished children is nine times higher compared to their healthy peers, while children with moderate acute malnutrition are three times more likely to die. Children suffering from prolonged undernutrition who survive may become locked in a cycle of recurring illness and faltering growth, with irreversible damage to their development and cognitive abilities. This highlights the importance of preventing a further deterioration of the food insecurity in this area. Key factors of malnutrition in the state are poor dietary diversity, low rates of exclusive breastfeeding, incidence of common childhood illnesses (malaria, diarrhea including cholera, measles etc.), food insecurity and poor water, sanitation and hygiene practices (JNAFSS, 2019). As per the July and August 2019 humanitarian situation update (OCHA), due to the ongoing heavy rains, disease rates are high, especially for diarrhea, measles and malaria. These diseases are underlying causes of malnutrition. For this reason, admission of new SAM cases has exponentially increased in July 2019. Therefore, there is high demand of maternal, infant and young child nutrition and CMAM ( Complicated and uncomplicated SAM management , MAM management and community mobilization) in Mafa, Kaga, Konduga, Chibok and Hawul LGAs in Borno and Michika LGA in Adamawa state. To address the needs of children under five, Plan International will do the following activities: treatment of complicated SAM cases in five general hospitals, and screening and referral of SAM at reception centers using MUAC and weight for height and behavioral change interventions including MIYCN by nutritionist. The number of the targeted beneficiaries is calculated using nutrition sector target for 2020.
