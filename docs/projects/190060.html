Project Summary

This project intends to provide comprehensive case management and other psychosocial support services for crisis affected children in Guji Zone of Oromia Region and Metekel and Assosa Zones of BGRS. Liben and Goro Dolla from Guji Zone are the target Woredas selected from Oromia Region. Whereas, PIE targets Dibate, Bulen, Dangur and Mandura from Metekel and Sherkole from Assosa Zones in BGRS respectively. Selection of targeted Woredas was made based on needs on the ground and PIE’s field presence in those areas.
Through this project PIE aims to reach 15,000 children with essential child protection services such as psychosocial support, awareness raising and case management including alternative care for UASC and family tracing and reunification services. On the other hand, PIE plans to reach 25,000 women, girls, boys and men through the GBV interventions planned as part of this project. In line with the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and the EHF 2021 second round standard allocation strategy, the project aims to provide psychosocial support and case management services including direct support services such as NFIs for most vulnerable children and dignity kits for adolescent girls as well as referrals for specialized services. Where and when possible, PIE will use cash as a modality to help adolescent girls meet their NFI needs. PIE will draw from its vast experience in using cash as an assistance modality in many project previously.
PIE will employ close working collaboration with local government partners as a key strategy to materialize project activities and thereby ensuring safe exit and sustainability. Through this project Plan International also aims to engage communities and children as drivers of change and as an essential means to build capacities of these actors.
This project is designed in consultation with the CP/GBV Sub-clusters at the national level and respective Woreda government offices at local levels. PIE has used the various assessments and field mission reports to inform program design. Additionally, PIE carried out consultations with the respective government partners in each of the locations to identify needs and secure government support.

1. - Projects activities

Unaccompanied, separated and other vulnerable children (girls and boys) have received comprehensive case management services including FTR and alternative care.FTR practices will be guided by existing national laws and established international standards and practices. UASC will also be provided with alternative care. As part of this effort, PIE will identify and train parents who can foster UASC and other vulnerable children. Selection of foster parents involves a thorough assessment and joint selection process that includes home visits, interviews to determine suitability to foster a child and criminal background check. PIE will work closely with the respective woreda WCYA Offices and the various community-based child protection structures to select potential foster parents. Besides, PIE will also train foster parents on positive parenting skills, communication with children and positive disciplining. After placement of UASC and other vulnerable children PIE will carry out home visitation to assessment adjustment of the child into the new family and also provide PSS support to the parents to encourage them continue doing the good deed. To defray the cost of supporting the fostered child, PIE will provide cash assistance to foster parents to help them fulfill basic household items such as mattresses, blankets and utensils. Children and their parents/caregiver affected by the crisis are provided with MHPSS services and achieve enhanced psychosocial wellbeing Strengthen Community Based Child Protection Mechanism (CBCPM) such as Child Protection Committees and Child Rights Clubs through trainings, material and technical support to promote, prevent and respond to CP/GBV concerns Strengthened GBV Case Management, Referrals and Legal AidStrengthened community based prevention and response mechanisms of SGBVImproved awareness of IDPs, host communities, government stakeholders and partners on GBV prevention and response

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Right from the onset, Plan International Ethiopia will involve local authorities in responding to protection issues including GBV. In most situations, there are Child Protection Committees at Woreda levels which can serve as an entry point to further enhance coordination with local partners to ensure smooth functioning of the project. Plan International will also conduct an assessment to identify protection partners providing various services in each location and develop a service directory and referral pathways. This exercise will enhance coordination among agencies and government line offices for the betterment of protection services for children. In particular, GBV intervention will require clear coordination among agencies providing specialized health services including clinical management of rape (CMR). Currently, CMR services are provided by government health care providers such as hospitals and health centers. Through the joint protection mapping exercise PIE will endeavor to identify these institutions in each targeted locations and develop a clear referral pathways. Accordingly, PIE aims to coordinate with these government institutions to ensure proper and timely referral of rape cases for proper medical attention. Besides, PIE will carry out as assessment and identify the material needs of these medical facilities and provide CMR kits to support their effort in providing quality and comprehensive CMR services to survivors. Moreover, PIE will also provide capacity building training on clinical management of rape to selected health professional to further enhance their capacities to conduct quality CMR. Coordination will also be made to ensure complementarity of services and avoid unnecessary duplications. PIE together with respective Woreda Women, Children and Youth Affairs Offices will conduct regular coordination meetings among relevant sector offices and NGOs to review and determine if there are no overlaps of services and the coordination is going on as it should be. Through this project, PIE will work with partners on the ground to develop beneficiary selection criteria to identify eligible beneficiaries to receive direct support such as NFIs and dignity kits. Additionally, Plan International actively participates in cluster and various coordination meetings at the national and regional levels. At national level, PIE will continue to attend and share information in the CP/GBV sub-cluster meetings, as well as submit the 5Ws to the Sub-Cluster Coordinators on regular basis. This will enable the Sub-Cluster to track the response undertaking in various part of the country and inform their response and gaps analysis thereby making informed decision on funding allocations and resource mobilizations. Plan International will also work closely with Woreda Women, Children and Youth Affairs Offices to revamp CP/GBV sub-cluster meeting where it doesn’t exist and continue to further strengthen where it exists. Also, Plan International, will endeavor to share the outcomes of this intervention and lessons to be learned under this action with relevant partners.
