Project Summary

Although the R-ARCSS resulted in improved security and opportunities for recovery, due to the cumulative impacts of conflict, destroyed livelihoods and persistant localised insecurity, 7.1 million people in 2019 were in need of humanitarian assistance. With the government having limited capacity to responds nearly 90% of assistance is provide by international and national NGOs. There is need for NGOs to share information on learning from humanitarian and development programmes, understanding of government policies and regulartory processes regarding providing humanitarian assistance. NGOs, particularly national NGOs lack access to security information to allow for for planning and ensuring safety and protection of staff and beneficiaries. National NGOs ofthen lack capacity to engage in coordination mechanisms to share learnings to improve programming. Coordination needs to be considered to minimise misunderstandings between NGOs, UN agencies and government, and, inevitably, a reduction in the available services for beneficiaries and improve the operating environment for humanitarian agencies.
Concern will continue to host the NGO Forum to support NGOs to contribute to principled and effective humanitarian assistance in South Sudan by members. The NGO Forum will provide information sharing for members on learnings to improve humanitarian and development approaches and forging collaborations between the INGOs and national NGOs. Security information will be compiled and shared with members to allow for planning of humanitarian and development programmes to ensure safety and protection of members’ staff and beneficiaries. The NGO Forum will coordinate collective voices of NGO members for common positions; and joint advocacy for enabling environment operating in South Sudan. Capacity of national NGOs will be reinforced through providing capacity building for NGOs through providing humanitarian training; sharing best practices in humanitarian delivery and information exchanges.

1 - Needs

Despite R-ARCSS and opportunities for recovery, due the cumulative impacts of conflict and destroyed livelihoods and persistant localised insecurity, 7.1 million people were in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019 (HNO 2019). 5.7 million received humanitarian aid so far in 2019 (OCHA, Aug 2019) and 91% of this is delivered by national and international NGOs most of whom are members of the NGO Forum. Currenlty there are over 24,000 South Sudanese who continue to work in difficult conditions and are huge risk to reach populations in need of humanitarian assistance. National staff are the majority of aid workers compared to also over 2,000 international NGO staff. The increase in state and national level requirements and regulations for NGOs by the government authorities have meant NGOs have become increasingly required to pay fees and requested for authorisations and taxes. While these process by government authorities mostly facilitate assistance, they often result in lack of understanding or confusion for aid workers and can impact on humanitarian activities where NGOs have not ensured compliance. There is a need to have a resource for mobilising NGO Forum, UN or government authorities to address these issues and reduce delays in delivering humanitarian assistance in a timley manner and improve cooperation with government authorities to facilitate humanitarian assistance. NGO Forum members have identified information sharing and coordination as a key activity of the NGO Forum. Members have stated the need to have access to critical information such as on security, humanitarian needsand government regulations and procedures in relation to humanitarian assistance. Information sharing allows for supporting networking between NGOs. Coordination with members encourages the development of common positions to enhance overall NGO engagement and delivery. NGOs need to have a stable and safe operating environment and this means safe and secure access to locations where there are populations in need for staff and partners. While NGOs have a duty of care responsibility for staff, ultimately it is the host government who has to ensure safety for staff and beneficiaries. NGOs need to have security information that is triangulated and relevant to make informed decisions on security procedures and risk mitigation strategies to safely deliver humanitarian assistance. Hence the security function of the NGO Forum is prioritised as a critical need by members. National NGOs play a critical role in being the first responders to provide humanitarian assistance and can easily access to populations due to local staff having strong relations with local communities. However, many lack access to networks of humanitarian organisations for learnings and coordination and lack capacity to expand and improve their programmes. External engagement and advocacy is an important need for members for representing NGOs’ interests with donors, government and UN agencies. This is particularly important for national NGOs who often lack the networks to engage with relevant coordination mechanisms, obtain information and advocate for their beneficiaries.

2 - Activities

CO1: The NGO Forum will compile and disseminate information relevant to support humanitarian, recovery and development activities and policies. This includes relevant government publications, circulars and announcements for NGOs and coordinating with relevant line ministries so they are easily understood by NGO audiences. A contact directory of different agencies will be maintained such as donors and Country Directors for NGOs. Briefings will be published on issues of common interest to NGOs and will be defined by the NGO Forum Steering Committee. The NGO Forum will consult with national NGOs to identify strengths and gaps in capcity to collate information to inform humanitarian, recovery and development programming. The NGO Forum will continue to maintain links with other coordination mechanism such as the cluster system to have accurate and up to date information. The NGO Forum will facilitate learning between NGOs and peer support to include coordination with donors, UN agencies and government. CO2: A key function of the NGO Forum will be security information sharing and coordination functions to ensure NGOs have access to information to make critical and real time decisions on security so as to ensure safety of their staff and beneficiaries. Members will be supported to develop risk mitigation strategies through frequent sharing of analytical products, compiling and dissemintaiton of relevant statitics and maps and weekly security meetings. CO3: To ensure timely access to populations in need of humanitarian assistance, the NGO Forum will continue to engage with government on issues including regulations and humanitarian access. More sensitive issues will be addressed through groups such as the Humanitarian Country Team. Regular meetings with the RRC and the NGO Forum will support more effectiveness and efficiency in implementing humanitarian and development programmes. National NGO members will be supported by the NGO Forum through the National NGO Focal Point and National NGO Information Officer to strengthen their capacity to share information and develop common approaches on humanitarian and development programmes. An National NGO resource centre will continue to be supported to provide a work space for National NGO staff and internet access for communication. Support will also be provided to national NGOs during incidences such as relocation of national staff from the field to country offices.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

NGO Forum members will adhere to humanitarian principles of neutrality, impartiality and humanity to ensure their programmes target based on need only. Access and misunderstanding of government policies and regulations are often a barrier to adhering to principles to meet needs of vulnerable populations. The NGO Forum will coordinate with government to ensure these barriers are addressed and allow for access for NGO members.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Information sharing activities will include sharing learnigns on conflict sensitivity through collaboration with the Conflict Sensitivity Resource Facility so members can ensure their programmes minimise impacts on social and conflict dynamics. These include considering needs of vulnerable host community as well as IDP and returnee populations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The NGO Forum will engage with government authorities such as the RRC to support NGO members to understand government policies and regulations regading humanitarian programming. The NGO Forum ahs strong relations with authorities at national level and this will increase acceptance to improve security of its members.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The NGO Forum supports information sharing on government policies such as nutrition, health and food security and supports members to incorporate these into their humanitarian and development programmes.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

While R-ARCSS has resulted in some improvements in security and humanitarian access, localised insecurity persists and there is still instability making it difficult to implement humanitarian programmes. Humanitarina needs persist with the government having limited capcity or resoruces to respond. The NGO Forum has capacity to provide warnings on potential security and protection risks affecting NGO operations in South Sudan. The programme will support NGOs in engaging with local authorities to address impediments to humanitarian responses and facilitate protection activities of NGOs through working with them to address access challenges, increasing protection funding and reinforcing capacities of NGO staff.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The NGO Fourm will focus on supporting national NGOs through the National NGO Focal Point and National NGO Information Officer to support networking of national NGOs and civil society organisations to share learning and develop common approaches on humanitarian and development issues. This will support NGOs to develop strong programmes and access funding with need for support from international NGOs.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Due to government having limited capacity and resources to respond to humanitarian needs, NGOs and UN agencies are required to provid 80-90% of humanitarian responses. NGOs implement programmes where there are security and protection risks to both staff and beneficiaries. In particular, national NGOs are often the first to respond and are in remote locations and face considerable security and protection risks. There are challenges including difficulties in staff recruitment, harassment of aid workers, diversion of aid, extortions and intimidation, blockages and illegal taxation, looting of humanitarian assets/materials, permits restrictions and restrictions of access to areas with populations in need of assistance. The NGO Forum will provide up to date real time security information to NGO members to enable them to plan and mitigate against security and protection threats to their staff and communities they work with. The programme will support NGOs to engage with local authorities and community leaders to access populations in need of humanitarian assistance.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The NGO Forum Secretariat annually surveys members to provide feedback on the performance of the Secretariat, the Steering Committee and the overall functions and services of the NGO Forum. To better meet needs of international and national NGOs, separate surveys are conducted while maintaining a core group of common questions in both surveys. The surveys include satisfaction levels and usefulness on the NGO Forum’s services. A Steering Committee represents NGO members and is comprised of 10 elected members. The Secretariat is comprised of a technical team of support staff lead by the NGO Forum Director. The NGO Forum consists of tow constituencies, national NGOs and international NGOs and are served by a joint Steering Committee of international and national NGO members coordinated by the NGO Secretariat.
