Project Summary

The project will work to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality through provision of ANC, safe delivery, early initiation of breastfeeding, and PNC. Services will be provided through the reproductive health units/clinics within the supported nutrition centres. SC will train midwives on IYCF-E concepts and best practices with special focus on early initiation of breastfeeding and skin-to-skin contact, including within the first few hours after delivery. To address the challenges to follow-up visits, community health workers (CHWs) will be trained to conduct basic ANC and PNC visits at household level and/or virtually, and to support with referrals and transportation. In light of COVID-19, new protocols and operational guidelines have been adopted for the clinics including limiting waiting room areas to 3-4 people and ensuring distancing between health staff and beneficiaries.
To ensure that the target population has sufficient immune protection against any wild polio virus transmission, SC will aim at 95% vaccination coverage among children 6-59 months of age against WPV in target areas (OPV3) while contributing to reduced morbidity and mortality due to other vaccine-preventable diseases by restarting the routine vaccination program (DPT3) through supporting multiple EPI centers in North West Syria.
Furthermore, the following ANC/PNC services will be will be provided through mobile units/clinics within IDP camps and hard-to-reach areas, and with the support of community health workers:
- Antenatal iron and folic acid supplementation
- Prescription for antenatal calcium supplementation
- Tetanus toxoid (in coordination with vaccination team), women will be referred to the nearest vaccination team after assessing their tetanus vaccination status.
- Basic blood tests (RH group, blood glucose) (available in the mobile clinics)
- Ultrasound scans, monitor foetal growth and movement (available in the mobile clinics)
- Screening for pregnancy-induced hypertension.
- Postnatal care checks for women who recently gave birth, check against main mother and baby danger signs, and refer cases accordingly.
Adolescents will be targeted in project activities - mainly by providing them access to the reproductive health, family planning and post-abortion care. Furthermore, activities will be implemented in areas easily accessed by PWD, and supporting people with physical disabilities to have access to the health services. The staff also will receive basic PSS training that can support in supporting the identification of people with mental health disorders. People with living with disability will be referred to service providers, if they are available in the area. Emergency cases are referred through the allocated ambulance car in the center, with the referral forms filled for each, while for non-emergency referral is made by community health workers and/or facilities staff, through indicating the health facilities where the patient can seek the specific type of services in.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, 12.4 million people are in need of health assistance, 5.6 million of them children. The health care system has been severely disrupted in the last decade, bearing the consequences of the ongoing conflict. In 2021, only 58% of hospitals and 53% of primary health care centers remain functional. The economic downturn, coupled with the effects of the global COVID-19 pandemic has further deteriorated the availability of services, and limited access to health care for the most vulnerable communities around Syria. Routine immunization coverage remains low, coupled with disruptions in the EPI program in 2020 due to COVID-19. For example, DPT3 coverage will remain below heard immunity levels in at least half of Syria’s governorates. The health sector severity scale indicates that the situation in both Aleppo and Idleb governorates is catastrophic. In these areas, women and girls have limited access to sexual and reproductive health services (HNO 2021). At present, one in three pregnant women in Syria is anemic and one out of four children between 6-59 months is anemic due to poor intrauterine growth, complications at birth and generally high risk pregnancies as a result of poor maternal health. Maternal malnutrition rates have increased fivefold between 2019 and 2021, with 21% of pregnant and lactating mothers malnourished in North West Syria (HRP 2020). In 2021, the HNO Summary by UNOCHA suggests an estimated 1.2 million mothers and women of child-bearing age who will continue to suffer the consequences of maternal anemia and/or malnutrition if not treated appropriately.

5 - Implementation of activities

The health response in NWS consist of 2 reproductive health clinics,7 EPI centers, 6 family planning and post-abortion care centers, and one mobile health and nutrition clinic. The CHW components are linked to these centers to ensure access to health services and deliver awareness-raining messages. These activities are implemented in partnership with local partners. M&E is mainstreamed within all projects, where data collection tools are designed and shared with partners to collect the data required for each project. This includes registration forms, CHWs visit tracker, patient files and recoding forms for activities carried out outside the facilities. Data is collected on a monthly basis. When possible, SC conducts third party monitoring to evaluate the progress and the project’s relevance to the population’s needs, also to ensure beneficiaries have meaningful access to two-way complaint and feedback mechanisms. SC and the partners continuously coordinate with other health actors, the Health Cluster and a number of working groups to map the available health services to avoid overlapping and duplication in efforts. This also enables to have referral pathways in place.All health project staff received training on nutrition aspects, mainly in IYCF-E, to disseminate awareness on the IYCF-E aspects including the importance of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding up to six months, complementary feeding and referral for women with IYCF difficulties, and support women initiating breastfeeding delivering in the facilities.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

SC is invested in the Centrality of Protection and is developing an action plan that focuses on capacity building of staff and partners, embedding of conflict sensitivity and protection analysis, strengthening safe programming and child safeguarding approaches, improving risk management and integrating protection into advocacy work. In addition, SC integrates protection mainstreaming across its programs, and these principles guide the implementation team to ensure safe and quality programming. Building on the foundation of all staff members being trained on Child Safeguarding, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and Safe Programming, SC is also expanding capacity building of staff in conflict sensitivity, protection analysis and participatory approaches. A child safeguarding risk assessment, gender and GBV audit, and conflict sensitivity analysis are conducted for each program. These identify gaps, inform the project design, and mitigate protection risks for children and their families while accessing SC facilities and using SC services. In addition, SC has prioritized more sustained capacity-building for staff related to disability and disability inclusion, as well as on gender mainstreaming and GBV.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Unstable security situation: SC regularly monitors the local context, and SC employs security focal points. SC staff complete mandatory security trainings. Context-specific Safety and Security Management Plans are regularly reviewed and updated. Money transfer issues and currency fluctuations (devaluation of SYP): SC mitigates this risk by using multiple money transfer channels and agents who have strong reputations and have been able to deliver funds in almost all circumstances inside Syria. All SC agreements are denominated in donor currency. Risks due to the COVID-19 pandemic: The situation may result in risks to children and their caregivers due to lack of access to programming sites; further exposure to protection concerns, including violence; and health risks to beneficiaries and SC staff members. Relevant programme adaptations have been developed and are currently in place. SC has also introduced Standard Operation Procedures for staff working remotely and to identify COVID-19 focal points in each programme area. Fraud management: All SC staff complete mandatory trainings, which include Fraud, Bribery, and Corruption and Aid Diversion and Financing of Terrorism. Financial, procurement and supply chain procedures further eliminate the risk through multiple vetting processes and checks, including the partners. Weak logistics capacity: To support an effective supply chain system, field staff receive technical and systems trainings on processes and management and contingency plans are in place in case of loss of access.
