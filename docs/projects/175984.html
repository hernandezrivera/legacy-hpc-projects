Project Summary

The project life-saving and resilience WASH response to vulnerable population affected by conflict and flood (IDPs, Returnees and hosts) aims to save lives and increase resilience to 30,000 (Women 10,440; Men 7,560; Girls 6,960; Boys 5,040) vulnerable population in Central Equatoria (Kajokeji, Yei and Terekeka), Warrap (Tonj North) and Eastern Equatoria ( Kapoeta East) counties. The project will contribute to improving access to safe, clean and adequate water supply, gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene services for vulnerable women, girls, boys, and men elderly people with disabilities. The project is expected to achieve its objectives through the use of an AAP community approach that will address the most needed WASH and protection related issues of the vulnerable population and to enhance their resilience by addressing the main drivers of vulnerability. The project will seeks to provide temporal and sustained access to WASH services for vulnerable communities affected by the disasters in the targeted locations. The response will aim to reduce the prevalence of water borne diseases and covid-19 pandemic, build resilience of the affected population, and increase sustainability of WASH infrastructures and to reduce risks of violence against women, girls, and boys and at risk groups when accessing WASH facilities. It will promote good hygiene practices through conducting 26 Hygiene Promotion sessions that include Covid-19 prevention in all the target locations. Since this includes a Behavioral Change process it will cover approximately 50% of the target population. Awareness activities will be conducted if full observance of COVID-19 SOPs among different groups including public places like schools, health facilities and market places. 56 defunct boreholes will be rehabilitated, 4 new boreholes will be constructed for areas with severe water shortage. Additionally 4,600 most vulnerable households will be supported with WASH NFIs, (jerry cans, buckets, basins, washing soaps and water treatment tabs) to improve the house hold water management. 2,600 most vulnerable women of child bearing age and adolescent girls will be supported with women dignity kits (MHM) to improve their menstrual hygiene management status and protect their dignity. While delivering the most needed WASH services, resilience of the affected population will be enhanced. Members from the community will be trained to oversee and manage the operation and maintenances of community WASH facilities with 30% women representation. Community hand pump mechanics will be trained with 20% women representation who will thereafter carry out repair and maintenance of community water supply facilities. Community hygiene promoters will be trained to regularly carry out hygiene awareness among the communities. Women and girls will be empowered to fully participate in activities and able to take appropriate decisions. The response is expected to reinforce and strengthened protection of the various groups affected by the conflict and flood. The project will install 39 hand washing facilities in public places, markets, schools and health facilities to mitigate spread of COVID-19. WASH related protection risks analysis will be conducted to guide on the project design fitting the local context in terms of sociocultural dynamic and mitigate risks among various groups. Awareness on GBV, sexual exploitation and child abuse related to WASH shall be conducted to inform the communities to enable them mitigate and prevent it. The response is expected to ultimately contribute to improvement of the general condition of the affected population, including protection related to WASH and recognition of the dignity of the affected population and subsequently reduce vulnerability and enhance resilience.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in the country remains challenging. According to OCHA HNO 2021, about 8.3m people will be in need of various kinds of humanitarian assistance across the country. 5.9m will need access to clean, safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services by the year 2021. These needs are closely linked to conflict, flooding, economic down turn that still exert further impact on already strained WASH services. Moreover, the huge needs is related to inter communal fighting, recurring floods and limited physical infrastructures. Increased displacement places more burdens on existing WASH infrastructures in displacement areas. According to WASH HNO 2021, it is estimated that only 36% of households have access to improved water source in less than 30 minutes. Whereas 64% rely on unsafe water sources also experience limited access to basic safe sanitation hence practice open defecation. Only 12% of the population in central Equatoria and 4% in Warrap have access to WASH NFIs, washing soaps, jerry cans, buckets and water treatment tabs. According to WASH periodic report in 2021 shows high prevalence of malnutrition in some of the project locations GAM rates 2021: Terekeka 22.0%, Kapoeta East 23.0%, Tonj North 15% are also link to poor WASH conditions as exemplified in the diarrhea – malnutrition cycle prevalence in vulnerable communities. As a result of poor access to safe water supply and sanitation services, women, girls are exposed to risks of gender based violence when accessing water and sanitation facilities. According to interagency assessment conducted in August 2020 in Tonj North county and IRW independent assessment conducted in October 2020 in Tonj north county, IRW independent assessment for Kajokeji county in Oct 2020, shows 52.16%, 49.71% and 42% of boreholes are nonfunctional respectively 42% Kapoeta East. This is due to lack of access to spare parts and tools that results to poor operation and maintenance. 86% of HHs in Tonj North, 78% in Terekeka, 54% in Kapoeta east and north and 34%% Kajokeji and Yei do not meet the sphere standards of 7L-15L/p/day. The situation that leaves a larger population (54%) without sustained access to clean and safe water supply and increase the burden of water collection for women, Girls and small boys. The situation that will also lead to protection concerns. Water shortage in Tonj North and east is more related to persistent communal conflict resulted to the displacement of hundreds of vulnerable people. Similarly critical gaps exists in WASH services in payams of Terekeka due to flooding that displaced thousands of people. The assessment reports indicates sanitation situation remains very poor. In Tonj North 89% of the population do not have access to HH latrines, only, 1% have access to shared latrines. Similarly in Terekeka, Kapoeta east and North access to HH latrines remains very low at 6%. In Yei where IRW has implemented WASH projects is relatively better about 61% however in Kajokeji the situation is dire as 15% as the population are using some kinds of traditional latrines. The needs to improve access to sanitation facilities remains a high priority in order to reduce the rate of water borne diseases and now Covid-19. In Tonj North only 5% HHs stated can afford soap for hand washing, 5% in Terekeka, 2% Kapoeta East and North. While 12% Yei and Kajokeji. Only 4% have hand washing containers in the HHs visited. Most of health facilities have hand washing containers but lacks functional boreholes. The need to increase water for hand washing among the affected communities and institutions remains a high priority. Women and girls interviewed from the various locations reported feeling unsafe at one point or another while collecting water. The need for improving protection related to access for water and sanitation remain high priority in all the targeted locations

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRWSS has an established policy on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse. The organization designs all its projects with focus on the policy, and safe guards all IRSS activities. IRSS will ensure the project is committed to ensuring that standards and measures of ethical conduct on mitigation and prevention of risks on forms of sexual exploitation and abuse are put in place through establishment of communication and dissemination of information on PSEA mitigation and prevention. IRSS will ensure that project staff including volunteers are well trained and reinforced with all the required knowledge on PSEA prevention. There will be awareness providing gender –responsive trainings, sessions to staff members and community leaders, committees, on the inclusion and ethical conduct, including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse focusing on the vulnerability of women and girls. Staff members and volunteers will be advised to avoid any action that could create the perception that engaging in sexual activities. Risk analysis for women and girls will be conducted that would lead to sexual exploitation and abuse. Putting in place a monitoring mechanism to prevent , detect and expose sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People living with disabilities will be identified and reached, ensure they are fully represented, a consultation plate form will be established that will recognized the grievances, complaints and feed backs from people living with disabilities. A verbal complaint/ feed back to the CFB focal person is advantageous to persons with low literacy level, will have the platform to express themselves and make a recommendation that can allow the organization further improve on the project. The deaf people should have a person that is able to conduct translation and explain back to them and get back their opinions and feelings about the project. Much attention will be put on people with special needs that includes people with disabilities, the elderly, those with low literacy levels, the deaf, the blind and make sure all of them get equal opportunity to air out their complaints/feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRSS will continue to work through the existing various levels of the Government and community structures and systems. The organization will work with county and payam departments of rural water supply and sanitation from county to payam levels who are integral part of the intervention. Moreover, Relief and rehabilitation commission as a major coordinating body between the various levels and the county and payam water departments are major stakeholders that IRSS will closely work with. While in the community systems, engagement of community hygiene promoters (volunteer), community based hand pump mechanics and water management committees, the established complains and response committees all play major roles in WASH service deliveries. The community members are also contact points for intervention including the mass hygiene campaigns. Through the water management committees, the community based hand pump mechanics and community hygiene volunteers’ sustainability, ownership and community feedback are highly considered. The response are done to the Government line departments. Information management is equally done through these departments down to the communities. The understanding of the whole cycle of the WASH response by the local community members, building community owned resource structures like the water management committees, complaint and response committees contributes to long-term intervention given that it empowers the community to play their own roles on matters of improving their WASH status. A complaint /feedback response committee is established and trained on community deemed vulnerabilities criteria, the members will be individuals from the water management committee, community hygiene volunteers and community based hand pump mechanics trained on the purpose of data collection from the community and report back through the established system. All groups including women, girls, boys, men, elderly, and people with disabilities and marginalized if any, can direct their complaints/grievances through this mechanism and receive appropriate feed backs timely. A CFB focal person is responsible for recording complaint from each complainant on the complaint/feed back form while greatly considering confidentiality of the complainant that is shared with the country office an appropriate decision and action to be taken. IRSS response to these complaints are always clear, accurate, balanced, simple, fair and easy to understand. All the community members from various groups ( including people living with disabilities, those with low literacy levels) can direct their complaint/grievances through this mechanism and receive appropriate feed back timely. All groups including women, girls, boys, elderly, individuals among the marginalized groups if any, can direct their individual complaint/feed back in person. Verbal feed back to the complaint focal person is advantageous to persons with low literacy level have the platform to express themselves and make a recommendations that allow the organization further improve on the project. Much attention will be put on people with special needs that include people with disabilities, the elderly and those with low literacy levels and make sure all of them get equal opportunity to air out their complaints/feed back.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries is considered as an important factor in achieving project objectives. The affected population will be fully engaged throughout the different stages of the project, during planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Information gathered from beneficiary feedback mechanism will be used for validation, testing of assumption, bringing out hidden issues, identifying red flags, enabling learning opportunities and to make adaptations based on changing needs and contexts.
