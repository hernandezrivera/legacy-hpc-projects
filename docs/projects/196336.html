Project Summary

Sector wide, agencies are supporting the scale up of the Myanmar Curriculum program, and IRC will be expanding its focus on supporting learners to enter the MC, in addition to targeting teachers and Sector-members with additional capacity building and knowledge sharing opportunities. Equity and technical gaps persist in access for learners, with strong disparities in learning seen between boys and girls in IRC's 2022 Pop Up Research. In 2023, IRC will focus on narrowing this equity gap, working with children (3200), caregivers (5360), teachers (100) and sector members to expand access as well as evidence-based programming in the Rohingya education response. The project is aligned with the Education Sector's 3 Objectives: Access, Quality and Community Engagement. The project will reach its goal through continuing and establishing learning centers for the expansion of the MC under the ECW MYRP consortia, through reaching caregivers to strengthen Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities through LEGO funded ECD programming, through expanding the teacher supply in the host community and through continued work leading the ERICC Education Research in EiE project. The project will expand the identification, mapping and use of evidence-based decision making in the Education Sector, with the goal of adding rigor and identifying priority areas for a new Sector level research agenda. The project will follow ISCG and education sector’s standard and guidelines during implementation and will share all required data/information agreed with sector as sector’s partner as well as ensure compliance with government law/policy and will work closely CiC, site management and education focal at camp level. The project will identify the safe learning facilities considering the risk prone mapping developed by ISCG. IRC’s emergency preparedness team will orient relevant stakeholders on DRR and emergency preparedness plan as well. Particularly focusing on the caregivers/community members, there will be capacity building and sensitization activity/messaging’s which will focus on gender issues like girls’ education and drop out, early marriage prevention messages, parenting, social cohesion between two communities. Even while designing projects, IRC will focus on the exact need of the two community and will divide the activities accordingly to reduce tension between the two communities. IRC in 2023 is also planning to support teachers from the host community particularly on the areas of emergency responsive teaching learning for the host community, teacher and student well being and sensitization.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to more than 860,000 (ISCG situation report, June 2020). Schools and learning centers were closed for more than 18 months due to COVID_19, finally reopening in late 2021 after some of the longest closures globally. These closures have had a still unknown impact on children's learning and retention in schools. Disruptions to education have left up to 70% of 10-year-old unable to read or understand a simple text, up from 53% before the pandemic. Children from the poorest households, children with disabilities, and girls were much less likely to access remote or distance learning during lockdowns than their peers, with existing inequalities exacerbated over many months of school closure for the poorest and most marginalized girls. In other low middle income contexts, school children are between 75% and a full school year behind where they should be, with millions reported as dropped out during extended COVID-19 closures. According to Bangladesh national 2020 data, 17.2% children in primary education have dropped out of the system which is likely to increase due to the long school closure.IRC's 2022 report, "The State of Learning for Rohingya Refugee Children Affected by Displacement and Crisis in Bangladesh" notes the following barriers hampering quality education provision: 1) a lack of qualified teachers, with specific challenges in identifying qualified female Rohingya educators; 2) complex language issues, with divergent language of instruction and home (oral) language; 3) curriculum restrictions and changes, with the shift from the LCFA to the MC; and 4) COVID-19, with extended disruptions to education and a lack of learning continuity. IRC's assessments showed that 78% of children are non-readers, with only 22% showing emergent English literacy skills; over 32% of children have not developed any math skills, 67% are gaining foundational math skills, and only 20% of children being able to conduct simple subtraction problems correctly. Overall, the achievement gender gap was stark, with boys outperforming girls in all learning measures. For example, in literacy, the differences were statistically significant across all EGRA subtasks, with boys scoring, for example, 20 more correct letter sounds per minute than girls. In addition, children with disabilities are behind in literacy tasks, as well as in assessed social emotional learning dimensions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IRC will seek to align with Principle #3- Integrated Approaches through continuing engagement in inter-sectoral camp-based coordination activities; providing services to both refugee and host community populations with an equity and needs-based lens; and focusing on strengthening relationships with the government in both camp and hosting communities. IRC's new funding seeks to focus on both camp and hosting communities, with a priority given to programming in the host community. This programming will include innovations in increasing teacher supply, seeking to mitigate the challenges of teacher loss due to the refugee crisis. IRC will be engaging the local government in the creation of a shared research agenda, highlighting, and prioritizing shared research gaps between the government and camp-focused implementing agencies and coordination groups. IRC will seek to align with Principle #4- Ensure Predictability and Continuity through ensuring stability in refugee-focused programming and improving relationships between key stakeholders and partners in the camps. IRC will seek to contribute to this predictability and continuity through its leadership of the new ERICC Education in Emergencies Research Project, in which buy-in from key government and sector stakeholders will contribute to the development of a shared research agenda, reflecting the priorities and perceived gaps with key stakeholder groups involved in the provision of education services in the refugee and hosting community. In addition to that, IRC’s current activity will focus to remain in the existing camps where IRC is working over three years to avoid duplication of effort with other organizations. Even for the new activities, it will try to be in the same camps where other activities are ongoing based on the gaps and needs identified. A close coordination with education sector, CiCs and camp focal will be maintained to avoid overlapping.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

IRC is a global leader in Protection and Child Protection programming, and currently implements Protection Rule of Law, Women's Protection and Child Protection programming in Bangladesh. IRC's Education department draws on this expertise to ensure rigorous approaches to child safeguarding, codes of conduct, and improved safety and referrals for children in education programs. IRC will continue to strengthen approaches to ensuring safety and reducing gender based violence in education programs. This will be accomplished through ongoing research efforts, which include gap analysis on safety and protection in education programs, with a potential focus to fund research into protection based risks and barriers impacting school aged children. IRC will work with parents and caregivers to raise awareness on codes of conduct, violence at home, and reporting mechanisms for violence in schools and on the way to school which impacts children's learning and development.

3.2 - Gender

Since IRC's most recent evaluations have shown a significant gendered gap in learning and SEL outcomes, IRC will seek to provide additional support for girls to narrow this achievement gap. This will include remediation programs, the design and development of technology-assisted innovation projects targeting adolescent girls and addressing holistic learning barriers. IRC will continue to support and equip female Rohingya facilitators, despite severe capacity constraints, to successfully deliver the MC project. Under ECW MYRP II, IRC BD has a target of 60% girls’ learners out of all the target it has for the mentioned project. In addition to that, for adolescent programming, IRC has a plan to enroll 300 adolescent’s girl and recruit 100% facilitator as female to support the girl’s education in the CBLFs. To support their educational activities, apart from the educational materials, they will be supported with dignity/menstrual kit quarterly.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

In addition to the mentioned Protection activities, IRC ensures a strict adherence to Child Safeguarding in all of its programming. All staff who work with children, in addition to all partner staff, are trained on Child Safeguarding policies, and these policies are posted in a visible fashion in all learning spaces. Violations of Child Safeguarding face a zero tolerance policy for all staff and teachers working with children. IRC will take a proactive approach to getting feedback from children, so that complaints are not made using only passive mechanisms. This will include the use of active Child Satisfaction Surveys for children in learning centers.

3.4 - Diversity

IRC seeks to provide equitable services based on need to all client populations. IRC's staff is 99% Bangladeshi, and facilitators are recruited from the refugee and hosting community. IRC's ensures that all program targeting is needs based, and this begins with understanding the challenges and barriers facing learners and communities. IRC's 2022 research report highlighted significant gendered discrepancies with children's learning and wellbeing that need to be sufficiently addressed by education actors.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to client populations is the cornerstone of all IRC's programming. IRC seeks to ensure all programs are based on participatory needs assessments, so that project design reflects the inputs of communities, including children. Ongoing monitoring seeks to improve engagement with children, whose voices are often overlooked in improving accountability to clients. Active mechanisms for engaging children, based on IRC's new Child Participation Guidelines (2022), will be employed so that child-friendly mechanisms are used to obtain diverse perspectives on design and implementation, and that safety risks facing children are understood and handled in an active and not passive manner. Not only children, but also parents also/caregivers and community members will be engaged in multiple ways through out the project design and implementation through community consultations, testing of contents developed and agreeing on a delivery mechanism which best suits the needs of the community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Under ECW-funded programming, IRC will conduct training for teachers and community members on DRM. The goal of these DRM trainings will be to understand risks, local capacities, and mitigation measures which can be utilized to reduce risks facing communities. Considering context-specific natural hazards and previous disaster impacts, DRM has been incorporated to reduce the risk of negative effects and adverse impacts on life and the environment; the project has established an information management system to ensure early warning dissemination, multi-hazard preparedness messages to increase awareness of target population, enhance knowledge and skills of frontline staffs and volunteers to implement disaster risk reduction interventions effectively and identify the risk of natural hazard through community risk assessment periodically.
