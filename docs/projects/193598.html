Project Summary

The project interventions were designed to respond to the HNO 2023 and the first and third specific objectives of the FSS HRP 2023 response matrix namely: SO 1: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced SO 3: FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, including civil society organizations, key national ministries and concerned donors.
The project will target three areas within Salfit governorate, namely, Deir Istya, Kifil Haris and Haris as confirmed by the MOA and RWDS/PARC needs assessment as the most vulnerable locations within Salfit governorate..
The project aims at enhancing the resilience of 61 vulnerable families (287 HH members) consisting of 67 women, 65 men, 70 boys, 73 girls, 9 elderly and 3 disabled, through improving the food security of vulnerable women farmers. In addition to raising awareness of 90 community members (60 women and 30 men) (HH 423) on topics related to women labor rights and value of their work in the agricultural sector, and reaching 1 million people through the social media campaign on women labor rights. This will be realized through the below mentioned components:
Component one: Rehabilitation of 45 plots of agricultural land to plant thyme seedlings to feed into thyme production units run by rural women following the value chain model.
Component two: Construct 16 greenhouses to be planted with various seeds and seedlings to support rural women livelihood and resilience
Component three: Increase resilience of rural women in the agricultural sector by lobbying and advocating with decision makers and government bodies about women labor rights, protection and valuing their work in the agricultural sector.
Moreover, it is worth mentioning that once the project is approved there will be an inception phase where RWDS will announce the project in the targeted communities and provide the applications for the interested women famers to apply for the project based on clear indicators that include priority to disabled family members . Then RWDS will establish 3 local committees (one committee per location) that consist of members of the local authority, ministry of agriculture directorate office, related CBOs and RWDS staff. Those committees will include both women and men to ensure gender equality. The committees will agree on a selection criterion to be used for selecting the applicant beneficiaries, visit the initial list of applicants and select the beneficiaries that abide by the criteria. they will also contribute to the monitoring and implementation of the project.
Finally, it is important to mention that RWDS will include in the announcement of the project its complaint mechanism (HAP) and the SPEA protection and complaints mechanism. Also, a handing over and exit strategy will be prepared by RWDS, MoA and the local committees for each targeted area and be included in the final report.

1 - Needs

This project was developed in close coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) that confirmed and approved the targeted areas and type of interventions. This process started by communicating with the MoA headquarters in Ramallah that after approval of the project idea and targeted areas referred RWDS to the MoA directorate office in Salfit for on ground coordination and follow up. Also, the project was designed based on a thorough humanitarian needs assessment carried out by RWDS in cooperation with PARC in 2018- 2019 and verified in 2022. The study focused on 7 locations (Deir Istyia, Qarawat Bani Hassan, Haris, Kifil Haris, Jinsafut, Immatin and Jit within the Salfit and Qalqilieh governorates highlighting the continuous needs for agricultural land rehabilitation and cultivation, constructions of greenhouses and supply of water resources mainly for women farmers who use the products of the land in food production. The need assessment ensured participatory and human rights-based approach in its methodology which was carried out via desk review, Semi structured interviews targeting 17 people (12 men and 5 women) with members from local councils and municipalities from the targeted villages and towns. Additionally, 3 Governorate senior staff had been interviewed. In addition to 11 focus groups meetings with women, youth, local authorities and community leaders.The project will target three areas within Salfit Governorate which are Haris, kifil Haris and Deiristiya that are located in area C that face difficult situation resulted from its geographical location; as its closeness to Israeli settlements where the villages have been subjected to numerous Israeli confiscations of land, for the purposes of constructing settlements, checkpoints, outposts and bypass roads on the village territories. This is in addition to the Segregation Wall. Israeli Occupation Forces confiscated 2,811 dunums (33%) of Haris village land to establish 4 Israeli settlements: Barqan industrial Zone, Revava, Qiryat Netafim and Barqan, all located on the village western lands to be separated from Haris village by the Segregation wall planned in the village. Also the Israeli forces confiscated 361 dunums in Kifl Haris to establish “Ariel” settlement south of the town, which is considered the second largest Israeli settlement in the West Bank in terms of area. As for Deir Istiya village, Israel has confiscated 4,071 dunums of land (12% of the total town area) for the establishment of six Israeli settlements; Emmanuel, Karne Shomron, Ginnot Shomeronm, Yakir, Nofim , Revava. And ma’ale shomron. These hard conditions prevent people from obtaining adequate standards of living. The Israeli settler continuous attacks on those villages has contributed to the loss of Palestinian lands adjacent to settlements as landowners are prevented from accessing their agricultural land. . Through conducting activities to ensure safe access to agricultural lands and support production which will affect the food security of HH including women and children. The needs assessment study was in line with OCHA reports that states the vulnerability of targeted communities in Salfit Governorate to add to that, the Jerusalem Aid & Human Rights Centre report stated that 18 communities in Salfit are suffering from the enormous expansion of settlements in the nearby area, and are surrounded with 22 settlements with a total of 43,478 dunums under Israeli jurisdiction. This threatens the humanitarian living conditions and rights of Palestinians living in Salfit. The findings of the needs assessment show high unemployment and few opportunities of access to work for women. The overall concerns to safety and security, current traditional and cultural barrier, and the preference treatment given to male working exclude them from paid opportunities, particularly outside of the village. However, the transition of men out of farming and herding activities is pushing more women and girl to unpaid jobs in farming and herding, with increased “Unpaid labor” this does not only affect women, but it affects the family as a whole. Women reproductive role and caring of the family mainly children is still considered woman’s only responsibility, while men ‘help out’ when they can. So a significant part of women’s work remains socially unrecognized till this date and none of this labor is accounted for in the country’s GDP. Studies show that women make up almost 65% of the agricultural labor force in Palestine, and own less than 5% of agricultural land. Yet women are not recognized as farmers therefore reducing their work to unpaid labors. The conducted humanitarian needs assessment and the contacts made with MoA and its Salfit directorate office prioritized Haris, Kifil Haris and Deiristiya villages as the most vulnerable locations in Salfit that need land resilience and protection, rehabilitation of Agricultural lands, green houses, water tanks and pumps, seeds and seedlings distribution, provision of agriculture inputs in addition to thyme grinding machine to the production units/groups/cooperatives that will benefit as many women as possible from the community . Consequently, the proposed intervention will respond directly to the vulnerability measures identified by the assessment team, which are access to land, and protection.Finally, and for the purposes of identifying the specific needs of women farmers for this project, RWDS organized a focus group meeting with 12 women from the three targeted areas at Deir Istiya Women Society premises and discussed with them the planned interventions. Accordingly, the agricultural inputs, needs and locations for establishing the thyme production units were identified.

2 - Activities

The project activities were planned in response to the needs of the targeted localities and in line with the HNO 2023 and FSS HRP 2023 response matrix strategic objective number one” The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced” and objective number 3 “FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, including civil society organizations, key national ministries and concerned donors.” Accordingly, the proposed project defined it's overall objective: " To reduce vulnerability and protect the livelihoods of food insecure rural women farmers and marginalized people in Deir Istyia, Kifil and Kifil Haris". In response to this objective and the identified needs, RWDS planned the below interventions organized under three main components as described below: Component one: Rehabilitation of 45 plots of agricultural land to plant thyme seedlings to feed into thyme production units run by rural women following the value chain model. This will include the following activities 1. Rehabilitate 45 plots of agricultural land, 15 plots of land in each of the three targeted areas to serve 45 women farmers (211 HH members, 50 men, 48 women, 108 children and 7 elderly) by plowing and fertilizing the land, installing irrigation networks, water tanks and pumps and planting thyme seedlings. 2. Prepare 3 thyme production units, one in each targeted area, by purchasing all needed equipment and material such as thyme grinding machine for processing the thyme (Zaatar) which is considered one of the most demanded products in Salfit governorate and all communities among the West Bank. Then purchase the needed packaging and labeling materials to present the thyme products in an attractive manner in the market. Component two: Construct 16 greenhouses to be planted with various seeds and seedlings to support rural women livelihood and resilience 1. Construct 16 greenhouses over a land size of half a dunum each (total 8 dunums, 4 dunums in Deiristiya and 4 dunums in Kifil Haris). The green houses will serve 16 rural women farmers with a total of 75 HH members (18 men, 17 women, 38 children, 2 elderly). 2. Supply and install irrigation networks, water tanks and pumps and provide various seeds and seedlings to be planted and marketed by the beneficiary rural women farmers. Component three: Increase resilience of rural women in the agricultural sector by lobbying and advocating with decision makers and government bodies about their labor rights, protection and valuing their work in the agricultural sector. 1. Prepare a policy paper about rural women challenges in the agricultural sector and the absence of a national protection system that includes health and injury insurance, valuing women work by considering women farmers as formal employers entitled for minimum wage, and all other labor rights. In addition to a compensation system during crisis, natural and or man-made (settler attacks, destruction of agricultural facilities and crops by occupation, etc. 2. Organize 3 lobbying and advocacy meetings with the ministry of agriculture and other related ministries and decision makers to present the findings of the policy paper in order to lobby and advocate for rural women farmers as formal employers covered by the labor law. 3. Organize 3 awareness sessions, one in each of the three targeted areas, to raise women farmers and community members awareness about rural women farmers’ rights in the agricultural sector in addition to GBV and SPEA protection mechanisms. RWDS will consult with the PSEA Network and protection coordinators on the best practices of the SPEA protection and complaints mechanisms. There will be one session per community attended by 20 women and 10 men. 4. Prepare a TV spot about women rights in the agricultural sector to be presented on social media and other media outlets to raise public awareness about this issue. The total number expected to reach is 1 million people. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that once the project is approved there will be an inception phase where RWDS will announce the project in the targeted communities and provide the applications for the interested women famers to apply for the project with priority to disables family members. The inception phase will include guidance discussions on the compliant mechanisms with focus on PSEA. Then RWDS will establish 3 local committees (one committee per location) that consist of members of the local authority, ministry of agriculture directorate office, related CBOs and RWDS staff. Those committees will include both women and men to ensure gender equality. The committees will agree on a selection criterion to be used for selecting the applicant beneficiaries, visit the initial list of applicants and select the beneficiaries that abide by the criteria. they will also contribute to the monitoring and implementation of the project. Finally, it is important to mention that RWDS will include in the announcement of the project its complaint mechanism (HAP) which will be elaborated from day one where rights holder (beneficiaries) and duty bearers (RWDS and Local Committee) will agree on conflict resolution approach and ensuring access to information, experience exchange and dissemination in addition to the SPEA protection and complaint mechanism. Also, a handing over and exit strategy will be prepared by RWDS, MoA and the local committees for each targeted area and be included in the final report

RWDS management has also sent an email to all staff and board members about the complaint system and has also published on RWDS Facebook page and website. All of those procedures and services will be highlighted during the launching event of the project and will be added in the beneficiary applications. Guidelines about PSEA will distributed among women beneficiaries that include referral contacts and numbers in case of sexual harassment and other violated cases to women rights that will be dealt with by RWDS specialized social workers. Three launching events will be organized, one in each area, to discuss the project interventions, protection and PSEA mechanisms, and distribution of beneficiary applications that include references to the protection, PSEA and complaint mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

RWDS primary concern is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase, whether through the focus group discussions (FGDs) or questionnaires. Hence, potential targeted beneficiaries are involved in the designing phase of the conception of the action. As soon as the action starts, RWDS will meet with all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the final accepted project and explains all different steps where beneficiaries will be asked to participate and contribute to the success of the action. This informative meeting is essential to be updated by targeted beneficiaries about their situation, needs, aspiration and involve them through a participatory approach. During this meeting, implementing partners will also be present to exchange on best practices and share information and complaints responding as a core value of RWDS work as well as environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project interventions and implementation strategies adheres to the social, political, and economic context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the conducted participatory needs assessment and RWDS HAP Policies. In regards to social sensitivity, RWDS identify, perceive and understands the conservative and traditional context considering the cultural and social impediments to women and girls in Salfit Governorate and will act on that in all project phases from the announcement that takes place in a wide, easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join and share their needs. For the political side, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location and will avoid to cause any risk for the beneficiaries and stakeholders and will follow up OCHA humanitarian safety and security reports. This will entail to avoid commuting to risk areas and postponing the implementation of the specific activities until the situation allows to do so. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aims to support rural women families to empower their production abilities and access to their assets and thus improve their living conditions and dignity. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyze potential risks that the Project team will take into consideration while progressing/implementing the project. Special concerns to risks of GBV and PSEA will be counted for in all phases of the program as well as if any signs of GBV and PSEA taking place within households are identified. The staff implementing the project will be trained to identify and take proper actions by requesting RWDS GBV experts and social workers interference and support. RWDS will ensure a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation of the project. The project is designed focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, nondiscrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights laws.
