Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve access to integrated and lifesaving services through static (PHCCs, PHCUs) and mobile health and nutrition facilities amongst vulnerable populations of Bor South and Uror of Jonglei state and Kajo Keji & Morobo counties of Central Equatoria state.
The intervention will focus on screening, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation, inpatients care and mobile outreaches, preventive health promotion campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery in facilities and community through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, clinical management of rape (SGBV services), regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunisation services and strengthened referral pathways. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with the CHD and in coherence with BHI. More female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure that women’s health needs are addressed.
The project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilisation and sensitisation will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout project life cycle to ensure programme ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunisation, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI and case management. This project will complement World Bank project in Uror and Bor and HPF project in Morobo and Kajo Keji in management of communicable diseases, immunisation, save motherhood (PNC, focused ANC and deliveries), disease surveillance, management of SGBV, and Mental health problems among others which are part of WB & HPF projects activities.
Approx. 472,582 vulnerable hosts, IDPs and returnees are living in Central Equatoria and Jonglei states. (Morobo County – 113,187 people – 57,737 male and 55,450 females; Kajo Keji County – 146,983 people 77,725 male and 69,259 females; Bor South County 149, 100 people – 75,683 male and 73,417 females; and Uror county 63,313 People – 30,593 male and 32,720 females.
In this project, THESO will engage communities during the selection of Mobile medical Units (MMUs) locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMUs equipment such as Metallic trunk for keeping drugs and registers, table, chairs, mats and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. MMUs and health facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs) will be equipped with medical equipment such as sphygmomanometer, Stethoscope, Respiratory beat timers, weighing scale, Fetoscope, thermometers and data collection and reporting tools. In addition, MMUs will store essential drugs and test kits, such as MRDT, rapid test for syphilis among others, and medical equipment for quality services provision. MMUs will be staffed with local health workers and build their capacities. Staff in each MMU will have a work plan for weekly visits to all reachable villages and weekly reporting. Home Health Promoters (HHPs) will mobilise the village ahead of the MMU team visiting the village for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. Integrated service provision, that includes WASH and nutrition services required for positive health outcomes, will specifically address protection concerns of the most vulnerable groups in the community. 1 - Needs

The triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 in 2020, left some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers. (SSD HNO 2021).Protracted conflict and displacement in South Sudan have drastically reduced the availability of and access to health services, safe water, food and livelihoods opportunities, aggravating already high morbidity and mortality amongst the most vulnerable and remote communities and resulting in loss of control over, and access to vital resources, and especially impacting life, safety and dignity of women and children. (UNOCHA 2021). Inter-communal fighting, cattle raiding, and revenge attacks between armed youth groups in Central Equatoria, Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA), Lakes, Unity, Western Bahr el-Ghazal and Warrap states resulted in hundreds of deaths and injuries, displaced hundreds of thousands, and led to the suspension of humanitarian services in some areas (HRW 2021). Women and children are disproportionally affected by the crisis, representing 85% of the IDP population, facing heightened risk of SGBV with mental health and SRH consequences. South Sudan’s MMR is one of the highest in the world estimated at 789 per 100,000 live births, whereas neonatal and under-five mortality rates are 39.3 and 99.2 per 1,000 live births respectively. Existing services are extremely limited and challenging to access (WHO 2021). Only 22% of health facilities are fully functional, and especially lacking basic maternal, newborn and child health (MNCH), obstetric and SRH services, often resulting in maternal and infant mortality (MoH 2021). In Bor South, Kajo Keji, Morobo and Uror counties, average of 49% of the population has limited access to health services due to insecurity, non-functional and lack of health care services, placing people at risk of disease burden (DHIS 2 2020). In Kajo Keji, recurrent insecurity have resulted in displacement of IDPs from Korijo IDP camp and relocation of 7 300 (1255 households) to newly created makeshift IDP camp on 18 January 2021, and suspension of humanitarian activities by patners. Additional relocations to the new camp are being organized for both IDPs and host community members (WHO 2021). According to IDSR report, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity and accounted for 57,348 cases (53.6% of OPD cases), followed by AWD and ARI. Despite health services provided in some Counties, many Payams and Bomas lack access to health services located within the same County due to insecurity and distance. Health facilities are 6 -15 hours on foot from many Bomas making it impossible to provide emergency care or for sick people to reach the facilities. Some health facilities in the above Bomas/villages have few competent health personnel and often lack essential medicines and medical supplies. According to recent IPC report (December 2020-March 2021 IPC report), people in Morobo and Kajo Keji of Central Equatoria state are in crisis (IPC3) and Bor South and Uror are in emergency (IPC 4) to deteriorate further due to effect of flooding, lack of access to food, livestock and market, affecting health, nutrition and WASH conditions of the population, resulting in increased incidence of communicable diseases in the community. Immunisation coverage remains low in the above counties. In addition, in these counties, there is limited preventive and curative services for SRH, MNCH, counselling for MHPSS especially amongst survivors of SGBV, and communicable diseases like HIV/AIDS, TB, malaria, acute respiratory infections and AWD, among others. THESO currently implements multi-cluster programme in Bor South through SSHF first allocation 2020.

2 - Project activities

THESO’s proposed response plan addresses above needs, aiming to improve and expand access to integrated emergency lifesaving primary care and essential public health through static (PHCCs, PHCUs) facilities and mobile clinic services empowering the most vulnerable and community populations of Bor South, Kajo Keji, Morobo and Uror counties, targeting 472,582 persons. Interventions will complement world bank and HPF funded projects gaps focusing on screening, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation, mobile outreach, preventive health campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, MHPSS services, clinical management of rape, regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunisation services. Currently THESO is implementing SSHF SA1 project in Bor South. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions, with special attention to the needs and rights of persons with disabilities, survivors of SGBV, MHPSS needs, case management and strengthened referral systems. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with CHDs and in coherence with BHI. Local capacities will be empowered to ensure sustainable and continued service provision. Female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure women’s health needs are addressed. Community engagement will be ensured through life of project and include regular consultations, information provision with community elders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and beneficiary representatives reaching to the grassroots community leaders such as women representatives, youth representatives, local chiefs and local authorities and CHDs, and SMOH. In addition, the project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilisation and sensitisation will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout project life cycle to ensure programme ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunisation, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI and case management. THESO will engage communities during selection of MMU locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMU equipment such as Metallic trunk for keeping drugs and registers, table, chairs, mats and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. Selected MMUs and health facilities will be equipped with medical equipment, essential drugs and test kits, such as MRDT, rapid tests for quality service provision. All selected MMUs will be staffed with health workers (Nurses, Community Health workers, midwife, vaccinators and community mobilisers). All staff will be recruited locally, and their capacities built. Staff in each MMU will have a work plan integrating community mobilisation, consultations, vaccinations, home visits for specific cases, weekly visits to all reachable villages and weekly reporting. The community mobiliser will mobilise the Boma ahead of the MMU team visiting the Boma for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. Integrated service provision, that includes WASH and nutrition services required for positive health outcomes, will specifically address protection concerns of the most vulnerable groups in the community. Beneficiary feedback will be encouraged and captured. Monitoring visits will be conducted by THESO, CHDs and community representatives/ local authorities to ensure needs and rights-based service delivery.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project is ready to prevent and address Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, with use of THESO PSEA communication strategy and appropriate community complaints and response mechanism. Community were engaged during assessment and will be engaged during the inception of project implementation so that more information on PSEA and safeguard measures will be provided to beneficiaries. THESO project staff shall ensure that all organisation staff are fully trained on PSEA Policy and beneficiaries shall be provided essential information on PSEA and community response and feedback mechanism.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Usage of THESO CRM focal point persons at community and health facility will ensure that feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) are registered, recorded and feedback provided timely.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project provide beneficiaries Information about project through baseline assessments, Focused Groups Discussions, key informant interviews and KAP surveys, conducted amongst target host communities, returnees and IDPs, with community elders, women and local authorities. These assessments aimed to understand needs and challenges, priorities and protection concerns amongst displaced communities. THESO will establish new and strengthen old Community response and feedback mechanisms to ensure effective Information sharing and faster solutions to any challenges or hinderance that affect project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project shall use the beneficiaries feedback to adapt project assistance through collation and analysis of the weekly, monthly and quarterly reports that will be collected by THESO CRM focal point at Community and health facilities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Gender sensitive awareness building sessions will be conducted with male community members to build trust and confidence on potential activities and related benefits for women and families. The key drivers of female, especially maternal and child, morbidity and mortality will be discussed in order to identify life-saving interventions in SRH and MNCH. Potential gender-responsive interventions and direct impact on lives, safety and dignity of women will be discussed before interacting directly with women of the community. Female staff will be assigned to implement community mobilization, health awareness building and provide SRH, MHPSS and MNCH consultations, counselling and service provision. Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example). Project activities include establishment and strengthening of referral pathways for survivors of protection incidents. THESO will work with other local actors, including protection, health, WASH and nutrition actors as well as local authorities to design and ensure the functionality of the referral pathways. THESO will follow up on the ground and advocate with OCHA, donor community, local authorities, clusters and the NGO forum. Humanitarian assistance and service provision will be brought closer to target communities (for ex. Mobile Medical Units and weekly visits to each target boma/village for vaccinations, medicine provision, supporting referrals, ensuring safe distribution points) and sound feedback mechanisms will be put in place to ensure that accessibility constraints and distance from service delivery points do not expose women and girls to risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Awareness will be raised amongst community members on the value, importance and benefit of the feedback mechanisms.
