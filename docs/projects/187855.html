Project Summary

In the poultry sector:
The objective for this sector is to contribute to the reactivation of poultry farms for reproduction and food security, and to support the poultry sector through the following activities:
1- SDH will support 100 poultry farms selected in each region according to specific criteria where the farmers will be responsible for raising the distributed chicks until they could financialise their farms by the chicken products.
2- Purchase (chicks - sawdust - medicine - fodder supplement - charcoal - power source (solar power)- vitamins and sugar supplements.
3- The other sub-activities will be (chicks & charcoal distribution, supervise the feeding process, monitor the overall condition of the poultry, provide healthcare for the little chicks, carry out immunization during the life of the little chicks under 6 weeks according to the vaccine program.
* A portion of the production estimated at 56,000 chicken (70 * 800) will be returned and prepared for distribution in camps for IDPs and people in need according to the selected criteria by SDH.
4- Support owners of the poultry farms by animal (feeding, breeding, diseases) training.
5- Distribute hatchers to breeders working by solar power, which have egg-laying chickens.
In the livestock sector:
The activities under this section aim to support the livestock herders, living in the North of Syria. which provides to preserve the health of livestock, increases production, and grants economic security for the working families.
1-support 500 families have livestock according to criteria.
2- SDH will provide these families with necessary supplies for the livestock, such as fodder, fodder supplements (Salt and vitamins) for three months, vaccines campaigns, and veterinary services.
Training for the herders will carry out by veterinarians; The training provides the herders with information about The care for livestock, the methods of forming the fodder mixing, and the main diseases that affected the livestock and its treatment.
In the vocational training sector:
in order to alleviate the unemployment rate, which is significantly widespread in northern Syria, and provide high-quality local products
Training (500) beneficiaries, especially the female ones, through a series of vocational courses in order to meet the local market need
In the agriculture sector:
1- Cultivation of 500 hectares of wheat, chickpeas, Bean,.….ect
2- Cultivation of 4 hectares of various vegetables (cucumber, tomatoes, okra, zucchini, pumpkin green pepper)
3- Cultivation of 150 hectares of mix-fodder for animals. The crops are alfalfa, Jalban, and Barley.
3- Increasing the agricultural experience for the BNFs and reduction of unemployment.
In the Food sector:
The objective of this sector is to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people to alleviate suffering.
1- Distribute food basket for 50,000 families in North of Syria, for 8 months along with the FSL and CWG recommendations to cover the Kcal needs for these households to be aligned with the food component .The beneficiaries in this component will be selected from IDPs (including disabled, female-headed households, women and young girls, PLW, and the elderly )The targeted beneficiaries are 6250HH (25000 individuals) (Men 5000, Women 6000, Boys 6800, Girls 7200) (from them 600 disabled),SDH will be provided food baskets for full 2100 kcal person/per day, Weight of food basket is 78 kg family size 6 Persons.
2- Distribute ready eat ratio for 50,000 families in North of Syria.
3- Distribute cooked meals for 50,000 families in North of Syria
The fund for the Project is secure for 0% and unsecured for 100% and it is 100% FSL sector.

4 - Needs

11 years of the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of The situation continues to deteriorate drastically with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, compared to 9.8 million in the previous year. The food insecurity PiN has risen by 45 percent in the last year. • At least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, an increase of 57 percent compared to 2021, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2021 and WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in November 2021 was 251 percent higher when compared to November 2020. The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has been severely impacted, with half (i.e. 50 percent) of national grain requirements met through imports. • According to Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO), high cost and limited availability of quality crop and livestock production inputs, damaged irrigation infrastructure across the country, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, coupled with the direct and knock-on effects of Covid-19 and climate-induced shocks continue to drive more people into food insecurity. (2022 Needs and Response Summary)

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities are expected to cover the period till the end of Dec 2021. The project start date has been set taking into consideration the urgent need for support livestock and chicken herders during the winter months. During the lean period, farmers rely on animal feed for their livestock also, cultivate the multi-crops for themselves also, we will distribute food Basket for the emergency status of IDPs. activities will be implemented by SDH in target areas and will involve and cooperate with local partners in all stages of the implementation process. A team will be assigned including a project manager and field coordinators (agricultural engineers and veterinarians) and we will work to include employees in the staff for supporting the families (most of which being female-headed). Contracts will be made with specialized instructors to provide support in training workshops. Purchases will be carried out in a transparent manner and in accordance with the procurement strategy approved by SDH and will be overseen by the relevant specialists to get the best quality. The local team will be responsible for the implementation of all phases of the project _ procurement, distribution, monitoring and evaluation, the preparation of periodic reports, and enacting any necessary updates at every stage. Trainers will be hired to add value to the planned business plans and ensure quality standards. Follow-up training will also be undertaken up to several weeks after the training.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SDH field staff have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over vulnerable communities/beneficiaries that hold PSEA risks.SDH has institutionalized policies on PSEA, Whistle-blower, Code of Conduct, Gender,Safeguarding and Complaint, and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM).Proposed activities have been designed and are implemented with a standardized and cohesive approach through Standard Operational Procedures guided by the principles of “Do No Harm”,confidentiality, impartiality/non-discrimination, the best interest of the child. Gender, age, and diversity are sensitized Mitigating measures: 1)Effective and comprehensive mechanisms established to ensure awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel; continuous training on the topic provided by dedicated staff members. 2) Effective capacity building & training on GBV&PSEA principles for all staff is ensured. 3) Ensuring regular monitoring and evaluation through impartial assessments of needs and risks. PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (PSEA posters will be distributed to beneficiaries, PSEA banners will be placed at the distribution site,PSEA messaging/videos and hotline information will be shared with bene carries through SMS/WhatsApp).project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies, to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle and to stress that PSEA and GBV issues will be dealt with expeditiously.
