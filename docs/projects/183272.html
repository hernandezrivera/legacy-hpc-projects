Project Summary

Purpose:
• Deterioration of Families’ Economic Conditions
The economic, financial, political, and pandemic crises in Lebanon, have affected every aspect of children’s lives, including education, nutrition, and mental health. According to the assessment “Children’s future on the line” conducted by UNICEF on June 2021, nine per cent of families sent their children to work, 15 per cent stopped their education, and 60 per cent had to buy their food on credit or borrow money.
The outlook of the future is grim. The World Bank says the economy looks likely to contract by a further 9.5 per cent in 2021 and has warned that there is “no clear turning point in the horizon” (Lebanon Economic Monitor, Spring 2021). The longer children in Lebanon are out of school, the higher the possibility that they will experience violence and exploitation, including child labor and child marriage. The increasing socio-economic pressure on families in Lebanon during the economic and COVID-19 crisis, increases this possibility. The increased pressure on children to support their families may lead to an increase in psychosocial distress and negative coping mechanisms.
• Migration from Private to Public Schools
In the scholastic year 2020-2021, around 54,000 students transitioned from private to the public schools. MEHE estimates that another 50,000-60,000 Lebanese students will transfer from private to public schools during the SY 2021/2022, resulting in 100,00- 120,000 Lebanese students migrating to public schools. This will pause additional challenges to an already stressed system. A hiring freeze of teachers in public schools has been in place for around 2 years. With the rapid increase in number of students in public schools, human resources are required to match the demand. However, due to the unprecedented diminishing value of the LBP, teacher salaries in the public system are now valued at less than 1 USD per hour, which is pausing yet another serious risk of teachers leaving the schools or be on strike more often.
The purpose of this project would be to provide direct community support measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children to retain them in education and to ensure that students affected by the economic crisis are provided relevant, inclusive and quality education adhering to national and international standards on education in emergencies and child protection by ensuring that schools have maintained their basic educational resources, including adequate human resources. Ana Aqra is suggesting the “Safe Enabling Learning Environments: a Shared Responsibility” project as part of the Lebanon emergency response plan.
Ana Aqra strives to make learning accessible to all children and works to support the education sector and the public school to respond to the specific needs of all children as well as empowering partnerships to partake in the responsibility for the safe and enabling learning of girls and boys.
Targeting criteria, intervention and mechanism:
This project will support 10,000 girls and boys and 75 public schools, including those receiving learners transferred from the private sector. The project activities will include provision of funds to ensure that students affected by the economic crisis are provided relevant, inclusive and quality education adhering to national and international standards on education in emergencies and child protection. The project will also ensure that schools will receive necessary stationery, books, and funds to cover operational costs and adequate human resources. It will also ensure school retention of most vulnerable girls and boys by providing them and their caregivers with direct community support program, including remote awareness sessions, remote PSS/SEL activities, responsive caregiving and remote basic and digital literacy to parents /caregivers, assistive technologies for children with disabilities, cash for education support and food parcels during the academic year 2021-2022.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Ana Aqra will outreach and provide direct community support measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children (3-14 years old). 4800 out of school learners will be identified, 3080 learners will be referred to basic services. Remote awareness sessions will be provided to 3840 individuals. 440 learners with disabilities will be provided with assistive technology. 3360 households will be supported with cash for education on a monthly basis for 8 months. Remote capacity building and educational support will be provided to 400 caregivers of preschool and cycle 1 children. PSS/SEL activities will be implemented remotely with 5600 parents and children. Homeschooling educational kits covering the basic literacy and numeracy foundational skills as well as critical thinking and executive functions for learning independently or in groups at home will be provided to 1600 families to facilitate access to education. The families will be supported with data bundles, power banks and will be followed upon to ensure that children are engaged and parents capacity is improved. Food packages will be procured for 1000 households. Ana Aqra will contribute as well in improving formal education of most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children through schools. Funds will be transferred to 60 schools to cover the fees of 500 students enrolled in school, as well as the fees of 320 teachers.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Ana Aqra is committed to protecting beneficiaries, especially vulnerable individuals and children, from sexual exploitation and abuse, by creating a safe culture for all beneficiaries, making available safe reporting channels, conducting awareness raising sessions for beneficiaries on SEA and how to safely make a complaint, responding to complaints, facilitating independent investigations, and providing survivors with access to immediate professional assistance. Ana Aqra policies reinforce that all Ana Aqra personnel will be accountable to fulfil their ethical obligations. Trainings will be delivered on compliance with data protection policies, soft skills, and “do-no-harm” obligations. These measures ensure that all interaction with the beneficiaries comply with the ethical guidelines, with Ana Aqra association code of conduct and safeguarding and child protection policy. Parental consents and confidentiality will be ensured. Boys and girls will be equally represented.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Involving beneficiaries in all stages of an education project has been a core practice at Ana Aqra. Sharing responsibilities, building a common understanding and remaining focused are regular practices implemented to jointly address challenges and ensure the wellbeing of girls and boys. Affected population and the communities are the main drivers for the success of any intervention. The needs assessment is the first step in highlighting priorities from the perspective of the affected population, raising awareness as well as informing about the opportunity provided by the project to manage expectations. The affected population will be consulted and empowered throughout the project. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunities to provide feedback and to raise complaints through FGDs and satisfactory surveys. The affected population will be involved in the monitoring of programs and their points of view on the success and impact of the project will be reflected and documented.
