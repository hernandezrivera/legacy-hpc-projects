Project Summary

SSWU is a women-led National NGO with strong local presence, responding to the glaringly humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable people in Upper Nile State. According to recent assessment reports by UN and partners WASH is the most commonly priority need in Renk with 65% of 199,980 in need of WaSH in 2024-2025. A study by Oxfam published in May 2024 reveals that returnee women in Renk spend up to five hours accessing clean water. As the number of new of new arrival grows, the overall WASH and WASH related GBV protection needs are increasing – due to lack of sanitation and poor hygiene conditions. A 2024 Protection Cluster report 2024 classifies Renk with extreme protection severity status Gender-Based Violence cited as the first among the five most occurring protection risks requiring immediate attention. IOM and RedR UK study in 2020 in South Sudan shows poor access to adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation services plays a significant role in promoting gender-based violence against women and girls.
Leveraging on the PRM Multi-year GBV funded project in Renk county, the intervention is in line with South Sudan HNRP 2025 and specifically contribute to WASH Strategic Objective 3: provision of WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation, in addition to meeting GBV Cluster 2023 – 2025 Strategic Objective 1: Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. The intervention aims to increasing the access to safe and equitable WASH services and appropriate GBV response of people affected by the conflict by Sudan conflict, coupled with the deteriorating context due to hyperinflationary economic crisis and impact of centennial flood including worsening food insecurity and the new rise of AWD/Cholera outbreak in South Sudan. The intervention will target 21,500 individuals (Women - 6,880; Men 6,6665; Girls 3,870; Boys 3,440; PWD 645). The total budget for this intervention is USD 475,900. SSWU has secured 30% of the budget from PRM which is supporting the ongoing GBV response until September 2025.
The intervention will support the most vulnerable women, girls, female-headed households and community facilities with deteriorated WASH conditions (based on needs assessments) to improve access to WASH services. This will be followed by the GBV response (GBV team) to address consequences of the deteriorated WASH situation manifested in high rates of GBV. The project will prioritize individuals and households with GBV survivors and at-risk groups to address their specific needs (such as construction and/or rehabilitation of gender-segregated sanitation facilities in schools) to be suitable for the use of girls, distribution of WASH-NFI supplies to consider female specific needs, etc.). This will be applied for GBV response as well, where women and girls will be prioritized for GBV services.
On resilience, SWU women through revolving Women Economic Empowerment Program (WEEP) will enrolled and trained 50 women and adolescent girls in a 3 month tailoring course and mentor them into the production and distribution of reusable sanitary pads project at SSWU centre in Renk. Through this innovative resilient intiative, the project will generate 1,000 reusable, bags and other garments per month and distribute them to a small fee to the community using social enterprise approach. The 50 trainees will also receive TOT training on menstrual health hygiene so that they provide training and awareness creation in schools and community. Through the project, the women will income and support their families. SSWU will regularly report implementation of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services

1. Needs

The massive influx of refugees and returnees fleeing the ongoing conflict in Sudan has caused humanitarian crisis in Renk county putting immense strain on the county’s limited resources, all exacerbated by the challenges of providing aid in a volatile environment. According to UN October 2024 report more than 560,888 people have arrived in Renk, South Sudan from Sudan since April 2023. The number of people arriving continues to increase, with about 600 people arriving daily as of September 2024. South Sudan itself is already burdened by protracted humanitarian crisis emanating from 2013 conflict, impact of Sudan conflict on economy is causing super-hyperinflation and ongoing centennial flood. The UN estimates that more than 7 million people (at least two thirds of the population) need humanitarian assistance across the country. According to multi-sectoral Humanitarian Information Needs collected by WFP and InterNews in Renk in September 2024, Concerns about sanitation, water supply, and hygiene are the most common ones brought up by the impacted community at the Renk Transit Center and other parts of the county. Nearly 60% of water sources especially boreholes in areas of SSWU operation are dysfunctional due to damage, lack of maintenance, absence of Water Management Committees). Many women have related poor management of water committee to lack of women representation. SSWU has observed that many communities are completely dependent on untreated water from the river to cope with their basic water needs. More than 50% of women currently benefitting from SSWU GBV project have reported lack or insufficient possession of WASH-NFI supplies treatment tablets, containers and other items necessary to enable access to safe water based on SPHERE standards for cooking and for personal hygiene. The overall water scarcity leads people to reduce water for hygiene purpose. Destruction of sanitation infrastructure by flood and lack of services have increased the risk of water contamination. Concerns about security, privacy and safety have been raised about the shared sanitary facilities between men and women, leading to calls for separate facilities to protect women's and girls’ safety and dignity. In a context of Cholera outbreak in the country, it is a priority to implement WaSH interventions in community institutions, with priority given to schools and mostly targeting girls and children with disability, to reduce the spread among the entire community. The identification of the WASH needs in schools will be done in collaboration with Ministry of Education, and education partners taking into account the severity scale.

2. Project activities

The intervention aims to provide access to adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation services and Appropriate GBV Response to 21,500 women, men, girls and boys including people with disabilities (PWD). Activities include:WASH Component: 1. Conduct participatory rapid needs Assessment in target locations in consultation with local authorities and other partners; 2. Distribute safe water collection and storage containers (Based on SPHERE Standard); 3. Conduct capacity training and support for Community Water Management Committee; 4. Distribute chlorine tablets and train recipients on water treatment; 5. Rehabilitate 15 gender-segregated sanitation facilities with handwashing stations for 5 schools to meet SPHERE standards (culturally sensitive WASH facilities adequate to the needs of female and male learners and teachers include children with disabilities; 6. Provide most vulnerable individuals with tools and equipment for digging construction of latrines; 7. Conduct training for hygiene promoters include on management of cholera outbreak; 8. Conduct hygiene promotion on improved hygiene practices, including water safety and support participatory community management of cholera outbreakGBV Component: 1. Conduct GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, to ensure access to safe, appropriate water, sanitation and hygiene services in secure, acceptable locations; 2. Provide WEEP resilient training for women and adolescent girls 3. Distribute menstrual hygiene kits and support resilience – production; 4. Provide in- person structured group PSS sessions for 15 women groups targeting 150 Women and 5 Girls’ Groups targeting 50 girls - 10 participants per group, 3 sessions per group); 5. Provide training for selected front-line community service providers and SSWU GBV staff to identify, refer and care for SGBV survivor; 6. Provide Integrated survivor-centered case management services to SGBV survivor including referrals to other specialized and non-specialized services; 7. Reproduce and disseminate IEC materials directly to community members and women SGBV survivors SGBV about SGBV risks and SGBV protection mechanisms; 8. Organize community sensitization campaigns and outreach for women, girls, men, boys and addressing harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality and informs communities about available services.
