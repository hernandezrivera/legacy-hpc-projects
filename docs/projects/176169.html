Project Summary

This project seeks a 12-month period starting March 2021 to provide Critical and Lifesaving Basic Services to Vulnerable Populations in hard to reach areas of Northern Bar el Gazal, Warrap and Lakes State
The project will aim at building capacities of the education system in order to ensure increased availability and accessibility of learning opportunities. The project will support learning environment, improve infrastructure and also increase WaSH activities. The project will also support education in emergencies as well as teacher training and provision of incentives. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with poor WASH conditions in schools to reduce the risk of Covid 19, and it also will complement existing long-term Protection of children while in schools
The project will provide equitable and uninterrupted access to safe, protective and inclusive learning, improving cognitive skills and coping abilities of male and female children, adolescents and school aged youths through education in emergency ECD, basic education, youth education and ALP, and ensuring girls and boys are protected through increased access to psychosocial support, life skills and referral pathways
The project seeks to solve; the many issues that prevent the educational infrastructure in South Sudan from reaching its full potential, including poverty, governmental failures, ongoing violence, poor health of its citizens, and inaccessibility to schools that are overcrowded, underfunded, and operated by unqualified teachers.

1 - Needs

The counties targeted fall under IPC 3 and above phase classification projections for the earlier part of 2020. Chronic WASH in schools needs and lack of children presence in schools continue to surface in parts of Warrap, NBeG, and Lakes state mainly affecting teachers and children majority of these multiply displaced. OCHA estimates close to 800,000 persons in the counties targeted are in need of humanitarian support due to adverse effects of conflict, displacement, food insecurity, economic hardships and lack of infrastructure. Torrential rains in the months of August – October 2020 have resulted in massive flooding thereby affecting access to basic needs and education. Remoteness and access issues into locations of Gorial,Aweil and Rumbek East have further worsened the already dire situation with majority of the schools assessed lacking essential WASH infrastructure, children and teachers reliant on unsafe water due to inadequate safe water sources/points and these makes them more vulnerable to Covid 19 and also unqualifying for school feeding programmeLess than 30% of the communities assessed reported their main source of drinking water is an improved source (borehole, tap stand & water-yard) and available in under 30 minutes, with over 70% of the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the main source of drinking water is an unimproved source (river, swamp, unprotected well), (REACH, 2019)South Sudan’s education system is characterized as a low investment, low capacity, but high demand system. The state building and peace building efforts of the national plans put high demand on the education system to expand fast, reduce inequity, and provide appropriate teaching. But public expenditure is one of the world’s lowest for education. UNESCO October report states that some 2.4 million children are out of school in 2020. The majority of them are girls. Children in rural areas and IDP, refugee and returnee children are most vulnerable. About 60 per cent of IDPs and returnees live in settlements where no more than half of the children are attending primary education, mainly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal,Gogrial and Lakes state.. The situation further worsened countrywide with the temporary closure of schools due to COVID-19, disrupting education and limiting children’s access to essential services like school feeding programmes, information on disease prevention and access to water and sanitation. COVID-19 response activities are entirely new and require special arrangements with additional resources to implement education in emergencies’ activities in order to bring back children to schools. Support to learning environment, teaching and learning, psychosocial support programming, infrastructure improvement, school WaSH activities, teacher training and provision of incentives exerts more resources than the previous response to meet the needs and protect children from the COVID-19 pandemic in schoolsAs Covid 19 increases and lockdown resumed, closure of schools further exacerbated the burden, many schools will struggle to reopen, given that most teachers have not been paid in a long time and in rural areas children have lowest access to education

8.11. Mitigation Measures

HACT will work to mitigate beneficiaries’ exposer to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services through AAP committee information sharing that seeks to reduce such associated risks including PSEA. This response includes Community Response and feedback Mechanism handled by trained staff with a clear referral pathway working in coordination with existing humanitarian actors in the project area. 90% of the project staff will be from project location who understand project area and security situation and impending desputes.HACT gives a main concern on applying and committing to Do No Harm principles through all project design, activities and results. HACT will save no efforts on following these principles and mainstreaming the protection through this project life cycle, including consulting with the stockholders, coordinating with partners and cluster, ensure safe and productive environment, providing equitable access to project services without discrimination and promoting the social cohesion. HACT will therefore apply the following principles during risk protection and application of mitigation measures. 1.Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: HACT takes great concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. But being safe is not enough if people do not have their dignity: people need to feel valued, have a sense of self-respect and feel they have some control over their lives. Prioritizing safety and dignity can also help to prevent or minimize the possible unintended negative effects that our interventions can cause for beneficiaries. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, HACT will seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Conflict and divisions within society often emerge from discrimination resulting in individuals or groups being unable to access services. Barriers to access can exist in different forms, including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: HACT will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Mechanisms for collecting feedback will use a combination of channels. Channels include active channels, where project beneficiaries perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); reactive channels, where project beneficiaries have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines); and finally, ‘open channels’ where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. 4. Participation and empowerment: HACT would strive to put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and beneficiary focused. Special attention will also be paid to ensure the participation of and consultation with, the most at risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. Humanitarian programming should also seek to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including (not exclusively) the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

When children are kept in school, it provides an enabling environment for them access other essential services like school feeding programme, Health and nutrition services as well as clean water and sanitation services. Maintaining WASH in schools has a direct correlation between poor WASH and malnutrition especially amongst under 5s children attending ECD classes. (ACF, 2020) Insufficient WASH facilities have played a big role in the rise of water-related and diarrhea diseases, which have undermined efforts to reduce levels of malnutrition High prices have made it difficult for the population to access WASH consumables such as soap, purification tablets, buckets, etc. Provision of emergency WASH services will reduce the likelihood of deaths due to water-related diseases. This response will decrease the global prevalence of acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4 with the arrayed activities.
