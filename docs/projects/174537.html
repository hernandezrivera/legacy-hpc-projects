Project Summary

Since 2018, Mopti region, especially Bankass, Koro and Bandiagara circles, are suffering a significant security deterioration. Inter-community conflict has quickly led to a complex humanitarian situation characterized by a weak humanitarian actors presence, mainly health and nutrition workers. Following this sudden deterioration, Première Urgence Internationale carried out field assessments (December 2018 and April 2019) which revealed a health coverage decrease in Bankass circle, mostly due to the insecure and economic degraded situation.
As a result, in May 2019, PUI began an intervention to restore access to health care for populations severely affected by the conflicts. Thanks to OFDA funding from September 2019, PUI was able to provide support to the populations in Bankass Circle by supporting 10 health areas (project ends in August 2020). Its current success has allowed the provision of emergency health care to vulnerable populations affected by the conflict. It also helped strengthening the failing health system so as to restore primary health care access for local populations, especially the most vulnerable (children under 5 years old, pregnant and lactating women). PUI also worked to build health workers and ASACO members capacities. At the same time, close collaboration with local health authorities helped to strengthen monitoring capacity.
Aware that the security situation is worsening day after day, but wanting to avoid an emergency assistance that could further weaken the health system, PUI has developed an exit strategy with the management staff (Circle level) from the project conception.
So as to continue the efforts already made to improve health care access of a community still suffering from the ongoing conflict, Première Urgence Internationale wishes to propose a second phase of this intervention thanks to USAID/OFDA support. This project aims at strengthening healthcare access and reviving the health system in order to ensure the permanence of health coverage in Bankass circle. This project also plans to strengthen the health system actors capacities (ASACO, health workers, community) with a focus on a progressive financing, monitoring and supervision empowerment, when basic requirements will be in place (level of competence, human and financial resources, infrastructure, procedures).
In inter-communal violence and in conflicts where civilians are targeted by regular or irregular armed forces, women and girls are very often victims of violence, including sexual harassment and rape, and sometimes even murder. Socio-cultural pressure and the weak presence of State services (security, justice) are obstacles to the use of the package of measures available for the management of cases of gender-based violence (medical care, legal assistance and psychosocial support). Indeed, health centres are often one of the first places where victims of violence go and it is therefore important to build a strong bridge between medical care and the various services provided by other actors. This will be possible thanks to the work of PUI's psychosocial worker who is based in Bankass and in charge of following up and referring GBV cases in the 10 health areas of the project. PUI will contribute to the implementation of the referencing system under the lead of the cluster/GBV coordination.

1 - Contexte

Malian crisis is still dominated by the conflict, the insecurity and the difficult climatic conditions, resulting in a violent atmosphere, becoming more acute by the day. Consequences are catastrophic. According to the Human Development Report (UNDP 2019), Mali’s Human Development Index ranks it 184e/189. Out of 20 million people in the country, (HNO 2020), 8.2 million people would be affected by the crisis and 4.3 million people would be in need (including 2.4 million children and 866,000 women); an increase of nearly 25% compared to the 3.2 million in 2019. Population remains very young, with 52% under 15 years old and 16% between 15 and 25. Households’ size is around 6 people. 2019 was mainly marked by an inter-community violence exacerbation and conflicts in the Centre (Segou and Mopti regions), which led to a significant deterioration of the security and humanitarian conditions. On March, 23, 2019, unidentified armed men attacked Ogossagou village (Bankass circle, Mopti region) killing 157 people and wounding 76. The intensity of this attack went far beyond previous incidents and led to a spiral of violence.Conflict has become increasingly complex in recent months: - Many armed groups, including radical groups throughout the region and the lack of control by Malian armed forces in some areas, involving in pre-existing conflicts. Insecurity has thus led to the creation of several self-defense groups or has prompted some people (particularly young people) to join radical groups operating in the region ;- Weapons proliferation and circulation;- Mines and improvised explosive devices;- Inter-community conflicts between Dogon and Peulh communities. These conflicts are linked to long-standing rivalries for access to local resources and leadership wars, aggravated by a weak local and regular armed forces in the circle and by radical armed groups. This situation is exacerbated by a stressing economic situation and a deterioration of the access to basic social services.- Military operations conducted by FAMA and international forces.- Road infrastructure sabotage (destruction of the only bridge, mined side roads etc...) which isolate Koro and Bankass circles. Consequences are the isolation of the populations causing fear and stress, and the economical asphyxiation of the area by slowing down trade. Persistent insecurity leads to population displacements which increased pressures and restraints on humanitarian access in a complex emergency environment. According to the population movement report, there were nearly 218,536 IDPs in Mali in February 2020. In Bankass circle, this situation has led to a decline in access to basic social services (health, education, water, hygiene and sanitation, justice, etc.), has impacted the populations nutritional status,particularly for the most vulnerable: displaced persons (5%), pregnant women and lactating women (5%), and children under 5 years old (22%), has caused a slowdown in socio-economic activities, a drop in subsistence means, and population movements. Mopti region has the highest number of IDPs, with 86,313 in February 2020 (i.e. 39% of the total IDPs number), including 18,264 in Bankass circle (where IDPs number has more than quadrupled in one year, with 4,884 IDPs in December 2018) and a dramatic increase over the last two years. Since January 2020, security deterioration in Bankass circle has worsened, and many villages suffer from recurrent attacks. . A new attack on Ogossagou in February 2020 has resulted in at least 31 new victims The number of village burned in 2019 is 75, with 320 dead, 123 wounded and 8 missing. These multiple attacks have led to significant populations’ displacement, leaving behind all their possessions. According to the HNO 2020, Bankass circle is classified in maximum severity (5 on a scale of 1 to 5) for protection incidents. Indeed, in recent weeks, there have been several population displacements in the southern part of circle due to attacks by unidentified armed groups who frequently, besides taking away livestock, set fire to food reserves. Several households with a high women proportion, children and elderly have migrated to Bankass circle chief town. These displacements accentuate person’s vulnerabilities (psychological, health and nutritional standpoints). Difficulties in governance and State limited capacity to provide basic social services,ensure security and tackle organized crime, exacerbate marginalization of vulnerable population and injustice feeling, a breeding ground for the spread of increasingly widespread radical groups.
