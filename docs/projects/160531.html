Project Summary

With the conflict in eastern Ukraine entering its sixth year, an estimated 3.5 million people are in need of humanitarian aid. Cyclical vulnerability to winter depletes household resources and negatively impacts agricultural activities, wherein households are often unable to adequately access necessary heating materials. From previous cash projects, ACTED has recorded compounding vulnerabilities: food consumption decreases during winter as negative coping strategies are used more often due to the diversion of household funds to cover costs of heating. With few economic opportunities available to vulnerable households with people with disabilities, elderly and/or homebound members, financial means are especially limited leading up to and during winter months, resulting in systemic inability to adequately prepare for the harsh winter. Increasing heating costs have not reflected the current income gap and inability to meet minimum subsistence requirements for most of the targeted beneficiaries, wherein the average income is significantly low at UAH1305 and falls to UAH684 for HHs with unemployed pre-pensioners (representing 40% and 21% respectively, of minimum actual subsistence level (2018 average: UAH3263 [Ministry of Social Policy]). Additional vulnerabilities include protection-related risks, which increase closest to the LoC, where notably 89% of households within 5km of the LoC reported shelling as being a main concern and 28% of HHs perceived the presence of mines and unexploded ordinances (UXOs) in their communities, impacting daily life and shelter needs.
As per the Shelter Cluster's 2019-20 Winterization Guidance, heating remains a top priority for assistance to areas within 5km of the LoC. The project accordingly aims to meet the basic winterization needs of vulnerable households in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in government-controlled areas (GCA) of eastern Ukraine, primarily households with people with disabilities and elderly people. Through direct cash or in-kind assistance, valued at 8,000 UAH/household (covering 50% of seasonal needs as per Shelter Cluster Winterization Guidelines), beneficiaries in isolated settlements along the Line of Contact (LoC) will receive or be able to purchase necessary heating fuel for essential survival during the harsh winter months. The proposed intervention leverages ACTED's experience providing cash assistance (cash for food security, cash for winterization, multi-purpose cash) in all raions/cities bordering the LoC since 2018 and ACTED's expertise as an active cash programming partner (incl. as co-lead of Ukraine's Cash Working Group). For in-kind donations, ACTED will leverage its global expertise and the experience of its partners in Ukraine – including under the ECHO-funded ACCESS Consortium. The intervention will ensure that settlements chosen for cash distribution include those with functional market access to fuel (or those connected to gas heating lines) and stable prices in order for assistance received to be most impactful – where those conditions are not met, in-kind assistance will be provided.

1 - Needs

The average income/person/month in the 5km area is UAH2000 (REACH 2018), or 61% of the actual subsistence level (2018 average: UAH3263). Many households in the 5km area receive social payments, including pensions, from the government but this is insufficient to cover basic needs: in 2018, the minimum pension payment was UAH1345 (41% of subsistence level). Average income for vulnerable groups is significantly lower at UAH1425 (39% of actual subsistence level as of August 2019). ACTED program data thus suggests large income gaps for women-headed HHs - 49%; women headed HHs with children - 69%; HHs of elderly - 38%; and single elderly HHs - 40%, of whom 90% are women. For households with unemployed pre-pensioners, average income is UAH783, or 21% of subsistence level. REACH 2019 data suggests that employment rises during winter, especially in the 5km area, where 5% of households report a gap in employment most often due to road damage because of bad weather (figure is indicative given the small sample of respondents).HHs with distinct vulnerabilities often face particular winterization concerns, where the increase of heating costs impact HHs’ ability to access adequate sources of heat throughout the winter. Wood and gas remain a primary source of fuel for heating in rural areas, (46% in Luhansk; REACH, Trends Analysis 2018). Heating costs for many HHs in eastern Ukraine have increased significantly since the beginning of the conflict (doubling in 2016 alone and costs of individual heating materials continue to rise) in areas connected to central heating grid. Regular ACTED-led market monitoring has shown stable upwards price trends in surveyed GCA locations. Average price for firewood in August 2017 was UAH440: as of August 2019, it increased significantly to UAH798. The price per ton of coal also increased from UAH2400 in August 2017 to UAH3838 in August 2019. In more rural areas which do not have ready access to main/central heating and must rely on traditional methods, such as coal and firewood, HHs face continued challenges to afford sufficient heating. Thus, 68% of households surveyed in the 0-20km area (GCA) by REACH in 2019 reported cost as the most frequent problem in accessing fuel, vs 9% reporting availability. A significant 39% and 37% of surveyed households also reported dissatisfaction with the quality of coal and wood, respectively, that are accessible on the market. This data justifies a mixed-modality approach to delivering winterization assistance.Winterization needs vary across gender and age groups – stemming from different socio-economic vulnerability profiles. A previous ACTED winterization cash program targeted – on the basis of vulnerability – 65% women and 33% of elderly: among those aged 60 and above, 77% were women and 33% men. Women are indeed more likely to have winterization needs – within 5km for instance, a greater proportion of women headed HHs in Luhansk spent multi-purpose cash assistance on shelter than men headed HHs (52% vs 38%; ACCESS 2018). Notably, single elderly women have the lowest ability to undertake shelter renovation (ACCESS 2018). For winter 2018, seasonal heating costs estimations represented 73%, 30% and 84% of the, average annual income of women headed HHs with children (ACTED program data).Post-distribution monitoring data from ACTED’s 2018-19 winterization project confirms that needs are high, as 61% and 37% of beneficiary households spent the cash assistance on fuel and heating, respectively. On average, HHs spent 52% of their assistance on fuel and 24% on heating (health was the second sector in which beneficiaries spent the assistance, noting also that a REACH 2019 assessment found that a relatively high rate of households reduce health expenditure as a negative coping strategy).

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome:Critical winterization needs of 800 most vulnerable households in GCA are met Distribution of cash and in-kind winterization assistance:As a preferred modality by a majority of beneficiaries surveyed by REACH within 5km of the LoC, cash is appropriate to meet household needs in the targeted settlements where heating fuel is available and markets functional. As some beneficiaries have expressed concerns about the quality and appropriateness of in-kind fuel provided by NGOs, ACTED remains committed to offering cash as a modality for enabling access to fuel. However, for beneficiaries for whom cash is not an appropriate modality – because of particular vulnerabilities e.g. limited mobility or lack of access to quality fuel on their market – or who simply prefer in-kind, ACTED will provide solid fuel (coal).Following Shelter Cluster recommendations, ACTED will select beneficiaries based on cumulative vulnerability and consider primarily HH housing and heating conditions. Based on the vulnerability criteria identified in the needs assessment and past programming, ACTED expects to target 65% women, 33% elderly, 15% persons with disabilities, and 5% IDPs overall, noting that the percentage of elderly and people with disabilities may be higher among solid fuel beneficiaries.ACTED will provide assistance eq. to 8,000 UAH/household (eq. 2 tons of coal), a slight increase from the 7,800 UAH that ACTED distributed under its 2018 winterization program (noting inflation and rising costs). This amount covers 50% of seasonal needs as per the 2019-20 Shelter Cluster Winterization Guidelines – PDM data from past projects suggests that this is sufficient to meet critical winterization needs.Based on data from REACH and partners, as well as past ACTED winterization vulnerability assessments and PDMs, ACTED expects to distribute cash to 50% of beneficiaries – i.e. 400 HHs/800 individuals – and solid fuel (coal) to the other 50%.ACTED distributes cash through Financial Service Providers (FSPs) who cover all locations within 5km of the LoC and offer a home delivery modality – thereby mitigating access/protection concerns for people with limited mobility, disabilities, or significant care responsibilities who prefer to receive cash. Beneficiaries will be able to select their Financial Service Provider during assessment, noting that they already know the two FSPs that ACTED works with as they also distribute pensions, and are thus aware of transportation, accessibility, and security concerns prior to disbursement.ACTED will consult at least 80% of communities targeted. The project and selection criteria will be presented in community meetings to local authorities, community representatives and volunteers to gather and incorporate input and feedback. ACTED's independent Appraisal, Monitoring, and Evaluation team will conduct PDM to measure satisfaction with the selection and distribution processes, modalities, and the amount of assistance received – looking at key indicators, beneficiary vulnerabilities, and household spending habits. PDM activities ensure that ACTED remains accountable to affected populations and provides effective and timely life-saving humanitarian assistance.ACTED has established appropriate CRM across all projects to enable beneficiaries to report any abuse by staff and others. All staff are trained on and all beneficiaries made aware of ACTED’s Code of Conduct which includes the CRM (through contact cards, information relayed at community meetings and on visibility materials such as posters). The CRM is available to people with disabilities through SMS, Viber and email. ACTED also facilitates exchanges through social workers and community leaders. All information gathered through these monitoring mechanisms will be analyzed, followed up on and relayed to project managers and staff for further action as required.
