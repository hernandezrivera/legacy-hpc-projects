Project Summary

South Sudan is one of the most severe protection crises in the world, with significant gross human rights violations. Ongoing conflict, economic fragility, and conflict-related shocks have exacerbated risks of violence, coercion, and deliberate deprivation, increasing the vulnerability of at-risk or marginalized people to exploitation, discrimination, abuse, and other threats to safety, wellbeing, and dignity. Intimate and large-scale violence are endemic, intertwined, and compounded by protracted crises that have undermined the rule of law, disrupted protective community structures and livelihoods, and reinforced inequitable and harmful social norms. Displacement trends and conflict dynamics are mutually reinforcing in South Sudan, with seasonal flooding along the Nile and mass displacement stemming from the Sudan Crisis contributing to competition for resources and housing, land, and property (HLP) disputes.
Evidence-based protection interventions and co-coordination of the will be implemented to support individuals, communities, institutions, and systems to address protection risks and vulnerabilities. Through mobile and fixed modalities, general protection interventions will encompass climate-sensitive and community-led proactive (preventive) protection strategies, delivery of comprehensive protection services including individual protection assistance (IPA-Cash) and protection case management, and the consolidation of skills and systems that strengthen the protective environment and increase resilience.
Under the HLP Area of Responsibility, HLP risks have increased for the most vulnerable populations, which include internally displaced persons, returnees, refugees, and host communities. Factors contributing to higher risks of HLP issues include conflict, influx of returnees and refugees from Ethiopia, Sudan and Uganda, secondary occupation, land grabbing, forced evictions, lack of access to legal Identity documentation for reclaiming housing land and property, theft, destruction of personal property and illegal occupation. This project seeks to address critical housing land and property needs through emergency response activities. This will be through community-based structures which include establishing and strengthening already existing collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms, through Information counselling and legal assistance and advocacy. This will help displaced persons claim and exercise their HLP rights. This project will address housing land and property risks by providing humanitarian assistance to women, men, boys and girls based on needs. Profiling will be done to identify the most vulnerable and this identification will be through age, gender and diversity which promotes inclusion of all groups such as persons with disabilities, the elderly, women and youth. The activities will seek to inform the community and the beneficiaries on their HLP rights, educate them on the security of tenure guidelines in the and the laws and legal frameworks in place in exercising HLP rights. It will enhance collaboration and multisectoral responses with the other AoRs, Shelter, CCCM and FSL clusters. Community interventions where local leaders and the community will be involved in identification and land tenure security as most of the land in hard-to-reach areas where NRC intervention are is community land.

1. Needs

South Sudan is among the most severe protection crises in the world, with an estimated 5.34 million people in need of protection. Political instability, inter-communal conflict, economic fragility, and climate-related shocks exacerbate pre-existing risks to the safety, wellbeing, and dignity of 1.37 million internally-displaced people, 810,000 refugees and asylum seekers, and an 840,000 returnees, as well as host community members. Intimate and large-scale violence are endemic, intertwined, and compounded by protracted crises that undermine rule of law, disrupt protective community structures and livelihoods, and reinforce inequitable and harmful social norms. A recent United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) report identified some 1,062 victims of inter-communal and political violence in the second quarter of 2024, a 43% increase from the same period the previous year. Furthermore, the ongoing Sudan Crisis has trigged large-scale displacement to South Sudan, with a further 345,000 new arrivals anticipated throughout 2025.Housing Land and Property issues are both a consequence and cause of conflict in South Sudan, climate change shocks and intercommunal conflicts have exacerbated HLP risks in several parts of the country that require intervention. We seek to address critical housing land and property needs through emergency response activities. The establishment of community-based structures, capacity building of local authorities on HLP rights and the legal frameworks on HLP as well as that helps in establishing and strengthening alternative dispute resolution mechanisms and HLP interventions through Information counselling and legal assistance and advocacy will enable the community including displaced persons claim and exercise their HLP rights. The Protection risk of theft, forced eviction and destruction of property as well as other risks such as secondary occupation, land grabbing, lack of legal identity documents and loss of ownership documents are the major HLP issues faced by the displaced persons, returnees, host community and refugees. In instances where climate change impacts such as floods and drought the need for communities to evacuate from their land to higher grounds the involvement of local authorities and community leaders is of great importance and beneficial to all the groups involved. Another pressing HLP issue is the delay in adoption of the land policy and creation of mechanisms of implementation is a gap and creates an obstacle for the population who want to return to the country or areas of origin. Lack of widespread knowledge of HLP laws and legal ownership with the population as well as limited access for women land rights despite legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, the protection of those rights remains limited in South Sudan. HLP issues remain a priority need to be addressed and HLP interventions necessary for the realization of HLP rights in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

Under Protection, NRC will deliver fixed and mobile services to women, men, girls, and boys with protection concerns and/or specific needs. This will involve rolling out civilian self-protection programming (community-based protection activities), with integrated humanitarian mediation components, to support 1,040 people in 20 communities to develop and implement civilian self-protection strategies.Individual response services will comprise individual protection assistance (cash) and protection case management services to support 5,161 people with non-cash assistance and 2,170 people with cash assistance. Furthermore, 21,050 people subject to specific and acute protection risks, or who have experienced a protection violation, will be provided with informationThought leadership and influence programming will scale up systematic protection monitoring activities in project locations to better understand their protection needs and enhance evidence-based and data-driven protection responses information dissemination and provide 21,050 people with information, education, and awareness messages to strengthen community-level understanding of, and ability to mitigate, protection risks and access response services, with self-, assisted, and facilitated referral available to support access to services. Awareness messaging will always be delivered as an augment to specialised service delivery to ensure quality and relevance of assistance. Housing Land and Property assistance will have integrated approach that will develop key messaging on rights of all population. Advocacy with the government officials and local authorities on the rights to land and housing by displaced population and host communities, Capacity building and training of officials working in key offices on HLP. Technical support to the various units on rights of IDPs, refugees and returnees and the humanitarian interventions related to HLP. This project will facilitate solutions to address challenges on securing HLP rights with provisions for IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities to be issued with legal identity documents to aid them in attaining their ownership documents. Awareness raising and outreach to the community through public gatherings and radio shows. Legal counselling to build knowledge on HLP issues on issues such as secondary occupation, land grabbing, loss of documents and forced eviction. Encouraging alternative dispute resolution mechanisms and capacitate the community leaders on how to handle HLP issues and encourage peaceful coexistence.
