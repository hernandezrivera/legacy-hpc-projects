Project Summary

Barely out of a wetter than normal Deyr rainy season, communities in south and central Somalia are grappling with a new wave of flooding during the Hagaa season in low lying areas along the Juba and Shabelle rivers. The impact of the ongoing flooding compounded by economic and social impacts of COVID, escalated conflict, and forecast of below-average Deyr rainfall from October to December 2020 may result in further deterioration of .humanitarian outcomes for affected communities through 2021. More cases of AWD are being reported in children under five years old, who are particularly vulnerable to disease, especially if their immune systems are compromised by malnutrition. While response is already underway, the WASH Cluster has reported critical gaps in Balcad, Marka and Wanlaweyn where no WASH assistance has been received despite high risk levels for acute watery diarrhoea (AWD).
Primary health care visits and vaccination coverage have sharply declined in Somalia amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, raising concerns that vaccine preventable diseases such as respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) as well as malnutrition could be going untreated, all while floods have forced tens of thousands of people from their homes. While the healthcare system’s resources are already limited and overstretched, this development could easily degenerate into a public health crisis without sustained outreach to those at risk.
RRP through this interventions in the stated localities will work towards strengthening and improving access to essential lifesaving healthcare aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality, mental health well-being services, controlling and eliminating the spread of COVID-19 among other emergency preparedness and response capacity at all levels in order to mitigate and respond to communicable disease outbreaks in an efficient, coordinated, and timely manner for crisis affected and host populations in Beletweyne, bulaburte and jalalaqsi districts of Hiran region Somalia. As a way of working towards provision of integrated life-saving primary health care services aimed at reducing the burden of morbidity and mortality as well as increasing the mental-health wellbeing of the locals, we will improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the residence within the target population to the nearest proximity, as for COVID-19, RRP will work towards provision of mask, hand sanitizers, handwashing stands in public areas while at the same time we will offer the COVID-19 tests to locals and a bi-weekly community intervention gatherings to educate the locals about the risks, preventive measures and ways the disease spreads.
RRP will work towards improving the vaccination coverage and measles immunization, pentavalent vaccination among other vaccine preventable diseases. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC services will be provided to the pregnant mothers. Staff and the community will be equipped with adequate information to manage emergencies and outbreaks that may arise from the dynamic natural and conflict related concerns. EDEWS reports shall be shared with the MOH and the cluster including proper coordination so as to respond to emergencies like AWD/cholera, measles, and some haemorrhagic fever outbreaks. RRP staff will also be equipped with trainings on ANC and BEmONC, IMCI, Emergency response preparedness and CHWs equipped with skills on community management and targeting. Community dialogue will be key in adherence with the AAP framework. RRP is currently having an integrated project in Jalalaqsi for both health nutrition and WASH, to be able to have continuity of the intervention, we will ensure the response plan captures the needs already identified in the area during the implementation.

Mitigation Measures

RRP will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that project stakeholders understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. We will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups. Gender disparities are visible in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. The project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. Gender mainstreaming has been considered in the design of this project at all levels. All targeted beneficiaries will be registered, profiled and data disaggregated by gender and age as for the following categories: 1. Women and pregnant women 2. Children under 5 years 3. Population under 18 years 4. IDPs resident in IDP camps 5. Access of men to transmissible diseases services. During implementation and monitoring of the services a participatory and gender sensitive approach will be adopted to allow equal opportunity to all. The hygiene and health promotion interventions will involve hospital staff, both health workers and support staff, patients, relatives and visitors with specific sessions addressed to mothers of patients in pediatric ward and women in obstetric department. Gender specific group discussions and interaction are organized. Hospital staff recruitment will respect gender equality ensuring opportunities to all genders on the base of the professional qualification.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed project centres on increasing communities’ resilience to short-term and chronic emergencies by strengthening health systems and improving the overall health of the beneficiaries. The program will address the immediate need to improve health care outcomes while working towards the longer-term goal of sustainably integrating mental health well-being into the health system framework, thereby attracting government strategic and financial commitments that will enable continuous provision of quality health services overall. The project will implement activities alongside or directly through MOH staff to ensure that the capacity and responsibility for the provision of both physical and mental health care services remains within government facilities. Targeted trainings and supportive follow-up will strengthen local partners’ (e.g. CHVs, health workers, community leaders) capacity to deliver quality services in the long-term. The project will specifically invest in CHVs as these individuals will remain in their communities to further health care and link communities to services. RRP will work towards linking the health systems with the MOH as well as coordinating approaches and/or leveraging ongoing programs and complementary to counter the impact of limited presence of humanitarian actors in these zones. Piloting community centred approaches along with NGO, donors and community presence in these inaccessible or hard to reach areas in order to enable protection programming at the community level and strengthening of communities’ self-protection mechanisms and resilience. Ensuring clearance operations, information sharing and risk awareness around explosive hazards; and ensuring more robust information sharing and evidence based advocacy on protection of civilians. Medical ethics on patients’ confidentiality will as well be adhered in the project implementation.
