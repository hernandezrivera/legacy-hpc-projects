Project Summary

OCHA plays a critical role in the leadership and coordination of humanitarian action in South Sudan based on a people-centered approach. OCHA brings together different stakeholders to facilitate coordination and analyze the evolving needs and adjust response operations where needed. This includes assessing and documenting humanitarian needs, negotiating humanitarian access, and compiling and synthesizing qualitative and quantitative data to produce a wide range of information products.

1 - Needs

In 2021, South Sudan continues to face increased humanitarian challenges as a result of compounding shocks including intensified conflict and sub-national violence; two years (2019-2020) of consecutive flooding; and the impacts of COVID-19. Overall food security worsened with some communities facing catastrophic needs. More children were acutely malnourished than in the past three years. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brink, especially in urban areas. Access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, was already limited and much of the service infrastructure was damaged, destroyed or closed in 2020. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. An estimated 8.3 million women, men, girls and boys need humanitarian assistance and protection services in 2021 according to the need analysis captured in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)

2 - Project activities

OCHA contributes to principled and effective humanitarian response through coordination, advocacy, policy, information management and humanitarian financing tools and services with partners. In South Sudan, these activities include:• Coordination of the various stages of the Humanitarian Programme Cycle including front-line coordination activities as close to affected population as possible, facilitating the development of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP); Coordinate and as relevant, management of humanitarian financing instruments such as the CERF and SSHF; facilitate humanitarian policy work for Accountability to Affected Populations, gender, age and disability, PSEA, cash modality, humanitarian-development-peace nexus, protection and co-chair the National Advisory Group on Solutions. • Provision of secretariat and other necessary support including chairing as relevant the Humanitarian Coordination Forum meetings, Humanitarian Country Team, Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, HCT-UNMISS Task Team, Information Management Working Group, Access Working Group, Needs Analysis Working Group and the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund Advisory Board, informed by timely, relevant, factual information to guide advocacy positions. • Support and build consensus guided by IASC standards to develop policy approaches to best serve people in need.• Provide Information management services to the humanitarian community to inform timely, effective, needs-based principled responses. • Leverage timely information of people’s needs and gaps, mobilize and engage the spectrum of financing instruments, mechanisms, including the country based pooled fund, and as relevant, the CERF, to ensure that humanitarian needs are met, humanitarian leadership and coordination mechanisms are promoted, and are complementary among themselves, coherent with development funding when possible.• Support humanitarian hubs and operational centres to facilitate access to hard-to-reach locations bring humanitarian assistance closer to the people and enhance inter-sector and inter-agency coordination.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

OCHA will continue to disseminate information to its staff members on the zero tolerance for SEA and sensitize them regularly about the need to prevent any SEA. This includes facilitating all staff completion of mandatory online training on PSEA and Sexual Harassment; facilitate that all new and current staff in the field and Juba sign the SG’s Bulletin; Conduct regular briefings and internal dialogue with all staff on PSEA to ensure ongoing awareness.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

OCHA will liaise with clusters to gather feedback channeled through Community-Based Complaints Mechanisms to inform the inter-sectoral needs analysis for different demographic population groups which will support designing of response strategies tailored to the needs of these population groups. OCHA will leverage its role to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members of the affected community informs advocacy messaging.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

OCHA works with the inter-agency Communication and Community Engagement Working Group to strengthen two-way communication with affected people across the response system. For example, OCHA staff interact directly with crisis affected people to hear from them the challenges they face and to understand their needs. OCHA plays a key role to promote accountability to affected people approaches are integrated into humanitarian activities, mainstreamed through humanitarian planning and programming, and reflected in humanitarian funding, specifically through the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund. This includes ensuring that the HCT engages in timely, strategic discussions on PSEA and CCE; undertaking missions to hot-spot areas and supporting the Humanitarian Coordinator’s advocacy efforts. OCHA promotes that that communication with the affected community with appropriate feedback mechanisms and referral pathways are in place, and leveraged to inform all humanitarian activities with timely and accurate information, including through timely needs assessment with data collected directly from the affected population. OCHA supports the HCT to meet its commitment to ensure that affected people are at the centre of any humanitarian response, and that collective mechanisms are in place to ensure that they are able to provide feedback on their own priorities and concerns around humanitarian action, and that these priorities and concerns are considered and addressed in humanitarian action in a meaningful way, as outlined in the HCT Compact.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

OCHA will leverage all feasible opportunities to remind affected populations of their right in terms of the zero tolerance for SEA. To the extent feasible, OCHA support the PSEA Task Force Secretariat to carry out Risk Mitigation field assessments to contribute inputs for an overall Risk Mitigation Plan; and support any Community-Based Complaints Mechanisms for community feedback on any challenges they identify
