Project Summary

The ongoing conflict, natural disasters, and outbreaks in Syria have disrupted the health system. The devastating 2023 earthquake also highlighted the critical need for increased investments in community resilience & RCCE, as well as better collaboration between stakeholders to improve emergency preparedness & response.
In Syria, EWARS requires ongoing support to sustain disease detection, investigation & response to suspected cases of epidemic prone diseases (EPDs). Control of VPDs, including polio eradication activities, is essential considering surging disease transmission risks (displacements, malnutrition, disrupted water networks, etc). Sustaining well-functioning public health laboratory services is essential for timely confirmation of aetiology during investigations. Finally, transforming paper-based HIS to e-surveillance utilizing DHIS2 will strengthen the health emergency response. WHO SYRIA will:
-Strengthen EWARS stewardship & implement 2023 evaluation recommendations
-Build capacity of EWARS & laboratory staff
-Enhance data analysis, utilization & preparation of EWARS information products
-Provide operational support to EWARS & RRTs
-Digitalize disease surveillance & integrate with other HIS systems into DHIS2
-Support active surveillance for VPDs, polio eradication activities & National Polio Laboratory
-Strengthen linkages between CPHL & EWARS
-Ensure availability of critical materials, commodities & supplies for EWARS.
-Maintain contingency stocks of essential medicines & supplies to prevent/respond to outbreaks of NTDs & endemic diseases
In NWS, the ability to prevent, prepare for, and respond to health security emergencies remains critical to keep communities safe. WHO will work with partners to systematically strengthen capacity & multi-stakeholder collaboration for a more coordinated preparedness & response to emergencies by focusing on:
-Strengthening EWARN & its 3 labs to rapidly detect an outbreak event
-Enhance data analysis, utilization & preparation of EWARN information products.
-Comprehensive evaluation of the EWARN system.
-Coordinating:
a. The surveillance, outbreak preparedness and response TWG
b. Initial response activities to disease outbreaks/EPDs clusters and other emergencies with health consequences
- Work with partners to develop a multi-disease operational plan for NWS
-Launch & implementation of MR, OPV & OCV campaigns guided by epi trends & analysis, in coordination with SIG.
-Enhancing trauma care package, encompassing prehospital care (first aid and life-saving referrals), emergency room care, trauma surgery capacity, intensive care & rehabilitation.
-Improving the referral system for emergency & non-emergency care
-Reestablishing the Trauma & Disability WG under the Health Cluster umbrella to coordinate & share info across partners
-Enhance community resilience and community understanding of health risks and promote positive health behaviors by improving awareness and information, especially during disease outbreaks or other emergencies, while enhancing community engagement and outreach through participation with affected populations
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
