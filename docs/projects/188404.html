Project Summary

During this project, TGH will contribute to provide appropriate assistance in areas with high needs for shelter support and NFI support to protect the most vulnerable people from harsh winter and to support return movements. In line with the Shelter Sector’s strategic objectives, TGH will contribute to reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing through addressing the urgent shelter needs in Idleb governorate and Rural Damascus.
This intervention will focus on the rehabilitation of damaged houses and seasonal winter kits for the conflict-affected newly returnees in the Khan Shaykun and Hajar Aswad, who are facing cumulative challenges, including the large destruction level of housing that lack the main protection elements from harsh weather conditions. HNO 2022 in Syria states that 5,92 million people are still in need of shelter support (estimated for 2023 : 6,09 million); the estimated number of people requiring NFI support 4.91 million in 2022 and this number is expected to continue to increase to 5,05 million in 2023. In Idleb governorate, the newly accessible areas are witnessing one of the largest percentages of displacement and return cases in the country. Idleb was identified as in severe need for shelter support according to HNO 2022.
The intervention will foster synergies with TGH Education and WASH activities that are currently taking place in Khan Shaykun and planed WASH intervention by TGH in Hajar Aswad. Adding a Shelter component by rehabilitating damaged houses in targeted areas, will further foster early recovery.
In June 2021, TGH conducted multi-sectoral assessments in Khan Shaykun and Hajar Aswad, which were complemented by a shelter pre-assessment done in October 2021 among 50 sampled households. This assessment was done in coordination with the local authorities (municipality and governorate representative) and enabled TGH field team to design detailed sketches and BoQs for each HH.
Through this project, TGH will address the immediate shelter needs of the vulnerable returnee families. A total of 350 partially damaged houses will be rehabilitated during this project in 2022, which will benefit about 1750 individuals (150 HHs in Khan Shaykun and 200 HHs Hajar Aswad), and 200 houses will be targeted in 2023, which will benefit about 1000 individuals. This will ensure safety, privacy and dignity standards in the rehabilitated houses and TGH will supply and install HH level solar electricity system to meet basic HH level energy needs. In 2022, TGH will distribute 350 Winter kits (Winter Jackets, Fleece Blanket) in 2022 and 200 winter kits in 2023, targeting elderly or female headed households and disabled persons. TGH always adjusts its distribution modalities (through door-to-door visits) to ensure that the most vulnerable effectively benefit from the intervention.
The project therefore aims at strengthening the resilience and cohesion of vulnerable communities by improving housing. Repair and rehabilitation of damaged houses will improve safe and decent living conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected populations (returnees’ families) selected based on vulnerability criteria (women-headed HHs, families with children under 5, elderly, with a member chronically ills and/or with physical/mental disability). This will be done in close coordination with the local authorities and MoLAE, with whom a previous MoU had already been signed by TGH in June 2020. This is expected to facilitate the approval process for this project. The primary beneficiary list with 150 HH has been acquired by TGH and the approval process was already started through a meeting with Idleb governor, SARC Idleb Head of branch in order to secure the needed approvals in timely manner.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, 5.92 million people are in need of shelter support and 4.91 million are in need of NFI, which are expected to increase in 2023. In October 2021, in coordination with local authorities, TGH conducted pre-assessments in the targeted location of Khan Shaykun and Hajar Aswad with high severity score and very vulnerable people, which complemented multi-sectoral assessments done in June 2021. These were conducted among a sample of 50 HHs taken from a list shared by local authorities, as per the official process, which focuses on the individuals the most in need for shelter, taking vulnerability criteria into account (e.g. family status, residence status).The main findings of the pre-assessments indicate that 30% of buildings are completely destroyed and the remaining 50% are partially damaged. Most homes have suffered from looting of doors and windows & damage to electrical and sanitary systems, which confirm the significant housing rehabilitation needs in this area, especially to cope with the winter. Acute winterization needs remain high for returnee families living in damaged houses, requiring large scale timely response to address their immediate needs through distribution of winter kits.In addition to other deficiencies already identified in the area: access to WASH services, health facilities, and access to education are remains limited in the areas. At the time of the assessment, the number of returnees was increasing according to local authorities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Due to the sensitivity of shelter project in this context, TGH has been working with the Shelter sector in order to collect all useful information regarding the approval process for such project (from December 2019). TGH pre-assessments included door-to-door visits to conduct individual needs assessments at HH level, engaging the beneficiaries. The findings were key to develop adjusted sketches and BoQs for each assessed HH. Targeted households were directly involved in the decision-making related to the project design namely through the selection of the rooms to be rehabilitated.TGH will also use the time required for this approval process to continue & finalize the technical studies of the targeted households in order to finalize the BoQs for each HH. Once this information is gathered, the implementing contractor’s selection process will begin. In the scenario where the MoLAE provides approvals progressively for some individuals, TGH will gradually start the tender process and rehabilitation works for the cleared HH. For each rehabilitated lot, a dedicated engineer staff will be responsible to monitor & follow up the works on a daily basis. Continuous monitoring will be done as well with the beneficiaries themselves through TGH complaint & feedback mechanisms.TGH plans to distribute Winter kits to the HHs whom houses will be rehabilitated in Khan Shaykun & Hajar Aswad. TGH will conduct door-to-door distributions, ensuring that each family signs a handover certificate.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights including civil documentation. TGH will have strong coordination and good relationship with Municipality, MOLAE and SARC to get approval on time, work together with MoLAE to ensure that alternative documents to ownership are respected by all governorates, Share the list of alternative documents to prove ownership with all partners, Work together with the local authorities, partners and communities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level, and work together with protection partners and referral for legal assistance.- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members: TGH will reduce this risk by ensuring a transparent process and collaboration with the beneficiaries and the authorities and by developing clear selection criteria (female/elderly headed HH, PwDs and CwDs, number of HH members, etc.) as agreed among sector members and with the MoLAE.- Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion): All staffs are aware and trained on TGH PSEA policy and code of conduct- Risk of physical harm at distribution points: Distributions will be made house-to-house. By doing so, the risk of harm within the distribution process is reduced to beneficiaries.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigation: all TGH staff must sign a Code of Conduct; provision of training on PSEA to all TGH staff; set up of TGH PSEA Policy; implementation of a PSEA hotline managed by qualified staff and communication about this hotline on the areas of intervention. In addition to the standard complaint mechanism, one focal point is designated in each operational base to collect the potential PSEA complaints and a dedicated hotline was set up to receive reports. The different channels appear in all visuals that are put in TGH office and areas of intervention (i.e. distribution sites, etc.). All PSEA focal points receive specific trainings on how to deal with SEA complaint and their roles and responsibilities are defined in Terms of References.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A-Security tensions impacting TGH and its activities: Acceptance approach, TGH presence in the area of intervention since 2020 maintaining the good relationship with local community, Security and safety protocols in place and regularly updated.B-Challenges in beneficiary list screening and approval by local authorities, long duration to obtain MOLAE approvals on the selected beneficiaries: Strong coordination and good relationship with Municipality, MOLAE and SARC to get approval on time. C-Fraud and corruption attempts: All staff are aware and trained on TGH anti-fraud and corruption policy, Systematic inclusion in contracts of all types a clause referring to the TGH's anti-fraud and corruption policy, Complaint and alert mechanisms are in place and known by staff and beneficiaries, Adoption of systematic cross-checking mechanisms, including local focal points and close daily monitoring is ensured by. D-Sudden changes in SYP exchange rate, inflation and economic crisis does not allow the organization to implement all activities with the allocated budget: Price monitoring and co-financing with other projects from other donors if possible, Update budget forecast on regular basis, Inflation monitoring based on market surveyE-Cases of COVID-19 continue to multiply: Continued adaptation of all activities in response to Covid19, Adherence to COVID-19 preventive measures and SOP.
