Project Summary

Conflict and displacement continue to affect North East Syria (NES), while needs are heightened due to the COVID-19 outbreak, impacting healthcare service provision, especially in underserved areas and IDPs/refugees camps in Hassakeh, Deir-ez Zor, Raqqa and Aleppo Gov. Through this Action, in continuation with previous phases (Jun.’17-Apr.'22), the NGO and health and protection local IPs , aim to ameliorate living conditions and save lives of war-affected persons in NES by improving access to emergency&primaryhealth care, life-saving obstetric and reproductive health (RH) services, COVID-19 preparedness&response, and to protection and prevention services. Continued support will be provided to PHCCs in Raqqa city and Areesha, Al Hol, Abu Khashab, Mahmoudli camps, in addition to new support to Serekanye camp PHCC; to essential RH services at secondary care level in Maternity Deps. in Tabqa and Manbij and in Raqqa Maternity and Pediatric Hospital, and to the COVID-19 Treatment Facility in Manbij. Continued support will also beensured to the Emergency Management and Coordination Center (EMCC) for both emergency and COVID-19 referrals. The protection intervention (described and budgeted in module entry HSYR22-HEA-188482-1) will ensure continuity of critical CP and GBV life-saving services for women, girls (WGs), and boys, as well as caregivers, in Safe spaces in Raqqa. Self-protection capacities of pop. will be strengthened through protection mainstreaming, prevention outreach/campaigns, and capacity building of local actors. Through a flexible structure and a wide geographic coverage, the Action provides the capacity to quickly assess and respond to emerging health and protection needs indifferent locations in NES.

3 - Needs

Prot. concerns remain for W, C and vulnerable groups such as PwD reported in all locations. Protection issues disproportionately affect vulnerable groups such as women, children,adolescent girls and boys, older persons, PwD. Child labour and early marriage are the most common copingstrategies in NES: 51% of res. and 62% of IDPs sent their children to work, and 20% of res. and 24% of IDPsmarried their children in early age as a coping strategy. 45% of the assessed communities identified child protectionas a priority need (REACH HSOS – Sept. 2021). NES GBV TF data shows intimate partner violence, family violence,denial of resources and early marriage (GBV HNO, Oct.’21). In Raqqa Gov: Based on UPP assmt., WGs continued to be the most at risk and GBV/early marriage was reported as a mainprotection concern. Child marriage was reported in 83,5% of the assessed communities in Raqqa Gov. KIs reportedthat in 99.4% of the assessed communities child labour occurred in the previous year, preventing schoolattendance (HNO MSNA, Aug -Sept. ’21).*Serekaniye camp (11,889 ind. 4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims at providing safe and equal access to life-saving health assistance, CPHC including community health, RH at primary and secondary level, referral system, and specialised care for COVID-19. Identified groups include: IDPs, refugees, HCs, girls&women of reproductive age, children&newborns, persons with complex medical conditions, PwDs. Ben. have free access to the services, designed according to specific needs of the targeted groups incl. gender&age considerations: *CPHC and HP in CPHCCs in Al Hol and Areesha camps, and in Raqqa city; *BCPHC and HP in BPHCCs in Mahmoudli, Serekaniye and Abu Khashab camps. Protection will be mainstreamed to ensure assistance is provided in a safe, dignified, accountable&participatory manner. Protection Prevention Outreach workers stationed in PHCCs in Al Hol Ph 1, Areesha, Serenkinye and Abu Khashab camps and Raqqa Rasheed PHCC deliver 1-1 and group prevention activities and community campaigns, referrals to multi-sector services and support in accessing FCRM. In Raqqa Gov: RPMH AfH team is supported in delivering GBV and CP Case Management services to beneficiaries accessing the health service, including survivors and at-risk women and girls.Monitoring and evaluation will reinforce a result-based M&E system that has Client satisfaction surveys, monthly, quarterly, and annual including donor reports, attendance, needs assessments, work-plan, and activity budgets. Inclusion and assistance for all gender & age groups with FCRM.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: PoC are exposed to higher level of community/HH tension due to messages provided during outreach and prevention activities. Mitigation: participatory approach to identify key messages tailored to each location. Contingency planning. Evaluation of messages. Mitigation: Train staff on Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy. FCRM in place. PSEA awareness raising. Risk: Persons with reduced mobility are excluded from the service due to reduced access. Mitigation: Adaptation of HFs infrastructure to ensure equal access. Household visits to spread messages. ProM assessments. Risk: Discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Mitigation: Ensure training of health staff on PSEA and Code of Conduct. Ensure consultation with communities related to safe and dignified access to services. Carry out safety audits and ProM assessments for beneficiaries’ access to site locations.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection will be mainstreamed into all delivered services to ensure assistance is provided in a safe, dignified, accountable&participatory manner; to increase access to protection services, and to ensure local staff’s understanding of integrity/protection principles, PSEA, protection threats and needs. The NGO is co-Chair of the NES PSEA Network and will streamline PSEA efforts to prevent the risk of SEA. Capacity building of staff on ProM, Protection core concepts, PSEA/Safeguarding. The project ensures PSEA & Child Safeguarding with training, sensitization & PSEA mainstreaming in FCRM, in coord. with the NES PSEA Network. The FCRM ensures any feedback or complaint can be raised and addressed, each PSEA complaint being addressed per the NGO guidelines.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks of further displacement affect NES due to protracted tensions along the Peace Spring Op.(PSO). In Al Hol camp, risks for NGOs and ben. accessing NGOs services increased in 2021 due to deteriorating security conditions and targeted attacks, and UPP security posture in PHCCs was heightened. Overall, scarce and unmonitored HW disposal areas in NES exacerbates environmental risks linked to the provision of health care. Protraction of crisis and depletion of socio-economic conditions are likely to worsen protection needs esp. for women and FHHs, boys and girls, and exacerbate neg. coping mechanisms- whilst the foreseen humanitarian aid funding cuts risk discontinuation or closure of life-saving services. UPP has designed a detailed country and project Risks analysis, scenario, and contingency plan identifying each risk (economic, geopolitical, political, environmental, programmatical, financial) that the project can encounter and defined mitigation measures, contingency planning, and scenarios to answer those risks. The contingency plan is to be updated and revisited throughout the project.
