Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection services, that is targeted to children, women and people who with vulnerabilities and at protection risk including children with low income, out of care kids, orphans, juvenile kids, survivals of violence, labor kids, survivals of armed conflicts. the project also will target women at violence risk or vulnerable and survival of sexual violence. The project also aims to provide mine action awareness activities.
Proposed activities:
- Positive parenting activities
- PSS support to children
- Case management services for children and women
- Mine risl education awareness activities
The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus: Kafar Batna township (the city of Kafar Batna, Ein Tarma, Saqba, Hamoria, Gisreen, Aftrees, Beit Sawa, Al Ahaary, Almohammadia) Daraya, and Qudsaya

4 - Needs

According to the preliminary estimates, 14.2 million people are in need of protection humanitarian assistance, including over 4 million PLWD and over 6.2 children. The deterioration of the economy is forcing families to adopt negative and harmful coping mechanisms. Children continue to be exposed to a variety of risks and forms of violence and their prevalence is reported at increasing levels in all governorates. Child labor was reported as occurring in 84% of the assessed communities, family separation in 36% and psychosocial distress was reported by 26% of the surveyed households. Girls are more at risk of early marriage (reported in 71% of the assessed communities, against 62% in 2021), harassment, sexual abuse and movement restrictions; boys are more exposed to heavy child labor, negligence and harmful discipline methods, recruitment, detention, family separation and engagement in illegal activities. Women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV and despite under-reporting (due to stigma, social and traditional norms, lack of awareness and support services),

5 - Implementation of activities

1. 1000 children will be selected and targeted for psychosocial support activities from two age groups (6-8 and 9-12). Every child attends 10 psychosocial support sessions. Each session lasts for 60 to 75 minutes. Each session receives about 20-25 children. 2. Proper parenting sessions (positive parenting) will be held for about 800 caregivers to raise their awareness of the importance of raising children in a protective environment that optimally develops them up to the age of puberty. 3. 450 teenagers from 13 to 17 years old will be targeted with life skills activities. Each teen attends 10 life skills training sessions covering 10 essential life skills. 4. 1000 person from all ages will be selected and targeted in an awareness raising activities. 5. 135 participants will be selected to participate in 9 community initiatives concerned with issues of child protection, mine protection and the prevention of gender-based violence. 6. Identifying 400 children from those who face obstacles that prevent them from following the progress of their classmates to participate in 5-10 educational therapy sessions aimed at enabling them to finish their school year at the best level. 7. Identify 450 children of those who are identified as cases of violence; suffer from health problems, lack care or juveniles, and work to provide them with specialized services. 8.Identifying 75 women exposed to gender-based violence and working to provide them with specialized services.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Lack of/low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm, discrimination, stigmatization. Mitigation measures include: training and supervision of case managers; service mapping including only providers implementing ensuring survivor centered approach; protected and confidential information systems.2. Resistance from the community on GBV/CP messaging and activities, resulting in low participation, stigmatization or harm to target groups. Mitigation measures: community and leaders’ engagement on all phases of the project cycle; adapting awareness and information messages to the specific context; only female staff working in WGSS; engagement of volunteers from the target communities. Risks related to the location of service provision (safety of movements for beneficiary, contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings). Mitigation measures: choice of locations done in close consultation and agreement with the community, conduction of safety audits. COVID 19: Not adherence to preventive and IPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission to beneficiaries. Mitigation measures include: monitoring of adherence to preventive measures; reduction of beneficiaries numbers for group activities; provision of PPE; trainings/refreshers; suspension of service provision if increase in the number of staff with confirmed/suspected infection occurs. PSEA: please refer to the dedicated section.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiaries seeking services at CRS facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. At organizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited and considered a gross violation of CRS Code of Conduct, which all CRS employees must sign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by CRS Safeguarding and Whistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete a mandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and on reporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism are also provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities, advocacy for uninterrupted access. 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of CRS activities from the community. Mitigation: Community engagement and awareness through culturally and context adapted tools and communication strategies, especially for new areas of intervention. 3: Delays in international bank transfers. Mitigation: Maintain more than one bank account; advocate for dedicated channels for humanitarian transfers; info sharing with partners and donors. 4: COVID 19 limit capacity to deliver services either because of lockdowns or infection among staff. Mitigation: waivers to allow movement of staff and mobile facilities; adherence to preventive and isolation measures; temporary suspension of services to protect beneficiaries from transmission, remote case management services. 5: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at facilities, premises
