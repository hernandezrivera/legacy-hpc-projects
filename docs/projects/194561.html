Project Summary

This project aims to respond to the critical health needs of 50,000 affected populations [11,920 returnees, 12800 IDPs, and 25,280 Host communities] (11,840 women, 11820 men, 12290 boys, and 14050 girls) and 7,500 people with disability in Tambura, Nagero, and Kajo Keji counties. The total budget required to reach this population is $1,300,000 at $26 per beneficiary over one year. This cost considers the annual service utilization per person, human resources for health, pharmaceutical, non-pharmaceutical inputs, and interventions that build health resilience, such as health for peace linking humanitarian interventions with development. Tambura, Nagero & kajo Keji are majorly remote and at extreme severity due to multiple shocks and stresses, including conflict, cattle raids, and crop destruction by cattle herders.
The plan is expected to support activities that reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services through static, mobile, and community-based health service delivery systems. Lastly, the project would increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, the disabled, mental health disorders, and the elderly.
The proposed implementation methodology uses a mix of static and mobile teams to increase population coverage. These will include; 2 PHCCs, 5 PHCU, and six mobile teams (1 PHCC and four mobile teams in Tambura, I PHCC and 2 Mobile units in Nagero and 5 PHCUs in kajo Keji County.
The planned activities include; OPD consultations, Case management for common diseases, Immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases, and Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) (ANC, Skilled deliveries, and PNC) Clinical management of rape, Mental health and psychosocial support, Capacity building, Health education, and promotion with the integration of peace through community consultative and dialogue meetings Infection control, prevention, and other activities as guided by the basic package for health and nutrition services, specific cluster strategy, and resource envelopes.
The Rescue Initiative-South Sudan commits to provide regular updates through the financial tracking system (FTS) on any resources secured to support this response plan. We are present in the counties with established offices and logistics targeted for this response.

1 - Needs

The integrated food security phase classification (IPC) grouped Tambura and Nagero in IPC phases 4&5, emergency, and catastrophe (IPC, April 2022). The key driver of vulnerability is the June 2021 intercommunal violence that displaced over 80,000 people within Tambura County or nearby areas, particularly larger towns in Western Equatoria State, to Nzara, Yambio, Ezo, and Nagero, who either stayed with their relatives, within the host communities or in IDP camps. Most health facilities and infrastructure, including medical supplies, were destroyed, damaged, or looted during the violence (REACH Oct. 2021). Disruption in livelihood activities, including farming and markets, affected the community’s production capacity. A recent analysis of the conflict indicates that only 8% of the respondents considered their communities very peaceful and almost half (47%) thought it somewhat safe. It also reveals that every ten had a certain disability. 29% have difficulty remembering/concentrating, 23% have difficulty hearing, 22% have problems with self-care, 21% have a visual impairment, and 20% have difficulty walking (TRI-SS Health and peace analysis, June 2022). Local population record indicates that Tambura hosts an estimated 16,184 people distributed in Hayati construction site, 493; Army barracks, 530 Saints Mary 5,015 and Ministerial complex 4,482. Other areas include Nabanga, with a population of 6,214, located 12 kilometres west of Tambura County. The actual population may be higher considering that relative calm recently encouraged some displaced people to return. The Internally displaced population sheltering in Nagero is estimated at 24,371 within four payams of Nagero, 15,284; Duma, 4,268; and Namutina, 4,819. Although the 2021 humanitarian response in Tambura and Nagero counties helped stabilize the situation, huge gaps remain, posed by lack of access and insecurity and the Health Pooled Fund’s (HPF) reduction in support for about 25% of Primary Health Care Units. Only five of the 16 health facilities are functional and overwhelmed (CHD/ RRC Nagero May 2022), which significantly affects access to health care services, including mental health and psychosocial support for the most vulnerable women, children, the elderly, and persons with disability (OCHA April 2022). Inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and a lack of accessibility to clean water contribute to poor health conditions within IDP camps (overcrowding), increasing the risk of diarrheal diseases, including cholera (OCHA April 2022). Low Immunization coverage and overcrowding in the IDPS resulted in a measles outbreak in the two counties in March 2022 (SMOH 2022). The latest reports suggest that despite the challenging space in kajo Keji, the refugee population in Uganda continues to return voluntarily. Returnees to Kajo Keji County number 56,000; 63% are women and children, with 48% being female-headed households (IOM DTM & UNHCR, 2021). Unfortunately, their vulnerability increases with limited access to food and health care. In Kajo Keji, our medical team reached 2900 persons, where children under five males were 319, (11%) and females under five were 435 (15%). Males above five years were 928 (32%), and females above five were 1218 (42%). Malaria cases remained the highest with a total of 1,363 (47%) followed by URTI 1044 (36%), water diarrhoea 261 (9%), and other diseases such as helminths and skin infections constituted the rest of the percentage.

2 - Project activities

The main activities selected for this response and aligned with the specific objectives for the HRP 2022 include; OPD consultations, Case management for common diseases, Immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) (ANC, Skilled deliveries and PNC), Clinical management of rape, Mental health, and psychosocial support, Capacity building, Health education, and promotion with the integration of peace through community consultative and dialogue meetings, Infection control, prevention, COVID-19 and EVD surveillance, and other activities as guided by the basic package for health and nutrition services, specific cluster strategy, and resource envelopes.

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project will be measured through the following indicators; Percentage of deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant, Number of health facilities supported with Interagency Emergency Health kits (IEHK), Number of consultations per person per year disaggregated by sex, age, and disability, Number of persons receiving MHPSS services disaggregated by sex, age and disability, Percentage of health facilities assessed using Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) scorecard, Coverage of PENTA3 in <One-year-old by administrative unit, Number of community health workers per 1,000 people, Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services by age and sex and disability, Percentage of alerts, rumors and outbreaks investigated within 48 hours of notification, Coverage of reactive measles vaccination of boys and girls between 6 months – 15 years, # of community health events conducted for behavioral change communication, # of people reached by health education and promotion during disease outbreaks disaggregated by sex, age and disability, Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanism, Number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs, participate in response, and reduce impact on their lives disaggregated by men, women, boys and girls and disability, # of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision quarterly and # of meetings conducted with humanitarian, development and peace partners.
