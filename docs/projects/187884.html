Project Summary

The aim of the project is to conduct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Trainings among schoolchildren and civilians living in mine contaminated areas of Syria, teaching them safe behaviour, what to do while finding mines and unexploded ordnances, encourage to behave in a way
that reduces the risk to people, property, and the environment, The objective is to reduce the risk
to a level where people can live safely, thus contributing to an environment where economic and
social development can occur free from the constraints imposed by EO contamination. Besides, we plan to t conduct non-technical survey in those areas. The purpose NTS is to use all appropriate non-technical means, including visits to field locations, to identify, collect, analyse and report information/evidence in
order to make recommendations about the definition of SHAs/CHAs, make recommendations about cancellation and/or subsequent reduction/clearance of areas, support priority setting processes and contribute to efficient and effective planning of subsequent technical interventions.The activities will be carried out in Damascus, Aleppo, Latakia, Syrian Arab Republic.

4 - Needs

According to the Mine Action Area of Responsibility Findings 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, Syria continues to be exposed to the explosive ordnance (EO) contamination issue. Based on the information collected during the needs analysis one in three communities in Syria is potentially contaminated by EO. As pointed out in the Humanitarian Situation Report of UNICEF (2021) humanitarian needs in north-west Syria were among the worst in the country with 3.4 out of 4.2 million people in need of assistance. According to the UN during the last few years over 3,5000 explosive ordnance accidents occurred in northwestern Syria. Exposure to explosive ordnance issue spread across all governorates to varying degrees. The exact scale of EO contamination in Syria has not been identified as a comprehensive country-wide survey has not been carried out. The efforts directed at addressing the presence of EO contamination by collecting information as accurately and reliably as possible, as well as identifying the socio-economic and threat factors, are of utmost importance for scaling the clearance of contamination. As to the assessments pointed out in Humanitarian Needs Overview 61 % of key informants surveyed have indicated that they know someone who has been injured and killed by explosive ordnance and high percentage of respondents indicated that they have not attended RE sessions in the past three months.

5 - Implementation of activities

Prior to RE sessions, each team will request the local authorities to provide them the detailed information on the community’s occupation, number of educational entities, and their availability to RE sessions. RE sessions will be between 45 minutes to 1 hour. The EORE session comprises four components: presentation of project idea, providing information on the threats (mines, EO and IEDs), The social, economic, environmental and psychological impact of the threats on the community, communication of EORE safety messages. Questions and Answers will summarize this part, interaction with participants (exercises, games, role-play) to measure the level of understanding and knowledge gained, conclusion, repeating the safety messages and providing necessary contacts. EORE trainings will be implemented by the relevant specialists both from CHDE and international. There will be 5 EORE teams, each team has 1 Team leader and 4 specialists among targeted groups, There will be 6 NTS teams. Each team consists 1 Team Leader, 6 surveyors, 1 medic, 1 logistics specialist and 1 translator. Operations will consists of phases; mobilization, operations and demobilization. The whole project will have 1 project manager.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The protection risks of NTS and EORE activities involve but are not limited to the following protection risks/threats: creation of expectations of response, content of message is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions with negative effects on attendance to sessions, exclusion of some groups due to the choice of the location, tension between IDPs and the host community, vulnerable groups might not have the same access, familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards, risk that local authorities may dictate mine action priorities not in line with humanitarian objectives, survey and clearance teams may be perceived as militants or affiliated with an armed group andother risks.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In order to address the effective risk management, the comprehensive information is essential as well as the provision of relevant information comprising the primary means to reduce uncertainty and so to reduce the risk. The risk management context is comprised of the external and internal factors within which it should be defined and objectives achieved. The external and internal context includes but is not limiting to: Social, cultural, political, legal, gender and diversity, regulatory factors on local level, UNMAS and IMAS respective regulations and requirements commitments, respective liaison with local authorities, the safety and security situation for the community destination, The Personnel composition, including gender and diversity dynamics, management structure, roles and accountabilities, capabilities, in terms of resources and knowledge, preparation of Personnel in line with UNMAS Standards, guidelines. We are getting regular updates on risk assessment, planning for newly identified risks, monitoring trigger conditions and contingency plans, and risk reporting on a regular basis. Also, at local level stronger coordination will be maintained with the local communities to ensure safety and security of project staff and the communities engaged in the community awareness sessions.
