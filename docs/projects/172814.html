Project Summary

Based on the success and learns learnt from the first two years of the Emergency School Feeding
Programmes in Libya in 2019 [HLBY19‐EDU‐151894‐1] and 2020 [HLBY20-EDU-162711-2], where more than 20,000 School-aged children has been reached with whether on-site school meals or alternative Take-Home-Rations when schools closed because of the COVID-19 pandemic. WFP will expand the complementary school meals programme as means to ensure children’s access to food in times of crisis and enhance diets through increased micronutrient intake. Assistance will take into account protection and safety, COVID-19 precautions, and where possible, contribute to their broader protection and community awareness. In 2020, the school rations including nutritionally‐fortified date bars, which are culturally‐acceptable, programmatically‐feasible and can contribute to enhanced micronutrient intake among children, were actually provided to over 18,379 school-aged children (Onsite feeding or Take-Home-Rations) against the original plan of 20,000 school‐aged children.
In 2021, WFP plans to reach 41,000 school-aged children in need for Education Sector's assistance, out of which 20,000 school-aged children will be prioritized for the humanitarian-oriented assistance under the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021. This programme could also support and strengthen the current formal education system and advocate towards a safe back to School, in order to allow a greater and at scale-reach of those already in school, girls and boys out of school or at risk of dropping out and those who are most vulnerable in the society, minority groups including (but not excluded to) girls. It will also help promote and encourage the enrolment of the children of the migrant populations in urban settings. The programme would review implementation arrangements to assure COVID-19 precautionary measures, strengthen synergies with education and livelihood sectors and increase nutrition sensitivity, gender sensitivity, local procurement and community ownership. This would promote community infrastructure, rehabilitation, and reconstruction ﴾including schools and health centres﴿ as well as promote the reintegration of displaced populations and returnees, with special attention paid to the different needs of women and men.
While implementing the Emergency School Feeding under the HRP 2021, WFP will invest its resources in the capacity strengthening to enable national and local authorities to respond, and foster schools’ resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses stemming from existing and future crises through strengthened coordination, evidence-based needs analyses, and strategic preparedness. WFP will also work with the Ministry of Education in conducting awareness raising sessions for the local Education officials and school representatives on nutrition and cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, environment and the prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (PSEA).
The project will be monitored by the local Education officials and WFP’s Third Party Monitors. The outputs data will be collected by WFP through the partners’ monthly distribution reports, and the outcomes will be assessed the Post-Distribution Monitoring on an annual basis.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021, 152,616 school‐aged children are in need of education. School feeding remains one of the key priorities set under Libya Education Sector in order to establish a resumption of normalcy in children’s lives, especially the protracted interruption in the last academic year because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The Libyan Ministry of Education has highlighted the problem of the triple burden of malnutrition which schoolchildren suffer from, i.e. undernutrition, micronutrients deficiency and overweight/obesity. With the COVID-19 pandemic and the long period children pent away from Schools, the situation surrounding them has gotten more challenging in 2020. The crisis in Libya has negatively impacted public education services, causing challenges for schoolchildren to enjoy safe access to fully functional schools and quality education. WFP signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Ministry of Education for the partnership in implementing the emergency School Feeding programme for the targeted group of 20,000 schoolchildren in four districts in the South of Libya. WFP and the Ministry of Education geographically targeted four districts based on the criteria of high concentration of IDPs, food security, school infrastructures and access, and all of primary and secondary schools within the targeted districts were reached through the Emergency School Feeding Programme. In 2020, WFP managed to assist some 18,000 schoolchildren at 58 public schools and one migrant school in Tripoli with vitamin-/mineral-fortified date bars, and nutrition awareness classes. Under the HRP 2021, WFP will target at the vulnerable districts of Alkufra, Aljabala Algharbi, Ghat, Misrata, Murzuq, Nalut, Sebha and Tripoli based on assessed education-related needs and food security-related needs. The daily schools meals will be distributed to all of the enrolled schoolchildren at the targeted schools in the manner sensitive to specific and/or vulnerable groups such as the disabled children and the children quarantined due to COVID-19.
