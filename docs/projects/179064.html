Project Summary

This project to the groups due to the lack of livelihood opportunities and the continuation of conflict in the areas, which caused big displacement influxes towards western and norther Idleb and northern of Aleppo.
The project aims to provide Multi-Purpose Cash Grants (MPGs) response for most vulnerable familise to help them cope with the harsh conditions and secure their needs in a dignified way, by distributing three rounds of cash grants with a total value of 100 USD per round per family (aligned with SMEB value). The project and the grant amount were designed to allow the beneficiaries to cover their basic needs of food and NFI since this cash modality will give them flexibility in choosing items they need and to give the project the ability to meet all changes in beneficiary needs.
Through the proposed project, GAN seek to target 1500 families of host and IDPs, the beneficiaries will be selected in close coordination with the local councils and civil authorities with 100 % verification from MEAL team in the targeted areas based on the vulnerability and eligibility criteria including displacement duration criteria since the newly IDPs are the most vulnerable groups to the shocks. Also, the project will prioritize the women-headed households as well as the households with children, disabilities, pregnant, and lactating women, in addition to, GAN will contact FSL members to support the same families with livelihood assistance to link with the current project when possible and aplicable.
GAN will benefit from exchanging experiences through sharing the lessons learned and the solutions to overcome the challenges with SFNI, FSL clusters, and CWG.
GAN could cover as much as can of the geographical areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, such as Afrin, Harim districts that have overcrowded IDPs to reach 1500 vulnerable and newly IDP in urban, rural or even camps. Since GAN, through its ‘on the ground’ operation team and its prior experience in Idleb governorate will be able to access the locations and markets during the project implementation.
Due to the lack of the banking system in northern Syria, GAN will identify the money transfer agents through conducting a competitive process after assessing the markets and the vendors' capacities in the local markets. where the identified vendors will be responsible for delivering the MPG's amount to the beneficiaries in the different locations.
prior to the MPG distribution, a rapid market assessment in terms of availability of the food items, prices, and the accessibility will be conducted to make sure the local markets are functioning and to ensure all beneficiaries have a good and equal accessibility to the different goods and services.

4 - Needs

13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance, with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. More than 90% of the population is now estimated to live below the poverty line. The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in a context where 50% of the work-age population is now estimated unemployed. In particular, there was a decrease in employment of 18.2 percent for males and 19.7 percent for females. Most enterprises lost significant revenue ranging from 40 percent to 100 percent, and about 13 percent had to close their operations. While hostility-induced displacement in early 2020 generated additional needs amongst the population in Syria for internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities. Facing deteriorated living standards, families are increasingly adopting harmful coping mechanisms. Seventy-one percent of households and 75 % of female-headed households have taken on more debt since August 2019. 28% of families now adopt ‘crisis’ or ‘emergency’ food-related coping strategies, including withdrawing children from school to have them work instead, selling property, migrating due to lack of food, and early child marriage. Protection Sector, January 2021 Debts and credits are a common coping strategy with 52.9% of households having taken on debt since displacement with an average amount of debt per household as SYP 701,443. The primary reason for taking on debt was to buy food. In addition to visiting the camps and meeting with camp managers and local councils to determine the best response method.sources: HNO2021, MSNA.

5 - Implementation of activities

beneficiaries' selection will be finalized in coordination with local stakeholders, and communicated to LCs and community members. Potential beneficiaries will then be identified through specific outreach and referrals from local organizations and the relief coordination administration. a scoring system will be used to prioritize based on weighting assigned to the selection criteria. Based on past experience, GAN will develop an appropriate mechanism to verify from the registered households in close coordination with the relief administration and the LCs. - Distribution: e-voucher cards will be distributed for beneficiaries and WhatsApp channels will be created for each group of beneficiaries to communicate with on time, a dedicated female staff will be maintained to ensure female access and protection. At DP, each beneficiary will be required to present his/her ID form. Cash vouchers will be designed and distributed before. Accompanied with the distribution process, MEAL team will be existed to observe the process and raised the beneficiary awareness to the complaint mechanism. - Post-distribution monitoring to monitor receipt, use and impact of assistance provided Following every distribution round, GAN will conduct a PDM and a price monitoring to gauge beneficiary feedback in relation to timeliness of the provision of assistance, quality and quantity of assistance provided, the distribution process, behavior of the distribution staff, perceptions on the targeting process (and knowledge of criteria), and the use of the cash provided (e.g. used, kept, shared.. etc.) as well as to keep an eye on the market any price inflation to adapt with better timeline and and mechanism of intervention.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members: Likelihood: high, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: - Use the recommended minimum standards of cash amount. 2-Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Likelihood: high, Impact: high. 1.Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2.Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make efforts to target % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support IDPs. 4. Establish feedback mechanisms and conduct follow-up visits if needs be. 5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 3- physical harm at distribution points (security, safety): Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. - Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or canceled. -Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding -Training to staff on establishing and managing distributions and crowd management. -Application of checklist at distribution sites. -Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk. 4-corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection: Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. 1. Strengthen over-sight mechanism. 2. ensure that their staffs have read/areaware of the Code of Conduct. 3. Outreach to affected population. 4. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - Markets are functioning properly in the targeted areas. - The money transfer agent / Hawala services are functioning. - Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries Risk: 1- risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allow the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, GAN would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are met. 2- Risk: Money transfer agent / Hawala services are no longer available: Mitigation strategy: Proactive identification of alternative cash transfer agents, Investigate further the possibilities of vouchers systems as an alternative. 3- Risks3: Refusal by the FSP to distribute the cash after payment up front by the aid agency:Payment after distributions: The transfers to the FSP after the cash is distributed. any issues or robberies happening to the FSP must be covered by the FSP itself as stipulated in the contract.4- Inflation – price increases for staple items due to lack of supply to meet demand (as cash transfers increase purchasing power and demand), causing harm to all affected people and other community members who use the market, the mitigation measures: - An estimate of potential above-average inflation through market analysis, and compare with normal price fluctuations, seasonal shifts.
