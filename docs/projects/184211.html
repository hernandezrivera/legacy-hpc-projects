Project Summary

Children in Somalia continue to live in one of the harshest environments in the world faced with repeated climatic shocks, continued insecurity, weak health system and natural disasters impacting on food security and on their health and Nutrition status.
In 2021, just as the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS), state authorities and partners are responding to another wave of COVID-19 across Somalia, abnormally low rains April to May) is obvious. This expected low rainfall coupled with COVID 19 will worsen food and nutrition security of the community who are still not yet fully recovered from 2019 draught. Desert locust infestation was more widespread in the 2020 Deyr than in the 2019 Deyr, leading to more significant crop losses.
According to Somalia Food security outlook , In agro-pastoral (including Burkahaba ) and riverine livelihood zones, most poor households will have below-normal food stocks and reduced agricultural labor income from the below-average 2020 Deyr harvest and the anticipated, below- average 2021 Gu harvest (SOMALIA Food Security Outlook February to September 2021).
The prevalence of acute malnutrition is also likely to rise, driven by reduced food and milk intake and seasonal and long-term factors. Based on nutrition experts’ analysis of current GAM prevalence and historical trends, GAM is expected to increase from Serious (10-14.9 percent GAM WHZ) to Critical (15-29.9 percent GAM WHZ) in rural livelihood zones in Bay and Hiram and central Hawd Pastoral livelihood zone. In agro-pastoral livelihood zones, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected in Bay-Bakool Low Potential Agro-pastoral.
According to FSNAU February 2021 projection, malnutrition burden in Burhakaba district is at 10,789 with SAM at 2006 and MAM at 8,789. UNICEF implemented expanded admission criteria to prevent MAM becoming SAM with agreement with WFP and Nutrition cluster in the past project and was one of the selected partners of UNICEF for the maintenance of the EAC approach in Burhakaba and this approach will be maintained in this call for proposal.
Children with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) have an increased risk of mortality, infections and impaired physical and cognitive development compared to well-nourished children. In parts of Burhakaba not considered chronically food insecure there are no supplementary feeding programs (SFPs) for treating MAM. Although there are still gaps in current understanding of prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition, there is a greater level of consensus around the management of SAM than of MAM. The short-term outcomes of children who have MAM in such areas are not currently described, and there remains an urgent need for evidence-based policy recommendations. is running stabilization center on voluntary basis with 90% of community contribution. Having on board with EAC for treatment of acute malnutrition will qualify our program to called fully integrated management of acute malnutrition and shall improve internal referral linkage among OTP, TSFP and SC in the same operational area.
A significant number of Mogadishu IDPs, forcibly evacuated from Las Anod of Sanaag region in the Northern of Somalia were reaching in Burhakaba and they were originated from Bay agro pastoral Livelihood particularly Buurhakaba where agro pastoral communities are more dependent on rain-fed farming system than pastoral (more crop dependent than livestock). The main causes of under nutrition is poor IYCF associated inadequate nutrient intake and infection, Water Shortage and chronic food insecurity.
The main objective for this proposal is to reduce morbidity and mortality due to malnutrition related causes in children, pregnant mothers and lactating women living in Burhakaba district of Bay region

1. - Projects activities

The project will focus on prevention of malnutrition through the BNSP program, the treatment of moderate acute malnutrition through the TSFP with a clear referral pathway and linkage with the Treatments severe acute malnutrition through OTP project as well as SC in Burhakaba. BTSC will also build the capacity of the staff through training on project implementation (work distribution, supervision and reporting) and the CHWs on active case finding, identification and referral of malnourished beneficiaries to the nearest SC. • Identify and treat severely acutely & Moderate acute malnourished children. • refer children with acute malnutrition & medical complications to the BTSC SC • provide a package of micronutrient supplementation, deworming to eligible women & children• Provide IYCF/NHHP/TFSP promotional messages to targeted communities and households• Capacity Building Training to the project staffs to harmonies their knowledge and skills in prevention, management and treatment of SAM and MAM.Community mobilization and sensitization - BTSC will conduct community mobilization and sensitization sessions at the community level to create massive awareness and educate the community on the concept of malnutrition. This will enhance the accessibility of health and nutrition services to the most vulnerable people to ensure the community participation in the prevention and management of malnutrition. The proposed action is aimed at enhancing uptake of the project interventions and ownership purposes.

Complaints can be made through a well-established Somalia based toll-free hotline, directly to BTSC monitoring staff and cooperating partner staff at programme implementation sites, through telephone calls to BTSC Hotline Number 2103 and email or short messaging to BTSC offices, and through local authorities and local leaders.
