Project Summary

Food assistance needs in Somalia are already high with an estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements in need of food assistance. Currently (July-September 2020), an estimated 1.3 million (i.e. people in IPC Phases 3&4) people have food consumption gaps even in the presence of humanitarian assistance. This number is expected to increase to 2.1 million people between now and the end of the year due to multiple risk factors (FSNAU Post Gu Key findings September 2020)
The erratic distribution of rainfall during the April to June Gu season has worsened Gu cereal production prospects in south-central Somalia. Nationally, the Gu harvest in July is now projected to be 30-40 per cent below the long-term average. Severe floods in April/May inundated more than 54,000 hectares of farmland, equivalent to more than 20 per cent of the 1995-2019 average planted area. The early end of the Gu rainfall season has also led to crop moisture stress in many areas. Although there are no reports of desert locust in the South, damage caused by crickets and other pests has been atypically high. Crop losses are highest in riverine areas and northern Bay Bakool Agropastoral livelihood zones, where Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are anticipated from June to September. (FEWSNET Outlook July 2020)
The People in Need (PiN) in Baidoa is 169,561; out of these people who need improved access to food through conditional and unconditional transfers are 58,277 (non-displaced and 105,780 (displaced). Also, PiN in regards to seasonal livelihood inputs support (agriculture and livestock) are 47, 425 (FSC Targets October 2020). Furthermore, PiN for Hudur and Dinsoor district is 44856 people.
In regards to markets, in Dinsoor, there is improved availability of key commodities in the market, which traders managed to smuggle into the town leading to decrease in prices. In Baidoa, prices of both local cereals and imported food items remain the same as last week. However, camel milk prices are high at $1 per litre. In Xudur, prices of local cereals either increased or remained the same compared to last week and are high. Red and white sorghum increased slightly by (2%) while white maize remained the same (Joint Market and Supply Chain Update October 4th 2020)
In response to this, READO in line with the FSC & HRP 2021 objectives aims to improve households’ immediate access to food through conditional and unconditional cash transfers in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor districts in the Bay region. Also, READO will protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through the provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs provide livelihood inputs/training and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings.

Project Activities

Objective 1: Improve households’ immediate access to food through the provision of conditional and unconditional assistance in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor districts in Bay region. Activities:- READO will prioritize unconditional cash transfers especially for 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) in the IDP population for four months. READO will prioritize cash in areas where markets are viable; are physically and socially accessible to all segments of the population. In regards to markets, in Dinsoor, there is improved availability of key commodities in the market, which traders managed to smuggle into the town leading to decrease in prices. In Baidoa, prices of both local cereals and imported food items remain the same as last week. However, camel milk prices are high at $1 per litre. In Xudur, prices of local cereals either increased or remained the same compared to last week and are high. Red and white sorghum increased slightly by (2%) while white maize remained the same (Joint Market and Supply Chain Update October 4th 2020) In line with recent Somalia Cash Working Group and FSNAU guidance, transfers will be 100 % of full Food MEB. Throughout the year, READO will coordinate with the cash working group to determine the value of cash transfers. Cash transfers will be done through mobile money as it will not only ensure the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients but limit fraud and diversion of funds. Activities:- Provision of conditional cash transfers (cash for work), which will target key infrastructure of community assets rehabilitation during the lean seasons to 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) for four months. The transfer will be conditional for completing water catchment rehabilitation work in Bay regions. For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labor-intensivework, specifically female heads of households with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers. READO opted for cash transfers instead of in-kind because cash transfers support the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs, provide recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets and promoting financial inclusion. Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through the provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings. Activities: Provision of livelihood specific inputs READO will distribute the seeds package to the 1,000 HHs (600 male, 400 female). In line with FSC guidance, each household will receive 40-kg which includes: Sorghum (25kg) Cowpea (15kg) and hand farm tools (Knife, Hoes, and Axe). Before the distribution of the livelihood inputs, the farmers will receive GAP training to improve their yield. Activity 3.2 Livelihood support through Vocational Skills training: READO will train 500 youth (300Female 200male) IDPs in vocational skills (masonry, traditional weaving, tie & dye, business entrepreneurship, tailoring, and carpentry, etc.) so that they can boost their income levels through increasing their employability. READO will also conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring after the distribution of the cash transfers and livelihood inputs. The PDM will also be disaggregated by persons with disability analysis of barriers faced by persons with disabilities and any instances of exploitation or other protection risks created through the delivery of assistance. In light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings and consider staggering of the distribution of livelihood inputs between 5-10 days. The distribution sites, will set up a supervised hand washing area with an adequate supply of hand wash solution and each site will allocate areas for health screening/ body temperature checks by READO staff in coordination with MoH.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Firstly this project is using cash modality instead of in-kind because markets in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor are functioning as explained further in the below section. Also, in light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings at distributions site by using mobile money transfer as modality of cash transfer which is safe and quick and will minimize contact and prevent the spread of the virus.Furthermore, cash transfer through mobile money modality will mitigate the risks associated with a cash project which include: corruption, diversion of assistance, delays of providing assistance etc as the beneficiaries will receive the cash direct on their mobile handsets. In regards to protection risks, READO will use mobile money transfer to avoid forced taxation/extortion to beneficiaries. Moreover, cash transfer through mobile money modality will prevent discrimination when targeting marginalized groups, for example, the elderly, people with disabilities, female-headed HHs will have equal access and receive their entitlement with ease, safety and dignity. Moreover, READO opted for cash transfers instead of in-kind because cash transfers support the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs, provide recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets and promoting financial inclusion.

Mitigation Measures

As far as protection risks are concerned, READO through security mapping will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the distribution sites of livelihood inputs and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. READO will, in turn, avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Also, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming. For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labour-intensive work, specifically female heads of household with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to food security needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for food security target locations, design, and services. In regards to protection risks, READO will use mobile money transfer to avoid forced taxation/extortion to beneficiaries. READO will also establish a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for accountability. READO will use information boards to display CRM phonenumbers during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaints. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. Mitigating protection risks Insecurity: READO will ensure that the distribution of livelihood inputs takes place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. In light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings and consider staggering of the distribution of livelihood inputs between 5-10 days. The distribution sites, will set up a supervised hand washing area with an adequate supply of hand wash solution and each site will allocate areas for health screening/ body temperature checks by health officials in coordination with the MoH. During distribution, READO will also allocate specific areas for the elderly, pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities and individuals with pre-existing/chronic underlying conditions & give priority. The CFW and UCT cash will be distributed to the beneficiaries via mobile money transfer which will minimize contact and prevent the spread of the virus. Also, READO will ensure that beneficiary lists are confidential and private. READO will not share information on the identity of beneficiaries unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. For instance, READO will use mobile money transfer which will ensure female, elderly, disable and others who face exclusions benefit. Besides, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which includes who has access to food services and who can influence decisions on the provision of food services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will employ the DO NO HARM approach.
