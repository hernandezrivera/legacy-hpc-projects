Project Summary

Due to dire needs identified throughout its previous Child Protection and Education activities implemented in 2020, and in line with the desire to implement life-saving activities, enhance protection and strengthen coping capacities with an eye to support the latter, this proposal aims to work in hard-to-reach areas where Voice of the Peace (VOP) has identified compounded risks and observed the impact of protracted periods of under-servicing, denoting the priority of this intervention. More specifically, Voice of the Peace (VOP) proposes a response that is static in Jonglei state, Pibor administration County with regular field-based roving activities to other hard-to-reach areas. Voice of the peace aims to provide child protection to vulnerable, conflict affected children through a holistic approach consisting of various activities which strengthen each other. The primary activity proposed intends to directly address lifesaving needs of children through structured and comprehensive case management, coupled with PSS provision (including PFA) and FTR services for girls and boys with protection needs (UASC, CAAFAG and GBV survivors), orphans and children with other specific needs identified including children with disability.
As an additional channel for the identification of protection needs as well as a space to provide PSS activities, malnutrition screenings and referrals for children, a CFS with daily activities in each location has proven to be successful. The intervention will also be characterized by a focus on community engagement activities, awareness-raising and risk mitigation achieved through a strong system of ToT whereby Voice of the peace (VOP) will provide training and engage community members in specific techniques for contextualized approaches to strengthen the protective environment for children. VOP will also strengthen caregivers’ coping mechanisms and resilience through thematic workshops as per the needs identified in each micro-community, this is vital to maintain since children directly suffer as a consequence of distress experienced by their parents. Customary negative discipline measures can contribute to further enhancing the sense of insecurity already perceived due to the tension in the surrounded context. To ensure the protection of children throughout the referral pathway, VOP also aims to provide support and training to non-CP actors in the areas of intervention As a holistic protection intervention, the project will directly support 10,450 (3900 adults-1950 men and 1950 women and 6550 children-3475 girls and 3075 boys)
While approximately 70% of the direct beneficiaries are part of the IDP community, an estimated 30% of the host community can be considered as directly targeted too. The objective of this intervention is to provide at least 1000 children with comprehensive case management support (CP and GBV). As stated before, the past has shown that the CFS is an important part of the integrated approach of this project. Therefore, VOP’s child protection interventions aim to reach at least 6,550 children through CBCPSS activities, through accesses to a total of 5 CFS and others. Other direct beneficiaries include all the community members targeted in relation to extending child protection by training adult caregivers and other community members on child protection, child rights, parenting skills and positive discipline methods. More specifically, numbers estimated for direct intervention include 3900 adults through the participation in workshops and regular meetings both physically while observing the Covid -19 SOPs and through social media plat forms like use of community radios and usual radios as part of the CBCPNs, 100 foster caregivers targeted to attend specific training on parenting skills and positive discipline methods. Furthermore, awareness raising activities seek to target at least 5,000 individuals across the population at large between host and hosted community. Organizations

1 - Needs

In Jonglei (GPAA) like other former states affected by the decades of conflicts in South Sudan, Rape and sexual violence among other child protection types have been perpetrated on boys and girls during crisis yet opportunities to access basic life-saving services have been limited. The need for multi-sectoral humanitarian response to address the multiple needs of the population and scaling up of existing services to meet the growing demand of most vulnerable and conflict affected population therefore becomes eminent. Pibor counties of former Jonglei State have experienced multiple displacements and insecurity due to internal conflicts and flooding. Key drivers of conflict in Jonglei, specifically in the above-mentioned counties were identified as food insecurity, flooding, cattle raiding/theft for commercial purposes and rising bride prices. COVID-19 pandemic has worsened the situation affecting the mental health of the children and adolescents with depression and anxiety are prevalent which requires mental health positive copying mechanisms where child protection will be mainstreamed in education to bridge the needs analysis gap.Children continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks which includes, recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, sexual violence, other types of violence and exploitation in accessing basic services such as education and health.Children under 14 often serve as bodyguards, cooks or servants for commanders and other armed groups. While boys are more commonly report to have received orders to kill civilians or loot property, girls are reported to have been used for sexual purposes. In May 2018, UNICEF reported a total of 806 children from the beginning of 2018 that have been released by armed groups, mainly recruited by arm groups most of them in Jonglei State but still many are recruited to date by armed groups. While men often take part in the armed conflict/inter communal violence. Women are mostly the sole breadwinners of the households and are forced to leave their children alone all day while walking long distances to fetch water, collect firewood and wild food. This leads to physical and emotional neglect of the children and increases their exposure to significant protection threats. The collection of water itself has been identified as one of the most dangerous activities which is many times a task given to young girls, exposing them to violence and child abuse. The heavy movements of IDPs during the last years and the lack of resources have increased the number of vulnerable children and, raising the importance of UASC making FTR services.Among the most urgent child protection risks identified are the high number of unaccompanied, separated children, orphans, and women headed families and children-headed households. Also prevalent are high levels of psychosocial distress of the community in general, among both children and adults, given by the high level of insecurity and the spread of various forms of violence. This is exacerbated by the lack of structures able to respond to the current insecurity in Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA)

2 - Project activities

Activities:• Activities related to Comprehensive Case Management - Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Su Identification, documentation, family tracing and reunification services for UASC or families of missing children conducted by CP Case Workers. - Identification of other vulnerable children (including orphans), elaboration of individual case plans, referrals and regular monitoring and follow-up visits. • Provision of safe interim care arrangements for UASC and orphans and training of foster families on principles of interim care provision and positive parenting skills. - Provision of individual material assistance to the most vulnerable UASC and vulnerable children as per their needs. • Facilitation of tracing process in close collaboration with FTR partners and accompaniment of children during reunification flights. Activities related to CBPSS activities in the CFS: - Provision of structured and unstructured PSS activities in the CFS through the use of recreational items (local games and materials) with a focus on improving children’s wellbeing. - Personal hygiene and health promotion activities in the CFS. – • Provision of focus on specialized CBPSS and MHPSS activities for adolescents. Activities related to community engagement activities, awareness-raising and risk mitigation: • Establishment and strengthening of community protection mechanisms through community meetings, awareness sessions, use of community radio talk show, awareness raising through radio social media, FGDs and house-to-house visits including advocacy on the importance of child protection, the rights of the children and signs and symptoms of elevated psychological distress. • Establishment and strengthening of CBCPNs. - Workshops with CBCPNs (parents, caregivers, PTA, authorities and community duty bearers) on children’s wellbeing, needs in different development stages, risks and threats in the community, positive parenting skills through active listening and constructive conflict resolution and strategies to prevent family separation. – • Awareness events, campaigns and public drama sessions on child protection issues facilitated by children and adolescents (girls and boys) attending the CFS to advocate for children’s right, inclusion of the most vulnerable children and community protection. - Initiatives designed and implemented by children and adolescents to improve safety and security in the community and safety mapping exercises.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

VOP staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Child protection and safeguarding trainings are also provided for staff and community structures that work with children. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. VOP staff will be training/ empowering the community on PSEA through community radio, radio talk show and through social distancing community meeting and awareness raising through door to door methodology

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Though installation of several complaint response mechanisms in confidential but accessible places, though use of community child protection volunteers who will use monthly door to door methodology to collect complaints and give feedback and through use of toll free lines which vulnerable people will use to report complaints and receive feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through participatory community engagement meetings, and also awareness and social mobilization campaigns. Children are consulted through participatory, child friendly techniques in safe and protective environments.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project conducts regular gender and age appropriate focus groupDiscussions in order to encourage feedback from the communities. Within VOP supported communities including schools , suggestion boxes exist to allow feedback from children and also feedback of complaint boxes are fixed in easily accessible but confidential places like at churches, trading centers among, schools among others.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

VOP has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the VOP. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the VOP to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. VOP’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as sexual exploitation and abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the VOP will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold IRC accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms
