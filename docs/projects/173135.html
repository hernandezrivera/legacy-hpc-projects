Project Summary

Humanitarian crises disrupt public health systems and increase the risk of the emergence and transmission of infectious diseases. Outbreaks can reach widespread geographical scales when undetected by surveillance and transmission is unmitigated by public health systems.
To support efforts to address this issue, the Government of Libya has established an Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN). The network aims to surveil 12 priority communicable diseases limited to public health facilities. International health regulations recommend community based surveillance in settings like Libya where the surveillance system in place does not cover widely used private health facilities and vulnerable population like migrants who also face significant barriers when trying to access public healthcare, including, but not limited to, stigmatization and discrimination. In the context of COVID-19, it is critical these gaps in health surveillance capacities are addressed to reduce infection risk exposure.
In order to complement the facility based EWARN, IOM have established syndromic and event-based surveillance covering seven priority communicable diseases including COVID-19. These surveillance systems ensure that migrants and other vulnerable populations whom do not visit or have access to public health facilities are screened and reported. Currently report is generated at PoEs, migration transit points monitored by DTM’s Flow Monitoring network and near-by migrant accommodation in 7 municipalities.
This project will built on the previous successful interventions to ensure comprehensive community-based surveillance is well established as per recommendations of international health regulation and provide an additional layer of alert and response mechanism to the existing facility based early warning and alert response network. The interventions in this project aim a. to bring together the information from the native community (key informants, local newspaper, community elders, journalists etc.), b. conduct active surveillance in field as per WHO and IOM event based and syndromic surveillance criteria, c. Investigation and verification of information and signals data by IOM surveillance team, d. develop linkages between surveillance team and rapid response teams of Ministry of Health and jointly conduct investigation and response as per alert and outbreak criteria of MoH approved EWARN criteria developed by WHO. In addition, the IOM protection and shelter teams will also support the IOM medical teams and MOH health authorities in timely response to any potential outbreak.
The community-based surveillance will also support the MoH efforts to conduct contact tracing for COVID patients in the selected municipalities and will ensure that the suspected COVID cases do not further spread the disease. Interventions also include building capacity of MOH staff and local teams on surveillance and response including strengthening of the existing infectious disease unit/ward/beds in each municipality for possible timely referrals in case of any outbreak.
This project aims to Increase access to lifesaving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance with an emphasis on the most vulnerable and early detection and response to disease outbreaks by expanding the community based surveillance sites to more migrant and other displaced population dense areas and to strengthen contact tracing mechanism for COVID 19 cases.

1 - Needs

IOM in collaboration with WHO and Surveillance Administration, NCDC started piloting the community based syndromic and event-based surveillance since 2019. During COVID 19 epidemic the surveillance was further strengthened, and immediate and weekly reporting was carried out at PoEs, migration transit points monitored by DTM’s Flow Monitoring network and near-by migrant accommodation in 7 municipalities. Lesson learnt from the previous project is to strengthen the investigation and response capacity of the IOM team in the field and to develop linkages with MoH rapid response team which currently is limited to reporting from the public health facilities. In current COVID-19 pandemic, the need of community-based surveillance has further increased as this is currently the only way to report the suspected COVID cases from the community and to have contact tracing of COVID-19 confirmed cases. The need to identify and establish isolation spaces for the vulnerable population, appropriate referral mechanisms and strengthening of infectious disease unit/ward/beds is critical. Community based surveillance data shows that male and adult age group is predominant and is consistent with reported figures of migrants, returnees, IDPS and other vulnerable population. During surveillance emphasis is to visit the family of the vulnerable population as well and to include the female, children and elderly if any. Data suggests that everyone is at risk of contracting COVID 19 but survival rate in elderly and the individual having diabetes, cardiovascular disease or any other co-morbidity is less as compared to other population. Active surveillance and contact tracing at the community level ensures that no one is left behind.

2 - Activities

• Active surveillance at POEs, migration transit points, nearby migrant accommodation: Continue with existing ongoing surveillance network reporting from 7 municipalities and further expand the surveillance sites to 5 municipalities. DTM enumerators will be further increased and trained on data recording and reporting. The data entered in electronic devices will generate signals and prompt the DTM surveillance team through a dashboard for signals verification.•Verification, Investigation and response by IOM and MoH surveillance teams: In all 10 municipalities, in addition to the surveillance coordinator (DTM), there will be two surveillance officers (public health officers) who will visit the sites and verify the status of signals/suspected cases received from the enumerators or by field teams • Capacity building of IOM enumerators and surveillance teams and provision of health education to vulnerable population: The enumerators will be trained on syndromic and event based priority communicable disease definitions and electronic data recordings well as to conduct health education sessions. Surveillance officers will be trained on signals verification and generating alerts based on EWARN criteria. IOM will liaise with MoH rapid response team and will investigate cases. If there is an alert or outbreak, the IOM mobile clinics, protection and shelter teams will also support the response efforts of MoH rapid response teams and ensure that all the mitigation procedures are followed. • Strengthening of infectious disease unit/ward/beds and referral mechanism: In each municipality, the existing infectious disease unit/ward/beds will be further strengthened to receive referrals in case of an outbreak.• Contact tracing of COVID-19 cases: enumerators and surveillance officers will be trained to document all the contacts of suspected/confirmed COVID-19 cases and conduct continuous surveillance of the cases as per contact tracing criteria of COVID-19 response strategy.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Accountability to the affected population is a key concern for IOM programming in all its projects, especially in the humanitarian settings. The project will promote a high level of community participation in the implementation of the project including efforts to strengthen the complaint and feedback mechanism for all beneficiaries. IOM’s mobile outreach teams and partner agencies will directly assess the target populations’ needs during the assessment phase. IOM will also collect information on how beneficiaries perceive services provided by IOM and other service providers from the complaint and feedback mechanism jointly developed by partners and integrate the feedback to adapt and improve project activities during the implementation period. The result matrix and the monitoring framework will be utilized to identify the completion of the activities. Monitoring and supervisory tools will be used to recognize areas if any corrective measures are required. IOM with other partners are involved in assisting the Ministry of Health in extending the District Health Information System and its form/tool will be explored for capturing the data on the interventions, beneficiaries and the referral mechanism.
