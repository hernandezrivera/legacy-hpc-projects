Project Summary

As a response to the increasingly needs on the PHC and SHC services, AHF established Alsham medical complex in 2015 as a children's specialized hospital, it is fully equipped hospital with a professional staff so it can deal with complicated cases.
This project will provide the PHC and SHC services to the affected population in northern of Aleppo, which is a crowded region especially after the displacement waves with thousands of IDPs who came from Rural Aleppo city, Damascus, Homs and Deraa and near the hospital there are about 10,000 IDPs in Sijjo camps.
The project will provide these services through supporting the health facility in Salamah town (Sijjo) in northern Aleppo near Azaz, in this project we plan to support supplying the hospital with the required running costs, staff salaries and medications.
We will receive a large number of patients from vulnerable groups (more than 80% of the total target beneficiaries in this project are from this groups) in the hospital, we will provide pediatric consultations, emergency services, hospitalization services, ICU services and other supporting services (Lab, pharmacy and Radiology department).
In the other hand, we will provide the medical consultations to the patients in 6 specialties, also ENT and ophthalmology surgeries will be available in this hospital.
The project will include the provision of the needed medications for the patients, and will provide the Lab tests too.
All these services will be free and we expect to reduce the numbers of patients who cross the border seeking medical care in Turkey.

Needs

As per HNO draft report 2019, 13.2 million people in need. 3.7 male and 9.5 female.A'zaz district is one of the most crowded districts in northern of Syria with an approximately 180,000 persons in need for healthcare assistance according to HNO 2019, with dozens of camps on the Syrian-Turkish border like Bab al-Salama camp and many other camps that were set up for IDPs near Azaz close to the border, including al-Shuhada, al-Rayan, al-Nour, al-Iman, al-Qatari, Sajou, Ehtimilat, Dabek, al-Bal, Shamarekh, Shamarin, al-Rahma, al-Mokawama, Yazi Bagh, al-Risala, Kaer Kalbein, al-Sawameh and Deir Ballout camp, according to A'zaz local council statistics, at least 25,000 families live in these camps, and the district have a number of 155,000 IDPs constituting about 62% of its population, since Izaz District consist of scattered villages and camps with limited access to health, the need for functioning this MUs is highly needed. In November 2017 ACU (The Assistance Coordination Unit) conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in the Euphrates Shield area named "DYNAMO report", this needs assessment was carried out in all eight sub-districts and 17 camps of the ESHA and includes the following sectors, demography, food security, health, WASH, education, shelter and NFI, and sectoral priorities. As such it aims to provide information on the general situation, humanitarian conditions, state of services, and priorities for sub-districts and camps in the ESHA. The main objectives of Euphrates Shield DYNAMO report were:- Providing approximate population figures, including on IDPs, within the assessed sub-districts to facilitate improved response planning by humanitarian actors.- Providing accurate sectoral information to facilitate setting accurate priorities for the assessed sectors.The assessment showed that the Priorities in ESHA were as follows:- Support for medical staff, especially in the Aghtrin, A’zaz, Ar-Ra’ee, and Suran sub-districts.- Setting up new medical centers, especially in Al Bab.- Increased provision of vaccines and medicine, and facilitating their entry into the area from Turkey.- Providing additional medical staff, and especially specialized medical staff, due to the acute shortage in the area.After that in 2018 A'zaz district received tens of thousands of displaced people from the EG, Homs and Daraa through the evacuation waves toward Northern os Syria and that play a great role in increasing pressure on vulnerable health services in the area.
