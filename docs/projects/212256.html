Project Summary

Raqqa is considered one of the areas where no monetary support is provided to people with special needs.
Now in its 13 year, the conflict in Syria Especially in Raqqa has inflicted immense devastation on Syria’s economy, social fabric, and children. Families across Syria face severely
limited employment opportunities, skyrocketing prices, shortages of basic goods and services, and widespread deterioration of household coping mechanisms. The economy has
shrunk to less than 10% of its 2011 level. The value of the national currency has sharply depreciated, and the country is facing hyperinflation, with 1000 % inflation recorded in 2023
Implementation Strategy & Technical Guidance:

0 - Project Summary (extended)

1- Building the capacity of service providers and child protection frontline responders in a sustainable manner, so that they may effectively design, deliver and sustain community-based child protection services which meet minimum standards in the key areas of child protection in emergencies; parenting skills development; case management; protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention, mitigation and response interventions; and mine risk education. Through the PSEA trainings, the professionals that deliver child protection services will continuisely conduct awareness sessions and deliver key messages during all their activities. 2- Strengthening of a formal case management system which allows for the identification and referral of children to specialized medical, psychosocial and other services, as required. Within this system, trained case managers are able to receive the identified girls and boys, develop individual intervention plans and refer them to child protection and other social services, with follow up until the case is closed. The case management of separated children acknowledges a need for interim care centers, family tracing and reunification, and supporting community-level protection committees and networks. This strategic priority is undertaken jointly with MOSAL/SCFPA in order to strengthen the statutory role of key government front line workers. 3-Based on the MHPSS manual, implementing community-based child protection and psychosocial interventions, such as structured and sustainable parenting and child protection activities and programs using recreational tools provided by UNICEF, art / drawing tools, early childhood development tools, sports and music groups. Severe cases requiring specialized interventions will also be referred to other service providers, in addition to the services provided to survivors of GBV. 4- Strengthen the protective environment through advocacy and awareness raising on child protection issues, in particular on the dangers of unexploded ordinances. Communication for Development forms a key element, with community based awareness sessions targeting different segments of the community.
