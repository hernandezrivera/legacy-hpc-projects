Project Summary

This project aims to improve the food security in NW Syria via enhancing the wheat value chain to make them more self-reliant through Agricultural and Livelihood. The project consists of four main components: 1) Supporting the wheat value chain through purchasing wheat seeds from the local harvesting season; 2) Supporting 250 farmers with agriculture inputs (wheat seeds purchased from the local market, fertilizer and pesticides) to support the wheat cultivation in Idleb; 3) Providing training on good agriculture practice, storing, marketing and strategic planning for 250 farmers and staff of the agricultural field Association in addition to members from the local entities at the area of Idleb. Fund is secured.

4 - Needs

An estimate of 3.3 million people, in NW Syria, as 80% of total population is put under food insecurity. 86% of population in Idleb governorate and 59% of population in Aleppo governorate need food assistance. In NW Syria, 2.8 million people up to 65% of total population is IDP. Among these IDPs, 1.5 million people is forced to live in camps, as the most vulnerable. Among them, 80% population is female and children. Since they do not have stable income source, they have to rely on humanitarian assistance. One of the fundamental concerns of Syrian food security is decrease of agricultural production. Agriculture is considered as a main industry in Syria, and around 70% of the labor population in the NW is considered engaging in agriculture. Due to the loss of farming lands and destruction of irrigation facilities caused by conflicts, cultivated acreage has shrunk. Financial and COVID-19 pandemic related crises gave serious impacts on the industry. In NW, inflation and shortage of agricultural equipment, like wheat seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, and fuels made it difficult for farmers to continue. Inadequate technical support regarding pest control hinders effective farming and growth of industry. At some areas, abandoned farming lands are used as IDP camps. Also, shortages of rainfall since fall 2020 and fraud resulted from climate changes influenced negatively to the farming of the region. AAR Japan aims to improve food security in NW Syria through agricultural assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

1st, Selection of Beneficiaries and Target Areas: AAR Japan will select the areas where the restoration of irrigation facilities is going on. Situations of demining in the areas will be considered as well. Regarding beneficiaries’ selection, vulnerabilities, IDP households, female led households, households with single parent, households with PWDs, and households with pregnancy should be prioritized. 2nd, Procurement of Agricultural Equipment and Distribution, AAR Japan shall distribute beneficiaries with wheat seeds, fertilizer, and pesticides to the vulnerable farmers. To make sure that the distributed items are used appropriately, the distribution will be conducted time to time with confirmation of beneficiaries’ cultivation situations by agricultural engineers. 3rd, Cultivation: After distribution of wheat seeds, seeds shall be planted within November. Throughout cultivation period, (7 engineers) each follow up 30-35 farmers.4th. Agricultural Workshop: AAR Japan and HIH shall provide agricultural workshops so that beneficiaries can learn effective methods of cultivation to improve their harvest and continuous farming. 5th, Harvest Survey and Monitoring: harvest survey will be conducted to check the indicators. The results will be used to motivate beneficiaries for next cycle planning. HIH MEAL team will also conduct detailed hearing for samples beneficiaries to track income changes, lessons learned, and feedback.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAR Japan and its partner uphold the 9 commitments of the CHS throughout the project, putting the Code of Conduct in practice and fulfilling its essential responsibilities as humanitarian organizations. To avoid conflict with communities due to misinformation of the services, pre-coordination with local councils shall take place to provide detailed information of the services to the beneficiaries, to ensure that beneficiaries understand their rights. Paying particular attention to women, children, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups, measures such as allocating a female staff in the field team will be taken to facilitate communication with communities and people affected, and to ensure that their feedbacks are properly reflected. Measures will be in place to ensure beneficiaries have safe access to handle their complaints and requests. Leaflets specifying the contact point for complaints will also be handed to each beneficiary receiving the service. All complaints will be addressed in a confidential and appropriate manner.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAR has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEAH (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment) and a responsibility to ensure that all people are treated with dignity and respect. Every staff member working for AAR and our partner organizations must always uphold the highest standards of behavior and accountability with everyone we encounter at all times. AAR ensures that all AAR staff and associates are aware of the high standards of behavior and adopt exemplary behavior to create, strengthen, and maintain healthy environments and minimize the risks of SEAH. This includes, but is not limited to, promoting awareness among AAR staff and associates and people in the communities, conducting safe staff recruitment, building safe projects and programs and ensuring good practice when using media. In case complaints and tip-offs regarding sexual abuse or exploitations are received, the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Officer shall intervene and respond to the case; an investigation team, under the direct chain of command of the Chief Executive Officer, shall report to the PSEA committee, and verify the facts on the case. HIH MEAL team shall report misconducts in the field in accordance with policies on preventing such acts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a precondition, HIH staff members must be able to operate safely in the target areas. The assumed risks are deterioration in security situation, intervention by armed groups, closure of border between Turkey and Syria, restriction of humanitarian activities within Syria by Turkish or Syrian governments, and the outbreak of COVID-19. Depending on the situation in the areas of operations, the project will be suspended till safe operations can be ensured. If no improvement in security situation can be seen, AAR Japan will consider changing access routes and target areas, or ceasing the project. Security assessment will take place before any field activity. To mitigate the risk of the spread of covid-19 in NW Syria, both AAR Japan and HIH have adopted their own policies to prevent Covid-19 infections among the staff members and beneficiaries. All staff members are required to abide by polices and restrictions imposed by the organizations and the local authorities, wear masks and gloves, wash hands on regular basis, measure body temperature, and take other precautious measures as necessary. Regarding agricultural workshops, each session will be provided with under 10 participants. In case of pandemic at target areas, online workshops will be considered. Placing the safety of the staff members and beneficiaries at the utmost priority, field activities will be postponed and rescheduled in case any security concerns are confirmed.
