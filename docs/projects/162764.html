Project Summary

ADRA proposes to work with IDPs SSR and host communities in the following states: Blue Nile State (ElKurmuk, Geissan, Bao), White Nile ( Al jabalen and Al Salam localities ) West Darfur (Krienk and Jabal moon, and Al geniena localities ) Khartoum state (Open areas of SSR in Jabal Awlia and Omdurman ).
ADRA’s approach to provide assistance through interventions focused in WASH. The Intervention of WASH remains the most critical area of need for this program. The focus of this intervention will be the prevention of waterborne disease, that leading to the protection of health of the targeted peoples. The project will support the villages in affected localities by increasing access to safe water sources through rehabilitating and improving the existing water sources, like hand Pumps, Mini water yards dug wells and Hafirs to improve water quality, making it safe for human consumption. The project will also continue to promote good hygiene and sanitation practices to reduce transmission of waterborne and fecal-oral diseases by encouraging people to construct and use latrines to stop open defecation habits through application of community based programs like CATS and CLTS. The CATS and CLTS will are suitable for areas of returnees and host communities in WNS and WDS where open Defecation practices is common.

1 - Needs

Water Supply: the Need of safe water supply is key issue in the targeted areas in WDS, BNS, and WNS, more than 65% of the existing hand pumps are not functional, in BNS and WDS and approximately half of water systems need rehabilitation, creating a major gap in safe drinking water supplies in these target locations. The lack of safe drinking water proper sanitation and poor hygiene are the main cause in spreading of waterborne diseases especially the breaking of cholera in BNS in August 2019. The risk of waterborne disease continues to increase, in 01 October 2019 FMOH and WHO reported breaking of cholera in BNS and Sinnar– according to the (FMoH) the cholera outbreak on 9 September 2019 in the state of the Blue Nile caused a total of 215 suspected cases of cholera were reported including 8 deaths, due to the lack of safe drinking water, less of sanitation coverage and poor hygiene.Due to the rainy season in June - August 2019. The unimproved, unprotected and exposed water sources are susceptible to contamination, including by feces, due to the high prevalence of open defecation. The hand pumps, mini water yards, seasonal Wadis and Haffirs are either broken down, non-functional or insufficient to meet the needs of the growing number of IDPs and hosts in the target locations. The insecurity, shortage of trained technicians and spare parts due to challenges in transport and supply chain have also contributed to the insufficiency of functional water sources. Untreated water from rainfed water catchment facilities coupled with the prevalence of open defecation surrounding the haffirs, use of water sources by both humans and animals, and presence of dead animals has been a major factor in the AWD outbreak in these locations. Hygiene and sanitation: Open defecation is common in the areas surrounding the water sources. The sanitation situation in the localities of Kurmuk, Bau and Geissan is particularly severe, with large market areas, high population movement, including nomadic pastoralists, and large areas with no latrine coverage. The interruption of education has contributed to a decrease in knowledge and awareness of good hygiene and sanitation practices. In Khartoum, at least 58,000 South Sudanese refugees continue to live in dire humanitarian conditions, despite renewed access for partners granted by the Government of Sudan in December 2017. These needs are compounded by underfunding of the inter-agency response plan, the continuous reporting of new comers to Khartoum Open areas and displacement of the refugees in the open area due to conflict with host community had created more needs in water supply and sanitation services. According to July report from UNHCR about 7000 SSR refugees were being displaced from camps in open area due to conflict with Host communities.As sanitation is a key issue in open area, the program will continue using cash for work to construct communal latrines, and HH latrines if the space are is enough.The project will rehabilitate the existed water systems and will add more tap stands in camps and extended water through pipes to cover outskirt of camps and nearby host communities,In WNS the need is more for sanitation, more than 75% of returnees in Jabalen and Al salam locality have no latrine and they defecate in open areas, the project will work closely with the ministries and the local partner to implement the CLTS programs especially in areas covered by newly established water treatment plants in Makhalif and Wad abkaraia.In WDS, the project is targeting to work in Jabalmoon, Krienik and Al geniena localities, will work on rehabilitation of Hand pump, mini water yards, renovating and upgrading of water yards with solar system.In all water and sanitation intervention the hygiene awareness and hygiene promotion, training for hygiene promoters ( especially in Returnees targeted area in Al Al salam and jabalen, - Makhalif and wadabkaria custer group of villages of returnees and host .

2 - Activities / Outputs

ADRA Sudan will concluded the activities in Rehabilitation of existing, non-functional hand pumps, mini water yards and water systems in the targeted localities. In order to rehabilitate the 1- hand pumps, water yards and water systems, ADRA will work closely with line ministry to assure the quality and specification to be attained. ADRA will carry out the implementation of the following activities: for hand pumps ADRA will rehabilitate the handpump and the drainage and fencing for the Hand pump, will rectify and replace all non - function parts of the hand pumps, mini water yards. For water system ADRA will rehabilitate the fencing around each Haffir, rehabilitation of the existing slow sand filter, provision and fixing of pumping units, construction of new water tap stands, provision and fixing of chlorine injectors, rehabilitation of elevated water tanks, connection of pipes to water tap stands, and excavation for a catchment area. 2- Sanitation construction of communal latrines with cash for work, and CAPs , CLTS and CATS proposed to enhance the Sanitation situation in the targeted localities especially the returnees in WNS. 3- Hygiene Promotion: Hygiene promotion will be conducted by program staff in coordination with WUC with technical input and support from the WES and MoH. In each village selected for water supply rehabilitation, the project team and WUC will organize community awareness sessions to focus on hand washing with soap (HWWS) at five critical times: after defecation, before eating, after changing diapers/wiping babies, before food preparation, and before infant feeding. Both male and female promoters will be trained to prompt household level visits with the community and equal interactions with both males and females in households. 3- Training program through consultant with specified curriculum will be conducted for Community Water User Committees (WUC) and hand pumps technicians: The mobilization of affected communities for water point management and cleanliness will be achieved through Water User and Hygiene Committees. Water Use committees will consist of both male and female members in order to ensure that females are equally involved in decision making as well as facilitate equal access to water. 4- WASH NFI: The project will support IDPs and conflict-affected persons with emergency WASH-related NFIs to maximize the benefits derived from improving access to safe water supply and improved hygiene. Each hh will receive 12 bars of soap and 2 x 10 liter jerrycans valued at roughly $45 of value. 5- also CLTS/ CATs for Construction of household latrines in Western Darfur, as well as training of communities in latrine maintenance. 6- Khartoum open area camps ( SSR ) ADRA proposes to construct communal latrines and HH latrine through a program of cash for work the worker will be selected from Youth in camps, training for labor on latrine construction , also special training will be conducted for selected skilled labor for mason works.- water supply system will be rehabilitated , ADRA will construct more tap stands, and supply and fixed bladders or Tiga tanks in areas out skirt of camps in the open area Karrary and Omdurman.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will highly promote the safe and equal access to water, and sanitation services with a special emphasis on preservation of resources, and especially ground water. The project will ensure efficient and sustainable taps to avoid water waste also the project will construct platforms and drainage to reuse the surplus water from tam stand and use it for trees around tap stands . Rehabilitation of water resources will be conducted in consultation with the communities and the environmental consultants to avoid causing harm to neighboring villages. ADRA conducts environmental evaluation prior to all rehabilitative activities, and consults communities on the all rehabilitative activities. Furthermore; Community Water Committees will conduct sessions on safe water usage as well as efficient and safe water storage. Efficient taps will be installed at all water points to avoid water leakage, and community committees will be trained on maintenance of taps. Furthermore, ADRA will conduct a feasibility study on the possibility of solar panel hand pumps in place of fuel powered pumps. Also ADRA will conduct heavy hygiene awareness session for cleaning of latrine from smell and bad odor in communal latrines that will be constructed in the open areas in Khartoum.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ADRA have well established whistle system to protect staff and beneficiaries , In addition, ADRA’s experience implementing in the target area and data from secondary sources highlighted significant challenges for women related to sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR), including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Women and girls remain the primary collectors of water and sometimes must travel more than two kilometers each way for water collection. This is four times more than the 500 meters recommended by Sphere standards. The distances involved in water collection and the proximity of hostile tribes, military and rebels give rise to frequent incidents of SGBV. Around 75 percent of IDPs collect water from unprotected sources that are often contaminated, as most water sources are shared between animals and humans; 80 percent of the population does not treat their water at home ; and 70 percent of the respondents in the target area practice open defecation. Fecal oral disease is endemic. From August 2016 to June 2017, 4,471, cases of suspected cholera/acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and 12 deaths were reported in BNS, although actual numbers were likely higher. Women and girls have limited options to access latrines, either going to a distant neighbor or using open fields with no privacy. This increases the risk of SGBV associated with open defecation and urination, particularly during the night.
