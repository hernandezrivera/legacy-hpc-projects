Project Summary

The project will implement activities to achieve the objective of ensuring all women, men, girls, and boys (including all age groups and persons with disabilities) in the Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host communities have access to WASH services. There will be three main areas of intervention as follows:
Improved access to water services: two small-scale solar-powered piped water networks will be installed in the newly developed areas for the relocated families to provide safe water to beneficiaries. IOM will continue the operation and maintenance (O&M) and upgrade the existing deep tube wells in IOM AoR Camps and water networks in camps 09, 12, 13, 18, 19, 20 extension and 24. In newly developed areas, and for the replacement of non-repairable deep tube wells, some deep tube wells will be installed in camps and host communities to ensure better access to the most vulnerable people in need. Six existing fossil fuel-managed water supply systems in camps 09, 12 and 13 will be upgraded to the solarized system along with the biological filter in camp 24 to reduce fuel consumption. IOM will procure, replenish, and replace the emergency stocks for cyclones, fires, floods, or other emergency responses.
Access to sanitation: to ensure dignified and safe access to the latrine facilities, IOM will continue to upgrade the existing poorly functional latrine facilities with gender-inclusive and disability-related features in consultation with the community. IOM will provide assistive devices (commode chair, commode chair with potty, and urinal bottles for males and females) to people with disabilities or with physical impairments to ensure their specific needs are met based on the assessment. IOM will continue the durable household latrine construction work to cover the sanitation gaps at the targeted host communities in Ukhiya. Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems-DEWATS will be built and the upgrade of lime-based fecal sludge treatment plants to the DEWATS system to meet the coverage gaps will be implemented. In addition, IOM will continue to build six more small-scale Intermediate Sludge Transfer Systems (ISTS) connecting with the FSM units to reduce the sludge transfer cost. To ensure the appropriate waste segregation at the source and engage the community effectively in the chain of Solid Waste Management (SWM), IOM will distribute garbage bins (Red and Green) again to reach each household as the last distribution was in 2020. The complete chain of the SWM monitoring system will be continued and strengthened at all levels.
Hygiene promotion and community engagement: Critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials, etc.) will be provided, with community education and mobilization activities targeting all age groups, genders, and persons with special WASH needs at the community level. IOM will continue the SCOPE distribution system in all IOM camps to better assist with hygiene kits for people in need. MHM kits will be provided to the reproductive age group (10-55 years) based on the field assessment and needs across all the IOM-managed camps. The community will be furthermore engaged in O&M of WASH facilities, waste segregation, and prevention of communicable and waterborne diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Scabies, COVID-19, etc. Real-time service monitoring and community feedback mechanisms will be continued and strengthened throughout the project period.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Needs of refugees Water Services: There is a total of 66 water networks, with 14 more to be completed (10 not started) in IOM’s area of responsibility (AoR), and some mini networks serving 76% of the population. IOM water quality surveillance shows that a total of 52% of tap stands were found functional, 46% with repair needs but functional, and 2% completely non-functional. IOM has also found that 35% of the water networks do not comply with sector standards on Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) presence at distribution points. Access to water supply in Teknaf remains a major challenge during the dry season with water trucking required again during 2021. Frequent repairing and maintenance of handpumps are necessary to keep the water supplies for meeting the daily needs of the targeted beneficiaries. It was experienced during the fire outbreak at Camp 9 that a rapid provision of water supply or a viable source of drinking water with supplemented water purification tablets distribution is necessary. Sanitation: Latrine coverage at camp level provides access at a ratio of 1 latrine to 20 people (in line with sphere standards), but block-level data show that at least in 5 of the 12 camps (9, 10, 11, 13, 24) that minimum standard is not achieved due to the high population density. The current functionality rate of sanitation facilities is on average 92% and is a continual process for partners. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86% of households reporting private bathing space inside but often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been undertaken to install women-friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. A continual concern regarding sanitation is the coverage of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements, or scale-up. SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated areas and 27% of household report garbage near their shelters. Hygiene promotion and community engagement: Community engagement strategies will focus on changing identified high-risk behaviors through participatory approaches and capacity building, such as safe water usage and solid waste containment reducing waste entering the surrounding environment. Strengthened hygienic practice monitoring and follow up, focusing on vulnerable people through increased cross sector linkages with Health & Education with epidemiological evidence of WASH-related diseases will guide the response for meeting needs for critical supplies, such as soap, water containers, and MHM materials. Needs of host communities As of August 2022, 284,309 host community members are accessing safe water for domestic needs. Twenty-six per cent of host community households report not having enough water to meet domestic needs. Most of the host community relies on deep tube wells as the main water source, the second most common source is shallow tube wells. Over the last few years, the use of piped water has increased in the host community. In Ukhia, 80% of the host community is covered by improved sanitation services. The most common sanitation facility for host communities is a pit latrine with slab, used by 63% in Ukhia. Host communities have been less supported by hygiene items and awareness-raising initiatives but surveys show encouraging data as 93% of the households reported having access to soap

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM will continue working with the modality of one implementation partner (IP) per camp to deliver all the WASH services to the beneficiaries. Intensify community engagement in the coming year. WASH user groups and block-level WASH committees will be strengthened and build up their technical skills and equip them with the necessary equipment so that community people can do the minor repairing of the WASH facilities and keep them functional in the longer run. Participation of women, men, boys, girls, person with disability, and the elderly will be ensured in the user groups and WASH committee to have an inclusive programme. In this way, more ownership among them will grow and sustainability will be ensured. IOM will reduce 25 % of the operation and maintenance cost for the WASH services for all the AoR camps next year. To make sure WASH services are provided as per the requirement of the beneficiaries and meet the standards one camp monitoring and coordination staff will continue working per camp. The main responsibilities of the camp monitoring and coordination staff will be to coordinate with all the sectors, CiC, DPHE etc., monitor the WASH services quality, and ensure technical support to the implementing partner. IOM will upgrade fuel-run water networks into solarized water supply systems in the next year to reduce the operational cost and negative impact on the environment.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Going out to crowded distribution centers is always a challenge for women, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Considering the difficulties to access the centralized distribution centers, IOM will continue distributing all the NFI WASH items to the household level by using the SCOPE system to meet the exact needs. To reduce GBV and protection risks, IOM WASH will ensure WASH staff, IP volunteers, and hygiene promoters are trained on gender sensitivity, Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and SEA referral pathways to make an appropriate response. After consultation with users especially with women and girls IP will construct privacy fencing to ensure safety and privacy of the users.

3.2 - Gender

IOM will be improving and upgrading the WASH facilities to ensure equitable access by women, men, girls, and boys inclusive of all age groups and persons with disabilities in consultation with the users. Implementation Partners will be forming and strengthening the MHM user committees at the camp level to get more information about the accurate needs of undergarments sizes, pads, and other feedback for the project.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Upgradation of existing latrines and bathing facilities containing unified disabilities and the elderly features, and will increase the access and practice of improved sanitation. Implementing partners will continue reaching children and different age groups to strengthen the hygiene practices at all levels through different innovative modalities. IOM and implementing partners will continue working to enable equitable and appropriate access to all the WASH services for all age groups.

3.4 - Diversity

IOM and implementing partners will be working to build the capacity of female and male staff and community members on hygiene promotion and practice monitoring to ensure the needs of women, men, boys, girls, LGBTI, the elderly, and persons with mental and physical disabilities. IOM and implementing partners will continue working on environmental protection through integrated ways of solid waste management for instance door to door waste collection, compost production, support plantation programme, wrap-less soap distribution, etc.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM will build capacity for all IOM WASH staff and IP staff on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) through facilitation of training to ensure the effective engagement of the communities at all phases of the WASH programme. The Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is already established and will continue to receive complaints from the affected community, IOM WASH staff and IP staff will make sure of timely and appropriate responses to address the issue and will get back to the community for updating.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, IOM will upgrade 6 existing fossil fuel-managed water supply systems in camps 09, 12, and 13 to the solarized system to reduce fuel consumption and at the same time reduce the negative impact on the environment. Solid waste management is one of the big issues in camps to minimize negative impacts on the environment and keep the camp environment clean and healthy for the camp dwellers. Implementing partners will continue operating MRF to manage solid wastes. Continue collecting wastes from households and transfer them to the MRF site, later on, convert organic wastes into compost, send inorganic to sanitary landfill, and recyclable wastes to scrap dealers. Distribute compost to camp dwellers and host community for homestead gardening or vegetation. IOM will continue testing influent and effluent from faecal sludge treatment facilities (FSTP) and will take corrective action to meet the standards.
