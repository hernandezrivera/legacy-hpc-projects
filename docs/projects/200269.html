Project Summary

Medair’s health strategy is in line with the Lebanese Crisis Response Plan (LCRP) and the Emergency Response Plan (ERP) aiming to improve access to primary and community-based health care with the overall aim of reducing morbidity and mortality amongst vulnerable Lebanese. Medair has a strong partnership with the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) and works in partnership with the ministry to ensure both access to the most vulnerable people whilst building into the longer-term sustainability of the health systems within Lebanon including implementation of LPSP since mid-2022 under the MoPH PHCC network. Medair has worked collaboratively with the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA) since 2014, supporting health centres in Social Development Centres where refugees and the most vulnerable populations without insurance tend to access health care, and has upgraded 4 of these centres to meet the MoPH PHCCs integration criteria with support of EU MADAD and GAC Canada until 2022. In 2022, in partnership with UNICEF and DG-ECHO, Medair worked with 18 health centres, conducting health outreach into the surrounding communities and running an immunisation project in the South & Nabatieh. With the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021 and 2022, Medair provided vaccines via a mass vaccination centre and VaxBuses (partially supported by DG-ECHO) in the Informal Settlements (IS). In November 2022, Medair began responding to the cholera outbreak through community outreach and vaccination, with 113 teams of mobile vaccinators in Bekaa, Baalbek, and the North, adapting the model of the VaxBus to conduct doing door to door immunisations.
Health activities that are aligned under the health sector of the ERP include the provision of medication, PPE provision and IPC training for health centres, integrated messaging through community health volunteers (CHVs) and provision of dignity kits. The CHVs are focused on reaching Lebanese populations and will be mobilised from pre-existing Medair networks, and while the remaining number will be newly recruited (with additional CHVs as part of the LCRP). These Community Health Volunteers will reach all populations within their geographic areas of intervention. Medair also aims to provide medication including testing devices with appropriate consumables for people diagnosed with diabetes. Medication will be provided free of charge to all those accessing the centres. Medair will also provide transportation to enable vulnerable people to access health services including PwSN, those with a chronic illness and PLW who all have reduced physical mobility (using buses to reach out to the community). Medair’s PSS volunteers will provide flyers and short awareness sessions on these buses. Medair will support 4 centres with IPC and PPE. Training and coaching health centre staff in adherence to IPC practices including appropriate use of PPE. Mediar will also focus on enhancing quality of care and protection and will identify and train medical staff on safe identification and referral of GBV survivors.
In the past, Medair’s health and shelter teams have jointly worked with the health facilities to ensure the continuation of health services, including WASH and solar options – both fridges for cold chain, and for the whole centre to enable lights, equipment and the internet (to access PHENICS) to function. Medair is therefore well placed to provide integrated health, shelter and WASH solutions.
Medair is an active part of the health sector, and has close working relationships with both the Ministry of Public Health, the Ministry of Social Affairs and other key stakeholders listed in the ERP.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Medair inducts all staff, casuals and volunteers on PSEA and other protection risks, and follows a standard schedule of refresher training, including the use of UN online modules. Medair has an active Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team, who conducts regular monitoring, including exit surveys at health facilities, assessments of health centres and post-implementation quantitative and qualitative monitoring to actively check if there is any cause for concern. Medair publicises the hotline through posters and distribution of hotline cards, with an established SOP and mechanism for the handling of complaints and feedback. Any complaints are investigated as appropriate, with the support of HR and Medair's Global Support Office as needed. Medair is a signatory to the IASC PSEA guidelines, and is audited for PSEA by UNHCR to ensure compliance and good practice. Protection, gender and social inclusion are considered in programming to ensure equitable access.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Medair's is fully committed to AAP, with MEAL team working with the project teams to ensure that the different aspects of AAP are implemented. This includes availability of information, through flyers and posters, including information about services available, costs and expectations of behaviour. It also includes participation of communities both in the design of projects, such as focus groups when developing projects, and ongoing monitoring and evaluation. The feedback system includes a hotline, via both WhatsApp and mobile lines, and via field staff, with SOPs implemented for following up and responding to calls/feedback. Learning is integrated into projects by both informal communication, regular reports and lessons learned events. AAP is identified within the country strategy, with dedicated sessions in inductions and ongoing trainings. Medair uses RIMS as a common platform that enables organizations working in all sectors and across all areas of Lebanon to coordinate referrals.
