Project Summary

In this project, Ataa is planning to target children and youth as follows:
• Aleppo governorate: Atareb, Daret Azze, Jarablus, Al Bab sub-districts.
• Idleb governorate: Dana, Harim, Salqin, Teftnaz sub-district.
The schools located in Aleppo are 10 off-camps whereas there are 19 schools located in Idleb, 14 of them are in camps while the other 5 schools are off-camps. The proposed project will target children who are in the basic and secondary education (1st & 12th grade) including CwDs along with vocational education to the most vulnerable students based on selection criteria which will be developed later. These communities prioritized based on 1) its high severity on HNO, 2) the concentration of new IDPs, 3) huge needs for education, 4) the security stabilization, 5) accessibility by ATAA 6) high rates of severe illiteracy and dropout in basic education especially among children of secondary and tertiary education.
In this project, ATAA’s concern is towards the dropped-out children, those at the risk of being dropped out and the newly displaced as a result of frequent displacement and difficult living conditions. The project will provide FE & NFE opportunities including TVET for 12553 children (Female; 6390, Male; 6163) as follows 545 ECCD, 8900 FE, 2540 NFE, 168 CWD, 400 TVET in addition to 623 educational personnel includes headteachers, teachers, cleaners and guards (Female; 283, Male; 340), the percentage of students (51% females and 49% males), the percentage of staff (45% females and 55% males).
The first component of the project will focus on the construction and rehabilitation in which 4 TECs will be constructed, each TEC includes 5 classrooms and one administrative room along with complete WASH facilities from prefabricated materials, the other 25 learning spaces will be provided with minor rehabilitation and school furniture as solar panel, whiteboard, desks, tables, cupboards to be friendly environments. In addition, visual aids, textbooks, recreational, ECCD and school in box materials will be provided. Recreational and CP activities considering the INEE minimum standards along with teacher students’ ratio will be there.
FE ( SIG curriculum) will be provided and NFE (Curriculum B, BLN and SLP) to those who have been out of school more than one academic year based on age and period of dropping out, TVET will be given to the dropped out and OOSC who are between 14 and 20 years since they are at the risk of being recruited to armed groups, early marriage and child labour. There will be an MoU signed with ED to recognize the students who got NFE in order to be referred to FE. Winterization and individual support activities will be there also.
CP will be given and tailored in the targeted locations; referral mechanism will take place in the targeted areas both within ATAA’s service network and externally to other NGOs and providers. ATAA will pay attention to reach to OOSC through using life-saving protection-oriented implementation modalities, school safety will be established when the construction starts to ensure all learning spaces comply with minimum standards.
Gender equality and inclusion of CWDs will be promoted ensuring the learning spaces are accessible by boys, girls. CWDs will be supported with tailored activities according to their needs. Educational activities will be community-driven and children’s participation will be encouraged in line with ATAA child safeguarding policy. ATAA aims to build resilience in individuals to protect children, families and to enable them to face challenges using their own resources and capacity.
Capacities of staff will cover FE and NFE, regular assessment to teachers by supervisors to provide them with training as TICC, PSS, Child Safeguarding, SLP, BLN, Inclusion to increase quality education. BTL campaigns will be conducted to increase awareness in which rate of dropping out decreases. ATAA will invest in strengthening community relationships in the schools by PTA.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2019, the last displacement waves have put the current infrastructure in both Idleb and Aleppo under enormous pressure. 2.6 million children have been displaced and over 245,000 education personnel in need of education assistance. An estimated 2.1 million children almost more than one third of school-age children became out of school and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out in IDP/returnee receiving communities which have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students, causing overcrowding, while in most communities, education services are deeply insufficient or nonexistent.According to JENA Dec 2019, 34% of reached students (582,239 children) are out of school and the findings show that the percentage of OOSC increases in higher educational levels. However, the main factors contributing to children's return to schools are “the provision of suitable and safe educational environment, provision of separated gender schools for female and male students, provision of catch-up classes for students to catch up with their peers in the grades commensurate with their ages. ATAA conducted an RNA in Feb 2020 for some villages in districts (Harim, Idleb, Afrin, Jebel Saman, Jarablus, Al Bab) to reflect how far the education status has been affected by the current conflict. The findings were as follows: 17% of the schools need moderate rehabilitation, the enrollment of ECCD is around 30%, BE and secondary enrollment is around 50% while it decreases to 10% in the high school and tertiary education. The main reasons behind the non-enrolment of different school-aged children are low income, child labor and the multi-displacement. The project will address incentives, textbooks, stationery, school rehabilitation, WASH facilities, provision of heating materials, in formal, non-formal and vocational education and targeted beneficiaries are 12553 children (Female; 6390, Male; 6163) as follows 545 ECCD, 8900 FE, 2540 NFE, 168 CWD, 400 TVET.

5 - Implementation of activities

ATAA will carry out the direct implementation through coordinating with all stakeholders (Education Cluster & Directorates, local communities, and parents). Ataa develops the capacities of its staff through providing different curricula: FE & NFE (SIG curriculum and Curriculum B along with catch up classes) in addition to TVET. Construction and rehabilitation will be very effective considering the schools as friendly environments for all students, especially for CWDs in which ramps will be established, latrine will be equipped with special tools to facilitate the needs of CWDs.Enrollment of students along with BTL campaigns will be there. Students, recreational, ECCD and teacher kits, winterization, individual support, transportation will be provided. Baseline and end-line ASER will be conducted to students in the schools. Conducting a complete assessment to the teachers by education supervisors provide the needed training as TICC, PSS, CP, Child Safeguarding, SLP, BLN, Inclusion to increase quality education. Hygiene promotion and safety school activities will be conducted to increase the awareness of both students and communities. CP activities and the awareness raising sessions will be conducted integrated within education such as PSS, life skills, Social Emotional Learning and the referral mechanism will be activated at each school.Teachers Learning Circles (TLCs) & Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs) will be established and activated on a monthly basis. ATAA will establish feedback and complaint mechanism using suitable methodologies for handling and receiving all complaints.Concerning MEAL: Checklists to monitor TECs, WASH facilities and rehabilitation of the existing ones and verify their quality and suitability for the disabled students, school safety will be measured by school safety checklist, educational trainings will be measured by both KAP survey and training evaluation and PDM will be conducted after each distribution. C&F tracker will be activated

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1):Incidents of abuse or violence against children by ATAA’s personnel, partners and service providersThe Mitigating:1-Mainstreaming the child safeguarding policy, including the child abuse violations reporting mechanism2-Training the personnel on child safeguarding policy, and the regarding code of conduct, and requiring all ATAA`s workers, partners, and service providers/suppliers to sign a policy commitment and code of conduct including penalty proceduresRisk (2): The risk of tension between caregivers due to forecasting on support number of children and excluding others through individual support and short case management.The Mitigating:-Defining clear and specific criteria to be understood and appreciated by all student caregiversRisk (3): Difficulty reaching of the children with disabilities to the learning spaces:Mitigating Measure:-Design school facilities to suit the needs of children with disabilities, including toilets and stairs-Providing short case management services (IPA) to facilitate the access of children with disabilities to education, including transportation costs.Risk (4):The children drop out of education, and direction towards child laborThe Mitigating:- Back-to-school campaigns- Providing short case management services (IPA) to facilitate the access of children with disabilities to education, including referral to the specialist case management teams.Risk (5):Incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse including workplace harassment by ATAA`s personnel, its partners, and service providers/suppliers towards students, their families, and the other indirect beneficiaries in the community.The Mitigating:-Mainstreaming the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse policy (PSEA), including awareness-raising sessions, the complaint mechanism, and recruitment proceduresRisk (6):Mixing different age groups in the same learning center/class:The Mitigating:Create separate times of attendance for each age group

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation, there might be a risk of shelling by the regime and its allied forces. • Ensuring that the learning spaces are in safe locations as much as possible • Suspension of schools during flyovers or insecurity• Training will be conducted for the teachers to instruct the students about safety issues.The security situation in Northern Syria may prevent project staff from travel or cause other problems for implementation• Putting in place an emergency plan• Having a field officer at each learning space who will be linked into local networksContinued risk of harm to children if negative pedagogical practices are not remedied through improved education personnel practices.• Ensure that teacher and education personnel trainings address assessed priority needs that focus on the well-being of the child.• Better ensure application of training content through pre-post training follow-up and Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism due to additional employment and/or leaving their jobs to seek higher paying work.• A standardized and harmonized stipend scale Lack of support for the project by the local community• Raise awareness of project in local community through regular communicate and Local Council communication.Delay or inability to deliver supplies • ATAA will be encouraged to locally purchase project supplies.• ATAA will liaise with local authorities and communities prior to distributions to ensure they are received in a safe and timely way.Loss of finance or other compliance documents due to bombardment, seizure/destruction of computers/files, etc.• ATAA will transfer soft copy scans and move hard copies of project and compliance data on a frequent basis.Ensuring no funds arrive at any entities supporting terrorism, the S&S Department will conduct a security check for all services providers to ensure they are not connected to any armed group in addition to transferring money by PTT only
