Project Summary

Out of the 923,000 refugees gathered in Cox´s Bazar, 55% are children, all highly exposed to neglect, abuse, trafficking, distress and other forms of right violation. Their forced displacement also hampered their chance for a better life, severely affect their rights to protection from all forms of abuse, violence and exploitation. There is disproportionate risk of gender-based violence, including domestic violence, forced/early marriage, sexual exploitation and trafficking, especially for adolescent girls.
This 12-month project targets about 6000 refugee and host community children and adolescents in Cox´s Bazar, who are deprived from access to basic protection services, and 3155 of their adult care givers, families, educators and community members. Our innovative strategy intends to promote a comprehensive community-based approach focused on Child Protection. The project aims at generating sound understanding of child protection issues among children, adolescents and adults in order to create efficient community-based child protection mechanisms and properly identify and refer cases of abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation.
The project will count on an active participation of children and adolescents themselves and community members including girls and women, through capacity building actions and by involving them in activities implementation as frontline educators, trainers and facilitators.
EDUCO will ensure operational coordination for optimal implementation of its project by participating in regular meetings of the CXB Child Protection sub-sector and the Protection Sector. EDUCO will also use its participation in these working groups to promote joint interventions with other partners working in the same sector and to share information on the implementation of its activities, focusing on the impact achieved on beneficiaries.

Needs

• Family structures have been significantly disrupted by violence and forced displacement.• Children face serious protection risks including psychosocial distress, neglect, abuse, separation from caregivers, child marriage, child labor and trafficking. • Adolescents boys and girls and youth face specific risks as they do not have access to education and self-resilience opportunities• Disproportionate risk of gender-based violence, including domestic violence, forced/early marriage, sexual exploitation and trafficking, especially for adolescent girls.• Adolescents are taking on economic and family responsibilities, which pose barriers to their participation in traditional forms of educational provision. • Adolescents are taking on economic and family responsibilities, which pose barriers to their participation in traditional forms of educational provision.• There is poor of mobility for girls, limiting girls' access to any education opportunities or social interaction. • Harmful traditional practices, such child marriage are highly prevalent. Apparently higher incidence of child marriage in the camp site.• High level of domestic violence against children.• Weak coordination between Child-Friendly Spaces and Learning Facilities, both providing similar activities with less focus on psychosocial or learning outcomes and more on recreational activities.• Weak link between different sectors, particularly between education and child protection. No comprehensive package for holistic development for children

Activities or outputs

Results - 1: Children and adolescent below 17 are protected from violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse by strengthening community-based child protection mechanism Activity #01: Community-Based Child Protection Mapping assessment to identify sub-vulnerable groups: girls, children with disability, child headed household etc., as well as identifying local capacities and processes when dealing with child protection issues within the community. Activity #02: Capacity building program for community leaders on child protection issues, as part of a community package including child protection (early marriage, child labour, domestic violence), as well as health, wash and education messages. Including the development with community members a referral mechanism.Activity #03: Community/youth led awareness campaign on EVAC (Ending Violence against Children) conducted on a bi-yearly basis.Activity #04: Home visitation program (“tea/coffee sessions”) to raise the awareness of community members on various child protection issues and services, parenting session, etc.Activity #05: Support monthly meeting of community-based child protection mechanism. Activity #06: Develop with adolescent girls and boys safe and anonymous reporting referral mechanism including exploring e-solutions. Result 2 :Crisis-affected refugee and host community children and youth have increased life-saving, life-sustaining skills (and personal resilience) and the use of safe referral pathways. Activity #01: Training of frontline educators (instructors, facilitators and tutors) on life-saving and life-sustaining skills, psychosocial support, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse, disaster risk reduction, safe identification and referral mechanisms for child protection issues. Activity #02: Training and sensibilization for adolescents on life-saving and life sustaining skills, psychosocial support, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse, child marriage, child labor, disaster risk reduction, among other child protection issues through the e-learning programme/app (developed under R1) Activity #03: Build the capacity of (enrolled education) children on how to identify and safely access child protection case management services
