Project Summary

The project will provide emergency agriculture and livestock-based livelihoods interventions and resilience building support for vulnerable farming and pastoral IDPs, returnees, and hosting communities in South, North, West, East, and Central Darfur states. Vulnerability of the target populations stems from the protracted crisis, the ongoing economic hardships at the macro and micro-levels and recent poor harvest because of localized droughts and pest infestations. This situation has led to high rates of acute and chronic food insecurity and malnutrition. FAO prioritized in a participatory manner 44 localities in the five states of Darfur based on the high degree of vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition.
This project will target 925,000 people among the most vulnerable communities including 277,500 returnees, 185,000 IDPs, and 462,500 residents. These interventions will benefit 283,050 women, 185,000 girls (less than 18 year of age), 271,950 men and 185,000 boys(less than 18 year of age). The proposed interventions are based on the basic and specific needs of the beneficiaries taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will target about 35,000 elderly people among the adult population including persons with disabilities.
The project will follow the FSL Sector Strategy that follows the Multi-year Humanitarian Strategy adopted by the Humanitarian Country Team in Sudan for the period 2017-2020. The FSL Sector Strategy entails providing livelihoods-saving interventions and addressing the root causes of food insecurity and malnutrition to tackle all aspects of food security, including physical and economic access, availability and utilization of food and the stability of food systems through complimentary emergency life-saving and resilience building through agricultural and livestock-based livelihood interventions and income generating activities.
The project objectives are aligned with the FSL Sector objectives 1, which includes addressing immediate lifesaving humanitarian needs through emergency agricultural assistance including crop, livestock and veterinary inputs and extension services targeting 492,800 people. This will include as well building the capacity of governmental institutions and sector partners to improve preparedness and response for new emergencies. Under FSL Sector objective 2, the sector aims to restore and improve food and nutrition security and enhance self-reliance of the most vulnerable communities to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. Agricultural production will be improved through provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small scale farmers will be targeted for reduced food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities. On and off farm livelihood assistance will be diversified and managed and coordinated to respond to food chain threats including animal and plant pests and diseases; In addition, communities will be involved in building, restoring and maintaining productive assets through cash and/or food for assets schemes. About 432,200 people (3,000 people will be targeted for cash transfer) will be targeted under Sector objective 2. Accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities to reduce risk to GBV, respond to the needs of the target population, promote climate change adaptation measures and ensure implementation of environment-friendly interventions.

1 - Needs

The 2020 HNO identifies 6.2 million individuals in Sudan that are in need of timely and adequate food and livelihoods security assistance, an increase from 5.7 million individuals in 2019. Out of the 6.2 million people in need, 4.7 million will be targeted under the FSL sector, an increase from 4.4 million compared to 2019.The overall objective of the Food Security and Livelihoods sector is to support improved food and livelihood security of conflict and disaster affected, vulnerable, and small-scale farming households. Food insecurity has major consequences on individuals’ physical and mental well-being, and living standards impacting their health and nutritional status. FAO efforts in Darfur are directed towards the overall improvement in the resilience vulnerable communities to adopt less negative and irreversible coping strategies. FAO will focus its response on households most vulnerable to food insecurity, addressing constraints in inadequate consumption of sufficient and diversified nutritious food, as well as limited and unstable livelihoods and productive capacities. Emergency livelihood and resilience building assistance will ensure access to food and income targeting 925,000 vulnerable individuals among the farming and pastoral communities including 468,050 women and girls, and 456,950 men and who fall under IPC phase 3 and 4. FAO in 2020 will focus on two main objectives to address the needs of the three main vulnerable groups identified under the HNO: i) To ensure man-made and natural disaster affected individuals receive timely livelihoods emergency assistance. Under this objective, FAO will target the needs of vulnerable individuals from the host, internally displaced and returnee communities who face levels of food insecurity as a result of limited accessibility and availability of adequate, sufficient and nutritious food ii) to improve availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis who suffer from food insecurity levels for enhanced resilience: targeting vulnerable individuals from the host, internally displaced, and returnee communities who face levels of food insecurity and small-scale farmers who face major constraints in farming because of high costs of production, post-harvest losses, access to farm lands, markets and information. Their needs are linked to access to improved agricultural and livestock inputs, enhanced capacities on good agricultural and veterinary practices and services, reduced post-harvest and animal losses, access to credit, etc. The FAO response for Darfur will focus on the five states including 44 localities with high levels of food insecurity identified through the IPC acute food insecurity analysis. The prioritized response was identified by FAO Rapid Needs Assessments conducted in five Darfur states in late 2019. Lack of certified seeds, extension activities, and veterinary and livestock services are among the most serious challenges behind low crop and livestock production in the traditional system. Identified needs included certified seeds, hand tools, ploughs, veterinary drugs and vaccines, animal feed, mineral licks, IGAs and live animals. Water for livestock use and farming, fuel efficient stoves and training activities on improved crop and livestock production are needed as well. There is a need for communal assets such as grain stores, hafirs, shallow wells and climate smart agricultural structures for soil and water management. Rural women represent more than 50% of the farming population in the rain fed sector in Darfur. Women carry out agricultural and small animal activities. This project will respond to the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will support environment-friendly interventions including climate change adaptation measures. GBV risk reduction measures and cash intervention will be mainstreamed in the project.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Like many other countries, Sudan in general and Darfur in particular, is experiencing more frequent droughts, floods and storms causing damage to the environment and people's livelihoods. Poorly managed livestock is much more implicated in GHG emissions and its intensity varies between regions causing climate change phenomena. The project will contribute effectively to the efforts that can build resilience and prepare for and adapt to climate change through some mitigation measures that are explained below: Increasing livestock productivity through supplementary feeding interventions will help in reducing the number of nonproductive animals which bear full responsibility in increasing emissions; Reducing the prevalence of diseases and parasites through the animal health component would eventually reduce emissions intensity as healthier animals are more productive and thus produce lower emissions per unit of output; Adoption of climate smart agriculture techniques by targeted trained farmers will result in incorporation of manure into soils which can reduce emissions while maintaining farm productivity; Pasture rehabilitation and tree plantation activities will store more carbon into the soils; Training the target groups on sound disposal of carcasses would reduce environmental pollution due to flooding.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will consider the protection of people depending on their needs and requirements, the project will provide suitable interventions that improve women’s capacity to produce all or part of good-quality food requirements and generate cash income for their families, contributing to empowerment and social protection of women, especially women headed households. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout all steps of this project design and preparation and will be directly involved in the implementation processing including beneficiaries selection, registration and monitoring of the implementation at the field and beneficiaries level. As explained in the above paragraphs, FAO follows the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas. The project is considering protection of women depending on the social environment in the mentioned areas. They will provide the training and input distribution in a suitable location, raise the awareness on GBV and use of fuel-efficient stoves to avoid firewood collection in far unsafe places.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

FAO accountability towards affected people starts from selection of types, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The identified inputs are selected in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FAO encourages the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result. FAO provides assistance to the beneficiaries for the formation of local committees and training of its members so that they can play an active role in the implementation and monitoring phases. Any beneficiary complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs will be captured during the monitoring missions. FAO shows continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FAO urged all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will be on enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources.
