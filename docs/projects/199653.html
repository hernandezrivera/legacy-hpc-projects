Project Summary

The results of the Joint Assessment Mission (JAM) conducted in 2021 by WFP and UNHCR reveals that most refugees’ camps in Chad identified the lack of regular school meals programme as one of the main reasons for children not attending school, after the limited number of classrooms and lack of school equipment. One of the main demands made by refugees and their host communities to help tackle the current malnutrition crisis and school dropout rates for both refugee and host community children is the introduction of a programme of regular school meals in the 213 schools in the camps and host villages.. Equal access to a school feeding package will enable refugee children to learn, thrive and develop their potential, build individual and collective resilience, and contribute to peaceful coexistence and civil society.
Under Strategic Outcome 1 in the WFP Country Strategic Plan (2019-2023), , WFP aims to support crisis-affected people in targeted areas to be able to meet their basic food and nutrition needs during and in the aftermath of crises. Under this outcome, WFP will implement school meals for crisis-affected populations and host communities in crisis-affected areas. The WFP school meals programme objectives are in support of the implementation of the Government of Chad’s National Policy of School Feeding since 2017.
In line with the strategy of the Ministry of National Education of Chad, schools in refugee camps are integrated into the Chadian education system. WFP plans to distribute nutritious and diversified hot meals procured from the local community where possible, through the promotion of local farmers to supply schools according to the basket and the quantities established. The school menus will be enriched with fresh products from vegetable gardens according to seasonality. Food procurement from local sources close to the project area will increase the income of smallholders, leading to an increase of their productivity and investment. In addition, better access to economic opportunities will contribute to the economic growth of the household.
In addition, WFP also links with its resilience activities inside refugee camps in Ouaddaï, Lac and Sila, which focus, in particular, on local purchase of food or joint planning for a friendly school feeding environment (kitchen, storage space, water and sanitation) inside schools.
To promote social cohesion between refugee and indigenous communities, meals will be distributed to refugee as well as host community students attending schools in refugee camps without any distinction of origin. In addition, during the implementation of activities, WFP will ensure that the canteen management committees will be made up of both local and refugee populations at the level of all decision-making and participation bodies.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Children in conflict-affected countries are more than twice as likely to be out of school, and specifically girls in conflict-affected countries are 2.5 times more likely to be out of school than girls in stable contexts. More than half of school-age refugees are reported to be out of school (estimated at 53%) in Chad and, according to the UNHCR education dashboard of November 2021, about 92,891 refugee children are enrolled in schools but there is no guarantee of their attendance and retention. The UNHCR/WFP 2021 Joint Assessment Mission (JAM) showed refugee sites recording up to 19.3 percent of acute malnutrition in children under five and 42 percent of chronic malnutrition. According to the latest 2022 SMART survey, a total of 3 out of 23 provinces are experiencing high acute malnutrition rates over the 15 percent emergency threshold. The findings of the JAM highlight the need for promotion of access to education, and the need for a regular school feeding program, among refugee population and host villages to overcome the current malnutrition crisis, and encourage attendance and retention of students in such a context. Poor school attendance poses potential protection risks to children of school going age. The JAM assessment findings showed that a growing number of women, girls and boys are exposed to protection risks as they adopt negative coping strategies (sex for survival, early marriage, recruitment into extremist groups, economic migration, exploitation of children, sexual exploitation etc.) to cope with food insecurity, unemployment, and lack of means of subsistence. This includes several cases of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), such asrape of women, early or forced marriages, teenage pregnancies, or withdrawal of girls from school. A school feeding program is seen as a potential incentive towards increasing attendance and retention of children in school and may also serve as a food insecurity safety net for most poor households. This may also serve as an opportunity to increase access to essential nutrients that would minimize risks for macro and micronutrient deficiencies among school going children.Specifically, for Refugees in Chad, school feeding interventions allow for targeting of children, potentially compensating for and/or complementing the general household food assistance that is often affected by funding shortfalls. As schools opened in October for the 2022-2023 academic year, WFP plans to scale up the school feeding program in refugee locations in early January 2023. This will complement other ongoing education support packages implemented by UNHCR and UNICEF. Currently, WFP supports about 22,000 refugee children in 14 schools located in Lac and Logone Oriental, out of 193 fundamental schools registered. This represents only 24% of the real needs. WFP therefore aims to extend coverage to an additional 83,000 children enrolled to cover all the 193 schools registered in the 18 refugee camps as per UNHCR data. All refugee schoolchildren will be provided with hot meals at school combined with take-home incentives that aim to encourage parents or caregivers of the girls to send and keep them in school. These will be provided through in-kind transfers and/or cash-based transfers. WFP will also strengthen school food management committees and promote the equitable participation of women and men. Overall Objective: Improve equitable access to quality education for all refugee and host population children including adolescent girls.Specific objectives: - Education: Improve school enrolment, attendance and retention of refugee children including adolescent girls.- Gender: Promote gender parity with special attention to girls.- Protection: in addition to other education inputs, school feeding contributes to facilitating the provision of a safe and secure environment.Through the distribution of meals, children and their parents view theschool as a secure platform where they can receive nutritious meals and learn in peace. This enables them to benefit from their full right to education. - Safety net: Provide a food security and nutrition safety net to vulnerable households through food and cash transfers to school going children from respective householdsFood will be provided in the form of hot meals for 180 days per school year (20 days per month). The selected food basket will provide 30% of the daily nutrition intake and, where applicable, it will be enriched with local fresh produce and some animal protein from school farms or community farmers.In addition, 704 volunteer cooks in charge of the preparation of meals at school level (ratio of 1 cook per 150 children) will be supported with cash transfers for a family ration of USD 0.450 per cook per day. 5.2 Home-grown School Feeding approachThrough its support to the implementation of the “Politique d'Alimentation de Nutrition et de Santé Scolaire” (PNANSS), WFP, in collaboration with the Government of Chad, partners and communities, will focus on promoting Chadian agriculture as per stated in the policy document. WFP will distribute nutritious and diversified hot meals procured from the local community to promote adequate access to nutritious food throughout the schoolyear. This project will promote local livelihoods by establishing links between school meals and local production (agriculture, livestock, fishing etc.). As part of the home-grown school feeding approach, the project will ensure that links are created between small farmers and school management committees for the sale and direct purchase of fresh produce (vegetables and fruits etc.). WFP believes that households, local farmers, and small businesses can benefit from the demand created by the school feeding programme, if procurement processes are designed to increase their ability to access the market and if efforts are made to increase their production. WFP will prioritize local purchases from organizations that include a high percentage of women producers, processors, and traders to increase economic opportunities for women along the school meal value chain. Schoolchildren will also benefit from food that fits their eating habits and preferences. Stimulated by the stable and sizeable demand for food, smallholders are more likely to invest in agricultural inputs, reach out to extension services, gain access to credit, and hence increase their productivity. Specific locations will be selected after a feasibility analysis is completed, taking into consideration market functionality, availability of food and local suppliers, and motivation of school management. WFP will ensure that contracts are established between farmer organizations (producers and sellers), and school canteen management committees within schools (buyers). In this context, WFP will use the results of the Gender Market Analysis Tool to strengthen the collection and analysis of sex-disaggregated data on the different roles of women and men in the markets. In addition, an analysis of agricultural value chains with a view to promoting better empowerment of women in the context of these local purchases for canteens will be carried out as soon as the activities are planned. A Value Chain Matrix for home grown school feeding that defines the roles and responsibilities of program stakeholders will be completed. It will provide a clear understanding of the roles of functional units within WFP, as well as the roles of external stakeholders (government at central and local levels). 5.3 Support to adolescent girls' education Except for pre-school enrolment, which is very underdeveloped and reserved for wealthier urban families where girls and boys are at parity, gender disparities are significant to the detriment of girls and increase as schooling progresses. The odds of access to school (admission to CP1) are 17% lower for girls than for boys and only 36% of girls complete primary school, compared to 56% of boys, a probability 30% lower. Dropouts are highest for girls at all levels of primary and middle education, and girls are half as likely as boys to complete middle school. In support of Vision 2030 to ensure equal access and approach equal completion of primary school for girls and boys and to strengthen parity at the higher levels of the system, WFP has put in place several household support initiatives for girls' primary and secondary school enrolment.a) Girls Take-home cash incentiveTo specifically encourage girls' enrolment and retention, WFP will provide a conditional cash grant of USD60 per schoolyear to the households of 16,114 adolescent girls, subject to their school attendance. The value of cash would be USD 0.200 /girl/day which represents the value in the local market of the oil distributed in the past as family ration. This will be provided on a quarterly basis through financial service providers.b) Girls’ encouragement kitsIn addition, all adolescent girls in primary and secondary schools (approximately 9,480 girls) that complete the school year will receive a yearly ‘’encouragement kit’’ of USD 60 in cash to purchase school supplies (bag, pens, textbooks, workbooks, and other material) to support the transition and retention from primary to secondary school. c) Girls’ excellence scholarshipsWFP will also establish a ‘’Excellence Award’’ program providing a one off USD40 cash prize to the three top performing girls and boys by class (primary and secondary) in each supported schools at the end of every schoolyear (based on school report card). 5.4 Capacity building activities a) Training on management of school feeding activitiesComplementary activities such as training for cooks and school food management committees and students associations will be conducted on menu planning, food hygiene, food storage, distribution, and the management of the school meals. b) Rehabilitation/building of kitchens and storage infrastructuresWhere necessary, there will be rehabilitation or building of kitchens, food stores and food serving areas to ensure proper functioning. This activity will be implemented with community participation especially in workforce. c) Institutional capacity strengtheningIn view of ensuring sustainability and preparing for handover in the future, the project will provide technical and financial support to National School Meals Directorate and cooperating partners to gradually implement sustainable home-grown school meals pilot programs in refugee areas. WFP will also conduct trainings for national school meals directorate staff at national and decentralized levels to strengthen capacities in school feeding programme management, logistics and food procurement. WFP will monitor the following output indicators during implementation:Outcome OutputsSchool attendance and retention for all refugee children including adolescent girls during the schoolyear Indicator 1.1.1: Number of boys and girls in WFP assisted primary schools Indicator 1.1.2: Number of schools assisted Indicator 1.1.3: Quantity of food distributed in WFP assisted schools by type of commodity Indicator 1.1.4: Quantity of food procured locally at decentralized level in comparison of the total of food purchased Indicator 1.1.5: Feeding days as percentage of total school days Indicator 1.1.6: Number of women, men, boys, and girls receiving cash-based transfers/commodity vouchers Indicator 1.1.7: Total amount of cash transferred to targeted beneficiaries Indicator 1.1.8: Quantity of non-food items distributed by type
