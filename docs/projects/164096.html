Project Summary

The WASH Assistance To Enhance Resilience (WATER) Project will target approximately 385,000 vulnerable individuals, including 107,800 men, 107,800 women, 84,700 boys and 84,700 girls, with urgent WASH assistance in areas of high severity across the governorates of Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, Homs, Rural Damascus, Quneitra and Dar’a. This project aims to provide both life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to communities underserved by the humanitarian community with critical WASH needs including difficulty of access to safe and sufficient drinking water in a sanitary environment due to infrastructural damage and disrepair and increased pressure on local water resources.
The WATER Project will provide life-saving assistance to highly vulnerable IDPs in the context of an emergency response through the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and hygiene kit distribution. Life-sustaining assistance will target highly vulnerable conflict-affected or overburdened communities with a high IDP/ spontaneous returnee population or those underserved by the humanitarian community and having experienced a recent change in control with life-sustaining activities. Community-level vulnerability will be assessed according to the presence of highly vulnerable groups such as the elderly, people with disabilities, female-headed households, women and children, and with high numbers of individuals resorting to negative coping mechanisms due to economic impoverishment. Activities will include the rehabilitation of water and sewerage systems, including boreholes and pumping stations, the provision of Reverse Osmosis units to communities with high nitrate levels, community and household water tanks, and solid waste management.
This project will adopt a participatory, community-based approach ensuring the participation of vulnerable men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities, the elderly, and female-headed households in the design, implementation and evaluation of the project.

4 - Needs

WASH needs remain critical across Syria with 10.7 million people in need of WASH assistance including 3 million in acute need. Access to safe and sufficient drinking water remains priority with approximately 37% of Syrians reporting access to an insufficient amount of water while 42% rely on informal and unsafe water sources to meet their daily WASH needs and only half of the sewerage systems nationwide are functional (HRP 2020), posing significant health risks to men, women, boys and girls. Based on ongoing projects in the area, ADRA identified critical needs for safe and sufficient drinking water in Hamourieh and Kafra Batna, Rural Damascus where high nitrates and leaking sewage have led the communities to resort to alternative water sources. In other areas, such as mountainous areas of Moadamieh, the water pressure remains weak due to a lack of rehabilitated boreholes able to supply water to the area. In other areas recently accessible, previously conflict-affected area, such as Dariyya, families return to their area of origin with limited access to resources. Deir-ez-Zor only became accessible to humanitarian partners in 2019, therefore, most of the governorate remains acutely underserved by the humanitarian community yet with urgent multi-sectoral needs. Communities such as Jafraa, Al Mayadin and Isbekhan resort to private water trucking to meet their daily needs while the risk of waterborne diseases is high with leaking sewerage systems creating an unsanitary environment, a lack of rehabilitated pumping stations and the presence of leishmania in the area in general. Aleppo governorate remains in urgent need of WASH intervention with many areas highly contaminated with UXOs in Aleppo city and rural areas. The accumulation of debris and a leaking sewerage system in Aleppo city, particularly the eastern side, due to the conflict poses daily protection risks to young boys and girls playing outside in an unsanitary environment.

5 - Implementation of activities

ADRA will undertake sustainable WASH interventions through coordination with the WASH Sector, SARC, the Ministry of Water Resources (MoWR), the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) and local authorities based on a needs assessment of the targeted areas consulting men, women, boys and girls. ADRA will rehabilitate / expand the existing or damaged water networks including boreholes and pumping stations to ensure the provision of potable water in 2 sub-districts of Deir ez Zor (DeZ), 1 in Dar’a and 3 in Rural Damascus (RD), sewerage networks in 1 sub-district of Aleppo, 1 in DeZ and 2 in RD. Works will include replacing damaged pipes in the water network, the rehabilitation and the provision of equipment of boreholes, the installation of Reverse Osmosis Units in Hamourieh, East Ghouta, the replacement of sewerage piping, sewerage desludging with high-pressure pumps, and manhole covers. Garbage bin distribution will be conducted in 9 areas of RD and debris removal in 1 area of DeZ and 2 areas of Aleppo to help maintain a sanitary and protective environment for vulnerable communities for men, women, boys and girls in the community. Community water tanks will be distributed in 1 area of Homs and 13 areas of Aleppo governorates. Life-saving interventions, including WASH facilities rehabilitation and hygiene kit distribution, will be implemented following extensive coordination with the WASH sector, SARC and other humanitarian partners to ensure the best coverage of needs and avoid any overlap in response as well as a rapid needs assessment in highly vulnerable, newly-displaced IDP communities, ensuring the participation of men, women, boys and girls, pre and post implementation of activities. Works will be monitored on a regular basis by ADRA Syria qualified technical, social and MEAL field officers and coordinators and handed over to local representatives of MoWR or MoLAE upon completion for operation and ongoing maintenance to ensure sustainability

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: 1. WASH facilities are located in areas that pose a GBV / SGBV or safety risk to women and girls or are not always accessible for men, women, boys and girls. 2. Injuries caused by exposure to UXOs during solid waste management implementation or movement of trucks pose protection risk to young or adolescent boys and girls, young adults, people with disabilities and the elderly.3. Use of child labor on project sites encouraging school drop-out and posing protection risks to adolescent boys and girls.4. Health risk posed during solid waste management through exposure to accumulated waste, bacteria and potential rodents in the area. 5. Target areas prioritize and empower one group over another, causing community tension and increasing the risk of GBV / SGBV in the community. 6. Community members are asked to provide services in exchange for humanitarian assistance. Mitigation:1. Ensure adherence to SPHERE standards including equal access for men, women, boys and girls, in central areas, well-lit area enabling young girls, adolescents, adult men and women, and the elderly ease of access. Involve men, women, boys and girls in project design.2. Conduct hazard awareness and UXO training with staff monitoring activities and ensure contractors take necessary safety precautions. Work closely with community members and stakeholders to identify areas of high contamination and ensure UXO removal.3. Explain to contractor ADRA Syria’s policy on child labor and verify no young or adolescent boys and girls are working on project sites through monitoring visits.4. Conduct safety training with staff, contractors and ensure proper use of safety equipment.5. Develop good relationships with Ministries ensuring humanitarian principles are stated clearly and promote ‘do no harm’ principle.6. Closely monitor all staff and contractors working in target area; ensure staff are trained in PSEA.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions/Risks: 1. The security situation in the targeted areas deteriorates, preventing access and the implementation of activities 2. Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or the value of the Syrian Pound deteriorates to a level that activities cannot be implemented within the planned budget3. Necessary permissions are unable to be obtained within a reasonable period of time for planned project activities or do not allow for an effective implementation of the program 4. Qualified staff unable to be retained/recruited in the project area 5. Planned activities pose protection risks to staff, stakeholders or beneficiaries 6. Undue pressure placed on ADRA staff by stakeholders to prioritize specific locations oractivities outside of the selection criteria 7. Potential for corruption or fraud, especially with project procurement Mitigation Measures:1. Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. Temporarily pause activities when necessary and utilize staff from the target area for monitoring. If the situation does not improve, select a new target are for implementation.2. Closely monitor exchange rates, use open and competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding to complete the works3. Apply for permissions in advance and conduct regular follow-up with the relevant ministries, including high-level meetings where necessary4. Recruit early in project life cycle5. Conduct protection risk analyses in target areas and plan activities in close coordination with community members to ensure support, or pause activities until the area is considered safe.6. Work closely with stakeholders and explain criteria and red lines in the early stages of target area selection. Where necessary, ADRA will coordinate with the sector leads and UN representatives or cease activities until support is granted7. Adhere strictly to the ADRA Syria Procurement and Finance Policies
