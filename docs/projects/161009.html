Project Summary

In line with the 2020 Health Cluster objectives, the proposed intervention should add to its overall goal the two-fold aim of reducing the morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases thus increasing access to lifesavings, essential and emergency health care services and improving access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations, including children U5 (boys and girls), PLW, older people and other vulnerable groups in Kapoeta South, East and North counties, in Tonj East and South counties, and in Twic (Mother Theresa Hospital). With a focus on enhancing an integrated approach to health, the project builds on the long-lasting experience and presence of CCM in the targeted areas, complementing the current health and nutrition programming to ensure safe and equitable access to critical integrated basic services to enable 21,917 beneficiaries to meet their basic needs. Through a holistic approach of health system strengthening, the two Health Cluster objectives will be achieved through the following strategy: 1. Focus on emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases, outbreak investigations, IPC in health facilities, capacity building of health workers, IEC production and dissemination, disease surveillance and outbreak response. Specifically, the current health and nutrition programming of CCM in greater Tonj and greater Kapoeta will be complemented and strengthened to address cholera prevention and control measures aiming for a strategic shift from response to prevention across all relevant sectors in order to (i) Enhance the preventive and response interventions at both HFs and communities’ level and (ii) support the strengthening of the capacity of the County Health System to prevent, detect and respond to such unexpected outbreaks. It is therefore crucial that CCM and SMoH/CHDs continue to maintain the ability to prevent, detect and respond to health emergencies shifting on prevention. 2. Focus on (i) coordination with other actors for the provision of essential health commodities in the supported health facilities (outbreak response commodities and essential medical kits and RH commodities) and (ii) ensuring support to a network of 65 health facilities and 3 hospitals through the provision of BeMONC/CeMONC services, the management of SAM with medical complications, the provision of qualified health workers and capacity building of skilled birth attendants, and the provision of outpatients consultations. Finally, the project will contribute to addressing the Health Cluster objectives through cross-cutting approaches that promote gender awareness and transform mindsets with the purpose of providing gender responsive health services and practice. In particular, cross-cutting themes in health such as gender will be prioritised and addressed through the promotion of equal participation of women and men in making decisions on critical matters of health thus reducing the gap between women’s and men’s rights to health and access to and control over health resources. Further, environmental health will be incorporated into the current health and nutrition programming, promoting Infection Prevention and Control practices into health care programs delivered to protect health workers, patients and the community from health care associated infections, which harm hundreds of people each year. The proposed programming has been designed in view of the effective and close collaboration established with the local authorities, at both County and State level, to ensure it properly responds to the critical needs of the targeted populations. Finally, an effective reporting system is integrated with sectors monitoring systems. Boosting the health data management at CHD and State level will promote the culture of using the evidence for planning, managing and sustaining programmatic interventions which sustains an efficient, cost-effective and equity approach.

1 - Needs

In 2020, and estimate 186,854 South Sudanese children, women and men in the targeted areas need humanitarian healthcare services. Specifically, the prevailing healthcare challenges are exacerbated by the lack of infection prevention and control measures, water quality control, waste management and high malnutrition. This requires a holistic approach to ensure equal and quality access to health for all. CCM intends strengthening all levels of health care in order to enhance delivery of integrated health services across the targeted areas. This will be achieved through a continuous capacity building process of the SMOH and CHDs, which will enable the provision of uninterrupted quality essential and emergency health services to local communities. The population in the targeted areas has poor health status as evinced by very low access to health care facilities and sub-standard maternal care coupled with malaria endemicity and seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including measles and cholera. Specifically, the health system in the areas faces several challenges, mainly attributed to: i) Low health service coverage worsens by the poor road network and the inaccessibility of some health facilities particularly during the rainy seasons; ii) Deplorable health facility infrastructures and lack of space to provide health care services at primary and secondary level; iii) Lack of administration and technical capacities at county health department level, in particular for data management and reporting, and drug supply chain and management; iv) Lack of qualified trained health workers in the area combined with high turn-over of skilled staff due to lack of accommodation and inappropriate retention strategy. As far as IPC concerns, healthcare facilities are ideal settings for the transmission of infections and instead of serving as points where diseases are controlled, they often become dangerous places for disease amplification among staff and patients and transmission back to the communities. In fact, according to WHO data on low/middle-income countries, 1 in 10 patients gets an infection while receiving care. The compliance of health facility workers with key elements of IPC remains low due to lack of educational efforts and awareness building. Other areas of concern in the targeted counties include poor awareness and knowledge about HCAI and IPC, lack of capacity of SMOH/CHD with the surveillance of HCAI and compliance with IPC practices, outbreaks and bacterial resistance, and limited resources for environmental requirements at the facility level (related to water and sanitation, waste management). Specifically, unsafe waste management represents a major challenge in the health facilities of the counties targeted. Experiences show that, often, health care waste management does not follow proper standards and procedures. Main need is therefore to assist the SMoH/CHDs to reduce HCAI by supporting with the assessment, planning, and implementation of infection prevention and control policies and guidelines at all HFs levels. Further, cholera represents a major strain in health services across the country and major gaps and concerns still remain particularly in Tonj East, where cases where registered in 2017 and Greater Kapoeta where cholera cases were registered in 2017 and 2018. These areas are typically at-risk due to inadequate access to clean water and extensive lack of basic sanitary infrastructure and, consequently, critical suboptimal hygiene practices (low practice of handwashing, high proportion of open defecation, improper water conservation and treatment at household level, poor food conservation). The situation may in fact worsen further because of the nomadic pastoralists moving from cattle camps to villages that have become a notable risk factor for spread of the disease. In Kapoeta, this is coupled with the already weak health system overwhelmed and further pressured by the influx of IDPs in the region.

2 - Activities

The proposed initiative will support the Health Cluster objective 1 and 2 by strengthening a holistic approach to ensure equal and quality access to health for all through the following activities. 1.1 Conduct an exploratory baseline assessment of the current situation at each HF level aimed at identifying local determinants of the HFs associated infections burden; 1.2 Collection of patients and community feedbacks on service delivery and health staff attitude; 1.3 Training of 130 health facility workers on IPC programme management; 1.4 Training of 23 health workers on IPC in outbreak response; 1.5 Training of 65 support staff in the health facility on appropriate and routine IPC practices; 1.6 Appointment of technical team (IPC team) made of trained professionals at health facility level; 1.7 Procurement of appropriate IPC materials and equipment (e.g alcohol-based hand rubs, soap and water, personal protective equipment (PPE), detergent, mops, buckets, etc.); 1.8 Provision (construction and/or rehabilitation) of adequate number and appropriate position of hand hygiene facilities; 1.9 Provision (construction and/or rehabilitation) of health care waste management facilities (e.g incinerators, waste collection containers for non-infectious general waste, infectious waste and sharps waste, etc); 1.10 Monitoring/audit of adherence with IPC standards; 1.11 Conduct health education sessions and sensitization events on health, hygiene and sanitation; 1.12 Design and production of IEC material (e.g leaflets and posters) and messages (including use of different channels, e.g community radio) for community engagement activities that are appropriate for the target populations in terms of language, educational level and cultural contexts; 1.13 Health and hygiene education campaigns through community mobilization activities aiming at the adoption of appropriate hygiene practices (such as hand-washing with soap, safe preparation and storage of food and safe disposal of the faeces of children, etc); 1.14 Training of health facilities workers and CHD staff on emergency response and prevention of disease outbreak in TE and KAP; 1.15 Definition of a joint plan of action at county level to describe the interventions needed in case of an outbreak and the distribution of roles and responsibilities among stakeholders; 1.16 Strengthen surveillance and information sharing by building the capacity for data collection and analysis; 1.17 Training of health facility workers on cholera prevention and control in TE and KAP; 1.18 Procurement of tools to ensure optimal coordination at county level (phones, dashboard, maps, etc); 1.19 Procurement and preposition of hand washing stations for 12 health facilities; 1.20 Training of BHCs and community volunteers on hygiene promotion, and timely detection and referral of suspected cases in TE and KAP; 1.21 Training key stakeholders on water quality monitoring; 1.22 Procurement and prepositioning of core supplies necessary for outbreak prevention (such as soap, buckets and jerry cans, etc); 2.1 Improve timely access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations; 2.2 Delivery of integrated child health and nutrition care (CGM, IMNCI, EPI, CMAM), and maternal health care (ANC, PMTCT and EPI); 2.3 Provide assistance to uncomplicated delivery (clean and safe) and skilled assistance to delivery (BEmONC at PHCC and CEmONC at Hospital level); 2.4 Delivery of SRH care (FP, prevention and treatment of STI, post-abortion care); 2.5 Training of 65 health workers on GBV management; 2.6 Conduct joint (CCM/CHD) supportive supervision to all HFs; 2.7 Conduct weekly coordination meetings with CHD. 2.8 Ensure appointment of qualified health workers in the supported health facilities; 2.9 Provide outpatient consultations at all supported health facilities; 2.10 Prevention, testing and treating for malaria among children-U5 affected by SAM and admitted in OTP/SCs programme.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Gender and Age considerations will be also included in the programming. The proposed interventions will be built on gender mainstreaming as a priority approach to guide the overall implementation of the program at different levels. The intention is to ensure that County Health Plans are responsive to gender and social exclusion barriers, and that related issues are promptly addressed. The project will further reinforce the commitment of the South Sudan government to give preference to those in the society who are most vulnerable with limited or no source of income living in remote and poverty-stricken communities. In line with the Health Cluster approach, vulnerable groups will be the priority target of most planned activities. Priority to gender and inclusion across the program will be given at different levels: community – HF and county level aiming at the same time to increase awareness on gender equality and GBV related issues and improve the quality of the related services offered, including training of health facility staff on GBV related issues and clinical management of GBV cases. During the project implementation, the mental health component will be developed aimed at strengthening the health system creating mental health awareness at the community level and reinforcing the capacity of the health workers. The same approach will be adopted as far as disability is concerned, focusing on awareness-raising and demand creation. CCM will work with the aim to promote disabilities identification at community and HF level, in order to improve the current service package delivered.In order to mitigate the risk of population movements seeking for access to health services, the established system of outreaches activities allows to reach the people in need in the most disadvantage and remote areas of the Counties targeted.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to the affected population will be streamlined into CCM strategies and project/monitoring activities in order to enable improved participation, information provision, feedback and complaints handling. CCM will adopt systems to monitor, collect feedbacks and evaluate its action, ensuring a transparent and fair management and promoting better quality of services provided. Specifically, CCM has developed patients and community feedback mechanism that foresees the organization of ‘exit interviews’, through carried out in sample HFs on a monthly basis. Literate patients receive the questionnaire to fill in and return to the HF staff, while illiterate beneficiaries are supported by the staff in their compilation. Data collected in the sample facilities is then passed on to M&E Officers and analysed into a report. CCM will engage the affected populations in all processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices by routinely providing accessible and timely information to the affected population about their rights and entitlements. Communities will be strongly and effectively involved throughout the project timeline, promoting their active engagement in the development of the CHDs. MtMSGs, HHPs and Bomas members will be involved regularly for defaulters tracing, promotion of correct nutrition practices and development of community responses to nutrition needs. This mechanism is aimed at accessing beneficiary’s satisfaction and to put in place strategies to address identified specific needs of the most vulnerable community members. CCM is producing info graphics summarizing the Whistleblowing policy and reporting mechanism; they will be posted in the HFs together with a ‘complaining box’. Moreover, CCM developed a SOP to assure that disclosures are collected from the boxes respecting confidentiality policies and that they are properly referred as per mechanism.
