Project Summary

Khartoum and Darfur region are considered the most affected area in Sudan’s ongoing war, with people's needs increasing day by day due to a lack of assistance provided to the Region. SUDO aims to provide health services to the affected people of Khartoum and Darfur. More than 11 million people in Darfur have been affected by war, including the already displaced ones from the protracted conflict and the ongoing one. more than 1,599,519 have been displaced, and more than half a million people have fled to Chad since April 2023. Over 6.1 million were forced to flee their homes in search of security. Majority of this number have displaced from Khartoum and over half were children and women. About 70% of health facilities in the conflict affected states are not functional creating huge pressure to the already stressed health situation. This is because of direct attack to the facilities, lack of medicines and medical supplies, reduced number of health workers and those who are serving have not received their monthly salaries for months, and lack of fuel to operate the facilities.
This project targets 148,090 individuals in Khartoum and Darfur states and aims to reduce the gap in health services to the affected people. This will be through the following activities:
• Renovating five large hospitals, one in each of the states (Central, North, West, East, and South Darfur); cleaning, painting, rebuilding walls; replacing flooring; and replacing windows.
• Rehabilitation of ten clinics, two in each state (Central Darfur, North Darfur, West Darfur, East Darfur, and South Darfur).
• Provision of medicine and medical supplies.
* Build capacity of health workers.
• Support operation of hospitals and clinics.
• Improve sexual and reproductive health.
SUDO is currently operating in all the targeted states responding to the health needs in these areas. Immediately after the start of the conflict, SUDO managed to support health facilities in Khartoum with medicines provided by Unicef and with the support of volunteers who managed to deliver and distribute the medicines to the health centers. In five Darfur states, SUDO is currently supporting nine health facilities (hospitals and clinics) with medicines, incentive for workers and fuel.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will improve access to health services for women, men, children, elderly including people with disability. The availability of health services in safe and accessible areas to people affected by conflict will reduce the risk of losing life because of lack of medical supplies, thus contributing to the protection of people. Communities in the project locations will be engaged in the project design, implementation and monitoring through the establishment of community committees for monitoring of the facilities. Conflict sensitivity and Do No Harm principle will be considered throughout this project, where the focus will be on building connections and reducing the dividers between the groups. The selection of communities and the committees as well as provision of services will consider the diversities of gender, religion, ethnicity, and culture. Assessment of the level of satisfaction or complaints from communities will be done as part of the accountability.SUDO Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy: SUDO Safeguarding Policy has the following objectives: • To protect beneficiaries engaged in SUDOs projects from all forms of abuse and exploitation. • To protect SUDO staff from all forms of abuse and exploitation and any potential false allegations of misconduct. 4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

SUDO has a safeguarding policy with the objectives to protect beneficiaries of SUDO projects from any form of abuse and exploitation as well as protect staff from the same and any potential false allegation of misconduct. Safeguarding Policy applies to; full time and part time SUDO staff, members of SUDO, Board of Directors, Volunteers and interns, consultants, visitors, people acting on behalf of SUDO and – to the relevant extent – to SUDO suppliers and partner organizations. SUDO take all reasonable steps to protect beneficiaries from any harassment, abuse and exploitation potentially perpetrated by SUDO staff or associates. All SUDO staff and associates are required to RESPECT the rights and dignity of individuals, families and communities with whom SUDO work. likewise, SUDO is committed to protecting the rights of the SUDO staff and to maintaining its dignified working environment free of any potential harassment, abuse and exploitation. SUDO ensures that safe, appropriate and accessible means of reporting safeguarding concerns are made available to staff and the communities. In the response SUDO follow up on safeguarding reports and concerns according to principles and obligations described in the SUDO Code of Conduct and Community Response Mechanism (CRM). The reaction to a complaint or report shall result in a confidential, sensitive, thorough and prompt investigation. Such investigation either may result in the reduction of concern or in further action which includes disciplinary action up to dismissal and/or reporting to relevant authorities.
