Project Summary

This project mainly aims to support the provision of Emergency Education services to marginalized children (boys and girls 6-18 years) in the conflict affected communities & those in IDPs camps in South and North Darfur States to ensure continuity of learning during and after the conflict. The project will for the most vulnerable school aged children both boys and girls in the targeted states of North and South Darfur that have also been affected by the current crisis and has a big number of internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable residents. The project will support 15,000 children in 12 localities of which eight localities will be from North Darfur and four from South Darfur state . The indictors/activities are: children including children with disabilities will be enrolled and access child-friendly learning spaces; provision of recreational materials kits, emergency/ complementary feeding programme and provision with MHM Education and sanitary materials/dignity kits to at least 7,500 boys and girls. Emergency handwashing stations and latrines will be rehabilitated or constructed in four localities with IDP camps; and temporary learning spaces rehabilitated or constructed. Objective 2 is to enhance structured lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being within a protective environment relates. These will be under the indicators/activities which includes supporting of 7500 girls and boys with emergency teaching and learning materials; 100 teachers/facilitators supported with emergency monthly incentives; and 150 teachers/facilitators trained on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection issues. HDPO will start by implementing the project in areas/localities which are relatively stable while monitoring the hot spot areas and as the security situation improves in those areas the project will be extended there for implementation. The project will improve and strengthen emergency learning environment to make it safe and protective for vulnerable children in these states; hence reducing the risk of child labor, recruitment into combat, and joining criminal groups and it will reduce the risk of girls dropping out of school which will reduce the early marriage and domestic violence.
The security situation continues to be volatile in the targeted localities, however HDPO will start by implementing the project in more stable areas. The security situation will continuously be monitored in coordination with relevant partners and stakeholders and as the situation improves in hot spot areas, the project will be rolled out in those localities.

1 - Needs

More than 8.5 million children require humanitarian support to continue or resume their formal education. The Improving access and quality basic education project aims to contribute to increasing access to quality primary education and improve learning outcomes for some of the most marginalized children in Sudan, where more than 8.5 million children are out of school and are in need of education services. Many of whom live in the most vulnerable and conflict-affected communities like IDP camps. This means that a higher percentage of all school-aged children more than 2 in 3 children in Sudan are not attending school or any formal learning following the current clash between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The Sudan Education Sector’s Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA, 2021) found that of the schools assessed, 52 per cent require major rehabilitation and 22 per cent of classrooms are damaged. Additionally, 46 per cent of schools have no access to clean water for drinking or handwashing and across the schools assessed, the average user to latrine ratio was 132 to 1. While lack of adequate school infrastructure to support children’s learning opportunities is a long-standing issue in Sudan, the situation has been exacerbated by the escalation of conflict in most areas of Darfur region. In South & North Darfur, more boys and girls from the IDPs and communities are vulnerable to child labor, recruitment into combat, and joining criminal groups because of lack of conducive learning and protective environment that schools would provide. Girls in particular are at increased risk of dropping out of school, exposing them to early marriage and domestic violence. Long travel distances to schools discourage families to enroll their children (both boys and girls) in schools due to insecurity. The conflict has also caused destruction of education infrastructure such as buildings, furniture and other school items have been looted for example in Nyala and Elfasher. Children with especial needs, the disabled and mother school aged girls are less likely to access their rights to basic and secondary education compared to other children. In addition, the poverty amongst the population has continued to rise significantly, affecting the ability of parents to meet the associated costs of providing their children with education requirements as most have lost their sources of livelihood. Malnutrition also continues to affect many children. In addition to pre-existing challenges related to insufficient learning resources and supplies, the crisis has left learners and teacher displaced and traumatized; damaged learning infrastructure and school spaces, and constrained access to schools. Hence this project will be implemented during this conflict moment through the following strategic outcomes: improved access to safe and protective learning environments for vulnerable children including children with disabilities; and improved structured quality of lifesaving education for 15,000 crisis affected children (boys and girls) through the rehabilitation and construction of emergency learning spaces in two Darfur states of North and South Darfur States where learning has been greatly affected by the current conflict. The project will also enhance the capacity of 150 teachers/facilitators on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection issues in order to effectively teach and support children to learn using child-centered teaching methods during the conflict/emergency.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project first outcome aims to improve access to safe and protective learning environments for 15,000 vulnerable children (8000 girls) including children with disabilities and those IDPs in 12 localities of North and South Darfur and 150 education facilitators. The objective for this outcome is to ensure vulnerable or marginalized children (ages 6-18) have access to safe and protective learning environments. The indictors/activities are: 15,000 children including children with disabilities enrolled and access child-friendly learning spaces; 12,100 girls and boys supported with recreational materials (kits); 4,000 girls and boys supported with emergency/ complementary feeding programme; 2160 girls provided with MHM Education and sanitary materials/dignity kits; 12 handwashing stations temporary improved and installed in schools; 12 gender responsive latrines rehabilitated or constructed in four localities with IDP camps; 36 temporary learning spaces rehabilitated or constructed; 9,760 of children (6-18 years) benefiting from structured, school-based psychosocial support activities; and 5,000 of girls and boys enrolled in Alternative Basic Education. The second outcome aims to improved structured quality of lifesaving education for crisis affected children. Objective 2 is to enhance structured lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being within a protective environment relates. These will be under the indicators/activities which includes 15,000 of girls and boys supported with Emergency teaching and learning materials; 100 teachers/facilitators supported with emergency monthly incentives; and 150 teachers/facilitators trained on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection issues. Of the target beneficiaries, 60% will be from the IDPs camps (33% of these will be girls), 20% returnees (13% girls) and 20% will be vulnerable residents (13% girls).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The schools in the targeted location that already desert and semi-desert area with lack of green coverage; hence school infrastructures mainly from the local materials such as woods and other trees- based materials then the limited tree in the area significantly affected by unregulated tree cutting and wood collection but also to humanitarian-aid related activities, especially construction, can lead to soil deterioration, desertification, loss of livelihoods and conflict (as a result of competition over land and resources), especially in this part of North & South Darfur where Issues around forest management, household energy, poverty alleviation, livelihoods and conflict mitigation need to be addressed together. It is essential to ensure that water outtake does not exceed the replenishment of the water source. The targeted localities are already facing depletion of groundwater; hence, having the school children and their families gathered in the area affects groundwater extraction. They also use it to supply water for their animals. Groundwater monitoring is essential, and in case the water demand exceeds available resources, there may be a need to cap wells and develop alternative sources.
