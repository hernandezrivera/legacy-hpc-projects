3 - Needs

The crisis in NES is characterized by severe protection, economic livelihoods, and food security concerns. By mid-2021 an additional segment of the population became food insecure, totaling 12.8 million people. An estimated 2 of 3 Syrians need food assistance, with an additional 1.8 million risking falling into food insecurity (HNO 2022). According to MSNA conducted by DCA on Food Security in 2021, vulnerable HHs spend a large proportion of their monthly income on basic food needs. Only 18.4% of HHs have ‘acceptable’ food consumption scores (FCS), and over 60% have poor FCS. Al-Thawrah and Menbij have particularly high proportions of HHs in the poor FCS category. Female-headed HHs are more likely than male-headed HHs to have poor FCS, and IDP HHs are more likely than residents to have poor FCS. The reduced coping strategy index is high among the respondents, with 27.2 as the average for the full sample. The average rCSI is the highest in Al-Thawrah and the lowest in Kobane. Female headed HHs have a higher average rCSI. IDP HHs also have a higher average rCSI than residents. The percentage of HHs with moderate or severe hunger is the highest in Menbij and the lowest in Al-Raqqa. Female-headed HHs are more likely to experience moderate or severe hunger than male-headed HH. While markets remain accessible, the two main issues with accessing food that recently displaced HHs face is that it has become too expensive, and they have less money than usual to spend on food.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide vulnerable HHs with food support in the form of Cash for Food assistance for 8 rounds in line with recommendation from the NES FSL working group. DCA will apply harmonized selection criteria for FS partners in NES, to which DCA has contributed significantly. These criteria include socioeconomic status, family head, Food Consumption Score (FCS), HH hunger scale (HHS) among others. The beneficiaries will be identified by local committees, and HHs will be able to request assessment through and complaint and feedback mechanism.Later, DCA will conduct a follow-up assessment of all HHs, identifying whether HHs qualify for further assistance. DCA will conduct market monitoring on a monthly basis to check the availability of food items and the price trends etc. to analyze the suitability of cash transfer programming. PDM will be conducted regularly throughout the project.For income generation activities (IGCs), Community Groups (established by DCA as a means to better coordinate the C4F intervention) will also help identify HHs that are socially and economically ready to attend vocational trainings (if funding allows). Trainings and cash grants for IGAs will be provided by DCA according to business plans shared by selected families/individuals. As a potential livelihood intervention, to support agriculture, communities and the agriculture department will be involved to identify irrigation channels that require rehabilitation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of inter- and intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: Ensure that a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Protection Risk: Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Ensure in depth consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access and barriers for those with injuries/ person with disabilities and communication mechanisms for person with visual/ hearing impairments.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: Low.Impact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation (with local networks) in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place. Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
