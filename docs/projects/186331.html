Project Summary

The prevalence of the civil unrest dupped “the anglophone crisis” in the North West and South west regions of Cameroon has resulted in the internal displacement of thousands of people, among which are women and girls (mostly from very rural areas). The excesses of the anglophone crisis has led to the loss of livelihoods of many families, inclusive of farming land and businesses run by women. According to UN OCHA 2nd July 2021 report, there are over 2,2 million people affected in the North West and South West, with 712.8K Internally displaced Persons (IDPs) spread across the country, 333.9k returnees and 67,4k Cameroonian refugees in Nigeria. In the same report, reported GBV cases within the region has gone up by 20% with 603 newly reported cases and 99% survivors being women.
This portrays the level of vulnerability faced by women and girls in the project location with respect to livelihood and financial independence, especially as interventions within the region have been in areas of food security, protection, health, GBV, Nutrition, Protection, CP and other activities. And of the $361.6M required to meet the needs of 1.6M targeted persons, only $77.6M has been raised with no interventions in targeted interventions for IDP commercial sex workers as per the same report.
The minimum monthly wage in Cameroon is 30,000 CFA or roughly £39.21. The average cost of living in the city with no dependents is between 70,000 – 80,000 CFA (£91.49 - £104.55). When IDP women arrive from conflict affected rural communities with children or dependents, to live with all of their needs met increases the cost per month significantly. And coming from the rural areas with no experience or qualification to land a meaningful job, these women are forced into doing menial jobs in very terrible conditions and are not even paid the minimum wage in return. Some are, especially the younger woman and girls, are forced into commercial sex work to assist the survival of their families. An article published by the Presbyterian Church (USA)’s mission to Cameroon in 2019, states that “the anglophone crisis pushes young girls into prostitution for survival”, and it could not be more accurately put.
Interventions towards the condition of these women is limited as resources are being channelled to the more glaring situations in the active crisis areas, and these women in urban towns are considered as “safe”. However, the actions of the Women’s Empowerment and Family ministry through her delegations has seen the training of some commercial sex workers in employable skills, however, their reach is limited to a small number of vulnerable women and in desperate need of scale up to meet women who are not just commercial sex workers, but suffering inhumane treatment and deplorable employment engagements.
There is therefore a great need to present the realities of these women who are considered “safe” (but wake up every day living every second in total fear and uncertainty) to the general public and all relevant stakeholders so has to garner support and invoke life saving interventions. Thus, giving these women a second chance at a hopeful purpose driven life.
Against this backdrop, this project is designed to provide relevant economic and livelihood support to vulnerable women and girls within major towns IDP dense towns in the NW, SW, Littoral and West regions of Cameroon, in a drive to provide them with a better living condition and a chance at a better life as the years unfold. Support within this project takes the form of direct multipurpose cash transfer, business creation via the provision of start-up kits and capital, as well as business training and the provision of real-time technical support to beneficiaries throughout, till businesses are sufficiently viable.
