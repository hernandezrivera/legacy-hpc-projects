Project Summary

The Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items (ESNFI) Humanitarian Response Project is prepared with the aim of strengthening access to appropriate live-saving ESNFI to safeguard the health and protection of the displacement affected population; and contributing to improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions.
The proposed project, cluster objectives, and activities were identified in consultation with the ESNFI cluster and concerned regional bureaus. Its preparation is also based on the key findings and recommendation of own assessment conducted in Kewet and Antsokiya Woredas (7 – 10, April 2022). Own assessment reaffirmed that almost all of displacement affected people are returnees and non-displaced people and no people who temporally settled in IDP centers in Brebra kebele & Mekoy cities of Antsokiya Woreda and Shewa Robit town of Kewet Woreda. Those returnees in both Woredas are sheltering in their partially damaged houses & settled with their relatives. Considering the number of previous returnees in this Woredas & town administration, it is feasible to provide shelter & NFIs as a first phase of the response because their houses are damaged and require shelter kits and NFIs, moreover distribution of ES/NFI kits is critical to provide immediate lifesaving responses for displacement affected returnees and host community.
The other key finding of the assessment is the presence of those who are unable to cover the cost of renting houses. Those most returnees are Mekoy town residents but could not cover their house rent since they lost all their properties during the conflict. Therefore, as an option we recommend conditional cash for rent for returnees. Since the markets are functional in Mekoy town and Shewa Robit, capital of Kewot worerda, conditional cash is recommended to purchase ES/NFIs to their immediate use. During our market assessment there are items on the market, but it is difficult to process bulky procurement due to the low availability of items in the market. So, cash for NFIs is advisable based on market availability.
The proposal is aligned with key findings of own rapid assessment conducted, the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) ESNFI Cluster Prioritized Locations and Activities; 2022 HRP cluster specific Objectives, Indicators and Target framework; and Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) - Estimates of Displacement by Region (March 2022); as well as Regional Disaster Prevention Food Security Programme Coordination Commission (DPFSPCC) emergency response plan. Furthermore, the remote rapid assessments are complemented with experience gained from CACH humanitarian aid implementation in Ethiopia. According to the Amhara regional Disaster Prevention Food Security Program Coordination Commission (DPFSPCC) report, over 2.3 million people were displaced from their original place, of which more than 1.4 million IDPs remain displaced across the region (DPFSPCC multi sectoral response plan, Dec. 2021). The majority of IDPs are women and children. Among the total displaced, an estimated 480,401 IDPs (DTM Ethiopia Faces of Displacement - Dabat Amhara v1) were returned starting in mid-December 2021 onward in South Wollo and North Shewa Zones Main cause of displacement is the conflict, whereby the fleeing persons lost their jobs, farmland, and livestock, and thus their livelihoods. The critical humanitarian situation forced most returnees and IDPs to live in extreme conditions with relatives and friends where shortage of any form of assistance is a big gap. The conflict has adversely affected agricultural production, crops, and livestock.

Implementing Partners

CACH works closely with its longstanding national implementing partners based on their geographic presence, to ensure continued delivery of services in areas and to build sustainable capacity of partners, and in adherence with the Grand Bargain localization agenda and the National NGOs’ Engagement - Strategy Paper. ECC SDCO has a very strong presence in the Country and is
currently implementing with CACH a rapid response intervention in North Shewa and Western Tigray.

1. - Projects activities

According to the national ESNFI Cluster Prioritized Locations Activities and Cost by Woreda (March 2022), CACH will focus on selected activities that address the pressing needs of the most vulnerable people and the most at-risk group including displacement affected returnees and non-displaced/affected host communities. During the start up the implementation, a rapid assessment will be carried out and a thorough discussion will be facilitated with ESNFI cluster both at National and regional level. The aim is to distribute standard kit packages, including Emergency Shelter Basic Repair Kits, Emergency Shelter (ES) and NFI Kits, to returnees and non -displaced people, including People with Disabilities (PwD). Technical support and guidance on Shelter Damage Prioritization, and Shelter Reconstruction Repair Minimum Package will be applied to the Response. To do so, we will be implementing the following set of key activities: 1. Provision of Emergency Shelter Repair Kit to households (HHs) whose houses are completely damaged that consider the needs of women, children, people with disabilities, and the safety of beneficiaries. 2. Provision of Non-food items (NFI) to reduce the likelihood of health and protection consequences. 3. Provision of NFI Kits to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness. The following are a detailed preliminary and operational tasks:• conduction of inception coordination, introductory and planning meetings to share clear communication about the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process. CACH fully employ ‘Ethiopia Emergency Shelter and NFI Cluster Targeting Approach August 2021’ in the process to be undertaken as an integral part of preliminary activities. • Beneficiary identification and registration will take place once a beneficiary selection committee and complaint handling committee are established for registration of beneficiaries. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be incorporated into the selection process.• The final verification will be done independently by CACH. The verification will be carried out at the household level by conducting a damage assessment. Groups with specific needs that will be prioritized for assistance, in accordance with the Shelter/NFI Cluster endorsed vulnerability criteria, include girls and boys at risk, persons with serious health conditions, women-headed households, older persons, persons with disabilities.• Procurement and transportation of emergency shelter repair kits and non-food item (NFI) kits for returnees' households will be carried out by CACH from Addis Ababa to project intervention areas. Kits are in-line with ES/NFI Cluster standards. • Emergency Shelter (ES) assistance - Procurement and transportation of emergency shelter items. ESNFI standard Emergency Shelter kits will be used.• Provide training for beneficiaries in constructing emergency shelters with the items in the kit, through constructing demonstration shelters.• Distribute of ES/NFI Kit for returnees and non-displaced households. • Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM).

As an integral part of aid localization, it is the first premise of CACH to building the capacity of relevant local stakeholders and actors at all levels. Local capacity of all stakeholders will be strengthened by committed participatory partnerships. This requires the delineation of clear roles / responsibilities with representatives, community leaders and committees. Based on these agreements the establishment of selection criteria for distribution procedures and policies including tracking of resources, and the planning and implementation of response activities are defined in a comprehensible way. As part of implementation strategy, CACH firmly believes the participation of all concerned actors in all phases of the project aiming to create synergies and to ensure continued delivery of services in areas and to build sustainable capacity of national partners in adherence with the Grand Bargain localization agenda and the National NGOs’ Engagement - Strategy Paper.
