Project Summary

Cameroon is currently grappling with a triple humanitarian crisis. a) Insurgency in the Lake Chad Basin, which has rendered Far North Cameroon as the second most affected after Nigeria (Northeast) by the Boko Haram activities. b) Escalating violence in the two English-speaking regions of North-west and South-west. c) Presence of over 325,000 refugees from the Central African Republic (CAR) residing in the East, Adamawa, and North regions of Cameroon with grave humanitarian consequences. This has created enormous humanitarian needs which are further compounded by structural development deficits and chronic vulnerabilities that further challenge the long-term recovery of affected people. Lack of legal and civil documentation (LCD) among crises-affected populations in the Far North (48%) and NWSW (38%) has been identified as a key protection concern. Main reasons advanced for the lack of LCD reported by the MSNA are: lack of supporting documentation, limited information, difficult access to civil registration and identification centres and limited access to legal assistance.
This project seeks to improve protection and enable enjoyment of legal identity rights for people affected by crises in the Far North, North West and South West regions. Through the provision of legal protection services including information, counselling, legal assistance and capacity building on legal identity, NRC will enable more people to exercise their rights and take steps to possess LCD. The project will increase the number of children in possession of birth certificates while more young people will be supported to obtain national identity cards to ensure freedom of movement, prevent arbitrary arrests and detention and enjoy other rights and general protection. NRC’s Legal Mobile teams will target IDPs, returnees, refugees and vulnerable host communities with general information on LCD while people identified with specific needs will be provided legal counselling and legal assistance. Capacity building will target municipal, judiciary, health and administrative authorities concerned with civil status registration to improve their knowledge of the legal framework so that they can provide quality and timely services. The project will also build the capacity of humanitarian actors and civil society leaders in charge of promoting and protecting legal identity and other human rights. NRC will work with her local implementing partners to effect this change.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The civil status system in Cameroon is generally weak with people facing enormous challenges while trying to obtain legal identity and civil documentation. It is more challenging for people in the Far North, North West and South West regions that are affected by armed conflict and natural disasters as most civil status centers are non-functional owing to destruction or closure. Nevertheless, the demand for civil documentation remains high especially among displaced populations. In the Far North, a special program for the establishment of birth certificates instituted by the government in 2021 has led to the establishment of more than 83,560 birth certificates for primary school children. However, the National Civil Status Registration Office (BUNEC) estimates that 326,319 pupils in this region are still in need of birth certificates. IOM’s Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment conducted in November 2023 show that 48% of people in this region need legal and civil documentation. This limits access to essential services including education as 9% of households surveyed also reported that their children were not going to school because they lack birth certificate. The MSNA also identified that 38% of people in the NWSW could not access legal and civil documentation because they lack supporting documentation (65%), information is limited (47%), lack of access to the nearest civil registration office or identification center (39%), no access to legal aid (30%) among other factors.Lack of legal and civil documentation remains a major concern among displacement-affected populations as people without documentation are exposed to many protection risks including restrictions on movements, arbitrary arrests, and detention. They also face restrictions while trying to access essential services and claim rights to property. Moreover, the lack of birth certificates limits access to education for children and prevents many from writing certificate exams.To address these needs, NRC through this project seeks to improve access to legal and civil documentation for crisis affected populations in the concerned regions which will enable them to enjoy protection and rights to essential services. To achieve this purpose, NRC’s Legal Mobile teams will provide legal protection services to the target populations - IDPs, returnees, refugees, and vulnerable host communities, in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan and protection objectives.• Provision of information and counselling on legal identity: NRC’s • Provision of legal assistance on legal and civil documentation• Capacity building of stakeholders in collaboration with BUNEC• support civil status structures with materials and equipment support civil status structures with materials and equipment joint field supervision of civil status activities in collaboration with BUNEC and the Ministry of Decentralization and Local Development (MINDEVEL)
