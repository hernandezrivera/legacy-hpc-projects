Project Summary

The project targeting 4 districts in Lower Juba seeks to improve access and be responsive to the shelter needs of displaced populations, especially those that have been in protracted displacement.
Kismaayo is believed to have the largest IDP population in the region mostly located in Farjano and Fanole areas. Recent IDPs profiling conducted by Norwegian Refugee Council indicated that there are 6,059 IDPs households in Kismaayo towns in 80 settlements. In Dhobley and its surroundings, there are 6 settlements with approximately 1,380 households.Considering Kismayo has the highest rate of IDPs emanating from Lower Shabelle, Banadir, Gedo, Bay and Middle Juba the area will be given more emphasis.
As of July 2019, 51,000 new internal displacements were monitored by the UNHCR-led Protection and Returns Monitoring Network (PRMN). Of the 244,000 new displacements monitored year-to date, drivers include insecurity (126,000), drought related (101,000), floods (6,000) and other reasons (10,000). In 2019, conflict/insecurity and drought are the biggest causes of displacement with 126,000 and 101,000 respectively. Lower Juba counts as among the top ten in arrivals in 2019, and fifth in July 2019.
HAPEN assessment indicate a high prevalence of households living in poor quality makeshift shelters ( made of simple twigs covered with tattered pieces of old clothes, and polythene), which offer little structural integrity and protection from the vagaries of nature. Poor shelter conditions also raise serious protection concerns as households are more vulnerable to crime, GBV and child protection issues. According to REACH initiative, shelter was listed as a priority need by 48% of households, with over half of households listing it as a priority in nine regions. Shelter was highlighted by 60% of IDP households as a priority need, the second most commonly identified need after food (89%).
In the project area they are settling, the UNHCR, Protection, Monitoring and Return Network cite high levels of human rights violations, including increased GBV incidents With those who have settled in Lower Juba being predominantly returnees, the projects will be designed to ensure the interventions are protection responsive. The interventions will target the most vulnerable giving emphasis to households with under five children, pregnant and lactating women Persons with Disabilities and old people.
Aligning to strategic and Shelter&NFI clusters strategies, HAPEN aims at improving the living conditions and protection of IDPs and returnees in sites whilst ensuring equitable access to services and assistance for the drought and conflict displaced IDPs.
In alignment with other clusters, HAPEN 1) build better coordination mechanisms structures at site/community level 2) protection mainstreaming through awareness raising and trainings, and creation of proper referral pathways for GBV survivors.
HAPEN will provide shelter/NFI assistance to 60,000 vulnerable beneficiaries. The beneficiaries support will include;Distribution of NFI Kits(10,000), distribution of emergency shelter kit (4,000), provision of transitional shelter assistance (900), and construction/rehabilitation of community infrastructure (10 infrastructures benefiting 5,000).
HAPEN will use CVA to deliver significant portion of the interventions. In this regards, in all projects with the CVA component, working with clusters, ICCG, and Cash Working Group HAPEN will undertake assessment to ensure the appropriateness and identify the best modality to use. In line with the in-kind programming, shelter-specific cash programming will be coordinated through the shelter program with any shelter integrated through ICCG. HAPEN shall report on agreed cash monitoring indicators in the 3W matrix and by sharing post-distribution monitoring reports with the respective clusters.

Please provide further explanation

In the project design, HAPEN has factored protection mainstreamimg by ensuring close cordination and integration with CCCM and protection interventions. The CCCM component of the project is designed to provide both the beneficiaries and other stakeholders with relevant cross-sectoral data to allow for an evidence based response, as well as to ensure that displaced people have the information they need to access services safely.In this regard, HAPEN will adopt the following key actions:a) Ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution.b) Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimises safety risk – be aware that beneficiariesmay face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their kits home.d) Design and construction of shelters must preserve the safety and dignity of the affected populations:e) Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond tophysical/sexual violence arising due to crowded shelter conditions.f) Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups' Strengthen participation and empowermenta) Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific shelter and NFI requirements and preferences for the location of distribution points and design of shelters.b) Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the responsec) Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage traditional construction methods, particularly in areas where transportation of shelter material is restricted for logistical or security reasons; equip beneficiaries with knowledge of safe construction practices.d) Coordinate with civil society specialising in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases. HAPEN consider different processes when developing shelter programmes. These processes encourage self-reliance to improve and maintain shelter solutions, maximising tenure security for men and women, and ensuring that short-term assistance is designed to take longer horizons into account. We ensure integration of Shelter needs with other essential services liked education, WASH and health and livelihoods. On livelihood HAPEN has been partnering IOM and GIZ to implement IDP and returnees responsive livelihoods. The project is closely linked with Durable Solution through ensuring it takes into account it take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. Advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent. Community engagement is critical to inform reintegration analysis and programing to make solutions lasting, locally relevant and supportive of social cohesion and to adopt a ‘displacement affected communities’ approach- inclusive of returnees, IDPs and host communities.
