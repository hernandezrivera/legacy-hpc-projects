Project Summary

"Save the Children (SC) is committed to strengthening protective factors and reducing risk factors in Cox's Bazar through its Integrated and Holistic Child Protection program. This program will operate at every level of the socio-ecological framework, aiming to reach 277,532 people of concerns including 1,87,159 children in 12 camps and 04 unions in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. SC's Child Protection Program will encompass both preventive and responsive measures to ensure a protective environment for children. It will focus on critical interventions and services tailored to the needs of children of varying ages, genders, and diverse groups. Special attention will be given to Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), child-headed households, child marriage and pregnancy, children with disabilities, domestic violence, and child labor.
The program will promote the overall well-being of children and establish a protective environment through a range of services, including case management for at-risk children, structured and semi-structured psychosocial support (PSS), life skill-based sessions, adolescent-led initiatives, parenting training, community-based activities, and System Strengthening. SC operates 98 Child Protection Multipurpose facilities in the camps to provide these services, strengthening community mechanisms as a core project intervention to build resilient communities.
To ensure inclusivity, SC will conduct surveys using Washington Set questionnaires to identify people, especially children with disabilities, for inclusion as project participants. Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centers (MPCAC) will accommodate children of different ages in various time slots and shifts, and materials will be procured and distributed with age-sensitive specifications and sizes. MPCAC also will be act as a child protection hub where will be organize capacity-building training, awareness-raising sessions, workshops, and meeting on child protection.
Capacity-building activities will be scaled up for various stakeholders, including government authorities (CIC, police, LEA agencies), service providers, community-based groups, and community volunteers, including Para social workers (PSWs), parents, and caregivers as part of System Strengthening. SC will also take the initiative to make the Child-Friendly Police Desk operational by providing technical and logistic support.
To address the Early Childhood Development (ECD) needs of children, a dedicated ECD corner will be established in existing MPCACs. Trained facilitators will conduct home-based ECD sessions, and caregivers will receive training and awareness focusing on ECD. SC will maintain regular coordination with Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS) and operate the program in alignment with the sector's strategy. Coordination with CPSS, PWG, and relevant working groups will continue, and SC will contribute to various initiatives, including dedicating staff for CP focal points and submitting different reports, including 5W reports.
Periodical training and awareness sessions on policies, including child safeguarding and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), will be conducted for staff, volunteers, and related stakeholders. SC will prioritize these policies to ensure protection against sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse throughout the project cycle. By targeting equality in access to services, SC will strengthen social cohesion.
Multiple accountability mechanisms in line with the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles will be applied to actively collect feedback while ensuring the safety and dignity of program participants. By fostering partnerships through localization, SC can enhance its technical capabilities and address areas of improvement proactively."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"In 2017, during the Rohingya influx, humanitarian responders witnessed the tragic deaths and injuries of both children and adults, marked by signs of violence, abuse, and severe stress. Although the initial impact of violence has lessened over time, more than six years after the influx, the Rohingya refugee crisis continues to be a protection emergency at its core. Children still live in insecure, overcrowded spaces, facing specific risks such as family separation, sexual abuse, physical violence, child marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, and child labor. Adolescent girls remain particularly vulnerable to risks related to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and trafficking into the forced sex trade.The Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA) 2023 paints a stark picture of the refugee community scattered across 33 camps, revealing a critical reality. A staggering 66.5% of households report their children's absence from community-based child protection and psychosocial programs in the last month. This issue is particularly pronounced in Teknaf, with a rate of 67.03%, closely followed by Ukhiya at 66.40%. The gravity of the situation is further underscored by the percentage of households with children experiencing psychological distress, highlighting the imperative for urgent mental health interventions.The host community's data mirrors the challenges faced by refugees, emphasizing the following of child protection issues, often exacerbated by socio-economic factors. Psychosocial distress is a pressing concern in Cox's Bazar, affecting 38% of refugee households in the last two weeks and 33% among the host community. These findings emphasize the shared challenges and the critical need for comprehensive support across all communities in the region.Cross-cutting indicators reveal the interconnected nature of these challenges. Limited access to education directly correlates with increased child protection vulnerabilities. A significant number of children, lacking structured learning opportunities, face heightened risks. This underscores the urgency for synergized interventions that encompass both education and child protection.The disturbing prevalence of child labor in specific camps and unions provides insight into the broader socio-economic challenges contributing to the child protection dilemma. These figures, when compared to global benchmarks, signal a compelling need for comprehensive, context-specific solutions.As well as both the host communities and refugee populations in Cox's Bazar face shared protection concerns and risks. They are vulnerable to the growing impacts of climate change and the increasing frequency of disasters in the district, including monsoons, flooding, landslides, and fires. Children in both communities are struggling to cope with the current situation, which includes reduced access to services, stressors in the home due to income loss, and increased anxiety. Child protection concerns continue to evolve based on emerging crises, emphasizing the importance of understanding and meeting their needs.According to the CPIMS+ 2023 data, Save the Children's analysis of Child Protection Cases reveals that children in the age groups of 12-14, 9-11, and 6-8 predominantly encounter instances of physical abuse. Conversely, children aged 0-2 and 3-5 are more prone to experiences of neglect. The findings highlight that sociocultural norm, coupled with a deficiency in knowledge and awareness regarding child protection among parents and caregivers, play pivotal roles as contributing factors to these incidents.Simultaneously, secondary data indicates a general increase in protection issues in the camps, especially related to crime and theft, along with a heightened presence of criminal groups with increased levels of control. It is now more critical than ever to provide life-saving child protection services in both the host community and refugee populations."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"Save the Children is committed to upholding the Rationalization Principles and actively participated in the rationalization process of Child Protection (CPSS) in 2021-22, which is set to be executed in 2023. As a result of this rationalization effort, our program is designed to be implemented in 12 camps.Within our operational camps, we offer a comprehensive Child Protection package that caters to the diverse needs of children across different age groups, caregivers, and the broader community. SC places a strong emphasis on integrating our programs with other key sectors, including education, health, nutrition, livelihood, and WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene). In addition, we have dedicated activities aimed at fostering coordination with other sectors, including relevant authorities. These activities encompass capacity enhancement, discussion sessions, regular coordination meetings, and collaborative campaigns designed to address protection needs in the broader community.SC has developed a robust fundraising strategy to ensure the seamless continuation of our projects, minimizing any potential interruptions. We have been operating in various sectors, including Child Protection, since 2015 and have built a solid rapport with both the community and the government. Through our system-strengthening activities, we have successfully established strong partnerships in Child Protection with the government and other service providers. These collaborations are instrumental in enhancing the overall impact and effectiveness of our program."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"Protection is the core of all Save the Children's programs. We unwaveringly adhere to a set of core protection principles, which guide our approach at every phase of our projects. These principles include Prioritizing Safety and Dignity, Avoiding Causing Harm, Ensuring Meaningful Access, Upholding Accountability, Promoting Participation, and Fostering Empowerment.To effectively implement these principles, Save the Children follows a rigorous process that includes risk mapping, mitigation planning, and regular risk monitoring for each of our projects. Risk mapping and mitigation planning are not just optional but are considered mandatory steps in our project implementation.In our quest to ensure safety and prevent further harm, we actively engage adolescents in safety mapping exercises. This child-friendly approach enables us to identify safe and unsafe places, along with the underlying reasons for these designations. Moreover, adolescents collaborate to develop strategies for mitigating risks, contributing to a safer environment.Child participation is a central tenet of our approach, and we strive to set exemplary standards for meaningful child involvement in our programs. Some of our successful initiatives include child-led projects and initiatives, periodic discussion sessions involving Camp In charge (CIC) and children, and regular feedback sessions with children. Importantly, we take this feedback seriously and actively address their concerns and recommendations to enhance our projects further."

3.3 - Age & Disability

"Save the Children remains deeply committed to delivering child protection services that are meticulously tailored to meet the unique and evolving needs of children across different age groups. SC will continue to employ finely crafted, age-appropriate interventions to address the multifaceted requirements of infants, young children, school-aged children, and adolescents. This unwavering commitment is at the core of our mission, ensuring that every child we serve will receive services and support that not only align with their developmental stage but also respect their distinctive individual needs.Aligned with our steadfast dedication to inclusivity, our program will initiate surveys utilizing the Washington Set questionnaires, a comprehensive tool designed for the identification of individuals, with a specific emphasis on children with disabilities. This diligent approach is specifically crafted to ensure their active inclusion as integral project participants. Beyond our regular activities, Save the Children will take decisive measures to address the specific needs of children with disabilities. These measures will encompass providing comprehensive support for assistive devices, enhancing overall accessibility, and implementing a comprehensive range of actions aimed at nurturing an inclusive environment where each and every child can thrive and reach their full potential."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"Save the Children is dedicated to enhancing child participation at all levels, ensuring the voices of both boys and girls across diverse age groups are amplified. This empowerment is evident through their active involvement in designing, executing, and monitoring program interventions, adopting a holistic approach to minimize harm and address the distinctive needs of girls, boys, adolescents, women, and men.Children's participation, including adolescents, is facilitated through methods such as Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) and participatory approaches. Transparency is maintained by sharing assessment and consultation findings, accompanied by clear explanations for decisions made. SC takes a proactive role in empowering adolescents by facilitating club activities where consultations occur, allowing them to voice their rights. Adolescents play a key role in designing activities, leading meetings, and executing action plans. They actively communicate with and engage Child and Adolescents, Community-Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) members to address concerns and participate in activities simultaneously.In community-level consultation and decision-making processes, committees, including children, assume pivotal roles in each protection space. SC adheres to child and adolescent-friendly versions of quality benchmarks for every Child Protection service, incorporating child-friendly response mechanisms. The organization has a Feedback and Response Mechanism guideline encompassing eight child-friendly feedback tools, such as dot voting, risk mapping, and youth focal points. To foster active engagement and open dialogue, SC maintains a toll-free hotline and helpdesk, diligently collecting feedback through regular group discussions and mechanisms like door-to-door visits, exit interviews, and post-distribution monitoring. This comprehensive approach reflects SC's commitment to excellence in child protection services and active participation."

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) stands as one of the cornerstones of Save the Children's core policies. We place a paramount emphasis on maintaining a robust internal mechanism that encompasses a comprehensive range of measures. These measures include the implementation of a Code of Conduct, compulsory induction training for staff, volunteers, and partners, and periodic refresher training. In addition, we prioritize the development and vigilant monitoring of risk registers and the continuous capacity building of PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CSG) focal points.As a manifestation of our zero-tolerance stance on PSEA, Save the Children ensures that PSEA and CSG visibility materials are readily available in all project locations. These materials constantly remind us of our commitment to the safety and well-being of all involved.To maintain a transparent and accessible reporting mechanism, Save the Children operates a toll-free hotline number, prominently included in the visibility materials. In terms of community engagement, SC also conducts regular campaigns and awareness sessions, providing children, caregivers, and community people with a spectrum of avenues to report incidents, provide feedback, and receive a response. When a PSEA complaint/issue is identified/registered/documented, SC's Safeguarding team works on the issue while maintaining utmost confidentiality. A TOR is developed for this purpose, including objectives, process, child-friendly or age-specific questions, a timeline, and investigation member names. According to the policy, a complaint has to be resolved within 90 days, and after resolving, the survivor is informed about the result.Furthermore, SC conducts project-based risk assessments and sector-wise PSEA assessments yearly. Besides having PSEA focal points, SC is also a member of the PSEA Network, hence maintaining an alliance with ISCG."

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"As an integral component of our Emergency Preparedness and Response efforts, we prioritize the vital dissemination of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) messages throughout the community. SC does pre-during-post scenario analysis and takes anticipatory early action to lower the impact of the forecasted disaster. Also, SC do contingency and business continuity plan to run in the CP activities during disasters. Moreover, our committed staff members are poised to actively partake in a spectrum of response activities, which notably include their involvement in the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) within their respective camps. Beyond this, they stand ready to adapt and fulfill similar roles as dictated by the ever-evolving circumstances.SC will do emergency response aligning with CPSS EPRP guideline. As part of country’s Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), SC will act as coordination partners of CPSS and will support the dissemination of early warning messages based on the CPP guidance. SC’s community-based committees will be capacitated on DRR and act as first responders in preventing protection risks, identifying CP risks and referring. SC’s camp level CP focal points will continue to provide updated referral pathway and emergency message. Effective linkage will be maintained among these community-based committees, volunteers, CP staff, camp-level CP focal points and Emergency focal points. Our approach is comprehensive and well-rounded, aiming to cover every facet of emergency preparedness and response which is aligned with the CPSS EPRP guidelines and its connection to child-focused interventions. We recognize the utmost significance of the tasks at hand, whether it's the dissemination of DRR messages or the active engagement of our staff. Each element contributes to our overarching mission of safeguarding our community in times of need."
