Project Summary

This project aims at enhancing the living condition of displacement affected communities by providing Non Food Items (NFIs), Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) and cash for the construction of transitional shelters. The targeted beneficiaries of the proposed intervention are 10,000 HHs for an approximate total of 60,000 individual beneficiaries. Protection Monitoring implemented throughout the entire duration of the project, will inform programming and effective decision making. The population in need of humanitarian aid in Somalia is estimated to be approximately 2.3m. This is also the estimated number of people affected by displacement in Somalia, including both protracted and recently displaced population. This project intends to deliver to the most in need NFIs and ESKs as well as cash for transitional shelter in line with the shelter cluster objective 1, 2 and 3. The Action will ensure that the targeted population enjoys an adequate standard of living while also working towards longer term solution and reintegration. The areas targeted by the project are some of the most affected by the displacement crisis (driven both by conflict and natural hazards) such as Bay, Lower Shabelle, Bakool, Banadir and Middle Shabelle regions. INTERSOS will also ensure that complementarities with other ongoing operations are maximized in the targeted areas.

Needs

Accrding to official data from UNHCR PRMN, 2,194,000 people in Somalia are internally displaced and in urgent need of protection and emergency sheter. Nevertheless, it is also estimated that the total number of displaced may be as high 2.6m. Banadir, Bay and Lower Shabelle are the most affected regions by displacement. The displaced population is a result of conflict as well as natural disasters such as drought and floods which have driven hundreds of thousands of people to seek for humanitarian aid in urban and peri-urban settings by largely settling in improvised IDP sites. The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where the conditions are very poor and forced eviction is a common threat. The settlements are often crowded with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions thus raising protection and health concerns. Emergency Shelter and NFIs support is paramount to ensure adequate living conditions and improve chances for longer term integration in the community. In the context of the IDP sites, young boys, girls, children, infants and women are by far the most vulnerable. Among the most urgent needs of the displaced are emergency shelter and protection from violence and abuse. This Action will address the basic needs of the displaced population in line with the three shelter cluster objectives in some of the regions most affected by displacement. The project will also respond to medium term needs of the targeted beneficiaries in form of provision of cash transitional shelters with the aim to support reintegration and durable solutions by targeting the displacement affected community at large.Moreover, in line with the HRP Objectives, thei Action will aim at promoting mobile communities and at enhancing the chances of re-integration in both ares of settlement or areas of origin by working both in areas of departure and arrival.

Activities

Activity 1: Creation of a selection committee and selection of beneficiaries. The objective of the selection committee will be to implement a transparent and fair process with full engagement of the affected community and to develop selection's criteria. The criteria for the selection will be based on protection risks of the HH.Activity 2: Market Assessment. The market assessment will not duplicate efforts but rather take into consideration existing guidelines. For each targeted location on a market assessment will be carried out and approved by the selection committee.Activity 3: Procurement of NFIs and ESKs. The procurement shall ensure timely distribution of the items and respect of INTERSOS' and Donor's procurement procedures. The Kits' composition will be discussed and agreed with the shelter cluster.Activity 4: Community Mobilization and workshop on best practices for the use of ESKs. The community mobilization will serve the purpose to explain/clarify: selection criteria, aim of the project, distribution modalities, emergency shelter, transitional shelters and camp management best practices, distribution of hotline number for feedback/complaints mechanism.Activity 5: Distribution of NFIs and ESKs to 9,000 HHs (in kind or cash/vouchers). Beneficiaries will be mobilized in advance before the distribution and modality of the distribution will be clarified. HHs details and finger prints will be taken before the distribution and verified at the distribution site. Activity 6: Cash distribution (1000 HHs) for the construction of transitional shelters. The cash per HH will correspond to 1000 USD which will be distributed in two installments.Activity 7: Post Distribution Monitoring. PDM will take place after the distribution in order to evaluate the impact of the activity and collect feedback from the beneficiaries. A comprehensive report will be developed, shared and discussed with the Donor and the Shelter Cluster.Activity 8: Protection Monitoring.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The Project aims at responding to emergency needs of the displacement affected population in Bay, Bakool, Banadir, Middle Shabelle and Lower Juba regions, thus improving the living standards of the affected population. The project, through the distribution of cash for the construction of transitional shelters for 200 HHs who are in possess of land titles will strongly contribute to promoting durable solution/resilience of displacement affected communities. This objective will be achieved by ensuring that displaced communities who intend to integrate in their current area of settlement fulfill adequate living standards. In order to achieve this result, the project will be integrated with other ongoing actions implemented by INTERSOS in the areas of intervention to promote access to basic services for the targeted communities such as schools and hospitals in order to build their resilience and to promote long term solutions to displacement. INTERSOS will take advantage of its long term engagement in reintegration programs to ensure excellent coordination with other relevant partners in the targeted locations. The proposed Action is linked to the second IASC criteria on durable solutions to guarantee an adequate standard of living. The main targeted population group will be the IDP community and vulnerable host community members in areas affected by displacement. INTERSOS will transfer responsibilities to the relevant local authorities/community members during the project implementation through enhanced coordination and engagement with the government and with the affected population. INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger, its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility to promote durable solutions to displacement. Moreover, the design of the project is implemented in coordination with the relevant government authorities and through a systematic engagement of the targeted communities in order to promote community ownership and sustainability by using existing community structures, such as camp management committees and camp leaders, in order to define priorities, implement activities and monitoring of the services' delivered.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

At the beginning of the project, adequate time will be dedicated to discuss with all the project staff protection risks related to each projects’ activity. This exercise will help to identify possible threats and to avoid or reduce their impact on the project. At level of planned activities, the main risk is related to the condentiality about the information and the proper selection and verification of the beneciaries. In this regard, INTERSOS guarantees that staff will ensure the repsect of selection criteria based on protection vulnerabilities with the support of INTERSOS Protection coordinator. The registration and verication of beneciaries will be done through an ad hoc committee to ensure fair representation of the most disadvantaged community members (i.e. minority groups, pregnant/lactating women, women headed HHs...). The relevant authorities will be fully involved from the onset of the project to minimize protection risks related to the implementation of the activities. In order to avoid risks related to miscommunication and unmet expectations, activities will be planned in advance with engagement of all the main stakeholders and beneciaries. Furthermore, different population groups will be targeted (both IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and conflict based on population's groups. This approach will be paramount in displacement affected communities in order to promote social cohesion in the areas of operation. Community mobilization will be critical to promote and fair and transparent program and to mitigate and prevent protection risks at community level. INTERSOS staff will be trained on the internal policies on child protection and protection from abuse and sexual exploitation. A feedback and complaints mechanism will be used during the entire implementation of the program and complaints will be properly reported and investigated.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Data regarding beneficiaries will be disaggregated to effectively inform programming. INTERSOS will identify groups who face difficulties accessing services and specific time will be reserved to discuss at the beginning of the project all the activities which could be critically affected by social exclusion. This exercise will help to identify possible threats and to avoid/reduce their impact on the project and it will be conducted together with local authorities and relevant stakeholders. At level of planned activities, the main risk is related to the exclusion of vulnerable population groups such as women, children and monority groups in all phases of the project, therefore, vulnerable groups will be strongly engaged at the planning phase of the project in order to ensure their opinions are embedded in the programming. In this regard, INTERSOS guarantees that staff will undertake trainings on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection Coordinator. The relevant local authorities will be fully involved from the onset of the project in order to minimize risk of exclusion of disadvantaged groups in the targeted locations. Project's activities will always focus on gender and age mark and ensure proper engagement of all the population. Priority during beneciaries' selection will be given to women and children. Moreover, different population groups will be targeted (both IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion based on population's groups. Real time review meetings will be implemented in order to share good practices and lessons learnt on a quarterly basis. INTERSOS staff will be trained on the internal policies on child protection and protection from abuse and sexual exploitation. A feedback and complaints mechanism through a hot line number will be used during the entire implementation of the program and complaints effectively investigated.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Project's activities will ensure proper engagement of all the population groups with priority to women and children. Different population groups will be targeted (both IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and to promote successful reintegration and long term solutions. This approach is paramount in displacement affected communities in order to enhance social cohesion and peaceful coexistence. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager and Field Coordinators. Proper engagement and information sharing will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. Most of all, community representatives will be engaged in order to promote project's ownership and to support local solutions to emerging challenges as well as communitye's decision making. Wide representation of the community will be taken into account especially over decisions on distribution of resources. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks thus allowing INTERSOS Staff to safely implement activities in all the targeted locations. Monitoring the evolving security situation in areas of operation will be also critical in order to ensure the safety of the staff implementing activities. Risks of fraud and corruption will be minimized by segregation of duties and strong monitoring system to ensure quality control of activities implemented with the ultimate aim of avoiding conflicts. Beneciaries will be provided with hotline numbers to refer their complaints or feedback. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address human errors thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services such as cash transfers.
