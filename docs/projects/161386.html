Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Nutrition Cluster objectives, namely 1. Increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women; 2. Increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women; 3. Increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive intervention from WASH, Health, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups; 4.Strenghten nutrition information system for evidence based nutrition response.
The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the nutrition care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups for what concerns treatment but also prevention and timely detection of acute malnutrition. In an area where GAM is close to 15% emergency threshold and 89% of households are food insecure, the project aim contributing enhancing the resilience and the capacity to cope with current and future crises of the targeted communities, including those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging, besides requiring specific response routine interventions are not used to provide. Such response will be strengthen by integrating the nutrition related activities with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both nutrition and clinical/psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach. WASH, FSL, Education will be given high attention, with the result of the applied strategy to be wide enough to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, as poor hygiene and access to clean water, limited food availability, low knowledge. Nutrition services will be provided at Health Facility level or through community based initiatives in their catchment area and under their supervision and guide, resulting highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the inter- related complications. The project will target both displaced and host population, the range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile) is expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current nutrition care coverage. The proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Targeted Counties’ increasing needs and challenging response are due to: internal conflicts, affecting services provision/access; IDPs burden over hosting families, whose resources are limited; inadequate HFs number, distribution, conditions (staff number/skills, equipment, supplies) for effective integration with and provision of nutrition care; failure of funding mechanism in sustaining routine services. In Former Lakes, Acute Malnutrition is critical, close to the emergency threshold of 15% for U5. The situation is even worst for CBA women (25% stunting). Malnutrition drivers are not properly addressed. Food Security is matter of concern for 89% of households, with 57.9% of population in crises/emergency/catastrophe status and 75% of population with poor food consumption and 85% with poor dietary diversity. WASH is poor, as confirmed by common occurrence of water borne diseases (79%,household self-diagnoses in 2018): 95% households practice open defecation, 66% do not have soap. While 82% would have access to protect water sources, 63% do not feel safe when accessing them. Insecurity is hampering access to resources for 46% of households, matter of further concern when considering 62% of households are headed by a woman, more exposed to violence when dealing with domestic tasks. Female headed households are more likely to be food insecure. Former Lakes State has the highest percentage of households where food utilization is decided by women (89%), but in a good part of them (23%) accessing food supplies implies safety concerns. Nutrition services integration at HFs is still to be completed, especially in case of different IP; ie. growth monitoring is not part of routine OPD package (36%), resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification. Nutrition services outcomes are positive (cure rate above 85%), but their coverage is limited and defaulter rate still high (11%). In 2019, more attention has been put on prevention and on field activities but results are below the ideal, with only 54% of pregnant population benefiting of individual counselling and positive nutrition practices poorly adopted (exclusive breastfeeding 56%). Group counselling is increasing, but participation is limited and unbalanced, with only 14% male attendants. BHI delay and the few nutrition staff at the HFs, forcing CNVs to operate there instead of moving to the villages, result in zero MYCN activities at household. This implies nutrition care being not easily accessible for the most vulnerable groups, whose mobility is constrained and whose status put them even more at risk od developing malnutrition: people with disabilities and/or mental disorders, IDPs, women, children, elders, sick. The size of their problem is difficult to estimate. HMIS Nutrition indicators are not disaggregated by gender/age/vulnerability. NIS asks for detailed information but the double system implies under rather than improved reporting. This hampered local authorities capacities and possibilities of taking informed decision on nutrition services strenghten. In Mundri East County (Former Western Equatoria State), various partners have started intervening on Nutrition in the peripheral area, being penalized by insecurity, which hampers services functionality and people access to them. Further, they are not in conditions to offer a properly operating Stabilization Centre. Lui Hospital, managed and supported by CUAMM, is the only facility that never stopped working since the beginning of the crises in mid 2015. It is serving as Stabilization Centre for the entire Greater Mundri Region and it represents a positive example of full integration between health and nutrition package, to be maintained and enhanced further. [2018DHIS, IPCSouthSudanAugust2019, FSNMRRound22, 2018&2019NIS]

2 - Activities

The intervention will strengthen Acute Malnutrition management institutionalization into Health System with CHD and communities involvement, to ensure responsiveness to most vulnerable. It will complement current intervention in 5 Former Lakes State Counties where all health providers (54) offer OTP/TSFP, while SC is operational in selected PHCCs (2) and Hospitals (3). Further, SC is sustained in Rumbek State Hospital, Adior PHCC (Former Lakes State), Lui Hospital (Former Western Equatoria State). HFs will be supported with staff (recruitment/training/supervision), supplies/equipment (acquired in kind or procured as per needs on ground), infrastructures upgrade, to provide a package of prevention/lifesaving treatment services as per national guidelines on CMAM management. Further, they will be referral site for Community Nutrition Volunteers, operating in the villages, for prevention and malnutrition cases identification and referral. The combination of static and outreach services delivery modalities will be effective in reaching the vulnerable/neglected groups, whose access to and utilization of care is challenging, affecting both enrollment in and adherence to nutrition programs. At this regard, CNVs are expected to reduce defaulters, through careful tracking and tailored solutions. For that and to promote people nutrition status, CNVs will work in cooperation with local leaders/community representatives, involved from intervention planning to monitoring, passing through direct participation in the proposed activities. CNVs and communities will realize Nutrition Cluster suggested “paradigm shift”. Prevention will be given new relevance, with IYCF practices wide implementation (individual/group counselling, including mothers to mothers support, social behavior change communication...), Vit A distribution mass campaign and a multidisciplinary approach, aimed to address malnutrition drivers. This will go beyond Nutrition/Health integration, expanding to Education, Food Security, Wash, Protection, which Stakeholders will coordinate to maximize intervention outcomes. Nutrition/Health integration will not be limited to site sharing. MUAC/Growth monitoring at EPI/ANC/OPD will make health services safe and accessible entry points for U5/PLW enrolment into Nutrition Programs and malnutrition complications will be promptly treated. Health staff will be trained/supervised on how to identify and treat or refer the most common diseases in malnourished patients. The sustainable and effective integration of Nutrition interventions in County Health plan will be promoted through improved nutrition data collection, analysis, reporting for informed/evidence based decision making. Early Child Development principles, piloted at health facilities, will contribute to nutrition component through SAM children stimulation. Nutrition messages will be included in school health education sessions. CNVs and Lead Mothers will involve families in kitchen gardens/cooking demonstrations to learn how to improve dietary variety with locally available food. HFs offering nutrition program will have improved WASH facilities, with functioning latrines, hand wash points and clean water. Nutrition education sessions will include hygiene promotion messages, WASH kits for U5 SAM patients will be considered. Protection approach will be streamed, ensuring due attention to particularly vulnerable/neglected groups (SGBV victims, people with disabilities/mental disorders). Nutrition services will be their entry point to specific care; they will be identified, handled and referred to specialized centers for psychosocial and legal support and follow up. Network with these facilities will be strengthen for concerned people adequate attendance. Staff will be trained/sensitized to respect the dignity and address of these patients needs. Specific data collection tools will be introduced to track their access to services and provide authorities information for adequate response

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project aims to benefit both host and displaced population. Actually, one of the problems it will address is the inadequacy of routine nutrition services to respond to an increasing population, under IDPs influx, especially when IDPs prefer to locate in remote sites, not served by any health/nutrition facilities. The adoption of mobile service delivery modalities is functional to bring the needed assistance at the closest possible level to beneficiaries, reducing both the distance they would have covered to reach static providers and the risk they would have been exposed to while moving, usually higher for displaced population.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CUAMM added value is mobilizing different donors on the same comprehensive objective, within a broader intervention, aligned to local priorities. This is functional to the effective integration between different sector and between humanitarian and development component, as in this specific case, with the present project being part of a wider program CUAMM is implementing in the same area, benefiting of various contributions (including HPF, UNICEF, WFP). All these constitute a solid ground, assuming Health System running costs and nutrition supplies availability, and their longer term perspective allows targeting local authorities and staff with capacity building, aimed to create the conditions for the taking over of health/nutrition integrated services management. Economical sustainability is unlikely to be achieved in the short/medium term. In the meanwhile, local authorities start resuming the responsibilities connected with system ownership, developing a sense of accountability. CUAMM signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the SMoH in the targeted areas, which specifies each part’s duties and task. CUAMM provides technical and material support for integrated health/nutrition services delivery and management, but the decision making process and responsibility is held by the SMoH, familiarizing with planning, prioritizing, allocating resources...acquiring the capacities it will need once NGOs and donors will step back. The present project will provide additional resources to narrow a specific gap, while already available funds will keep sustaining the overall system and the above mentioned process of capacity building. During project implementation, CUAMM will collect all elements needed to discuss with local authorities and other donors an eventual priorities redefinition and consequent reallocation of existing funds to ensure current proposal activities continuity and/or to raise new resources for this purpose.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to Nutrition Cluster objectives, which are reflecting Ministry of Health priorities, starting from acute malnutrition management institutionalization into the Health System

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Former Lakes Counties are affected by internal clashes producing IDPs movements within their own borders, merged to those coming from other States. Greater Mundri situation is normalizing after years of conflict, but local militia are still maintaining some instability pockets in some areas. In spite of this complex and uncertain context, CUAMM yearly presence in the area, widely recognized and appreciated by both communities and authorities, facilitates the implementation of the proposed activities, which aim is to help local population to overcome all obstacles (including those related to insecurity) hampering access to and utilization of services

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Project targeted Counties are the same CUAMM is present as local health authorities main Implementing Partner for the provision of routine health and nutritions services, role it is playing since many years, in deep cooperation with State Ministry of Health, County Health Departments and local communities. The project complement this long term intervention. It is intended to guarantee prompt response to any emergency occurring in the targeted sites, which routine services would not be in conditions to handle, and/or to complement routine services coverage and capacities to cope with effective demand, including that of those groups not benefiting of any assistance but presenting specific vulnerabilities

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CUAMM will follow Nutrition Cluster orientations to ensure intervention being informed by a people centered approach. CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives. These latest belong to the Health Facilities related Health Committees but also to other community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. CUAMM and CHD will ensure HCs gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, confirming them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. CUAMM and CHD will make the existing coordination and supervision mechanisms as much participatory as possible, to ensure a wide range of actors and beneficiaries to be given the opportunity to present their perspective and share their concerns regarding implemented services and remaining gaps. The recently introduced Client Satisfaction Survey offered the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries. The questionnaire is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient and it has been piloted to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different education background. CUAMM will also consider to install a box where patients will be allowed to put their comments on the received services, working also as anonimous complain feedback mechanism. When possible, CUAMM will adopt Globa Nutrition Cluster operational framework indicators to monitor intervention accountability level
