Project Summary

Building on the humanitarian work delivered by Street Child on education and protection, Street Child will provide crucial livelihoods support for 3,000 vulnerable families to participate again in the local economy. Street Child will prioritize households where extremely vulnerable categories of children have been identified and work to ensure that families build the financial capacity to meet their long-term needs. Families comprising IDPs in Host Communities, returnees and host communities will benefit from assistance in the form of cash grants, business training and mentoring, and supported savings schemes. 70% of the beneficiaries that receive the family business grant will be women and female headed households.

Needs

The most common and most important consequences of conflict events in North East Nigeria are displacement, experienced by 26% of conflict-affected households, and lost economic opportunity, experienced by 28% of conflict-affected households. Large numbers of IDPs (1,8 million; DTM XXIII) as well returning IDPs continue to place a heavy strain on already vulnerable communities with pre-existing inadequate levels of access to basic infrastructure and services. Limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands, leading to societal tensions between IDPs in Host Communities, returnees and host communities. Over 5.5 million people in need, of them 1 million are IDPs in host communities and 1.5 million IDPs and returnees. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods, especially for girls, youth and women. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs (whether they choose to return, locally integrate in their place of displacement, or relocate elsewhere in the country) and restoring social cohesion. Without addressing this urgent need for livelihood restoration, a premature return of IDPs (or refugees) could lead to secondary displacement & deepen IDPs’ vulnerability to any even greater extend. Micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women is essential to address negative coping strategies which many households have already resorted to. These include: buying food on credit, selling of productive assets, borrowing cash from others and more importantly there is severe protection concerns including early marriage, child recruitment, child labor, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse. As of now 52 % of households reported having no income and resorted to debt contraction.

Activities

Due to the current gender inequalities in these target communities, where women have lower opportunities to create and access economic empower, Street Child will prioritize female beneficiaries in this programme. 70% of the 3,000 beneficiaries that receive the family business grant will be women from female-headed households. Street Child’s previous programmes have shown that investing in supporting women from these households has a higher impact on improving children return to school, as female caregivers tend to spend more money on their child’s education than male. Market analysis: Street Child will conduct a market analysis to identify the most profitable types of business in the areas of operation, map existing value chains and lessons learned from previous interventions. A key learning was when operating in LGAs with lower levels of security there is a need to provide more secure methods of transferring the money to female beneficiaries as they often report feeling unsafe with traditional forms of cash transfer. Therefore, Street Child trialed using a third party mobile money company that facilitated the transfer of cash in a safe setting at community level which removes the need for the beneficiaries to travel outside of their community with larger amounts of money. The community level rapid market assessments allow the collection of data that help inform the business training and ensure it is based on the reality of the local market. Vocational trainers are hired to teach the beneficiaries skills which are marketable in their local community. This will include desk-based research and interviews with other agencies, as well as primary research carried out by the livelihoods team & consultation with local community stakeholders. Street Child’s existing tools and training content will be adapted based on this assessment. Tailored business assessment & plan creation: Following selection and registration onto the programme, the livelihood officers will carry out an assessment of household income and caregivers’ capacity to undertake business. On this basis, a trained livelihoods officer will provide varying levels of tailored support to enable each beneficiary developing a business plan for the use of the cash transfer. Vocational and business management training: Two cycles of four-day training courses will be carried out, following Street Child’s proven model. Afterwards the livelihoods officers will train each beneficiary in basic business skills, including bookkeeping, pot saving, and basic business strategy. The market analysis will also identify key areas for vocational training relevant to selected business types. Cash based transfer: Upon completion of the training, Street Child will give beneficiaries a cash grant of NGN 30,000 to establish a small business. Monitoring, mentoring & savings: The livelihood caseworkers will visit the beneficiaries the week after disbursement to monitor investment of the grant, and carry out bi-weekly monitoring thereafter for months to provide business advice and encouragement, and to collect weekly savings (NGN200 per week). At the end of the 15 weeks saving period, the savings will be provided back with the full amount they managed to save and encouraged to reinvest in their business, thus supporting sustainability. Street Child will also support the creation of a peer-to-peer mentoring system, encouraging the use of adashe informal savings groups and cooperative groups. These will be similar to the Village Savings and Loans Association scheme (VSLA) and Street Child will engage members of the communities to help support the setting up and running of these groups to ensure continuity beyond the project.Street Child has learnt the benefit of having a continuous presence in the community and therefore, as well as having implementing partners how regularly visit and monitor field activity, employs and trains community members to support beneficiaries on an ongoing basis.
