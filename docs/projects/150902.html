Project Summary

Overall context of crises in Ukraine:
The human toll of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical, with 4.4 million people affected by the crisis, of whom 3.4 million require humanitarian assistance and protection. The shelling of urban areas and civilian infrastructure means that 60 per cent of 200,000 people living along the 457-kilometre ‘contact line’ in the Government-controlled area (GCA) are affected by shelling regularly, and almost 40 per cent are affected every day. There is just under a million individual crossings of the ‘contact line’ each month, which is rapidly becoming one of the most mine contaminated stretches of land in the world. Food insecurity has doubled since 2016, with 1.2 million people food insecure, and there are escalating cases of multi-drug resistant TB, HIV and even polio. HIV prevalence among pregnant women in conflict-affected oblasts is significantly higher than the national average. These impacts are aggravated by Ukraine’s extremely harsh winter, severe restrictions on humanitarian access and limited livelihood opportunities for those affected by the crisis. As hopes of a political solution have waned, so have people’s savings and their ability to cope. Four years into the crisis, millions of people, including 1.6 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), half of whom are the elderly, are being forced to make impossible choices between food, medicine, shelter, heating or their children’s education. The unique demographics of the crisis in Ukraine is that the elderly make up almost 30 per cent of people in need. Those who are most vulnerable are increasingly resorting to risky means to cope, including survival sex, trafficking and alcoholism. Today, the dire humanitarian crisis in eastern Ukraine is protracted and complex, whilst the response is severely underfunded and largely forgotten by the international community.
(HNO2019)
Logic of project intervention is based on Theory of Change and aimed to contribute to resolve main problem of affected population by elimination of core causes according to following program concept:
Main problem
Basic needs of affected population are not secured
Resolved if:
Affected people are secured in food and winterization
Core cause 1
Lack of access of beneficiaries to needed goods
Eliminated if:
Access to necessary goods is ensured. Beneficiaries have appropriate conditions to keep goods.
Project is going to work in 2 program sectors (FSLC, Shelter and NFI). The intervention is focused mostly on buffer zone GCA, especially 5 km distance from the contact line.
According to HNO2019, the overall problematic in the area of intervention described as following:
*Up to 116,000 people are severely food insecure in Donbas (103,000 people in NGCA and 13,000 people in Luhanska and Donetska oblasts GCA . 78% of households in GCA households applied negative coping strategies (2018 Food Security and Livelihoods Assessment)
*With an estimated 200,000 persons residing in this area, this means that approximately some 22,000 persons (some 9,167 households) have insufficient winter heating (Winter Assessment of Government-Controlled Areas within 5km of the Line of Contact Ukraine, February 2018)
Core causes related to access:
*During cold season, kilocalorie requirements intensify and access to food becomes restricted due to food price increases (whilst households face increased costs to cover heating expenses and fuel requirements), lack of availability of agricultural produce, depletion of food stocks and winter weather complicating access to markets (HNO2019)
Framework of project intervention for 2019-2020 to eliminate core causes and contribute to resolve main problems is following:
Overall goal: Needs in food and winterization of 9800 IDPs and residents of buffer zone are secured
Food security
Outcome: Beneficiary has improved coverage of needs in nutrition
Output: Beneficiary obtained 62000 kcal of food per month
Objective: Most vulnerable conflict-affected groups have immediate access to food.
Activity: Distribution of 8800 food packages to 4400 most vulnerable affected people by mobile teams
Geographical scale: GCA, 0-5 km of buffer zone
Winterization
Outcome: Household has improved coverage in heating materials for cold season
Output: Household obtained 2 tons of solid heating materials to cover gap of half of cold season.
Objective: Address essential shelter and NFI needs to most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through in-kind assistance.
Activity: Distribution of 10800 tons of solid heating materials to 5400 conflict affected people by mobile teams
Geographical scale: GCA, 0-5 km of buffer zone
Project uses such feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries as on-line form, boxes for complaints and recommendations, hotlines in each centers. In addition, post distribution monitoring allows to improve the quality of activities
Based on HNO 2019, project is focused on provision of complex humanitarian assistance in critically affected areas, GCA paying attention to balance of vulnerability and gaps in needs of affected people. Gander age equity of beneficiaries and protection issues for access to the aid is taken into account in design of distribution process as well as accountability mechanism and participation of local communities.
To provide access to the most vulnerable people in need project uses several approaches that are efficiently worked out in 2018 for all sectors, namely:
- high-level access is provided by mobility of teams who assist people in remote areas, especially rural ones with bad infrastructure
- targeting of people who are exactly in high-scale humanitarian crises is provided by selection of the most vulnerable affected people with highest needs paying strong attention to the income level and coordination with other local aid providers
- protection is provided by home visits to households to evaluate critical needs and gender roles
- mitigation of tensions between those who obtained assistance and those who did not is provided by participation of local activists in settlements of distribution in process of registration, needs evaluation and distribution approach.
- sustainability of beneficiaries is provided by using possibilities to redirect beneficiaries to Caritas transitional and social projects as well as services provided by other NGOs and governmental agencies.
Caritas Ukraine as implementing partner of interventions of Caritas Germany funded by GFFO has experience of efficient implementation of humanitarian projects since 2015. Interventions in the first half of 2019 will be covered by the approved and currently running project (15.12.17-31.07.19). For the following 1,5 years a follow-up project is planned. The activities are implemented through 4 local centers located in Mariupol, Kramatorsk, Severodonetsk, Zaporizhya. Capacities include highly trained field staff and management, safety& security system and MEAL system that are integrated at the field level.

Needs

Food Security (HNO 2019)While some positive trends lately observed in GCA, 825,000 people (general population, including IDPs) are still facing different levels of food insecurity in Donbas and humanitarian needs remain high on both sides of the Socio-economic situation in eastern Ukraine has changed dramatically since the beginning of 2014. Most indicators in the two most affected oblasts deteriorated in terms of the average values for the country as a whole. Industrial production in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts decreased significantly during 2014-2017, when compared to 2013, which resulted in reduced employment opportunities in the region. In Donetsk oblast unemployment rate doubled through 2013-2018, while in Luhansk oblast it almost tripled . It is estimated that up to 406,000 unemployed working age people including IDPs are in need of livelihoods assistance.Overall in Donetsk and Luhansk GCA, food insecure population decreased from 410,000 people in 2017 to 267,000 in 2018. Among those, around 13,000 are still severely food insecure. As for IDPs outside of Donbas, it is estimated that around 37,000 are food insecure and in need of assistance, which is almost twice lower compared to the last year, yet significant.The share of the frontline population with inadequate food consumption in GCA reduced to 16% in comparison to 21% in the winter period. However, situation has deteriorated when compared to 2017 pre-winter level of 13% and should be considered as a seasonal trend. It is estimated that 30% of female-headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue to be more vulnerable than male-headed households in GCA as well as 19% in NGCAThe amount of social payments including pensions have stayed relatively the same low during the 2014-2017 period. Even following the pension reform in October 2017 food insecurity has improved for some pensioners, however, latest data highlights that the increase in is not sufficient to cover rising prices. At the same time, the actual costs of the WFP monitored food basket in Ukraine increased significantly between June 2017 and June 2018 and reaching the highest historical level of UAH 996 in June 2018Winterization (HNO 2019)The population on both sides of the ‘contact line’ has a growing seasonal needs for items related to winterization. The cost of heating has risen dramatically, and there is a nation-wide effort to improve energy efficiency, households along the “contact line” rarely have resources to invest in insulating their homes.More than 2.5 million people in the Donetska and Luhanska oblasts will have challenges in allocating financial resources to purchase sufficient solid fuel for heating, pay the utilities, and purchase winter clothing, to prepare themselves and their families for the next winter.In the coming winter, more than 300,000 vulnerable families (3/4 of whom in NGCA) may be forced to limit even their expenditures for food if not assisted in the purchase of sufficient solid fuel for heating, in the payment of utilities, and in the purchase of warm clothing and other essential winter items.

Activities & outcomes

Food security: Result 1.1: At least 80% of beneficiaries improved coverage of their needs in nutrition 1 month after final food package obtaining.Activity 1.1: Distribution of 4400 food packages to 2200 conflict affected persons in 2019.Methodological issues:- individual receives 1 package to cover gap in nutrition during 1 months. Depending on food sufficiency, individual may obtain assistance up to 2 times during 2 months.- nutrition value of 1 pack is based on Sphere standards and provides 62000 kcal per individual per month- contain of a package is designed to meet nutrition priorities of adults and children, lactating mothers, elder people. Revision of contain will be based on PDM results each half a year.- prioritization of settlements for distribution will be based on coordination with FSLC.- in case of rapid emergency, the content of food packages may be used for field kitchens. Winterization: Result 2.1: At least 80% of beneficiaries improved coverage of their needs in heating materials 2 months after obtaining assistance for half a winter season.Activity 2.1 : Distribution of 5400 tons of solid heating materials to 2700 conflict affected households 2019.Methodological issues:- 1 household obtains 2 tons of sunflower brickets (coal or wood in equivalent) for half a winter season. Depending on sufficiency of heating material for whole winter season, a HH may obtain assistance 2 times during cold season.- quality of fuel and logistics capacity will be main criteria for tender procedure of choosing supplier to ensure accessibility and sufficiency of aid. Shipment and packing of heating material to a HH will be convenient in further disposal for women, elder and disabled people.- prioritization of settlements for distribution will be based on coordination with Shelter Cluster.
