Project Summary

WFP will contribute to the continuity of operations at Beirut Port, through two inter-linked components: a) provision of wheat flour through the Port of Beirut and b) temporary deployment of bulk grain handling equipment to facilitate the arrival and discharge of bulk grain. Most of the grain will be discharged directly to the facilities that will process the grain though they may be a need to have some storage capacity in the port when direct delivery is not possible.
WFP will provide a one-time injection of 12,500mt of wheat flour to fill the immediate gap in the wheat supply chain, to be distributed via the national bread distribution channels, in order to stabilize the price of bread across Lebanon.
Through its port operation, WFP will temporarily augment the capacity at Beirut Port by providing temporary bulk grain handling equipment that will allow for the handling of at least 3,000mt of grain per day, such as grain evacuators, grain grabs, forklifts, front-end loaders, along with temporary bulk storage capacity. This will enable the continued flow of grain into Lebanon, and ensure there is no disruption in the commercial food supply chain.
The focus on the bulk grain handling capability and storage capacity will restore key capabilities that were lost during the incident in the Beirut port. The bulk grain handling capability will support the commercial sector to continue to bring in the grains necessary to run their operations that are critical to maintaining stable food prices, in particular bread prices.

Lebanon Flash Appeal 2020

Plan fields

1 - Needs

Lebanon is a food import-dependent country, 80 percent of its wheat is imported and most of this is through Beirut Port. The destruction of Beirut Port and accompanying structures has had a direct effect on the country’s food availability. Specifically, the explosion caused structural damage to the port infrastructure used to bring in bulk grain as well as the vertical grain silos used to store this cargo which had a capacity of 120,000mt. In the absence of functioning bulk handling facilities at the port of Beirut there will be strain in receiving the bulk grains that have been ordered months in advance. In light of the disruption of the port in the aftermath of the explosions, there is an urgent need to re-establish the flow of bulk grain for human and animal feed into Lebanon through provision of temporary storage and bulk handling capacity, and to stabilize the price of bread, a staple of Lebanese diets.

2 - Activities

Provide infrastructure and light rehabilitation support to augment port operations and food procurement.
