Project Summary

To mitigate health impacts of COVID-19 in Yobe state, CRS proposes a twelve-month project focusing on RCCE to foster adoption of government and international best practices to prevent further infection and transmission. The goal of this project is to enable targeted Covid-19 affected communities of Yobe State survive with dignity. CRS proposes to work in Damaturu, Potiskum and Gujba LGAs. Damaturu and Potiskum are designated by the government as high priority LGAs for COVID-19 interventions due to their active market centers and potential exposure to the virus. The project will assist 45,000 vulnerable host, displaced and returnee households (270,000 persons). The project will run June 1, 2020 - May 31, 2021.

1 - Needs

Nigeria’s first case of COVID-19 was confirmed on February 28th, 2020. As of April 26th, 2020, Nigeria Centre for Disease Control had confirmed 1,273 COVID-19 cases in 33 States. This number includes 86 cases in the Northeast region. The index case in the BAY states was a patient with no history of regional or international travel, potentially indicating community transmission. As COVID-19 continues to spread within Nigeria, BAY states face compounded concerns due to pre-existing humanitarian crises. In 2020, over 7M individuals are expected to need humanitarian assistance with an estimated 4.4M people requiring urgent assistance. This number includes 2 M individuals displaced by conflict in BAY states. Yobe state alone hosts over 134,000 IDPs; of which 91% are integrated in host communities. Crowded living conditions paired with a dearth of resources for safe, personal hygiene practices contribute to a surge in COVID-19 cases. WHO-recommended protective measures are more challenging in cramped and confined settings with limited cash reserves or livelihoods options. In lower-income areas, government-imposed lockdowns may trigger secondary harm, including hunger, malnutrition, non-coronavirus-related health problems, and death. Research shows that conflict affected households are more vulnerable to diseases such as measles, tuberculosis, malaria, and HIV and AIDs, which could present compounded health concerns or pre-existing conditions causing COVID-19 mortality. Women face increased risk of gender-based violence (GBV) due to confined spaces, heightened stress levels, lost livelihoods and isolation measures. Preliminary CRS Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) suggest households have limited access to reliable information about COVID-19 risks and protective measures. As of April 30, 2020, one case of COVID 19 has been confirmed in Yobe. Earlier in the month, a COVID-19 task force has been established to disseminate information, gather resources, coordinate actions amongst stakeholders, and trace new infections. It is crucial to act preemptively and curtail the spread of COVID-19 within Yobe state. The government has moved to close national and international borders, ban large gatherings, and promote social distancing to the extent possible. However, humanitarian interventions remain vital for enabling communities to protect themselves and to access critical resources. CRS proposes to work in Damaturu, Potiskum and Gujba LGAs. Damaturu and Potiskum are designated by the government as high priority LGAs for COVID-19 interventions due to their active market centers and potential exposure to the virus. Damaturu and Gujba are currently hosting some of the highest numbers of IDPs in the state; while Gujba has the highest number of recent returnees. In Gujba, CRS will also be building on previous emergency work funded by OFDA and FFP in addition to recovery activities funded by the Germany Government.

2 - Activities

COVID-19 Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE): CRS will use both in-person and remote messaging to introduce information about COVID-19, how it is spread and how people can protect themselves. Material will be contextually appropriate, consistent and available in local languages, working to address rumors or misinformation, reduce stigma, and mitigate risk. CRS will coordinate communication with the Government, UN agencies, and other actors to reduce cost, minimize duplication and increase coverage. In-person communication will be carried out using Community Mobilizers who will also carry out routine hygiene promotion. CRS will support an enabling environment introducing nudges – visual and physical cues - to practice physical distancing. Hygiene Promotion: CRS will work through local partners to recruit and train Community Mobilizers (CM) to deliver hygiene promotion messages, and to engage communities in the development of water and sanitation infrastructure. CMs will be responsible for carrying out RCCE activities as well. CMs will reach 45,000 HHs, each head of HH will cascade information to approximately 6 HHmembers. CRS will use diverse hygiene promotion approaches such as direct discussion, radio programs, dramatization of messages, demonstration activities and FGDs.Water Supply: CRS will increase and improve access to water at the community and at the health facility levels. At the community level, CRS will rehabilitate / upgrade 5 water sources and train and provide repair kits to 5 WMCs. A handwashing station, consisting of a re-fillable water container with a tap and soap, will be installed at each rehabilitated water point. This is expected to increase access to water for drinking and handwashing for 5,000 individuals (1,000 persons per water point). CRS will include guidance on COVID-19 prevention measures at water points in addition to operation and maintenance (O&M) trainings for WMCs. WMC members will also be responsible for daily chlorination of water points with tablets, which will be monitored by CRS project officers. At the health facility level; CRS will rehabilitate / upgrade water points at 48 primary health centers through increasing storage capacity or reticulation to distribution points to facilitate handwashing. Specifications and required work for each health facility will vary based on the type of water point in the facility.Sanitation: In communities and health facilities without adequate access to latrines CRS will construct 200 new latrines, desludge up to 100 latrine blocks, rehabilitate the superstructure of up to 100 latrines and provide latrine cleaning materials to 30 committees. Rehabilitation will only take place in areas where existing latrines can be desludged and their support structure is still intact. In areas without enough functioning latrines, priority will be given to the construction of new latrines. All latrines will be sex-segregated and will be handicap-accessible. Each latrine block will be equipped with a handwashing station. In communities where latrines are built, CRS will create and train latrine cleaning committees which will be responsible for the maintenance and cleaning of latrine facilities. Each committee will be provided with tools, cleaning supplies, a maintenance kit and soap to carry out their responsibilities. At health facilities, CRS will coordinate with the MoH or local health actors supporting the facility. The same materials as those that are given to the community committees will be made available to health facilities for the maintenance of their sanitation facilities.WASH NFI: CRS plans to provide 2,000 HHs with NFI. As market restrictions become more likely, CRS will prioritize NFI delivery through direct distribution as local markets may not be regularly accessible to HHs. Each HH will receive a WASH NFI kit containing items recommended by the sector such as soap and water storage containers.
