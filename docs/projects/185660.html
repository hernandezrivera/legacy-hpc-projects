Project Summary

In 2022 UNICEF and partners aim to reach 87,196 girls, boys, women, and men, including persons with disabilities and other gender orientations with GBV response, risk mitigations and prevention interventions in camps and host community (80 percent will be in camps and 20 percent in host).
UNICEF will continue to work with government institutions and Department of Social Services (DSS) to strengthen child protection systems, through capacity development of the social workforce, and technical support to police probation officers and social workers to improve children's access to child-friendly services. UNICEF will also work with ORG for system strengthening on birth registration.
UNICEF will strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation, improve responses and ensure preparedness and response for emergencies including supporting human resources in PERU and prepositioning 10,000 Dignity kits and ensure that women and girls receiving dignity kits, are oriented on proper disposal of the non-reusable materials to address environmental issues.
UNICEF will actively empower adolescents and youth through Peer Education as one of the key strategies to prevent Gender-based Violence such as Intermate Partner Violence, child marriage and hence create a social environment free from violence crucial for the adolescents and youth in Cox’s Bazar. Community engagement will be prioritized towards building the protective environment in a durable and sustainable way.
UNICEF will focus on; i) enhancing the capacities of 900 case workers, social workers, and case managers through trainings, mentorship and coaching, ii) strengthen institutional systems to provide Multi-sectoral services including specialized services for children and adolescent survivors, iii) maintaining 23 safe spaces for women and girls in camps and host community to provide response services (Case management, Psychosocial support, referral for medical, legal and safety) in a confidential space, while adhering to COVID 19 safety measures, iv) engage the community and empower adolescents to address and challenge negative social norms and harmful practices, v) Mitigate GBV risks through safety audits and collaborate with sectors (Health, WASH, CP, Nutrition, Education) on mitigating risk in respective sector programs, vi) strengthening Child and adolescent survivors initiative and vi) advocacy for equitable access to justice and social services, vii) engage 4,000 women and girls including PWD in structured skills activities, awareness sessions on GBV, nutrition, hygiene, orienting adolescent girls on Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health information using “Girl Shine” curriculum. Also, empowering adolescents in Preventing Gender-based Violence through Peer Education, will be explored. UNICEF supported SSWG in co-location areas will be utilized to implement joint activities enhancing social cohesion among refugees and host population.
Community outreach mobilizers, volunteers and community groups will play a crucial role in reaching religious leaders, Mahjis and other stakeholders with messages on GBV prevention and risk mitigation, creating protective environment for children, women and adolescents while 12,000 individuals will be trained and engaged using evidence proven strategies to address negative social norms. Including Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) which aims at engaging men in transformative individual behaviors change and male engagement in a way that improves, rather than endangering, the lives of women.
UNICEF will strengthen Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) by facilitating complaints and feedback mechanisms, and monitoring to ensure that feedback improves programming and UNICEF as a Co-chair of the PSEA Network, will bolster UNICEF and partners’ PSEA and child safeguarding mechanisms, through training of UNICEF and partners on PSEA, conduct partners’ PSEA assessment and implementation of institutional PSEA mechanisms.

1 - Identified Needs

Approximately 907,796 people are estimated to be living in Rohingya refugee camps, with 52 percent being children (GoB/UNHCR Fact Sheet 31-Oct-2021) . The population relies heavily on humanitarian assistance. Children and women, especially Unaccompanied and Separated Children, female-headed households, persons with disabilities and alternative gender orientation are exposed to all forms of violence, including trafficking, child marriage, and sexual and Gender Based Violence (GBV). This is compounded by restricted movement and other protection risks as well as natural hazards (monsoon, cyclones, and landslides) which adversely impact safety and wellbeing of the population in camps and host community. Negative social norms and harmful practices including gender discrimination and child marriage prevail (J-MSNA July-Aug 2020) . The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and 2021 has exacerbated the child protection landscape and could be a contributing factor to the heightened occurrence of all forms of violence, child marriages and GBV incidents . According to GBVIMS, Q2 report 2021, Intimate Partner Violence constituted 84 percent of reported GBV incidents of which 98 percent occur in survivors’ homes where children and other family members live, . The impact of IPV does not only affect survivors but also children and adolescents witnessing the violence. Child marriage remains a huge concern although incidents reported through GBViMS are few. The Rapid Gender Analysis revealed an increase in child marriages, indicating that it may be a negative coping mechanism during the COVID-19 pandemic. Although women and girls are disproportionately affected, by GBV, studies have also shown men and boys to be significantly affected by violence including sexual violence (Women’s Refugee Commission (WRC), 2018. In addition to the camps, 472,002 individuals in the host community are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance. According to J-MSNA September 2021, 65 percent of the females reported needs for protection services, and due to deep-rooted patriarchal social norms and violation of human rights, women and children, remain at higher risk of exposure to all forms of violence including sexual exploitation and abuse, child marriage, GBV. While robust GBV interventions to respond, prevent and mitigate risks exist, there are still huge gaps to be addressed, to improve the quality of services and access: The gaps include i) limited access to quality multi-sectoral services (Health, PSS, Legal, Safety), ii) lack of adequate capacity among actors and the social work force, iii) cultural barriers hindering disclosures, normalizing GBV incidents, iv) inadequate engagement of key gate keepers and adolescents in challenging negative social norms and harmful practices, and v) Lack of knowledge of available services (GBV SS 2020).
