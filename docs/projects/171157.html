Project Summary

Women and children are disproportionately affected by violence, emergencies and displacement, which exacerbates the protection risks faced, especially those in IDP populations who are at grave risk of exploitation, violence and abuse and are vulnerable to a range of protection concerns and bear the brunt of these vulnerabilities resulting in negative coping strategies. Groups such as women-headed households, unaccompanied children, socially marginalized and discriminated communities are at particular risk and face further protection concerns. Many children are exposed to risks adopt negative coping strategies including enlisting into armed groups/forces and other harmful activities like criminal gangs and substance abuse among many. According to the GBV AOR, 95% of all incident reported in 2019 were women and girls while 76% of these were from displaced communities. Further, due to cultural barriers and weak protection institutions, inequality and disrespect of women and children’s rights continue with dire results such as separation of families, unwanted pregnancies, transmission of STI’s and mental disorders among others. The objective of the project is to strengthen the protection of most vulnerable girls, boys, women and men including people with special needs affected by conflict and climatic shocks by reducing risks and vulnerabilities and increasing community capacities. It will employ participatory and right based approaches, survivor- centre approach, AGD approach and community-based approach which will involve, inclusion of the target population; refugees/asylum seekers, migrants, IDPs and most vulnerable host communities in addition to local authorities in the project cycle. The project aims to build and strengthen local capacities within the community and support them to establish a protection framework to prevent and respond to violations. It will focus on improving capacity for immediate response to violations and remedial action as well as strengthening the protective environment in Somalia.On immediate response, the action will aim to address acute protection needs of girls, boys, women and men, stemming from violence, including GBV, child rights violations, as well as coercion, and abuse. Using a survivor centered multi-sectoral approach DRC, vulnerable women and girls will receive GBV services through Women and girls safe spaces, psychosocial support (PSS) and case management. To enable recovery and restore dignity of individual and community victims of violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation and to improve positive coping strategies and prevent further violations, DRC will establish child friendly spaces through which psychological first aid support will be provided. In addition, material assistance and livelihood skills training for GBV survivors will also be provided. On environment building, DRC will reinforce formal and community institutions for better legal and community-based protection of vulnerable groups from violence, coercion and abuse. Key to achieve this objective, activities will include awareness-raising and outreach among communities on protection issues, capacity building and training for stakeholders on GBV, CP and HLP. In addition, DRC will map out civil identification documentation required in Somalia to obtain/access government services such as ID's, passports, title deeds as well as birth and death certificates. The information will be disseminated to strengthen governance structures and to support service delivery to affected populations. On advocacy, DRC will strengthen coordination of protection services and enhance advocacy on protection with relevant stakeholders. Key activities will include strengthening of referral pathways, mapping of service providers and Protection Monitoring of rights violations. DRC will further identify and provide protection-oriented support to people with disabilities. The proposed action will reach 55,700persons (3500G, 3500B, 24000M & 24700W).

Project Activities

1). Conduct survivor-centered multi-sectoral support (medical,legal, PSS, material and safety) and case management (3 women and girl safe spaces will be established and 2 existing strengthened). Material support will include dignity kits. 2). To reduce the risks of GBV, the project will distribute solar lanterns to survivors and those at risks and install street lights in selected locations. 3). Social- Economic reintegration and empowerment for GBV survivors through livelihood skills training, legal support and cash-based assistance Awareness raising, advocacy and public outreach campaigns on GBV prevention, response and mitigation. 4). Information on GBV prevention and response as well as services will be shared through various means including one on one and group campaigns, leaflets, TV and Radio. 5). Training of duty bearers and humanitarian staff on GBV prevention and response. 6). Protection mainstreaming trainings for duty bearers and humanitarian workers targeting mainly non protection actors. 7). Awareness-raising and outreach on housing land and property issues (HLP) and civil documentation. DRC will conduct awareness sessions on procedures, rights and responsibilities related to HLP targeting duty bearers and community members and will also map out civil identification documentation required in Somalia to obtain/access government services including but not limited to national identity cards, passports, driver's licenses, title deeds, birth and death certificates. 8).Conduct Protection monitoring through the SPMS managed by the Protection Cluster to systematically and regularly collect, verify and analyze in order to identity violations of rights and protection risks for populations of concern for the purpose of informing effective responses. The SPMS produces quantitative and qualitative data and information related to the protection environment, protection trends over time, rights violations and protection risks – threats, vulnerabilities, and capacities – of the affected population. Quantitative analysis will feed into the periodical joint analysis workshops conducted at regional and/or district level on a quarterly basis which involves discussions with members of affected communities (displaced and non-displaced) on the findings of the SMPS as well as the root causes, gaps and recommendations.

Mitigation Measures

1. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of marginalized community’s and minority groups: Minority groups in Somalia are rarely targeted in humanitarian project cycle and are mainly marginalized and invisible. Women and children and persons with special needs among minority groups are even more sidelined and excluded and do not benefit from interventions that accords them some degree of protection. The environment in Somalia as a result of ongoing armed conflict and cyclic often deem minority persons and groups more vulnerable. In addition, at least 15% (10% children)of the population are people with disabilities who face heightened risks and exclusion from services. To mitigate on this risks, DRC will intentionally target and empower, protect and promote the rights of minority communities, with a focus on women and children who experience multiple-discrimination on account of their gender and minority status. DRC will follow a selection criterion for beneficiaries that include members of minorities as vulnerable groups that require special attention. Selection of beneficiaries will be through participatory methods in collaboration with trained community representatives. Through protection monitoring, DRC will identify trends of exclusion and marginalization. Data collected will be used for targeting and prioritization, ensuring that no one is left out. DRC will consult people with disabilities and/or their representatives during the design and Identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of women, girls and boys Women, girls and boys remain vulnerable to protection risks and harmful cultural practices, GBV, child recruitment and hazardous child labor. Their ability to access justice and remedies and to enjoy rights is often limited and further weakened in case of displacement. Furthermore, stigma related to abuses often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. To address this, DRC will include specific needs of women, girls and boys when designing and implementing the project. DRC will seek ways to increase the women and girl friendly spaces providing opportunities for recognizing their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes. 2. Mitigation the risk of external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection There are significant number of displaced communities and people in locations that are not easily accessible to humanitarian assistance, this could be due to insecurity and people living in far to reach areas. In addition, there are instances where external stakeholders demand targeting of particular groups or prevent others from receiving assistance. To mitigate on these challenges, DRC will use beneficiary selection criteria that considers vulnerability as an entry point to assistance. DRC will engage in consultation processes adequately gathering relevant information including power dynamics from the communities and other stakeholders and taking these views into account when selecting beneficiaries and implementing the action. DRC will work with existing or revitalized community committees among the target groups that will be part of the beneficiary selection.3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: DRC will involve the target groups from designing the projects, selection of beneficiaries to service provision and they will be capacitated on the project and its deliverables. DRC has a comprehensive complains and feedback mechanism which will guide the project to ensure all community members can access information on the project and share their feedbacks and complains which will be effectively addressed. DRC has different avenues of collecting such information including our complains hotline 332 that is manned by a dedicated staff who in turn receives all complains and feedback and directs them to respective projects manager

Please provide further explanation

Durable Solutions: Recognizing that the project is set in a protracted and complex emergency context, the project will partially contribute to durable solutions. The project will aim at addressing all 3 dimensions of safety, key to the achievement of durable solutions – physical, material and legal. Material safety will be addressed through integration with DRC response in other sectors in which protection will be mainstreamed. The program is designed taking into account the needs and perspectives of the communities that ensuring that the intervention will contribute to physical, material and legal safety of the targeted communities. DRC will conduct conflict analysis before and during project implementation and identify among other things, root cause of conflicts and other factors that may lead to conflicts. The information from conflict analyses studies will inform program design and re-design. Social cohesion and peaceful coexistence will be supported analyzing how targeting can minimize intercommunity conflicts. The action is directly linkage to linked to government processes and will aim at building long term protective environment through building capacity of government officials on human rights which will at the end improve governance. In addition, community based structures will be strengthened through training and awareness creating leading to a more empowered community that can prevent and respond to violations on their own. Through protection monitoring, DRC will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. Based on this, DRC will further engage in evidence based joint advocacy with other protection actors, as well as the international community to address these protection concerns and ensure that rights of displaced communities are addressed. Duty bearers will also be part of the quarterly protection monitoring joint analysis workshops. In collaboration with the HLP sub-cluster DRC will monitor eviction risks and ensure securing housing, land and property rights is part of both protection and shelter activities. DRC will also work closely with key stakeholders to map out civil identification documents issued or required in Somalia to obtain government services including but not limited to national identity cards, passports, driver's licenses, title deeds, birth and death certificates and any other document required to access government services. The information will be disseminated to strengthen governance structures and to support service delivery to affected populations.
