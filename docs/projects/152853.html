Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Greater Kapoeta Counties (East, North and South) to provides essential primary healthcare emergency clinical packages to the most vulnerable displaced populations, Host communities and Returnees targeting 10,751 (1,531 males, 1,428 females, 299 children under 5 years and 330 children under 15 years in Kapoeta East, 1,528 males, 1,427 females, 299 children under 5 years and 329 children under 15 years in Kapoeta North, 1,528 males, 1,426 females, 298 children under 5years and 328 children under 15 years in Kapoeta South) provision of Medical consultation and treatment for common childhood illnesses, maternal health and other medical condition (ANC, delivery & Post-natal care), Preventive intervention; Health Education on integrated health (WASH, Nutrition GBV etc); - psychosocial counseling, expanded program on immunization and the project will support active surveillance for epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, cholera, measles and meningitis throughout implementation periods, referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, scale up TB/HIV/AIDS/Disability care, Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications and Procurement 12% of emergency health pipeline to bridge in gaps for medical supplies and other essential equipments’ through provision of mobile clinical packages in 4 hard to reach areas, support 3 Primary Health Care units (PHCUs) and 2 Primary Health care centers (PHCCs).
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment & Needs (OPEN) Implemented SSHF round one with support from Health cluster in Kapoeta East and SSHF round two allocation is currently ongoing in Kapoeta South to complement efforts toward to lifesaving given few functional health facilities in the Counties and the proposed activities will bridge the huge health needs and contributes to HRP 2019 strategic Objective 1 of save lives by providing timely and integrated multisector assistance and services to reduced acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys.

Needs

The Greater Kapoeta areas are pastoralist communities, Accordingly REACH Report, in June 2018, the majority of Population in Kapoeta North County of (53%) reported with inadequate access to food, Kapoeta South County has high market prices (60%. Access to market was reported to be especially poor in the rural areas of the Greater Kapoeta area, which are isolated from major market supply routes and trade hubs due to poor physical infrastructure. Only a few markets exist in Kapoeta, Narus, Lotien and Riwoto towns, which are all located in the western portion of the Greater Kapoeta area. To access market commodities, residents of Kapoeta East County and the northern portion of Kapoeta North County typically have to send members of their community to travel to the livestock market in Kapoeta town by foot with animals, a journey which can take up to two weeks during the peak of the rainy season, and which is often impossible to undertake given the deterioration of road conditions following heavy rainfall.An estimated 83,790 South Sudanese women, men and children in Greater Kapoeta (East, North & South) Counties will need humanitarian healthcare services in 2019. Due to high disease burden resulting from endemic, epidemic, pandemic and neglected diseases the population targeted for humanitarian healthcare services is 46,923 including refugees and an unknown number of returnees projected to need health services as a result of the peace agreement. The prevailing health systems challenges are exacerbated by lack of water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management and expansive malnutrition necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health securities to mitigate disease spread and enhance appropriates access and utilization of health care services to all most vulnerable population.The number of people targeted for healthcare services based on multiple factors including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a challenge to a health system which is already fragile. Displaced populations face the most complex challenges in accessing health care services. Children <5 years are one of the most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases owing to poor nutrition and low levels of Immunization coverage and immunity. The coverage in 2018 for all vaccine preventable diseases remain under 50%, as a consequence 42% of the children <1year are at risk of measles. Women of reproductive age face serious health risks as they have inadequate access to skilled personnel during pregnancy and childbirth. Sexual and gender-based violence is rampant recoding high figures of 4,310 in 2016 and 5,732 in 2017 respectively with survivors lacking access to adequate services. Cross cutting issues like HIV/AIDS, TB, Mental health, disabilities and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services.

Activities

This intervention will focus on provision of Medical consultation and treatment for common childhood illnesses, maternal health and other medical condition (ANC, delivery & Post-natal care), Preventive intervention; Health Education on integrated health (WASH, Nutrition GBV etc); - psychosocial counseling, expanded program on immunization and the project will support active surveillance for epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, cholera, measles and meningitis throughout implementation periods, referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, scale up TB/HIV/AIDS/Disability care, Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications and Procurement 5% of emergency health pipeline to bridge in gaps for medical supplies and other essential equipments' through provision of mobile clinical packages in 4 hard to reach areas, support 3 Primary Health Care units (PHCUs) and 2 Primary Health care centers (PHCCs) in Kapoeta Counties of East, North and South.
