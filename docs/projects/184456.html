Project Summary

The project will benefit 1,360 households (8,160 persons who include 3,006 IDP Households and 5,154 vulnerable non-IDP households- 1,799 men, 1,873 women and 4,488 children). These beneficiaries will include IDP communities in Banadir, Kismayo, Dollow and Abudwak; agro-pastoralists smallholder farmers in Dollow and pastoralists from Abudwak. The project also purposes to target 816 persons with disabilities with the planned interventions. The project will facilitate 530households (3,180 people) with conditional and unconditional cash transfers to facilitate immediate access to food for these vulnerable communities living in areas with severe acute food insecurity. These beneficiaries include 2,226 Displaced persons, 954 persons belonging to the host communities and 318 persons living with a disability. This figure also includes 1,749 children. To ensure that the most at-risk persons are targeted 230 households (1,380 people) with pregnant and lactating women, PWD, the ill and the elderly will receive food vouchers. Community-Based Targeting will be employed during the selection of the beneficiaries. Selection will be targeting IPC 3 and 4 HHs, prioritizing women-headed households or households with pregnant women, persons with disabilities, the elderly and children below 5 years who are malnourished. The beneficiaries will be registered through KOBO collect. NAPAD will undertake beneficiary registration exercises and a verification process to establish whether the identified beneficiaries meet the above-agreed selection criteria.
430 households including IDP and non-IDP households, who are food insecure, will be targeted. 200 of these households will receive unconditional cash transfers worth $ 124 each for three months. On the other hand, 230 highly vulnerable households will also be supported with food vouchers worth $ 90 per month for three months. 100 households will benefit from conditional cash transfers of $7 per day for 20 days working in the rehabilitation of community assets in Dollow and Abudwak. 100 households (600 persons) will be targeted during Cash for Work Interventions. Priority will be given to women who are physically capable of participating in agricultural and livestock assets rehabilitation. All cash rates will be based on the rates set by the Cash working group of the targeted regions and will be transferred to their registered and verified mobile phones from mobile money service providers (i.e Hormuud) The amount mentioned above is not inclusive of transfer charges. Conditional and unconditional cash transfers will increase the purchasing power for targeted households to access food commodities available in the local markets for at least 3 months as they recover their assets and livelihood. The cash is a dignified approach to supporting these households to make choices of types and quantities of food to purchase. Post Distribution Surveys will be conducted to monitor the use of transferred cash and redeemed vouchers and the benefit this support has brought the families.
The project will also support protecting livelihoods assets with various approaches. 200 smallholder farmer households (1200 persons) in Dollow will be supported with smart farming training and provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs as well as short term training on pest control and use of crop cover to reduce flooding and loss of crop swill ensure farmers reduce the loss of yield through pest. 500 pastoralist households in Dollow and Abudwak will also receive treatment for livestock diseases and support increasing fodder production. The project also targets 30 IDP women and youth with income-generating activities to increase household food security and 100 IDP women-headed households will be facilitated with knowledge and skills to establish kitchen gardens which will increase the household dietary intake. This 12-month project will be implemented in the Jubaland state and Banadir and Galmudug regions.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The UCT modality will provide the beneficiaries with the power of choice on thepreferred foods to purchase and at what quantities at a time. UCT is also safeand does not present any risks to the beneficiaries, unlike in-kind support. WithUCT it is easy to consider more vulnerable persons such as women, PLWD,elderly thus this is chosen to ensure more inclusivity in the targeting. UCT willalso help in strengthening local markets as the beneficiaries will purchase thepreferred foods from local suppliers. This will not only avoid disruption of thefood markets that may be occasioned by in-kind food supplies from outside butwill also inject cash in the local markets.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

NAPAD currently have staff working and residing in the targeted project districtswho continuously provide information on the local markets during the design ofthe project. The staff monitored the trends in the availability of differentessential foods in the local markets and the prices. Additionally, they gatheredinformation on the quality of different foodstuffs in the market. Observations inthe target district markets are that the markets have experienced minimalfluctuations despite the movement restrictions brought by COVID 19 outbreak.Additionally, NAPAD continuously read the WFP Joint Market and Supply ChainUpdate. The generally stable markets informed the choice of UCT in the project

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

To mitigate against risks highlighted above, NAPAD will ensure that all conditional andunconditional cash transfers which include (CFW) will be conducted to beneficiarieselectronically through local mobile service providers. This is after informationverification during and after registration of beneficiaries. These measureswill ensure the safety of beneficiaries. All beneficiary data will be safely stored byNAPAD for accountability. Distributions will be conducted from targeted villages toensure that beneficiaries do not have to travel to other location to get their vouchers.All vouchers are redeemable at local stores ensuring that beneficiaries do not have totravel far and in insecure areas to access required commodities. All cash rates will bebased on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. NAPAD will be keento issue food vouchers to the most vulnerable and in particular female-headedhouseholds, child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women with under 5-year-olds and older persons and unaccompanied children in their name. Both IDP andhost community members will be targeted to receive the vouchers and CFW to avoidconflicts, tension or harassment over the food and resources. It has been a practice ofNAPAD to consult the community, clan and local authority leaders in the identificationof beneficiaries of food vouchers and the individuals involved in Cash for Work. Thishas ensured that there is a balanced division of resources, community ownership forbetter management of assets and provision of security for beneficiaries as well asassets. Distributions will be conducted regularly to ensure that beneficiaries will notrun out of food commodities until the next distribution. NAPAD has ensured that itsfood security field officers are gender-balanced and include different local clansincluding the Bantus. This was effected to ensure that women and girls, minority tribeslike the Bantus and people with disability issues are attended to adequately especiallyduring beneficiary registration and distributions. NAPAD’s balanced staffing profile hasand will continue to enhance NAPAD’s reporting and feedback mechanism.Communities have access to NAPAD staff numbers and NAPADs email address forany information sharing. NAPAD will also conduct pre and post-distribution surveys tocapture any complaints and recommendations from the community. NAPAD staff andcontractors have signed a child protection policy and will also be expected to beformally committed to adhering to humanitarian code of conduct during allinteractions with vulnerable populations. NAPAD will continue to conduct refresherchild protection and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training to itsstaff and affiliates so they continue to have capacities to identify and react effectivelyto protection issues. Continued engagement of school-based clubs in child advocacywill ensure that children and especially girls will have skills and knowledge on theirsafety during voucher distributions. Additionally, project inception communitymobilization meetings will be key in sharing protection information to leaders andbeneficiaries on modalities of maintaining individual security during distributionsactivities. This will also be an opportunity to share with especially women and girl thatdo not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving foodassistance and that they should ensure their safety by walking in groups to their localdistribution points.
