Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to ensure that crisis affected children and youths in South Sudan continuously access quality learning and training opportunities that are relevant to their psychosocial, emotional and cognitive development in a safe, inclusive and protected environment. NRC will ensure that conflict-affected children and youth have strengthened resilience to current and future crises, and can contribute to the peaceful socio-economic development of South Sudan.
A total of 32,759 (16052 girls/16707 boys) children and youth will benefit from the project. Additionally, 600 PTA members and 800 (20% female) teachers in target schools will benefit from the project through training, awareness raising, support supervision, education materials and monitoring. The target group will include displaced, returnees and host community children, youth, and adults, particularly the most vulnerable, including girls who are most at risk of harm and abuse and children with disabilities who often experience barriers or discrimination in access to and uptake of education services, in government and opposition areas. The project will focus on strengthening and improving quality in delivery of education in emergency affected areas through four strategic response areas: 1) support to formal basic education in static operations; 2) first phase response through the mobile rapid response mechanism; 3) Alternative Education Programme for older children who had dropped out of the education system; and 4) youth education and training. The strategic response will enhance access to quality education and skills programmes for children and youth affected by the crisis in static IDP/PoC sites and host communities, as well as in new emergencies and hard-to-reach locations. The project is in line with the 2020 South Sudan Education Cluster objectives (CO1, 2 and 3), the South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies, the South Sudan General Education Strategic Plan 2017-2022 (GESP). Emphasis will be placed on Strengthen the education system response capacity of the community and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergency on children and youth through training of PTA on preparedness and response planning and engagement of community in project implementation, decision-making, accountability, and ownership.
Key project activities will include but not limited to constriction and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, provision of teaching and learning materials, staining of teachers on pedagogy and psychosocial; support, provision of school feeding in IPC 3&4 areas, awareness raising on life saving messages and protection, construction of WASH facilities and hygiene promotion as well as training of PTA. The project will leverage integrated programming to ensure children learn in a safe and inclusive environment. provision of segregated WASH facilities for boys and girls, tree planting, clean school environment, sensitization of school communities on protection, child safeguarding, teachers’ professional code of conduct, as well as referral pathways for reporting cases of abuse. ensure access to legal documentation for ALP students and youth establishing own business. School feeding linked to both health and education outcome.
The rapid response mechanism will target children aged 3-5 yeas old and the primary education will target boys and girls age 6-14 years. This will include children with disability from both IDPs, Returnees and host communities. Adolescent boys and girls 14 years and above will be provided life skills and training support. Girls are particularly more vulnerable to drop out of school and they will be targeted with protection messages and provision of sanitary kits.
There will be no risk of duplication as the project will target Northern Bahr El Ghazal,Western Bahr El Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Warrap and Jonglei where NRC already has static presence.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is still plunged into conflict and socio—economic crises as millions of people remain displaced in and out of the country leaving many children without access to education services. According to out of school study conducted in 2018, an estimated 2.2 million children were not attending school in South Sudan (UNESCO & MoGEI, 2018). More than half of the country’s population (6.35 million people 54%) is severely food insecure according to the most recent Integrated Food Security Phase classification (IPC) assessment conducted in August 2019.The conflict has led to destruction of schools facilities and some schools forced to close down due to insecurity. Up to 60% of primary and secondary school, classrooms are either partially or completely damaged (EMIS data, 2018). Additionally, 20 % of schools are non-functional, insecurity being the main cause of closure (Education Cluster, 2018). Many children are growing up traumatized by the violence they witness; an estimated 900,000 children suffer from psychosocial distress (UNICEF, 2017). Children also experience widespread and horrific sexual violence: nearly 25% of documented incidents of conflict-related sexual violence affect children. Despite signing of the Revitalised Agreement on Resolution of Conflict In South Sudan in September 2018 and subsequent, up to 95 security incidents were reported between September 2018 to April 2019 (USAID, Crisis Fact Sheet#8, 2019). NRC assessment reveal displacements continues in parts of the country due to insecurity and at the same time returnees and IDPs requires education support. An estimated 19,362 individuals 3,227 HHs Returnees / IDPs who came to Lainya County and surrounding Payams and villages such as Lainya Centre, Logwili, Kenyi Bereka and Limbe (NRC, June 2019). According to rapid assessment conducted by NRC in Greater Kajokeji, all the 88 pre-primary schools (nursery) were non-functional. Only eight (8) primary schools out of 104 were operational and all the schools in Nyepo County were non-functional (NRC, June 2019). The conflict also increased the likelihood of girls being married early. South Sudan is ranked the fifth worst country in the world for child marriage, which is viewed as a way to escape poverty and a method to protect girls, but which causes girls to drop out of school. In 2017, Jonglei had the lowest level of girl’s enrolment in the whole country at 31%. Additional barriers to girls’ attendance and performance in school include: economic and social demands such as household chores and caring for younger siblings; strong cultural norms that favour boys’ education when a family has limited resources; insufficient numbers of female teachers to act as role models; and inadequate school sanitation facilities. The essential inputs required for the provision of equitable, quality education such as qualified teachers, female teachers, text books and learning materials, learning spaces for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees, and water and sanitation facilities in schools remain in extremely limited supply. In addition, young people drop out of school due to long distances to schools, incomplete schools, insecurity and displacement, inability of parents to purchase education materials for children due to poverty, and little to no support services in schools for children with disabilities. Despite improvement in school enrolment, education completion among young people is very low, it is estimated that 86% of students fail to complete primary education cycle. Besides, only 17 percent of teachers currently teaching in primary schools are trained.NRC conducted protection needs assessment in several locations targeted by the project, both those intended to be served by static and mobile activities. Data collected during the assessments informed a protection risk analysis that particularly considered the needs of women, recently displaced persons, older persons and persons with disabilities

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

NRC ensures that all conflict affected children and youth affected by displacement has right to education. The project will strengthen the capacity of the parents, communities members and IDP children to gain their right to education. To address the education needs of all age groups, NRC will support enrolment of older adolescent boys and girls in ALP centers to ensure access to flexible education opportunities to older children.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The priority will be given to IDPs, returnees and conflict affected host communities.The target group will include displaced, returnee and host community children, youth, and adults, particularly the most vulnerable, including girls who are most at risk of harm and abuse and children with disabilities who often experience barriers or discrimination in access to and uptake of education services, in government and opposition areas.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

NRC will work with the government structures and school structures in delivery of the project. The PTA and School Management Committees will be central to the education support provided and their capacity built to contribute to the school management and community engagement. At County and Payam level, the project will build the capacity of county inspectors of schools and Payam supervisors on support supervision and inspection.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is in line with the 2020 South Sudan Education Cluster objectives (CO1, 2 and 3), the South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies, the South Sudan General Education Strategic Plan 2017-2022 (GESP).

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project is linked to the longer term durable solution for the returnees and IDPs, the provision of Temporary Learning Spaces will help provide longer term education for IDPs and returnees. The knowledge and skills provided to the teachers through the capacity building and teacher training will enable the teachers to continue providing quality education services for children and youth. The project will also build resilience of the school communities through PTA training and support to school development planning and implementation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure sustainability of the project, the project will be implemented in close collaboration with relevant education stakeholders at national and state level. The school communities will be involved in the project activities right from inception and throughout the project cycle. All project assets including the TLS, latrines will be handed over to the school authorities. The training provided to the PTA will also enable them continue supporting the school administration and mobilizing the children to take children to school after the end of the project.

NRC has policy and standards to enable all external stakeholders, especially the people we serve, to provide feedback and issue complaints on NRC’s organizational performance, and for NRC to address such feedback and complaints in a safe and responsive manner.
