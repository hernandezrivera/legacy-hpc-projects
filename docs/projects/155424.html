Project Summary

The project seeks to improve access to safe and sufficient water and standard sustainable and high quality awareness of hygiene practices in 5 targeted districts in both urban and rural service centres. The 5 districts have poor water and sanitation structures and practices and this is made worse by the drought situation and the rise in water borne diseases in particular cholera. The 5 districts including Epworth, Gokwe North, Mutoko, Mt Darwin and Tsholotsho have had low access and utilisation of health services and adequate WASH services and the project seeks to improve the capacity of the health system to provide services through capacity building of health workers in preventing and responding to water borne diseases. The health centres will be stocked with key water treatment options for easy access for communities. Poor WASH coverage, knowledge and practices in targeted rural and urban areas will be addressed thorough repair and rehabilitation of water points and community led awareness and education for all groups and in in institutions including schools and churches. The project will reach at least 18,750 people with improved safe water sources in Epworth, Tsholotsho, Mt Darwin, Mutoko and Gokwe North which have a poor WASH score. 62,500 people will be reached with health and hygiene messages in the same districts. Key vending areas especially in Epworth will be targeted for hygiene education and provision of water treatment options. The project will target to train and equip 100 water point committees and associated pump minders who will manage water and sanitation resources.

Needs

Need to rehabilitate or upgrade strategic and perennial water points and sanitation facilities and targeting vulnerable communities affected by drought will improve sanitation coverage and prevent and control emerging diarrheal diseases. There is need to provide safe and clean water for the communities due to the water shortages worsened by the drought situation. The water situation was mostly poor before the drought. Need to conduct PHHE sessions and awareness in communities with high WASH score will improve knowledge and practices and will include training of EHTs, VHWs and CHWs supporting community based counselling of caregivers on infant and young child feeding practices on critical lifesaving WASH messages / hygiene practices. Need for water sources which are inclusive i.e accessible to people with disability, elderly and most vulnerable. The distribution of hygiene enabling NFIs and hygiene education pertaining to safe and hygienic child care and feeding practices to prevent occurrence of diarrhoeal & other water related diseases. Local level coordination mechanisms will be used to ensure sustainable adoption of practices and behaviours. There exists poor water and sanitation practices in the the communities. 18,750 people will be reached with improved safe water sources in Epworth, Tsholotsho, Mt Darwin, Mutoko and Gokwe North which have a poor WASH score. 62,500 people will be reached with health and hygiene messages in the same districts.

Activities

Upgrading of water points including drilling and equipping of boreholes, rehabilitation of boreholes and water quality testing. Hygiene Promotion with a focus on safe hygiene and sanitation practices, participatory health and hygiene education and water conservation (Training of EHTs/Extension workers on PHHE lifesaving messages, training of VHWs on PHHE lifesaving messages, dissemination of critical hygiene messages through support groups and health clubs, IEC material development & distribution, Dissemination of messages focused on sanitation improvement, household water treatment and water conservation, Monitoring diarrhoeal diseases, PHHE sessions in institutions(schools and clinics) Distribution of NFIs to households, including logistics and monitoring. The project will also develop local capacity for disaster response though training in DRR and setting up ward and village committees to develop action plans to deal with disasters. The project will address the challenge of access to water through rehabilitation of 100 boreholes in the 5 districts and building capacity of local people to sustainably manage assets as well as promote proper hygiene practices for better nutrition. The local management structures will include the Water point committees and village pump minders and these will be trained on management of water points and setting up sustainable maintenance plans. The integrated approaches builds resilience of communities improved & equitable use of water and sanitation resources.
