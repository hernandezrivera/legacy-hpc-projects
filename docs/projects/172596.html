Project Summary

The project is designed to improve livelihoods and natural resource management for the vulnerable host communities. The proposed project will strengthen the food security and livelihoods of 60,000 beneficiaries (of which, 50% women) comprised of 12,000 farmer households of the host communities at Ukhia and Teknaf sub-districts in Cox’s Bazar. The specific objectives of the proposed project are 1) enhance the livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host communities and support social protection interventions in cooperation with the Government of Bangladesh 2) Strengthen sustainable Natural Resource Management (NRM) for Rohingya and host communities. The project will apply five approaches to attain the desired Food Security Sector (FSS) Joint Response Plan (JRP) -2021 Cox Bazar outcomes. The first approach focuses on supporting different categories of farmer households to address their varying needs. The project will build the capacity of farmer groups, both technically and socially, to improve productions and increase income. The second approach is to strengthen the institutional capacities to provide improved agricultural extension services, restore/rehabilitate, and conserve natural resources.
The third approach is to foster linkages between community groups and the rapidly growing markets to identify gaps and weaknesses in the existing supply chains (vegetables, fish, chicken, etc.). The fourth approach focuses on developing and maintaining functional partnership relations with key agricultural actors while the fifth approach will promote ICT-based extension services to implement the project successfully. A data-driven holistic landscape approach will be applied to ensure better management of the natural resources.
FAO will mainstream gender by addressing gender gaps and creating opportunities for women. The changes will be measured through gender-sensitive tools with a special focus on women mobility, access to resources, and decision-making. FAO will effectively materialize the principles of the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to strengthen the voice of vulnerable communities.

1 - Identified Needs

Over 1.3 million people including Rohingya are with food insecurity. The COVID-19 has shaken the economy and increased social tension on employment. In the agriculture landscape, income and profitability of farming depend on easier access to the factors of production, improved technologies, and post-harvest practices. The marginal farmers are at the risk of losing their purchasing capacity of agricultural inputs like- seeds, fingerlings, chicks, feeds, fertilizers, etc. The gaps in demand and supply chains, inadequate farm machineries and market systems volatility. The scarcity of high-quality inputs (seed, fertilizers) also influences agriculture negatively in Cox’s Bazar. Major needs for the agriculture farmers are:• Improved irrigation water facilities; • Improved agro-machinery supports to reduce production cost;• High quality inputs (like-seeds, tools, organic fertilizers, etc) to the local farmers; • Improve market systems; • Appropriate training on crop production and extension Services; In the subsistence livestock farming system, there is substantial knowledge-gap among the farmers’ communities on livestock management and care. Goat rearing faces huge risk of the “Peste des Petits Ruminants” PPR disease. Besides, shelter facilities and management capacity are poor. Chicken and ducks rearing also face quality feed shortage. In aquaculture (Pond fish culture) sub-sector, training on improved aquaculture and sufficient supply of quality feed and fish fry are among the key priority needs for the fish farmers. Traditional fish marketing practices, limited use of non-conventional marine foods. Sea safety knowledge, life-saving equipment, and alternative income options have been pointed out as major needs of the marine fishermen. Increased population created massive demand for fuel wood, which caused severe pressure on local natural resources, services, and existing infrastructures. According to the satellite imagery analysis done by FAO in 2017 using the forest boundary delineation map, around 2,685 ha’s of forest lands have 7,220 hectors of forestland located inside and outside the camp experienced different level of degradation. This is an enormous threat to entire ecosystems including water resources and groundwater reserves. This unusual situation decreases the potential of the natural resource bases. Therefore, awareness-raising and advocacy efforts for planning and mitigation of both are sorely required.Major needs: • Restore/rehabilitate additional 1,000 hectares of degraded forestland and wildlife habitat;• Maintain 2,650 hectares of restored/rehabilitated land;• Effective dissemination of Early Warning Messages on disaster risks and ensure implication of Nature Based Solutions (NBS) in the restoration/rehabilitation initiatives ; • Watershed management to conserve ecosystems for sustainable production; • Build capacity of the local nurseries on organizational development, management, and quality planting materials production;• Build capacity of the Forest Department and relevant stakeholders in Forest protection, biodiversity conservation, and to promote ecotourism;The economic consequences of the pandemic and its associated preventive measures have had one of the most significant impacts on women, girls, men and boys in both communities, affecting their livelihoods (IN THE SHADOWS OF THE PANDEMIC October 2020). FAO has conducted gender sensitive situation analysis of the host communities. Based upon the documents: • Income generating/self-reliance activities are needed for women farmers to increase income • Women farmers collective capacities on improved farming practices need to be strengthened • Create opportunities for women farmers to increase market linkage • Establish digital hub/center to get update agriculture and market information, etc. These interventions will contribute to increase women farmers’ mobility, access to resource, establish linkage with market and voice.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Build capacity of the farmers' groups on improved production technologies of three potential value chains (crops/vegetables, fisheries & dry fish and livestock);Activity 2: Promote high value crops (vegetables) cultivation and establish community-based nurseries on high value vegetables and fruits;Activity 3: Provide high quality agricultural inputs (i.e. seeds, agro-machineries, feed, tools/equipment, vaccine kits, safety drier sets, etc.) to the potential farmer groups;Activity 4: Implement ecosystem based agricultural farming systems through watershed improvement practices including buried pipe irrigation technologies; Activity 5: Conduct market linkage workshops/meetings with and between the market actors/private sectors and farmer groups to establish business agreements ;Activity 6: Establish and strengthen aggregation centers to encourage farmer groups in collective production and marketing and link them to WFP led market outlets and potential higher markets;Activity 7: Build capacity of the government and private sectors on “collective production and collaborative market development” to objectively deliver improved agricultural support services; Activity 8: Strengthen capacity of Local Service Providers (aggregator, mechanic, and vaccinator) to support local farmer groups with need-based agricultural services;Activity 9: Create additional income generation opportunities for the natural-resource dependent households;Activity 10: Set up effective systems for utilizing surface and rainwater;Activity 11: Support restoration/rehabilitation and maintenance of degraded forest areas, wildlife habitat and biodiversity hotspots through watersheds management approach in and outside the refugee camps;Activity 12 : Support and build capacity of the local nursery associations on producing quality planting materials ; Activity 13: Effective dissemination of Early Warning Message on Landslide and other natural disasters to the local communities and promote Nature Based Solutions;Activity 14: Support development of innovative Eco-tourism models;Activity 15: Support in capacity development of Forest Department and relevant stakeholders.
