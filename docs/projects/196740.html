Project Summary

The project will target returnee and IDP youth in order to build their capacity to advocate for their needs. In PCSC’s work with youth in both al-Hole camp and in Raqqa city, IDP and returnee youth have expressed their desire to be empowered and to have opportunities to improve their lives. Youth want to reduce negative stereotypes that limit their opportunities to make positive contributions to their community.
The project will prepare them to collaborate through experiential exercises and games that build empathy, trust and teach them to communicate their needs. Youth will learn conflict analysis and key concepts around peacebuilding in order to identify the root causes and grievances that contribute to the key challenges they face. As part of preparing youth to engage civically, youth will be trained on their rights and responsibilities and engage in a series of dialogues to prioritize a common interest.
Through training on advocacy and strategy development, youth will organize around the common interest by developing an action plan and carrying out a small initiative.
The project will target a broad group of female and male marginalized youth as targeting only returnees could cause them to be further stigmatized and may lead to an unwillingness of returnees to participate in the project.

3 - Needs

During the research’s FGDs, Some of the IDPs mentioned that some of the house owners are asking the IDPs to either leave the house or give the owner the aid kit.At the same time the hosting community mentioned the NGOs should remove the displacement to be eligibility criteria, as there are a lot of hosting community members that are also economically vulnerable but don't receive aid because of the IDPs. The research’s FGDs and interviews have shown that most of the problems that occur between residents and displaced people, especially among the youth are due to:Marginalization, lack of awareness, unemployment among youth, stereotypes of different groups, high house rent, poverty, and lack of resources. Residents consider that the displaced have taken aid opportunities from organizations and services, and the displaced perceive themselves as marginalized because the host community has better access to employment opportunities.According to evidence from the focus groups sessions, the returnees to Diriyah said “that they feel marginalized and discriminated against and that the returnees are not treated in a good way from the surrounding communities. They feel weak and broken and cannot obtain their full rights due to the inferiority, stigma and stereotypes that society places on them.”

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

2-Clashes or armed conflict in Al-Diriyah neighborhood. PCSC’s security focal point will monitor the security situation. In the event of active fighting, trainings and meetings will be canceled to ensure that the participants are not injured on their way to the activities. 3-Returnees’ fear of stigmatization: The term “returnees” will not be mentioned in any of the meetings or files. The Peace Center will mention that it targets a diverse group of young people from the Diriyah neighborhood and will not disclose the identity of the returnee participants to the authorities or to the other participants. The meetings, trainings and sessions will be a safe space for the exchange of experiences, expertise, ideas and capacity building. PCSC will ensure that returnees understand that they do not have to disclose their identity and that it will be their choice if they wish to discuss their background.
