Project Summary

This project will improve living conditions for 2000 most vulnerable individuals along the contact line in both Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts in GCA.
Cash transfers will grant each households access to priority items most important to them. By providing three consecutive rounds of cash transfers to beneficiaries, this project will enhance living conditions of the most vulnerable households by addressing their economic fragile situations as well as support local economy and small-scale businesses through indirect impact.

Needs

During 2018 TGH has been collecting data from 14,000 vulnerable households in 11 rayons along the contact line. Among others, data on Food Consumption Score, income, expenditures and coping strategies were collected. TGH has thus an overall and comprehensive overview of the economic situation of households living in this area. Households identification conducted by TGH within ongoing projects showed the following breakdowns of households’ expenses: food (44%), health (26%) shelter (19%) and hygiene (11%). These data show that the weight of foods’ expenses on vulnerable households resources remain high with few disposable cash left for meeting other priority needs. Access to medicine, ability to pay communal and shelter expenses are thus economically challenging for these households, forcing them to use negative coping strategies to face unforeseen expenses. According to TGH data, the average monthly income of households near the contact line is as low as 1,207 UAH per person and per month, far below the official minimum subsistence level in Ukraine (established at 3,235 UAH/per/month). Average monthly income of families with children under 5 years old and of women-headed households are even lower – respectively 957 UAH per person for the first group and 921 UAH per person for the second one. Furthermore only 23% of surveyed people stated skilled employment salaries as their first source of incomes. Close to the contact line, a vast majority of the population depends on social support and pensions (65%). 60% of surveyed households did not have a second source of income to complete their revenues. These social support and pensions are not sufficient for households to meet their needs. Indeed, minimum pension is of 1,435/per/month UAH and average pension is of 2,480 UAH/per/month when the minimum subsistence level set by the Ukrainian government is of 3,235 UAH/per/month.Overall, data collected by TGH shows that vulnerable groups alongside the contact line continue to live at an income level below the minimum cost of living. As a result 12% of surveyed people had used one of the following livelihood coping strategies in the past 30 days (borrow money to meet basic needs, spend savings to meet basic needs, ignore or skip on necessary medical care), and 20% of people relied on coping strategies regarding their access to food (buy cheaper products, buy on credits, rely on friends and family). Relying on these strategies, when no improvement of the conflict is foreseen, place these households in a really fragile position and put them at risk of not being able to recover from these coping mechanisms. Studies and TGH experience have showed that availability of goods in not the main constraint that vulnerable households face. A market study held in August 2018 by the cash working group showed that 93% of interviewed persons designated the high prices as the main problem in accessing markets and commodities. In this situation, provision of unconditional cash transfers would allow beneficiaries to adjust the assistance provided to their needs and improve their dignity by allowing them to choose for themselves how the money granted will be used.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Economic access to market for vulnerable households in the buffer zone is ensured through cash transfers.TGH has been using bank transfers since 2016.This modality is cost-effective and allows reaching out the most remote areas thanks to the good network of Ukrposhta and OschadbankPreferences for this approach were expressed by TGH beneficiaries. Importantly, it enhances local economy and strengthens solidarity mechanisms inside communities. Activity 1. Individual identification of beneficiariesTo ensure a transparent process and accountability towards vulnerable populations, identification of beneficiaries will be done in close cooperation with local authorities. The criteria and selection process will be clearly defined and publically expressed.TGH will select the 2,000 most vulnerable beneficiaries through a comprehensive questionnaire focusing on individual’s characteristics (men/women, children/adults/elderly), food consumption score, share of expenditures for food, shelter, health and hygiene, income per person, as well as other vulnerabilities (chronic diseases, people with disabilities...) The selection will be done based on households characteristics, particularly households with poor FCS and low CSI, IDPs, people with specific vulnerabilities including elderly (mostly isolated ones), HH hosting disabled or chronic diseases; single headed HHs with children, HHs with no active employment and female-headed HHs will be particularly targeted. As mentioned previously, the vulnerability criteria are strongly gender sensitives, as the project seeks to implement a crosscutting dimension of gender equality. Activity 2. Programming and transfers of cash.TGH will use the services of Oshadbank and Ukrposhta depending on localities. The organization has already been working with them in the past, allowing TGH to master the process. Once selected beneficiaries will receive on their phone a notification indicating availability of cash in the nearest withdrawal point. Each individual in selected households will receive 970 UAH as recommended by the CWG.3 distributions will be held, each of them covering 1 month. The support provided will address the needs of the most vulnerable persons along the contact line by enabling them to regain economic access to market and reduce the use of negative coping mechanisms. Outcome 2. Monitoring and accountability of the project are ensuredActivity 1. Implementation of a mechanism for feedbacks and complaints.Respectful of international accountability standards such as Accountability to Affected Population, TGH will open a hotline for the affected population, effective from the start of the project. This will enable a close monitoring of the project from the start and enhance TGH accountability towards beneficiaries and donors. Feedbacks and complaints mechanism will also be put in place and will allows fast and efficient responses to feedbacks and complains as well as effective decision-making if required. Activity 2. Monitoring of project implementation, coordination with key actors.Baseline information will be collected at the project start and will be used as a reference all along the project to measure outcomes. Data on FCS and CSI will be collected in order for their evolutions to be continuously and fully monitored. PDM will be ensured after the end of each distribution round. TGH will closely monitor the use of money to identify the coverage of needs but also the impact of its project on the food consumption score and on the coping strategy index. TGH will coordinate with relevant key actors, such as other international or national NGOs operating in the region, at each stage of the project to avoid overlaps and to ensure better coverage along the contact line. Indeed, TGH wishes to conduct a coordinated and complementary action with other humanitarian actors in the region in order enhance the efficiency of humanitarian assistance.
