Needs

Due to the heavy rains in 2018, water has become more readily available in both natural and man-made storage across much of the country. However, significant gaps exist for vulnerable populations. Lack of sustainable access to water: Large discrepancies exist between host and IDP communities in Sool (46-point difference) and Nugaal (31-point difference) regions, in which water access for IDPs is the worst nationwide (JMCNA 2018). In Sool and Sanaag regions, respectively 69% and 35% of household need more than 30 minutes to reach their main drinking water points. Lack of basic WASH services for IDPs and returnees: As reflected by REACH assessments, water remains one of the priority needs reported by crisis affected households: 84% of them in Baidoa district, 60% in Elwaq, and 55% in Kismayo listed it as one of the top 3 needs. Lack of proper hygiene practices and training on hygiene: hygiene promotion initiatives are required to keep AWD/cholera outbreaks at bay countrywide, in view of the anticipated above average Deyr rains expected between October and December 2018. Gedo and Lower Juba are prone to floods and will be targeted with hygiene promotion activities as areas are prone to AWD/Cholera. Populations in this area rely on unprotected latrines which are not connected to any sewage or treatment system, posing serious health risks particularly in densely populated areas such as IDP sites.Lack of coordination and preparedness: Local capacities must be strengthened to ensure continued O&M of crucial WASH facilities. IDPs face a large risk of eviction from improvised settlements constructed on private lands, with over 130,000 individuals evicted as of April 2018, with Kismayo and Baidoa being two of the seven most affected districts (Eviction trend analysis dashboard, April 2018). Thus, continued support with sustainable WASH services and durable solutions is required.

Activities

Crisis anticipation and response facilitated: ACTED will conduct rapid assessments including gender analysis at the onset of emergencies to inform response. WASH indicators will also be monitored in its areas of operation to anticipate crisis where possible and share information with stakeholders. Linkages will be established when possible with longer term resilience oriented intervention such as social safety nets projects. Supporting access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for crisis affected households: ACTED will rehabilitate/construct 12 strategic boreholes and 7 shallow wells using contractors and water treatment. WMCs will be trained on sanitation, O&M, leadership, protection mainstreaming and water treatment. To improve sanitation, 1319 communal latrines will be constructed. ACTED will construct gender segregated latrines accessible by persons with reduced mobility and equipped with locks for IDPs/refugees who are displaced by drought or conflict. Sustainable mechanisms for waste management will be supported. ACTED will improve protection mainstreaming especially for activities taking place in IDP camps through establishment of a GBV referral system and will install solar lamps in sites, to decrease protection risks linked to the use of WASH facilities by women and girls especially at night. Sanaag, Sool and Nugal experience drought and they will be prioritized with sustainable WASH solutions.15,000 Hygiene kits will be distributed alongside hygiene promotion sessions ensuring women and girls are provided with sanitary materials and households receive soap, aqua tabs and jerrycans. PHAST trainings will focus on hand washing practices, chlorination of unprotected water sources, water treatment and safe storage. More women will be engaged in hygiene promotion activities (70%) as they are mostly in charge of collecting, handling and treating water.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In ACTED project areas, the IDPs live within the host community or adjacent to neighbourhood of host communities, thus durable solutions are enabled. In locations where there is no security of tenure for the land, temporary solutions are provided as negotiation with authorities take place to ensure IDPs absorb shocks.To ensure continued access of beneficiaries to the services provided, ACTED undertakes assessment to establish nature of ownership of the land where IDPs, returnees or host communities are residing and also where water points are located before intervention. Interventions are undertaken if the communities own the sites where the water facilities are located. This ensures that they legally own the services/utilities. The project will be linked to government/community systems. ACTED undertake need assessment with inputs from authorities to ensure community prioritized needs are addressed, and that the intervention will be supported by the local authorities in the long-term. Sanitation facilities are constructed in areas identified by communities, with provision of solar lighting and hand washing stations. Design of the sanitation facilities is finalized in consultation with the future users, to ensure that gender- and age-specific needs are addressed appropriately. To enhance the project’s sustainability, established or existing water management committees will be trained on operation and maintenance of the new/rehabilitated water assets, which will be handed over to them after project completion. WMC will also be trained on leadership; on water treatment; on book keeping; and will be provided with borehole operation and maintenance tool kit to undertake minor repairs when need arises. Sanitation facilities will be handed over to WASH committees who take care of them to ensure they are safely used. ACTED will also use Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) and Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) for behavioral change. Environmental Sustainability: All water infrastructure will be located 30 metres from water points and away from flooding areas. At the same time, through hygiene promotion activities, ACTED WASH team will strengthen communities' understanding of environmental sanitation principles, in order to ensure that water points are not polluted by open defecation practices. Disaster risk reduction: the WASH facilities will be located on higher ground to mitigate the risk of flooding. Community will be trained on water management to reduce water loss. Shallow wells will be covered by apron and berkads also to reduce water loss through evaporation. At water kiosks, soak away pits will be established to eradicate water stagnation and thus mitigate water borne diseases.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Assistance and services do not reach the most vulnerable categoriesLikelihood: Low Impact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community through the use of inclusive community based targeting. Beneficiaries’ selection criteria are developed through participatory process to ensure community understanding and inclusion of marginalized groups. This will prevent discrimination and mitigate conflict. inclusive community governance structures representative of all the different component of the affected population will help in identification of most vulnerable and more in needs HHs to be targeted with the interventions. Additionally, a complaint and response mechanism (CRM) is in place to allow beneficiaries to raise complaints, including on beneficiaries’ selections and verification processes, and share feedback which is used to inform programming. Risk: Vulnerable groups such as IDPs/minorities do not contribute to project designLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will involve and consult all categories and groups of the affected population in identifying and responding to WASH needs. Engaging the community (across age and gender groups) and committee representatives to identify solutions will promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Location, distance and access routes to the WASH sites are not accessible to all members of the community.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will ensure that the rehabilitation of basic infrastructure at site level remains gender sensitive and accessible by all, especially the most vulnerable. In order to increase safety especially for women at night, ACTED will procure and install solar lamps in the sites. This will support the reduction of GBV and encourage women and girls to move around more freely within the sites (for example, use the latrines at night). Additionally, the latrines will be gender segregated and at least 10% of them will be accessible by persons with reduced mobility. Risk: Age- and gender-specific hygiene needs are not taken into account throughout the proposed interventionLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: WASH and shelter kits’ content will be elaborated based on guidance from the WASH and Shelter/NFI cluster, to respond to the specific hygiene needs of women, girls and older persons. Hygiene promoters will be also trained to the specific needs of women and girls, and will be encouraged to sensitize women and girls to menstruation needs and dedicated hygiene habits. ACTED will also ensure that during data collection activities, the information collected at site level is disaggregated by age and sex. Enumerators collecting these data will be aware and trained on Child protection issues. Data will remain protected and confidential and will be used to inform programming.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: High needs vs. limited resources/services provided Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: To avoid community tensions, ACTED will strive to provide the host community with access to WASH camp assistance in situations where similar shortages of basic WASH services and facilities are experienced. Both displaced and host communities will be consulted about WASH needs to assess whether WASH interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the communities. Inclusive targeting and participatory design are also strategies that ACTED puts in place to ensure that tensions are avoided among beneficiary communities. Risk: Project operations are hampered by volatile security environment Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over time, ACTED has developed a low profile access strategy with solid security measures. A participatory approach is used to update security risk analysis and triangulated with other sources to provide regular security briefings to staff. Working closely with targeted communities, engagement with local partners and prioritizing community sensitization and participation, ACTED and partners build acceptance of activities from the bottom up.
