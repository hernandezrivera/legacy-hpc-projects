Project Summary

The International Rescue Committee will implement the project in the districts of Beitbridge, Chimanimani, Chipinge, Chiredzi and Nyanga where it is operational. 18040 households (90 200) people will be targeted. In Chimanimani district, 550 households affected by Cyclone Idai will be supported with multi-purpose cash transfers. In Beitbridge, Chiredzi and Chipinge districts, inorder to minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration, the IRC will provide support to 14 990 households by improving access to stockfeed and support fodder production, processing and utilization as well as support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management which will provide immediate benefits to drought affected communities, protecting livestock-related assets and assisting the rebuilding of key assets among the drought affected communities. Households in very dry parts of the five districts will be supported with access to small grain seeds.

1 - Activities

The main drivers of the deteriorating food security situation are reported to be on-going economic challenges, price increases, declining household income and poor crop production. As a result, households are increasingly engaging in negative coping strategies due to limited food access. These factors have affected rural and urban communities in Beitbridge, Chipinge, Chiredzi, Chipinge and Nyanga where the IRC is operational. ZIMVAC 2019 reports that droughts (98%) and crop pests and diseases (95%) are the most prevalent shocks experienced by communities and most households perceived that they were not equipped to deal with most livelihood and economic based shocks such as weather, loss of income source and price changes. The nation experienced a 60% decrease in average cereal stocks compared to 2018 farming season and an average of 33.1kg per household of cereal stocks were harvested in Manicaland, Masvingo 49.9kg while Matabeleland South was 26.9kg, (ZIMVAC 2019). According to the FEWSNET November 2019 outlook, Zimbabwe is experiencing one of its worst food security crises in recent years with atypically high humanitarian food assistance needs. Record-high temperatures in late-October to early-November impacted water sources, agricultural activity, and livestock. Livestock conditions continue to deteriorate especially in southern and western areas where a high number of cattle deaths have been reported due to poor pasture conditions and water availability. Beitbridge district has experienced livestock poverty deaths with 2 560 cases reported in November 2019, increasing the levels of food insecurity. In Chipinge and Chiredzi the continued dry conditions have resulted in poor grazing and drying up of water sources creating competition between livestock and humans. As a result of the poor nutrition, the body condition of livestock deteriorates rendering the animals susceptible to diseases such as January disease. The persisting poor macroeconomic environment continues to impact livelihoods and remains a key driver to the near-record levels of rural and urban food insecurity with over 51.4% of the rural population expected to be in IPC +Phase 3. IRC will provide four cycles of multi- purpose cash transfers to households directly affected by cyclone Idai in Chimanimani in order to help them recover. Guided by the calculations from the National Cash Working Group on the Minimum Expenditure Basket, the IRC will provide a MPCT valued at $66/HH/cycle. The value of $66 is 55% of unmet needs by the household. IRC participates in weekly market analysis in Chimanimani and has found that the markets are functional. In Beitbridge, Chiredzi and Chipinge districts, inorder to minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration, the IRC will through voucher schemes provide support to households by improving access to stockfeed and support fodder production, processing and utilization. Assistance will be provided to farmers on disease surveillance, treatment and management. IRC will draw on its previous experiences with voucher programs inorder to improve access to veterinary services and products for farmers. Farmers benefiting from the voucher schemes will be those vulnerable farmers with breeding bulls, cows and calves that are progeny from artificial insemination. To strengthen ownership and for sustainability, the farmers will pay a percentage of the voucher value. The IRC will also support the Veterinary department in the district level vaccination programs. Households in the drier northern parts of the five districts will be supported with access to small grain seeds such as sorghum, cowpeas, groundnuts. Biofortified sugar bean and maize varieties will be promoted inorder to improve nutrition outcomes of communities. Using the lead farmer approach, 17490 farmers will be trained on climate smart agriculture and will receive extension and advisory services.

2 - Indicators

The project's success will be measured through the number of households receiving multi-purpose cash transfers (disaggregated by gender), number of farmers receiving support on fodder production, number of livestock vaccinated, number of farmers receiving seeds. The IRC Monitoring, Evaluation Accountability and Learning team will measure the indicators through direct observation, field monitoring visits, Post distribution surveys and end line survey.
