Project Summary

The aim of the project is to (1) strengthen the capacity of health facilities and public health interventions in the hard-to-reach communities; (2) enhance emergency preparedness and response; and 3) address urgent and protracted essential health needs at and around Points of Entry (PoEs) and in the mobility corridors. The proposed action will strive to protect the most vulnerable, including women of reproductive age, pregnant lactating women (PLW), children under five (U5), elderly, people with disabilities, people living with HIV, and ethnic and religious minorities, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities, and people affected by natural hazards or conflict especially the military conflict between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Response Forces (RSF) that commenced on 15 April 2023.
Against this backdrop, IOM will support the health facilities in affected areas to address the urgent need for provision of lifesaving basic package of primary healthcare services (BPPHS), and to improve emergency preparedness and response capacity. IOM will also support the local health system to improve their coordination with state and/or federal level Ministry of Health (MoH) and WHO, by strengthening public health interventions at PoEs and supporting community-based surveillance of infectious diseases. IOM will also promote the continuity and sustainability of BPPHS to (1) ensure the quality of essential, timely, and lifesaving preventative healthcare; (2) enhance risk mitigation efforts and close monitoring of disease outbreak trends; and (3) improve overall community health services targeting approximately 390,000 people including IDPs, returnees, and host communities.
The project’s objectives will be achieved by strengthening capacity of healthcare service providers, including community healthcare workers (CHWs) and community midwives (CMs). Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities will also be implemented through the community health structure that IOM supports, to empower the community to prevent, detect early, and minimize the impact of public health emergencies, such as disease outbreaks. This activity is in line with the Strategic Objectives of the HRP and promotes resilience of the community to multi-hazard risks. The planned activities will be extended to support the state MoH and border authorities to enhance disease prevention and response measures at prioritized PoEs. Some of the targeted localities are located on the intersection of diverse human mobility patterns, including returnees, asylum seekers, and irregular migrants especially with the intra/interstate and cross-border movements due to the conflict and new displacements. IOM will therefore enhance public health interventions at the identified PoEs to mitigate the risk of transmission of communicable diseases including COVID-19. This will be achieved through building capacity of key stakeholders such as the state MoH, border officials, and frontline services workers including health service providers.

Implementing Partners

Non-governmental organizations (NGOs), Civil Society Organizations (CSOs), state and federal Ministry of Health (MoH). Potential non-governmental partners will be selected through a competitive bidding process, including thorough due diligence. In emergency health response, IOM may provide direct support to some of the targeted clinics if there are no NGOs or CSOs with a physical presence and demonstrated a strong capacity in delivering emergency health responses. The interventions will be implemented by providing medical and healthcare service providers with capacity building including training, along with the provision of essential medicines, medical equipment, supplies, and furniture as needed. The direct intervention will be conducted for at least three to nine months to address the current emergency, after which IOM will prepare a clear handover plan to state MoH, if the security situation permits.

1 - Needs

The project will assess the drivers of needs of the population across crisis-affected areas with focus on delivering primary health services and strengthening health systems. High rates of new internal displacement due to the conflict that erupted between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) on 15 April 2023, and inter-communal clashes or disasters caused by natural hazards have overwhelmed the already limited resources and capacity of local health systems. The vulnerable groups already living inside or outside of IDP camps and host communities are at high risk of morbidity and mortality due to unavailability or inaccessibility of sustained health services in the target areas. There were significant health needs amongst the most vulnerable populations before the military conflict, but the escalation of the fighting which has killed 700 people and injured 5,500 people so far and involved dozens of attacks on health facilities and medical professionals while interrupting vital supply chains, has resulted in severe and catastrophic health needs. The conflict has affected healthcare in Khartoum the most with approximately 70 per cent of the health facilities across Khartoum out of service due to disruption of the power grid, water piping systems, fuel availability, and limited mobility of health staff. Inter-communal conflicts have also been reported leading to new displacement and overwhelming host communities the humanitarian situation is notably worse for those who are newly displaced or experiencing secondary displacement who lack access to critical and basic primary health services. Additionally, the upcoming rainy season may disrupt the delivery of medical supplies, damage multiple health facilities, further limiting access to healthcare for the communities they serve. Hundreds of thousands of people continue to suffer from disease outbreaks (e.g., cholera, malaria, and dengue) in yearly cycles as a result of the floods and limited access to safe water, vector control strategies, low coverage of vaccines, and unavailability of essential medicines. Furthermore, among crisis-affected populations, women and children are especially at risk of malnutrition, and gender-based violence (GBV), and high maternal mortality rates due to limited access to reproductive, maternal, and child health services. The overall number of people in need for health assistance has increased by an estimated 37 per cent reaching 11 million people, including over 700,000 newly displaced people, and 7.5 million people expected to need immediate life-saving services. The inability of the fragile health system to respond to the public health needs disproportionally affects the most vulnerable groups. For example, children under five (U5) are exposed to vaccine-preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization (EPI). The national vaccination programme has been further disrupted amidst the ongoing conflict exposing children U5 to increased risks of morbidity and mortality. Only 31 per cent of critical medications are available in public health facilities and 30 per cent in private health facilities, indicating a significant decline from the previous year (43% in 2021), with looting and armed parties controlling the medical facilities these percentages are likely to decrease further. The capacities of the MoH on surveillance and early warning systems are further limited with an urgent need to provide public health interventions at PoEs since the transmission of diseases is a critical dimension of human mobility with IDPs and returnees in the target localities often residing close to borders and disease outbreaks spreading between neighbouring countries with high cross-border mobility. Although the risk of disease transmission is high within the targeted localities given the volume of cross-border mobility, there are limited to no community-based surveillance early warning and response initiatives.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provide basic package of primary healthcare services (BPPHS) at one or two targeted health facilities per locality, depending on people in need and catchment areas encompassing: a) Outpatient consultation and treatment for communicable and non-communicable diseases, support for a prevailing disease surveillance, early warning, and response system; b) Reproductive health services to enhance the quality of antenatal and postnatal care and increase delivery services by a qualified health professional; c) Child health services with a focus on immunizations; d) Mental and psychological consultations; e) Environmental health e.g., waste and vector management; f) Referral and mobile services with mobile medical teams to increase access to health services for the mobile populations and local communities who are facing limited physical access to health facilities; and g) Health and nutrition promotion services. 2. Organize and conduct capacity building activities: a) Train healthcare staff at the targeted facilities on provision of quality BPPHS and RCCE; b) Train CHWs and CMs on community-based health and nutrition promotion services. The training will be designed as training of trainers (ToT) aimed at building their capacity to support community members and pass on the knowledge. Health and nutrition promotion activities will be delivered by encouraging direct participation of community members to tackle issues of malnutrition and negative health seeking behavior and practices (e.g., an establishment of community health management committees and mother-to-mother or father-to father support groups). c) RCCE will be emphasized in an effort to understand risks, perceptions, behaviors, knowledge gaps, misinformation associated with infectious diseases such as COVID-19 and provide accurate information to manage public health risks. d) advocacy efforts on migrant inclusive services will be emphasized and this will address a right of displaced persons and returnees to receive essential services including testing and the COVID-19 vaccine. 3. Refurbishment of health facilities including installation of handwashing facilities and screening points to prepare for future infectious disease outbreaks and control. 4. Strengthening public health intervention at and around PoEs along with mobility corridors focusing on: a) Capacity building for key stakeholders on the activities such as infection prevention and control (IPC) or population mobility mapping, b) Developing public health emergency response plan at the prioritized PoEs, and c) Supporting establishment of cross-border coordination and information sharing mechanisms in coordination with MoH, WHO and local authorities. The following components may be addressed: 1) Health screening to mitigate the risk of transmission of communicable diseases; 2) Risk criteria consisting of potential for exposure and self-reported or observed signs and symptoms; 3) IPC measures and implementation of protocols for handwashing, disinfection, and waste disposal; 4) Facilitation of referral; and 5) Strengthening community-based surveillance by enhancing community engagement in the response to disease outbreaks. RCCE conducted at targeted PoEs including the promotion of appropriate health and hygiene practices to combat communicable diseases, dissemination of information, education, and communication (IEC) materials to address the misconceptions such as disease related stigma, like COVID-19 or monkey pox. d) Procure and provide a minimum set of medical equipment, supplies, and furniture for the public health intervention at PoEs. 5. Conduct regular monitoring and post assessment, organizing and conducting the sector joint monitoring and assessment to assess health situation and identify urgent needs and gaps among the most vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees, and host communities. IOM will also ensure a regular attendance in the sector meetings to share key outputs and challenges for future programming.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of community to create a protective environment. The project will pay special attention to women of reproductive age, PLW, children U5, and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health services and are thus more vulnerable to large scale epidemiological events and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities, strengthening women’s participation, and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers and addressing negative health seeking behaviors to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. For the protection against GBV the project will strengthen the coordination between the supported health facilities and GBV Sub-cluster, by facilitating the capacity building of the healthcare staff for clinical management of rape (CMR), psychological first aid (PFA) and being aware of the way to maintain the confidentiality and referral pathways.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will implement key principles ensuring accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase by conducting an immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected populations as well as local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women (especially PLW), children, youth, and people with disabilities. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community-based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, CHWs will be trained as mentors and key messengers addressing negative traditional practices as well as protection threats such as harassment, sexual exploitation and abuse against women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearers to inform the sector partners in health and protection of any protection concerns. Applying IOM's expertise in enhancing the protection of migrants, including IDPs and returnees, and crisis-affected populations through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to victims/survivors of SEA and GBV in strong coordination with the sector partners as needed.
