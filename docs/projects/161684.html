Project Summary

Mercy Corps with funding from USAID is currently implementing a food security Responsive Economic Assistance to Conflict Affected Households (REACH) in North East Nigeria. The multi-pronged program aims at increasing access to both staple and nutrient-dense food through local markets using E-vouchers while also shaping behaviours to support optimal nutrition. The program is expected to extend in 2020 and during the extension complementary nutrition interventions are planned to maximum response impact and its contribution to prevention and detection of malnutrition among vulnerable individuals living in 13,512 Households in Damboa and 5000 Households in Bama Local Government Areas (LGAs). Planned complementary activities include a). Promotion of adequate dietary practices within the first 1000 days of life, through appropriate maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding practices using enhanced social behaviour change frameworks and approaches i.e. use of nutrition promoters, Mother Support Groups, Male Engagement and Mass Sensitization, b). Provision of fresh food vouchers to households with malnourished individuals and pregnant/ lactating women to provide immediate quality diet diversification, c). Kitchen gardening and backyard poultry farming to ensure increased sustainable access to nutrient-dense food items among vulnerable households. d). Community based nutrition surveillance and gap filling for Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) services to increase coverage and timely care.

1 - Needs

Various assessments done in Borno State report global and severe food insecurity with about 41.8% of the population being food insecure. According to the Cadre Harmonisé (CH), out of 27 LGAs in Borno state, 21 LGAs fall in the crisis and emergency phases, including Bama and Damboa, accounting for 1,750,143 individuals affected by food insecurity. Since April 2019, there has been an increase of 2.4% food insecurity prevalence reported among the displaced population compared to February 2018. The most recent preliminary findings of the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by Mercy Corps in September 2019 underscores the deteriorating vulnerability status of displaced populations in these localities. The findings show that IDPs across the state are the most negatively affected population in terms of food security and livelihoods, while returnee Households (HHs) in Borno are affected severely. 56% of HHs in Borno are considered more vulnerable (IDPs and returnees at 42%) compared to the other two states in the Northeast. Overall, Borno has the highest percentage of HHs with a poor food consumption score, mostly in conflict-affected areas. In Borno State, both IDP and returnee HHs have an average Food Consumption Scores (FCS) in the borderline category (average FCS of 33% for IDP HHs and 34% for returnee HHs across the state), while Bama is among the top 5 LGAs out of 27 for having poor FCS at 26%. With regards to nutrition situation, the 2019 Round 8, NENFSS reported a slight reduction in GAM rates (8.1%) measured using Weight for Height Z-Scores in Borno State however the rates are still poor according to WHO classification. The KAP survey conducted by Mercy Corps in March 2019 further revealed good practice in some key IYCF behaviour promoted for example 93% of women sampled had breastfed their children and 92% were still breastfeeding. However, some practices were still poor for example, Hand washing at critical times was at only 68% and concerning complementary feeding, none of the sampled children had received a Minimum Adequate Diet. In addition, the current Mercy Corps Program nutrition screening data, reveal reducing prevalence of acute malnutrition in children U5 living in Damboa, however the situation is still in critical state (>15%). The Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) coverage assessment conducted in Damboa earlier in the year also revealed program coverage at 53.7% below the recommended standards for Urban settings (70%), the findings also note that some villages are at a far distance to CMAM centers and there is a considerable long waiting time for service, which discourages/ limits service delivery. Limited number of Community Health Volunteers who are inadequately trained were also reported and as a result, there are wrong referrals of malnourished cases, which discourages seeking for CMAM services once re-assessed. In the proposed program extension, Mercy Corps will support an enhanced holistic food assistance program integrated with strengthened and at scale nutrition services to ensure improved quality, coverage and timely care for malnourished individuals.

2 - Activities

Various food assistance complementary nutrition activities are planned including Promotion of appropriate Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding practices through Social Behavior Change (SBC) and diet diversification. The project extension will review and build on the success of the current SBC approaches, frameworks and platforms to strengthen and scale up behavior change especially that which promotes good nutrition in the first 1,000 days of life - the time spanning roughly between conception and one's second birthday. This will be achieved through enhanced social behavior change frameworks and approaches e.g. Use of nutrition promoters, Mother Support Groups, Male Engagement and Mass Sensitization. Diet diversification will also be enhanced through distribution of fresh vegetables, fruit and animal protein vouchers to increase availability and access of quality nutritious foods among admitted malnourished children, pregnant women in 2nd and 3rd trimester and lactating women with children 0-6 months that seek health services at health facilities. To ensure sustainable dietary diversity among households with malnourished children, caregivers of these children will be linked to Nutrition support groups promoting backyard/ kitchen gardening and small-scale home poultry production. Mercy Corps will support simple and sustainable SMART agronomy and livelihood techniques that bring in a longer-term quality diet diversification most especially production of vegetables, fruits and animal protein food sources, which are usually missing in the general food / voucher distributions. This will include equipping and skills building on agronomy to facilitate kitchen/ backyard gardening, small-scale poultry production and nutrition education to promote proper utilization. Lastly, Mercy Corps will support and strengthen nutrition surveillance systems and where gaps in CMAM services exist, Mercy Corps will complement existing partners through establishing mobile nutrition teams to provide CMAM outreach services to improve coverage and timely detection and treatment of children under 5 and pregnant and lactating mothers in undeserved areas. Mercy Corps will also continue to support and strengthen active case finding, referral and follow-up of acute malnourished children and PLWs at community level by existing Nutrition Promoters and Women Nutrition support Groups. Additional Nutrition promoters will be deployed in densely populated areas. All Nutrition Promoters will receive refresher training on correct measuring of MUAC and checking Oedema, they will be equipped with MUAC tapes, necessary data collection and referral tools and periodic monitoring and supportive supervision conducted by program staff. Periodic mass nutrition screening will be organised to mop-out and increase coverage. Towards the end of the project, Mercy Corps will conduct a SMART survey and SQUEAC assessment within Damboa LGA and / Bama LGA to evaluate the effect of project activities on prevalence of malnutrition and coverage of CMAM program.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As described above, Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community Relief Committees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, with representatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard and addressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members, helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter and services. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) will be continued in the RAI Borno 2 project to enable a safe and effective feedback/complaint system for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. The CARM includes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps in Gombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate beneficiaries) to give feedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered through designated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by the focal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of all complaints within a specified timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also be used to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under the Protection-sector specific programming described below; that information will be used to reduce existing and newly identified risks. The program will monitor the security situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situation deteriorates.
