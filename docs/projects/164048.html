Project Summary

This project will aim to support the households of both host community and IDPs of men, women, boys and girls which are affected by the Syrian crisis to experience less impact of the conflict and economic crisis by safely accessing humanitarian assistance through provision of the agricultural inputs for 550 farmers (wheat cultivation) and livestock support for 300 IDPs (poultry and fodders).
The agricultural kit will contain: Wheat seeds 250 KGs, Composed Fertilizer N: P: K (15:15:15) 250 Kg, Azote Fertilizer Urea 46% fertilizers 150 kg, herbicide 1 liter, insecticide (sun pest) 1 liter and one irrigation fees (diesel cost) which is equal to 75 $. These inputs are enough to cultivate 1 hectare and in line with the Recommendations for Response Package for Sector Objectives 2 and 3 issued by the FS cluster. In addition to the appreciate training modalities which contains integrated pest and disease management (IPM), fertilizer application, harvest and post-harvest management, Water-efficient irrigation, weed control, climate smart agriculture, resources management, and waste management.
For the poultry support, we will target the IDPs families who have not benefited from wheat cultivation activity will be supported by the poultry kits. Wherein, we are going to distribute 300 poultry kits (13chicken and 2roosters) with its necessary amount of feed (150 Kg) for once in order to secure the food needs of their families from eggs as well as the possibility of selling part of the production to create small income. The beneficiaries will get the needed technical trainings on poultry health and raising.
The Beneficiaries in the project will be selected according to the selection criteria, targeting the most vulnerable people, and prioritizing the most in need.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2020, 7.9 million people inside Syria are considered to be food Insecure with an additional 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity Physical and Mental Wellbeing. Where before the crisis in Syria, the farmers' situations were good in general, livelihoods were in average and the inputs were accessible. By the start of 2012 all the Agricultural Directorates (related to regime) has stopped working in the non-regime area, which resulted in decreasing the living situation of the farmers, increasing the prices of the agricultural inputs and losing some others and increasing the irrigation fees due to the high prices of the fuel and the electricity outages which stopped most of the machinery used for agriculture. As more families adopt negative coping strategies and dependency on food aid, the risk of malnutrition increases as their ability to supplement the food aid with fresh food decreases. The most vulnerable groups are young children, pregnant and lactating women, women of childbearing age, elderly and chronically/acutely ill. Due to the result of HNO 2020 and needs assessment that conducted by MASRRAT team, the project will target two sub-districts Idleb -Maaret Tamsrin and Aleppo – Jabal Saman - Atareb. Where most of the villages are in need to support in terms of agricultural inputs as well as support the IDPs within the food production as small livestock and animal feed distribution or any assets to produce food. Most of the farmers, don’t have sufficient ability to farming, because of poverty and lack of income and lack of job opportunities. While for the other HHs who do not have agricultural land from IDPs and HHs, these reasons contribute to increasing the suffering of the population in the region, which affects negatively to the quantities of production and agricultural inputs in the markets.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will target the wheat farmers (women and men) by distributing wheat seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, and irrigation fees. The beneficiaries of this activity will be from the local community and IDPs who settled for a while, own or renting land of one Hectare suitable for wheat and have irrigation resources. The beneficiaries will be selected according to the score system considering the most in need and the vulnerable households. While for livestock distribution activity, a number of the IDPs in the proposed location will benefit from the poultry kits with its necessary amount of feed enough for 6 months. The procurement for the wheat activity will be inside Syria and for the Poultry activity, the procurement process will be from Turkey (the poultry will be vaccinated and resistant to diseases).The project will be announced and mobilized in the community. Then the registration phase will take place in a suitable place for the potential beneficiaries in terms of safety and access. The data will be collected, cleaned and analyzed remotely (in Turkey) to ensure transparency. A verification phase will be conducted to ensure the validity of the given information by the potential beneficiaries. GAP training will be conducted to the wheat beneficiaries in order to raise their agricultural acknowledge and support them with the modern agricultural methods. After issuing the final accepted lists, a period will be given for complaints. All the complaints will be treated according to the accountability system of the organization. a proper place in the community in terms of safety and accessibility for the beneficiaries as well as in time for the activity type in order to be useful for the beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

According to the project activities, the following protection risks and mitigating measures are presented:1)- Protection risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: High. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. 2)- Protection risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. 3. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. 4. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. 5. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 6. Child Protection messaging to non-Protection actors 3)- Protection risk: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favouritism, including risk for project staffLikelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonised as possible.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk management process designed to include all of project life. It includes processes for risk management planning, identification, analysis, monitoring and control. Many of these processes are updated throughout the project life-cycle as new risks can be identified at any time. According to the assumptions: the targeted locations of the project are considered relatively safe as there weren't any kind of conflict for a period of time, and the targeted locations are accessible, in addition to, all materials and required inputs are available in the local markets inside Syria. MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: 1- High prices of agricultural inputs /and other materials and changes in exchange rates.Mitigation: price freeze for the prices with the vendors2- lack of some material and inputs. Mitigation: Import from other regions inside Syria or from Turkey3 -Deterioration of security situation in the project’s areas.Mitigation: Postpone the implementation process until the stabilization of the security situation.4- The beneficiaries Farmers might sell the allocated inputs.Mitigation: Commitments and guarantees from beneficiaries and the local council not to sell inputs and monitoring the process of agriculture for the beneficiaries. 5- Urea distribution risks:Mitigation: Supervising on the use of all the distributed quantity of urea by the field team and the local councils (this material is 46% caliber and it is considered much less dangerous than other types).7- All chickens are delivered to beneficiaries without any deathsThe risk: death of 1% of animals during transport them from the crossing to the distribution sites due to atmospheric factors or delay during transport.Risk mitigation: Work on moving animals at moderate temperature
