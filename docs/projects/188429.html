Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food security situation and increase the self-reliance for the women headed families by starting their own projects and gaining their incomes, in addition, to strengthening local production capacity and supporting the local markets by availability and diversity of processed food, therefore, the community will be stable against external conflicts. This project will support 60 women-headed families through vocational training, Tools and equipment, and raw materials to prepare dairy products, in order to start their own projects and secure incomes to cover their families’ needs.
Each family will receive:
Tools and equipment up to 950 $ maximum based on the available items at the home to save more money and purchase more quantities of raw materials.Three types of training: Food Processing (dairy production), Profit project management, and Marketing.
The beneficiaries will prepare the dairy products using the provided items and sell them in the local markets and gain income to secure the family’s needs and cover the cost of the next month. (The feasibility Analysis is attached)
The selected beneficiaries will receive three types of training by professional trainers as the following:
Vocational training about food Processing/dairy production: This training will include the best practices for food processing including processing principles, packaging, safety and security procedures, environmental consideration through the production.Profit project management: This training will include designing and planning the project, budgeting, financial management of the incomes, and project development for the future.Marketing: This training will include a better understanding of the markets and better marketing procedures in addition to planning to sell the products and increase their profit.
The beneficiaries can add their profit to the capital in the next month to ‎purchase more quantities of raw materials and produce more products, which will maximize the generated.
Provision of Tools, equipment, and raw materials to the selected: Each beneficiary will receive: Tools and equipment up to 400 $ maximum based on the available items at the home to save more money and purchase more quantities of raw materials, the items include (Cooking pots with different sizes, Gas cooker, Gas Bottle, Refrigerator, Thermometer, Strainers with different sizes, Cheese presser, cloth gauze for filtration, Plastic washtub, Spoons, knives, Foldable table with umbrella, Food weight scales, Plastic bag welding machine, Jerry cans with the size of 25 liters, Apron, face masks, gloves, medical hair caps, and packing material: jars- plastic bags -plastic pails)Raw materials for 2 months up to 450 $ per month, will include (milk, rennet, salt, black seeds) which will be sufficient to process 40 kg of milk per day. Provision of technical support to the beneficiaries through house-to-house visits Those visits aim to monitor the beneficiaries’ work, ensure the food quality, correct the errors, and ‎ ensure the food safety principles by CBC experts. Those visits will conduct over 3 months during the project period to ensure that the beneficiaries can save sufficient income to sustain the operations after the end of the project. Support the women to market their Products: This support will be through the following actions: Advertising of their products in the social media accounts. Support them to show their products in the public local markets/Exhibitions/Pazars by coordination with the local councils to not impose fesses for them Provision of transportation cost to the regular public markets Exhibitions Pazars 25 $ per beneficiary per month over 2 months Provision of foldable Pazar table with umbrella per beneficiary to be used in showing her products.CBC team will hang awareness posters against COVID-19 in streets and gathering places and stick awareness stickers on the kits that will be distributed to the beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2021, the food security situation in Syria has steadily worsened over the past three years, 56 percent increase in the number of food-insecure people from 7.9 million in 2019 to 12.4 million by the end of 2020 is unprecedented. Two out of three Syrians are in need of some form of food and agricultural assistance, with an additional 1.8 million at risk of falling into food insecurity. The number of severely food insecure people has more than doubled, from 0.6 million in 2019 to 1.27 million people in 2021 (six percent of the population). HNO 2021 also mentioned that the economy has experienced irreparable harm since the crisis began, with the gross domestic product having declined by 60 per cent and the government increasingly unable to raise sufficient revenue to subsidize essential commodities such as fuel and bread on which the most vulnerable familiesThe Syrian pound is in virtual freefall having lost 78 %of its value since October 2019, while price increases for staple goods are at an all-time high. More than 90% of the population lives below the poverty line. 60-65% are estimated to live in extreme poverty, up from 50-60% in 2019.This project will support 60 women headed families with vocational training, Tools and equipment, and raw materials to prepare dairy products, in order to start their own projects and secure incomes to cover their families’ needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: M. - Impact: H. -Mitigating: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute them in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. **The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. -Likelihood: w. - Impact: H.-Mitigating: 1- We will choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. 2- 4- Ensure that the complaint mechanisms for reporting SEA/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email,..) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.3-In case any exploitation happens, CBC will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referring the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.
