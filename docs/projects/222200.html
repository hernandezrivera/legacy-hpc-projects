Project Summary

The Protection for Resilience Building initiative directly contributes to the overarching 2024 Protection Sector Objectives in Nigeria, which aim to safeguard vulnerable populations, reduce risks, and promote resilience in the context of protracted displacement and ongoing conflict. This integrated response prioritizes a rights-based, survivor-centered, and inclusive approach to addressing protection concerns in fragile settings across Nigeria.
Core Components Aligned with 2024 Protection Objectives
Protection Case Management:
Consistent with the sector’s focus on ensuring access to timely and appropriate services, this component offers individualized case management to address acute protection risks. By linking survivors to healthcare, legal aid, and psychosocial support, it ensures the well-being of displaced persons and fosters their recovery. Special attention is given to survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), aligning with the sector's emphasis on mitigating GBV risks and strengthening referral pathways.
Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Support:
The HLP component addresses tenure insecurity—a key barrier to sustainable reintegration and recovery for displaced populations and returnees. Activities such as resolving land disputes and promoting access to secure housing align with the sector’s goal of reinforcing durable solutions and community cohesion, critical for long-term stability.
Individual Protection Assistance (IPA):
Direct material and financial assistance provided under IPA bridges immediate gaps in safety and dignity, echoing the sector’s 2024 objectives of meeting urgent protection needs. By reducing exposure to risks like forced displacement or exploitation, IPA strengthens resilience and empowers affected populations to recover and rebuild their lives.
Protection Monitoring:
Through the collection and analysis of data on protection risks and violations, this component supports evidence-based programming and advocacy—both integral to the 2024 objective of enhancing the accountability of humanitarian actors. Regular reporting on trends and gaps informs response strategies and ensures that resources target the most vulnerable populations.
Trafficking in Persons (TIP) Prevention and Response:
TIP prevention activities address vulnerabilities that fuel exploitation, such as poverty, lack of education, and displacement. By strengthening awareness campaigns and referral systems, this aligns with the sector’s commitment to addressing specific protection concerns while promoting the safety and dignity of survivors.
Purpose and Target Populations
Aligned with the Protection Sector’s 2024 objectives, the program aims to:
Reduce Protection Risks: Address immediate vulnerabilities of displaced populations and host communities by improving access to protection services.
Strengthen Resilience: Empower communities to withstand future shocks through capacity-building, social cohesion initiatives, and sustainable support mechanisms.
The initiative targets internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities, returnees, women, children, and survivors of violence in Northeast Nigeria. Prioritized locations include areas experiencing acute displacement and those transitioning toward recovery, such as Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States.
Impact and Alignment with Sector Goals
This intervention reinforces the Nigeria Protection Sector’s 2024 goals by:
Promoting Access to Services: Ensuring marginalized groups can safely access life-saving protection services.
Strengthening Coordination: Enhancing synergies with national actors, local governments, and humanitarian partners for a comprehensive response.
Advancing Durable Solutions: Supporting sustainable recovery for displaced populations through HLP, resilience-building, and social cohesion initiatives.

1 - Needs

The project is designed to address a range of urgent protection needs in the Bay states of Nigeria—Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe—areas severely affected by ongoing conflict. The assessment of these needs has highlighted several critical vulnerabilities that the project seeks to mitigate through its targeted activities: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection: One of the most pressing needs identified is the widespread occurrence of gender-based violence, including sexual violence, exploitation, and abuse, particularly affecting women and girls. Conflict, displacement, and breakdown of social structures have increased the risks of GBV, with survivors often facing barriers to accessing medical, legal, and psychosocial support. Additionally, children, especially unaccompanied minors, are at high risk of exploitation, forced recruitment, and trafficking. The project will provide specialized protection services to address GBV and child protection needs, ensuring survivors have access to healthcare, legal assistance, and psychosocial support. Displacement and Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Insecurity: The prolonged conflict has caused massive displacement in the Bay states, with many individuals and families unable to return to their homes or secure safe, habitable housing. The lack of clear land and property rights poses a significant barrier to durable solutions, leading to uncertainty and further marginalization. The project will support individuals facing housing insecurity by providing legal assistance to address HLP issues, resolve property disputes, and facilitate the return or relocation of displaced persons to secure living conditions. Trafficking in Persons (TIP): The displacement caused by conflict, combined with limited protection mechanisms, has significantly increased the risk of trafficking, particularly for women, children, and vulnerable individuals. These populations are often forced into exploitative situations due to the lack of protection and support systems. The project will focus on preventing trafficking through awareness campaigns, capacity-building for local actors, and direct support for trafficking survivors, including legal aid, psychosocial services, and livelihood assistance. Limited Access to Basic Services and Support: Many communities in the Bay states face limited access to essential services, including healthcare, education, and social protection systems. The lack of access to such services increases vulnerability to exploitation, violence, and neglect, especially among displaced persons and host communities. The project will address these gaps by offering direct assistance to individuals in need, including through Protection Case Management, which will connect vulnerable individuals to healthcare, legal services, and psychosocial support. Emergency Assistance for Vulnerable Populations: Internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities, and returnees are facing significant challenges in accessing basic necessities such as food, shelter, and sanitation. The project will provide Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) to meet immediate needs, such as emergency cash assistance, household items, and relocation support, particularly for those at high risk of exploitation and harm. Protection Monitoring and Data Gaps: Another identified need is the lack of systematic protection monitoring in the region. The absence of accurate, real-time data on protection risks and violations makes it difficult to respond effectively and allocate resources efficiently. The project will address this gap by using mobile technologies and data-sharing platforms for real-time protection monitoring, ensuring that emerging risks are documented and responded to in a timely manner. Inadequate Community-based Protection Mechanisms: There is a need for stronger community engagement and local ownership in protection efforts.

2 - Activities

The activities and targets selected for this intervention are based on a thorough assessment of the needs and vulnerabilities of populations in the Bay states of Nigeria—Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe. These states have been severely affected by conflict, leading to ongoing displacement, violence, and other protection risks. The region faces critical challenges such as gender-based violence (GBV), child protection issues, trafficking, and insecurity related to housing, land, and property rights. These issues, compounded by the lack of access to essential services and support systems, have left many individuals and communities in desperate need of assistance. This intervention is designed to address these gaps and support the safe recovery and resilience of affected populations. The chosen activities aim to address the most urgent protection needs in the region while ensuring that interventions are effective, accessible, and sustainable. We have specifically tailored our approach to ensure that services are provided in a flexible and inclusive manner, with special attention to marginalized and hard-to-reach groups. Protection Case Management is a key activity, designed to support survivors of GBV, trafficking, and other protection risks. Given the region’s ongoing challenges, such as displacement and trauma, protection case management is vital in offering individualized support. Caseworkers will assess survivors' needs, provide immediate assistance, and ensure they are linked with necessary services, such as medical care, legal support, and psychosocial assistance. The use of both face-to-face and remote case management ensures that even people in remote or conflict-prone areas will receive support. Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) support is critical in helping displaced persons and those who have suffered violations of property rights. As many displaced individuals struggle with housing insecurity, our intervention will provide legal support to address property disputes and facilitate access to safe housing. Resolving HLP issues is essential for achieving durable solutions, enabling individuals to regain stability and rebuild their lives. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) is another essential component of this project. By providing emergency cash assistance, household items, and relocation support, we will reduce the vulnerability of individuals at high risk, particularly those exposed to exploitation and trafficking. This intervention ensures that the immediate needs of vulnerable populations are met, while also providing them with a sense of security and dignity. Protection Monitoring will be an ongoing activity throughout the project to track protection risks and gaps in services. Mobile technologies and data-sharing platforms will allow for real-time monitoring of protection violations and emerging threats, enabling the intervention to adjust strategies quickly and effectively. This data will also inform advocacy efforts to ensure that resources are allocated to the most pressing needs. The prevention and response to Trafficking in Persons (TIP) is another critical aspect of the project. The ongoing displacement exacerbates the risk of trafficking, particularly among women, children, and vulnerable individuals. To address this, the project will conduct awareness-raising campaigns and provide capacity-building sessions for local authorities and community leaders. Survivors of trafficking will also receive specialized reintegration support, including legal aid and livelihood assistance. In terms of targets, the intervention is focused on reaching populations most at risk, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities, women, children, and survivors of violence. The geographic focus will be on the areas most affected by conflict in Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa states, ensuring that even the most marginalized populations, including those in rural or isolated areas, are included.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure effective protection mainstreaming throughout the project, we will implement several key modalities designed to integrate protection concerns into all aspects of the intervention. These include: Staff Capacity Building: All project staff, including caseworkers, community mobilizers, and field officers, will receive comprehensive training on protection principles, such as gender-based violence (GBV), child protection, trafficking prevention, and the rights of displaced persons. This will ensure staff can identify risks, provide appropriate referrals, and respond in a survivor-centered manner. Community Participation and Feedback Mechanisms: The project will establish community-based committees and grievance redress mechanisms to enable affected populations to voice their concerns and participate in decision-making processes. This ensures the project is accountable to those it serves and that protection needs are consistently addressed. Data Protection and Confidentiality: Strict protocols will be in place to ensure that all sensitive data, such as case management information, is securely stored and handled with the highest regard for privacy. This protects the safety of beneficiaries, particularly those at risk, like survivors of violence or trafficking. Gender and Age-Sensitive Approaches: The project will adopt a gender and age-sensitive approach to address the specific needs of women, children, elderly persons, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups. Tailored interventions will be made for different needs, such as safe spaces for women and children and specialized GBV services. Protection Monitoring: Continuous protection monitoring will be embedded in the project to track emerging risks. Real-time data collection through mobile apps and feedback from communities will help inform operational decisions, ensuring that protection issues are identified and addressed promptly. Inclusive Approach: The project will ensure that marginalized groups, especially those in hard-to-reach areas, are not excluded from essential protection services. All activities, such as case management, HLP support, and trafficking prevention, will be designed with inclusivity in mind. These modalities ensure that protection principles are woven throughout every stage of the project, from design to implementation, and that protection risks are effectively managed and addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

o ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, the project will implement accessible and inclusive mechanisms, prioritizing safety and confidentiality: Inclusive Feedback Channels: Community-based committees, grievance redress mechanisms (GRMs), and focus group discussions (FGDs) will be established, ensuring participation from diverse groups such as women, youth, persons with disabilities, and IDPs. These channels will facilitate open communication, allowing community members to share concerns, suggestions, and feedback. Technology and Safe Spaces: Mobile technology will be used to collect feedback from remote or hard-to-reach populations. Additionally, safe spaces will be created for women and other vulnerable groups to provide feedback without fear of reprisal. These spaces will offer a secure environment to discuss sensitive issues like gender-based violence and trafficking. Monitoring and Accessibility: The project will regularly monitor and follow up on feedback, ensuring concerns are addressed. All mechanisms will be available in multiple languages and accessible formats, including braille or audio for persons with disabilities, ensuring inclusivity for all community members.
