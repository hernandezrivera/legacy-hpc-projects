Project Summary

Context: In the shelter sector, the Beirut Port Explosion on 4 August 2020 directly impacted almost 300,000 people and caused damage to 72,000 apartments across 9,200 buildings (OCHA, August 2020). The damage varies from one area to the other according to its proximity to the epicenter, which —apart from the port— hit residential buildings, shops, hospitals, schools, and universities. Areas of intervention are categorized in epicenters in reference to the explosion with least, moderately, badly and severely damaged.
Given the dire socio-economic and financial crisis that Lebanon has been going through and especially since October 2019, compounded with the challenges borne out of the COVID-19 pandemic, the average Beiruti family can simply no longer afford to rehabilitate their homes.
Nusaned’s Roofs That Shield program aims to provide the basic human right for shelter so that individuals, families and communities have the opportunity to have a place to live in that protects their health, nurtures their well being and fosters an environment of security, stability and opportunity for self-development and economic growth.
Project goals: The objective of this project is to rehabilitate 550 out of the targeted total 2,050 residential units in the neighborhoods that incurred damage due to the Beirut Explosion. Those units include 80-120 sqm houses of lower-income families, and fall under TWO levels; LEVEL 1 (LIGHT) AND LEVEL 2 (MODERATE).
Project description & Charitable purpose: Nusaned’s Roofs That Shield program is one of Nusaned’s Core programs. We have gained experience in the field of rehabilitation, and have a track record since we started in December 2019 with the objective to rehabilitate 1,000 houses in marginalized areas all over Lebanon.
Our Roofs That Shield Beirut sub-program focuses entirely on Beirut, where we have already mobilized a team of experts, volunteers, and partner organizations to expedite the assessment and intervention through our Shelter Hotline which we launched on 10 August. We have received almost 500 requests during this early post-crisis period, and are currently committed to rehabilitating them.
Our Target for Phase 1 is to rehabilitate a total of 2,050 units:
a. 750 units IN LEVEL 1 (minor repairs and replacement of shattered glass only).
b. 750 units IN LEVEL 2 (moderate repairs, partial replacement of interior doors, replacement of exterior doors & windows, rehabilitation of electro-mechanical networks).
c. 550 units IN LEVEL 2 (substantial repairs, replacement of exterior and interior doors & windows, replacement of electro-mechanical networks, as well as renovating 1 bathroom and kitchen).
Within the next 3-month period, and within the FLASH appeal project, we are targeting to complete 550 units;
250 units IN LEVEL 1 (LIGHT)
300 units IN LEVEL 2 (MODERATE).

1 - Needs

We have now migrated our quantifiable, value-based needs assessment to the urban context & complexities of Beirut. As such, vulnerable groups, including women-headed households, vulnerable women & girls, the elderly, & people with disabilities are given priority. We are therefore able to assess the socio-economic status & health needs of the potential beneficiary, as well as conduct a detailed assessment of the residential unit (including furniture), at the unit as well as at the building scale. The damage assessment at the unit scale includes:1. Unit ref # + unit location2. Personal Info: Name, Ownership, Nationality, Phone #, Profession, Source of Income, family members + YoB, Nationality, need for temporary shelter, other needs (food, clothes, diapers, etc), copy of deed or rent contract, copy of ID/passport, beneficiary oral report, health needs (medicine, equipment, vulnerabilities, assistive devices, physiotherapy, prosthesis, health coverage3. Building Info: plot #, building name, building age, # floors, # basements, # units, # empty units,4. Unit assessment: unit type (apt or house), floor, unit #, unit area, and then a detailed assessment (qty,type,damages) of components including doors & fenestrations, MEP network, kitchen, bathroom(s), floor tiles - as well as their condition (good, needs repair, needs replacement), and finally loose furniture and equipment (Fridge, Oven TV) needs.5. Building assessment: if we are intervening at the building scale, then needs are assessed starting with a structural review, as well as building entrance lobby and core, and MEP systems. This assessment is finalized by a photographic survey (interior and exterior, when needed) to document the existing conditions, and when works are completed to upload the "after" images. This documentation is 1 component of our reporting mechanism to donors and partners, of the impact achieved per unit.

2 - Activities

1. Shelter Hotline: Since 9 August, Nusaned has been operating through our shelter hotline that has received almost 500 requests to date, including referrals from our partners. These requests have come from all over the city, and thus our response during this early relief phase has been in addressing shelter needs in all neighborhoods that have incurred various levels of damage. 2. Needs assessmentOur Team leaders & volunteers (currently 15 teams, including 4 from partner NGOs) conduct the initial site visits and needs assessment after a) receiving an on-line training session to go through the app user guidelines, safety & security measures + mandatory COVID precautions, as well as walk through signing the MoU with beneficiaries and b) job shadowing for at least one day.To date, assessed 230 units, and have dispatched our structural engineering team to review and assess relevant cases to ensure that we can intervene safely. 3. Data consolidationOnce needs are identified, damage assessments are conducted, and priorities of intervention are set based on the value outputs (with priority given to HHs with vulnerabilities) data is consolidated for action.The project activities then focus primarily on implementation of rehabilitation under our shelter program (Roofs That Shield) and supported by the distribution of needed goods under our relief program (Goods in Need).The data is aggregated and generated on TWO different dashboards to visualize a) the shelter project status (assessed/under rehabilitation/rehabilitated) b) goods distributed 4. MoU signatureAfter a beneficiary is identified, we sign a Memo of Understanding (MoU) delineating scope of intervention and timeline, as well as terms of agreement to establish commitment, trust, and transparency for both parties, a copy of which shall remain with each party. 5. Implementation:Shelter: unit rehabilitation falls under 3 categories: LEVEL 1 ($1,500), LEVEL 2 ($4,500) LEVEL 2 HIGH ($10,000)Since the unit & building damage assessment includes quantities of main items (floor area, fenestrations, doors, glazing, etc), we are then able to extract data for various purposes: a) generate a Bills of Quantity per unit for budgeting purposesb) using this BoQ, communicate scope w (sub)contractors, and agree on budget & timelinec) communicate info with beneficiary d) once a (sub)contractor is dispatched, members of our Site Supervision team follow up on project to ensure implementation in a timely and proper manner. e) bring project to closure + documentation for reportingTo date, completed 50 units, and 60 are under rehabilitation. Our shelter program in Beirut will also include creating or rehabilitating community spaces, as already implemented in our Roofs That Shield Akkar sub-program. Goods:f) extract data from needs assessment to strategize distribution of relief items with focus on vulnerable groupsg) add recipients to our List of Beneficiaries to be able to provide sustainable assistance 6. Monitoring & ReportWe have developed the processes to streamline communication from assessment to implementation to reporting; the Nusaned app is 1 platform to share our impact transparently w our donors and partners. 7. FundraisingOngoing efforts to raise funds to implement the project of rehabilitating 2,050 units at $10M total project budget. Financial standing to date:secured funds USD 850k + 1,600,000,000LL,Promised USD 300k, Prospect USD 500k 8. Nusaned BasecampTo optimize team mobilization-Program Leads, Project Coordinatorsx2, Team Leadersx10, volunteersx50, foremenx2, contractorsx10 (to date), partners & support administrative team-& to facilitate implementation on the ground, the 350sqm space in Mar Mikhael (zone 39) will serve as site offices, storage facility, etc, in addition to our main office in Verdun, and our shelter operations office in Hamra.
