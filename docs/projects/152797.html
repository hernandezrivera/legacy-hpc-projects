Project Summary

As a narrative of peace implementation takes tall county-wide and in places where populations are returning, and as stability takes scene in some parts of the Country, fighting still continue being experienced in some parts of the Country namely, Central Equatoria State, Western Bahr El Ghazal and Upper Nile.
However, based on the September IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4).
But with calmness experiencing in many parts of the Country and where agriculture activities took place, food security has improved slightly with the green harvest in September relative to July and August, and further improvements are expected in the post-harvest period between October and December 2018 when the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse is most likely to reduce to 4.4 million (43% of the total population), with 26,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5).
In the early of the year 2019, an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). KEY IPC FINDINGS: SEPTEMBER 2018- MARCH 2019.
With economic meltdown and severely reduced crop yields due to late rains, flooding and outbreak of Fall Army Worm in the country, deterioration of food availability in areas around markets as a result of the protracted fall of local currency against the dollar which has also further caused eroded purchasing power of displaced and returning populations, experiencing of humanitarian access challenges, un predictable insecurity hampering movement of populations to produce their own food due to attack incidents while cases of malnutrition have been on the increase a situation that has left South population with limited livelihoods options hence in need of assistance.
Currently POH is present in Kapoeta East and Luri Counties implementing FSL and GBV integration programs and it is at this leverage point that FSL activities will be scaled up to provide a holistic package to the most vulnerable households. An integration approach with GBV sector will be at center stage to ensure that vulnerable women and girls and other people with special needs and returnees will be targeted by the interventions. The project will also take cognizance that women and children are the mostly affected populations when a conflict breaks out. They are usually left with the burden to source food and other basic needs for the family hence the project will envisage to build resilience and foster sustainability in women and youth at risk especially girls with this livelihoods option of increasing food production to support the coping mechanisms.
Business Skills development will be key component in driving the agenda of self sustenance and resilience building among the target groups. Targeting 60% women for the assistance under this project reaffirms POH position in addressing issues of gender disparities and also protection of women against gender based violence. Gender and SGBV will be main-streamed through out all the project activities in close coordination with the relevant sectors and other stakeholders.

Needs

With the deterioration of food availability in many parts of the country and with the KEY IPC FINDINGS: SEPTEMBER 2018- MARCH 2019 projections of an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season resulting in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) gives the justification of the following need identified for 2019 interventions in order to implement this project by increasing food production and build resilience to the most vulnerable household. These includes,1. Saving lives through food assistance by cash voucher and cash transfer2. Distribution of crop seeds, fast maturing vegetable seeds and agriculture tools3. Establishing demo plots for farmer for practical learning sessions4. Training of community agriculture extension managers on farm skill management, site management and reporting skills and Monitoring and Evaluation from the beneficiaries5. Training beneficiaries on farm preparation, seeding, crop management, pest/disease control and post harvest/storage techniques and management6. Training beneficiaries on market access techniques and marketingIn the best interest of value for money and increasing efficiency in humanitarian programming, POH will upscale its experience gained in other locations in cash programming under this project with a target of 30% of the funding being cash based and these will be preceded by market assessment and continuous market price monitoring. Taking into consideration issues of environmental management, the project objective also include cash for work activities aimed at promotion and preservation of a safe and climate smart environment through cleaning and planting of trees. With professional staff capacity and capacitated humanitarian organization, with FSL coordination team support from the head-quarter and at the field based staff implementing the project will be fully supported to ensure that the set objectives are achieved.

Activities

The following activities will be implemented in close integration with POH Protection team taking into consideration the needs of people with special needs 1. Saving lives through food assistance by cash voucher and cash transfer to 300 HH2. Distribution of crop seeds, fast maturing vegetable seeds and agriculture tools to 300 HH3. Establishing 3 sites measuring 100m x 100m demo plots for farmer for practical learning sessions4. Training of 15 community agriculture extension managers on farm skill management, site management and reporting skills and Monitoring and Evaluation from the beneficiaries5. Training 300 participants at a HH level from beneficiaries on farm preparation, seeding, crop management, pest/disease control and post harvest/storage techniques and management6. Training 300 participants drown from HH level of the beneficiaries on market access techniques and marketing.
