Project Summary

Project Summary/ Резюме проекта/ Резюме проекту
(4,000 characters max) The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine is well into its seventh year, and multiple attempts at ceasefires having yielded little respite overall. In 2019 so far, conflict intensity does seem to be on a downwards trend, with a decrease in the number of civilian casualties and security incidents as compared to earlier years. However, the great majority of conflict-related incidents – 94% (INSO data) – continues to take place within 5km on either side of the Line of Contact (LoC), meaning that populations in these areas continue to be disproportionately affected, on a quasi-daily basis, by the conflict and associated protection risks. On top of the effects of the conflict, COVID-19 has spread across Ukraine impacting almost every aspect of daily life and compounding vulnerabilities of populations affected by conflict. According to the REACH 2020 MSNA, 34% of the population surveyed living within the 20km line has experienced loss of or severe diminishment of access to basic services and 20% have experienced complete loss or diminished source of income. Additionally, there was a slight rise in the percentage of households who reported being moderately and severely food insecure between 2019 and 2020 (8% to 10% and 1% to 2%, respectively). Furthermore, a substantial proportion of the population has overlapping vulnerabilities (e.g. elderly with a chronic illness, households with multiple children and members with a disability, etc.), and have continued severe humanitarian needs.
Ukraine is also experiencing overall inflation, which is reflected in the steady increase in the actual minimum subsistence level: this translates into constantly increasing food price. Cost of food was further increased at the onset of COVID-19 – on average food prices changed by 11.6% from February to April 2020 (ACTED, 2020). Incomes of vulnerable populations, especially those living in the 20km area – noting disproportionate representation of pensioners and pre-pensioners – struggle to follow. The gap between incomes and prices is especially pronounced in winter, with food and fuel costs rising sharply during this season, thus decreasing even further ability to meet basic needs and increasing the use of negative coping strategies. As COVID-19 cases are on a sharp upwards trend in Ukraine, the expectation is also that household (HH) resources will be depleted faster/more significantly than usual during the upcoming winter.
In response to this situation, ACTED will implement this project in continuation of 3+ years of Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) programming, to provide the most vulnerable conflict-affected individuals residing close to the LoC in Donetsk and Luhansk (GCA) with the means to cover their critical needs and protection requirements through MPC grants. Focus will be on the most vulnerable groups, who do not have sufficient income to cover these needs, including women headed HHs, HHs of elderly, HHs headed by pre-pensioners who are not eligible for pensions, and HHs who’ve lost their income source due to COVID-19. Other vulnerable categories identified through the vulnerability assessment may also be targeted, in line with ACTED’s selection methodology which is based on a cumulative vulnerability approach approved by the CWG and adapted by ACTED to the Ukrainian context.

1 - Needs

Needs/Нужды/Потреби(4,000 characters max) Though the actual subsistence level has been steadily increasing (34% increase since September 2017), the incomes of vulnerable populations living close to the LoC have not followed the same trend. As per analysis undertaken by ACTED for the CWG in 2019, vulnerable population’s average income is less than half of actual subsistence level (45%). Increasing income gaps hinder the ability of vulnerable populations to meet basic needs especially due to prohibitive transportation (access to markets), healthcare, food, and NFI costs. ACTED program data suggests larger income gaps for households with particular vulnerabilities: 49% for women-headed HHs, 69% for women headed HHs with children, 38% for HHs of elderly; and 40% for single elderly HHs, of whom 90% are women.COVID-19 has put further a strain on individuals due to the decreased availability and increased price of goods. An ACTED-led Rapid Market Assessment in April 2020 found that many HH were not able to fully access basic supplies following the onset of COVID; 15% reported not having complete access to food, 25% to hygiene materials, 58% to medicine, and 66% to personal protective equipment (66%). Food and hygiene item prices also increased by 11.6% and 6.8% respectively from February to April 2020. Access and affordability thus remain key issues to meeting basic needs, especially in light of COVID-19. Also relating to food, the 2020 REACH MSNA shows that the proportion of HHs in the 20km area with poor and borderline Food Consumption Scores is currently at 11%. When disaggregated by gender, women headed HH have a slightly higher proportion (13% compared to 8% for male headed HH). Notably, the population within 20km of the LoC is disproportionately female (59%) and elderly (34%) (REACH, 2020). Women head 71% of all HHs and 90% of single parent HHs (GCA), and make up 40% of those above 60. Women heads of HHs are more likely to be widowed, and thus isolated, than their male counterparts.With over 50% of all HHs in the region retired, poverty is driven by very low pensions (minimum 2,100 UAH per month in 2019; ACTED): while reforms led to pension increases, the great majority of pensioners within 20km of the LoC report that this is insufficient to meet basic needs – in 2018, 79% received pensions that were below actual minimum subsistence level (FSL Cluster Analysis). Individuals funded by the ACCESS III consortium had an average income of 2,0340 UAH. As noted above, the income gap for HHs of elderly is 38%, and increases to 40% for single elderly HHs – 58% of women headed HHs are in fact single, elderly and isolated women. For HHs living close to the LoC with unemployed members aged 50-59, coping capacity is most stressed as this group struggles to find employment but is too young to receive pensions.REACH data (2020) highlights the prevalence of overlapping needs among conflict-affected populations. This is particularly the case for the 5km area, where 33% of households reported overlapping needs, compared to 15% in the 20 km area and 9% further than 20 km. Rural areas are also more likely to experience overlapping needs, with 19% of rural households overall, compared to 15% of households in small urban areas and 7% of households in large urban areas.ACTED will target the following, as groups with the lowest ability to meet basic needs:- 60% women;- 34% elderly;- At least 9% children;- 10% with a disability;- 8% IDPs.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

OutcomeThe most vulnerable individuals closest to the line of contact increase their purchasing power and can meet their basic needs. Multi-Purpose CashACTED will provide 1 round of 3 months of MPC assistance to 4,500 individuals in Donetsk and Luhansk, urban and rural areas within 20km of the LoC. Assistance amount is benchmarked based on CWG recommendation–1150 UAH/per/person until revision.MPC is a cost-efficient modality to provide rapid and efficient assistance to highly vulnerable populations where banks and markets are functional and accessible. ACCESS III endline analysis shows that MPC distribution can reduce average CSI score by over 30 points; 78% of households reduced their CSI score, with 22% showing non-increasing tendency.According to the Rapid Market Assessment led-by ACTED in April 2020 cash remains the preferred modality for 91% of HH in the COVID-19 context. Additionally,100% of ACCESS III beneficiaries were satisfied with a cash modality (ACTED 2020). Under ACCESS III, at least 97% of HHs surveyed post-distribution reported satisfaction with amount of assistance received and beneficiary assessment, selection and distribution processes.ACTED will coordinate with CWG partners to avoid geographic overlap and beneficiary duplication. Referrals will be sought from local and humanitarian (incl. protection) actors. Selection is based on cumulative vulnerability approach as recommended by CWG and adapted by ACTED. For cases of extreme isolation or specific vulnerabilities, ACTED will refer to projects with in-kind modalities and/or protection or sectoral assistance. ACTED will also facilitate referral of eligible beneficiaries to government safety nets programs. In 2021, ACTED will also provide 2 rounds of 3 months of MPC assistance to 600 individuals. The targeted individuals under this activity will consist of 67% women, 15% pre-pensioners and 50% elderly (mutually non-exclusive categories). The elderly are especially highlighted because of the high number of/potentially overlapping vulnerabilities, they are the majority of the population, and are rarely targeted by other humanitarian or state recovery programs. These grants, aim to support individual’s ability to directly produce food and/or generate income to increase overall food consumption patterns and cover basic food needs. In addition, the recipients of these grants will be referred to trainings under a complementary GAC-funded ACTED program which aims to build capacities to improve long-term sustainability of the activities funded under these grants. ACTED will consult at least 80% of communities targeted. The project and selection criteria will be presented in community meetings to local authorities, community representatives and volunteers to gather and incorporate input and feedback. Communities and individual beneficiaries will be informed about and have access to ACTED’s Complaints and Response Mechanism.Post-Distribution Monitoring will include a beneficiary satisfaction survey to assess whether negative coping strategies have been prevented as well as monitor the use of cashACTED will use financial service providers (FSPs) to transfer MPC. Beneficiaries are invited to choose their preferred FSP, noting that one FSP has a home delivery system (to mitigate protection risks, reach beneficiaries in remote locations, and address particular vulnerabilities such as beneficiaries with limited mobility). Beneficiaries already know and are comfortable with the two FSPs that ACTED works through as they distribute pensions. Needs assessments and market monitoring surveys do not suggest protection/access issues concerning women which need to be mitigated in target areas. Boys and girls are targeted through heads of HHs, who receive the cash: HHs with significant care responsibilities can choose to receive the cash at home.
