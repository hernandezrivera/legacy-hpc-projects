Project Summary

The project aims to provide assistance in Eastern and Western Sudan (Kassala/Gedaref; North Darfur) through provision of WASH, FSL, Protection and Shelter/NFIs, Energy and Environment services for IDPs and vulnerable host community.
Moreover, the project will mainstream protection services to the target population through service mapping and referrals. This project mainly targets IDPs and vulnerable host community members. In North Darfur and in Kassala the project interlinks with a BMZ project to operationalize the Triple NEXUS, strengthening resilience and peaceful co-existence among refugees, IDPs and host communities and while in Red Sea the project interlinks with a SIDA funded project on resilience project. All resilience programming is cross cutting, therefore in most instances actively contribution to life-saving activities e.g., hafir construction. For FSL component targets group focuses on Host Communities and IDPs in the Eastern Sudan and North Darfur.
In total approx. 21,000 beneficiaries will be reached under the FSL component translating to about 11,100 male and 9,900 female beneficiaries.
Small holder pastoralists and agropastoral farmers will be targeted, using different criteria considering vulnerabilities level. Women and youth will be prioritized for activities such as skills development training for access to employment opportunities and micro-finance ventures.

1 - Needs

The number of severely food insecure people in Sudan was estimated at 6 million between October 2021 and February 2022. The number now stands at 12.1 million people , largely due to high food prices and conflict within and between communities . About 14.3 million people-30 percent of the population-will require humanitarian assistance in 2022. This represents an increase of 0.8 million people compared to 2021. Civil unrest, inter-community conflict, higher than average food prices, high inflation, limited local supply of wheat flour, and currency devaluation continue to reduce purchasing power and impede access to food for households in need. In North Darfur, the project's target groups are vulnerable households classified as food-insecure and in IPC Phase 3. Access to food and basic services is disrupted due to ongoing armed conflicts between different tribes. This massively affects household resilience. In Kassala and Gedaref states and on the Red Sea, vulnerable refugees are housed in camps under the supervision of UNHCR and the Commission-er for Refugees (COR). These camps are remote, have temporary shelter structures, and dysfunctional markets, making it difficult to access and connect with key service providers for agricultural inputs and supplies. Competition for limited available resources often leads to conflict with host community members (primarily smallholder farmers), who are also inadequately served by government agencies. They also face rising prices due to the removal of subsidies for fuel and food. At all locations, women and girls are highly exposed to incidents of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Both in the refugee camp around Rakoba and in the host communities, there are unreliable water sources for household use and livestock. Inadequately maintained canals that collect rainwater for food production need to be repaired. Crop failures in most parts of Sudan, such as Darfur and Red Sea, Kassala, and Gedaref states are another factor, putting residents in the IPC3 situation, North Darfur even reaching the emergency state of IPC 4 (IPC, 21.06.2022). The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 estimates that 26% of North darfurains will have a need of immidate assistance for the coming year (HNO, 2022). The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is projected to remain very high at 13.6%. This translates into an increased risk of morbidity and mortality in children under five years of age (OCHA, 2021). Other specific causes of malnutrition in Sudan include deteriorating health and WASH services, suboptimal care and feeding practices for children under five and pregnant and lactating women, the Covid 19 pandemic, recurrent flooding, and ongoing conflict. Accord-ing to UNICEF (2020), malnutrition rates in Sudan have not improved over the past 30 years, and worse, the number of children who are underdeveloped (too small for their age) and "wasted" (emaciated and acutely malnourished) has increased since 1987, especially in the conflict-affected Darfur region and the eastern states of Kassala and Gedaref. In its annual report on nutrition for 2020, UNICEF notes that 3 mil-lion children under the age of five suffered from "wasting" in 2020. Prices for staple foods remained extremely high in the harvest period from October 2021 to April 2022. This is due to seasonally lower market supply, increased demand and high production and transport costs. In addition, the scarcity and high cost of imported wheat is a key factor. Staple food prices are 100 to 200 percent higher than last year and 360 to 430 percent above the five-year average. With about 85% of Sudan's wheat supply coming from imports over the past decade - a significant portion of which came from Russia - the country faces a particularly difficult situation given the current price increases and uncertain market conditions.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will address the needs of refugees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities through a multi-sector response. In the area of WASH the project aims to improve the access to safe and sufficient water supply, through rehabilitation of water sources, such as Hafir and hand pumps. The project also aims at improving access to sanitation facilities, through CLTS approach at HHs level. Hygiene and COVID-19 messaging will be mainstreamed in the project. Wash activities are implemented in North Darfur, Red Sea and Gedaref states. In the area of FSL, the project aims to provide timely food support and enhance the self-reliance of the target group by providing training in agriculture and livelihood activities, seeds and tools distribution and livestock management skills support. The states targeted with FSL assistance are ND, Kas, Ged, RS. In the area of SNFI, the project aims to provide vulnerable host communities and IDPs with essential SNFI support. The response also includes prepositioning of key SNFI items, market assessments as well as sensitization and training for the target group on mitigating the impact of natural disaster to improve preparedness. For this component, ND is targeted. IDPs and host communities alike are targeted for protection sensitization which is very relevant to the context since their localities host refugee populations who feature increased protection vulnerability. North Darfur and will be supported with capacity building (protection, SGBV case identification and referral, complaints mechanisms), linkages to line Ministries and relevant camp actors, and in-kind support (stationaries) for their daily management. For Protection ND, Ged and Kassala are the targeted states

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The intervention area is characterized by resource pressure and degradation of natural resources. Water is a key natural resource which is scarce in all the targeted areas. The project aims to enlarge access to safe drinking water through rehabilitation/construction of water sources and reduce the potential for deforestation through promotion of fuel-efficient/solar stoves among the target groups, as mitigation measures. Through other more resilience interventions, WHH will also aim at increasing locally available seeds through the establishment of communal storages and seed banks as well as communal forests. Those measures/activities applied in other interventions will also benefit the present project

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHH mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet the project minimum requirements. WHH is committed to (Do No Harm) by having regular monitoring visits, follow-up with the targeted groups, focus group discussions and interviews with key informants to monitor the general context during the life of the project implementations, to share the information on project FSL approaches and adjust where it is required as well as the insurance of protection mainstreaming towards the affected population. WHH prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoids causing harm by preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its interventions that can increase vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without discrimination. WHH considers people with specific needs, ensuring access to assistance and services. Through community-based monitoring, target populations will be able to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints, thereby supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the right to shelter, food, water, sanitation, etc. WHH will adhere to Sphere Protection Principles to implement activities under this project according the following: (1) Enhance people’s safety, dignity and rights and avoid exposing them to further harm; (2) Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance, according to need and without discrimination; (3) Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion or deliberate deprivation; and (4) Help people claim their rights. These three principles are interdependent and can be carried out at the same time. The principles promote the right to dignity in three ways: • mainstreaming protection risks, activities and related information across all humanitarian programs. • integrating specific protection objectives into assistance projects; and • promoting specific and/or specialized protection activities that address specific protection risks and violations. In addition, WHH takes care to design program activities that consider findings from a risk analysis. The agency also actively looks for ways to minimize threats and vulnerabilities and work with different groups of the affected population, specific at-risk groups, and the organizations they trust. Such approaches help WHH understand how best to address needs and any positive or negative consequences.
