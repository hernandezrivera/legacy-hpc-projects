Project Summary

UNICEF Nutrition strategic approach is to urgently deliver of lifesaving nutrition assistance in order to avert further deterioration and to address critical needs of boys, girls and women, particularly internally displaced persons (IDP), and host community members in remote areas with limited access in the North West and South West regions.
UNICEF Nutrition planned nutrition response aims at addressing critical gaps in maintaining good coverage of critical lifesaving interventions for treatment of acute malnutrition and prevention of under nutrition in North West and South West Regions. This is in tandem with the with the nutrition cluster response priorities which are (i) treatment of acute malnutrition in children aged 6 -59 months; (ii) Prevention of malnutrition (IYCF-E, deworming, Vitamin A supplementation etc.) ;(iii) Strengthening nutrition surveillance system and (iv) Strengthening coordination and information management.
UNICEF and partners will conduct community screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition and appropriate treatment consistent with the national protocols and guidelines. This will also include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E) as key strategy to support and protect optimal breast feeding in the ongoing response in the NWSW. UNICEF will also support micro-nutrient supplementation especially Vitamin A supplementation with the aim of addressing micro-nutrient deficiencies and this will be provided both routinely and during health campaigns. To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities UNICEF will prioritize capacity building initiatives. To inform evidence-based programming, UNICEF will strengthen the nutrition surveillance system by collating screening data, SAM admission data from the nutrition partners and conduct a rapid nutrition assessment (RNA) to complement the programme data if access and security improves. Furthermore, UNICEF aims at strengthening nutrition cluster coordination and also enhance linkages of nutrition services with health, food security and WASH activities in order to address the underlying factors that contribute to deterioration of the nutrition situation.
The target groups will include boys and girls under-five for community-based screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming. Caregivers for children aged 0 -23 will be targeted for the Promotion and protection feeding practices through sensitization. Health workers and community health workers will be targeted for trainings on SAM management (mobilize, screen and refer acutely malnourished) and IYCF-E.

Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions of the country continues to appear to be worsening with frequency infighting and incidents reported among the non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and the military . 1.3 million people are estimated to be in need of assistance with more than quarter of the population and over 530,000 people are internally displaced. The nutrition results from the emergency food security assessment (EFSA) conducted by WFP in January 2019 revealed a proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 4.4% and 5.6% for the NW and SW regions respectively with further deterioration expected in the coming months. However, high levels stunting and poor Infant and young Children feeding indicators existed prior the crisis with stunting prevalence of 36% (near the emergency threshold) and 28% in NW and SW respectively (MICS 2014). The Pre-existing chronic malnutrition is an evidence of a context of multiple deprivation for girls and boys under the age of five years. Exclusive breast-feeding rates of 42.5% and 58.3% in NW and SW region respectively. The proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at less than 30% per cent. The coverage of Vitamin A supplementation is reported to be at 54% and 73% for NW and SW respectively. The drivers of malnutrition, namely food insecurity, sub-optimal childcare and feeding practices, morbidity, lack of safe water, poor sanitary conditions and repeated internal displacements. Reduced food production, reduced prices in rural areas, increased prices in urban areas (prices are 30 to 50 percent higher) and decline in food consumption as a coping strategy adopted in most households have resulted to substantial deterioration of food insecurity and household livelihoods. This has exposed IDPs and poor host populations in urban areas to poor living conditions and acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC 3) . Poor WASH situation has been reported with over 55% of not having enough water for their needs and more than 40% of the assessed communities practice open defecation . Health service utilization of the functional Health structures is also affected due security incidents and a weak disease surveillance system hence putting the population at risk of epidemics with late detection and limited response capacity. Disease outbreaks mainly measles and AWD/Cholera outbreak have been reported in some pockets (Bakassi and Ekondotiti Health districts) . A combination of food insecurity, AWD/Cholera and measles outbreaks is a serious driver of acute malnutrition and mortality. The coverage of the nutrition interventions and especially SAM treatment in both regions is low and so far, approximately only 17% (415) out of the total estimated SAM caseloads for 2019 (2,500) have accessed treatment for severe acute malnutrition hence a need to scale up and increase coverage of this lifesaving interventions. The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate in the coming months since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months considering deterioration of the drivers to malnutrition.
