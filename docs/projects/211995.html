Project Summary


After 13 years of ongoing and escalating hostilities and economic crisis, the situation in Syria worsened significantly when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria in February 2023. This led to the displacement of hundreds of families and caused severe damage to the infrastructure previously weakened by conflict. Many families lost their primary breadwinners due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, further exacerbating the vulnerability of hundreds of thousands of people who were previously unable to meet their basic needs. The ability to provide essential services continued to erode, with water, sanitation, and public health systems facing immense pressures in a context where there is almost no developmental investment. The food insecurity contribute to increased mortality and morbidity rates, including higher rates of malnutrition and basic humanitarian needs.
Idleb province has transitioned from acceptable levels to severe levels of comprehensive malnutrition. By October 2023, the cost of the food basket had doubled compared to January of the previous year and had quadrupled within two years. The crisis in Idleb creates multiple dimensions of continuous cycles of vulnerability, disproportionately affecting the most vulnerable individuals, including persons with disabilities. The population in the area is living in a state of near-food insecurity, with 90% living below the poverty line. Widespread displacement of the original population, destruction of infrastructure and homes, crop destruction, and damage to agricultural land have increased poverty levels and decreased income. Food indicators have also declined due to economic sanctions imposed on the country, weakening purchasing power and making it more difficult to provide agricultural inputs and food supplies. Therefore, improving livelihoods and food security is of utmost importance for securing a decent life and enhancing the physical and mental health of individuals and communities, especially as this sector faces a range of harsh challenges, starting with the lack of electricity supply, which is as important as the absence of supportive capital for any service to be provided.
Activities:
Providing a grain sieve within the agricultural region (Saree), which suffers from poor production quality control, while providing the solar energy necessary to operate it.
During this activity, 12 workers will be employed (livelihood support) as direct beneficiaries, thus supporting their families’ livelihoods to achieve self-sufficiency.
On the other hand, the project will meet the needs of all farmers in the region to screen grain crops, thus reducing production costs for the farmer and reducing the purchase cost for the consumer.
Protection Risk Analysis:
Exploitation by sieve workers has a high impact; mitigation involves training workers on community protection policies and imposing strict penalties on offenders, including dismissal.
Injury to a worker has a moderate impact; mitigation involves providing a medical kit inside the sieve.
Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Strategy:
Al-Birr and Social Services Association in Idleb adopts a code of conduct and protection policy that all employees and volunteers must adhere to, prohibiting any behavior or action that indicates exploitation, harassment, or sexual abuse and does not tolerate any behavior that may compromise the dignity or privacy of beneficiaries. The association has assistance and protection offices that receive any complaint following confidential procedures ensuring the safety and security of complainants. Training related to protection policies and procedures for individuals and beneficiaries is provided as needed.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigation Measures:
Lack of electricity/high impact/mitigation: Installation of a solar energy system to ensure continuity of work.
Fires/high impact/mitigation: Provision of fire extinguishers.
