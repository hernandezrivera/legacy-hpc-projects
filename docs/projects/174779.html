Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the capacity of very poor households in northern Mali to meet their basic food and nutrition needs by limiting the use of negative coping strategies.
ACTED will provide social net assistance in order to enable the targeted very poor households to meet their basic needs. This assistance may take different forms depending on the security context and the state of markets but will preferably take the form of unconditional cash transfers. In total, 1200 very poor households (about 7200 people) will be targeted and each household will receive 180,000 FCFA/year. This decision is supported by the results of MEB evaluation conducted through the Cash Working Group which established that the monthly food needs per household is 60.000 FCFA (215.83 USD).
Cash distribution methods will respect national programs to align with national social protection policies. Transfers will be made during the lean season depending on the zone (agricultural and pastoral) to enable households to preserve their livelihoods and avoid resorting to harmful survival practices. Considering that women are responsible of subsistence food purchases, schools supplies and health care expenses, the recipients of the transfers will be primarily women from beneficiary households in view of their role in household management. To do so, male heads of household will be sensitized in order to ensure that women are the main recipients of the cash to guarantee its proper use for the benefit of the entire household.
The distributions will be done in three separate transfers at key times in the seasonal calendar (pastoral lean and seedling preparation: April-June; lean: June-September; harvest: September-December). These transfers will allow households to cover basic food needs during key periods and to ensure greater monetary availability within the household. The following effects are expected:
• Households have access to food items during the period that the stocks are at the minimum and prices are increasing ;
• Access better quality food to have a more balanced diet in order to prevent malnutrition ;
• Households debt will be limited during the pre-lean and lean periods and, consequently, will limit pressure to sell crops in order to repay debts after harvest ;
• Facilitate repayment of debts incurred earlier and limit the impact of repayments on household livelihoods;
• Crops storage will be protected by reducing early sales at low price for the repayment of debts.

1 - Contexte

Northern Mali has been facing a complex multidimensional crisis since 2012 that has significantly deteriorated the state of social cohesion and unity of the country. Beyond accentuating important humanitarian needs, the conflict in the northern region between an increasing number of armed groups, militias and national and international armed forces has reinforced ethnic, geographic and political tensions undermining the Malian social fabric.While humanitarian assistance has enabled hundreds of thousands of people to survive, the situation has worsened in certain spheres and, despite some progress in the implementation of the 2015 Algiers Peace Agreement, insecurity and violence have spread from the northern to the central regions, considerably aggravating structural vulnerabilities across half of the country. About 5.1 million people, over 27 per cent of the total population, live in areas affected by insecurity. About 18% of the Malian population is food insecure and this situation is particularly worrying in the Northern regions, strongly affected by the lack of livelihood opportunities and massive internal displacements. From 2015 to 2018, the total number of food-insecure people increased by more than 60% in the Liptako-Gourma region, reaching more than 875,000 people in 2018. Since 2017, the number of security incidents affecting civilians but also increasingly humanitarian workers is on the rise. Humanitarian partners have registered 188 security incidents since January 2020, which severely affects access to remote and crisis-affected communities and prevents the rapid delivery of aid in the North.In conflict-affected areas of the north and especially in Ménaka region, populations are structurally vulnerable to natural disasters (flooding, drought, etc) which affects their access to basic infrastructure and services but also causes destruction of their livelihoods and hinders their recovery. Besides natural disasters, the lack of access to fully functional markets and socio economic services, populations have been strongly affected by the economic, food and nutrition crisis that struck the region over the past years. Moreover, access to markets and goods are severely compromised by insecurity in both regions, and especially in Ménaka which presents the highest Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) in Mali (9,191 FCFA, a 10% rise since march 2017) mainly because of procurement challenges caused by insecurity. Even with the presence of several humanitarian actors in the area, some remote communes still struggle to meet their basic needs and recover from shocks. Projections elaborated by the Harmonized Framework indicate that the population in Ménaka region will most likely be in phase 2 or 3 of the food insecurity scale, between June and August 2021, with 32% of the population (25 322 people) projected to be in phase 2 of food insecurity (stressed) and 10% (7 913) projected to be in phase 3 (crisis). It is also important to keep in mind that projections from the Harmonized Framework have proven to be often too optimistic and needs observed in 2021 might be much higher than the projections presented. This risk has been highlighted by humanitarian actors who anticipate that at least 20% of the populations currently in IPC phase 2 might fall into food insecurity (phases 3 and above). These projections increase the likelihood to resort to negative coping strategies which often result in the loss of livelihoods and the perpetuation of a vicious circle of vulnerability. According to the SMART survey conducted in September 2019, the population of Menaka region is one of the most affected by acute malnutrition with 15,3% of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), 12,0% of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and 2,4% of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in Mali.
