Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria , Muzun is planning to respond in the most vulnerable camps in Idleb based on the severity of WASH needs
The camps that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by conflict and are in need of additional WASH support, based on a rapid muzun assessment in some of the selected camps, consultations with Syrian and international NGOs, and reliable secondary data, including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020. The project is designed to meet specific life-saving needs around water sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and is aligned to HRP 2020 for Syria. The project focuses on WASH response with gender mainstreamed, and with a well-defined response strategy suitable to access situation and security challenges implementation through reliable and experienced site engineers, carefully screened private contractors, and coordination with other actors. The total estimated populated, as per muzun, is over 17395 people. This estimation has to be taken as a rough guide, given that many people have been displaced, with many IDP and returnee fluctuations within Governorates and Districts across Syria.
Target locations have been proposed according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of population in need. They may be altered depending on the evolution of the context on the ground, access and people's movement.
The project aims to support life-saving WASH interventions as well as activities that focus on 1.2. Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems and 1.3. Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems including the provision of WASH materials, equipment and services i.e. water taks, water trucking and emergency WASH facilities.
Muzun planned interventions will be inline with: SO1 that captures efforts sector Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria Of by providing emergency WASH assistance:
1) Provision of temporary water supply to newly displaced and/or highly vulnerable populations living in 7 IDP camps
2) Provision of small water tanks (2 cubic meters) and needed latrine blocks including toilets and baths, as well as, water trafficking for 6,000 people throughout 90 days.
3) MHD will implement solid waste management and waste hole vacuuming in the targeted 7 IDP sites for 8 months.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO, The assessments revealed that, despite overall slight improvement of WASH conditions in Syria, vast .Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites , as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based. and smooth transition into regular programming, in particular in NWS where large scale movement of IDPs is continuously observed. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls.42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, and in extreme case of Idleb up to 85 per cent. 23 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities .According to the needs assessment conducted by the Muzun organization moving further north due to renewed intensive aerial bombardment. Displacement happening in winter months is further exacerbating the vulnerability of people in need.In light of the high population density and the dependence of civilians on alternative sources such as water trucking, which are absolutely unsafe due to the unreliability of water from wells transported by tanks, new concerns arise from the spread of diseases due to the lack of water, especially among children. The most urgent need currently is the safe drinking water, in addition to personal hygiene items, moreover, an emerging need for sanitation facilities due to the escalating numbers of IDPs.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.Adequate quality and quantity of water: Water supply will ensure through water trucking. Muzun will sign an agreement with a contractor to supply water to the targeted camps on daily basis (23 L per person will be provided on daily basis). This water will be sufficient to cover the drinking, bathing and household need of the individuals. If required by the targeted families, MHD will also distribute Jerry cans. This will be contributed by MHD. 2. Water testing and treatment (chlorination):MHD team will conduct water testing and chlorination before it is available for use by the beneficiaries. This will help to eliminate the risk of using contaminated water. 3. Waste removal service (solid waste management): Through a service provider (contractor) MHD will ensure to collect the garbage from the camps and deport it to the agreed damp sites on daily basis. This will improve the overall environment and decrease the risk of diseases spread. MHD will also place garbage bins 4. Operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities: Sanitation facilities (latrines and bathing areas) will be gender segregated and will have protection measures this will be considered during the regular maintenance of the facilities (lockable doors, lighting of paths to facilities). 5. Hygiene awareness activities (such as awareness sessions) will be organized in order to raise awareness of the population of the personal hygiene as well as the importance of keeping environment clean.
