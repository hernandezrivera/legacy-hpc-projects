Project Summary

Over reliance on rain fed urban agriculture has resulted in the increased vulnerability of rural populations to food insecurity brought about by the erratic rainfall patterns. FEWS NET indicates that there was below-average to average national cereal production from the 2017-18 cropping season due to poor rainfall distribution, and a prolonged midseason dry spell in December last year and January this year. Dry conditions persisted in parts of March and April in some southern districts, further stressing crops . Based on the El Niño forecast of October 2018-January 2019 and looking at similar trends in previous years, below-average rainfall and a late start together with erratic rains will lead to crop failure. Violent storms, prolonged dry spells, flash floods, cholera and tropical cyclones cannot be ruled out as the season progresses which will further worsen the current food insecurity crisis. Migratory trans boundary pests and other diseases are expected to continue to be a problem. The fall army worm is expected to continue to remain a threat to crop production in the coming season and other pests including quelea birds, large grain stalk borer, and wild animal attacks on crops, livestock and humans will be a possible threat.
Project Activities
The project seeks to promote sustainable livelihoods and enhance long term coping strategies to drought for people in Masvingo, Matobo and Epworth. It will create short term employment for early economic recovery through cash for work and enterprise development initiatives. Communities will get $100 per person per month after working for 14 days per month. The work that the communities will be engaged in will include rehabilitation/establishment of productive assets such as nutritional gardens, water points, rehabilitation of irrigation schemes, dip tanks and other assets of economic value in the target areas enabling to get cash to meet their household requirements through the cash for work. Boreholes will be rehabilitated and communities will be trained on water, sanitation and hygiene. Affected communities will be trained on business skills development for various sectors which includes but not limited to crop and livestock production with the aim of making markets work for the poor while building resilience to droughts. The project will also promote establishment of off-farm activities for livelihood diversification and reduce over reliance on rainfed agriculture. Communities who will be engaged in off-farm activities and will be supported with vocational skills trainings to further enhance their market competitiveness. The communities will be trained on the internal savings and lending and linked to micro financiers. Startup financial kits will be provided for deserving groups after thorough capacity assessments. Producers will be organized into commodity associations for organized marketing.
