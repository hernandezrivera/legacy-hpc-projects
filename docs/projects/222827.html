Project Summary

Sexual and gender Based Violence prevention is key in the well being of communities and especially women and girls, boys and men who have be through a lot of traumatizing events. South Sudanese population continue to recover from the trauma of conflict, displacement related to climate change crisis and intercommunal clashes that have inflicted trauma and pain to many.
Cases of gender-based violence continue to be reported in the country and thus need to support the communities with initiatives to reduce this vice and empower the communities to be champions of change and reduce cases of violence in the community.
Women for Justice and Equality (WOJE), proposed project will provide comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response interventions to the conflict affected communities in Kapoeta East county and Malakal Counties in Upper Nile state. The proposal will contribute to the overall GBV cluster target reaching to 1000 beneficiaries in Kapoeta East (200 boys; 200 men; 300 women, 300 girls) and 1000 beneficiaries in Malakal (200 boys, 300 girls, 100 men and 400 women) of Upper Nile State.
Women for Justice and Equality WOJE, proposes to target 45% of this population as IDPs, 35% Returnees, and 20% as Host Communities during the implementation of comprehensive lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. This will target population that have been affected by conflict in Sudan, those displaced by flooding and other issues like disease outbreaks.
The proposed project will aim to implement a comprehensive approach to GBV prevention, response, and risk mitigation. It will focus on delivering quality and timely age-appropriate case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. Additionally, the project will extend cash assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls visiting Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) who face financial challenges. This support is intended to facilitate their access to multi-sectoral GBV services, prioritizing survivors in need of clinical management of rape (CMR) and those affected by Intimate Partner Violence (IPV). The initiative aims to ensure continuous access to health facilities, legal aid, and safe spaces providing psychosocial support.
The project's proposed activities will also include Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group, GBV awareness and risk mitigation measures, Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers and strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of Gender Child and Social Welfare. Women for Justice and Equality, WOJE also intends to strengthen gender-based violence prevention through EMAP and community care programs to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
The target counties are in IPC 3 and 4 according to the IPC analysis report November 2024 and severe food insecurity increased the vulnerability to violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. There is need to support the communities with integrated GBV services to the vulnerable and affected populations in the 2 target counties.
WOJE will work very closely with the health partners and other government institutions like courts and the police department to ensure that there are clear referral pathways for those in need of health services and legal aid.

1. Needs

According to a study done by WHO, South Sudan accounts for the second highest prevalence case of GBV in the east African region. According to the WHO in 2018 study, shows South Sudan is the second after Uganda reported for high Intimate Partner violence (IPV) (41%, physical and/or sexual. Another study conducted by SUDD institute, the prevalence of IPV among married women aged 15-49 is rising (standing at 49.6%, physical and/or sexual). According to the study, substantial proportions of women (aged 15-64) in South Sudan experience GBV either in form of physical (34.0%) or sexual (13.5%) violence in their lifetime. This is even worse for women and children who have been displaced by conflict and and climate shocks like flooding and draught. The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs and response plan 2024 HNRP estimates that 9 million people will be in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan in 2023. Of these, 24% are women, 54% are children and 15% are people with disabilities. According to the report, out of the 2.8 million people who need protection assistance related to gender-based violence (GBV), 33% are in a serious situation and 22% are at the extreme level while 14% are in catastrophic levels of severity. The GBViMS statistics (2023) reports that there has been an increase in GBV cases reported in the second half of 2023 compared to the same period in 2022. The situation is not expected to improve in 2024 as 97% of reported cases in South Sudan affected women and girls, and 21 per cent of survivors were children, of which 79 per cent were adolescent girls. Physical violence, commonly by an intimate partner or someone known by the survivor, continues to be the most common form of GBV, accounting for 42 per cent of reported cases according to the assessment Report conducted by Nonviolence Peaceforce in 2023. South Sudan continues to receive returnees who have been traumatised by waves of violence in Sudan and are in need of support and interventions to heal from the violence they faced in the conflict situations. They are also confined in displaced persons camps that also increases their vulnerability to more violence and sexual abuse. Accodring to IOM DTM data, over 800000 people had crossed to South Sudan from the conflict out of which over 70% are south Sudanese returnees. Malaka county is one of the destinations for the returnees and is hosting a number of the vulnerable populations and therefore thereThis desperate situation has increased the GBV needs in the proposed counties which remain severe as violence and violations persist as a reality of daily life for girls, women and children. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions.Access to GBV services for the targeted locations remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of GBV prevention and response services. The prevalence of gender-based violence (GBV) in Bentiu and Malakal is deeply entrenched in cultural beliefs, attitudes, and perceptions that perpetuate an unequal power dynamic between men and women. These cultural factors contribute significantly to the normalization of violence against women and girls within the agro-pastoral communities. Moreover, survivors face a considerable barrier in accessing available services due to the pervasive fear of retaliation through revenge killings. Women For Justice and Equality, WOJE will aims to address and transform these cultural norms and beliefs to mitigate the risk factors associated with GBV in Kapoeta East and Malakal counties improving access to integrated GBV services to the affected populations.

2. Project activities

1. Strengthen Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group 2. Strengthening GBV awareness and risk mitigation to the communities affected by conflict and effects of climate change. 3. Strengthening Engagement of Men in Accountable Practices EMAP and ensure that they are champions of reduction of gender based violence in the communities.4. Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions in the target counties and strengthen the local communities and leaders to champion practices that prevent gender based violence.5. Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation. This will ensure that women and girls are safe especially during dark hours and have safe menstrual management commodities that often offer protection threats.6. GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers.7. Construction of WGFS using acceptable standards and ensure that they are disability inclusive and accessible to all including persons with disabilities.8. Strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways and Support women and girls through economic empowerment in cash assistance to initiate small scale business in Kapoeta East and Malakal. 9. Work closely with other actors especially WASH to support integration of protection in their programming to carry out activities that reduce gender based violence like safety and disability audits.
