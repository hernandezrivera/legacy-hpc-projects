Project Summary

Across Central Equatoria State and the Country of South Sudan as a whole, girls and boys and their families have been facing risks associated with conflict, violence and displacement including family separation, Sexual Gender-based Violence (SGBV), mine risk, psychological distress, and child labor for the last three years consecutively.
Pilgrims of Hope will ensure the provision of timely and rights-based child protection services to the most deprived and marginalized girls and boys especially those who are now on the street of Juba City, especially those affected by conflict and displaced from their homes from other States. These services will include prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV); material support including food and clothing, weekly cleaning, washing and bathing services, vocational training, and life skills training for extremely vulnerable children and adolescents affected by conflict; creating safe places for play, recreational activities and the provision of psychosocial support to children displaced or affected by conflict.
To prevent and mitigate child protection risks in communities within Juba county, those affected by conflict and displacement and are staying in camp setting like Mangala IDPs, POH will strengthen the capacity of social workers, law-enforcement staff, as well as investing in capacity building of the Central Equatoria Child Protection Working Group (CECPWG) on Child Welfare and other humanitarian Child support and care, and reinforcing the resilience of communities and strengthening the capacities of child protection institutions within the state. Supporting community based child protection mechanisms and civil society organizations is also critical to disseminate information on relevant services, contributing to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases; promoting safer environments for children especially those on the street are priorities.

1 - Needs

With the increased armed conflict related displacements that has taken tall in the last three years of 2018, 2019, and 2020, violence against civilians in four Counties namely, Terekeka, Lainya, Yei and Juba has risen. Like in Juba county, inter-communal conflicts between farming communities and nomadic-pastoral communities of Terekeka and Juba counties and from other neighboring states including Jonglei, Warrap and Lakes over access to, use of, and management of resources continue to cause insecurity resulting in new displacements as killings, displacements, rape and sexual abuse. Children affected by fighting, particularly those displaced, remain exposed to various protection risks such as exposure to violence, family separation, deteriorating living conditions and recruitment by armed actors among many child abuse. These risks have a devastating impact on their well-being, physical security and future prospects with girls often bearing the heaviest brunt of the conflict, and more at risk of specific risks such as sexual violence. Displaced populations, whether displaced by conflict or natural disaster like those with floods in Mangala IDPs camp, often are forced to flee with little or no belongings, and face challenges in accessing life-saving and life-sustaining services, whilst often suffering from additional protection risks. This is compounded by limited access to income opportunities and prevalence of child labor, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as SGBV risks. With the overall security situation in these locations not significantly improving despite significant agreement on the safe returns of pastoral communities back to their original, inter-communal conflicts between farming communities and nomadic-pastoral communities over access to, use of, and management of resources, especially land continue, to cause insecurity resulting in new displacements on and off. This coupled with limited economic opportunities for those in the camp in Mangalla, has increased the strain on the available resources to cater for the host, IDPs and the returnees in the State, Central Equatoria. In addition to the protracted conflict within the state, the whole country, South Sudan has been experiencing an influx of refugees from neighboring countries, and People’s physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. And such indicates decreased capacity of the host communities to support the population needs including the much-needed protection services as most of them prefer stopping at Juba before proceeding to the states.

2 - Project activities

1. Provision of material support (based on the need) to girls and boys identified with protection concerns. Referral of girls and boys with identified needs to medical, legal support and other services. 2. Provision of psycho-social support to girls and boys identified with protection concerns or psycho-social distress through regular age-appropriate and gender-sensitive psycho-social activities. Referral of girls and boys to specialized MHPSS support and formal counseling services. 3. Training of social workers (from government and other agencies) and interim caregivers on case management, and CP information management system (CPIMS). 5. Support to the establishment and functioning of Community-based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs) including training of members of the CBCPNs on how to prevent, identify and support child survivors of violence and abuse especially to those at the street in Juba, and on referral mechanisms. 6. Training and capacity building support to social workers, law-enforcement staff and linking with health-service providers on child rights, child protection, child safeguarding, and child-appropriate response to SGBV cases. 7. Strengthening of the reporting and referral mechanism that is safe and confidential for girls and boys who experienced sexual violence. Provision of gender-sensitive and age-appropriate support, care services and life skills training to girls and boys SGBV survivors. 8. Child Protection and SGBV community outreach activities including on the Juba streets. 9. Support the establishment and operation of Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) to work as a safe space for provision of structured MHPSS and recreational activities.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Yes. POH will establish referral mechanisms and GBV Help desks for case management in the locations inhabited by refugees, returnees and IDPs. Children identified to be most vulnerable (through vulnerability criteria) and require further case management support will be referred to agencies offering specialized services. Members of community based child protection network, GBV networks will be trained/equipped with CPiE skills, including life skills, self-protection skills, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) strategies, better parenting skills to support children within their communities build resilience. The CFS established will act as strong referral hubs. Adolescents, young children boys and girls, and including community networks will be strengthened and encouraged to mainstream child protection in their daily activities especially those on the street in Juba. POH will continue to build the capacity of the State Ministry’s Social workers, including the existing systems/structures, such as schools and local leaders within Juba, to support child wellbeing in the county targeting IDPs, refugees, returnees and host communities of Juba County. The community volunteers are also given hands on trainings within their various points so as to ensure they are able to sustain the project post funding. At the same time, POH has been recruiting project staff from the implementation areas to ensure that the communities have their own resource persons. A special consideration will be given to the most vulnerable Children who are now on the street i.e. the disable, the girls especially the ones living with the community and are being used to bread win for the families where they stay. Joint monitoring visits bringing together GBVWG will be done on quarterly basis to project locations to increase interactions with beneficiaries, recording the most significant changes and use the feedback to modify sustainable strategy.
