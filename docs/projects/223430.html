Project Summary

Mozambique is regularly impacted by cyclones, especially in the low-lying coastal areas that are home to a majority of the population. Since 2017 alone, some 5 million people have been affected, mostly in the provinces of Zambezia, Nampula and Sofala. Moreover, cyclones are the natural hazard with the greatest attributable economic losses. Tropical Cyclone Idai, which hit Mozambique in 2019, is one of the two most costly events (estimated at US$ 1.96 billion) in Africa over the past 50 years. The annual cyclone season in Mozambique usually lasts from November until March. However, events may also occur outside of this period. Historical data from the National Institute for Disaster Management and Risk Reduction (INGD) shows that the impact of cyclones in Mozambique has varied, affecting between 73,000 people (Cyclone Chalane in 2020) and 1.5 million people (Cyclone Idai in 2019). Women and girls were among the most vulnerable groups during and after the disaster. They faced increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV), displacement, and loss of livelihoods. Women, particularly in rural areas, were also heavily affected due to challenges in accessing relief and recovery resources. In 2023, Tropical Cyclone Freddy hit Mozambique twice, causing destructive winds, extreme rainfall, and widespread flooding. In 2024, Cyclone Chido has affected over 450,000 people in Cabo Delgado and Nampula. Despite accurate early warnings and coordinated disaster management, significant damage occurred, affecting infrastructure, displacing populations, and disrupting essential services. Roads were cut off, hindering social services, trade and provision of healthcare thus worsening the cholera outbreak that had started in September 2022. The cyclones that hit the Mozambican coast are increasing in number and intensity . Since 2000, Mozambique has been impacted by a major tropical cyclone every other year on average

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique is a country highly exposed to the impacts of climate change. The extreme events that Mozambique is exposed to include drought, floods, cyclones, extreme wind and rainfall, among others. Women and girls were among the most vulnerable groups during and after the disaster. They faced increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV), displacement, and loss of livelihoods. Women, particularly in rural areas, were also heavily affected due to challenges in accessing relief and recovery resources

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The anticipatory activities will be implemented in 10 priority districts in Nampula, Zambezia, Sofala and Inhambane provinces, in line with the national standard operating procedures. These districts were identified by the national technical working group on AA based on an analysis of historical exposure to cyclones and of vulnerability. The observational trigger based on windspeed will enable early response to be implemented in affected districts in the following provinces: Cabo Delgado, Nampula, Zambezia, Sofala, Inhambane, and Gaza. The RC/HC may decide to expand the geographical scope of early response activities based on the observed impact, including cyclone-induced flooding. All partners have confirmed their operational response capacity.Partners will jointly target temporary accommodation centers identified together with the government. Agencies will collaborate to identify households, including women-headed households receiving joint assistance as a preparedness activity. After cyclone landfall, partners will coordinate their activities, including targeting, through regular government, civil society organizations and OCHA-facilitated coordination mechanisms

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

OCHA and humanitarian partners has been placing women, adolescent girls and children in the communities at the center of the response, in accordance with collective commitments on Accountability to Affected People (AAP) and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). This effort contributes to the implementation of the IASC GEEWG policy and its accountability framework and will follow the guidance provided at the IASC Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action to integrate gender equality programming in humanitarian action. This includes ensuring effective, inclusive, adapted and transparent communication to enable informed decisions by affected communities; using feed back mechanisms to strengthen accountability and inform adjustments in the response; and providing meaningful opportunities for meaningful community participation in humanitarian action. The Linha Verde hotline, run by WFP on behalf of the humanitarian community, has been available as an inter-agency complaint and feedback mechanism ,including on sensitive issues. The Mozambique PSEA Network has been serving as the primary body for coordination and oversight of activities related to PSEA mainstreaming by humanitarian personnel and will provide technical support to the clusters, agencies and partners involved in the response.
