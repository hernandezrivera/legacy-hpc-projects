Project Summary

Following over a decade of conflict and an enduring economic downturn, the plight of Syrians further deteriorated after devastating earthquakes struck northern Syria and Türkiye on February 6 2023. These calamities have exacerbated the already dire housing crisis across the country, particularly highlighted in Latakia, where the February earthquakes left a trail of destruction, rendering many homes uninhabitable and forcing families into displacement. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2024, an estimated 6.2 million people in Syria are in urgent need of shelter, with many residing in conditions deemed unfit for habitation.
In response, initiatives are underway to rehabilitate damaged housing in jableh city and their countryside, 185 HHs will benefit from this project by rehabilitation damaged housing focusing on restoring essential living spaces including bedrooms, bathrooms, and vital infrastructure such as plumbing, electrical systems, doors, and windows. These efforts are critical in providing displaced and affected families with safe and dignified living conditions amidst the ongoing challenges.
In Jableh, a city in the Latakia governorate of Syria, the earthquake that struck in early February 2023 had a catastrophic impact on housing. The tremor's immense force caused the collapse and severe damage of numerous residential buildings, rendering many homes uninhabitable. This destruction displaced thousands of residents, forcing them to seek temporary shelters or live in precarious conditions amid the ruins. . The reconstruction of damaged housing in Jableh and the broader affected area became a critical need, calling for substantial international aid and support to address the urgent housing crisis and facilitate the community's recovery.
