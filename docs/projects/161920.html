Project Summary

Firstly, Tdh aims at improving the protection of children and their caregivers through access to case management (CM) services, psychosocial support (PSS) and community based alternative care support. In addition, Tdh will implement protection promotion activities through the production and dissemination of a research paper on adapted services for boy survivors of sexual violence. Within the primary health centre ran by Tdh in camp 26, that is opened 24/7 and benefits to host communities and refugees, Tdh implemented a Child Play area for children, who benefit from recreational activities and PSS. In 2020, Tdh aims at continuing running this approach in the PHC, in integration with Health and WASH.
Secondly, Tdh will emphasis on reaching women and girls in host communities, both in Child Protection and in WASH, as it is the population group the hardest to reach but most vulnerable. By enhancing the learning and living environment for more protective, safer and cleaner schools and houses, Tdh aims at reducing the violence against children and at reducing the risks of WASH-related diseases. Particularly, as part of Tdh’s community mobilization and behaviour change approach, Tdh will emphasis on sensitization, awareness rising and ownership of the community. Tdh is targeting in 2020 three unions in the surroundings of Teknaf: Nhilla, Sabrang and Teknaf, for this complementary approach in schools.

Needs

Needs for refugees: More than two years into one of the largest and ‘fastest growing’ refugee crisis in the world, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection and solutions crisis at its core. Since August 2017 approximately 744,000 Rohingya sought asylum in Bangladesh (of which some 1,132 arrivedin 2019); joining approximately 170,000 who sought refuge prior to the 2017 influx. Psychological distress as a result of traumatic events experienced before and during forced displacement from Myanmar have been further exacerbated due to the harsh living conditions, additional protection risks and solutions uncertainties to which Rohingya refugees have been exposed during their displacement in Bangladesh.The lack of formal status attributed to refugees, inadequate access to livelihoods and education, as well as movement restrictions, access to civil documentation, and formal justice mechanisms continue to impact negatively on the protection environment, contributing to harmful coping mechanisms, including incurring mounting debts, exposure to trafficking and dangerous onward movements, and exacerbating child protection and gender-based violence issues. An unstable security environment in and around camps in 2019 impacted negatively on the physical and psychological well-being of both refugees and host communities (in particular for women), limiting access to public spaces and thus hindering effective access to basic services. It is within this context that social cohesion has deteriorated, exposing communities to safety and security risks.Finally, with one third of the population exhibiting particular vulnerabilities, tailored community-based and individualized protection responses are required to ensure access to basic services for persons with specific needs. In particular, those with mobility restrictions caused by socio-cultural, gender identity/sexual orientation, or physical limitations, such as the elderly, persons with disabilities, and female headed households face heightened protection risks.Needs for the host communities: Like refugees, host communities living in Cox’s Bazar District, especially those residing in in close proximity to refugee camps; in particular the rural less-developed Unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas, have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services (especially Healthcare and Education) and inadequate infrastructure and markets. The Rohingya influx has inevitably had a significant impact on competition for limited existing resources, access to land, and the environment in host communities as well as inter-community relations. Moreover, an unstable security environment has resulted in criminal elements exploiting communities, while existing bottlenecks in the formal and informal justice system limit access to legal remedies. The abovementioned factors are all contributing to heightened humanitarian and protection needs of particularly vulnerable segments of host communities. Based on assessment findings, host communities are particularly concerned with a perceived deterioration of the security environment, attributed to the presence of criminal groups engaged in violence, harassment, and abductions for ransom; leading to barriers in accessing basic services. Particularly vulnerable host community families are adopting negative coping mechanisms, including incurring mounting debts to pay for urgent medical treatment, engaging in child labor and child marriage, and resorting to smuggling and trafficking networks. Host communities perceive their urgent needs to have been overlooked by humanitarian actors and in parallel have very limited knowledge of community-based self-protection mechanisms.
