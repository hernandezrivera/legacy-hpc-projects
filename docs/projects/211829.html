Project Summary

Needs: In 2023, CARE delivered GBV case management services through integrated approach with SRH, supporting a total of 891 survivors across NWS. The breakdown of CARE's GBV case management caseload shed light on various types of GBV incidents. Specifically, 42% of cases reported physical assault, 19% indicated psychological/emotional abuse,20% reported denial of resources, while sexual assault cases comprised 7% of the total reached, which align with VOICES from Syria 2023 report when findings indicated that women and girls across Syria experienced a range of abuses, including physical violence, psychological and emotional violence. They faced sexual violence, including assault, exploitation, harassment, and rape, as well as technology-facilitated violence and social violence, such as stigma and discrimination. Adolescent girls subjected to early marriage, and denied rights, resources, opportunities, and services specifically education. Furthermore, 64% of perpetrators were identified as intimate partners according to CARE GBV CM tracker, while 24% were identified as family members, which suggests the presence of entrenched patterns of violence within domestic relationships. CARE efforts to address GBV will continue to include tailored support services addressing the specific needs of survivors and interventions aimed at GBV prevention and changing harmful gender norms and promoting healthy, respectful relationships.
Intervention, Implementation of activities: CARE will continue providing protection and GBV services within 3 static community centers,5 WGSSs, GBV teams included in 5health facilities, 2 helpdesks, and one mobile WGSS and 14 outreach units. CARE utilizes different approaches through static and mobile services to reach to the most vulnerable people including GBV survivors, women and girls at risks, PWDs, and people living in camps including windows camps. CARE will ensure integration between the GBV and SRH services in addition to the MHPSS and legal counselling. Additionally, services will include individual and group PSS counselling, legal counselling, GBV response services (GBV Case Management/CVA and referrals, PSS for GBV survivors), GBV risk mitigation and prevention services including safety audits, training for Non GBV actors on GBV, structured awareness raising on GBV, , skills development trainings, life skills targeting women and adolescents, engaging men and boys through information sharing and tailored PSS and recreational activities.
Protection Risk Analysis: CARE is committed to establishing internal referral system between health and GBV teams, accompanied by training and supervision for both teams to ensure that GBV survivors accessing services within health facilities encounter no risks of stigmatization. CARE will engage with local authorities and introduce project services and selection criteria to ensure community acceptance and avoid any backlash from local communities towards the project. Moreover, some of the project GBV activities will engage different groups such as community leaders, men, and adolescent boys to ensure strong allies for girls and women rights. CARE will ensure a solid and comprehensive cash risk assessment before deciding the CVA modality in GBV CM, and increase collaboration with other sectors including cash, shelter, basic needs, and livelihood to avoid additional harm to the GBV survivor from the community and family members upon CVA distribution.
PSEA strategy: CARE is actively working to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation, abuse, and child abuse. Any reports or allegations are handled with the utmost gravity, as CARE adopts a survivor-centered approach that places paramount importance on the needs and dignity of survivors. Ongoing staff training, community-level awareness campaigns on PSEA and safeguarding, as well as maintaining accessible channels for feedback and complaints, are among the measures to be consistently upheld through implementation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

More info about Intervention, Implementation of activities: The protection response activities include providing legal counselling, basically for GBV survivors, and to other individuals who experience legal protection concerns. In addition, CARE will provide PSS individual and group counselling. The GBV response activities include GBV CM (including CVA for survivors), individual PSS sessions, referral, PFA. Whereas the GBV prevention and women empowerment activities include awareness raising on GBV (adopting the GBV SC Awareness Toolkit, Engaging Men and Boys manual, and Tipping Point manual), PSS group activities to GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV (life skills and vocational training and play based PSS), and supporting women led networks. GBV risk mitigation activities will include conducting GBV safety audit and training to non-GBV humanitarian actors on GBV concepts.Partnership with local experienced NGOs in northwest Syria is key for CARE and has been instrumental in the successful implementation of the protection project activities with technical and administrative support is provided from CARE to its partners to ensure quality implementation and services. CARE developed and will utilize a GBV CM supervision and coaching guidance to its staff and local partners to improve GBV case management quality by setting clear processes for supervision and coaching between GBV caseworkers and their supervisors or by improving the current practices with additional methods and tools in line with the IASC and GBV SC SOPs and guidelines utilizing the M&E toolkit.CARE monitoring approach includes independent verification visits, contracting with TPM companies, developing and localizing global MEL tools, and contracting with technical field consultants who support the frontline teams' technical capacity. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating measures: CARE prioritizes security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments, acknowledging that complete risk mitigation is impossible. CARE focuses on developing robust systems, identifying emerging risks, and collaborating with the UN and donors to enhance community-wide responses. Project activities are directly monitored at various levels, but the consent agreement since Sep 2023 impacts program continuity and essential services provided by UN agencies. CARE has drafted a contingency plan detailing planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to adapt programming. Displacement remains a concern, prompting outreach-focused protection activities to address population movements. Contingency plans are in place to manage significant outbreaks in NWS while safeguarding field staff. Child safeguarding and staff code of conduct are central standards in CARE's operations, with dedicated staff ensuring safe programming for adults and minors.
