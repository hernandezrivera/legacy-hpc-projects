Project Summary

Since the beginning of 2015, the north-east of Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violation of the human rights resulting into forms of Gender Based Violence (GBV). More than 1.9 million individuals have been displaced due to the conflict and displacement continues to be a significant factor in 2018, with the highest number of displacements and returns taking place in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe State ((IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 23). Despite the fact that some displaced individuals have started to return home after relative security has been restored and more areas are now accessible to humanitarian actors, the situation on the ground remains dire and several remain in inaccessible areas. Through this twelve-month project under the HRP, IOM will contribute to protecting and promoting the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) wellbeing of conflict affected people in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states, by: a) supporting existing MHPSS mobile teams (direct MHPSS and protection service provision to affected population through MHPSS teams and safe spaces/resource centers); b) carrying out capacity building initiatives for protection and MHPSS service providers working with affected populations (IDPs and hosting communities) and supporting national institutions’ capacities; and c) Mainstreaming MHPSS, protection including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), counter-trafficking (CT) and GBV risk mitigation into humanitarian response sector activities.
The project aims to protect and promote conflict affected populations’ mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. In particular, and in line with Nigeria’s Protection Sector’s objectives, the project will ensure the provision of targeted protection services through the provision of tailored MHPSS services. In addition, the project will contribute to the mainstreaming of protection and MHPSS approach through other sectors. With the overall aim of helping ensure programming in North-East Nigeria adheres to the diversity of protection needs of the affected population during the implementation of services, IOM will strengthen the capacity of humanitarian workers to mainstream protection principles in their programming. This will be carried out with the overall objective to improve the health, security/safety, privacy and dignity of conflict-affected populations in displacement settings and in areas of return. With the objective and the centrality of protection in mind, IOM will seek to target humanitarian actors who deliver services in both displacement sites and areas of return. Services in camp and camp-like settings and areas of return act in accord, whereas the improved resilience is essential to adapt to new situations and challenges associated with transition i.e. transitioning from a situation of displacement to re/integration in areas of return. Promotion of protection sensitive programming in camp and camp-like settings should seek to enhance the sustainability and impact of initiatives in areas of return, while they together promote coherent service provision and the sustainability of transition-oriented objectives. IOM’s capacity building activities will include training relevant stakeholders, partners and institutions involved in the field of mental health and psychosocial support. IOM will work closely with the national institutions, through its role as co-chair the MHPSS working group; working with the Borno state Ministry of Health as chair of the working group.

Needs

The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. In the three-worst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9 million people. Over 80 per cent of IDPs are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis, and over 60 per cent are living among host communities while other IDPs are living in formal and informal camps, collective settlement centres, and transitional centres and in host communities(IOM DTM Round 23). The insurgency has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, the destruction of health facilities, security, dysfunctional legal and justice systems amidst increasing safety and security concerns. Termed a protection crisis, human rights violations including GBV occurring within IDP camps and other settlements are widespread. Insecurity, triggered by the insurgency and military operations against NSAGs, has increased the number of displaced persons with an estimated 130,000 new arrivals in various locations since the end of October 2017. These movements present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources in arrival locations. The ensuing insecurity continues to compromise safety, propagate a lack of access to essential services for the communities and hinder the capacity for humanitarians to provide much-needed lifesaving support; hence the difficulty in humanitarian aid delivery. The insurgency exacerbates challenges related to the protection of women and girls. Furthermore, as the context continues to evolve, the recent past has witnessed a significant threat of evolution with an upsurge in the instrumental use of women as perpetrators on the one hand and their position as survivors of GBV, abductions and detentions on the other. The resultant direct and indirect consequences are kidnapping, early forced marriage, rape, sexual harassment and extra judicial killings. These actions indirectly lead to reduced access to education in general and bare even worse implications for girls, already subject to minimal participation in the region. Women and girls endure negative impacts on reproductive, psychosocial as well as their general health. This group is exposed to an increased vulnerability to sexually transmitted diseases, unsafe abortions and reproductive health challenges. Human as well as economic and social rights of women and girls are frequently violated, and there is an increased pressure on women to act as heads of households amidst high levels of poverty for these individuals in particular. An estimated 3.0 million people need protection from GBV across the conflict affected states; of these 1.1 million are IDPs, 1.0 million are returnees. 920,000 care givers (of whom 80% are women) in the three most affected States are in vulnerable conditions. Widows, women headed households, single caregivers with several children, and foster parents are considered particularly vulnerable. In addition, assessments and recent interventions have shown that conflict affected people have a heightened vulnerability to trafficking in persons. Affected people face additional stressors created by the displacement itself, such as family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. Exposure to GBV risks also includes exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse. Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments by armed actors, arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms of violence. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survival sex. Thus, need for provision of continuous psychosocial support, engagement of women and girls in livelihood activities, encourage women participation in decision making and ensure we mitigate all risks that might predispose women and girls to GBV.

Activities

IOM will carry out the following activities under this project: Activity 1: Support the existing deployment of (select and deploy) 120 psychosocial support mobile team members, including to newly accessible areas and areas of return in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa state. Activity 2: Provide culturally appropriate structured recreational activities for adults, teenagers and children as well as informal education for adults and youths through psychosocial support mobile teams. Activities will be tailored according the age and gender categories. Activity 3: Provide focused psychosocial support services through lay counseling and support groups provided by trained psychosocial support mobile team members. The focused services will be tailored on an individual or problem based basis, taking into account age and gender categories. Activity 4: Organize focus group discussions and safety audits to identify protection risks and refer to other relevant sectors Activity 5: Support affected populations with increased access to specialized services and follow up for individuals suffering from mental disorders (through supporting referrals and treatment, through the deployment of governmental mental health care workers and capacity building and resources for governmental facilities Activity 6: Provide monthly training and weekly supervision for the psychosocial support mobile teams. Activity 7: Provide training on MHPSS concepts for relevant stakeholders and partners involved in the field of mental health and psychosocial support. Strengthening institutional capacities in MHPSS response and coordination (capacity building activities with the University of Maiduguri). Activity 8: Provision of re/integration assistance through vocational training and small-scale livelihoods activities integrated with MHPSS. Vocational training and livelihood activities will target vulnerable protection and MHPSS beneficiaries, as well as survivors of GBV, trafficking in persons and sexual exploitation and abuse. Adopting a do no harm approach, and to avoid a possible identification and stigmatization of beneficiaries, livelihood beneficiaries will also include non-extremely vulnerable persons both the IDP and host community population. Activity 9: Mainstreaming Protection and MHPSS approach intervention (through trainings and technical guidance) targeting various sectors of the humanitarian response. Provision of technical guidance to CCCM, NFI/shelter, site planning, WASH in integrating GBV risk mitigation. Activity 10: Provision of an integrated Counter Trafficking in Crisis response (Strengthening institutional capacities). This will include awareness raising targeting conflict affected population, provision of direct victim assistance through National Agency for the Prosecution of Trafficking in Persons, capacity building of relevant actors. Activity 11: PSEA sensitization and trainings for relevant actors. Activity 12: Streamlining existing referral mechanisms across units for better coordination, monitoring, and follow up for efficient referrals for child protection, GBV, health, and lifesaving concerns of the affected populations. This includes support to life saving and emergency health referrals.
