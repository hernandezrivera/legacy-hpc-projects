Needs

Refugees have little to no access to income as livelihoods interventions are largely prohibited, leading to overwhelming idleness, lack of opportunities for self-sufficiency, and lack of resilience to shocks. Refugees lack income to meet basic needs, remaining largely dependent on food distributions without many options for diversifying or increasing food intake. The host community is suffering from reduced income and livelihood opportunities due to the refugee crisis. The large influx of refugees has driven down the wages for day labor, which is a major source of income in Cox’s Bazar. Cox’s Bazar was already one of the poorest districts in Bangladesh, which nearly a third of the population living below the poverty line and nearly a quarter unemployed. As a result, both communities are resorting to negative coping mechanisms to meet basic needs. Idleness, a lack of opportunities, lack of hope for the future can breed resentment within and between refugee and host communities who are fighting for scarce resources, leading to problems with social cohesion that can only grow with time if there is no outlet. Cash programming is also needed as a modality for delivering aid that allows people to meet their exact needs with dignity, and avoid wasted in-kind aid. Cash could also be critical as a fast and easy way to put aid in the hands of a large number of people in the aftermath of a natural disaster.

Activities or outputs

IRC planning to enter the livelihoods sector in the coming year to address the needs of host community and refugee youth and adults (men and women) with basic livelihoods programming that meets sector standards. IRC would conduct market assessments and consult with communities in finalizing exact livelihoods activities to meet community and market demands, and the needs of specific persons. IRC works in a highly integrated manner and livelihoods work would be integrated with planned and ongoing protection, GBV, and education programming to comprehensively meet beneficiary needs. IRC will launch a complaints response mechanism in the first half of 2019, to ensure accountability across all programming. IRC is relatively new to Bangladesh and is still building capacity to evaluate and work with partners; we plan to do a partner mapping exercise in the first quarter of 2019 (under funding already secured) to select potential partners for 2019. Once selected, IRC would work through partners to either build organizational capacity or technical capacity or both, depending on need. IRC has conducted a strategic planning exercise for 2019-2020 that included this programming; these plans were presented to the RRRC for comment and endorsement. "> All of the following activities are not stand alone, but rather integrated into larger health, protection and GBV programming. Exact support for income generation activities may evolve throughout the year depending on the demands of the current cohort of beneficiaries. - IRC will launch basic Cash for Work programming in the form of construction of new structures and centers in the camps; payments to community health volunteers, who support IRC by conducting messaging about available services and public health issues; and kitchen gardens to support beneficiaries of IRC’s GBV programming. - Small business start-up support: IRC will support beneficiaries identified through our protection programming to provide business and financial literacy training, conditional cash grants and/or in-kind support to small business start up. The type of support will be targeted to the capacity and needs of the particular beneficiary.>Cash to meet basic needs in emergency. As part of IRC’s emergency preparedness and response, we will conduct a basic cash readiness assessment and put in place internal structures in our support team to manage cash distribution at scale in the event of an emergency. This could include cash in hand distributions or SIM-based delivery systems, depending on what is allowed by the government and preferred by refugees.
