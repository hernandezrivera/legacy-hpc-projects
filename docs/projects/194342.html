Project Summary

Sudan faces unstable political situation and a serious financial constraints. This issue along with the widespread conflicts and natural disasters put a serious risk on the vulnerable communities particularly people who live with poor infrastructure services and vulnerable settlement including houses and assets. Besides, lack of the resources within the houses and settlement institutions limit the opportunities to provide safe and adequate houses for most vulnerable areas. Therefore, This project initiated to support shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) cluster objectives and sector strategic goals aiming to contribute in providing timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance. The purpose of this project is to ensure that vulnerable communities that affected by crisis have access to basic domestic needs and obtain a creative and more practical shelter solutions. The provision of timely shelter and NFIs for crisis affected people will allow newly and, protracted IDPs, spontaneous returnees and other hazard affected populations to restore their dignity and reduce exposure to potential risks associated with protection and health. The project will focus on the vulnerable groups including pregnant women, chronically ill people, elderly, unaccompanied minors, and people with disabilities. Similarly, the project aims to support resilience of returnees and integration of IDPs through early recovery activities by supporting shelter solutions and the transition from dependency to self-reliance.
The project will target 100,927 (24,707 girls, 23,738 boys, 27,291 women, and 25,191 men)
in 19 localities. These localities have a high severity ranking of people in needs which distributed across 6 states as follow: North Darfur, Central Darfur, West Darfur, South Kordofan, Aj Jazirah and Khartoum.
The project will adopt practical solutions approach in order to provide protection from life threatening diseases and restore dignity to all persons of concern. This will be achieved by ensuring IDPs, returnees and vulnerable communities have access to basic domestic items and several shelter solutions (emergency and transitional) that provide privacy, security, protection from the elements, and a space to live and store belongings in a dignified manner. The project will support provision of NFI assistance and shelter solutions using several modalities including exploring Cash-based Interventions such as cash to support construction of new shelters or improvement and or rehabilitation of exiting shelters. Also, the project will focus on supporting transition, recovery and reintegration of returnees, vulnerable and protracted households and IDPs through provision of appropriate tools and/or
environmentally friendly transitional shelters and provision of shelter construction materials for self-building or supported with Labor costs for vulnerable households with lack of capacity to implement. Additionally, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable communities will receive essential NFI kits and/or distribution of emergency life-saving shelter items. Also, Community awareness on the impact of Shelter/NFI activities on the environment and mitigation measures will be reinforced. Addition to this, the project will implement constructive training program on alternate building techniques and technical guidance for effective shelter delivery. Furthermore, the project will support prepositioning of NFIs and materials to support timely response for expected and potential hazards. Moreover, the project will ensure gender and environmental markers and other cross-cutting issues have been addressed through ensuring that the needs of all beneficiaries are addressed. Beside this, as part of accountability to affected populations; complains and feedback mechanisms are in place.
Finally, the project will ensure synergy with other sectors to ensure synergy in the intervention and to avoid duplication of services.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), observed that household and community capacities are being overstretched due to the extended economic crisis characterized by sustained high inflation, weak public service provision and a volatile political context; with repeated shocks including widespread intercommunal conflicts, social marginalization, resource limitations, natural disasters and disease outbreaks.Data from HNO 2022 showed that, the total number of the crisis affected people increased from the previous year with about 7% to reach 2.9 million people in Need (PiN) for Non-Food Items (NFI) assistance and shelter solutions. HNO will target this year about 1.5 million people with focus on the conflict affected areas. The interagency HNO showed an increase in the number of PiN for humanitarian assistance by 1.2 million distributed in 135 localities across the whole country. The identified people are mostly protracted, newly displaced people, returnees, and vulnerable communities. It is expected that, most states will remain in critical situation in 2023 and the need for emergency response including shelter and NFI assistance will be the main focus in hotspot areas such as South Kordofan, Blue Nile, East Sudan, Red Sea and Darfur states. In 2022, data revealed that over 3 million are protracted IDPs across Sudan, in addition to more than 900,000 returnees are in need for humanitarian response including provision of emergency shelter solution and urban integration through provision of environmentally friendly transition shelters, accompanied with training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience, adding to that more than 350,039 vulnerable residents including pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities, chronically ill, elders and unaccompanied people are also need for NFIs and shelter replenishment or upgrading. Given the peace agreement and increases in inter-communal conflict in 2022, there is high likelihood of increased IDPs and returnees in 2023 in needs of shelter and NFI assistance. On the other hand, lack of the institutional resources to provide adequate land and free space led to severe constraints across the camps and overpopulation within the camps, while insufficient space to accommodate additional household shelters for new arrivals, led to congestion and overcrowding and increased health risks. The issue of overcrowding with high population across the settlements and camps led to critical health concern where residents regularly face challenges for the poor infrastructure situation such as lack of drainage system with multiple ponding prone areas. Also, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable community and in particular, women and girls, face high protection risks including gender-based violence (GBV). Indeed, within this condition, it is not possible to ensure privacy and dignity as well as ensure mitigation of risks associated with health, exploitation and abuse. Additionally, the lack of access to basic services such as water and basic needs, continues to drive negative coping mechanisms, such as selling of humanitarian aid and NFIs to pay for food, medical services, water, and school fees, etc. Furthermore, the political and economic situation in the country with the seasonal flooding and COVID-19 pandemic are the driver force of deteriorating humanitarian needs, and increasing the cost and time taken to delivering shelter and NFI assistance. In August 2022 around 84,488 people affected by the torrential rains and flash flooding across the country. The floods affected 12 out of 18 states across the country. Aj Jazirah and River Nile being the worst hit states in Sudan in 2022. 12,758 houses are damaged and 5,892 are totally destroyed. River Nile for an example showed more than 2,563 houses that have been totally destroyed, and 739 houses damaged per only one locality in River Nile leading to temporary displacement.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance will concentrate mainly on providing adequate shelter solutions and basic life-saving items to the most devastating areas including returnees, newly displaced IDPs and vulnerable communities. Save the children will focus on maintaining quality standard through adopting cost effective modalities including cash based interventions which involve provision of cash to support construction of new shelters or improvement and or rehabilitation of exiting shelters. Surely, the cash based intervention is conditional based on the context aiming to promote community own driven approach. Likewise, the project will promote to shift from the released phase to recovery and adopt the use of environmental friendly materials aiming to reduce the impact of hazards in the future such as the flooding. Besides, the project will link with other sector in order to provide income generating and livelihood activities such as provision of shelter construction materials for self-building or supported with Labor costs for vulnerable households with lack of capacity to implement, provision of stabilized soil bricks, woven grass mats, and sleeping mats. Also, the project will work on solving the issue of overcrowding and congestion of shelters which will lead to reduce health risks. This activity will be accomplished through closer linkages between shelter and site planning interventions using geospatial analysis for camp selection and appropriate design for the shelter. Protracted households, returnees, IDPs and vulnerable communities will be provided basic tools and materials in areas where are not available. Also the shelter activities will include distribution of shelter materials considering the quality to vulnerable households particularly the areas that affected with seasonal flooding. In addition, settlement interventions under this project will focus on increasing the flood preparedness mechanism through improving the infrastructure condition by strengthening community based response mechanisms. Furthermore, the timely shelter solutions will involve local capacity training program targeting to improve the skills of the beneficiaries on construction methodologies and techniques in order to achieve an effective shelter delivery.Concerning the NFI assistance, the project will focus on preparedness to reduce the impact of flooding and other hazards through the pre-positioning of NFIs in strategically located warehouses through wide networks supporting vulnerable households to receive core-relief items. This activity will be relied based on the rapid response mechanism including need assessment. NFI kit normally consist of standard tools and items include plastic sheeting, blankets, sleeping mats, jerry cans, mosquito nets, a solar lamp and the kitchen set. The assessment, will verify the importance of the kits to ensure that these kits are in need for the targeted communities.Generally, planning and implementation of shelter and NFI activities aim to mitigate negative impact of hazard to ensure synergy with afforestation activities, and selecting durable shelter/NFI materials. The NFI activity will consider pre and post distribution monitoring in order to ensure all affected population with crisis receive their needs. The shelter and NFI project will work in close collaboration with other sectors and partners to ensure integration and quality of the implementations. Also, community empowerment will be addressed with other cross cutting issues. Finally, the project will aim to strengthen the accountability mechanism to mitigate any risk of safety and security in the project. SCI has capacity to undertake emergency shelter and NFI distributions across 12 states in 2023 in response to rapid-onset emergencies and displacement, e.g. following intercommunal conflict or flooding events, which cause a sudden spike in needs.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Save the Children main objectives is to enhance the impact on the environment with mitigation of the negative environmental consequences through following its Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, also SC committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of all programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. SC will include environmental considerations into all interventions and throughout all project life cycle including the assessment, procurement, planning, design and intervention phases. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, will be highly considered to select the appropriate option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. The project will promote the use of cost effective and environmental friendly materials in shelter interventions that exists locally contributing in resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources by involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. SC Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation in Sudan.This project directly and indirectly can cause environmental and natural resources impacts. The direct impact can be through material usage, waste generation and construction activities. The indirect impact through promoting detrimental interactions between people and the environment.This project will work in mitigation the negative impact for potential hazards through managing and conserving the natural resources and community participation in the planning and interventions phases in order to identify the health measures and enhance environmental condition. Environmental risk management will be considered during the project with several key components such as safe and secure site planning, using friendly materials for shelter construction considering the ecological manner and preference to use local materials. The project will consider to raise awareness among the community and other practitioners regarding the risks associated with environment. The project will develop risk plan to identify the risk and consider the key measures to mitigate the risk also, the project will assess the needs for the infrastructure and services. Furthermore, the project will conduct rapid assessment for environmental impact of shelter and construction materials to ensure environment does not affect because of our actions. Additionally, the project will consider to provide durable NFI kits in order to limit the beneficiaries from purchasing harm tools or materials and reduce wastes generation from beneficiaries.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Save the Children will work collaboratively with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs, protection cluster) to avoid inadvertently exposingaffected communities to risks and unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. SC aims to enhance coordination between stakeholder aiming to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. SCI will guide shelter and settlement design the inclusion of gender, age and diversity to ensure vulnerable groups are well protected. Also, SC will ensure program design and implementation is informed by context analysis. SC Sudan will coordinate with protection cluster and protection staff to support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. SC will prioritize most families reside inadequate shelter setting to reduce protection risk. Gender Equality Specialist will support gender sensitivity programs and will be assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.This project will consider protection through ensuring that the project activities target the most people in needs, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. These elements will be adopted in the general project management as central to delivering durable shelter solutions and NFI assistance. In order to Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm, the project will obtain data and adopt a systematic approach in registering beneficiaries for distributions considering gender, age and diversity. Also, women and disabled will be engaged through the project design to ensure the location, distance and access routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community. Beside this, the project will provide NFI kits that can meet the specific needs for vulnerable groups including: children, women, elderly and disabled. In terms of the shelter activity, the project will make shelter adaptation to shelter structure in order to cover the specific needs for vulnerable groups. SCI will advocate for provision of security tools within NFI kits including solar lamps, flashlights and/or whistles to enhance personal safety and mitigate the risk associated with GBV. The project will adopt community own-driven approach to develop local capacity and provide the community the sense of ownership.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

SCI has clear approach regarding the participation and accountability which includes timely community participation through all stages of the project. SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing and working together.Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, through adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all, and adopting child-friendly methodologies. SCI will ensure that affected people for the crisis including vulnerable groups will be consulted in a meaningful way in programming at all stages of the project lifecycle, and have regular opportunities to provide feedback and concerns. Save the Children will provide timely and accessible information (in local languages and culturally appropriate mediums) to affected communities on proposed interventions and deliverables, expectations of staff behavior especially with respect to our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct, and how to raise concerns or provide feedback. SC Sudan has a well-established Complaints & Feedback Response Mechanism, including a telephone hotline, and multiple further modalities which provide affected populations opportunities to give feedback anonymously and/or privately, and options for those literate and illiterate. Field level MEAL staff are responsible for receiving complaints, managing responses, and reporting them to Country Office MEAL staff who report to Senior Management. Data is anonymized, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. There are clear processes for responding to PSEA and other safeguarding concerns raised through any feedback channel. Save the Children has safeguarding specialist staff at Country Office level, who will provide additional support and conduct monitoring visits.Regarding the accountability to affected populations, SCI will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about the organization, the staff and the strategic goals. SCI Sudan will establish a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism with multiple community friendly channels. This will include both reactive and proactive channels. The complaints and feedback channels introduced in the projects will allow targeted communities and children to safely and confidentially report serious complaints including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and breaches of the code of conduct. Additionally, the project will form a committee from the community that can represent the community which involve all categories of the affected population in identifying and responding to shelter/NFI needs.
