Project Summary

This project will deliver lifesaving emergency health services including maternal, neonatal and child health and contribute to disease prevention and strengthening national, State and county emergency preparedness and response capacities in Maiwut County for an estimated 25,000 crisis affected population in need of healthcare. Through deployment of trained health workers including midwives and community health workers in facilities and mobile teams, primary health care services including treatment of common childhood illnesses, as well as emergency obstetric and neonatal care for pregnant and lactating women will be provided. The proposed action complements NIDO's core interventions in Maiwut County and the intervention focuses on an integrated, multi-sectoral response to floods, displacement and conflict, providing life-saving services. To maximize impact, this project will provide evidence-based curative, promotional and preventive services and will enable Complementarity with nutrition including priorities such as deworming, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, vitamin deficiencies, & micronutrient supplementation. In collaboration with Health Cluster, NIDO will support the appropriate management of children with SAM and cholera co-morbidity admitted in AWD/Cholera treatment centres. Scaling up the WASH component as part of an integrated and coordinated response, will focus on disease outbreaks, especially cholera. Thus, the integrated action focuses on increasing response in areas of highest need and lowest levels of predictable access in Maiwut.
NIDO supported health facilities in Maiwut will be equipped with essential medicines and equipment and trained staff will provide quality services. Emphasis will be laid on Maternal and Child Health, including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care and Expanded Programme on Immunization to IDPs and most vulnerable communities and attention will be paid on application of treatment guidelines as per the Essential Package of Health Services and Primary Health Care. Support to outreach immunization for hard to-reach areas will be promoted to ensure equity and reaching a wider catchment.
NIDO will deliver comprehensive reproductive health care to women of reproductive age, men and adolescent boys among the target beneficiaries and promote health seeking behaviour including adoption of preventive practices through campaigns, health and hygiene education sessions for men. NIDO will also integrate surveillance and emergency preparedness ensuring adequate stocks for epidemic outbreaks of AWD/Cholera and measles are prepositioned. The project will also contribute to strengthening early warning disease surveillance through weekly submission of Communicable and Disease Surveillance reports from all the functional supported health facilities. Referral system for patients seeking secondary healthcare services will be strengthened. The project will also refer GBV victims to health facilities for comprehensive care and psychosocial support.

1 - Needs

Access to adequate health services, particularly for women and children, is extremely restricted by social barriers, poor infrastructure, poor service quality, high levels of insecurity, and a limited number of health facilities and trained health workers. Relative security in Maiwut county provides an opportunity to significantly improve health services. This proposal specifically focuses on the urgent health care needs to be implemented over one year. The project targets 25,000 beneficiaries with healthcare services out of which 2,000 beneficiaries are people with disabilities. The proposed intervention is aligned to the Health, WASH, Food security and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. In Maiwut county where the intervention will be implemented is characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to poor waste disposal, deforestation, land degradation and water scarcity . By increasing access to nutrition services through use of mobile teams and community focused approaches, the project reduces abnormal migration of human to other areas where they inflict significant environmental damages by exerting pressure on existing services. In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. This has been partly responsible for the periodic outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) in IDPs and villages which increases the risk of high morbidity and mortality rates among vulnerable groups. The project will use an integrated approach with WASH to provide latrines with hand washing facilities that will ease the level of public health risk posed by providing safe places and consequently check the spread of human waste. Whose location will be far from the water wells to check the risks of contamination. Public health will be ensured through proper disposal and management of medical equipment, supplies and waste, including the use of incinerators at the health facility. A component of nutrition training will also include lessons on environmental awareness with the objective of providing skills to the beneficiaries on better waste management, which doesn’t endanger their immediate environment. The project will jointly with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of community groups (Community Health Committees, camp management committees, Mother support groups) to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to NIDO’s resilience program and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities. The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project will adhere to government policies and will be designed jointly with community user groups.

2 - Project activities

ACTIVITY 1: Maternal, reproductive and neonatal health - NIDO will provide basic obstetric and neonatal care in its supported Health centres in Maiwut County. The supported facilities will be equipped with maternity beds for 24 hour observation of post delivery cases. Other services which will be provided through the health centre would include; family planning counseling and service delivery, antenatal care, post natal care, safe delivery care, immunisation, nutrition (Micro-nutrient supplementation and education) etc. ACTIVITY 2: Child health - NIDO will train the medical staff of the health centre so as to diagnose and treat childhood illnesses based on the IMCI guideline Health centres. The following services will be provided to children: treatment of acute respiratory illness (ARI), Diarrhea diseases and Management of Anemia. ICCM package will be provided at the community level by CHWs. ACTIVITY 3: EPI - Routine immunization for both under five children and women of reproductive age will be provided. Mid- year micro planning involving the local community through the Health Committee and the SMOH/CHD would be held. ACTIVITY 4: Conduct Health and hygiene education sessions targeting male and female caregivers of children attending Health centres. ACTIVITY 5: Training of 60 staff (40 male, 30 female) on BeMoNC, iCCM and IMCI . ACTIVITY 6: Sharing of weekly EWARN reports and programme trends through the Health Clusters 4W matrix ACTIVITY 7. Participate in national and state/county cluster meetings, working groups and coordination mechanisms.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations. Lack of static facilities that can provide comprehensive package of health services in IDP settings due to poor shelter is a risk factor denying them timely response and needed referral services. Poor sanitation and hygiene within the IDP camps causes the easy spread of communicable diseases. To address these risks, NIDO will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive package of health services within the IDP camps by providing health services within the IDP camps. Mobile teams will be supported to provide services to IDPs and referral mechanism to secondary healthcare outside IDP camps. In order to avoid community tensions, host communities will be provided with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. Both displaced and host communities will be consulted about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. In cooperation with protection actors, reporting mechanisms will be established to report security incidents concerning GBV cases and preventing of displaced populations from accessing services. IDP communities will be involved in identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. To overcome these risks of exclusion based on gender and age, the implementation will be based on a conducted needs assessment that has desegregated data by sex and age. Location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services will be accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Aged and disabled groups will be supported by ensuring facilities they access have railings and handlebars. Where possible, transportation and enhanced referral will be provided so that they can access services. Health facilities will have lights outside and on the access route to the facility and health services will take into consideration cultural practices and will consider separate waiting areas for males and females. Facilities will be encouraged to have proportionate number of female health staff to address cultural preferences and sensitivities. To ensure equity, mobile teams will be used to reach rural communities avoiding their exclusion. Health staff and health committees will be gender balanced and will have representation from all groups within the community. The protracted conflict in South Sudan is characterized by its longevity, intractability and mutability. Lack of respect for IHL means lack of respect for human rights and causes immense human suffering. The conflict in South Sudan causes widespread displacement, multiplying the cumulative impact on basic services and livelihoods over time, and the sharp increase in “war poor” populations. Destruction of facilities in conflict is real and this deprives communities’ critical services. NIDO will mitigate the impact and the consequences of armed conflict through the promotion of rights-based approach to service provision, providing access and services to all affected people without discrimination. Prepositioning essential supplies in strategic hubs for immediate response, providing outreach and mobile health services to displaced communities, providing referral services for critical cases. Rehabilitation of facilities will be given consideration so that damaged facilities can be operationalized to provide services. Strengthened monitoring and risk assessment will ensure data informed intervention and promotion of the rights of the most vulnerable groups.
