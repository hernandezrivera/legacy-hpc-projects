Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to restore and maintain access to safe, inclusive and quality education for children of school going age while supporting those out of school acquire foundational skills and knowledge requisite for learning and integration into formal learning opportunities. Additionally, children who dropped out of school for one reason or the other will be supported to re-enroll back to school. The project will aim at addressing multiple social, cultural, economic, gender, linguistic, status and geographical barriers that limit children from enjoying their right to education across the target states. Guided by a vulnerability criteria, the project will target the most vulnerable children and adolescents aged between six (6) to eighteen (18) years. The focus will mainly be on IDPs, returnees and affected host communities, paying more attention to children from hard to reach areas, including those with disabilities. A total of 37,400 beneficiaries (37,000 children, 200 Teachers and 200 SBMCs) will be targeted in 2023. Education response will be based on identified education needs across Borno and Adamawa states and will be designed around three major outcomes in line with the education sector strategic plan 2021-2023:
• Increased Access to education for emergency-affected girls and boys
• Increased continuity of education for emergency-affected girls and boys
• Existing learning environments are child-friendly, equitable, and inclusive
• Improved quality of learning for emergency-affected girls and boys
Specifically the project will provide of non-formal education for those who have never been to school as well as catch up programs for children who dropped out of school in order to position them to re-enroll back; construct/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces/classrooms; recruitment of non-formal facilitators as well as provision of teaching and learning materials. Additionally the project will facilitate and manage transitions from non-formal to formal education through working with state agency for mass education (SAME) to assess and certify the learners transitioning to formal schools. Learning facilities will be supported to be inclusive, child-friendly, disability- friendly, and gender sensitive through teacher training, PSS integration, and establishment of referral pathways for specialized services beyond the scope of the project. Teaching and learning materials will be provided and teachers will be equipped have sufficient knowledge and skills to deliver quality education. Psychosocial support will also be integrated into the teacher training packages to equip them with skills to integrate PSS into learning activities as this is crucial so social and emotional wellbeing of learners.
The project will target beneficiaries from various LGAs in Borno and Adamawa states, some of which include; MMC, Jere, Monguno, Pulka and Gwoza in Borno state; as well as Yola, Mubi, Madagali and Michika in Adamawa state. Implementation of Education interventions will be done in accordance to the Education sector response plans, the contextualized Nigeria Minimum Standards for EiE, Education sector response plan and HNO 2022 among others. NRC will pay keen attention to the trends and dynamics across various states, bearing in mind that approaches may not uniformly apply across all locations. NRC will work in close collaboration with education authorities and key stakeholders such as SUBEB, SAME as well as other education partners through the EiE working group for ownership and sustainability.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 indicated that 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022. Of these, 2.2M are internally displaced; 1.5M are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. The Education Sector Response Plan 2023 documents a high number of People in Need (PIN), highlighting high number of IDPs and returnees in some LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states. For instance, in Maiduguri LGA, out of a total population of 846,658 people, 237,509 are IDPs in and out of camps; while in Jere, out of a total population of 561,143 people, 291,416 people are IDPs in and out of camps. Michika LGA in Adamawa records a high number of returnees at 150,472, out of a total population of 221,702; while Gwoza records 147,136 IDPs and 92,832 returnees out of a total population of 241,532. The HNO 2022 further indicates that 1.9 people are in need of education across the states. The conflict and displacement has negatively affected the social structures and the economy of the region. Boko Haram opposes “Western” values, particularly the formal and secular education provided by the government and their factions regularly target education, destroying or damaging schools, threatening and killing teachers and abducting students for ransom and publicity. The impact of this crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without opportunities to learn and even more vulnerable. According to the EiE Working group strategy 2021-1023, the number of children with chronic needs in education remains high across the BAY states and the crisis has put additional pressure on an education system that was already fragile. Estimates indicate that nearly 60% of primary school-age children are not attending school, with girls disproportionally affected. 41.8 % and 42.2% of primary school age children are not attending school in Adamawa and Yobe states respectively, compared to 27% at national level. Children and teachers in the BAY states have also a continuing need of psychosocial support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. Poverty remains the greatest barrier to access education, with parents being unable to afford education fees and levies. The poor quality and lack of infrastructure such as classrooms, school furniture and WASH facilities, are also critical barriers. The insufficient number of qualified teachers and lack of teaching and learning materials have also contributed to poor attendance, retention and learning outcomes of learners in schools and learning centres. A UNICEF report 2021 indicated that there were 1,004 kidnappings from 25 school raids across North East, North West, and North Central of Nigeria. A total of 11, 536 schools were closed since December 2020 due to abductions and security issues. NRC has been at the forefront in delivery of Education alongside other actors in BAY states since 2015. However, glaring gaps remain prevalent which justify the need for continued support, expansion of services and diversification of strategies to reach the neediest communities in both accessible and hard to reach areas.

2 - Activities

The following interventions will be implemented under the project;Outcome 1: Increased Access to education for emergency-affected girls and boys; The project will enhance access to alternative learning opportunities for children affected by conflict through provision of non-formal education for those who have never been to school as well as catch up programs for children who dropped out of school in order to position them to re-enroll back. Additionally construction of temporary learning spaces to provide safe and conducive environment for learning and recruitment of non-formal education facilitators will be provided. The project will further promote sustainable access to formal education for children affected by conflict by supporting boys and girls in formal schools through provision of teaching and learning materials, teacher training and rehabilitation of classrooms among others to create a conducive environment for learning. WASH in schools will be prioritized through provision of Water, construction of gender segregated latrines and hygiene awareness promotion activities in schools.Outcome 2: Increased continuity of education for emergency-affected girls and boys: NRC through the project will work closely with education authorities and stakeholders to facilitate and manage transitions from non-formal to formal education. NRC will work with state agency for mass education (SAME) to assess and certify the learners transitioning to formal schools as well as provide teaching and learning materials for continuity of learning. The project will further train SBMCs to equip them with skills and knowledge necessary to manage schools and develop school development plans.Outcome 3: Existing learning environments are child-friendly, equitable, and inclusive: The project will ensure that supported facilities are inclusive, child-friendly, disability- friendly, and gender sensitive. This will be done by ensuring that teachers have capacity to address specific needs of learners enrolled in schools and referral pathways established to refer learners to access more specialized services beyond the scope of the project. Additionally, school infrastructure and environment will be adopted to meet the different needs of children including gender and disability specific needs.Outcome 4: Improved quality of learning for emergency-affected girls and boys NRC through the project will ensure to Children and teachers in supported classes have adequate teaching and learning materials that have a direct impact on the quality of learning as well as learning outcomes. Additionally, teachers will be equipped have sufficient knowledge and skills to deliver quality education, as measured by pupil learning outcome. Psychosocial support will also be integrated into the teacher training packages to equip them with skills to integrate PSS into learning activities as this is crucial so social and emotional wellbeing of learners.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will benefit children from IDP, returnee and host communities, teachers, school based management committees and indirectly benefit the surrounding communities. Protection will be mainstreamed in all aspects of the project. NRC has developed a Safe and Inclusive Programming Action plan to ensure that programming promotes various aspects of safety and inclusion in all phases of the project cycle. Consultation and feedback mechanisms will be established to enable beneficiaries share their concerns or feedback related to the services provided. Conflict sensitive education principles will be applied during the implementation of the project so that all interventions minimize the risk of causing conflict among the beneficiaries. Education staff, Teachers and school based management committee will be trained on child protection, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and safe and inclusive programming so that all education interventions will promote safety and protection of all beneficiaries NRC’s existing child protection and safeguarding measures will guide the project; and all staff, teachers, consultants and any other persons engaged in the education programme will sign the code of conduct and child safe guarding policy as a commitment to protect children and other rights holders that NRC will be supporting during the period.
