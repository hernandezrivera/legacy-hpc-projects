Project Summary

In South Sudan at least more than 8 Million people in the country needs humanitarian assistance including health services. Legacy of conflict, food insecurity, flooding, disease outbreaks, weak economy and poor infrastructural development have resulted in a fragile health system. Moreover, the previous occurrence of COVID-19 Pandemic and the current influx of returnees, refugees and asylum seekers due to the Sudan crisis posed additional constraints to an already weak system. Women, men and children are in need of sufficient health care services whereas South Sudan’s still has poor health indicators including maternal mortality rate of 1,223/ 100,000 live births, child mortality at 81deaths/1,000 live births are at alarming level. Greater Pibor Administrative Area corroborates the national level and in need of humanitarian response. The health system is worsened by frequent population displacement and interruption of services due to sub-national violence and flooding. A substantial number of individuals in hard-to-reach remain without adequate services (only 12 Bomas supported by BHI). Health services is limited, only 17 (69%) of the 27 Health Facilities which are functional. The referral system is weak and worse during the rainy season due to physical access constraints. Health facilities structures are dilapidated and 60% of County health department (CHD) health workforce are not qualified. There is need to address issues of continuous low routine vaccine (50%) coverage which results to building up of cohorts of “Zero Dose” and under-vaccinated children, rendering them susceptible to the outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases (VPD). In reproductive health, while proportion skilled birth attendant is at 44%; women attends antenatal care 1 (ANC+1) at third trimester leading to low (26%) of mothers attending at least 4 ANC visits during pregnancy. The GPAA prevalence of wasting is above WHO emergency threshold i.e Pibor County GAM of 18.8% and Pochalla County increased from 10% to 19% (IPC Nov 2023 report). Menstrual hygiene management and handwashing practice at critical periods remain lacking. Barriers to access to health facilities, particularly for women include lack of female health workers, poor roads, leading to higher protection risks such as sexual abuse and harassment; and Mental Health and Psychosocial challenges.

To improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV, ForAfrika formerly (JAM), will work with MOH/ CHD and coordinate with other partners, cluster for integrated response that includes nutrition, protection and WASH. The plan is to support three (3) additional health facilities to meet WHO IPC Minimum Requirements, improve vaccine coverage, engage Community health workers to reach out to 20,000 individuals as well us consultation and treatment. ForAfrika intends to reach to more additional 420 pregnant women with skill birth services, 1383 persons with disability, Mental Health, Psychosocial support and at least 20 SGBV cases; key staff will be trained on case management to ensure eligible cases access basic services are referred accordingly. To respond to SO2, reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and SO3; ForAfrika will ensure notification of diseases of potential outbreak within 24hrs, last mile deliveries of Interagency Emergency Health kits and at least 20 SGBV survivors receiving CMR services. Leveraging other interventions and platform, behavioral change communication including building capacity of Integrated community mobilization networks and BHI committees and other stakeholder, staff on AAP, lifesaving communication and other actions, consultative meetings shall be undertaken to identify needs, enable participation to reduce health impact.

1. Needs

Chronic gaps remain prevalent across the health system when assessed along WHO’s six building blocks. In GPAA health infrastructures are either collapsed, dilapidated or non- existing while low health facility density, inadequate basic amenities, equipment, and diagnostic capacity constrain service delivery and service utilization rate. Only 15 facilities out of 27 are functional. Non–Communicable Disease (NCD) goes undiscovered and untreated. 51% of ANC+1 visits is at 2nd or 3rd trimesters in pregnancy, women deliver before completing ANC+4. Currently the ANC4 is at 39% (DHI2- Dashboard September,2023). Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC), Clinical management of Rape (CMR), PMTCT is provided at only 3 facilities; whereas Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) is only offered in 60% of health facilities, with basic laboratory diagnostic capacity estimated at only 4%. The services are minimal in Otallo, Gumuruk and Lekuongole which are 45km away from main county hospital indicating the dire situation of access to life-saving interventions for major obstetric and newborn causes of morbidity and mortality. ForAfrika Gender Safety Audit 2023, mentioned the disparities in access to healthcare and decision-making power and increasing risks of exposure to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Moreover, children are predisposed to VPD, due to low vaccine coverage and pipe-line breakage as a result of poor road network.Women and girls face barriers such as cultural norms, discriminatory practices, and limited educational opportunities that prevent them from enjoying their full sexual and reproductive health rights. Current trends in the country indicate a high teenage pregnancy rate of 65.9% (births per 1,000 women aged 15–19) attributable to the high rate of child, early, and forced marriage. In addition, persons with disabilities (PWDs) are amongst those most vulnerable since they face stigma and numerous cultural, attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers that hinder them from accessing quality healthcare. Health workforce: There are no Health Science Institutions in GPAA; high level of illiteracy and notable lack of adequately skilled human resources for health, 60% of the CHD staff are untrained. There is poor capacity for medical supplies management high cost of delivery that includes last mile delivery, lack of appropriate storage infrastructures exposes supplies to climate hazards and looting during conflict. In Health Information Management System (HMIS): Completeness and timeliness in HMIS reporting have remained low while weak governance system at the County Health Department, sub-optimal at the subnational level and weak downstream-level community ownership and accountability mechanisms is noted. ForAfrika, Pibor Positive Deviance Inquiry Study - 2022 Report; finds that, only 40.2% of the population in Pibor utilize form of health care services.There is need to Improve health infrastructures, health care center with proper Obstetric and emergency services with clear referral system; laboratory investigation; mental health and psycho social support, HIV and TB services within the locality. Their need to provide essential clinical package through mobile in order to reach out the underserved communities. There is need to recruit qualified staff, Moreover, there is also a need to ensure that the health workforce trained can deliver integrated health and nutrition services. There is need to have advance preposition of supplies of Medical commodities and supplies in Pibor. Due to occurrence of disease outbreaks, there is need to improve Community, facility and county disaster response mechanism and address outbreaks and prevent case fatality rates. There is need to improve the referral mechanism within the location and comprehensive address the SGBV clinical cases prior to referrals for other services.

2. Project activities

In line with Sectoral objective 1, 2 and 3 that is- Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and SGBV health services to crisis affected populations (SO1). Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases (SO2). Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms (SO3).ForAfrika will maintain its Vast experience in Pibor County due to its nutrition and food security interventions that give a better understanding of the context. In this project ForAfrika implementation will be guided by hosting country policy within the W.H.O pillars of health system and guidelines. The proposed response will work in coordination with MOH, W.H.O, UNICEF, UNFPA and CHD for technical guidance, access to medical supplies and commodities including (IEHK), Surveillance reporting and coordination through the cluster. The plan is to contribute to real time disease surveillance through EWARS/IDSR and Maintenance of Health information systems and timely submission of validated data including the DHIS 2 and 5W. This will contribute to submission of data's for diseases of potential outbreak alerts. The plan is to ensure the project is integrated to the nutrition intervention to ensure management of acute malnutrition; Cumulative number of persons, planned to be reached in this project is 20,000 individuals that includes children under the age of 5 years. ForAfrika will provide curative services to 9,807 individuals with major on treatment of communicable diseases and non- communicable diseases: capacity building of the local available staff. Provide – MCH (antenatal care, skill birth and postal-natal) to the target 420 women of the target population to pregnant women; For Afrika will ensure staff are trained on basic EmONC, provision of focused Ante- Natal Care and emergency obstetric care to pregnant women, using skilled personnel and provision of postnatal care; provision of LLINs and Clean Delivery Kit (CDK) will be considered; Referral for complicated delivery to skilled health care worker, this aimed at improving maternal health and reducing <5 morbidity.There will be provision of routine EPI and involvement is supplementary immunization (SIA), routine vaccination and ensure weekly transportation of vaccines with other supplies to improve vaccination coverage of both routine and reactive to improve PENTA3 in <One-year-old target (1620) and measles vaccination of target 5220 (2558 boys and 2662 girls) between 6 months – 15 years respectively. The project will ensure TB and HIV cases access treatment through referral of cases to the County hospital and support in routine follow-up. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MPHSS) and SGBV cases will be linked and supported cases will be referred according to the referral pathway Referral in addition to the training of frontline health staff on the basic MPHSS and SGBV – The project will ensure referral of SAM cases with Medical Complication to County Hospital Stabilization Centre. To Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms, community health events and monthly meetings will be conducted through facilitation of County Health Department; At least three supported facilities will have community feedback mechanism additionally annual community consultative meetings will be conducted to identify needs, participation in response, and reduce impact on their lives including persons with disability. This project will be integrated with Peace and Social cohesions awareness creation using the health platforms including during health education and health events to promote peaceful coexistence within the project location.
