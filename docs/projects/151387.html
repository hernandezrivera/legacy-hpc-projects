Project Summary

The project aims to support emergency, transitional and durable shelter and NFI solutions in Somalia. ACTED aims to contribute to the protection of newly displaced people and those affected by natural hazards with life-saving and life-sustaining assistance, also targeting vulnerable host communities when needs arise. IDPs in protracted situations will be provided support to improve their living conditions, through provision of transitional shelter solutions and settlement (re-)planning, especially those that suffer from overcrowding and which are at risk of fire and disease outbreak. To the extent possible, market-based and owner-driven approaches to shelter implementation will be preferred.

Needs

Although the drought has receded, at least in certain areas of the country, the situation remains critical as households' resilience and coping mechanisms have been severely affected by years of protracted crisis (FSNAU September 2018). Returns to area of origin are very limited, since majority of the affected population lost sources of livelihood, and insecurity often does not allow safe and dignified returns. The 2018 Joint Multi Cluster Needs Assessment findings show that over half of all assessed households (52%) reported living in emergency and semi-permanent shelters, which offer limited defense against environmental elements and leave inhabitants vulnerable to a variety of protection concerns. IDP and minority clan households (80%) are more likely to live in emergency or semi-permanent shelters than host community (44%). Regions with significantly higher rates of emergency and temporary shelters for IDPs include Nugaal (64%), Bay (59%), Sanaag (48%) and Banadir (48%). These shelters often do not have doors that are lockable from the inside, lighting fixtures, or internal separations; 71% of IDP and 37% of host community shelters were lacking in two or more of these features (JMCNA 2018). Even long term displaced population often live in this precarious shelters, lacking basic non-food shelter items like mats, blankets or plastic sheets. Additionally, evictions continue to be a major protection issue for displaced population. Over 204,000 people were evicted between January and July 2018, almost double the 122,000 evicted over the same period last year (HLP July 2018). When evicted, affected populations lose their belongings, including humanitarian assistance received, and have to rebuild their shelters in a new location. Where land is available or there is possibility to negotiate with local authorities, there is thus urgent need to move to construction of temporary shelters, in order to guarantee dignified living conditions to affected populations.

Activities

Project will target IDPS with a possibility to include vulnerable host communities in order to promote peaceful coexistence and reduce tensions over resources. Whenever ESK or NFIs items are available in the market the interventions will be conducted through a conditional cash transfer.S/NFIs cluster standard kits will be generally followed and adapted based on feedback received by the community during participatory needs assessments. Emergency Shelter Distributions: provision of emergency shelter kits to newly displaced or evicted population. If the shelter conditions are critical and it is not possible to negotiate land for construction of T-shelters, ESK will also target the most vulnerable that have been displaced for a protracted period.Non Food Items Distributions: provision of emergency NFIs kits to newly displaced or evicted population, or to the most vulnerable among long terms displaced population. Temporary shelter construction: Whenever context allows, ACTED will work in close cooperation with HLP colleagues to secure land and construct temporary shelters. ACTED will coordinate with partners to make sure that other services are in place or accessible for the affected population in the selected areas. Links to durable solutions will be ensured through coordination with existing durable solutions forum, consultation on intentions of the affected population, link to other existing long term/resilience building projects in the targeted areas. Protection Mainstreaming: strict vulnerabilities criteria for beneficiary selection will be followed. Community will be involved throughout the intervention. Appropriate protection measures will be in place before, during and after the distributions, including CFM hotlines. Shelter and NFI kits will be designed to maximize protection impact on affected population.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In areas where evictions are less rampant and public land is available ACTED will promote transition to local integration as durable solutions, particularly through: - Promoting and conducting consultation with affected population on intentions through needs assessments cognizant of age and gender diversity;- Participating in durable solutions forums at the local level (and advocate for their creation), inclusive of all the stakeholders, with the local authorities playing a key part in the planning for durable solutions;- Advocating for land and resilience services for affected population, in close cooperation with HLP colleagues; - Once land is secured with a stable formal stable agreement, building sustainable transitional shelters with active involvement of the local population; - Link construction of Transitional shelters to other existing long term and resilience building interventions; - Through market assessments and analyses, ACTED will strive to increase the ratio of market-based assistance and deliver shelter assistance and NFIs through conditional cash. This will contribute to diversify and strengthen local resources exploitation and livelihoods. Local suppliers and materials will be prioritized to support local markets and reduce the cost of importing materials.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Assistance and services do not reach the most vulnerable categoriesLikelihood: Low Impact: HighMitigation measures: Project aims to target IDPs settlements, through its connection to the Camp Management sector. ACTED strictly applies the 4 core humanitarian principles plus Do Not Harm in all its interventions. Through its regular physical presence in the sites with CM team, ACTED will build strong relationships with the displaced communities. Inclusive Community Governance structures representative of all the different component of the affected population will help in identification of most vulnerable and more in needs HHs to be targeted with the interventions. An integrated and multi sector CFM mechanism for the site will be in place giving the chance to every individual to present feedback and/or complaints, including on beneficiaries’ selections and verification processes. Risk: The project achievement is hindered by lack of land tenure arrangementsLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will monitor through close cooperation with the HLP colleagues access to land tenure agreement including equal accessibility, in order to monitor if there are specific groups who are discriminated in access to land and land tenure agreement and will take appropriate measures to prevent this to happen, including cooperation with local authorities and advocacy measures. This inclusive approach will be reflected when planning settlements for T-shelters.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Age- and gender-specific needs are not taken into account throughout the proposed intervention.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: Through Inclusive Community Governance structures representative of the all the different component of the affected population, ACTED will pay particular attention to gender and age representation. Involvement and consultation of all categories and layers of the affected population will help in identifying and responding to the shelter/NFI needs. ACTED will particularly pay attention to the protection component of the intervention and it will therefore focus on protection proxy indicators such as presence of internal partitions and lockable doors, distribution of multipurpose solar lights (to be carried to the latrine and to enlighten the shelters), involvement of the community in the identification of the NFIs basket through participatory needs assessment. Trainings conducted by ACTED have specific modules on Protection and GBV mainstreaming and on humanitarian principles and code of conduct. Risk: Location, distance and access routes to the shelter infrastructure are not accessible to all members of the community.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will look at adapting interventions in order to make them tailored and inclusive of people with disabilities (i.e. people with mobility issues), especially when it comes to design of T-shelter or identification of an appropriate and accessible distribution site.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: High needs vs. limited resources/services provided Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will target new arrivals and evicted communities with emergency shelter kit and NFI distribution or conditional cash assistance: this will provide at least short term relief to HHs in serious distress because of displacement, often connected to widespread violence, especially in south central Somalia. In order to avoid tension over resources and according to the level of need of the host communities, interventions will be designed to include vulnerable host communities HHs to increase peaceful coexistence. Risk: Volatile security environment hampers project operationsLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over time, ACTED has developed a low profile access strategy with solid security measures. A participatory approach is used to update security risk analysis and triangulated with other sources to provide regular security briefings to staff. Working closely with targeted communities, engagement with local partners and prioritizing community sensitization and participation, ACTED and partners build acceptance of activities from the bottom-up.
