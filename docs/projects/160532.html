Project Summary

The project is intended to assist communities in desperate need of Shelter and Non Food Items materials to provide immediate lifesaving support and improve their ability to cope with recurrent shock and build resilience.
In order to have effective and efficient response to the needs, Plan International will continue to implement its mobile response in Lakes to reach many people who are in need of Non Food Items and emergency shelter. This will include detailed need assessments, mapping of communities, coordination meetings, mobilization of resources and community awareness to respond to the immediate needs of the population through a well-coordinated cluster approach.
The main activities of this project will also include:
1. The assessment of the needs of beneficiaries and identification and registration of beneficiaries.
2. Distribution of Non Food Items and shelter materials through in-kind and cash voucher
3. Conducting Post-Distribution Monitoring.
4. : sharing key messages on sensitization and awareness of communities and beneficiaries on Ebola epidemic and HIV/AID prevention during S/NFI distribution at the start of mobilization, targeting and implementation of the project
5. work with local authorities and host communities to assess issues of housing, land and property in regards to IDPs and returnees and come up with appropriate measures to ensure that provision of shelter materials recognizes this preconditions
Plan International has a field office in Rumbek and Awerial in Lakes and has vast experience implementing Shelter/NFI projects in Lakes state. this project is designed to complement current interventions by Plan International and its partners CCOSS and Titi Foundation in Lakes . The project will target a total of 3,333 households or 20,000 individuals to support the access and maintenance of Shelter/NFIs for the most vulnerable population. The Shelter and NFI distribution will cover the immediate shelter and protection needs of all men, women, boys and girls by providing them with safety and protection.
Beneficiaries will be involved through various stages of the project implementation. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be ensured by regularly engaging with communities to understand their needs and by providing mechanisms in which beneficiaries can air their grievances and complaints. Telephone hotlines, suggestion boxes and management committees will be just some of the means available to the communities to use to ensure Accountability to Affected Population.
The project will also use a gender analysis to understand the power dynamics and inequalities that exist between women and men, girls and boys during conflict and address the physical and strategic needs of the most vulnerable population in Lakes State. This will include directing the protection concerns of women, unaccompanied minors and girls in particular, who are often at risk of sexual abuse and even more so without shelter. It is expected that they will be further protected by this provision of housing materials.
Plan international is committed to ensure communities and people affected by crisis participate in any decisions that affect them. From the onset of the project, Plan International will institute community engagement and dialogue to ensure communities understand, accept and engage in the project, supporting its objectives and outputs/outcomes. Plan International will also ensure that community helpdesks and a mobile hotline are put in place for the beneficiaries to provide feedback and complaints that Plan International can address. This feedback and complaint mechanism will be strengthened for the community through raising awareness in the community about their availability and encouraging community members to use them and share their thoughts about the project with Plan International, including, what they like, any reservations and how they would like the project to improve or be changed.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in South Sudan has led to an estimated 1.9 million people being displaced within the country (OCHA) and 2.3 million living as refugees in neighboring countries (80% of whom are women and children). More than 7 million people (about two thirds of the population) are in need of humanitarian assistance and as of 2019, around 80% of people are living below the poverty line (Relief Web 2019). It is estimated that more than 230,000 people are currently in need of emergency shelter with 80% of the affected population being women children, youth and elderly. In Lakes State, inter-communal conflict has been persistent, resulting in displaced communities who have lost their main assets and means of protection. The conflict between Abiriu and Duony Payam of Cueibet County (Lakes State) started in 2015, with ongoing tension and conflict between the clans, and frequent revenge attacks being launched among the people of Abiriu, Duony, Hoayok, Goktiar, Aciminty, Mayong-Thon, Cwer gak and Malou yol. Attacks are directed towards revenge, so the loss of lives, destruction of property, and raiding of animals is common. Western Lakes and Eastern Lakes State shared a border at Bar-gel (Agangrial Payam in Lake State) where Ngap and Bele clans originally lived before the creation of more Counties and States. Since Agangrial became a County, both clans have disagreed on being recognized as part of Agangrial County of Gok State, dividing the community of Bar-Gel into two and creating further displacement within the region. This same border dispute occurred between Kilos seven (Agangrial) and Tuonch (Warap) where the population divided in two, resulting in some people being displaced. This has left people particularly vulnerable due to the loss of assets after fleeing from their homes. The lack of shelter and other non-food items (NFIs) means that newly displaced people are much more likely to be exposed to extreme weather conditions and diseases. In addition, the risk of abuse to women and girls in particular also increases when there is a lack of appropriate shelter. Some displaced persons have integrated within the host communities, causing the limited resources available in the community to be depleted. This has caused friction within the communities, leading to fears of worsening relations between the host communities and IDPs. A needs assessment conducted in May by the Shelter Cluster revealed that IDPs, returnees and host community members are usually sleeping in structures (local rakubas and tukuls) that have been abandoned or belong to relatives and/or friends. These are usually not in good condition. Local construction materials (such as poles) are readily available in Yirol East however grass (usually used in roofing) is hard to come by due to overgrazing of cattle. In addition to this, there is a lack of tools and major protection issues when it comes to collecting local materials, particularly for women who fear being attacked whilst in the bush. Other vulnerable people in these communities (such as elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers and children) also cannot easily (or safely) go to the bush to collect shelter materials and often end up in inadequate shelter.In both target locations, Internally displaced people (IDPs) who are living in the cattle camps have access to less NFIs as they don’t live with the host community and have no utensils for cooking. Instead, these IDPs are using empty containers that were used to store cooking oil to cook food, which impacts the taste and smell of food. Many people have created mosquito nets from pieces of cloth, and are sleeping on leaves collected from coconut trees. Some have made beds out of wooden poles and grass. Consultations with children revealed that they had a very hard time when it was raining heavily and many had to sleep outside, putting them at risk of various diseases as well as abuse.
