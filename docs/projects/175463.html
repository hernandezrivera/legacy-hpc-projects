1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project aims to improve the life conditions of families displaced by the conflict and host communities. It foresees an initial assessment on NFI and shelter needs for the most needy displaced families and families hosting IDPs fleeing from conflict areas. Shelter construction traing will be privileged for IDPs in the newly established accommodation sites created in close partnership with local authorities and reference clusters. The trainings will promote sustainable mid- to long-term solutions integrating AVSI’s urban planning expertise readapted to accommodation sites. To this aim, community engagement will be crucial in order to adapt the proposed intervention to the needs of beneficiaries in regards to the development of social and economic life around the new shelter environemtn created and livelihood-wise activities. The beneficiaries lists created by the government must be verified by AVSI shelter staff in order to proceed with shelter and NFI distributions (activity 1.4 and 1.5) and therefore to avoid duplications and overlapping during the distributios. The project entails also cash-for-work (through vouchers distributions) aimed at improving livelihoods through improved coockstoves (ICS) production, basic agriculture infrastructures, and maintenance works within and outside the newly established accommodation sites whereas host families have been affected by people displacement to due the conflict.The distributions will be followed up and assessed against monitoring assessment created with Kobotool.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1.1 Shelter and NFI needs assessment for IDPs and host families. A vulnerability assessment will be conducted to identify the most needy families among IDPs and families hosting IDPs so that to understand what to distribute and to whom1.2 Training on resilient shelter construction. Taking into account the rapid changing context more biased towards mid- to long-term solutions, it will be necessary to envisage resilient shelter or shelter that could be upgraded as the time passes by.1.3 Focus groups discussions aimed at shaping livelihoods activities around newly established shelter environment. The livelihood activities that IDPs will carry out will depend also and be shaped by the new environment where shelter will be built up.1.4 Beneficiary lists verification. Distribuitions will be carried out in close coordination with local authorities. To avoid duplications of beneficiarie mentioned in the government list, verification is deemed necessary.1.5 Shelter kits distribution. After assessment (1.1) and verification (1.4), distributions will be carried out. It has been estimated that emergency shelter kits will be provided for 620 households in the District of Montepuez, 620 households in the District of Metuge and 80 households in the District of Ibo. The kits will be composed of 13 wooden sticks (80 cm), 100 bamboo sticks, 3 kg of 3” spikes, plastic rope, 1 tarpaulin.1.6 NFI kits distributions. After assessment (1.1) and verification (1.4), distributions will be carried out. It has been estimated that NFI kits will provided for 3,700 househoulds in the District of Montepuez, 3830 households in the District of Metuge and 1887 households in the District of Ibo, and 1400 in the District of Pemba. The kits will be composed of a solar lamp, basic kitchen tools and a blanket.1.7 Cash-for-work (CFW). CFW is foreseen by the proposed action with a twofold aim: on the one hand, CFW props up livelihood activities, in particular through the cration of improved cookstoves production groups women-led. On the other hand, CFWa aims to enable basic agriculture infrastructures, and maintenance works within and outside the newly established accommodation sites whereas host families have been affected by people displacement to due the conflict.1.8 Post-distribution monitoring. All the distributions will be monitored by means of digitalized surveys (Kobotool).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Potential harms and risks may arise in the general access to re-allocation procedures, access to services provision, with special concerns towards vulnerable categories, such as un-accompanied minors, female headed households, elderly, people with disabilities, victims of abuse. AVSI will ensure participation of all groups within the decision making process and governance designing of the re-allocation/re-settelement area. Also, through constant monitoring – both through the said multiple CFM and routinary HHs assessments – and sensitizstion campaigning on protection standards, PSEAH, GBV prevention the community will be capacitated and well informed on rights and prerogatives, as well as on referral and complaints systems. To avoid the major protection risks of resistance, exploitment and/or conflict between IDP and host community consultation meeting will carried out in order to create a community engagement comprehensive mechanism.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project itself is based on the strong participation of the beneficiaries for the activities implementation. Before the beginning of the project, focus group activities will be planned to understand which are the beneficiaries expectations concerning project impact. These meetings will be organized and held in close coordination with local leaders and the existing government structure (provincial, district and administrative levels) and at community level. Firstly, community’s opinion on how to adapt the general aspects of activities implementation and coordination according to local culture, customs, beliefs and capacity, will be taken into account and incorporated into the work methodology. It is worthy to note that AVSI has adopted an internal policy on accountability to Affected People on the March 23th, 2020 based on the IASC Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In the present project proposal, the participation of people with disabilities have been included. During their implementation, the detection of people with disabilities will be reported (disaggregated by gender and age) and given special attention. This translates into prioritizing the abovementioned target population as to the assistance for Shelter, NFI and Protection, especially for those cases having motor disabilities to which AVSI Foundation will provide logistics support. Moreover, it is worthy to note that very often people with disabilities are discriminated within their own communities. This can be a compounding factor when people with disabilities arrived in a host community, as in the case of IDPs. AVSI Foundation will ensure the implementation of sensitization sessions throughout its activities to ensure that these people are supported within the community and given special attention when coordinating and planning activities with local authorities and partners.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

AVSI aims to promote a sustainable and healthy environment in order to improve the wellbeing of the community. Given the experience of AVSI Mozambique in energy efficiency-related projects, method to save charcoal and wood during cooking demonstration will be also promoted so that to sensitize the target population on preventing deforestation that in turn impact positively on family finances by saving wood and charcoal. In addition to that, the use of local materials will be prioritized in order to adopt an environment-friendly approach, by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. AVSI can rely to an internal Policy on environment on May 11th 2020.
