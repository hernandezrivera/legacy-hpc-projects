Summary:
Overall goal of the proposed project is to “increase civilian security by protecting lives and property through removal of explosive remnants of war and sustainable risk education”. During the suggested twelve months of the action, this will be achieved through two main objectives, namely by improving safe access of local communities to land and infrastructure through land release and disposal of explosive remnants of war in suspected contaminated areas; and improving the safety of local communities through increased knowledge and promotion of safe practices on risks associated to ERW. One Multi-Tasking Team will compile Survey and Hazard Report Forms, collecting information from the local communities regarding recent accidents, suspected and confirmed HAs, use and ownership of land, and local security dynamics. Findings from the assessment will support the prioritization of contaminated areas for clearance, to be conducted in line with international standards, national guidelines and DCA SOPs. During the action, DCA will handover to local communities at least 150,000 smt of land released from ERW threat, benefitting approximately 15,000 beneficiaries, including men, women, boys and girls. In support of the CL/RE component, one team will conduct RE awareness sessions, with trainings of Community Focal Points and Peer Risk Educators. Selected representatives among adult and children groups will be trained to deliver safety messages to their families, peer groups and communities, and to identify dangerous areas, and reporting potential hazards to relevant authorities and mine action stakeholders. For this project, DCA plans to reach 9,000 beneficiaries with RE awareness sessions, equally divided among opposite gender and adult/children. Moreover, DCA will train, monitor and support 150 CFPs and 150 PREs in the targeted villages, equally divided among male/female representatives.
Considering the prioritization of humanitarian needs, contamination severity, accessibility and DCA experience in the country, DCA will target contaminated areas in Former Central Equatoria (Juba county) and Eastern Equatoria States (Magwi and Torit counties), in coordination with MA stakeholders and pending approved tasking from UNMAS as to avoid duplications. Finally, field operations will be pending security clearance, as to minimize risks for DCA staff, beneficiaries and other stakeholders.
As for targeted groups, beneficiaries will include host community, IDPs and/or returnees, based on priority areas of humanitarian need. Through a participatory approach, DCA will ensure all community groups are involved in the project, paying a specific attention to groups most at risk, such as children, and most vulnerable, such as women.

Needs

Decades of conflict, still ongoing, has littered vast areas of South Sudanese land with landmines, Explosive Remnants of War (ERW), Unexploded Ordnances (UXOs), and cluster munitions. In addition to the risk posed to their safety, ERWs prevent the people’s safe movement, including those fleeing from violence or trying to return to their homes; preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid; and inhibit socio-economic development by injuring community members and making inaccessible local land, resources and infrastructures. As result of recent fighting and decades of previous conflict, former Central and Eastern Equatoria States are among the most contaminated areas in South Sudan, with respectively 200 and 124 registered open hazards out of 534 reported countrywide (data IMSMA updated September 2018). ERW exposed due to floods during the rainy season and small arms proliferation also contribute to spread death and injuries across the local communities. In the two former states, 960 and 168 individuals reported injured due to ERW accident, but the full extent is unknown due to lack of complete information. In addition to impacting the lives of local communities, displaced and returnee populations are also susceptible to this threat, as they are often not familiar with the threat of ERW’s in the local context. Thus, the removal of mines and ERW is a fundamental step in minimizing insecurity, improving access to resources, and securing protection of access to basic rights.

Activities

In line with the overall goal to “increase civilian security by protecting lives and property through disposal of explosive remnants of war and sustainable risk education”, the action shall include two main components, namely land handover to communities through release and clearance of suspected hazardous areas, and Risk Education awareness sessions and trainings. Triangulating inputs from contamination severity maps, beneficiary vulnerability information and community feedback during field surveys in suspected areas, the Multi-Tasking Team team will prioritize and proceed with clearance operation in contaminated areas. During the project duration, DCA plans to benefit at least 15,000 beneficiaries with hand over of at least 150,000 smt of land released from ERW threat. Crucial will be the Community Liaison and Risk Education component, providing awareness sessions about the risks associated with the presence of ERW. In the targeted villages DCA will engage at least 9,000 beneficiaries in RE awareness sessions, with the training of 150 Community Focal Points and 150 Peer-Risk Educators. Trusted members of selected villages will be trained as CFPs, acquiring the capacity to pass safety messages to other community members, while identifying and reporting to Mine Action stakeholders and local authorities the presence of eventual hazards. CFPs are selected on voluntary basis, receiving information materials to support their outreach efforts in targeting their families, peers, and communities. Similarly, selected among volunteers and children representatives identified in collaboration with school teachers and principals, children in local schools will be trained to be PREs. DCA will provide PREs with knowledge and materials to engage their peers in spreading safety information to avoid the dangers of ERWs in their surroundings. CFPs and PREs are paired per location, to reinforce the outreach of the action, and the CFPs will provide support and supervision to the trained children in case of reporting. Moreover, DCA will follow up on trained beneficiaries with monitoring activities, tracking eventual reporting of dangerous items and sharing relevant information with MA stakeholders for further investigation and eventual clearance. All activities will be conducted in line with DCA SOPs, international standards and national guidelines, ensuring progress is regularly documented and reported to the relevant mine action stakeholders. Proposed duration for the action is 12 months. After the finalization of recruitment, training, accreditation and procurement, the team will be deployed and conduct clearance and RE activities throughout the project duration. DCA will ensure that monitoring activities are included in the operations plan, including needs assessment, post clearance survey, pre/post KAPBs, house-hold surveys, as to document the action contribution to the relevant HRP objectives.
