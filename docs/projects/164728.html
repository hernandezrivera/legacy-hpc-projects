Project Summary

This project aims to provide health services directly and indirectly to the affected People, IDPs and host communities, Considering the area fragmentation in Syria due to the conflict, and the scattered health facilities; the support of an ambulance system will allow a maximization of the remaining functional health resources to continue providing life-saving services to the patients, including:
1. Increasing the coverage scope of medical evacuation using the ambulatory system.
2. Enhancing the efficient utilization of the referral system through applying TRIAGE to hospitals to emergency cases only.
3. Enhancing the response speed by linking the ambulances’ systems with a communication network that would contribute to a better use of existing resources and increased coordination.
4. empowering the health directorate in managing the referral system By sharing one communication network and the same operating room to avoid all types of duplication and overlapping
The project aims to support running of existing ambulatory referral system with 20 ambulances and 40 paramedic & 40 drivers.
The ambulances will works (in Idleb and Aleppo) in the most crowded areas with IDPs
all the ambulances are equipped now with all the medical equipment’s and devices needed for first aid and critical cases.
the ambulances will be distributed in 5 work branches in Idleb and Aleppo.
and there is fully coordination with the health directorates in the need assessment and in the implementing of the project and distributing the ambulances among the communities
there will be a main center of the system which will be at of the emergency room in the health directorate building, from where all the operations will be managed,
and we will use the same communication system with the health directorate to direct the ambulances to targeted areas or to the response to the calls of the hospitals to refer the patients.
The staff of each ambulance are 2 drivers with basic first-aid experience and 2 paramedics with advanced first aid experience
The twenty ambulances will work daily 7 days a week and 24 hours a day.
The system will work to mobilize the critical patients from the field or between specialized hospitals or to transport critical patients to diagnostic centers and to refer to the Turkish hospitals
All services will be provided free of charge and there will be no discrimination based on age, gender or race.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team are responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
•Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued in monthly basis.
•Satisfaction survey among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
•Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project-term.
The project activities will be coordinated with other actors including partners through the referral TWG meetings and health cluster meetings, in addition to the regularly meetings with IHD and Hatay health directorate, our ambulance system is a part of the central ambulance system in Idleb so we have a full coordination with all parties working in the same field, and for the community within project areas we are coordinating with the local councils through regular visits will be conducted by the ambulance system administration manager.

4 - Needs

AHF conducted a needs assessment in Apr. 2019:The level of referral and ambulance services provided in the targeted communities: - 97% said that the ambulances’ staff have a good experience and dealing with medical cases. - 79% said the speed of response is fast. - 90% of respondents said 24 hours per day (Full Time) is the best work system. - 52% said the level of ambulance equipment is good and 42% said it’s medium. Need for additional ambulatory systems to support hospitals in referral and ambulance services: - 35% said hospitals’ ambulances don’t cover the needs and they need the support from the ambulatory systems. - 34% said not enough but it can deal with the needs.- 31% said hospitals’ ambulances are fully cover the needs of the hospital. - Suggestions from KIIs: * Working to find a unified landline number for ambulance and referral servises. Increasing the number of ambulances and staff and provide the ambulatory systems with ICU ambulances. Adding a ambulance center in Jabal Alzawiyah. Developing ambulances equipment to include monitor and ventilator in all ambulances.Difficulties & challenges when going to medical facilities: - 10% of women said they won’t accept transportation by ambulance with male staff, 30% said they accept transportation with male staff but would feel embarrassed and 60% said they accept and have no problem as it an exceptional condition related to the treatment. - 0% said they don’t face any difficulties or challenges when going to medical facilities. - 33% said the high transport costs is the first challenge. - 26% said the lack of transportation is the first challenge. - 19% said there is no nearby medical facility is the first challenge. - 22% said the security challenges is the first challenge. - 42% of patients are not complete treatment when they were referred to another hospital due to lack of transportation or its costs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: average, effect: high mitigation: hiring a female staff of paramedics to deal with female patients –Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism and activating the role of the M&E team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with these problems. -Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: average, effect: high mitigating measures: Employing female staff to ensure that services are being provided women–Ensuring that the wheelchairs are available for elderly and disabled people - Activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems. 3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: average, effect:high mitigating measures: A comprehensive services mapping of the area and selecting accessible and safe locations of ambulances. Monitoring the risks: Post-monitoring/ feedback forms are used. Resources Required: Monitoring staff. Acceptable Level of Risks: Yes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The availability of Maintenance & spare parts of cars The availability of the qualified trainers Minimal or no team change The ambulance system using TRIAGE as the ideal criteria to sort injuries, Clashes air strikes bombing the targeted area as the main reasons of the injuries in the area, whereas, in case of resettlement, the system will act as the standard first aid and referral systems. Availability of the current system including the personnel and the infrastructures is highly critical to operate. Risks: Escalation in hostilities in project location Not enough qualified local staff Low cooperation and competing NGOs . Market prices change continuously inside Syria, local currency fluctuates against USD Core members leaving the project before completion The invasion of the area leading to collapsed humanitarian and medical situation, besides the unavailability of the resources, human and infrastructure to operate the system, further, the disability to maintain the existed ambulances or replace it. Mitigation: The AHF Health S Program Manager will be responsible for the ongoing monitoring of the local security situation and will communicate with all personnel any changes in the conflict which require reactionary activities to minimize disruption. Project proposal will thoroughly evaluate the existing availability of staff working within the ambulatory system and surrounding areas to ensure the proposal is realistic and salable. Continuous coordination with existing health structures and NGOs in that area, conducting a baseline survey to ensure service areas not covered by other agencies. Tendering in both Syria and turkey Using USD as main currency for project transactions Allow a margin of price fluctuation in budget Terms of the contract allow for the replacement to be found in time
