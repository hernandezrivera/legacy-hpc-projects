Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to improve the self-reliance and resilience of the communities in Yirol East and Awerial Counties resulting from the consequences of conflict, acute food insecurity, poverty, basic needs and poor access to WASH services by 2025. They are empowered to cope independently with crises and to prepare for recurring stresses and strains. The aim here is to mitigate the negative effects of crises and enable them to gradually find ways to deal with their impacts permanently through structural changes. Following the principle of promoting participation and self-help, the development capacity of the affected people and structures is strengthened and promoted in a targeted manner.
Transitional development assistance focuses on strengthening resilience on three levels: individual, household and community/region (subnational level). In order to strengthen the resilience of people and local structures, three capacities should be strengthened. Stabilisation capacity enables people to meet their basic needs, and enables structures to maintain their functionality, thereby ensuring their survival in times of crisis. Adaptive capacity empowers people and structures to adapt to long-term changes, mitigate negative impacts and, ideally, minimise them. Transformative capacity promotes structural change to create sustainable livelihoods by tackling the root causes of vulnerability (power structures, economic status, etc.). The proposed intervention builds on the latest lessons learned from the leading initiative in South Sudan, which prioritizes: 1) participatory community engagement to align with local needs and build trust, 2) strengthening resilience efforts in rural areas, 3) utilizing flexible, community-centered accountability, and 4) exploring collaborative funding models with local actors to support community-led projects for sustainable impact
This will be achieved by delivering the community WASH service to 14,483 beneficiaries in Yirol East and Awerial Counties. The activities include; the identification of targeted Communities for nutrition support for Water through in-depth rehabilitation of water points, strengthening/improving water infrastructures in IPC4/5 hotspots, flood-resilient designs, motorized water distribution systems (generator, solar or hybrid), construction of new water points in underserved areas, establishment of water committees to ensure systems are self-sufficient and sustainable, etc. For sanitation: infrastructures constructed with solid blocks, self-sustained faecal sludge management systems, community/household-based maintenance systems, CLTS, etc. Establishment of water/hygiene committee for the sustainability of infrastructures; Hygiene promotion campaigns; WASH NFI distribution (targeted or blanket depending on context); Disease-specific preventive and control measures as per cluster response guidance (i.e. AWD/cholera, Ebola EVD, Hepatitis E, etc.).

1. Needs

Over the past three years, the observed trend of South Sudanese requiring humanitarian assistance significantly increased through 2021 (8.3 million) to 2023 (9.4 million) with a slight drop in 2024 (9 million) largely driven by continued conflicts, widespread flooding, acute food insecurity, hyperinflation, poverty, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services.The October IDSR wk 39 and EWARS data revealed that Lakes State is experiencing high disease outbreaks like ARI, AWD, Cholera, EBS, Malaria and Guinea Worm cases among returnees and IDPs. The targeted counties (Yirol East and Awerial) with GAM prevalence of 28.2% and 8.2% GAM rate. About 62% of South Sudanese lack basic WASH services (HNO,2024). Many returnees and IDPs continue to drive protection risk for children (Boys and Girls), and women while acute food insecurity due to conflicts, climatic shocks, high population of IDPs and returnees, poverty, poor macroeconomic conditions led incessant food shortage and poor household nutrition. The proposed interventions align with the Standard Allocation Strategy 2024, initiating the improvement of the resilience of the communities in Yirol East and Awerial Counties resulting from the consequences of conflict, acute food insecurity, poverty, basic needs and poor access to WASH services The response also adheres to the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2024, aiming to provide critical sectoral essential services by the cluster objectives for WASH. This project is dedicated to promote the resilience of the communities in Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial Counties resulting from the consequences of conflict, acute food insecurity, poverty, basic needs and poor access to WASH services.Contribution to Cluster/Sector Objectives: The planned response aims at promoting the resilience of the communities in Yirol East and Awerial Counties line with the WASH Cluster 5 Main Specific Objectives (SOs); CLWSH/CO1) Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition; CLWSH/CO2) Contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality; CLWSH/CO3) Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster; CLWSH/CO4) Disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility; CLWSH/CO5) Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster.
