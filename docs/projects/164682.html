Project Summary


Using its rapid response mechanism, NGO X’s proposes an action of emergency and winter food and NFI distribution, with pre-positioned in-kind and cash assistance, with the latter being the preferred modality. NGO X will refer highly vulnerable beneficiaries in need of support outside NGO X’s mandate to specialized community services in line with the Protection sector guidance.
These activities have been informed by Rapid Needs Assessments (RNA) conducted in Idleb on November and December 2019 as well as an RNA conducted in North Aleppo in December 2019 as well as lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and other assessments conducted by both NGO X and other actors.
NGO X will implement the proposed action in partnership with NGO Z. Please note that NGO Z will input into the OCHA HPC separately. This decision was agreed between both parties and submissions have been coordinated to ensure there is no duplication with regards to either budget or beneficiary targets. NGO X's partnership with NGO Y is based on complementarity of modalities and geographical coverage. NGO Y has broad access across North Aleppo in Al bab and Jarablus. NGO Y has the capacity to deliver assistance through electronic platforms which is in line with NGO X’s delivery mechanism strategy.
The proposed activities outlined below will target approximately 30,597 households in Idleb and North Aleppo.
Important Note: this is an integrated response mechanism which includes Multipurpose cash Assistance (MPCA), RTE, Food and NFI kits.
Project budget across sectors is as per below (based on the SMEB):
• FSL: 70 %
• SNFI: 15%
• WASH: 15%
The cash value of the MPG will be aligned with the SMEB. The currency applied is USD. NGO X has already secured funding to target 20,597 HHs and are awaiting donor approval of funding for the remaining 10,000 HHs.

4 - Needs

These activities have been informed by RNAs conducted in Idleb in Nov and Dec 2019 and in North Aleppo in Dec 2019 utilizing affected people surveys to collect data as well as lessons learned from previous projects, monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and assessments conducted by NGO X and other actors. RNAs are conducted by NGO X, as part of every emergency response, using the RNA template developed for emergencies programme. They determine the place of origin of the affected population, BNFs’ current location, shelter situation, protection risks faced, and the estimated number of affected HHs, as well as basic information on multiple sectors, IDPs' priority needs, and whether any key items are available in local markets or not.FOOD: NGO X 's assessment in Idleb shows an average of 64% of respondent HHs achieving an acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) (42 or more); 29% achieved borderline score, and 7% had a poor score (28 or less). IDP camp residents in North Aleppo also mentioned food among priority needs. SHELTER: Damage to shelter and concerns over shelter not offering adequate climatic protection were referenced by 88% ( NGO Z) and 53% ( NGO X ) of survey respondents. MARKETS: NGO X’s assessment in Idleb shows that 82% of respondents did not face any difficulties reaching the market and 98% did not face any shortages of items in the nearest markets NGO Y’s assessment in Idleb and Aleppo showed similar market accessibility trends. WASH: In an RNA conducted in camps in North Aleppo in Dec, NGO X found that 3% of respondents didn’t have access to clean water and 33% of respondents didn’t have access to hygiene items. In Idleb, 100% of respondents said they didn’t have access to hygiene items and 33% said they didn’t have access to clean water. COPING STRATEGIES: Of the 85% of HH reporting food shortages in Idleb, the most common coping strategies cited included relying on less expensive foods and prioritising child feeding.

5 - Implementation of activities

This action aims to meet beneficiaries needs in relation to food, NFIs and WASH (as per the SMEB) through in-kind supplies, multi-purpose cash, or a mixed-modality response. Cash transfer is the preferred modality where markets are accessible and functional and security conditions allow. Responses will be designed to meet a family's needs for 1 month. When a shock occurs, NGO X will conduct an RNA. NGO X’s primary method of response will be through utilization of multipurpose cash transfers as long as markets are functional, cash is accepted by communities and security conditions allow. The cash value of one-off cash transfers will be based on the harmonized SMEB, developed by the CWG as the minimum required culturally-adjusted items for survival for a HH of five for one month. Detailed activities as per the sector/cluster framework are outlined below. 30,597 households will be reached through the proposed action. NGO X will carry out distribution monitoring (DM) activities and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of all distribution responses under the proposed action. NGO X’s distribution monitoring teams will carry out the distribution monitoring process in all distribution sites. For Distribution monitoring (DM) Sample size: distribution monitoring (DM) visits are carries out by the MEL team, distribution sites were randomly selected with consideration to the program team’s daily distribution plan with aim to cover 60% of the distribution site. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow NGO X and partner staff to carry out distributions and monitoring activities- NGO X 's strategies and mechanisms to prevent armed and sanctioned actors from interference in programmes that violate the JOPs remain effective- NGO X can continue to support cross border activities Risks:- Continued military offensives presents the risk of death or injury to NGO X staff damage to or destruction of NGO X premises) or assets; reduced access to programme areas and of border closure- Continued targeting of certain opposition factions in the AoO, presents the risk of those factions directing their suspicions at NGO X- Remote management presents the risk of staff losing legal status; sudden border closures disrupting operations- Data breach of beneficiary data occurs Mitigation strategies:- Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures are in place, and an experienced security team - Ongoing communication with actors operational across NGO X 's AoO - Maintain regular communication lines with Turkish authorities- Ensure that beneficiary selection and verification processes remain rigorous - Beneficiary data is encrypted and saved on secure servers. Access to the system is restricted. - Creates awareness about its complaints and response mechanism and solicits feedback in diverse ways, verbally shares information at distributions, incorporates it into MOUs it signs with vendors, suppliers and councils and posts written information. NGO X tracks the number of complaints it receives by type and location and measures the length of time between receipt of the complaint and response., ensuring that all communications received are registered in its database and receive a full response
