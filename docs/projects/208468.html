Project Summary

PUI will support 1 PHCC and Community Based health program within Pulka, Gwoza LGA. The Health Facility (HF) and the community base health program will deliver services in line with the Nigerian Essential Package for health services including integrated management of childhood illness (IMNCI), EPI services, disease surveillance, prevention and malaria treatment as the most common cause of morbidity and mortality in U5 children, support 24/7 BEmONC services (Antenatal care (ANC) and Postnatal Care (PNC) will be provided by skilled birth attendants at HF). Family planning (FP) services will be provided and promoted in communities, whereas follow-up will be done for ANC and EPI defaulters. Clinical management of rape (CMR). PUI will pay MoH staff incentive and encourage community and SMoH participation in facility management. The iCCM program will complement HF services by engaging Community Health Workers (CHWs) which are community volunteers who will be trained on the iCCM. Volunteers are selected at community level to ensure that they have the capacity to identify, screen, provide treatment, manage U5 children with fever, cough, diarrhea within their villages and conduct referrals to the health facility.

1 - Needs

According to the famine monitoring system analysis, Gwoza LGA, where Pulka is located, has the highest crude mortality rate (CMR) and under five mortality rates (U5CMR) with 4.66 deaths/10,000persons/day and 9.27 deaths/10,000/under-fives/day recorded in the northeast. These indicators are above the emergency threshold of 1/10,000/day for CMR and 2/10,000/day for U5CMR recommended by the sphere standard. Pulka PHC receives around 687 outpatient new consultations monthly. Further analysis showed that service utilization rate is low, around 31%, even though majority of people already are in poor living conditions in the area are prone to diseases. It is worth mentioning that Pulka population has water access issues, and it affects trend of communicable diseases especially water-borne diseases. As of 2021, about 1,001 cases (39 deaths) of cholera were reported.

2 - Activities

PUI will support 1 Primary Health Care Centers (PHCC) to provide comprehensive Essential primary healthcare services that includes Outpatient Consultation, EPI, ANC including HIV testing services and PNC, referral services and BEMOC services including Clinical management of rape. ANC and PNC and will be provided by skilled birth attendants. Early ANC will be promoted to ensure women complete Intermittent Preventive Therapy (IPT) for malaria prevention during pregnancy and increase ANC2 coverage. During the third trimester, the action will provide safe delivery kits for each pregnant woman living over 5KM from the nearest HF.PUI will procure essential medical supplies for the static health facility and community-based health programing. PUI will also collaborate with WHO and UNFPA for medical supplies and equipment through the core pipeline. PUI will provide incentives to staff to provide essential primary health care based on the standard staffing requirements for the level of service delivery. All HWs will be trained on IPC and waste segregation bins will be used in ANC clinics and labor and delivery units to separate medical waste. All collected waste will be disposed in the facility incinerator which are locked to prevent access. MHPSS case workers also work with the CHW to provide awareness to survivors of GBV. SGBV referrals will be made through different partner agencies using the Inter-Agency Individual Case Referral Form which forms a part of the Clinical Rape management.Through the iCCM program, the community health workers will be trained to provide treatment to children under the age of five years using the IMNCI guideline.Community Health Workers (CHWs) will conduct awareness raising sessions in the community on the need for women and adolescents to access ANC, delivery and PNC services at HF.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

This Action will comprise part of a multi-sectoral intervention coordinated jointly via OCHA. PUI health program is implemented under the guidance of and collaboration with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH). Active referral system, exchange of resources, clinical expertise and coordination exists between PUI, WHO and UNFPA through pooling of resources and expertise to maintain program quality standards following the national guidelines. Subject to their availability, through its linkage with key health actors, the action will be supported with gift in kind (GIK) assistance from core pipelines including vaccines and supplies; HIV testing supplies; mosquito nets and malaria drugs; UNFPA reproductive health kits. Similar to previous years, PUI will continue to be an active member of the Humanitarian Response Plan, attending multi-sectoral coordination meetings with other actors. PUI will build the capacity of the MOH staff to provide services in the health facility beyond this project lifespan.

Throughout the course of the implementation of this action, PUI’s Complaints, Response and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) will be employed to ensure meaningful participation of beneficiaries and create effective channels for feedback to be incorporated into program implementation. These will be done through weekly MEAL-led face-to-face sessions; recording and escalation of complaints and feedback through community committees; use of hotline phone number, complaints and feedback boxes as deemed fit by program beneficiaries and other community members.
