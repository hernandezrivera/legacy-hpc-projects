Project Summary

(Planned – SO1 )
The project intends to expand its provision of integrated community-based and individually targeted protection interventions to conflict-affected populations across Northwest Syria. The Project aims at enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs providing protection, and Child Protection and response services to most vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls, prioritizing critical gaps in life-saving protection services, and designed to be people-centered project supports self-sustainable protective environment for the most vulnerable IDPs, host communities and returnees in targeted areas.
Miner Al-Sham organization will provide the child protection services in schools through 4 outreach mobile teams.
The project targets 4000 boys, girls. Women, and men through mobile outreach teams covering 18 camps surrounding sites. The community in targeted locations will have access to community-based protection services, including awareness-raising over general protection, Child Protection with adherence to cluster SOPs.
The project will contribute to helping to create a safer and more secure environment for children in targeted locations, In line with the strategic priorities of 2020.
Main project activities are:
• Conduct rapid child protection assessments in target locations.
• Run recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in learning spaces.
• Awareness-raising activities for children, parents, humanitarian workers and community members on child protection concerns, focusing on following topics (Child Labor, Family tracing and reunification, Basic CP, Child Rights), using IEC materials, home visits and advocacy with service providers
• Provide parenting skills program for parent
• Identification, and referral of vulnerable children for sector-based services based on needs.
Training on CP, child safeguarding and Psychological First aid for CP and Education staff

4 - Needs

The HNO 2019 identified that protection remains a great concern in Syria. These concerns are varied and inter-related and therefore must be met through inter-linked protection services. Specifically, children continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks: death, injury, involvement in armed groups, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access. These risks affect children’s wellbeing – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. It is estimated that over 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk. Minber Al Sham conducted rapid assessments in the 18 camps of Jan 2020 in those camps to verify the number of population and to check on the available services to ensure no overlapped activities are included in this project.
