Needs

GC has been working in Atmeh since early 2016. Our staff continue to monitor displacement trends and humanitarian access, as well as assessing needs on the ground as part of ongoing programming; information gleaned from continuous monitoring has informed the activities and overarching strategy presented for this program.

Implementation of activities

From our office in Gaziantep and sub-office in Qah, GC will be responsible for overall program strategy and design, reporting, grant administration and compliance, procurement, vendor and sub-award payments, monitoring and evaluation (M&E) and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC), and capacity building of staff. Program staff will oversee direct implementation of all proposed activities. Our Syrian staff will enter Syria as appropriate to monitor program implementation.

Protection Risk Analysis

Consistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines in implementing activities. The Program is based on the "Do No Harm" principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. In settings where humanitarian needs are extreme and resources are scarce, GC recognizes that providing any material assistance has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns, or to negatively impact local economic conditions. We will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from program resources by maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation; and promoting open community engagement and transparency in beneficiary selection criteria and processes, and materials distribution and assistance. GC incorporates beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanisms into program design. Our existing mechanism includes providing beneficiaries with feedback forms and contact numbers when assistance is distributed. We publicly post our anonymous feedback hotline number at public and program locations (such as child-friendly spaces and women and girls safe spaces) in target communities and in Atmeh, so that community members may send feedback to us by phone, SMS or WhatsApp. We provide the hotline number to individual HHs during beneficiary verification, assistance distribution, and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) visits to each HH, to ensure that the most vulnerable or disadvantaged have access to the information and the ability to submit his/ her own feedback.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The award will include approvals as requested by GC at the time of proposal submission forrestricted items such as long-term vehicle rental and equipment (i.e., generators).• The Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are opened andaccessible;• GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations;• Active violence does not directly impact GC's offices in Gaziantep, or Qah for a sustained period of time;• Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for deliveries of humanitarian aid, and allowing GC to maintain acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality;• Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance; and• GC is able to obtain appropriate export/re-export license renewals in time to allow for program implementation according to the approved work plan.
