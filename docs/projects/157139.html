Project Summary

Kassala is one of the most vulnerable states in Sudan to extreme weather phenomena, droughts and floods in particular. Long periods of drought cause loss of crop and livestock, reduce the amount of groundwater, wear down forests and increase the risk of wild fires.
The consequences on the livelihood of the local population are disastrous and add to the already precarious living conditions.
Kassala region is one of the poorest and most marginalized in the country despite the good potential for agriculture and livestock and is location nearby important commercial hubs.
Kassala bases its economy on agriculture and pastoralism.
The recurrent and prolonged periods of drought, accompanied by sporadic torrential rains that cause flooding and destruction of the vegetation cover, increasingly deteriorate the subsoil, reducing its capacity to absorb water.
In addition, the quality of the soil has been ruined by the mesquite, a plant introduced in the '80s for its fertilizing properties.
The local diet is very poor (consisting mainly of sorghum and vegetables) and the parcels of land are too small to meet the needs of families. Communities therefore depend on the purchase of food at the market, where prices are extremely volatile and rise dramatically as a result of droughts or floods (which cause the loss of crops and livestock with a consequent reduction in the supply of products on the market). This is aggravated further by the devaluation of the local currency.

Needs

Following field visits and meetings with the institutional representatives of the Ministry of Health, Ministry of Agriculture (Ministry of Agriculture, Irrigation, Forestry), SDWC and Ministry of Animal Resources and Fisheries (Ministry of Animal Resources and Fisheries), HACs and local communities (displaced and resident), the following needs of the population of the target locations of the proposal have been identified:1. Need to increase the amount of water available through rehabilitation and upgrading water structures including deep wells (MWY) and surface wells equipped with hand pumps.2. Need to improve the management of the installations by the members of the WASH Committees and the SDWC;3. Need to improve knowledge, attitudes and hygienic-sanitary practices (to increase the health and nutritional status of beneficiaries);4. Promote the adoption of environmentally friendly practices through awareness campaigns on the sustainable management of natural resources, with a view to increasing the resilience of local populations and reducing risks and disasters.5. Need to improve and diversify agricultural production through:- Distributions of seeds and agricultural tools;- Livestock feed distributions;- Training on new sustainable agricultural techniques.6. Need to improve breeding through:- Livestock supply;- Improvement of veterinary services

Activities

Result 1 WASHA1.1- Rehabilitation and upgrading of an existing water scheme with the installation of solar pump and distribution systemA1.2 Rehabilitation of four surface wells for agriculture and distribution system (Hand Pump)A1.3 Rehabilitation of 8 MWY deep wells with related distribution facilitiesA1.4 Creation, formation of committees for the management, maintenance and maintenance of water structures, follow-up and distribution of small equipmentA1.5 Implementation of 13 H & S awareness campaigns for beneficiaries of water schemes (including students from beneficiary communities' schools) Result 2: FSL and resilienceA2.1 Implementation of actions to strengthen agricultural activity: supply of agricultural tools, cereal seeds, vegetables and fruit treesA2.2 Implementation of interventions to strengthen pastoral activity: large-scale vaccination of livestock (goats and sheep)A2.3 Implementation of reforestation interventions, through the creation of nurseries and fodder banks, environmental education and mitigation of the effects of climate change

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project livelihood component specifically the livestock health services related activities (vaccination & treatment) have a potential negative environmental impact unless proper waste disposal measures are taken. Accordingly, the proposed project recognize the risk and put in place proper handling of wastes during and after the planned vaccination and treatment campaign.
