Project Summary

The proposed project will target 26,600 people (school children, teachers, PTAs and education officials and improve equitable access to safe and protective quality education services for 26,111 (boys=14,361 girls=11,750, boys with disabilities=1320, girls with disabilities=971) emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) in 87 schools (including primary, ALP and ECD) in Upper Nile (Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk), Warrap (Tonj South), Eastern Equatoria (Budi), Western Equatoria (Yambio) and Central Equatoria (Terekeka) states through the implementation of the following activities.
Establish 28 disability inclusive Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019, rehabilitate 14 disability inclusive TLS/schools , establish 84 stances (42 blocks) of gender sensitive and disability inclusive latrines according to INEE and sphere standards, rehabilitate 28 blocks (56 stances) of gender sensitive and disability inclusive latrines according to INEE and sphere standards, provide dignity and Menstrual Hygiene Management kits to 5828 adolescent girls, provide learning materials to 4700 learners, teaching kits to 300 teachers, provide Early Childhood Development kits to 2068 children and recreational materials to 4700 learners. The project will train 300 teachers for 5 days on Education in Emergency, basic pedagogical skills, classroom management, Psychosocial Social Support, Child Protection and referral mechanisms. It will further train 154 PTAs/SMCs and 35 education officials on Psychosocial Social Support and Disaster Risk Reduction in education, provide Information Education and Communication materials with prevention and mitigation messages on Child Protection, disaster risk reduction in education to 189 PTAs/SMCs and education officials. The project will also facilitate 14 (2 per county) back to learning campaigns (BTL) to encourage out of school children to return to schools.
ADRA has presence in all the targeted locations and identified critical education gaps that this intervention will respond to. The intervention is designed to build synergy with other existing ADRA interventions in Food Security, Education and emergency response. For instance some of the targeted schools benefit from school feeding and school gardening programs that ADRA implement in partnership with WFP., and will therefore benefit from complementary activities in the proposed interventions. In most of the counties targeted, ADRA implement in-service teacher training and continuous professional development for teachers, construct/ rehabilitate permanent classrooms and TLS, for which the proposed intervention will address gaps( additional TLS rehabilitation etc.) ADRA is the state anchor for GESS in Warrap and Eastern Equatoria and also implements (in partnership with UNICEF) teacher continuous professional development in Warrap, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria states. We also implement NORAD funded education projects in Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria, work with WFP in food distribution, school feeding and food for Asset in Warrap, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria. ADRA also implements SSHF projects in Western Equatoria and Upper Nile states. In designing this intervention, ADRA has taken into consideration gender, age, disability and diversity mainstreaming to carter for the needs of the diverse community groups targeted. ADRA has ensured that the proposed project will be phased out into existing and planned ADRA interventions in the targeted locations.

1 - Needs

Effects of protracted conflict and flooding in South Sudan resulted into displacement of communities, loss of property and lives and, food insecurity and low government support to social services such as education. COVID 19 pandemic that resulted into lock down and closure of schools in 2021 exacerbated an already dire humanitarian situation in the country. Education cluster nationwide assessment report (2021) recommended rehabilitation of infrastructure, teacher salaries, and school grants as key to school functionality. The assessment also indicated that 40.9% of the assessed schools have classrooms that are not accessible for children with disabilities while 59.5% of the schools reported that their latrines were no accessible for children with disabilities. The report also highlighted that 40% of the schools lacked referral mechanism while only about a quarter reported having a functioning general protection referral mechanism. The report further highlighted that COVID 19 school closures resulted into increase in protection cases such as early and forced marriages, early pregnancy. Budi is one of the counties facing critical education gaps due to inadequate education partners supporting the county. An Education Sector coordination forum established in the county brought together education actors in October 2021 who identified high dropout of girls as a result of early pregnancy/marriage, inadequate qualified teachers, few female teachers available, lack of school feeding in some schools resulting into low enrolment and attendance, lack of scholastic materials for learners, teaching materials for teachers and recreational materials as gaps.According to ADRA SS needs assessment in Yambio Payam in October 2021, about 12,762 individuals from Tambura were displaced into Yambio county and settled mostly within Yambio Payam. About 4,000 IDP children from Tambura integrated into host community schools in Yambio. The critical educational needs for both the IDPs and host community children included learning materials, classrooms/TLS, shortage of latrines and shortage of recreational material incentives for teachers and inadequate WASH facilities. Cases of GBV such as early marriage and rape were also reported as affecting women and girls. Although the conflict in Tambura has subsided at the moment, the IDPs will not be going soon and will still require support in Yambio county.An education assessment conducted by ADRA in Tonj South in October 2021 identified inadequate WASH facilities in schools, inadequate scholastic materials, , low capacity of teachers, low community support to education, teachers not getting their salaries in time and as such are demotivated and inadequate learning spaces. Women and girls also faced GBV cases such as rape, forced marriages, early pregnancies among other. The assessment identified about 6400 children in need of education services. An ADRA assessment in Maiwut, Ulang, Nasir and Longechuk in July 2021 identified the following needs: Teachers incentives, low capacity of teachers, inadequate learning spaces, lack of teaching, learning and recreational materials, inadequate WASH facilities and lack of ownership and support to schools by parents. Terekeka is one of the neglected counties in Central Equatoria State. It suffers from inter – communal violence and flooding. A needs assessment conducted by ADRA in February 2022 identified inadequate latrines and handwashing facilities in schools, inadequate teaching, learning and recreational materials, few qualified teachers, delayed payment of teachers’ salaries and inadequate learning spaces/classrooms as the main issues in education. In all the assessments conducted, ADRA identified that Early Childhood Development is a neglected priority that needs to be addressed to carter for children under five years. It was also established that the participation of elderly people in education discussions and decisions especially in the PTA/SMCs is low.

2 - Project activities

The proposed project will work in 5 states and 7 counties and target 26,6000 people (school children, PTAs, teachers and education officials. It will strive to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 26,111 school children (boys=14,361, girls=11,750, boys with disabilities=1320, girls with disabilities=971) emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). It will further more improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments and finally strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities by undertaking the following key activities:Establish 28 disability inclusive Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019, rehabilitate 14 disability inclusive TLS/schools , establish 84 stances (42 blocks) of gender sensitive and disability inclusive latrines according to INEE and sphere standards, rehabilitate 28 blocks (56 stances) of gender sensitive and disability inclusive latrines according to INEE and sphere standards, provide dignity and Menstrual Hygiene Management kits to 5828 adolescent girls, provide learning materials to 4700 learners, teaching kits to 300 teachers, provide Early Childhood Development kits to 2068 children and recreational materials to 4759 learners. The project will train 300 teachers for 5 days on Education in Emergency, basic pedagogical skills, classroom management, Psychosocial Support , Child Protection and referral mechanisms. It will further train 154 PTAs/SMCs and 35 education officials on Psychosocial Social Support and Disaster Risk Reduction in education, provide Information Education and Communication materials with prevention and mitigation messages on Child Protection, disaster risk reduction in education to PTAs/SMCs and facilitate 14 (2 per county) back to learning campaigns (BTL) to encourage out of school children to return to schools. The BTL events will bring together education stakeholders to raise awareness so that out of school are encouraged to enroll in schools. In all the activities, ADRA will provide special attention to the needs of girls and ensure they have access to mentoring services by matrons to give them skills to avoid early marriages/pregnancy, access to dignity and menstrual hygiene kits and gender segregated WASH facilities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Efforts will be made to conduct focus group discussion with a group of persons with disabilities to get their feedback and also through engaging their organizations/leaders to get their feedback. People who are not literate can be engaged in focus group discussion and key informant interviews using local languages to get their feedback. We shall use female teachers to get feedback from girls, conduct FGD and KII with women and girls to get their feedback. The project will be collect feedback from the community taking into consideration the most disadvantaged members of the community such as the elderly, persons living with disability, women and girls. For instance during community dialogue sessions and FGD sessions to collect feedback, every effort will be made to include these marginalized groups. The community will be sensitized on the vulnerability criteria and informed the percentage of representation for the most vulnerable groups. Women and girls will form 60% of the organized community dialogues to collect feedback. Also ADRA will mobilize homogeneous groups sessions to ensure women can have a free environment to speak up and voice their feedback whenever it may seem they cannot speak freely in a mixed group.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will make community aware of the existing systems for representation of their different interests and needs. We shall also establish Accountability for Affected Population Committees who will be oriented and will participate in project reviews. In each school/community, the project will establish a community complain and feedback mechanism (complaint and feedback desks in schools), at RRC offices etc and by placing suggestion boxes in schools, initiating community focus group discussions with ,members of the community. Complain forms and hotline will also be provided where necessary. Project staff will also be trained on how to best serve beneficiaries and be accountable. Beneficiaries will also be sensitize on their rights and entitlement in the project sites. The community members were already involved in the design of the project and will be engaged during implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the planned and on - going activities. The project will conduct awareness session with the affected community to inform them about the project, feedback/complaint mechanisms. Through focus group discussions, the project will collect views of the community on the preferred methods of communicating complaints and receiving feedback. The project will also use informal community dialogue sessions to listen to the community complaints and provide feedback for the complaints that are addressed. IN the project locations, complaint/feedback committees will be established and trained to receive complaints from the community and be another medium to communicate feedback to the affected community. Age gender and diversity will be considered while collecting complaints and providing feedback

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

We shall train beneficiaries on PSEA and constantly create awareness through the use of IEC materials/banners, community meetings. PSEA will also be a content during project inception and reviews, monitoring and reporting.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries will be incorporated in review of the project to ensure we address the issues raised and provide better services based on the feedback. Through out the project cycle, ADRA will seek to get feedback from the beneficiaries about the satisfaction of the project and collect feedback on their needs and how best they can be met. From the consultative discussions, ADRA will use the information to inform the relevant interventions including and not limited to choosing when to conduct activities (to accommodate the needs of women, girls, the elderly), the type of supplies needed etc. Any complaints registered will be investigated and addressed in a manner consistent with humanitarian principles and standards.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: This will be mitigated by conducting multi - sector GBV safety audit to identify associated risks and agree with women and girls on the appropriate time of activities, train project staff, community leaders and government stakeholders on PSEA and other forms of GBV. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: This will be mitigated through dissemination of the complaints and feedback mechanisms to beneficiaries and stakeholders and monitoring the use of the complaints mechanism, as well creating awareness on the accountability mechanism to the communities. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area: This will be mitigated by creating awareness on mine risk education and working with local authorities and stakeholders to map out areas with explosives and either advocating for the removal of the explosive or sealing such areas off from use by beneficiaries. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: This will be mitigated through setting a clear selection criteria in consultation with all stakeholders and conducting selection in a transparent and accountable manner.
