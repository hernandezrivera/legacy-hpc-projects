Project Summary

Information on the humanitarian situation in Eastern Syria is difficult to obtain and access to affected communities remains the main challenge.Humanitarian actors are reliant upon one-time and remote assessments like the SINA and the MSNA and ad hoc data from partners.Frequency and quality of current information flow are not consistent.There is general recognition among partners that Eastern Syria contains many sources of information,many are only accessible from the ground.This project will help OCHA Turkey(and relevant coordination stakeholders through OCHA Turkey)to tap into those sources of information/data,verification/fact checking of information,aggregate and compile them into reports on the humanitarian situation in Eastern Syria.We propose to run a team of field officers and assistants in consultation with OCHA Turkey in Eastern Syria as Community/Service Observers.Field team will be managed by supporting team based in Gaziantep,Turkey.We'll share the reports with OCHA Turkey on a periodical basis within detailed criteria about requested information and will be further shared with senior leadership and relevant stakeholders and mechanisms including RHC,DRHC,clusters,ICCG,HLG,SSG,ISG,Access WG and other relevant for a.The project aims to:1-To provide a better understanding of humanitarian status in Eastern Syria through an independent and neutral source of information and add another layer to current information sources(clusters,SINA,MSNA and working groups).2-To establish an early observation program for emergency humanitarian cases in Eastern Syria,to help OCHA Turkey and cross-border clusters and coordination mechanisms based in Turkey to respond quickly and effectively.3-To conduct ad hoc visits on THF funded projects on ad hoc basis and as per the request of THF for simple fact-checking.
Project goals will be achieved through the following:1.1-Recruitment & equipment procurement.1.2-Staff training in Gaziantep, Syria and any other relevant location.1.3-Interview and liaise with key informants, including local administrative Authorities, local NGOs, relief offices, community leaders,local media sources that the Field Assistant deems appropriate to contact.1.4-Where possible,the Field Assistant will engage with local authorities and key stakeholders on humanitarian access issues.1.5-Support local communities,civilian administration entities and humanitarian workers in Syria to have a better understanding of Humanitarian principles through training delivery,supervised by UNOCHA Turkey.1.6-Project support staff in Gaziantep will liaise and coordinate with Field team and engage with a similar set of key informants based in Turkey,or in Syria via the internet.1.7-Field visits to follow up THF funded projects upon request of the THF,clusters,OCHA and the humanitarian leadership channelled through the project coordinators.1.8–Support the Turkey-based clusters, coordination mechanisms and humanitarian leadership in their outreach to local communities within the AAP framework including the establishment and field check of relevant feedback mechanism on quality of aid delivery.Reports about community issues and perceptions will be shared with relevant coordination and collective feedback mechanisms through OCHA.Outcomes will be followed up through the following indicators: Field team will I.1.1send weekly reports during the period of project OCHA Turkey includes (but limited to):collected data from field visits,focus group discussions, and key informant interviews.I.1.2provide project coordinators in Gaziantep with contextual analysis on Humanitarian situation and Humanitarian Access. I.2.1provide emergency reports as required about emergency and humanitarian response. I.3.2 Provide project coordinators in Gaziantep with visits’ report.1.3.3.Deliver training when possible and in close with OCHA Turkey to selected targeted groups.1.3.4.organize and support outreach of cross,coordination mechanism to affected community in SYR

Needs

Information on the humanitarian situation in eastern Syria is difficult to obtain. Currently, OCHA Turkey is reliant upon one-time and remote assessments, such as the SINA and the MSNA, as well as ad hoc data from partners. While useful, the frequency and quality of current information flow are not consistent. At the same time, there is general recognition among partners that eastern Syria contains many sources of information, of which many are only accessible from the ground.

Implementation of activities

The project is designed to address Coordination SECTOR OBJECTIVE 1” Provide effective coordination support across response modalities and a reinforced response” and in-line with activities no. 1, 2, and 3 1. Support inter-sector/cluster coordination through the provision of timely and regular information around the humanitarian situation and the implementation of THF funded projects 2. Provide the tools and resources to enhance common situational awareness of humanitarian needs and principled humanitarian response, and enable more informed decision-making 3. Support the strengthening and establishment of mechanisms in the framework of AAP This will be done by implementing the following activities: Activity 1.1: Interview and liaise with key informants, including local administrative Authorities, local NGOs, relief offices, community leaders, local media sources, and any other sources of information that the Field Assistant deems appropriate to contact and share relevant information with clusters, coordination mechanisms and humanitarian leadership Activity 1.2. Conduct Field visits to follow up THF funded projects upon request of the THF, clusters, OCHA and the humanitarian leadership channelled through the project coordinators Activity 2.1.1: Where possible, the Field Assistant will engage with local authorities and key stakeholders on humanitarian access issues Activity 2.2: Support local communities, civilian administration entities and humanitarian workers in Syria to have a better understanding of Humanitarian principles through training delivery (supervised by UNOCHA Turkey). Activity 3.1: Support the Turkey-based clusters, coordination mechanisms and humanitarian leadership in their outreach to local communities within the AAP framework

Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with communities related to location; 3.Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to site locations. The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-family tents and multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of intimate partner violence and other forms of domestic violence. Where situational and risk analyses are not systematically conducted, these risks might not be identified and rectified. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe. B) Consultations with women, girls, boy and men should be organized, c) community safety audits.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1- Military operations in the area and clashes based on scenarios anticipated by the NGO. 2- The employee may be kidnapped Assumptions: 1- Secure the field staff to fulfil the tasks within the project period 2- Secure the information and equipment within the project period Mitigations 1- The NGO developed a contingency plan and the steps of implementation 2- The NGO developed and secured duty of care policy 3- The NGO revised and kept the risks scenarios updated
