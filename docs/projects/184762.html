Project Summary

With a total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022, about 2.9 million people are estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country, Somalia has one of the highest numbers of IDPs in the world. Of these, 2.2 million require urgent humanitarian assistance. IDPs are chronically more food insecure and vulnerable than host communities. Displacements are typically rural-urban in nature, where displaced people moving into urban centres often lack the skills required for urban livelihoods and IDPs and rural migrants are frequently confined to poor-paying unskilled jobs, if any at all. As with IDPs, their needs are driven by pre-existing vulnerabilities, as well as recurring multiple shocks. Poverty is pervasive, particularly in rural areas and areas where access remains a challenge, and the current estimates of 71% of the population living below the poverty line are expected to remain at similar levels for 2023. Afgooye/Awdhigle district in Lower shabelle region have a total of 320,985 people in need of food assistance and of these 102,715 are in stress and 218,270 in severe livelihood situation . FSNAU and FEWS NET Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. Despite multiple shocks in 2021, high levels of sustained humanitarian assistance and government support have played a critical role in stabilizing and preventing worse acute food insecurity outcomes in Somalia. Based on results of assessments conducted in July and August 2021, approximately 3.5 million people are currently facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or 4) higher outcomes through December 2021 in the presence of humanitarian food assistance.
sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance and government support are currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2021. The 2021 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2022 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly.
SRC project will therefore, intends to provide immediate access to unconditional and conditional cash assistance to 6,498 most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men including disable population in IDPs and host communities, it will also provide capacity enhancement, farming tools as well as farm inputs to these target farmers in Awdhegle-Afgooye in lower Shabelle and Ceelbur district in Mudug as well as well as agricultural seed inputs and tools to increase their farming production thus improving the nutrition of the target population. The project will provide through cash for work will enable to provide immediate access to household income thus improving the overall well-being of the target communities. SRC will ensure to coordinates the foods security and livelihood activities closely with other humanitarian actors and concern ministries at regional and federal level to ensure better information sharing among the south west and Galgadud regional states in the target district

1. - Projects activities

1.Provision of emergency unconditional cash transfer to 1083 Household (3,498 IDPs and 3,000 HC individuals) vulnerable IDP and host community households considering 15% of the beneficiaries registered as vulnerable disable women and children household in Lower Shabelle and Galgduud regions. Each household will have received 95 USD per months for 3 months’ period. The unconditional cash transfer will provide immediate life-saving and life-sustaining opportunity among the vulnerable communities in emergencies hence this will be enhancing the household food basket thus improving the livelihood. 2.Provide cash for work activities to 100 men and women household heads and enable create short term job opportunities and create income to the vulnerable drought displaced and AWD affected IDPs and host communities to enable improve the community livelihood structure hence provide alternative livelihood. The CFW beneficiaries will work 26 working days per month for three months and they will be provided 6 USD per person per day. SRC will ensure inclusion of 10% disable beneficiaries and minorities in the cash for work activities 3. Conducts community awareness and mobilization to ensue the community memebers understand the project feedback mechanism thus reduces the risk of beneficiaries mismanagement 4.Conduct training to 79 lead farmers (30 men and 40 women ) on proper preparing, harvesting as well as management of farm inputs storage hence enhancing their knowledge and thus inculcate skills for their livelihood, the farmers will be provided both farm input and farming tools to ensure enhanced capacity and management of their farming, the farm input and the farming tools distribution will largely target female headed household as well as the disable group to ensure inclusivity of the affected ppopulation

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

To minimize the risk of beneficiary’s assistance SRC will engaged local money vendors such as Hormud and will ensured selected beneficiaries were provided with unique cards for verification and post distribution monitoring purposes. The cash will be transferred through mobile company/financial service provider which will be selected based on availability of network and/or services, readiness and ability to provide a prompt delivery, and reasonable service charge this will further reduce any beneficiary Cash diversion to anywhere else except to the intended and selected one . This service will be purchased locally and in accordance with SRC policies, SRC propose that providing unconditional cash assistance and conditional cash grant is the most appropriate intervention at this immediate response stage and will allow beneficiaries to priorities their most pressing need, and by use of mobile technology will both easy the business of doing work as well as reduces the risk of cash involvement. This will further provide adequate attention to both disable population , the vulnerable IDPs host communities as well as minority group

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

According to SRC monthly market assessment report for the months of sept, 2021, there indicated a high raising of the cost with the Somali shilling inflation going upwards against USD and this this intervention will be based both market survey and needs in the target locality while aiming the market bases intervention to responds to IDPs and host communities affected by drought coupled with the COVID-19 pandemic and locust infestation that have hindered the livelihood of the already in crisis communities in South central Somalia, SRC will ensure provision of life-saving and life sustaining cash response to the affected population in Lower Shabelle and Galgadud region target location through vouchers for unconditional and conditional cash transfer to vulnerable household. Providing a market based approach also strengthens the sustainability of the action by ensuring the operation and maintenance of the assets by either the targeted communities’ relief Committees.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing permanent structure or own a land for food security and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, SRC will ensure IDPs and disable group have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing cordial relationship between them and with the host communities and local authorities. Based on the food security and livelihood Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced, disable, minorities group and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities. Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. SRC intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneficiaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and food security and livelihood infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, SRC will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the cash collection sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women SRC will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement on better livelihood. Building community structures including local temporary spaces will have provided in consultation with community/member who owns the land to ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities seeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination to vulnerable disable and minority population
