Project Summary

"Since the arrival of the Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar District in 2017, Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas have suffered the challenges of massive amounts of waste generated in an area where had no functional systems of waste collection. While in the refugee camps, Solid Waste Management (SWM) systems have immediately been set up by WASH partners of the international humanitarian response, host communities have suffered the major consequences of waste threatening the health of people and the environment. In 2019 UNDP initiated a project to establish basic systems of waste collection in the most affected Unions of Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas (Raja Palong and Palong Khali, Whykhong, Nhilla and Baharchara and Teknaf Municipality) and 27 camps in Ukhiya. However there are still major gaps and needs that the new project proposes to address/reinforce (see section needs). Based on the lessons learned of that ongoing initiative, the current project aims at supporting the local government to lead quality and community-driven service, with particular attention to gender, age and people with disabilities barriers to participate in the SWM systems in place, and continuing the waste management operations for final waste disposal and collection of recyclables in the refugee camps.
Based on the lessons learned of that ongoing initiative, this project aims at approaching with the same solution the remaining 7 camps in Teknaf Upazila and operational maintenance of camp 20 ext.
PROJECT GOALS:
1) Technical Support to the local government and wash sector to establish basic systems of waste collection and safe disposal and build their administrative, financial and governance capacity to take over the overall management of waste collection, recovery and generation of green job opportunities.
2) Supporting the District level authorities (DPHE, WASH Sector) to operate and maintain the large SWM facility camps for waste recovery and final waste disposal.
3) Supporting the camps transportation of waste to the sanitary landfill to ensure proper and safe disposal of waste, and recyclables to the recycling sector in host communities.
4) Improve and consolidate the business modality and marketplace of the Recycling sector, as well as the camps access of the association members. Zero tolerance will be applied to child labour within the recycling waste collection.
5)Proper collection and management of solid waste significantly contributes to reduce the impact that solid waste causes to the environment. SWM also contributes to mitigate climate change "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will work in close collaboration with WASH Sectors, local government, and other relevant stakeholders to support DPHE overall solid waste management strategy. The project will contribute to improve camp-based coordination for landfill access at the camp level. So, it will contribute to improving waste management service delivery for Ukhiya and Teknaf camps. The project closely works with the RRRC office to get access for the camps and other government departments. UNDP is aiming to work closely with the WASH sector coordination platform to improve the SWM systems and Sanitary landfill activity in the camps, including the construction of a new Sanitary Landfill in Teknaf camps. In host communities, UNDP will work with the WASH sector in improving the coordination, communication, and prioritizing of the SWM issues in the local government agenda. UNDP will also monitor the SWM systems established in host communities including the financial modality to identify the feasibility of future funding options.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

" UNDP’s experience indicates that refugees and host communities are both very much aware that their conditions are unsanitary and sub-optimal. In fact, many people from host communities and refugee camps are hyper-aware of these conditions, changing their daily route to avoid bad smells or reduce their exposure to local waste. Such awareness naturally brings with it a sense of shame or frustration due to a lack of effective service provision in the community but may also augment a sense of helplessness many already feel in regard to their circumstances. One person alone can do very little to clean his or her community – without coordinated and effective management and support, many local people likely feel powerless in the face of the sanitation and health challenges too large and complex to manage alone.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The proposed solid waste management project will help to rehabilitate areas of the local environment which have been damaged by improvised and unplanned waste disposal, improving the local water quality and restoring key natural areas. Waste contributes to the damming of waterways, contributing to the formation of stagnant pools which hinder movement, increase flood risk, and contribute to the erosion of fragile grounds. Clearing waste and providing basic waste management systems will have a critical impact on the daily lives of the vulnerable people who must navigate these areas daily. Improved waste management systems are critical investments towards protecting the few remaining forests and biodiverse areas in the district.
