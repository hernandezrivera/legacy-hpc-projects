Project Summary

Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) objectives, NADA aims to mitigate the risk of WASH-related diseases among populations affected by displacement. The organization will enhance the capacity of these communities to adopt safe practices, fostering improved hygiene behaviors and thereby reducing health risks associated with inadequate WASH practices. Employing a variety of technical approaches, NADA will provide the necessary infrastructure for secure water access, enhanced hygiene, and safe sanitation.
Target Group:
NADA's headquarters will focus on the most vulnerable individuals and communities affected by crises, including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities. The WASH interventions planned by NADA in the BAY states aim to benefit 26,500 individuals (15,000 females and 11,500 males). The targeted beneficiaries encompass:
Internally Displaced Persons: NADA will address the needs of vulnerable individuals displaced due to various crises in the BAY States, including those affected by multiple displacement situations with limited WASH services, individuals in protracted displacements, host communities, and IDPs residing in collective buildings with restricted access to WASH services. This also includes those who are unable to return to their areas of origin for security reasons or have not consented to reintegration in displacement areas.
Returnees (including refugee returnees): This category includes IDPs returning to their original places of displacement.
Vulnerable Host Communities: NADA will support vulnerable families within communities hosting at least 50% IDPs compared to the host population. The organization recognizes that competition over limited resources over time can lead to tensions, causing further harm.
Persons with Disabilities and Those with Special Needs: This group comprises individuals with disabilities, those with special needs, and the elderly. NADA anticipates that by bringing water points and sanitation facilities closer to these populations, beneficiaries will be less exposed to protection risks and will have more time for income-generating activities for women and school attendance for children.

1 - Needs

In accordance with WASH Sector data, a total of 5.1 million people (59 per cent children, 22 per cent women and 19 per cent men) require WASH services across the BAY states, compared to 3 million people the previous year. Of these, 1.13 million are IDPs, 1.21 million are returnees and 2.77 million are host community members. While, generally, the needs of IDPs have been higher than those of host community members, most disease outbreaks, especially cholera, have occurred in host communities where a lack of water and sanitation have been more prevalent. In 2022, heavy flooding resulted in displacement and high rates of acute watery diarrhoea, severely impacting access to safe water, dignified sanitation and hygiene awareness. This has led to negative coping mechanisms, including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation and poor personal and household hygiene. During the flooding, the situation in over-congested camps deteriorated significantly, limiting access to safe sanitation. The cost-of-living crisis and inflation have limited households’ ability to procure soap and other non-food items, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under five at high risk of diseases. Access to menstrual items is still lacking in over 90 per cent of households, according to the MSNA. Menstrual hygiene disposal has been a continuous challenge and sector partners are advocating for re-usable sanitary towels for women and girls In the BAY states, crucial Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) challenges persist. Acute water shortages prevail in various locations due to distant water points, unprotected sources, and a scarcity of containers. Water truck vendors are the sole water source in some areas, leading to reliance on unsafe sources during the dry season. Returnees encounter difficulties accessing safe water, facing long queues. Rampant open defecation raises concerns about diarrheal diseases, exacerbated by limited space for emergency sanitation. A majority lack adequate soap, with low handwashing rates. Gender-segregated latrine use requires community sensitization, hindered by poor access and disregard for gender markings. In 2023, WASH needs are projected to increase for a variety of reasons, including continued displacement, flooding and the increased cost-of-living.Further disease outbreaks during the rainy season, especially for acute watery diarrhoea and cholera, will increase WASH needs among affected communities. Various locations in the BAY states still have acute water shortages and rely on water trucking, as seen in Damboa, Monguno and Dikwa. The dry season will increase the need for water, which, in turn, will force communities to resort to unsafe water sources. The needs among 2.9 million children, 1.1 million women and 985,000 men will continue to rise due to population growth and increased population movements to locations with minimal services, which place further strain on scant resources.

2 - Activities

hygiene: NADA aims to foster hygienic practices by: Delivering culturally appropriate hygiene messages tailored to gender and age groups in affected communities, aiming to minimize waterborne diseases through sensitization sessions. Establishing WASH Committees, providing maintenance tools, and creating School Hygiene Clubs. Training Hygiene Promoters, conducting promotion activities, and preparing for cholera responses. Distributing hygiene, WASH NFI, and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, along with contextualized Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials. Conducting safety audits on WASH facilities and consulting key informants for feedback.sanitation: Enhance sanitation access by ensuring gender-segregated facilities for affected populations, including: Ensuring safe, dignified access to improved sanitation through desludging and cleaning of latrines as per sector standards. Constructing new sanitation facilities (latrines, showers), rehabilitating existing ones, and maintaining these facilities to standard. Installing handwashing stations with soap in public facilities and institutions. Constructing gender-segregated sanitation facilities (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) in institutions and public areas per sector standards and in consultation with the community. Providing waste bins, disposal pits, and managing garbage transportation to disposal sites. Establishing and training community-based water management committees for boreholes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NADA commits to mainstreaming protection principles in crisis response, integrating humanitarian safeguards to prevent unintended negative impacts (do-no-harm). Our approach prioritizes needs, emphasizing safety, dignity, and local empowerment. Grounded in participation, we hold ourselves accountable to affected communities, ensuring their needs are met and human rights upheld. NADA will fully embrace the concept of Protection Mainstreaming and Centrality of Protection, delivering impartial humanitarian assistance without regard to legal status. All affected individuals, regardless of their status, are entitled to our support and protection.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

This project is designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and based on lessons learnt from our previous implementation in the target LGAs. NADA shall use a people-centered feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach will be used to form a LGA level project committee that will include all community groups' representative and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process. NADA project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the already existed community-based project structures to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. NADA will also use a feedback mechanism through focus group discussions to gather perceptions of the project beneficiaries and community members on the services rendered which will form part of the discussion in the project implementation review meeting with the community-based project committees at the end of the project.
