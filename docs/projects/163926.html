Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its SNFI intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA) of affected population in NW Syrian, need severity for SNFI assistance, and HNO2020key messages, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and SNFI Cluster. The project will address SNFI needs for totally 59,370 INDV (27,925M/ 31,445F – 15%PwD) of the most vulnerable people of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees in NSAG controlled areas in Aleppo and Idleb GOV, including provision of 150 short-term livelihood opportunities through Cash for Work modality (workers will be selected based on eligibility and vulnerability criteria)
First component will contribute to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support targeting 53,520 INDV (25,156M/28,364F). In this component, BO will respond to the emergency or high severity situations such as new displacement waves, natural hazards, or critical shelter adequacy; especially in hard-to-reach areas, last resorts, and informal settlements. As for mentioned situations, BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date SNFI needs data (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). As for the response to NFI needs, BO will procure core and essential 2,000 NFI kits, 5,000 seasonal and supplementary winter kits (including heating fuel), and 1,500 sealing-off kits. Next, BO will distribute the kits, based on the vulnerability criteria, to ensure that the affected people have their core and essential NFI support, and they are protected from harsh seasonally conditions. The kits will be customized to meet different age and gender needs, i.e. children (<5), PwD, and elderlies (>59) may need diapers, or females in reproductive age will need sanitary pad. Likewise, BO will respond to Shelter needs through rehabilitation of 12 collective centers and 300 unfinished buildings by CfW (70 workers) to shelter the most vulnerable people to ensure that they are provided with shelter support for short/ medium–term
Second component will contribute to reinforce an enabling protection environment and cohesion of communities by improving housing targeting 5,850 INDV (2,769M, 3,081F). To achieve this, BO will provide shelter rehabilitation/ repairing support, through CfW (80 workers) to increase shelter adequacy for the most vulnerable people of landlords and tenants, with considering specific needs of all age and gender groups to increase safety and dignity, and to decrease protection risks or GBV. Along with the rehabilitation, BO will provide essential trainings to members of LCs in the project area as well as to BO staff targeting 150 INDV (100M, 50F) in order to equip them with proper capacities and skills in field of HLP due diligence, resilience and quality-oriented shelter, and basic legal contracting
Throughout the project, BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with SNFI Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP verification will be included in case of providing shelter support). As for the rehabilitation works, BO will assess project area to select most suitable buildings, then prepare BoQs and SoWs for the buildings, and developing the layouts to meet SPHERE Standards with considering community norms and protection risks, especially for women and girls. Principles of Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected People are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation

4 - Needs

Developments from mid-2019 in Idleb and Aleppo Gov have increased SNFI severity across NSAG territory that is already overburdened with limited shelter and hosting capacity. Besides, plummeting of SYP has a negative effect on purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their SNFI needs, especially during harsh weather - potentially causing households to resort to harmful coping mechanisms Also, HNO2020 shows that female headed HHs, youth, elderly and PwD have reduced access to basic services including shelter, and reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to SNFI. Each group has had a unique experience of the crisis and are often exposed to different risks and vulnerabilities. Women and girls as well as PwD face distinct protection risks, including gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse In NW Syriao Shelter adequacy is a critical issue in most communities, especially HHs living in collective centers, informal camps, managed camps, and transit camps face much higher rates of adequacy issues, of which 83% report facing three or more adequacy issues. Common issues are lack of lighting inside or around the shelter, lack of heating or insulation from cold, lack of privacy and inability to securely lock their homes. However, shelter and repair materials are too expensive; repairs require professionals but cannot afford the costso NFI needs remain substantial, particularly the high numbers of newly displaced in need for core, supplementary and seasonal assistance. Also, communities require resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability to withstand future shocks and stresses. While market access appears to be gradually improving still items are unaffordable such as hygiene kits, cooking fuel & utensils, heating, clothing, lighting sources, mattresses and blanketso Aleppo: Shelter PiN: 1,291,693 /NFI PiN: 554,553o Idleb: Shelter PiN: 932,434 /NFI PiN: 804,687

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about SNFI needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators.CLSHL/CA1: provide 2,000 HHS ~ 12,000 INDV (5,640 M/6,360 F) with core and essential NFI kits, including mattresses, blankets, cooking utensils, solar light, plastic sheets and hygiene kits. CLSHL/CA2: provide 5,000 HHS ~ 30,000 INDV (14,100M/15,900F) with seasonal and supplementary NFI kits, including winter clothing, shoes, and heating fuel. CLSHL/CA3: provide 1,500 HHs ~ 9,000 INDV (4,230 M/4,770 F) with standard winter Sealing-Off-Kits (SOKs) including construction materials, tools, fixings, and personal safety equipment.CLSHL/CA4:• Rehabilitation of 12 collective centres for 120 HHs ~ 720 INDV (339 M/381 F)• Rehabilitation of 300 unfinished buildings for 300 HHs ~ 1,800 INDV (846 M/954 F) from informal settlements.• The activity will include installing partitions, installation of doors and windows, wiring, plumbing works, insulation and painting.CLSHL/CA5: 950 houses will be rehabilitated targeting 950 HHs ~ 5,700 INDV (2,679 M/3,021 F), of which 250 FHHs. Rehabilitation works will include; wall maintenance, installation of doors and windows, wiring, plumbing works, and painting. CfW Modality will be used to implement CA4 and CA5 activities to provide short-term livelihood opportunities for 150 INDV. CLSHL/CA6• Bonyan will conduct training for 150 INDV (100 M, 50 F) who are members of local councils and BO staff.
