Project Summary

According to FSNAU technical release in Sept 2021 the projection does not look good with anticipated below-average Dyer (October-December) rainfall hence worsening trend projected. Based on the results of household (HH) surveys and field assessments conducted in June and July 2021, more than 2.2 million people face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in the presence of planned and likely humanitarian assistance during the July to September 2021 period. An additional 3.4 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 5.6 million. From October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and, other food security-related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. Without sustained humanitarian food assistance, 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021. An additional 3.7 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million.
Rural populations in particular are experiencing multiple declines in food and income sources. The 2021 Gu harvest in southern Somalia is 60 per cent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2020. It is the third lowest Gu harvest since 2010. The lower production in 2021 Gu mainly reflects the impact of the poor rainfall, ongoing civil insecurity, river floods, shortage of farm inputs and pests. In pastoral and agropastoral livelihood zones, although heavy rains between late April and mid-May partially replenished pasture and water resources, these resources are inadequate to support normal livestock production until the start of Deyr season rainfall in October. Furthermore, floods caused further population displacement and damaged crops and farmland in riverine areas of Hiraan, Shabelle, and Juba regions. A majority of the estimated 2.9 million IDPs across Somalia are poor with limited livelihood assets, few income earning opportunities, low communal support, and rely heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, one-third of internally displaced persons (in both rural and urban settlements) face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021.
The project will strive to contribute to Improve access to food for 3.1 million of people facing severe acute food insecurity including million IDPs, 2 million non-displaced people and persons with disabilities in 64 districts. CARE will target 6,500 households with 3000 households with UCT and 3500 CCTs.
Cyclical climate shocks and ongoing conflict are challenging traditional rural livelihoods and causing
growing number of urban poor and internally displaced people in Somalia.
Displacements due to armed conflict and political tensions, drought, and lack of livelihoods are expected to continue in 2022, further exacerbating food insecurity in many areas.
Protection concerns are likely to increase for all groups, mostly manifesting as negative coping strategies, especially for young women and men from extremely poor households as household food security situation deteriorates. In agropastoral livelihood zones, where the main shocks include erratically distributed rainfall and conflict, poor households have experienced substantial crop losses and low income from agricultural employment. With few alternative sources of food and income after current food stocks are depleted, they face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021.

1. - Projects activities

Unconditional cash transfers; This project will provide unconditional cash grants to the most vulnerable households, to minimize the social and economic impact of the triple crises among the worst affected communities. The project aims to support with 3000 households representing 18,000 individuals for a period of 3 months. The purpose is meet the immediate food needs of the affected populations and builds their resilience through unconditional cash transfers. CARE will work with reputable cash transfer companies with clear procedural agreements to ensure the protection of beneficiaries and other standards. Cash will be delivered safely via the robust hawala system and, where possible, though mobile money transfer - as recommended by the Somalia Food Security Cluster and the cash working group- so as to ensure beneficiary privacy and reduce risks to staff and beneficiaries. CARE will develop beneficiary selection criteria while prioritizing pregnant and malnourished women who are GBV survivors, non-pregnant GBV survivors, female headed households affected by COVID 19 from IDP communities. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) exercise will be practiced to every month of the cash transfer to know its impact.Conditional cash transfer; The project will undertake 4-month (64 days in total) Cash-For-Work (CFW) activities to provide temporary income opportunities for communities in 3,500 households in 6 regions who have experienced shocks due to climate-related disasters, floods, droughts, COVID19 and desert locus. The purpose is meet the immediate food needs of the affected populations and builds their resilience through conditional (CCT), while rebuilding damaged communal infrastructure such as access roads, health facilities and communal berkets and also laying the foundation for restoring local markets to full functionality in the future. A total of 3500 households (with estimated household membership of 21000 persons) will be engaged in micro-projects to rehabilitate prioritized roads, health facilities and communal berkets and ensure medium term recovery from recurrent shocks. Beneficiaries will work for 16 days per month and will be paid US$ 5 per day (US$80 per month). CARE will select beneficiaries with well-developed community-based beneficiary targeting and selection guidelines. This selection process will be shared with target communities to improve understanding of how CfW will work.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Somalia’s humanitarian landscape is characterized by recurrent multi-layered crises that underpin chronic poverty and vulnerability to disasters. The Country is currently facing compounded shocks from worsening drought conditions, the COVID 19 pandemic, desert locust infestation and the residual effect of the 2019/2020 floods, all of which have eroded the coping capacity of millions of vulnerable Somali communities. According to FSNAU technical release in Sept 2021 the projection does not look good with anticipated below-average Dyer (October-December) rainfall hence worsening trend projected. Based on the results of household (HH) surveys and field assessments conducted in June and July 2021, more than 2.2 million people face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in the presence of planned and likely humanitarian assistance during the July to September 2021 period. An additional 3.7 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million. FSNAU/FEWSNET reports that Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are widespread across much of the region, noting a 40% increase in households in crisis, compared to 2020. Drought conditions led to some livestock losses in northern and central Somalia in early 2021 and increased household spending on animal feed and water. Dry spells and the early end of the Gu rainfall season also caused crop losses and reduced agricultural labor income, particularly in Togdheer, Sool, Nugaal and Bari, where some of the most severe rainfall deficits in the Country have been experienced and pre-drought alerts were issued in the first quarter of 2021. In March 2021, the Puntland government raised the alarm on the prevailing drought conditions and critical water shortages with a drought risk appeal. An estimated 17% (745,741 people) of the population of Puntland was in need of humanitarian assistance (IPC3 and 4). CARE was one of the agencies that responded with emergency water trucking, Health, Nutrition, protection, education and unconditional cash assistance. CARE is an active member Somalia Cash Technical Working Group (TWG), the co-chair of Somaliland Cash TWG and a member of Puntland cash TWG which provide platform for information sharing, harmonization and resource distribution

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project staff will continuously assess vulnerabilities to identify most vulnerable members of the community, such as ethnic minorities and the disabled persons. Members of the marginalization groups will be invited in the consultation meetings. Community awareness campaigns will be conducted in order to address the barriers. The safety of the needed people covered by the project will be prioritized. Through CARE ‘s complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM), the project will provide a safe and confidential ways of receiving, managing and responding to the excluded and the marginalized communities. The project will engage the community to select sites for CFW which are near and accessible to both men and women to avoid long distances which could easily expose them to GBV. The timing of work will also be flexible for women considering they have other household chores to take care of.
