Project Summary

To strengthen the leadership of the GBV Sub-Cluster in the occupied Palestinian territory to tackle increasing rates of GBV in the context of the sustained COVID-19 pandemic and in the wake of the May 2021 escalation, through effective coordination of GBV prevention, mitigation and response interventions, identification and response to GBV gaps, trends and concerns, capacity building and technical support for both GBV and non-GBV specialists, GBV data collection and management and GBV mainstreaming in other clusters.
The project seeks to respond to the prolonged humanitarian crisis in the occupied Palestinian territory, compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic and the May 2021 escalation, by contributing to the protection of rights and dignity of the Palestinian population, especially most vulnerable groups, in accordance with IHL and IHRL, and with a cross-cutting focus with other sectors such as Health and Child Protection, especially for case management and referrals. The project is aligned with the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster as it 1) seeks to increase the respect for the IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as 2) to put in place protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation, conﬂict related violence and COVID-19.

1 - Needs

A key protection concern in the oPt, GBV has become more acute due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the May 2021 escalation. The situation has exacerbated pre-existing risks of gender-based violence (GBV), including early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Based on the MSNA and HNO people in need, 453,660 people are in need of GBV interventions in oPt. 10% of households report that women and girls avoid areas near settlements, checkpoints, community areas and markets, and using public transportation because they feel unsafe. 31% of households in the West Bank and 19% in Gaza are concerned about the safety and security of girls, while 24% and 17% respectively are concerned about the safety and security of women. (oPt MSNA, 2021) The disproportionate impacts on women and girls are underpinned by socio-cultural patriarchal norms, which continue to undermine women and girl’s rights. Furthermore, according to UNFPA, COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation resulted in an increase in reported GBV cases. SAWA, an emergency helpline, reported a 135% increase in caseload in May 2021 compared to May 2020. The context in oPt has a detrimental impact on employment, income, education, and access to services, including specialized GBV services. Women and girls, including women and girls with disabilities, at high risk GBV may be forced to adopt negative coping strategies that expose them to further exploitation and abuse. High quality GBV services are extremely limited and access is restricted due to COVID-19 and the Israeli occupation. As such, the GBV SC will prioritise improving high quality, multi-sectoral services for GBV survivors, including people with disability, highly sensitive cases (incest, rape, and sexual abuse within the family) and cyber violence against women and girls. Ensuring the availability of, and access to, life-saving GBV services is also a priority, including case management, health services, MHPSS (survivors and service providers), safe shelter and legal aid and representation. The GBV Sub-Cluster will also focus on advocacy and coordination for effective delivery of essential GBV services and a strengthened national referral system. This project will support the implementation of the Humanitarian Response Plan Strategic Objective 1 and 2. Through the Needs Overview, the GBV Sub-Cluster has identiﬁed the following key priorities: Increase high quality, multi-sectoral service provisioning for GBV survivor;Enhancing GBV advocacy and coordination to ensure high quality GBV services among the humanitarian clusters and other relevant actors. Additionally, analysis shows that certain groups have a much higher risk of GBV. These include vulnerable female-headed households, women and girls living in refugee camps, Bedouin women, women and girls with disabilities, women and girls in Hebron H2 and Area C and female farmers and heads of households. Against this background, GBV coordination is needed to keep identifying and meeting GBV priority needs in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt), including by ensuring GBV services align with global GBV minimum standards and survivor-centered, address gaps and reduce duplication in humanitarian response. It ensures that each aspect of the humanitarian programme cycle is applied as part of a joint effort that uses available resources and capacities. This role is performed by the GBV Sub-Cluster, which is chaired by UNFPA and brings together more than 60 national NGOs, INGOs, UN agencies and line ministries. The Sub-Cluster meets on a bimonthly basis in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, and ad hoc based on emerging needs. The Sub-Cluster uses the ‘Inter-Agency GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ as the guiding document for provision of services in the humanitarian setting as well as the recently published global guidelines on COVID-19 response from the GBV AoR. UNFPA as chair of the GBV Sub-Cluster has strengthened the coordination of interventions by mapping gaps in services, facilitated collaboration between government and non-government GBV service providers, improved access to GBV services through regularly updating and disseminating referral pathways and strengthened GBV mainstreaming in other clusters. Additionally, it has established and updated GBV service directories on a regular basis, promoted Protection Mainstreaming among GBV partners, conducted rapid GBV assessments in light of the pandemic and shared inputs for all OCHA Situation Reports. It has also contributed to the OCHA 4W platform, and has started the preliminary process to roll out the GBVIMS+/Primero platform as an offer to GBV partners in oPt, and in order to improve the overall crisis response. However, gaps remain, and GBV Sub-Cluster members require support to scale up emergency services, build their capacity in providing quality GBV case management, especially remotely, and provide self-case and stress management to their frontline staff in light of the recent violent conflict and the sustained COVID-19 pandemic. Lastly, support is needed for UNFPA to carry out its Sub Cluster coordination role.

2 - Activities

Given the large membership (approximately 60 organizations) of the GBV Sub-Cluster and the multi-sectoral service provision for GBV survivors coordinated under the Sub-Cluster, human resources are needed to ensure leadership, information management and coordination. In addition to this, a key objective of the GBV Sub-Cluster is to build the capacity of all GBV Sub-Cluster partners, and national organisations (women-led and women-focused) to improve their ability to provide services in alignment with GBV minimum standards, to access humanitarian funding, and to track and monitor humanitarian interventions. The GBV Sub-Cluster targets their partners with capacity development interventions to enable them to engage more comprehensively in humanitarian situations, as per the localization agenda. Working with the Protection Cluster, other Areas of Responsibility (AoRs), with the members of the GBV Sub-Cluster, and in close coordination with Al Muntada Forum to Combat Violence Against Women, WESAL Network in Gaza and the Hemaya Network in Jerusalem, the GBV Sub-Cluster coordination team will work on 5 main components:- COORDINATION AND SUPPORT TO GBV SERVICES: Provide leadership to the GBV Sub-Cluster and strengthen GBV services by undertaking day to day coordination of the platform, including convening regular meetings to identify and address GBV gaps, trends and concerns among UN, Government and non-government entities, by updating GBV service directories on a regular basis, by identifying and proposing actions to respond to priority GBV concerns and gaps, by supporting partners with resource mobilization capacity and protection mainstreaming through their interventions, including on access for people with disabilities and accountability to affected populations, by providing quality GBV inputs to humanitarian processes (e.g. HPC), by establishing a tracking tool to follow on the progress of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) implementation throughout the HRP GBV projects that receive funding and by ensuring coordination with national gender and GBV platforms and the work of Protection Cluster AoRs, especially the Child Protection and MHPSS Working Group, in order to conduct joint initiatives, fill response gaps jointly when relevant and ensure equal access to and gender sensitive protection responses.- ADVOCACY: Coordinate joint advocacy efforts, including by developing factsheets, policy papers and analytical reports on top priority GBV needs, gaps and trends, ensure the participation and leadership of the GBV Sub-Cluster in key advocacy processes such as the annual 16 days campaign against GBV, and provide GBV updates at donor briefings and protection briefings at HCT+ meetings. - CAPACITY BUILDING: Continue working with other Clusters to mainstream GBV in the overall humanitarian efforts and providing trainings to non-GBV specialists on GBV detection and referral. Upon the request of GBV Sub-Cluster partners, keep improving the quality of GBV services by organizing capacity building sessions on the safe delivery of remote GBV services, on culturally appropriate self-care/stress management solutions, as well as the GBV mainstreaming with humanitarian clusters in Gaza based on the GBV IASC guideline and the Inter-Agency GBV Minimum Standards for prevention and response in emergencies. The GBV Sub-Cluster will also undertake a GBV Case Management Capacity Building Initiative training of trainers, with the aim to provide GBV service providers in humanitarian settings with a harmonized approach to building capacity on GBV Case Management.- GBV INFORMATION MANAGEMENT: Strengthen the GBV information management system of the Sub-Cluster by increasing the number of GBV organizations sharing data in a safe and ethical manner, by updating the system in partnership with OCHA based on GBV Sub-Cluster partners’ feedback and HRP monitoring needs and by analyzing its content to inform the Humanitarian Needs Overview as well as develop GBV reports. In 2022, the GBV Sub-Cluster in oPt will be rolling out the GBVIMS+/Primero platform to improve programming and advocacy for the benefit of GBV survivors through the safe and ethical collection, management, sharing and analysis of service provision data. To that end, further capacity building of GBV Sub-Cluster partners is necessary. PREVENTION OF SEXUAL EXPLOITATION AND ABUSE: Building on the work done by the GBVSC and the PSEA Coordination Network on awareness raising on PSEA principles and protocols and integrating PSEA in the GBV national referral systems in 2021, the GBVSC will be leading the roll-out of the PSEA Victims Assistance Protocol in Palestine in 2022, in close collaboration with the PSEA Network Coordinator. The aim is to establish a common set of norms and standards based on existing frameworks to strengthen a coordinated, system-wide approach to the provision of adequate assistance and support to PSEA survivors in Palestine.

The GBV Sub-Cluster is working in close collaboration with the PSEA Network Coordinator to ensure that GBV partners’ interventions, as well as the GBV referral system is safe and accessible to all i.e. women, girls, men, and boys and other at risk groups both for GBV and SEA; to monitor GBV risks; to maintain a safe and ethical feedback mechanism for the affected population in line with IASC GBV guidelines and global minimum standards, providing an entry point for SEA reporting by the community; and to provide survivors of SEA the necessary assistance and response services, in line with GBV high quality services, that are safe, accessible and appropriate for the special needs of GBV and SEA survivors.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

All GBV Sub-Cluster partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centered Approach and the ‘GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies’ thereby prioritizing safety, respect, confidentially and do no harm, providing meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. The GBV Sub-Cluster will continue to provide capacity building to partners on these principles including on GBV Minimum Standards and safe GBV remote service delivery.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Protection Mainstreaming has been prioritized as a critical issue to be addressed by the GBV Sub-Cluster as early as 2019. The second key principle of Protection Mainstreaming, “ensuring meaningful access”, was identified as a starting point with special focus on people with disabilities (PwD). This came as a result of a discussion among GBV Sub-Cluster partners on the findings of a study conducted by UNFPA in 2019 on GBV and women and girls with disabilities, which highlighted the importance to scale up support for them as they are more exposed to violence compared to other groups and the existing services offered to them are inadequate. In 2020, the GBV Sub-Cluster partnered with Stars of Hope society in the joint review of their manual on Disability Mainstreaming in VAW programming. Efforts in mainstreaming and ensuring meaningful inclusion of PWD will continue in 2022 through the UNFPA coordination role in the GBV Sub-Cluster.
