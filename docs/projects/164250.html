Project Summary

Dorcas plans to implement integrated protection assistance projects targeting women, children, people with disabilities, people with specific needs and elderly through targeted assistance. General protection, child protection activities, PSS activities in group and/or individual sessions, as well as mine-risk education activities are planned to children, their parents, women, survivors of GBV and elderly. Services are provided by trained staff according to the social work methodology.

4 - Needs

Amongst Syrian civilians that remained in Syria, 6.2 million have been displaced, some of them already several times due to changing front lines. In this ﬂuid and uncertain situation, children and women bear the brunt of the conﬂict since many families live in dire needs and parents struggle to provide for the basic needs of their children. In the meantime, savings are depleted after several years of conﬂict and poverty is on the rise. Many men have been recruited into the armed forces or have joined opposition groups; many of them have not returned since and have left behind their wives with children in need of protection. Excess male mortality and outmigration, especially those in the 20-35 age brackets, are evidence of this trend (UN Survey, 2018). We also observed in our community centers that elderly often focus on covering needs of their children and support them to cover needs of their grandchildren. They don’t claim support even if it is urgently needed. This self-imposed marginalization can be addressed by including elderlies in services offered at community-based protection activities and through providing material assistance to them if they are vulnerable. But the dire economic situation of families has also other consequences, a frequently observed coping mechanism is that boys and girls drop out of school: boys so that they can assist in earning income, and girls taking over the domestic role of their mother (UN Survey, 2018 ). This negative coping mechanism severely hampers the future prospects of the youth and of school-going children. In the community centers already supported by Dorcas, we observe mainly the following child protection risks: Child Labour, Children having Psychosocial Distress, Violence against Children, Child Marriage also explosive hazards have been widely reported in Syria and continue to put thousands of people's lives at risk. Men/boys are considered most expose to the direct immediate threat of explosive incidents.

5 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas plans to work through local civil society actors and local NGOs through three existing community centers that are located in Hanano city, Aleppo, in the old city of Homs and in Kashkoul, Jeremana Rural Damascus. Following the social work approach, trained staff in the community centers, who are receiving people and opening social files will be able to identify individuals who could benefit from case management for CP & GBV, PSS and recreational activities. But also through outreach activities and awareness sessions, people will be identified offered with Psychological First Aid (PFA) and referred for the community center for more focused activities. During the awareness sessions on CP, GBV and MRE, staff will be able to identify women and children who are in need for more support. They will be able to offer them PFA and refer them to PSS structured sessions, case management, individual counseling. During PSS sessions, staff will be trained to identify PWI, offer them PFA and in case MRE cases need specialized services refer them to other NGOs (Terre des Hommes) for physiotherapy or other support if needed. Social workers who are also doing case management will be able to refer children and/or adults to PSS group sessions or individual PSS sessions if they see the need and to specialized services to other NGOs in the area. For elderly social activities will take place, to integrate them into the society. Because during the crisis many elderly were left alone, because their families had to flee and often left them alone with no one to take care of them. These people who refused to leave their houses, found themselves alone with no caregiver or social support.The focus of MRE activities however lies on implementing MRE sessions in targeted locations with MRE flyers/brochure distribution, hold awareness campaigns with MRE flyers/brochures distribution.psychological support, psycho-education, PSS individual we plan 8-10 sessions, PSS group sessions 6-8 sessions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Dorcas/partners will ensure training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards; 2. Dorcas/partners will work with partner agencies to ensure that services are not provided by any staff who has not received anddemonstrated proficiency in training modules; 3. Dorcas/partners will include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision; 4. Dorcas/partners will continue coaching service providers onsite and remotely;Risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1.Dorcas/partners will continue involvement and coordination with relevant authorities; 2. Dorcas/partners will train and coach social workers to ensure communication with the family is professional and not confrontational;Risks of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services; Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1. Dorcas/partners will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA; trainings for all locations are planned to take place in the first half of the year. 2. Dorcas/partners will communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended; 3. Dorcas/partners will reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE. 4. Dorcas/partners has installed a beneficiary feedback mechanism which is an important component of AAP. It is possible to alert Dorcas through, internet, hotline, complaints box.
