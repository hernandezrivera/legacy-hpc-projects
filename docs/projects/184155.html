Project Summary

Protracted political instability, continued population’s displacements, ongoing armed conflict, and enduring environmental stress have rendered many of the country’s estimated 15.7 million people to be acutely food insecure. It is projected that drought will be one of one of the primary drivers of food insecurity in the country in 2022. This is largely attributed to the La Nina condition experienced in the greater Horn of Africa region.
2021 witnessed a delayed start, early end, and erratic rainfall distribution of the Gu rainfall season as a result, cumulative rainfall was below the 40-year average across much of the country, especially in central and southern Somalia. Forecasted La Niña conditions are associated with an elevated likelihood of consecutive below-average rainfall seasons, and Somalia faces a high risk that drought conditions will develop by early 2022.
According to Post- Gu assessment done by FSNAU and FEWS NET in September 2021, 3.5 million persons would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through to December 2021, if urgent humanitarian assistance is not provided. Of this, 1.1 are Internally Displaced Persons who are likely to face moderate to severe food consumption gap. 640,000 persons of the 3.5M people in need of urgent food assistance are facing significant food gap, this is envisaged to continue until late in 2021. Further 3.7M people in IPC Phase 3 (stressed) that engaged in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs may push the total of acute food insecure population to 7.2M by December, 2021.
Cyclic climatic shocks and perennial conflicts are negatively impacting on traditional rural livelihoods resulting influx to urban centers. This is expected to continue in 2022 further exacerbating food insecurity that already biting in the country. It is projected that girls and women will be disproportionately impacted if urgent and deliberate interventions are not put in place. Girls and women are likely to take on opportunities to place food on their families’ tables, fetch water and other duties that would not only burden them but also put them at risks of violations and exploitations.
Other drivers of food insecurity include lack of information by minority and marginalized groups stemming from lack of consultation and participation in decision making, pushing the included groups to the brink of starvation.
Impact of COVID-19, crippling poverty levels, and protection concerns; particularly among
vulnerable groups like Internally Displaced Persons, women headed households, girls, elderly and minority communities and persons with disabilities are likely to exacerbate food consumption gaps among urban communities.
Aid Vision proposed project is aimed at providing access to lifesaving and sustaining food security and livelihood interventions to 9,000 crisis affected population in Banadir and Middle Shabelle Regions, Aid Vision has presence in the two regions for over two decades and established networks of community structures and local authorities. The project will provide Cash Based interventions, rehabilitation of farming infrastructure, provision farm inputs, irrigation support, training of farmers on contemporary farming techniques and provision of agricultural extension services. It is envisioned that the interventions will go a long way in not only improving food security in the target regions but also enhance community resilience to shocks in future.

1. - Projects activities

Project ActivitiesActivity 1.1: Provide immediate food security and livelihoods assistance through Food vouchers (commodity voucher) valued at 60 USD to 500 women, girls, boys and men including people with disabilities and minorities in crisis or stress for 3 months for selected households of IDPs and host community, with consideration to women headed households, HHs with malnourished children (boys and girls), elderly persons with no economically active persons taking care of them. This method of intervention is feasible in the target area as : a) food commodities readily available in the market – markets are functional b) credible vendors to provide the food items are locally available c) the intervention will not distort the market as no food items are brought from elsewhere d) local businesses will be supported as the target beneficiaries' purchasing power will be increased – redeem vouchers from selected vendors within beneficiaries’ locality e) beneficiaries will be spared of the indignity of queuing for long hours at distribution points as they will get their supplies anytime of their choice f) it reduces protection risks to women and girls g) it is easy to ensure inclusivity and targeting with gender and age lenses h) distribution points/vendors store are within the reach of the elderly, girls, women, boys, pregnant and lactating women and thus reducing protection risks of beneficiaries. Activity 2: Distribution of emergency agricultural inputs packages to targeted 1000 farmers. The package will be comprised of: provision of irrigation support, assorted certified seeds and farm equipment to 1000 farmers (40% women) in Balcad and Jowhar districts, Middle Shabelle Regions. This will enhance food production, give impetus to recovery and build resilience benefiting around 6,000 women, girls, boys and men affected by crisis to protect their assets and generate income for their households. Provision of assorted fertilizers and pesticides will be done to improve soil nutrients and protect farmers from loss of yields to pests respectively.Activity 3: Training and provision of agricultural extension services to 1000 farmers on good farming practices.This will increase farmer’s capacity and build their resilience to shocks. Further, this will have positive impact on both quality and quantity of yields as farmers will benefit from training on best farming practices from land preparation to post-harvest -storage and marketing. The farmers will also be trained on basic book-keeping. Farmers will also have opportunity to share knowledge and experiences. Provision of agricultural extension services to farmer will enable them receive guidance on best farming practices and enhance quality of their produce. Trained agronomist and agricultural extension officerswill be available to targeted farmers throughout the project life ensure farmers get guidance and have their right skills/knowledge in real time.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The markets in the targeted regions are functional and thus will help to bolster local markets. It provided beneficiaries flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities, it is more dignified than to receive goods in kind, recognizes beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disaster (makes beneficiaries in control and empowers them), boosts local trade, and it is cost effective and efficient for both beneficiaries and agencies.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project carried out survey of the local markets to ensure they are functional. This was done through establishment of market prices of both basic food and non-food items and the reliability of the supply chain. The survey involved both traders and consumers.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups and socially excluded groups like women, children, people living with disabilities, elderly and people suffering from chronic diseases risk being excluded in consultation, decision-making and targeting as beneficiaries. This will be addressed by deliberately targeting them in the selection criteria of community representatives and target beneficiaries. All committees formed will be representative of all sectors of the community, with particular focus on the marginalized and the often socially excluded groups, decisions making process will involve women, girls and boys who are often excluded. The project will provide clear guidelines on who the beneficiaries are, and how they are expected to benefit/participate in the project. There is the risk of women and girls exposed sexual violence, sexual exploitation and abuse. To safeguard women and girls from being exploited/abused by organizational staff, project staff and vendors will be inducted on Aid Vision’s Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse policy and staff code of conduct that stipulate the consequences of engaging in SEA. The community will also be educated on their rights and how to report on incidences of sexual exploitation and abuse by organizational staff and contractors. Sexual violence may be meted on women and girls on the way to/from distribution sites. To forestall this, vendors selected will be from within the proximity of the communities served, thus girls and women will not have to travel for long distance to get their entitlements/ redeem their vouchers. The food voucher system used will be flexible to allow beneficiaries redeemthem at any time of their convenience. This will go a long way in the protection of women and girls from sexual violence.There is also the potential risk of humanitarian assistance affecting peaceful coexistence and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. For instance, selection of Food Voucher hostilities between IDPs and Host communities or agrarian communities and pastoralists. Aid Vision will, from the outset, conduct sensitization and education of the project to make who should benefit from it clear and involve both IDPs and Host Communities in decision-making. The selection criteria developed will be inclusive and participatory. The project will also target the most vulnerable of IDPs and Host communities that meet the agreed upon selection criteria.
