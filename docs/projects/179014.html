Project Summary

This proposal aims to provide summarization NFIs to the most vulnerable families who returned from their displacement areas to their homes in their origin areas mainly in Deir Ez Zor city and its countryside, with special focus on vulnerable groups, youth, women headed households and people with disabilities, who have lost their livelihood means and sources, and as they do not have any stable income to properly survive, due to lack of work opportunities, thereby increasing their reliance on host communities support to survive, and as they do not have access to humanitarian lifesaving assistance and services provided by the limited number of humanitarian actors present in the area of intervention and its operational capacity which is not commensurate to meet the scale and scope of the crisis and its impacts mainly on the returnee families to Deir Ez Zor. And due to these negative impacts of the crisis on all sectors, combined with deteriorating underlying factors such as water, sanitation and heath care services, electricity supply and food security, BICA, through the medical teams and the assessment teams and its volunteers has conducted several home visits to majority of these families at their residence areas where the initial assessment estimates, among returnees at the targeted geographic areas, there are more than 6,400 of returnee families of 38,400 people are in urgent needs of humanitarian assistances.
As the targeted returnee families are now experiencing the worst summer, the proposal recommends providing summer NFIs to alleviate the suffering of these vulnerable groups in summer as the climate of DZ is characterized by severe dry weather and high temperature in summer which reaches more than 45, and lasts more than five months, and as the project proposal has been initially approved, it is expected the distribution operation of the assistance items to the targeted beneficiaries will be launched by mid of June.
Moreover, Deir Ez Zor city and its countryside currently suffer of shortage of the electricity supply, in which the rate of feeding of electricity supply does not exceed 2% of the total of electricity produced at Syria level. This situation has affected negatively on living conditions and wellbeing of the poor returnees families. And despite these summarization and essential core NFIs are available at the local markets but these families unable to get it for its high prices, as a result to the depreciation of the Syrian pound against the US$ where the exchange rate of the US$ against Syrian pound has exceeded SP 3,000, leading to declining of purchasing power of the crisis affected families in particular the returnees, in the face of skyrocketing prices of the basic living commodities including NFIs.
This project proposal has been made in consultation and coordination with the humanitarian actors in the targeted areas, concerned line ministries especially DoSAL and SARC, UN agency especially with HCR, Local and international NGOs as well with the NFIs sector, and the views of targeted communities and beneficiaries has been taken into account related to the plan of action and the response to their NFIs needs.

4 - Needs

The assessment team conducted field trips to locations in DZ countryside, which identified by HNO and paid visits to the residence of the returnees in their homes, especially the most needy poor families, female headed families, families with PWD and families who lost their livelihoods, The assessment showed that there are 6,400 HHs out of the returnee families, meet the criteria and in need to the following urgent core and summer needs of NFIs:1. Solar Lamp: 6,400 pieces, one per HH.2. Rechargeable Fan: 6,400 pieces, one per HH. Disaggregation of assistance items by locations: 1. 400 solar lamps and 400 rechargeable fans are allocated to Eastern Deir Ez Zor rural area (Khasham, Hatla, Marat, Mathloum, Al Huseinieh and Al Tabieh, which inhabited by 1,500 HHs), benefitting 400 targeted HHs (Two items per HH) 2. 500 solar lamps and 500 rechargeable fans are allocated to Western Deir Ez Zor rural areas (Al Bghelieh, Ayyash, Al Shmetieh, Al Tareef, Al Msarab and Al Tebni) which inhabited by 1,800 HHs, benefitting 500 targeted HHs (Two items per HH).3. 2,600 solar lamps & 2,600 fans are allocated to Abu Kamal town and its rural area (Jalaa, Sabkha, Tothiyeh, Qataa, Abbas and Salhiyeh) inhabited by 15,000 HH, benefitting 2,600 targeted HHs (Two items per HH).4. 2,900 solar lamps & 2,900 fans to Al Mayadin town and rural area (Sbekhan, Mehkan, Saalo, Al Ashara inhabited by 18,000HHs), benefiting 2,900 HHs (Two items per HH).Needs assessment sources:Association assessment teams through field visits to the areas of interventions in close coordination with DoSAL and SARC, LNGOs and INGOs present at DZ, local communities, representative of the targeted HHs, and in close consultation with the NFIs sector and UN Agencies.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by an ad hoc distribution committee composed for this purpose, which comprises association field staff, and representatives of qualified beneficiaries including women which will be selected by the beneficiaries themselves, local community and municipality, DoSAL and SARC and UN agency partner. The distribution operations will adhere to the action plan and timelines as of the PCA. The association approach that followed to implement the project activities in most of the projects has been as the following: 1. Carrying out the required field assessments by qualified trained staff in close coordination with other partners including those who are to be responsible for the response especially logistics specialists, in order to identify the targeted beneficiaries and the needs which assessed against a knowledge of what is required. 2. Purchase the assistance items:When local purchase is available, the association logistics specialists conduct market assessment and produce reports on the availability of the items in the local markets, prices which kept secretly as a reference to avoid cheating by suppliers , specifications, quality and quantities including the entire logistical arrangements, then the association management drawing up open tender requests as of required specification, quantity, quality and with clear understanding on 4W logistical arrangements. Supplier, who offered the best prices combined with the required conditions, and provided that prices should not exceed the confidential prices, will be awarded the contract.3. Receiving items, storage and stock control operations.4. Launching the distribution activities as of the work plan.6. Carry out M & E and reporting.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

BICA considers protection risks through assessments and secondary sources. To reduce protection risks, BICA applies its operational guidelines.(1) BICA recognizes that Population of Concern have the right to expect that those who provide assistance measure their interventions by the best standards and practices of Humanitarian action.(2) Protection of beneficiaries and do-no-harm principle will be considered when planning and assuming program decisions;(3) BICA follows humanitarian standards its responses and vulnerability criteria for selection of beneficiaries, appointing community committees, project implementation, considering that the project results a continuation of current programming, possible risks that might be encountered in project implementation have been identified based on context analysis, daily monitoring and observations. The analysis takes into consideration several stakeholders that might influence the context, access as well as the planning and implementation of activities. These are grouped and summarized hereafter: Gender protection issues: violence against women. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: BICA will address all reported cases to the UN-agencies with relevant capacities and experience to address the needs. BICA will also report the observed cases to the Protection sector lead. Child protection issues: early marriage and child labor where the most common issues were early marriage (below 16 years old) and child labor). Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation measures: BICA will address all reported cases to the UN-agencies with relevant capacities and experience to address the needs. AAH will also report the observed cases to the Protection sector lead.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

BICA is committed to mainstreaming the protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. The project is deigned to make the concerns and experiences of women, girls, boys and men an integral dimension of it. BICA field team will select community volunteers from both genders and rely on their feedback and inputs throughout the implementation period. Men and women will also be separately consulted with regards to the day-to-day logistical considerations, such as regarding the most appropriate locations for gender-segregated distributions points. As a result, women and men have an equal the right and opportunity to participate in decision-making. The risks of PSEA will be identified through publicly discuss and address ongoing violence where men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender-age including persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV as identified. The assessment phase will highlight different gender- age specific gaps, such as unequal access assistance and services for men and women, in addition to likely protection risks for women, boys and girls. And as prevention measures that: 1. Employees sign code of conduct related to sexual exploitation. 2. Training of employees on procedures related to sexual and material exploitation. 3. Posters that include the complaints numbers. 4. Assigning a specialized team to follow up and verify complaints in order to respond immediately.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: The security situation in targeted areas is unstable and restrictions on access to beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: BICA coordinates with authorities to maintain access to target area. BICA will reach out to local authorities with trust building activities to further develop relationships and capacity to avoid or mitigate restrictions in BICA capacity to operate in targeted potential areas. Financial challenges risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, BICA will analyze timely the scope of the challenges, create proper solutions of options, and communicate it with UN agency partner and the NFIs sector. In this regard, recently we faced such financial challenge because of the fluctuation of the Syrian pound and following coordination with the concerned relevant sector, we found the proper solution and option and eventually we overcome the challenge successfully.Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption Likelihood: medium. Impact: high.Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures updated and followed. Ethical risk: related to any kind of abuse (Sexual, Power, etc...) Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide compulsory trainings to staff member on organizational ethical policies.COVID 19 risk: Vulnerable households including persons with disabilities are mostly affected and in need of support. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Support the beneficiaries to implement the health protocol.
