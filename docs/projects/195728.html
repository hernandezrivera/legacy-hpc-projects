Project Summary

PUI's FOCOS strategy continues to be undertaken with 2023 representing the 7th year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area-based approach (IABA) in Syria where PUI makes sure to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living in line with PUI’s I4SC approach (initial standards, self-sufficiency and social cohesion) and the food security and agriculture sector’s objectives, PUI aims to support vulnerable conflict-affected populations toward self-sufficiency.
As per HNO 2023, the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies, including issues with water scarcity and availability, damaged infrastructure and productive assets, disrupted services and scarcity of energy resources, especially diesel and electricity. In addition, the high prices of agriculture production inputs, especially fertilizers, herbicides, and others, continues to limit agriculture input use by farmers and adherence to standard seed and dose rates. This is resulting in reduced actual yields, reduced crop productivity and reduced food availability. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed crops production systems but also to irrigated crops such as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables.
Through the distribution of agricultural inputs, grants, and livestock, alongside securing sufficient water mount for agricultural purposes, PUI will contribute to making nutritionally rich foods, dietary diversity, and food fortification accessible for beneficiaries to ensure a nutrition-sensitive approach. The diversity of these foods will improve levels of nutrition and prevents nutrient losses, and accordingly utilize the dimension of food and nutrition security, including health, education, economic, environmental, and social aspects.
The project will be focused on addressing these issues to mitigate food insecurity and develop and enhance income-generating capacities. Noting that the targeted locations and the activities were selected inline with the aforementioned strategies and based on the sector's guidance and recommendations to ensure the provision of a comprehensive range of services across various sectors:
1- PUI will support 670 families associated with an agriculture-based economy through the provision of Agriculture inputs (Kits or other needed support based on the needs), 20 families in Aleppo, 100 families in Deir-ez-Zor (Saliha and Tishrine), 50 families in Hama (Suran), 100 in Homs (Talbiseh), 60 in Idleb (Ma'arrat An Nu'man), 200 in Lattakia (100 in Salanfa and 100 Ein El-Bayda) and 130 families in Rural Damascus (30 in Shafuniyeh and 100 in Dhameer).
2- 135 Agriculture grants will be provided to families to support their needs related to agriculture, and these grants will give them a variety of choices to fulfill their needs to improve the food security of their families. These grants will be provided in 2 Governorates, i.e., Dar’a (35 grants in Rafas) and Deir-ez-Zor (50 in Salhiyeh and Tishrine each);
3- Provision of livestock support to 445 most vulnerable families in Aleppo (60 in Dayr Hafir and 50 in Maskana), Dar’a (100 in Busra Esh-Sham, 75 in Mzeireb, 60 in Sheikh Miskine and 50 in Ghasm), and 50 in Lattakia.
4- Support to the community in the rehabilitation of the Irrigation System: 1 in Aleppo (Dayr Hafir), 1 in Dara, 3 in Deir-ez-Zor (Al Mayadin, Deir-ez-Zor and Salhia) and 3 irrigation channel rehabilitation in Lattakia (Al-Haffa, Rabee'a and Salanfa);
5- PUI will also support the rehabilitation of 2 Bakeries, one each in Aleppo (Hanano) and Dara (Kafr Shams)
Through the proposed interventions, PUI is planning to reach 99,750 individuals in the targeted Governorates while all the interventions are planned at the admin 4 level.

3 - Needs

As per HNO 2023, about 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68% of the total population, the result shows a 7% increase in the PiN from last year. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The alarming increase of people at risk of food insecurity during 2023, if the crisis continues at same worsening levels, is of major concern to the sector. The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. This includes economic worsening and weakened local currency, soaring food and non-food prices, as well as drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity, and limited energy supply. In addition, food and agriculture commodities prices increase, inflation, lack of purchasing power due to limited livelihood and lack of income sources, population movements that show no sign of abating, and the Ukraine crisis have had an important economic impact on markets and commodities cost. The needs require the sector and partners to go beyond short-term responses, and to also focus on scaling up early recovery, restoration, protection, and promotion of livelihoods, over the short-to-medium-term, and to work around the entire food system and its related value chains, including water

4 - Implementation of activities

Regarding the distribution of agriculture inputs, PUI has standard SoPs as follows:1-Assessment Phase: PUI will conduct community consultations with the local communities and representatives from local authorities to discuss needs and how to best respond to each need.2-Announcement registration phase: PUI will announce its intention to implement the agreed-upon activity, launch beneficiaries' registration, and conduct verification visits. 3-Selection of beneficiaries based on pre-established selection criteria.4-Training the selected beneficiaries on relevant topics based on the activity (agriculture best practices for Agriculture grants);5-Provision of needed materials, goods or equipment, PUI will launch a procurement process to supply the beneficiaries with the needed materials.6-Distribution of the materials to the selected beneficiaries;7-Conducting monitoring visits to provide needed guidance and assistance for the beneficiaries;8-Conduct PDM surveys for the implemented activities to measure beneficiaries' satisfaction and the impact of the activities.While for livestock support, PUI will consult with the local communities and stakeholders for the breed and other requirements. For the MPCA, PUI will register the names of beneficiaries based on selection criteria and provide the selected beneficiaries with multiple installments.Noting that all activities will be designed and implemented inline with the sector's guidelines and recommendations.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PUI identified the key protection risks before the launch of the intervention to ensure mitigation measures are in place to reduce the likelihood and impact of these risks on the project. Below are the risks identified, along with related mitigation measures:-Risk: The security situation deteriorates, and humanitarian support (whether directly from PUI or contracted vendors) cannot reach the target area. (Likelihood: High – Impact: High)Miti: 1. Assess the situation in targeted areas before the intervention and constantly during the implementation; 2. PUI and the donor retain certain levels of flexibility linked to the selected areas of intervention in case it requires shifting due to the lack of access.Risk: Selection of beneficiaries and lack of community ownership can create community tensions. (Likelihood: Medium- Impact: High)Miti measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the intervention; 2. Ensure as much as possible, considering the accessibility constraint, to provide equal services in areas where tensions exist; 3. Ensure local community participation in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project to ensure good acceptance and avoid the creation of any tensions at the community level due to the intervention. Risk: Incidents of child labor, youth marginalization and GBV (including Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers). Likelihood: Medium- Impact: High) Mitigation measures: 1. Cooperation with other stakeholders.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Première Urgence Internationale has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexualexploitation and abuse (SEA). As such, PUI is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse. Risk: Vulnerablebeneficiaries are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse by PUI staff or other related entities; (Likelihood: low. Impact: High)Mitigation measures: Raise the awareness of existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to these risks. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train Staff and other involved personnel on positive discipline; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. No curfew or lockdown imposed by the government due to security or pandemic;2. Acceptance of the local population for PUI´s interventions remain high;3. Humanitarian access to the proposed locations continues;4. Approvals for the proposed activities are obtained;5. The security situation remains stable;6. No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas;7. No major fluctuation in exchange rates Risks: 1. Lack of accountability creates high unmet expectations;2. Aids is diverted to armed groups;3. Limited facilitation by partners contributes to a delay in implementation;4. Variations in exchange rates negatively impact local procurement;5. Independent access to beneficiaries is restricted during monitoring stages;6. Timely approvals from relevant authorities for distributing inputs in link with the seasonal calendar;7. Material/inputs procured through the project are sold on the local market by beneficiaries
