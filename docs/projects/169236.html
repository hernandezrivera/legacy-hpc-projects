Project Summary

Flash Appeal Beirut Explosion Project Summary:
Legal Action Worldwide (LAW) will provide free legal aid to those affected by the explosion in Beirut 4 August 2020, with a particular focus on the most marginalized and disadvantaged groups including, but not limited to: women, migrant workers, the LGBTQI community, people living in poverty, refugees and disabled or injured persons. Legal assistance has been identified as a priority need in the Flash Appeal, and the UN Protection Working Group has concluded that legal aid for SGBV survivors, civil documentation, legal residency, support to migrant workers and support in housing, land and property related issues is needed. This emergency response will build on existing efforts, adapting well established and successful methods of outreach to disadvantaged groups and individuals including innovative methods of reaching the most vulnerable during COVID-19. LAW Legal Information Officers (LiOs) and lawyers will provide legal information and assistance to those most vulnerable and disadvantaged reaching out through door to door as well as through partner referrals, hotline and social media. LAW has two offices stablished in areas most affected by the blast: Gemmayze and Burj Hammoud. (The Gemmayze office is being repaired and will be functional shortly and the Burj Hammoud Office is running as usual). In addition, LAW will use temporary set-ups for legal information and assistance, such as tents, use of partners’ offices and Social Development Centers (SDC).
LAW LiOs have been extensively trained in how to obtain civil documentation, legal residency, protection orders, salary claims and other claims related to migrant workers. The team has also undertaken training on sexual orientation, gender identity and expression and sex characteristics (SOGIESC). LAW is running three parallel legal assistance programmes in Lebanon, providing free legal aid to a variety of clients facing different legal issues. The LAW team of expert lawyers and LiOs (legal information officers) offer a wide variety of services, including legal aid for Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian women and survivors of gender based violence, gendered crimes, civil documentation such as birth and marriage registration, legal support for all types of discrimination for migrant domestic workers, the LGBTQI population, and the stateless population other disadvantaged groups.
Overall Objective: To contribute to the efficient protection of people affected by the blast.
Specific Objective: To provide access to justice and address the legal needs of those most marginalised and disadvantaged who have been affected by the blast.
Result 1: Persons affected by the Beirut blast receive relevant legal information and assistance and meaningful access to justice.
Activities
1.1: Raising awareness of availability of free legal aid, through printing of leaflets, boosting of social media campaigns and additional announcements of LAW hotline. Delivery of legal information sessions for target groups on topics relevant to their legal needs, such as legal aid for SGBV survivors, civil documentation, legal residency, support to migrant workers and support in housing, land and property related issues.
1.2: Legal assistance and mediation/negotiation on behalf of clients. Through its experienced lawyers, LAW will legally assist clients identified through the information sessions, partners’ referrals and hotline to solve their legal problem. This could include obtaining legal documents, protection orders against abusers, and to support salary claims and other claims by migrant workers. Lawyers will be able to negotiate with the authorities relating to the release of bodies, against the confiscation of identity documents and defending eviction proceedings as a result of the blast.
1.3: Efficient referral: LAW will use existing and new referral pathways to refer clients with additional needs; such as psychosocial support, food/cash assistance and health

1 - Needs

With over 300,000 homeless as a result of the explosion, the needs for legal assistance and awareness are enormous. The protection working group, and in particular the Legal Actors Working Group (LAWG), subcluster to Protection Working Group which convened on 12 august, identified particular groups of people that are more vulnerable to, and exposed in the aftermaths of the explosion. The LAWG, UN women and others have also identified particular legal needs related to these groups, as follows: The most vulnerable groups that were discussed having particular needs were most marginalized and disadvantaged groups including, but not limited to: women, children, migrant workers (MW), LGBTQI community, people living in poverty, refugees and disabled and injured persons.Previous evidence from disasters and crises demonstrate that LGBTIQI individuals face increased vulnerability, particular risks and discrimination during times of crisis. Women are highly vulnerable to disasters, and it is important to keep in mind that they are not a homogeneous group, and that age, marital status, sexual orientation and disability often exacerbate these vulnerabilities. Migrant Domestic workers were already before the explosion extremely marginalised, facing a large variety of discrimination and protection needs from aspects varying from unpaid salaries, limited freedom of movements to physical and sexual abuse. Refugees (Syrian and other origins) also face a multitude of legal issues related to civil documentation, Sexual Exploitation and abuse (SEA) etc. The LAWG further identified that legal services should encompass awareness, counselling and legal assistance as a first step, as legal representation is a more complicated and time-consuming process. Legal needs identified are not limited to a certain group, and it is important to keep in mind that these issues are often interlinked. The Protection Working Group identified the legal needs as:• Legal protection orders against SEA and domestic violence for women, migrant worker, LGBTQI population and children. • Legal needs in terms of obtaining civil documentation, lost/destroyed documentation to be able to address other legal needs such as relocation due to lack of legal residency, burial and transfer of corpses back to country of origin and difficulties to exit the country.• Legal issues related to mediation with hospitals for unpaid bills, general labour issues, general HLP issues, general insurance issues, as well as information/assistance related to the High Relief Commission and compensation mechanism.LAW is working closely with other legal aid providers and is part of the Legal Actors working group (UNHCR, NRC, DRC, CLDH, etc), the migrant workers coordination group (ILO, IOM, ARM, Egna Lena etc). It is always in our interest to make sure there are no duplications and that referrals are smooth and that activitis and services are not overlapping or duplicating.
