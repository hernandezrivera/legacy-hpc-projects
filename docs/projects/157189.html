Project Summary

The project will target 22,220 direct beneficiaries comprising of IDPs and rural underserved vulnerable host and resident communities, suffering harsh climatic conditions. The project’s overall objective is to improve household food and livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable populations in target areas. The project aims to contribute to an improvement in food and livelihoods security for IDPs, poor and marginalized women, men and children in WKS. The proposed activities under this action will address access to food sources which will impact on both food insecurity and thus dietary uptake. The FSL approach adopted by VCO for WKS recognizes that food security cannot be treated as a basic need, separate from wider livelihoods considerations. Households may go hungry in order to preserve seed, tools, animals or other assets. VCO is thus proposing the interventions below, which will strengthen the resilience and adaptability of vulnerable households:
Improving access to food sources and food availability will be provided by increasing access to agricultural tools and improved crop and vegetables seeds (drought-resistant), followed by training on best agronomy practices and post-harvest conservation. These inputs will be utilized in household vegetable drip irrigation gardens, and on staple and cash crop production. This activity enables families at risk or suffering from malnutrition to increase their food intake and their nutritional diversity. Livestock assets protection services (including vaccination and treatment services and refresher training of community animal health workers–(CAHW) to restore livelihoods and assets of targeted communities.
During community consultation potential small-scale enterprise were explored; small tuck shops, tea selling, handicrafts, sewing machines for tailoring and simple food processing. Thus VCO is proposing IGA for female-HHs; incorporating cash assistance through VSLAs and small startup cash grants, coupled with business trainings and market linkages. VCO will determine the amount of the cash grants through market scanning and consultation with women groups. Administration of the startup grants will be through VSLAs, the cash grants will be safely deposited in the VSLA bank accounts. VSLA members will apply and acquire cash grants from the VSLA. VCO will disburse the agreed amount of the startup cash grants per each member of groups. VCO will monitor the progress of this activity on a real-time basis; at grassroots level, VCO will engage community-based independent monitors (salaried community volunteers trained on data collection methods) to rigorously monitor the progress of the VSLAs work. VCO will provide these volunteers with specialized and simplified tools to collect and record data. VCO will establish a database to keep these records.
During VCO needs assessment, it emerged that firewood is the main source of fuel for HHs in the targeted localities. The household dependency on firewood collection leads to deforestation and increase the protection risks for women and girls, therefore VCO proposes training of women on the production of Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES)/FAO mud stove modality, and this will enable target beneficiaries to switch to the environmentally friendly FES.
In WKS, it is noted that for many HHs’ livestock plays an important role in promoting food security by diversifying food sources and providing a potential source of cash income. The importance of small ruminants in improving food security among women and children is especially great. Therefore, VCO proposes to distribute goats to target communities. In WKS, because of high demand for firewood, foliage coverage is poor. VCO will work with the Ministry of Agriculture’s Department of Forestry to plant tree seedlings (for sheds/fruit tree) in targeted areas and around water yards. VCO will work with the community to establish/rehabilitate 6 Nursery to insure sustainability of seedlings production and availability.

Needs

The needs of the targeted populations were assessed utilizing both primary and secondary data derived from the following sources:1. Analysis of cumulative findings and needs identified during the post-harvest assessment carried out by VCO and WKS Ministry of agriculture (during October to December 2018) under the previous consortia project lead by MC-Scotland;2. A prioritization of the targeted population's needs by the FSL sector based on the recent monitoring visit.3. The latest FEWSNET outlook report for the period of June 2018 to January 2019.4. VCO own needs assessments carried out during the period of Nov to Dec 2018 in the proposed three targeted localities with participation of WKS Ministry of Agriculture and WKS HAC Office. In this participatory process an estimated 211 individuals, including 110 women, were consulted using a mix of assessment tools and methodologies including household interviews; FGDs, community meetings; key informant interviews; meetings with locality officials; as well as field monitoring visits and direct observations. The main assessment findings concluded the following:• The collection of firewood in the targeted areas contribute a considerable amount to the deforestation and loss of vegetation, most of the households gatheringfirewood have to currently walk around 12 Km per trip. Firewood collection mostly falls under the responsibility of women exposing them to the risk of physical harassment. • The current socio-economic, political and environmental situation in the Sudan has resulted in chronic food insecurity, increased poverty levels and high levels of vulnerability in the proposed targeted localities.• Uncontrolled natural resource extraction resulting in environmental disturbance and desertification.• Lack of skills to use locally available resources as a sustainable mean of livelihoods.• There are also key climate change issues in the region related to rainfall and deforestation that are affecting the food security; this includes: rainfall pattern and significant reduction in average rainfall, low agriculture production and lack of sustainable agriculture practices and Forest cover is declining and has negative impact on livelihoods as wood is being used for household cooking, brick making etc.Furthermore, during these assessments the particular focus was given to the active participation and feedback from women members; female-headed households; pregnant and lactating women; families with under five children and orphans and elderly people, ethnic minorities and vulnerable groups of these targeted communities. This, in particular, enabled VCO, in collaboration with targeted beneficiaries, to analyze the different needs of women/girls, men/boys, ethnic and diverse community groups, elderly and people with special needs, so that we are able to integrate these needs into the planned activities in an efficient and effective manner.

Activities

Based on the needs identified and prioritized by the target communities, VCO under this project proposes the following activities:1. Provision of improved staple and cash crop seeds for rainy plantation season and vegetable seeds for winter plantation season, to 4,444 households who have access to farming land in the target locations;2. Provision of agriculture tool sets to seeds recipients (each set will consist of: wedding and planting hoe and a rake) and donkey plow on sharing basis;3. Infield training for seed package recipients on modern agronomic practices and post-harvest conservation;4. Training and provision of household vegetable drip irrigation kits and improved high yield vegetable seeds (cash-based i.e. through vouchers) to 600 female headed HHs;5. Refresher training & provision of vaccination & treatment kits for 30 community animal health workers; 6. Vaccination and treatment of 99,975 animal head belongs to 3,999 animal owners. Solid Waste Management campaigns will be insured to mitigate the environment. 7. Establish 12 VSLA groups for vulnerable women and build their capacity on VSLA methodology and small-scale business / enterprise;8. Provision of start-up cash grants to 240 members of VSLA groups;9. Training and support of 300 women on production of Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES)/FAO mud stove modality; 10. Restocking of 900 small ruminants- (cash-based i.e. through vouchers) - (with offspring sharing approach as per the sector standard) to the following 300 households (HH): HH headed by females, HH with pregnant and lactating women, HH with malnourished children, and HH with elderly and/or disabled persons.11. Establish/rehabilitate 6 public/community nursery Sites, provide start up seeds, training & other inputs and distribute 6,000 seedlings (for sheds/fruit tree) through vouchers to 1,200 HHs.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The situation on the ground in targeted areas is progressively deteriorating as climate change continues to affect the food security and livelihood situation; communities in the proposed project targeted are highly dependent on fuel wood for energy, climate change may threaten the availability of fuel due to the expansion of agricultural lands into forests (deforestation) and cutting of trees to sell as a source of income; this combined with growing socioeconomic pressures, climate variability and climate change are likely to intensify the desertification in the proposed targeted localities. Additionally the restriction of movement of livestock have led to concentration of livestock in the already fragile grazing areas within the targeted localities leading to overgrazing, competition and frictions over natural resources, spread of livestock diseases and losses, high food prices, poor livestock productivity and production and poor market opportunities have left the communities extremely vulnerable to any future shocks.Rain-fed agriculture, agro-pastoralism with farming and livestock rearing are the main livelihoods means and the main sources of food are the products generated from these activities supplemented by purchasing additional food at local markets. Women are most affected as they shouldered the majority of family management; this situation is clearly observed in targeted localities. VCO proposes an intervention aimed at strengthening community resilience, mitigating environmental degradation and improving nutritional status. This will be achieved through providing these affected target communities with seeds and tools for the rainy season plantation along with capacity building on modern agronomic practices and post-harvest conservation, provision of Agriculture Climate-smart Technology inputs (improved vegetable seeds and drip irrigation kits), Livestock asset protection services including vaccination and treatment services, Establishment and provision of cash grants to VSLA groups, provide livelihood restoration packages (goat restocking), provision and capacity building on fuel efficient stoves (FES) and Safe Access to Firewood and Alternative Energy (SAFE) and rehabilitation of public/community nursery to support reforestation and facilitate production, distribution and planting of assorted multipurpose trees’ seedlings (including fruit trees).
