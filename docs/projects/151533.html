Project Summary

Save the Children intends to sustain and expand the provision of basic WASH services to drought, flood, AWD and conflict affected populations in target regions in Somalia through the provision of water, sanitation and hygiene services in communities, schools and health/nutrition facilities. The proposed project is also designed to ensure equitable access to WASH services considering people with disabilities and elders, safety of children as well as dignity and privacy for women and girls. Coordination will be maintained with nutrition and WASH clusters at national and state levels, in addition to coordination with key actors in target areas. The project will contribute to the recovery and resilience of the target community and health systems ensuring participation of beneficiaries in the project cycle. The project will address gender issues and collect sex and age disaggregated data which will be analysed to identify gaps, provide gender sensitive interventions and ensure participation and empowerment of women. The accountability to affected populations will be ensured by involving communities, information sharing and engaging them in the complaints and feedback system. Protection of beneficiaries will be ensured during access and utilization of services reducing further risks. The interventions also fit under the sector specific objective to sustain and expand WASH services for IDPs and the affected population.
The proposed project activities will include construction of strategic water sources, pipeline extensions and distribution systems, provision of operation and maintenance tools, construction/rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in the target villages, IDPs, schools and health/nutrition facilities, trainings and dissemination of hygiene best practice through IECs with and distribution of hygiene kits.

Needs

In spite of the international community’s response to the 2017-2018 Horn of Africa Drought Crisis, the humanitarian situation in Somalia remains of high concern. According to OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin of 4th October 2018 there are over 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia continue to live in critical conditions which is contributing to high levels of acute humanitarian needs and an estimated 2.9 million people are in need of WASH assistance across Somalia. Flooding isprevalent in Somalia, in March 2018, heavy rains started in large part of Somalia and continued for over two months resulting in additional displacement and destruction of shelter and water supply facilities including in IDP locations. Cholera is endemic in Somalia, in 2018, as of 7th October, a total of 6,464 cases and 43 deaths reported from 22 districts and 4 regions of Somalia, over 45% of the cases were children under 5 years old. In addition to drought-related displacement, the ongoing conﬂict continues to reduce the resilience of communities, triggers displacement and impedes civilians’ access to basic services and humanitarians’ access to those in need. The combination of drought, conflict and insecurity has worsened protection concerns with violations against civilians (including sexual and gender-based violence) as well as against children and it is expected to continue in the months ahead. Therefore, the project will provide a combination of lifesaving, and resilience building support to drought affected communities in Hiran, Baidoa and Kismayo. The proposed project activities will include construction of strategic water sources, pipeline extensions and distribution systems, provision of operation and maintenance tools, construction/rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in the target IDPs, schools and health/nutrition facilities, trainings and dissemination of hygiene best practice through IECs with and distribution of hygiene kits.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will implement the “durable solutions” approach to the IDP crisis in Somalia, which provides a unique support package for IDPs by shifting assistance from predominantly short terms emergency assistance to a balanced combination that addresses both the emergency WASH needs as well as their long term sustainable WASH needs. SC is therefore using this approach to catalyze transition from aid dependent IDPs to self-reliant urban residents through construction and rehabilitation of more sustainable WASH facilities. To ensure the emergency interventions are linked to durable solutions, the Project will drill boreholes in strategic location while also rehabilitating the none functional boreholes, pipeline extensions and distributions systems. Additionally, the project the project will also include sanitation marketing in order to encourage more sustainable sanitation facilities. Although the project will be implemented by Save the Children and it’s local partners, it will be done in close collaboration with the private sector and the Government to ensure the long term sustainability of the project. The project will work with the district level government authorities in project implementation from beneficiary identification to project evaluation. At regional and national level, the project will undertake strategic interventions through capacity building and support. This collaborative approach allows government to legitimately claim ownership of the project outcomes and is more likely to make resources available for future maintenance. Moreover, this approach enhances community-government relationships and dialogue. Increasingly, in addition to working in partnership with communities and the government, SC is achieving sustainability in WASH programmes through engagement with the private sector and the establishment of public-private partnerships. SCI will continue to build on existing relationships with the private sector.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Assistance targeting IDPs and returnee populations can often result in increased tensions with the host community, who in many cases are equally impoverished and vulnerable. This often exacerbates already existing tensions over already limited and overstretched resources. In overcrowded IDP settlements, like in Baidoa, there are also existing tensions within IDP and returnee populations fueled by clan conflicts, as well as exclusion of minority groups, which could also be exacerbated through the beneficiary selection process. To address these challenges, SC will ensure that marginalized and vulnerable groups are considered when designing WASH interventions by conducting appropriate participatory planning exercises and through having a deep understanding of the power dynamics in target communities. SC will also ensure that vulnerable groups are not excluded and that minority groups are considered thoroughly in the design and work with the community, whether this is disabled; elderly; minority clan; female or child headed households. This approach will ensure adequate community consultations and participation in implementation, thus enhancing community ownership of the project. The use of a vulnerability based targeting criteria and use of community based targeting approach will ensure selection of the most vulnerable and those who deserve the assistance. Project complaint mechanisms, post distribution monitoring and third party assessments will continually support identification of any negative impacts that might arise from the project implementation with the aim of implementing corrective measures.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In Somali culture it is women and children, especially girls, that are responsible for the collection of water for the household. If water points are few and far between then it results in them having travel further and wait longer to access water. This increases their exposure to risks of violence and abuse. Women and children, both boys and girls, are also at risk when they access communal latrines, especially at night. Women and girls are key stakeholders in the consultations on which water points and latrines should be constructed and/or rehabilitated. All latrines will be gender segregated and lockable to ensure safety. With regards to WASH committees, the pre-eminence of religious and customary clan-based systems contributes to women’s exclusion from public decision-making fora. The project will not only ensure 50% of committee members are female, but also provide the necessary support to ensure their active and meaningful participation. Women and girls will also receive regular supplies of locally appropriate personal hygiene items to ensure dignity. SC will ensure that WASH staff are trained to identify child abuse, and confidentiality. The project will also collect sex and age disaggregated data which will be analysed to identify gaps and provide gender sensitive interventions and ensure participation and empowerment of women. Accountability to affected populations will involve communities, information sharing and engaging them in complaints and feedback system. Protection of beneficiaries will be ensured during access and utilization of services and to reduce further risks. Targeting approaches will consider support to the elderly, ensuring they are represented in community health clubs, DRR planning and feedback mechanisms and that training and service delivery is tailored to address their particular needs. Conflict analyses are done to ensure that water resource projects adopt a ‘do no harm’ approach and do not exacerbate tension or marginalization.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict and violence is a major challenge for humanitarian partners in Somalia to reach the affected population with lifesaving WASH services. In mitigating this, Save the Children maintains comprehensive security guidelines with specific recommendations for teams operating in Somalia. These guidelines are updated regularly in order to ensure appropriateness to the prevailing context. All Save the Children staff will be briefed in the content of guidelines and the need to adhere strictly to these guidelines. SC has also area specific security plans in place and each area office has a Security Focal Point who plan, regularly monitor and update all staff about the security issues/concerns. In addition, Save the Children will participate in the regular INGO – Donor – UN coordination group that is monitoring humanitarian access in critical areas of Somali. Should access to the area of intervention (or parts of it) be limited due to political/military events/decisions, Save the Children will do everything possible to negotiate access without compromising security. Should the area become and remain inaccessible Save the Children will discuss with the donor about further procedures.
