1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (OPT) remains a protracted political crisis characterized by more than 55 years of Israeli military occupation, internal Palestinian divisions, lack of adherence to international humanitarian and human rights law, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. The results are chronic protection concerns and humanitarian needs which will continue unabated in the absence of a sustainable political solution and opportunities for further development. The oPt has become more fragmented over time; physically, economically, socially, and politically. At the same time, it has undergone rapid demographic growth and urbanization, trends that will continue for the foreseeable future. The oPt is divided into two physically distinct geographical regions, the Gaza Strip, and the West Bank, with the latter further divided into East Jerusalem and Areas A, B and C. In Gaza, Israeli occupation and 15 years of an Israeli-imposed blockade has contributed to Gaza’s dire living conditions. In June 2007, following the military takeover of Gaza by Hamas movement, the Israeli authorities significantly intensified existing movement restrictions citing security concerns, virtually isolating the 2.1 million Palestinians in Gaza from the rest of the oPt,. Israeli restrictions were exacerbated by restrictions imposed by Egypt on the movement and access of people at goods at Rafah Crossing. The intra-Palestinian divide between Hamas and the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority (PA) started in 2007 remains unresolved, deepening territorial fragmentation, and reducing the capacity of local institutions in Gaza to efficiently deliver basic services to the population. At the same time, rapid population growth coinciding with degradation, de-development and limited eroding resources further deteriorate living standards and development prospects in Gaza. The recent crises have heightened risks and exacerbated the vulnerabilities of young people in Gaza, in particular, resulting in high rates of anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder, all of which may increase high-risk behaviour (such as violence, drug use, and suicide).In the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, Israel’s military occupation continues to impede upon the basic human rights of Palestinians. Military operations, demolitions, evictions, settlement advancement, and settler-related violence are the results of ongoing Israeli policies and practices. Consequently, these drivers of conflict damage socioeconomic progress, breed a climate of mistrust and tension between Palestinians and Israelis, and undermine political solutions. As such, the geographic and administrative fragmentation in the West Bank isolates families and communities from each other and from needed services, directly affecting the psychosocial wellbeing of Palestinians by destroying a sense of home, safety, community cohesion and cultural identity. During 2023, humanitarian partners will continue to address the needs jointly identified by the humanitarian community in the 2023 HNO, by protecting the rights of Palestinians living under occupation, providing access to basic services for those who are vulnerable, and supporting the ability of Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, the protracted crisis, while more sustainable solutions are sought. There is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in oPt to maximize achievements, while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. OCHA’s unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues therefore to be of critical importance. The specific context of the oPt warrants that the international community explore efficiencies in the ways in which to respond, including through strengthening of the humanitarian development linkages, where appropriate, to identify response activities that can be carried over to development sectors. There is thus a continued need for OCHA’s expertise and coordination to facilitate the inter-cluster and inter-agency work on humanitarian planning and the Humanitarian-Development Nexus as appropriate for the oPt context, while continuing to advocate for principled humanitarian action. OCHA, in partnership with the clusters will also strengthen information management and assessments that underpin the HPC to create a foundation for shared analysis of need and joint results based planning of humanitarian action. Gender analyses and sex disaggregated data will be furthered strengthened through the support of the UN Women and the Humanitarian Gender Group (HGG) of the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) . While humanitarian partners in oPt are increasingly focused on advancing gender equality in humanitarian response by mainstreaming gender in cluster response plans and projects developed for potential funding under the HRP, it is important not to lose momentum. There is thus a continued need to enhance partnerships and gender mainstreaming of humanitarian action further, including through the HGG. Similarly, there is a continued need to maintain and build on existing good practice in the areas of protection mainstreaming and Accountability to Affected Populations, this includes community engagement work and providing secretariat support to the PSEA network, throughout the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC).

2 - Activities

OCHA plays a number of critical roles in the oPt humanitarian context which are unique ascompared to other OCHA country oces globally. Within the politically-charged context ofthe oPt, OCHA plays an authoritative role in ensuring an unbiased common situationalawareness of the humanitarian needs – and the drivers of those needs. OCHA’s work inthis regard is widely recognized and relied upon by other UN agencies and oces, byMember States in country and at the capital level, and by operational humanitarian actors.To achieve this common situational awareness, OCHA provides thorough informationcollection, analysis, management, and reporting services. Information is triangulated andanalyzed in detail by OCHA staff, and further verified through OCHA’s extensive networkwith operational partners, Israeli and Palestinian authority counterparts, civil society andaffected people, to ensure accuracy and to mitigate for bias. Information is then conveyedthrough factual reporting, operational coordination to ensure there is timely and evidence- based emergency response, as well as coordinated humanitarian advocacy. There is alsodeep demand for OCHA’s coordination of operational interventions which is based on asound evidence base resulting from strong eld presence and robust IM capabilities. OCHAplays the key role in convening the humanitarian community around strategic emergencyreadiness activities for macro-emergencies, as well as in the development of localizedemergency response plans for micro-emergencies arising largely from protectionviolations, affecting individual communities at risk in the West Bank, including EastJerusalem. OCHA supports the development of a prioritized humanitarian responsestrategy, enabling six clusters, and more than 100 operational partners to deliver targetedassistance and protection to the most vulnerable across the oPt and to ensure effectivemonitoring and reporting on the national humanitarian response strategy. OCHA supportsthe HC in mobilizing system-wide funding and in ensuring pooled funds to achieve thegreatest results for the highest number of people possible. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ approach is mainstreamed in all aspects of OCHA’s work. Data and information collected from communities is done so with consent and a clear description of how the information will be used. Community consent, in coordination with the Protection Cluster, is obtained prior to organizing any advocacy (be it through the HCT AWG, OCHA senior staff, the ICCG, etc.) potentially impacting communities, be in public advocacy or diplomatic visits to affected communities. Field visits are organized to ensure a diverse spectrum of persons speak. Databases are built to ensure that sensitive information (phone numbers, ID numbers, details of incidents, etc.) are stored in a secure manner. Coordination between Clusters also takes place to ensure that advocacy advised by one Cluster (e.g. protection) does not place at risk the assistance provided by another to communities, or if it does, informed consent to proceed is obtained. OCHA also ensures that AAP , inc. PSEA, is mainstreamed throughout the HPC, including in development of the HNO and HRP.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Humanitarian Programme Cycle, coordinated by OCHA through the project,ensures the ecient delivery of humanitarian aid in line with humanitarian principles,including impartiality. OCHA ensures that sound needs assessments form the basis ofthe HNO and HRP , with attention to sex and age disaggregated data that informsanalysis on the different needs of girls, boys, women, and men, as well as persons withdisability and the elderly. OCHA ensures Cluster agreement on priority vulnerablegroups to be targeted with humanitarian responses. OCHA manages a number ofcritical data sets that ensure that the humanitarian community has access to factbasenecessary to ensure a needs-based response (e.g. Gaza early warning indicators, Gazacrossings, Gaza electricity tracker, West Bank demolitions database, etc.) OCHA alsomanages the 5Ws database that contributes to monitoring the equal and impartialdelivery of assistance.
