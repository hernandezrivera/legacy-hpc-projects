Project Summary

In 2022 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including WASH, reaching a total of 185,027 beneficiaries. In 2022 ZOA was able to secure $ 4,185,282 in funding, of which $ 1,313,293 was SHF funding. ZOA was able to allocate $ 700,000 for MPCA, reaching out to 16,890 beneficiaries. For 2023 ZOA has to date secured $ 2,085,141 with around $ 230,000 secured for MPCA.
Through the proposed project ZOA is estimating to target 15,000 beneficiaries. The secured funding is for 3,525 beneficiaries within a farming community in Menbij Aleppo, where ZOA has done rehabilitation of irrigation infrastructure. Within this particular project in Menbij ZOA aims at rising the resilience of the farming community so that recipients of the MPCA are self-reliant. ZOA is aiming at expanding its MPCA activities during 2023.
ZOA prioritizes communities with high severity needs & selects vulnerability & selection criteria in line with sector standards. To ensure adequate beneficiary selection mechanisms are met, ZOA’s beneficiary selection will be in close consultation with local leaders who represent target beneficiaries. Wherever possible, ZOA will carry out household assessments in the target location. If beneficiary lists are shared by local authorities for the target location, ZOA will still carry out verification for vulnerability assessment to ensure that beneficiaries meet the set criteria, and that assistance is inclusive and reaches the most vulnerable. Beneficiary selection is contingent upon multiple dimensions based on age, gender, diversity, specific needs, displacement status, and other characteristics. ZOA’s vulnerability criteria also incorporate protection concerns including PWDs. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project & implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach & impactful support to HHs. Project committees will be set up to oversee the project implementation & take ownership of the project activities after the project ends.
ZOA will also take into consideration the CWG recommendations. Therefore, all Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance will be 440,000 SYP on average per family. As the organization has limited financial capacity to do these distributions over 8 months, all MPCA distributions are planned for 3 months. As a lesson learned from previous projects, ZOA will also pay 10,000 SYP for each transaction as a transportation fee. ZOA will inform its plans through the CVA mapping exercise shared by OCHA and CWG.
Through the sector as well as bilaterally, ZOA coordinates with other national and international organizations in the same area to ensure complementarity and to avoid overlap. ZOA also actively participates in the monthly meetings of the Cash Working Group, which is valuable in case any updates are shared by the Group. Through its flexible programming ZOA will be able to adapt to the changes based on the new recommendations.
ZOA has wide experience in CVA activities & uses mixed modality according to location. In the past years ZOA was able to successfully implement a few MPCA projects of which 2 were funded by SHF.

3 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered its 12th year & the humanitarian needs continue to be extraordinarily high. The HNO 2022-23 is indicating a 1.2M increase in the number of the people in need. Conflict, displacement, returns & the destruction have severely affected Syrians’ livelihoods. There has been an increase in the number of people in need, largely due to households depleting their savings & assets & struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their basic needs as the crisis remains protracted. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment & poverty coupled with high food prices, sanctions & inflation have all contributed to food insecurity, as households struggle to meet their daily food needs. Due to devaluation of the Syrian Pound & loss of job opportunities due to the impacts of COVID-19, Cholera outbreak, including those in agriculture production, it is likely that food insecurity, malnutrition & adoption of undesirable coping mechanisms will increase, exacerbating existing substantial humanitarian needs across the country. All targeted sub-districts have severity score 3-4 according to the PiN on multisectoral level.

4 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will work closely with the local authorities, CWG & local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. The activities undertaken to achieve results are: 1 Conducting needs assessment to ascertain the most vulnerable households.2 Consultations & coordination with affected communities. Consultations with beneficiaries including decisions on the contents of agricultural tools and materials, distribution planning & assistance.3 Training & capacity building of project staff. Training will include topics on accountability to beneficiaries, protection & gender mainstreaming, safety and security, COVID-19 and Cholera awareness. 4 Market assessment.5 Cash feasibility assessment. 6 Market monitoring7 Identification and selection of beneficiaries.8 Three rounds of cash disbursements.9 PDMs ZOA’s M&E is the responsibility of the country-based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and demonstrating impact.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1 Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the CWG or the sectors, such as WFP’s beneficiary targeting tool; 2 Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible throughout protection service providers; 3 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/ changes. R2: Selection criteria influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 1 Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2 Ongoing engagements with community leaders and clear community communication; 3 Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1 Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2 Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3 Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4 Regular staff training; 5 Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1 Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels are required in order to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Coordinate well within the sectorial coordination spaces, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2 Continue to engage closely with ministerial entities; 3 Continue to build capacities of local actors & continue to implement through them. R2 Market & exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2 Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3 Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3 Security related issues during interventions in the field. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous assessment of the intervention areas; 2 Coordination with local authorities & community leaders. R4 Fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2 ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3 Operational policies in place for the mitigation of fraud & corruption risks; 4 All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption & abuse; 5 All financial transactions are documented & filed; 6 Regular finance spot-checks & unannounced audits are carried out.
