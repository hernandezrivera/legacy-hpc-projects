Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan FSL HRP Cluster priorities for 2024-2025 and cluster objectives, Food and Agriculture Agency (FAA) project is aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 5, 4 and some in 3 target Akobo, Pochalla, Rubkona and Maban Counties. The project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of timely and appropriate main season agricultural inputs; crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), capacity building, asset building and market support and training in improved agronomic practices. The provision of emergency assistance and livelihoods support will save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute climate shocks. With this project, FAA plan is to support 20,000HH in the targeted Counties with food, agricultural and livelihood kits. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisherfolk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs.

1. Needs

WFP/UNFAO CFSAM report indicates that 2022 net cereal production (after deduction of post-harvest losses and seed use) in the traditional sector is estimated at about 936 200 tonnes, 11.5 percent above the 2021 output and 15.8 percent above the average of the previous five years (2018-2021).Below-average rainfall across much of South Sudan in July and August has eroded the cumulative rainfall performance across the country increasing portions of the country with deepened cereal deficits (FEWS NET, September 2023). A recent IRNA at Paloch transit point in Maban indicated Proxy GAM among children 6-59M was 44.2% and pregnant and breastfeeding women at 43.0%, the nutrition situation among the children and PLWs is at a critical phase and requires urgent food security and nutrition intervention. This is compounded by low seed and tools access, monoculture, seasonal flooding, droughts, low area under crop production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance. Deterrents associated with limited and lack of means for income generation and limited access to markets, have been worsened now due to insecurity and poor road conditions as a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed HHs) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets.The OCHA July snapshot reports Akobo County as one of the five counties with highest access constraints due to active hostilities, physical environment and constant violence against humanitarian personnel and assets. 15.3% and 23.8% rated extreme & catastrophic needs phases and this is most likely to worsen into 2024 due to multiple effects of macroeconomic crisis, flooding, localized drought , low preparedness by state authorities, the global crises, conflict and lingering effects of COVID 19 pandemic. An IRNA assessment in Rubkona County (Aug 2023) reported dire humanitarian needs on returnees influx into the area. The families resorted to eating wild fruits and leaves to sustain themselves. Many households are residing in schools and poor makeshift houses that have no basic sanitation facilities putting the women and children on the risk of contracting malaria or waterborne diseases in cases of outbreak. Women & girls in SS continue to be denied ownership rights to land, property and housing (SSLS Housing Land and Properties disputes 2019) due to customs. 22% of male respondents thought women should be prohibited from owning land hence the need for effective restitution and women empowerment through provision of resources they have control over and capacity building activities.

2. Project activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct Stakeholder consultation FAA will undertake consultation with communities, ministries and other relevant stakeholders at various levels including state, counties and payam. The aim will be to explain the objectives of the proposed project and the specific activities involved and get their buy-in and input throughout the project cycle.Activity 1.2: Conduct community mobilization and sensitization Focusing on participant selection criteria, explanation of the modality, assets creation, information on food assistance entitlements, timelines and the importance of voluntary participation of able-bodied beneficiaries in value-added activities.Activity 1.3: Target, identify and verify eligible program participants The targeting and registration will be done in collaboration with the RRC, chiefs, and PMCs based on vulnerability and other targeting assessment criteria with input from community members especially women. Activity 1.4: Asset creationThe key assets for rehabilitation will be identified through community-based participatory planning meetings which will comprise of the community representatives that are inclusive of various socio-economic groups; men, women, youth, local leaders, people with disability, religious leaders. The assets to be identified might vary from households to community infrastructures, like the multipurpose ponds, rehabilitation of access roads, constructions of dykes.Activity 1.5: Distribution of livelihood kits, food assistance FAA will distribute inputs to vulnerable 10950 HHs (at least 56% female) with seeds and tools plus fishing gears and distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetables (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine). Distribution schedules will be developed and communicated to target communities. Preparatory tasks such as production of visibility materials, preparation of beneficiary lists, positioning of distribution furniture and materials, BSFP support materials, first aid kit and help desks will be carried out.Activity 1.6: Capacity building: FAA will conduct training for 1000 farmers, fisherfolks. The trained farmers and fisher folk will build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Training on crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning.Activity 1.7: Support provision of veterinary services through community-based initiatives to 50,000 livestockActivity 1.8: Monitoring, evaluation, learning and feedback: FAA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project implementation. will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, FAA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns.
