Project Summary

Plan International will support and work to address the needs in project targeted locations by provision of immediate live saving nutrition interventions and scaling up nutrition interventions in the area where there are vulnerable communiteis who are affected by armed conflict , the aim is to improve the nutrition status of children and PLW and enhance their survival and resilience.This project is aimed to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates and the impact of poor growth due to malnutrition among children under five and pregnant and lactating women through a scale-up of nutrition services among vulnerable communities focusing on conflict affected area and the area which hosted people who are displaced because of the armed conflict ,the area will include : Khartoum , White Nile ,Kassala,Gedaref, Aj Jazirah,North Darfur, South Kordofan, and North Kordofan states, the project will benefit a total target of 515,800 (206,320 girls, 154,740 boys, 103,160 PLW and 51,580 men) individuals through several nutrition interventions. The project will be targeted 40 nutrition centres across these states (Khartoum,Gadarif,White Nile ,North Darfur, Kassala, North& South Kordofan) ,over 28 localities supporting 40 nutrition centers to scale up the nutrition services among vulnerable communities by increasing the intervention for provision of lifesaving prevention and protection intervention for children and PLW. The project will implement life-saving and prevention activities which includes early detection of malnutrition among U5& PLW referral and regular
MUAC screening and community and HH level, IYCF messages distribution, MSGs,
Strengthening and scaling up breastfeeding program, provision of referral cost of SAM cases
with medical complications as well as management of acute malnutrition for children and
PLW, in addition, to provision of support and transport the supplies from SMOH level to
targeted nutrition centers, and capacity building to the services providers on IYCF, CMAM, and surveillance and reporting system.
The project team will also ensure coordination with other interventions. The project will
deliver these activities through community services adopting the sector objectives and
approaches, besides integrating some of the WASH interventions into nutrition centers to
ensure the minimum IPC is applied.

1 - Needs

According to the Sudan 2023 HRP Nutrition Sector, the humanitarian nutritional needs are projected to remain at elevated high levels or worsen further in 2023, making Sudan one of the top countries in the world with the highest estimated acute malnutrition caseload. While the overall global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%, the prevalence of GAM varies significantly across the localities with very high situations (GAM above 15%and above as per WHO classification reported in 64 localities). These localities include four where 2022 SMART surveys were conducted. Five of the 64 localities have GAM prevalence above 30%, classifying the situation as catastrophic. It’s estimated that around 3 million children under 5(49% girls and 51% boys) and over 936,000 PLWs need treatment and prevention for acute malnutrition. Malnourishment is rampant, with over 611,000 severely malnourished with complications in need of in-patient treatment services.Prevalence of stunting is at a critical level in 15 out of the 18 states of Sudan.Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 in Khartoum having devastating consequences for civilians. The conflict also spread in other parts of the country (such as Darfur and North Kordofan). As of May 4th, at least 736,000 people have reportedly been displaced inside Sudan since 15 April, according to estimates by IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). According to rapid need assessment done by save the children recently the current clashes are creating an unprecedented humanitarian crisis in Sudan, and especially in Khartoum, West, North and South Darfur and North Kordofan. The conflict is deeply affecting important part of children’s lives. In Khartoum and greater Darfur and North Kurdufan health facilities are collapsing limiting access to care, malnutrition treatment (15,000 of malnourished children seeing their treatment interrupted) and vaccination for children under five (millions of vaccine doses have been destroyed). the number of acutely food insecure is expected to rise to 19 M across Sudan in the next 3-6 months, the fighting has disrupted lifesaving treatment services to about 50,000 children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), including 1,700 children with medical complications. According to market assessment done by mercy corps there have been a range of impacts on markets depending on the stability of the area and proximity to Khartoum. In general markets are open; in some areas, neighbourhood shops have closed because of insecurity or lack of goods. Universally, prices have increased dramatically for staple goods and there are particular shortages of imported goods and food , banks closer and even some have been looted and burned in Khartoum and with lack of cash in state banks and due to network issues to the using mobile apps to make payments , in communities where people cannot work, they are running out of both cash and money is affected all these factors have affected the purchasing power .Plan International Sudan currently operates in Kassala State where the prevalence of stunting is at 47.7% and White Nile where the prevalence of stunting is at 39.9%,; North Darfur where the prevalence of stunting is at 45.8 %, and North Kurdufan where the prevalence of stunting is at 45.1% according to last reports shared by FMoH National Nutrition Program. In most of the project targeted location the food insecurity is pervasive and more U5 children and PLW are prone to malnutrition due to lack of food, high cost or un availability of nutrition services .There is a urgent need to provide life saving nutrition interventions including treatment of malnutrition for children and PLW ,provision of IYCF services , and lifesaving preventive and protection intervention. there may also be a need to provide cash for food and health care .

2 - Activities/Outputs

Plan International will support and work to address the needs in project targeted locations by provision of immediate live saving nutrition interventions and scaling up nutrition interventions in targeted locations , the aim is to improve the nutrition status of children and PLW and enhance their survival and resilience.The response will be integrated with other sectorsl ike health ,WASH ,and education &FSL to support implementation and scale up of life-saving preventive and treatment nutrition responses linked to sector objective 1: 1.Conduct counselling of Pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months on MYCN.2.Vitamin A supplementation per child (two doses).3.Iron and Folic Acid during pregnancy.4.Early detection of malnutrition among under 5 children by mother MUAC (household).5.Early detection of malnutrition among under 5 through MUAC mass screenings/ referral and regular at household levelThe response plan aims to increase the children & their mothers (PLW) accessibility to quality nutrition services through community & facility-based services in 40 OTP &TSFPs centers, this is linked to cluster objective 2 which aligns with strategic Objective 1 as it aims in saving lives of acutely malnourished children under-fives and PLW through provision of:1.SAM treatment for children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (first 6 months).2.SAM treatment for children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (Second 6 months)3.MAM treatment for children 6-59m.4.GAM Treatment in PLW.The proposed response plan include nutrition specific preventive interventions , as well as lifesaving response activities that contribute to nutrition resilience , these lifesaving activities contributing to resilience are very critical and top priority in nutrition humanitarian responses because they target PLWs and children below the age of two years who have the highest nutritional needs and deficiencies that expose them to heightened morbidity and mortality risks than any other age group.
