Project Name

Start Date

31/05/2020

End Date

29/06/2021

Project Summary

Overall Objective:
Support access to remote/distance learning through radio programming as part of COVID-19 response for conflict-affected, out of school, displaced, returnees and host communities’ school-aged girls and boys in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States of North-East Nigeria.
Specific Objective:
1. Support distance/remote learning through radio access to 850,000 students (ensuring 52% of girls through provision of essential teaching materials and solar-powered radios.
2. 750,000 additional learners benefit from the SUBEB radio remote education programming
3. Improve the capacity of 21,250 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% of females) in Covid-19 mitigation and prevention as well as distance education through radio learning
4. Increase access to safe learning spaces during COVID-19 with WASH hard-materials (handwashing and soap) as well as soft-components (sensitization materials on COVID-19)
5. Training of 21,250 SBMC members (40% females) on Covid-19 prevention and mitigation measures as well as the provision of remote/distance education services to children during Covid-19 and school closures

1 - Needs

The Director General of the World Health Organization (WHO) declared the outbreak of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) on 30th January 2020 a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC). On 27th February 2020 the Federal Ministry of Health announced the confirmation of the first case of Corona virus disease in Lagos State, Nigeria. In the same communication the Honourable Minister of Health announced that the Multi-Sectoral Coronavirus Preparedness Group led by the Nigeria Center for Disease control (NCDC) has immediately activated its National Emergency operations Center4. Since then, in less than 2 months, Nigeria has reached more than 50 cases across the country. NCDC keeps and updates figures daily. On March 19th, 2020 a circular 6 from Federal Ministry of Education has granted an approval for the closure of all school for a period of one (1) month commencing from Monday 23rd March 2020 to prevent the spread of the Corona virus (COVID-19). Each State in North-East Nigeria has contextualized this circular. In Borno State, on 20th March 2020, the Governor appointed the Deputy Governor to head Borno’s response team and asked the team to tighten IDP camps and directed that schools close in one week. Not only will the closure of schools affect close to 46 million students throughout the country, 4.2 million students in BAY States, the most vulnerable groups of children targeted by the education partners through the mechanisms of the HRP are likely to be impacted the most. About 400,000 IDP children attending some form of learning in the camps and host communities will be affected by the stoppage of learning activities. Planned activities for the first and second quarter of 2020 will not be completed as planned. The education system in North East was already stretched before the coronavirus pandemic as a result of multiple crises and population displacements. The goal of Nigeria Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG) remains preparedness and response plan 1) Reduce morbidity and mortality due to COVID-19 among school learners, teachers and school’s stakeholder in North East Nigeria2) mitigate the school closure negative impact on children learning and teacher wellbeing and 3) ensure effective, inclusive and safe return to quality learning for learners, teachers and SBMCs. Given the suddenness of this crisis and its impact to the Education system, it clearly appears that response is a matter of urgency. The response should start with school administration, from (FME, UBEC, SUBEB, LGEA, Head teachers …) Federal level to LGA level. Priority of LGAs will be determined on accessibility basis. Humanitarian, development and government partners in Nigeria are already experiencing some challenges in the implementation of response to COVID-19. Such challenges ranges from funding requirements, protection of staff and capacity to deliver 100% while working from home. Despite these significant challenges, humanitarian and development organizations and Governments are implementing a range of interventions.

2 - Activities

In 2020, UNICEF will focus on increasing remote access to quality and inclusive basic distance education for 850,000 (52% girls) school-aged conflict-affected, displaced persons, returnees, host community and OOSC children (52% girls) in the targeted LGAs in 3 states (Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe). The specific activities include the following:ACCESS: 1. Safe operation of remote/distance learning centres where students can access their learning materials and receive teacher-supported and radio-led guidance for their study2. Distribution of learning materials (school in a box) to targeted schools including one radio per school-in-a box as well as radio-learning specific materialsQUALITY: 3. Train 21,250 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in COVID-19 prevention, mitigation, remote-learning and safe classroom management (shifts, social distancing, handwashing) during this pandemic Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 4. Support SUBEB in the development and deployment of a Radio-based remote/distance learning program to reach 1,600,000 learners (850,000 students within UNICEF program and an additional 750,000 in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa respectively)5. Train 21,250 SBMC members (at least 40% females) in Covid19 prevention, mitigation and safe remote-learning for students during this pandemic, including a focus on the importance of girls’ education and safe return-to-school measures once schools re-open Indicators:ACCESS:• 21,250 classrooms operate remote/distance learning support in targeted areas (reaching 850,00 children). • 21,250 Schools in a Box are distributed to targeted sites (reaching 850,000 children)• 21,250 Solar powered radios are distributed to targeted sites (reaching 850,00 children)QUALITY: • 21,250 teachers are trained in remote/distance learning and safe classroom management during COVID-19 System strengthening and Response Capacity of Education Service Providers:• 21,250 SBMC (40% female participants) are trained in COVID-19 prevention, mitigation and safe remote-learning for students during this pandemic, including a focus on the importance of girls’ education and safe return-to-school measures once schools re-open.• SUBEBs development and deployment of the radio learning program in the BAY States reaches 1,600,000 learners
