Project Summary

The proposed project will support the continuation of the existing 11 health facilities (3 primary healthcare centres and 8 Health Posts) and establishment of one new primary healthcare centre in the camp 1E as per ISCG health sector health facility gap assessment in Cox’s Bazar to provide access to 200,000 Rohingya population. These health facilities will provide primary healthcare services according to the minimal essential primary healthcare services package with special focus on SRHR, maternal, neonatal and child health (MNCH); and Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH). The health facilities will provide outpatient medical consultation, immunization for children and women, ANC, PNC, FP services, referral services, routine laboratory investigations and medicine dispensation. All the four primary healthcare centres will provide round the clock SRH services including indoor facilities. There will be 24/7 ambulance services to facilitate round the clock transport of the pregnant mother and other emergency referrals. All health facilities will provide health support to survivors of GBV cases and will help with further support from protection units in the camps through a referral mechanism. The health facilities will also provide basic management support i.e., consultation, need based physiotherapy, counselling and referral to persons with disabilities with distribution of assistive devices as per their needs. As a BRAC priority, special focus will be given to providing services and making the health facilities inclusive for persons with disabilities. In addition, the project will focus on adolescent reproductive health and engaging men in SRHR services. The proposed project will give special attention to IPC by providing training to service providers and provide personal protective equipment to ensure service delivery during the COVID 19 pandemic.
The project will also focus to improve the health seeking behavior and health practices of the Rohingya and host . For this the project will train and engage community health workers to conduct awareness session with women, men and adolescents in the Rohingya Camps and the host community. In addition, in the host community the project will provide basic antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning services. The community health workers will conduct regular household visits and refer eligible children and women for immunization, pregnant mother for facility delivery.
Promoting Gender equality and empowerment of women and girls through health services will be the key focus of this project. Therefore, whatever the interventions are designed women, girls and other marginalized people will be at the center of the services. So, all the health facilities will emphasize on an inclusive approach to reach women, girls, elderly, persons with disabilities and gender diversified individuals. Health practitioners, assistants, workers will be trained on clinical management of rape (survivors), confidentiality and privacy, survivor centric approach in screening protocols, referral services, safeguarding, gender equality, inclusion and protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to protect project beneficiaries. There will be provision for clinical management of mental health issues and rape. Further, awareness raising activities will target different actors for gender norms change on who controls and influences the decision-making process of SRHR, Family planning, maternal and other emergency health issues of women and girls.
To ensure uninterrupted health service delivery during any disaster and disease outbreaks BRAC has already upgraded the health facilities to be more disaster ready and taken measure for optimum infection prevention and control. There are trained and equipped Mobile Medical Team in each health facilities. There are ready for support any emergency. Also, each health facility has oral rehydration therapy corners and isolation room.

1 - Identified Needs

"In 2017, Bangladesh experienced a huge influx of Rohingya people from Myanmar. At present, approximately 907766 Rohingyas reside in 34 camps under Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar District. Women and girls comprise 52% of the total Rohingya population while children under 18 years comprise 52%. (Source: GoB UNHCR Population Factsheet, October 2021). Over the last four years, the health and nutrition needs of the Rohingya refugees has changed from emergency to regular and long-term needs. Though a lot of efforts have been given to improve the health seeking behaviour and utilization of primary healthcare services, women and adolescent girls still have limited access to these services due to gender inequalities emanating from harmful cultural practices such as SGBV, restricted mobility, disenfranchisement and little participation in socie-conomic activities thus limiting women’s exercise of their sexual and reproductive health rights, and the ability to make key decisions regarding important issues that affect their lives. It is estimated that 2.4 percent of the Rohingya population are pregnant and lactating women. According to the midterm review of the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2020, only 64% of the pregnant women received at least 4 antenatal care consultations by the time of delivery and only 53.2% of total child deliveries are being conducted in the health facilities. As a result, many pregnant women are not accessing adequate Antenatal Care (ANC) services and deliver their babies at home increasing the risk of maternal mortality and morbidity. The major reasons behind the home deliveries include cultural norms, influence of traditional birth attendants, lack of caregivers/attendants to take care of children when the mother is admitted in the clinic for delivery and poor access to transport in the camp especially at night. Critical gaps have been found regarding family planning services in the Rohingya camps. Long-acting reversible contraceptives are neither widely available nor an accepted practice in the camps. Intrauterine Devices (IUD) are offered in only 43% of health facilities inside the camps. In addition, comprehensive medical care for sexual and gender-based violence (SBGV) survivors are only offered in less than 50% of primary healthcare centres in the camps. The above gaps shows that lack of gender equality is firmly linked to poor access to SRHR services among the Rohingya community and explains how the context devoid of such rights provides a naturing environment for SGBV and other forms of abuse of women to thrive The onset of COVID-19 and the already weakened healthcare system and compromised infection prevention and control initiatives in the Rohingya camps further increased the risk of widespread infection. As of November 14, 2021, a total of 3,525 COVID-19 cases and 34 deaths have been reported in Rohingya camps. Cox’s Bazar, has been affected by this refugee influx and economy of this district has been impacted a lot. According to the 2017 Bangladesh Demographic Health Survey, health seeking behaviors such as ANC, postnatal care (PNC), and institutional delivery rates are lower than the national average in Cox’s Bazar. For example, the use of modern family planning methods in Cox’s Bazar is 45% compared to the national rate of 52% and unmet need for family planning is 18% compared to 12%. According to Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey 2017-18 in the Chottogram division only 36% mothers receive 4 ANC compared to the national average of 47%. In light of these risk factors, the maternal mortality rate in Bangladesh has remained fairly stagnant at 173 deaths per 100,000 live births since 2010."

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Comprehensive primary healthcare service delivery: The proposed project will support existing 11 and 1 proposed BRAC health facilities to deliver health services to the 200,000 Rohingya refugees in 10 camps of Cox’s Bazar (1E, 7, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 22). Among 12 health facilities, 4 are Primary healthcare centres and 8 are health posts. These health facilities will provide healthcare services according to the Minimum Essential Services Packages (MESP) with special focus on SRH, health support to GBV survivors, adolescent health, support to persons with disabilities and MHPSS. Activity 2: Establishment of a new primary healthcare centre: Currently, BRAC is operating three primary health care centres in camp 1E, 8E and 13. As per JRP 2021, there are gaps for primary healthcare centres (PHC) in different camps thus this project proposes to establish another one in in camp 1E. The proposed health centre will provide access to essential primary healthcare services to around 25000-30,000 refugees. The services will be delivered as per minimal essential healthcare service packages and guidelines from government and ISCG Health Sector. Activity 3: Community outreach activities: To improve the health seeking behavior and health practices of Rohingya Refugees, project will train and engage community health workers to conduct awareness session with women, men and adolescents in the Rohingya Camps. In addition, to improve the health seeking behavior and health status of the host community, the project will engage community health workers to disseminate awareness message and to deliver basic sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRH) services i.e. basic antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC) and family planning services, and to conduct health awareness sessions and follow up of health practices at the household level. Activity 4: Capacity enhancement of project staff: To ensure quality service delivery from 12 BRAC health facilities and at community level, the project will train (needs are identified through internal training assessment) and build capacity of the service providers on modern methods of contraception, mental health and psychosocial support, clinical management of rape (survivors), infection prevention and control, adolescent reproductive health. The Community Health worker will receive training on basic CHW packages, adolescent reproductive health, and men engagement for SRHR. The project will also develop capacity of project staff on data management and reportingActivity 5: Disaster preparedness and response (Including Monsoon Preparedness): To mitigate the risk of outbreaks and the potential disruption of health services during the potential disasters BRAC has already upgraded the health facilities to be more disaster ready; has mobile medical teams and trained them on first aid, mass casualties, and psychological first aid; also BRAC will ensure the appropriate requisition of drugs and medications, and will also create oral rehydration therapy corners in every health facility in case of a potential AWD outbreak.Activity 6: Community participation and feedback and complaint mechanism: BRAC acknowledges the importance and value of involving affected communities throughout the entire project cycle. This project has been developed in accordance to the needs and feedback provided by the Rohingya refugees. BRAC will ensure continued utilization of the Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism (BFM) to ensure accountability and interaction with beneficiaries in relation this project.Activity 7: Monitoring, evaluation and Reporting: The project will collect daily and monthly service data disaggregated by age, sex and disabilities and report to BRAC MIS, Health sector and DHIS2. The project will monitor the quality by using different tools and survey.
