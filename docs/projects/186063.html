Project Summary

The prevalence of the anglophone crisis within the two English speaking reasons of Cameroon has resulted in untold suffering on the path of civilians especially in relation to access of life-saving services like health care. According to the OCHA report for January to June 2021, of the $15.5 Million required for Health interventions in 2021 within the nation of Cameroon, only $2.5 Million had been raised, thereby presenting a large proportion of unmet health needs within the national territory.
Limited access to essential health services to the crisis affected population is a function of both massive destruction of public property (such as clinics and hospitals) and targeting of health personnel (like doctors) for ransoming, which has resulted in increased mortality and complication of minor issues, forcing them to deteriorate into much worse life-threatening conditions. Limited access to healthcare is especially burdensome to women and girls of child bearing age who are unable to receive ANC and are finally put to bed without the aid of a trained medical personnel.
Fractures and wounds on the bodies of many people within the hard-to-reach areas of the crisis regions receive no medical aid, forcing the situations to aggravate to levels that result in severe consequences like amputation. This is so because not only are health professionals relatively scare and more sporadic within these hard-to-reach areas, but also because even the few health facilities left standing lack relevant equipment to meet the surgical needs of these set of beneficiaries.
That notwithstanding, families are still faced with severe financial crisis and are unable to foot the health bills, resulting in death in extreme cases. Malaria death toll within the crisis region has seen a significant increase especially among the displaced populations in the bushes who are not only financially blocked from health services, but are equally physically blocked.
Thus, this project is designed to take on the approach of material and technical support to standing health facilities, utilization of the mobile clinic and training of community-based community workers, to contribute to the fright against preventable situations within the health spectrum.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Increased mortality rate from malaria and other treatable conditions within the anglophone crisis hit regions has resulted from poor access to medical services and limited availability of relevant drugs and equipment to internally displaced persons and host community members in hard-to-reach areas. Also, the rate of preventable physical disability from bone fractures (broken bones) amongst vulnerable persons in hard-to-reach areas resulting from inadequate health systems and economic vulnerability. Equally, there’s the inability for young women and girls to access lifesaving Health interventions especially before and after putting to bed, this gravely endangers their lives and places them in a situation of great vulnerability, necessitating the relevant health interventions
