4 - Needs

The United Nations (UN) and partners have significant concerns for the civilian population impacted by hostilities in south-east Deir-ez- Zor (DeZ) Governorate. Over 60% of the city is damaged causing lack of shelter for population in DeZ city has led to an inflation of rent prices. Scarcity of basic services (water, electricity, health, education, markets, bakeries) are increasing. Unexploded Ordnance (UXO) and other explosive hazards are present. There is a significant concern for the situation of IDPs in Ar-Raqqa, many of whom are reportedly not adequately equipped for winter. Despite a decrease in fighting since the implementation of the ceasefires, at least 75,000 people remain displaced as of 19 November 2019. The majority of the displaced are residing in host communities across Hasakeh, Raqqa and Aleppo governorates, where towns are overwhelmed by the influx. In Idlib, intensified military hostilities between government forces and non-state armed groups in the north-western areas of Idleb displaced approx. 700,000 people since beginning of December 2019. IDPs are moving further north towards cities and IDP camps in the northwest. They live in poor, overcrowded conditions with high unmet needs, particularly WASH, health, food, and shelter. UN Syria is preparing for humanitarian interventions in the newly liberated areas including areas across the Cross Line (XL), however, without additional security capacity, it is not possible to support the ever increasing operational momentum, including non-UN mission requests in the immediate terms. Currently the UNDSS resources have become meager compared to the needs on ground to support the UN and non-UN staff and the expanding security requirements for staff safety and without immediate additional financial support to beef up the other extant resources, unfortunately, will no longer be capable of supporting expanded programmes. On average, DSS is supporting 100+ staff at each of the locations in this project.

5 - Implementation of activities

Objective of the project is to provide adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery in Deir Ez-Zor, Ar-Raqqa, and Idleb Governorates. Implementation for each of 3 locations mentioned above: Field Security Coordination Officers (FSCOs), Local Security Assistants (LSAs) and a Security Operations Center (SOC) are essential to maintain and create the security risk management mechanisms and procedures as well as defining strategies and apply mitigation measures to enable the presence of humanitarian and development organizations and the International Community. Security Communications Centers are instrumental to enhance the awareness of the UN system in a location and to quickly respond to security incidents or emergency situations on the ground. They are part of the established Emergency communications System, integrating Smart phones, radios and satellite phones into a seamless 24/7 command and control room that can help to start the right reaction to contingencies in a minimum time. The INGOs and Implementing Partners in each of the areas receive security situation reports and relevant analytical products from each of the hubs under the project. The regular fusion meetings at the local levels headed by the FSCOs both facilitate the exchange of information and harmonize operational coordination. The partners who will be deployed for Idleb interventions will enjoy the similar enabling support. All UN and INGO partners will be included into the coordination and support network through remote and direct contacts, meetings and sharing of info.Since the 1st quarter of 2019, DeZ and Ar-Raqqa security teams (8x2=16 Staff) were funded through XB which will discontinue on 31 Mar 2020. Including Idleb, it will need 24 staff team (3 teams) for support under HRP from 01 Apr 2020UN Syria funds approx. 48% of DSS staff costs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In DeZ and Ar Raqqa, the already identified threats and risks affecting the humanitarian staff are Terrorism threats including IED attacks, Ambushes, Complex Attacks, Hostage Taking, occasional armed conflicts and clashes among the threat actors in the complex threat environment, Crimes of Robberies /Banditry, Kidnapping for ransom. Civil Unrest including violent demonstrations/protests against UN and other International Organizations in Al Hasakeh is a commonplace. The threat and risk scenario of the aforementioned threats in newly liberated areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorates and the areas across the XL are notched up due to dissipated control and years of of armed conflict in the areas. The additional threats of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) in the new areas is Very High. The UNDSS teams under this project will be mandated to make continuous quantitative and qualitative analyses of the threat and risks affecting the humanitarian programs in their respective AoR to advise and coordinate security measures. The preventive and mitigation measures will involve wide range of support and enabling activities from security advisories to physically accompanying the humanitarian missions to acessible areas. Since the project focus is on UN and Implementing Partners protection for humanitarian deliveries, other listed risk scenarios are not relevant to the UNDSS project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation in Syria continues to remain extremely volatile as hostilities continue throughout the country. With an increasingly complex security situation and heightened risks for UN staff and assets, there is a need for adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery inside Syria. UNDSS is responsible for providing oversight and operational support for the security management system in Syria, and plays a crucial role in supporting UN operations. Furthermore, it is crucial that UNDSS maintain presence in Deir Ez-Zor, Alraqqa, and Idleb to provide 24/7 security-related operational and analytical support to the UN Agencies and implementing partners and strengthen risk-based humanitarian delivery to the most vulnerable in Syria while keeping humanitarian personnel safe. In addition, UNDSS will continue to undertake awareness and training sessions (SSAFE, First Aid, ERW, Defensive Driving, ETB, etc.) for UN agencies' staff and humanitarian actors. Mitigation measures are dynamic and depend on the prevailing situation. In general, the project personnel will be responsible to continuously evolve, recommend, monitor, analyze and update the preventive and mitigating measures to reduce the risk levels of the identified risks to acceptable levels in order to ensure safe delivery of the mandate of supported entities.
