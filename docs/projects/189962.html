1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In addition to its protection by presence and community-based approach, IOM provides integrated protection-MHPSS services for all vulnerable groups, including women and girls. IOM’s direct implementation covers all the activities under IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid, considering the gender-related vulnerabilities and risks. IOM will target IDPs in sites and living with local communities and host communities with specific GBV activities. IOM will continue implementing awareness-raising activities to reduce GBV risks. In addition, IOM will conduct safety audits in the sites in coordination with GBV AoR and CCCM Cluster to identify risks and to provide recommendations. In line with that, IOM will provide counselling and referral services for the GBV survivors within the existing referral mechanisms developed by the Cluster and AoR. IOM will target the communities with psychoeducation activities on gender equality, women’s rights, and violence with the target of behavioural change in the long run. These activities will be completed with focused socio-cultural activities to provide communities, particularly women and girls, with safe spaces to express their concerns and needs in creative ways. In order to strengthen the capacities, IOM will provide training in cooperation with the GBV AoR to the government and community focal points. The capacity building activities will target IOM CCCM, Shelter, DTM and Health programmes as well to improve service provision sensitive to GBV. These training will provide the participants with the key information and referral knowledge, which will strengthen prevention and response support.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM employs an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks through outreach to IDPs through its activists and by conducting regular protection/safety audits in the district and the provision of referrals and assistance.IOM’s community-based activists are trained to identify vulnerable groups, including children and adolescents and the elderly, refer them to available services, advocate and assist them in gaining equal access to available humanitarian aid and protection services. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted. IOM’s Protection-MHPSS Programme ensures awareness raising and capacity building on PSEA at the field level. All of the community workers and team members are trained on PSEA by IOM PSEA focal points and/or members of the PSEA Network. In line with that, all IOM programmes are targeted with PSEA training and refresher sessions conducted by IOM PSEA Focal Points. In addition, IOM Protection-MHPSS Programme conducts regular PSEA awareness-raising sessions not only for the communities but also for government counterparts at the district and provincial levels. IOM will continue increasing its and partners’ capacity in PSEA while ensuring communities’ access to the reporting and complaint mechanisms.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

This project aims to increase access of IDPs to the protection and MHPSS services. IOM has a multi-disciplinary core team and community field workers working at the site/district levels. The teams identify protection risks and needs and address them either through direct service provision or referral to the more specialized services. Male and female community workers conduct door-to-door outreach to reach individuals at the risk of marginalization due to limited access to the services (e.g., elderly, persons with physical disabilities, persons with chronic diseases) and the individuals who live in distant locations. Moreover, all community workers were selected based on their language capacities in cooperation with the social services to ensure that IDPs can express their needs, concerns, and requests in their native language. In addition, IOM cooperates with and supports the government entities to reduce the barriers in access. For example, IOM conducts joint mobile brigades with government health and social services to target individuals with limited or no access to the facilities. In order to implement these activities, IOM works very closely with the community leaders and members in the sites and host community settings. The activities at site level are designed in cooperation with the communities to ensure that their needs are addressed in culturally appropriate manner.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams and community-based protection activists identify and refer people with special needs including individuals with physical and mental disabilities to adequate services. IOM’s trained MHPSS professional are able to provide counselling and mitigate some mental health impact of displacement. IOM pays special attention to ensuring that persons with disability, incl. albinism, are protected from human trafficking, abuse and exploitation. under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Proposed project does not include any construction work or other activities with significant environmental impact. In IOM’s community-based MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is induced in some awareness raising sessions with communities.
