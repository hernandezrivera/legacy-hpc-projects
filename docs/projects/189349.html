Project Summary

This project will aim to provide integrated emergency lifesaving health services will target a total of 20,000 vulnerable individuals in Pariang Unity and Duk of Jonglei state Respectively. Pariang County Children 4,218 (Girls 2,505 and Boys 1,713) Women 2,102, Men 1,068 and Elderly 232 Duk County Children 7,746 (Girls 4,641 and Boys 3,105), Women 3,029, Elderly 623 and Men 982 through static sites and Mobile approaches in 2022.
Hold the Child will provide health care services adapting to the current COVID-19 standard Operating procedures on prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while providing essential lifesaving health services. PHCC Primary health care Units, PHCU and Mobile clinics to ensure wide coverage and strengthen referral pathways on Mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined
Disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS).
Hold the Child is anticipated to utilize $ 400,000; target to reach 10% of the health cluster target in the two counties of Duk and Pariang in 2022 Project cycle.
Hold the Child shall align achievements tailored to the 3 Health Cluster strategic objectives, Cluster Objectives, Indictors and activities to contributing to the wide Outcome in 2022 through; 1- Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to meet their basic needs, Specific Objective; Decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, among 27,551 population by 2022; 2- Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action, Specific Objective; Reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including gender-based violence, through the provision of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services 3- Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions to displacement with respect to their rights, Specific Objective;

1 - Needs

The population of South Sudan is generally characterized by poor health status as evidenced by several key health indicators which include, Maternal Mortality Rate, Neonatal Mortality Rate, Infant Mortality Rate, U5 Mortality Rate, Stunting Rate and etc. The maternal mortality and mortality rates of children under five years are severely high at 789 per 100,000 and 99 per 1,000 live births, respectively according to (WHO 2020 Annual report). Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. The percentage of assisted deliveries by a skilled birth attendant is among the lowest in the world at 19%. The OPD attendance for the following diseases (DHIS 2021) is 44.8% for malaria, 15.3% for diarrhoea, and 9.8% for pneumonia. Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone diseases including acute watery diarrhea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers and children under 5 years of age. Communicable diseases constitute, based on routine data generated through DHIS 2 platform showed that close to 10% of all OPD consultations in 2020 are related to Non Communicable Diseases which include; Strokes and cardiovascular diseases were the leading causes of death. Additional improve management of health system resources, and strengthened health partnerships and document flooding, inter-communal conflict, the economic crisis and COVID-19 are the drivers of the disaster gripping South Sudan. According to the Humanitarian assessment conducted by REACH Jan - March 2021, Indicated early depletion of food stocks, limited access to livestock, increasing market prices has resulted into food insecurity. Although flood water is receding, flooding, insecurity aggravated with increased cattle raiding in Duk County.Health and Nutrition key information suggest that high levels of food insecurity and limited access to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) infrastructure combined with high disease prevalence have negatively affected the health and nutritional status of populations across Jonglei State. Based on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Acute Malnutrition AMN analysis for November 2021 to March indicate all counties in Jonglei are in Phase 4 (critical) except for Twic East and Pochalla in IPC phase 3.The proportion of assessed indicate protection concerns for women (50%) and girls (49%) remained higher men (35%) and boys (33%) by March 2021. Main protection concerns show early girl child marriage increase from 47% to 74% according to REACH report conducted in Jan 2021.According to IPC analysis 2020 Jan Duk County is estimated to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), Health severity 3 and Inter-sectoral severity 5; While Pariang Health Severity 3 and Intersectoral severity 4 is GAM of 26.61% and Pariang 19.59% above the 15% threshold respectively for Duk and Pariang. IPC severity levels of Duk and Pariang is level 4 and 3 respectively. Out of the 73 counties at severity level 4 and 5 in 2021, 37 counties were at level 3 in 2020, per the 2020 HNO analysis, signaling a deterioration in people's situation.Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Low coverage of preventable diseases, Low skilled human resource especially skilled birth attendants as opposed to delivering mothers attending maternal services from traditional birth attendant and dilapidated health. In Jonglei State, food insecurity remains high with an estimated 1.4 million (73% of the population

2 - Project activities

1. Conduct disease surveillance in 2 Counties. Hold the Child will support WeeklyReporting through IDSR for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for IDP Sites. 2. Establish 2 emergency team that will ensure timely Preparedness and Response to any outbreaks within the 2 Counties for Potential diseases of importance like Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis and Hepatitis E among others. 3. Capacity building for health care workers at health facilities in integrated emergency preparedness and response. 4. Integration of WASH at all health care facilities will be central to the implementation of this project. WASH will be integrated at 6 PHCUs, 4 Mobile Clinics across the 2 counties.(5). Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines and supplies. (6). Support 8 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 6 PHCUs and 4 Mobile Clinics across the selected Counties. (7). Conduct curative consultations at 10 health facilities to reach estimated 27,551 beneficiaries. Collected data shall target Gender, age and Disability aspects disaggregated by gender where applicable to provide tailored intervention. (8). Training of health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. Hold the Child will aim to train 52 health care workers in total, 6 clinicians and 20 nurses in malaria and 20 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries, 6 Data Clerks. (9). Establish and provide SGBV and MHPSS services at 10 health facilities, Provide rehabilitation services to disabled and Psycho-social support services to those with mental illness at 10 supported health care facilities. (10). ProvidedMHPSS services at 6 health facilities to reach 862 beneficiaries of SGBV as well as MHPSS services.

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project is designed observing inclusion of different beneficiaries with every person has equal rights. Production of IEC materials locally translated with pictorial communication to ensure the fraction of illiterates is maximumly involved during project implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Hold the Child has a developed culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own the project. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Girls, Youth groups, religious leaders, and people with disability. HCO will engage communities using appropriate language that the community members understand best to express their concerns, views and feedback. These feedback will form part of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Reporting of PSEA related incidents will be mandatory through the project cycle. All staff, associates and volunteers should receive regular and refresher training on child safeguarding and PSEA, do no harm, psychological first aid, and risk assessment. Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan is facing; through this protect specific measures and activities will be tailored. Through our child safeguarding policy and other related policies like PSEA will be maintained and always will be referred to for guidance. All staff must sign a code of conduct before providing frontline services and engagement with beneficiaries. Through awareness of other services and referrals will all contribute to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Hold the Child Organization, shall organize Joint Monitoring sessions with stakeholders at all levels, Focused Group discussions organized to understand community complaints, positive and negative feedbacks, corrective measures and way forward/recommendations provided. Through community events, Local leaders are given platforms to communicate and provide detailed plan how the projects must be achieve its targets, Objectives and activities aligned to the community. Hotlines and suggestion boxes installed to observe privacy.
