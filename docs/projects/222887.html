Project Summary

The proposed project is integrated including education, protection, livelihood and also cross cutting sectors. The education interventions are ECD and Myanmar curriculum grading system thorough the Community based Learning Facilities (CBLF) and Learning Facilities (LC) and will implement by Educo directly and partner organizations-JCF, FIVDB, SKUS, Prantic Unnayan Society (PRANTIC) and GUK at the camps 1W, 4, 5, 8E, 8W, 10, 12, 14, 17 and 18 and adjacent host communities under the Ukhiya sub-district. Considering the education sector objectives and goal, project designed on 3 objectives 1. Provide safe, inclusive and equitable access to learning opportunities for Rohingya refugee/FDMN children and support education services for host community children. 2. Provide quality learning following the Myanmar curriculum and other education services for Rohingya refugee/FDMN children and host community children and 3. Enhance community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure quality and accountable implementation of Education services. The project will target 13860, (3000 are 3-17 aged & 10860 are 18-59 aged) Children, adolescents, youth, men and women by the humanitarian crisis.
Under this project, total 80 learning facilities – 20 LC, 30 CBLF and 30 ECD centers (15 ECD centers are in host communities) which will be repaired for access and disability inclusion and total number of learners are 3000, detailed breakdown is 20 LC * 60 = 1200 and 30 CBLF * 30 = 900 and 30 ECD * 30 = 900 who will receive education through Myanmar Curriculum grading system, additionally will provide their community approved mental wellbeing activities like recreational activities, indoor and outdoor sports events, life skill, and others learning activities. Others rest of beneficiaries 10860 above 18 to 59 are national and Burmese teachers and facilitators, parents and caregivers, community leaders and majhis, and finally religious leaders and community based influential person will be engage through community consultation meeting, home visitation. Moreover, national and Burmese teachers and facilitators will be capacitated through the pedagogy and other relevant training. In addition, community and education support group (CESG) will be function and activated to ensure learners attendance at the LCs, awareness message dissemination for girls education, ensure quality of education services and contribute to enhance community leadership approach for peace building activities.
Under this project, the key activities are learning center construction and maintenance considering the child with disabilities (CWD), enrollment of learners, supply learning materials of teachers and learners, capacity building training for teachers and facilitators like basic foundation training, subject based training etc., learner assessment for ECD and Myanmar curriculum, provide school feeding to ECD learners, and community engagement activities with umber of Parents, caregivers, community members including CESG or SMC members, Majhis, Religious leaders received awareness session/training on education interventions, inclusive education initiatives, promoting learners’ attendance, Girl’s education, PSEA, CSG & CP and DRR (disaggregated by sex). In parallel this project will enhance the protection to ensure a safe, gender responsive and protective environment and beside that provide inclusive quality education for gender mainstreaming emphasize on female volunteers and teachers along with crosscutting issues. The project will engage and equip communities to prevent and respond to educations in appropriate and approved channels, be able to identify them, and adopt the necessary mitigation measures. Simultaneously, the intervention will ensure that children feel safe in their communities and are able to cope with external shocks, and parents receive positive parenting training to improve their relationships with children.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Despite advancements in education and wide range of positive change in education in camp level but their some moderate and Sevier concern issues that affect in the overall education status and progress. According to the Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA) 2024, 22% of children are not access to education, on the other hand, 44% children do not interest to enroll at LC for various type of causes, issues and limitation of access education and lack of learning materials. In addition, 41% children do not receive education that also concern for us to create new opportunity and access to reach them under education intervention. Significance concern is that 8% adolescent girls (15-18 aged) receive education and enrollment are gradually decreasing between the aged of 9 to 18. Meanwhile, 8% stated that they need more teaching and learning materials, 7% requested separate classrooms for girls and boys, 6% mentioned the need to improve the physical conditions of the LFs, and another 6% expressed that having more qualified teachers would encourage them to enroll in the learning facility. 25% girls (5-14 aged) out of school which is another significance gaps in education. Significant challenges also exist for children with disabilities contributed by insufficient skill and training of educators, poor awareness, negative attitudes, and stigma related to CwD. LF also commonly lack ramps and accessible seating arrangements, toilets, and water facilities, combined with surrounding terrain that is often steep and enough and difficult to navigate and there is poor availability of assistive devices. To bridge the learning condition gaps for both the host community and refugee children, a comprehensive approach is proposed. This includes investing in infrastructure enhancements and exploring solutions to minimize lengthy travel distances. Enhanced security measures, in collaboration with local authorities, aim to address safety concerns during travel. Strategies to counter recruitment by armed groups involve community collaboration and awareness campaigns. The recruitment and training of qualified teaching staff, contribute to creating a supportive learning environment. Adapting the Myanmar curriculum, ensuring material availability, and engaging communities with cultural sensitivity further enhance educational access. Collaboration with NGOs, regular monitoring, and advocacy for to foster inclusivity and security in learning environments for both communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is a integrated project, so will be emphasized on cross cutting issues-CP, GBV, PSEA, DRR and also referral mechanism approach, where will be enhanced the protection to ensure that children and adolescents girls will ensure in access to education and improved child protection services in the camps and adjacent host communities. Multi-purpose learning centers will function as child-friendly spaces where they can access life skills training, participate in activities that build their resilience, and receive gender-sensitive mental health and psychosocial support, case management and referral pathway assistance, structured PSS interventions, awareness sessions on child protection, GBV, needs-based literacy and numeracy training, and be reached through home visitation programs. Strengthening the community-based child protection system is the key approach to the action, which includes mobilizing caregivers, child protection actors (social workers, community volunteers, and government officials), community leaders (Majhis), and non-child protection actors into community-based child protection committees (CBCPC) and adolescent club members, when building knowledge and changing the attitudes, behaviors, and/or practices of target groups, the project will focus on the prevention of and protection from physical and sexual violence as well as related major child protection risks. The project will engage and equip communities to prevent and respond to child protection risks through appropriate and approved channels, identify them, and adopt the necessary mitigation measures. Simultaneously, the intervention will ensure that children feel safe in their communities and are able to cope with external shocks, and parents will receive positive parenting training to improve their relationships with children

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project aims to directly support 13,860 people. This project will prioritize vulnerable Children and adolescents with special needs, including those who are not enrolled with education facilities or at increased risk of abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation. Support will also extend to families of persons with disabilities, separated or unaccompanied children and adolescent, child-headed households to strengthen their education quality and getting benefitted.Project participants will include Children and adolescents from both refugee camps and host communities, the Education Community and Education Support Group (ESCG), local community members, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. Approximately 5236 community members—including caregivers, teachers, and religious and community leaders—will be engaged in promoting awareness for safe and quality education.The project, led by partner management, will promote disability inclusion, ensuring that project staff are sensitized to the rights and entitlements of persons with disabilities. An indicator has been added to the results matrix to monitor age- and disability-inclusive livelihood activities, supporting the engagement of individuals with disabilities in income-generating opportunities. Additionally, tailored, door-to-door support will be available for child with disabilities to safe and quality education. Educo and its partners will actively involve persons with disabilities in all educational learning initiatives, coordinating closely with other NGOs, UN agencies, and partner programs. Aligned with Education sector goals and the JRP 2025, the project will aim to include around 3 % of Child with disabilities in each targeted education activity, supporting their inclusive education and community integration.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The monitoring approach of the project serves a dual purpose of learning and ensuring accountability for results, despite operating within a challenging context. Educo-Bangladesh and project partners will engage the target population, including adolescent girls and boys, women, and men, using a gender-integrated and child-friendly approach during project implementation. Formal and informal consultations, data collection exercises, and a structured feedback mechanism, including a complaint box and help desk, will be employed. Quarterly satisfaction surveys and focus group discussions (FGDs) supervised by MEAL units will enhance the monitoring process. Educo has established a Suggestions, Complaints, and Commendations (SCC) channel, comprising an SCC Box, an email address (scc.bd@educo.org), a mobile contact (01765542903), Open Day events, stakeholder consultation meetings, an online platform, and a Community Focal Point. The Bangladesh National SCC Committee, consisting of five members, is responsible for implementing the SCC policy, conducting orientations, receiving and resolving SCCs, capturing lessons learned, maintaining regular interaction, and supporting the Global SCC Commission in preparing the annual SCC Report. The project emphasizes accountability to the affected population through participant linkages and takes a comprehensive approach to accountability, ensuring the involvement of refugee participants of all backgrounds and ages, including persons with disabilities, in decision-making processes for comprehensive community engagement.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

As sector instruction, Educo and partner organization are committed to take necessary initiative to develop LC/facility wise DRR plan by the DRR expert to ensure sustainability. Mainstreaming disaster risk reduction Educo and partners should continue to undertake concrete and innovative protections and sensitive preparedness interventions to mitigate the risk of landslides, cyclones, and floods, particularly in areas where refugees are unable to evacuate to safe areas. Contingency planning is essential in advance of seasonal events such as monsoon preparedness, cyclones, and potential outbreaks of diseases. Both Educo and partners need to establish stand-by/roster systems for key staff during the weekend and extended holyday periods, during the cyclone and monsoon seasons, as well as any kind of emergency. In addition, the project will have close collaboration with disaster management committees both at camps and host. The project will obey the instructions of those DMCs. Besides, project will increase awareness among the target groups regarding human or climate-related protection issues. It will also help Community and Education Support Group (CESG) and CBPCs incorporate these factors into their plans of action.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project has taken Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to enhance resilience and ensure effective response during emergencies and also focus on LC wise DRR plan and strength learning facilities. The project allocated contingency funds are integrated into the project’s financial structure, enabling immediate action for urgent needs arising from emergencies such as cyclones, flash floods, or fires. This funding provides the flexibility required to respond promptly to sudden protection needs resulting from crises, whether they are natural disasters or significant social disruptions. The project also emphasizes community capacity building by equipping community-based child protection structures with the necessary skills and resources for effective emergency preparedness and response. Training for child protection actors, community volunteers, and caregivers on disaster risk reduction (DRR) and response readiness is a core component, complemented by awareness-raising sessions focused on building protective capacities within the community to anticipate and manage risks effectively. By embedding these EPR measures, the project aims to enhance the overall resilience of Rohingya refugee communities and their caregivers, ensuring they are better prepared to respond to emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Youth engagement (ages 15-18) is a central component of this education, child protection designed to reach youth (15 -18 aged) for education through the Community based Learning Facilities (CBLF) and LC based education through Myanmar curriculam grading system as well as leadership opportunities and participatory activities, the project empowers youth to become economic contributors and change agents in their communities. These skill-building efforts aim to equip young people with practical, marketable skills, fostering economic self-reliance and reducing reliance on external aid.Youth also receive structured psychosocial support through individual or group sessions that focus on coping strategies and stress management, promoting emotional resilience and contributing to a stable community environment that supports livelihoods. They actively participate in courtyard sessions, community campaigns, and awareness days, promoting child protection, gender equality, and violence prevention alongside their economic empowerment. Training on child protection also covers safe working practices and equips youth with the knowledge to recognize and respond to risks, including those that impact livelihood activities, such as disaster-related hazards and workplace safety.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes: The project promotes self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees by equipping Youth and adult with vocational skills, financial literacy, and livelihood opportunities aligned with market needs. Families are supported with conditional cash grants and livelihood diversification, reducing economic pressures and enabling investments in education. Mental health and psychosocial support services help youth cope with trauma and build emotional resilience, while inclusive approaches ensure access for people with disabilities. These initiatives empower refugees to achieve economic independence, enhance coping capacities, and contribute to more resilient communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community are closely linked to development initiatives and align with Cox’s Bazar District's development and growth priorities, including the District Development Plan and other planning instruments. By promoting, education, vocational training, livelihood diversification, and entrepreneurship, the project supports economic empowerment through eduction, a key district priority. It also addresses resilience through disaster risk reduction training and mental health services, aligning with strategies to enhance community well-being. Collaboration with local authorities and stakeholders ensures coherence with district-level development goals, fostering sustainable outcomes for host communities.
