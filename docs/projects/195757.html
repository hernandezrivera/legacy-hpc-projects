Project Summary

The intervention aims to respond to shelter needs for the most vulnerable population in NWS, where we plan to implement dignified shelter units for sheltering 500 HHs from IDPs whose shelters are located in significant flood risk areas, besides implementing diester risk reduction activity targeting NWS camp, through providing the essential infrastructure rehabilitation for 15 km of roads and drainage.

3 - Needs

Primarily in northwest and northeast Syria, 2.05 million IDPs reside in last resort sites, a marginal increase from 2.02 million in the previous year. Such locations lack crucial infrastructure and rely on emergency shelter solutions and continued humanitarian support. The majority of displacement is currently in excess of 3 years and 37 percent of households have reportedly been displaced more than three times since 2011. "HNO 2023".In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 percent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19 percent reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified.Among WoS figures, Dana sub-district are at a noticeable level of vulnerabilities and severity scale and highest level of PIN for Shelter, with 672,848 PIN at 4th level of severity scale. Atma camp comes as highest number of 109,382 PIN.

4 - Implementation of activities

For temporary dignified shelter units, GC will work closely with the most in-need camps and communities which hosted a large number of IDP to identify the safe and proper locations for the activity implementation and examine the recommended guidelines in site selection and preparation standards for creating the planned camps, and considering HLP due diligence for the site land, topographic levels and flood considerations, enough farness from the front lines, nearness to exist settlements, main roads, and vital locations, and availability of the essential services in and around. We will identify the beneﬁciaries from those who are living in shelters that experienced a flood or the risk of flood is significant, and their camps are located in flood vulnerable locations. GC will rehabilitate critical infrastructure in the NWS camps to facilitate the delivery of life-saving essential WASH services and improve the overall shelter status of the Camp residents, particularly those affected by seasonal flooding. We will gravel roads, repair and/or redirect drainage. GC will follow the HLP guidance outlined in the SNFI Cluster’s due diligence guidelines, where we will implement the proposed activities on the land with no HLP issues or problems.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the planned activities, we expect the following risks:Through the temporary dignified shelter activity, there is a risk of Eviction due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter. This can be mitigated through 1.Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2.Do not proceed with the site preparation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines. 3.Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed. 4.Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form. In this manner a high risk of exclusion from shelter assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID may arise in the field. This can be mitigated through Working closely with communities, community leaders and authorities to identify alternative way of identification.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In order to mitigate is a high risk of Abuse of Authority by actors (PSEA, GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). This can be mitigated through 1. Ensuring that GC’s and its implementing partners’ staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct. 2. developing a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. 3. Ensuring active inclusion of complaint mechanism in place. Meanwhile, there is a high risk of Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. This can be mitigated through 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites, 2. Provision of gender segregated/appropriate privacy in mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit, 3. Provision of gender-segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, 4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, 5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them, 6. Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.
