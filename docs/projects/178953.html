Project Summary

The intervention is aligned with the framework of the objective 1, 2 and 3 of the HRP 2021. Taking into consideration the health system capacity in context of COVID19 and to complement efforts of Health sector in locations of intervention. As reported in WHO Syria annual report 2020, 53% of 1,800 fixed PHCCs monitored in 2020 were reported fully functioning, as 28% reported as completely non-functioning. In addition to an extreme lack of appropriate infrastructure, health workers in Syria struggle due to a shortage of essential medicines and working medical equipment, despite continued WHO health sector support through medical shipments. The provision of quality essential health services is also limited due to a shortage in qualified health workers. 50% of medical professionals fled the country over the past decade, resulting in limited access to medical services for child health, nutrition, communicable and non-communicable diseases, sexual and reproductive health, and mental health.
The project seeks to deliver essential Health Services Package, integrate MHPSS, GBV, NCD care and referral support by implementing life-saving services for diagnosis, follow up and treatment of host community, returnees and IDPs in Rural Damascus (Jaramaneh) and Aleppo (Jabal Samaan) governorates. Activities will be identical in both locations taking into consideration the deference in number of beneficiaries based on needs, not only by establishing new Primary health care centers with three main clinics of Internal medicine, Gynecology & Obstetrics and Pediatrics to provide medical counselling, treatment and referral if needed in coordination with the implementing partners, and support physical rehabilitation activities including Physiotherapy/PSS for PwDs supported by mobile teams and distribution of assistive devices, provide Birth/Delivery vouchers (both normal and Caesarean births) and covering rehabilitation surgeries for conflict victims. So unmet needs of the most in need amongst the vulnerable people can be served. In context of COVID19, supporting the PHC system in areas of implementation – knowing its health facilities capacity and situation – will increase capacity of its secondary and tertiary health care system to response to more complicated medical cases.
The project plans to conduct focused trainings for staff and volunteers of SARC, SCFPA and MoH to build their capacity, and provide structured trainings on GBV, MHGAP, case management and referral and all updates on COVID-19 shared by Health sector. (MhGAP), this training will increase staff capacity on understanding and assessing individuals that are at risk of mental health risk or self-harm The community officers were oriented on the community based mental health, in addition to improving communication skills during direct interaction with beneficiaries. GBV training of care providers dealing directly with survivors to identify, provide appropriate care in every stage of medical services (prevention, assessment, diagnosis, and treatment) and offer referral to additional internal and external services.
All activities will be supervised by TdH staff and will be carried out in coordination with local partner. The project will be supported by M&E framework that will be developed within this intervention, providing high quality information through accurate data collection and analysis while data will be continuously collected through dedicated forms. Registration and attendance will be then collected for each beneficiary, as well as schedule of implementation. Regular field visits and spot-checks will be conducted by M&E Officer to monitor activities besides communicate with randomly selected beneficiaries who received Birth/Delivery vouchers through the PDM process. FGDs will be implemented to assess quality and outcomes of the overall intervention and to collect suggestions and complains. Also, complaint boxes will be provided for feedback

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention is interested in the framework of the strategic objective 1,2, and 3 of the HRP 2020. It is designed to complement the efforts of the Health sector in areas of Implementation. By Increasing access to primary health care (PHC) through the provision of essential PHC to cover the health care needs of vulnerable populations, including RMNCH, as well as continuity of treatment for non-communicable diseases.Ensuring comprehensive trauma services and referral pathways for acute and post-acute management phases. Medical referral systems must also address the needs of people living with chronic disease who develop complications, emergency obstetric and new born care (EmONC) cases, and patients with acute illness who cannot access treatment in the area.Implementation of activities will be through implementing partners and as following:• Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referral: Provision of life-saving services through well-equipped unit and well trained staff. Provision of access to PHC services through fixed clinics. Identification of pregnant women and follow up on the antenatal, delivery and postnatal care by voucher system. Identification of cases in need of physical rehabilitation and follow up with them through Physiotherapy and PSS activities. Identification of rehabilitation surgeries and follow up with them pre and post operation.• Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services: Training of mobile team staff and CHWs on health topics and identification of epidemic cases.In addition to the required monitoring mechanism for each health project at the program

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if the health services of the project are provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others, M1: TdH will Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken, and will engage the beneficiaries in defining the vulnerability characters of each community especially women and girls that should be integral part of the design in terms of consultation of activities, R2: The risks of GBV by overcrowding and lack of privacy in the PHCs that will result negatively on access and data protection M2: TdH will ensure the confidentiality of service providers, to protect information gathered about women and children, and to ensure that data collecting, keeping, sharing, and storing information, in addition to the respect of beneficiaries’ privacy during the provided services R3: the PHCs are not accessible to beneficiaries M3: TdH will ensure that the selected location is safe and accessible to beneficiaries especially PWDs, in addition, TdH will ensure that the working hours of the PHCs is suitable for women and girls. R4: staff abuse to beneficiaries M4: all TdH staff and local partner volunteers will be undergo an training for protection and will sign TdH code of conduct , in addition to the protection sub-committees that are assigned in each project location to report and respond to any case.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities, M1: SARC and SCFPA displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations, However, if SARC or SCFPA lost the access to project locations, TDH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with local actors, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue.R2: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M2: TDH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TDH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU. R3: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria, M3: TDH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R4: new quarantine by the local authorities due to COVID outbreak M4: TdH has on-line policy for approvals and working from home, also, the PHCs will follow COVID measurement protocols , in addition, TdH health Advisor will conduct training to TdH staff and local partner volunteers for COVID measurements during humanitarian response and will distribute PPEs to both beneficiaries and staff.
