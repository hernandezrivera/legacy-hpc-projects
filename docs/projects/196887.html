Project Summary

The objective is to enable children affected by conflict and displacement in Syria to meet their basic needs, increase their protection, human dignity and benefit from community-based initiatives that ensure their inclusion, through an integrated multi-sectoral approach including Shelter, WASH, education and child protection in schools, juvenile centres and within communities, in partnership with local partners. Tdh will contribute to this objective through the direct provision of an integrated package of service-oriented assistance to build the resilience of children and communities, particularly children affected by the conflict across 5 governorates in Syria. Tdh conducted various needs assessments during 2022 in juvenile centres and in schools. The needs remain tremendous! Limited access to non-formal education activities and specialised PSS services, damaged buildings, critical basic living conditions, including lack of food and non-food items, electricity and water. In addition, for children in conflict with the law, there is a clear need for legal support via a lawyer who represents them in court, and reintegration after release into their families and communities. The project is therefore structured around a series of complementary and integrated activities at different levels and crosses several sectors. Firstly, Tdh will address the specific needs of children in juvenile centres, through prevention, protection and reintegration services for children at risk, in contact or in conflict with the law. Through capacity building activities on CP and PSS, the project will address some of the root causes of the problems leading to the detention of children, through awareness raising communities and referrals. The strengthening of non-formal protection systems such as community centres, civil society initiatives and community child protection committees contributes to an overall increase in the accessibility and quality of protection services. The proposed response will also cover food distribution, light rehabilitation of the critical infrastructure damaged in juvenile centers and provision of MHPSS and Non formal education. Protection services will help children in juvenile centers to obtain legal documentation and represent them infront of the courts and advocate for their release from the detention while the protection team will also support the referrals to the different services and work on the restoration of the family links through family visits and phone calls wherever its possible. In addition, recognising the importance of supporting these children after their release, Tdh will also ensure their reintegration into their families and communities. In parallel, Tdh will continue to rehabilitate schools to encourage families to send their children to school or non formal education classes for those who cannot send children to the school. Tdh aims to rehabilitate 4 schools and give children as well as teachers and staff access to functional and safe school facilities through rehabilitation works that will be inclusive for all, gender sensitive and environmentally friendly and sustainable through the use of solar panels. They will have access to clean water, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion on good practices and behaviours to protect themselves from water-borne diseases, with the distribution of hygiene kits. All schools and classrooms will be rehabilitated and equipped so that users can benefit from a safe and child-friendly learning space. In 2023, Tdh's proposed intervention will target 14,038 children, 120 teachers, 3740 families and 184 child protection workers, including 6,640 girls and 7,398 boys, aged from 0 to 17 and 5,819 women and 4,997 men from 18 and above 50, with a significant proportion of IDPs and Host communities, using an integrated area-based approach that addresses immediate education and child protection services provided to communities. Tdh has so far secured 20% of the total budget.

Implementing Partners

Terre des hommes will be in charge of the rehabilitation work with all the hard components, especially in schools and youth centres. A technical team will be responsible for carrying out WASH activities. In addition, due to its long experience in child protection, Tdh will also provide training to the actors who deal with children on a daily basis in order to ensure and increase their well-being.
Related activities in the juvenile centres, including specialised child protection services through case management and referral, legal advice or assistance in ongoing court cases, individual and group psychosocial support as well as formal and non-formal education through remedial courses, vocational training and life skills, will be directly implemented by the Syrian Society for Social Development (SSSD), which has extensive knowledge and long-standing experience in the juvenile centres.
Furthermore, as Terre des hommes is registered under the umbrella of the Syria Trust for Development (ST), our local partner will be in charge of facilitating the approval of the project as well as the coordination with the ministries involved. ST will also be active during the project in collecting civil documents of girls in conflict with the law who do not have any in order to facilitate legal assistance to them.

3 - Needs

In 2022, Tdh conducted site visits to 2 schools in Arbin and Dhameer and 4 juvenile centres in Damascus, Rural Damascus and Latakia. The visits consisted of direct observation, technical assessment by the WASH & Shelter team, and semi-structured interviews with the SSSD and juvenile centre management teams. Additional visits to schools and juvenile centres in the rural areas of Damascus, Aleppo, Homs and Tartus will be carried out in 2023. Overall, the schools and centres assessed are very often poorly maintained and have enormous needs in terms of capacity, access to clean water, food and electricity. In addition, with regard to children in juvenile centres, the main observations indicate that they face - Unhygienic and generally poor living conditions (overcrowded rooms, unsanitary toilets, too many children per bathroom, lack of daylight, lack of heating, limited meals limited, washing clothes on the floor of toilets and limited outdoor activities); - Extreme mental and psychological distress (minimal communication with families, withdrawal behaviour, self-harm and suicide attempts); - Inadequate legal assistance (delays in legal cases leading to prolonged detention, violation of children's due process rights, lack of child-friendly legal aid and lack of civil status documents); - Limited support for safe reintegration after release, compounded by lack of non-formal education opportunities (stigmatisation of children and their families and lack of follow-up services).

4 - Implementation of activities

In this particular project ST will be responsible for assisting Tdh with obtaining approval of the project and coordination with the Ministry of Social Affairs and the Ministry of Justice. In addition, it will also be active during the project in collecting civil documents of children and youth in JCs who do not have any in order to facilitate legal assistance to them. Direct monitoring of the project will be carried out by Tdh in partnership with the implementing partner SSSD, specifically to monitor and report on the progress of activities, ensure quality, and ensure that the voices of beneficiaries are taken into account in implementation. Upon need, ad-hoc consultants might be further required for specific areas of expertise and Tdh regional team will support mission with the development of the technical aspects of the intervention. In precise, based on the type of interventions, the partner and Tdh will be required to adopt the outlined monitoring approaches. Depending on the type of intervention, the partner and Tdh will need to adopt different monitoring approaches : test the quality of the drinking water, participatory approaches via Focus Groups Discussions and surveys, post-distribution monitoring, pre and post wellbeing surveys. Finally, Tdh and SSSD are jointly considering how best to implement the CFRM in this particular context, with no harm to anyone.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (WASH): Distribution of expired unhealthy items. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Establish and monitor expiry date of supplies prepositioned in warehouse, ensure relevant storage provision and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. Protection Risk (WASH): Gender insensitive contents varieties; of kits with inconsistent items. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Harmonise and standardise gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use; hygiene promotion. Protection Risk (WASH): Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration. Make sure that clear instruction for use is available for chlorine products primarily for household use. Protection Risk (EDU): Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal and sexual abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk (EDU): Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. Protection Risk (EDU): Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place by the organization should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. The goal of child safeguarding is to create and maintain a safe learning environment through preventive actions that protect children from actions, or inactions that may lead to harm.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection Risk (EDU): Delay or non-approvals of planned activities or planned processes. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Organizations should work with local education and other relevant authorities and community members to develop a positive relationship. Possible education support should be discussed with the local education and other relevant authorities and community members before it starts. Protection Risk (PRO): Quality of intervention (capacity building activities) is scarce due to inadequate service provision and inadequate capacity. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium: Mitigating Measure Assess impact of the trainings introducing appropiate methodoly and base lines, Consult with staff in charge to design/ implement programs on their perceived capacity gaps and training needs; conduct training needs assessments Financial Risk: Currency fluctuations. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Conversion to local currency postponed, in order that if the new exchange rate is changed, losses will be minimized. Engagement in framework contracts. Good cash flow forecasting for effective currency risk management. Data Risk: Missing data. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating Measure: Collection of only the necessary information using simple and user-friendly data collection methods (such as platforms, training of data collectors). Follow-up on data documentation procedures.
