Project Summary

The objective of this project is to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services for conflict affected people, vulnerable host communities and people in disease prone (high GAM famine and outbreak related) areas including protection related actions to mitigate GBV, strengthening of communities’ resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks. Over all the project will reach to about a population of 800,000 with WASH services and facilities.
This emergency WASH project will comprise of situation assessments, preparedness, frontline emergency WASH response, strengthening partners’ capacity, and cluster coordination as well information management. UNICEF will deliver emergency lifesaving support by providing access to WASH services as per Sphere Standards.
The project will focus on addressing the following:
- Effective Cluster coordination mechanism in the country
- Provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees
- WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention with a minimum package of interventions.
- WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level (i.e. Ebola, Cholera, etc.)
- While implementing projects a special attention will be given to mitigate WASH-related GBV.
The project will also support expansion of partnerships with local and international NGOs and CBOs in areas highly affected by the conflict to ensure basic services are provided and long-term capacity built. The activities will be implemented in complementarity with other sectors such as Health, Nutrition, Protection and where possible Education projects including disaster risk reduction (DRR) focusing on strengthening community coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

TThe continuing internal conflict in South Sudan has caused over 1.9 million internally displaced people and another 2 million refugees in neighboring countries. Over 250,000 people remain in the POCs, and as per the WASH Cluster HNO, an estimated over 5.1 vulnerable people are in urgent need of WASH services. Even after the signing of the Comprehensive Peace Agreement on 31st October 2018, critics are not very optimistic about this deal. The country lacks access to water and sanitation services, which is because of inadequate infrastructure, from the blocking or destruction of infrastructure due to insecurity and the deteriorating economic situation that made it difficult for the population (as well as suppliers) to purchase WASH commodities. Data obtained through the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) shows that approximately 29% of the population countrywide have access to an improved water source in 30 minutes or less without facing protection within, in particular, GBV concerns. This indicates that the remaining two-thirds of the population are required to spent more considerable periods access to water, are unable to access an improved water source, and are reliant on surface water or water sources that are not protected. Data also indicated that less than 10% of the population does not practice open defecation. It suggests that around 90% of the people do not have access to or are not accustomed to use a primary sanitation facility when defecating. In combination with inadequate sanitation facilities, only 13% of the population reported having access to WASH NFIs. The interlinked impact on the well-being of the people from insufficient water, sanitation, and low access to WASH NFIs can be seen through the health and nutrition status of the population. 75% of the population reporting that household members had been self-diagnosed with a waterborne illness in the past two weeks. South Sudan also faces recurrent cholera outbreaks, which is closely associated with the deteriorating WASH services amongst the IDPs, host communities as well related to the movement of pastoral communities. The last epidemic ended on February 2018, a cumulative of 20,438 cases, and 437 deaths (CFR 2.13%) were reported from 26 counties countrywide. Over 47% of the affected population are children. Diarrheal diseases are also closely linked to malnutrition.In addition to conflict and recurrent disease outbreaks, a range of other risks hamper access to WASH services in South Sudan. Lack of clean water is one of the factors that force women and girls to venture outside POCs, either to bath or to collect water for household use, which exposes them to the risk of becoming victim to sexual violence. Flooding and drought have been reported in various locations and have a significant impact on the WASH sector capacity. In 2019, flooding in Jonglei ( Bor) and part s of Upper Nile, Western Bharghazal, Lakes and Unity triggered the displacement of over thousands of people. A countrywide economic crisis is deepening in South Sudan. With an inflation rate of over 600 percent, most families cannot purchase their necessary WASH supplies like safe water, soap, and hygiene items. As a result, the population has even less access to safe water and sanitation services than before the conflict. The continued insecurity, disease outbreaks, nutrition crises and other natural emergencies combined with economic crises and poor infrastructure throughout the country highlight the need for UNICEF and humanitarian partners to continue providing emergency services to vulnerable communities.

2 - Activities

Outputs: 1. Effective leadership continues for WASH cluster/inter-agency coordination, with links to another cluster/ sector coordination mechanisms on critical inter-sectoral issues. 2. Provision of timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees. 3. Population affected by famine and high GAM reached with a minimum package of WASH. 4. People in the outbreak (related to cholera/AWD, typhoid, EVD, HEV, Ebola, etc.) prone areas reached by WASH-related preparedness and response activities 5. User-friendly and gender-appropriate WASH facilities and services for people affected by GBV. Activities: Output 1 - Conduct regular WASH Cluster coordination meetings - Conduct coordinated WASH assessments to improve preparedness and response. - Ensure coordinated WASH response and avoid duplication of efforts and minimize gaps in the WASH emergency preparedness and response including IRRM missions. - Improve information management and data collection and dissemination in the WASH cluster Output 2 - Maintain safe water provision for IDPs in the POCs and highly concentrated areas of IDPs including water quality monitoring and treatment. - Re-establish urban water supply systems in conflict-affected states. - Provide new water points and rehabilitate existing facilities in IDP locations, emergency affected areas and host communities, - Support the establishment of sustainable management structures with active women’s participation and representation. - Support solid and liquid waste collection and management in PoCs and IDP locations. - Maintain emergency and semi-permanent latrines and bathing facilities at IDP camps and host communities with separate blocks and appropriate facilities for males and females. - Support vulnerable communities on the construction of family latrines. - Develop relevant information, education, and communication materials to support social mobilization for behavior change. - Promote improved hygiene practices, including water safety and cholera control, in IDP camps, settlements, vulnerable, and host communities. - Support participatory community management of cholera outbreak and support resilience-building activities. Output 3 - Children admitted for SAM treatment discharged with WASH/Hygiene kit from functional OTP/SC. Children admitted for SAM/MAM treatment have access to safe water at OTP/SC Output 4 - WASH-related diseases vulnerable population provided with access to safe drinking/chlorinated water. - WASH-related diseases vulnerable population provided with functional handwashing facilities by soap or 0.05% chlorine running water - WASH-related diseases vulnerable population provided with a WASH minimum package of activities adapted to their vulnerabilities. Output 5 - GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff to ensure safe water and access in a secure location as agreed by them. - GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff to ensure safe sanitation and hygiene access in a secure location as agreed by them.
