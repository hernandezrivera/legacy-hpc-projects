Project Summary

North East Syria (NES) continues to experience conflict and displacement, whilst access to health care for the population remains a serious concern, particularly in hard to reach and recently retaken areas of Hasakeh, Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor (DeZ). Un Ponte Per (UPP) with its local partner contribute to improving access to health care for war affected persons.
Through this action, in continuation with previous project phases (Jun. 2017-Jan.'19), partners aim to ameliorate health conditions for the population in the humanitarian response. Through community based approaches it will provide prevention outreach in health&protection for supporting local resilience. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured to address barriers for vulnerable groups. It will provide health services to all free of charge, with specialized services to women and children. Public health system is supported with improved hospitals emergency capacity, ambulance system and referral pathways.
UPP and its partner provide timely and quality access to life-saving treatment and life-sustaining primary health care for war affected persons, where few providers can reach. This action will provide continuity of services to the Emergency Primary Care Center (EPCC) opened under phase I in west Raqqa city, including trauma care due to explosive remnants of war (ERW) to returnees in need, while the city is being rebuilt. In conflict ridden DeZ, action will respond to needs of IDPs in camps and undeserved areas through an ambulance-mobile clinic network (25 ambulances), the continuation of EPCC service in Areesha and set up a basic EPCC in Abu Khashab camp, where needs identified are high.
The expected result is to provide access to care reaching 96.000 beneficiaries over 12 months including IDPs, returnees, refugees, local population. It also expects to train 200 local staff operating in emergency. In such a volatile context the project provides the capacity to quickly adapt to emerging health needs in other NES locations.

Needs

The Syria conflict is entering its 8th year and deterioration of the health system continued. Access to health care remained a major gap, particularly in recently retaken and hard-to-reach areas of Hasakeh, Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor (DeZ) and Aleppo Governorates in North East Syria (NES). Of 16 public hospitals functioning pre-conflict, only 2 were fully functioning, 9 partially functioning. Of 278 public primary health centres existing before, 1 was fully functioning and 103 were partially functioning (Health Cluster Bulletin, Oct. 18). A serious lack of professional training and local health staff and resources on trauma limited the response. Local authorities were not capable to fully cope with the humanitarian needs. In 2018, poliomyelitis reappeared and outbreaks of typhoid fever, bloody diarrhoea and other water-related infectious diseases hit DeZ Gov. and IDP camps. An unprecedented scale of ERW/IED contamination continued to pose significant protection and health risks. RAQQA: According to OCHA, 166.305 persons had returned in the city since the end of the end of the battle for Raqqa in Oct. 17. However, conditions were not fully met for a safe, dignified and sustainable return (OCHA, Dec. 2018). The city's health facilities status remained critical. The National Hospital remained closed, while UPP and local partner reopened its maternity section in Sept. Raqqa relied mainly on private hospitals, but treatment costs remained high (WHO, Sept.2018). UPP and local partners established an EPCC after the end of the battle for Raqqa, located in one of the most populated neighborhood, covers a catchment population estimated at 50.000 (1/3 of the tot pop.). Serving an average of 250 patients/day, it remained amongst few providers of 24h emergency trauma care, specialized treatment for patients with chronic diseases (UPP assessment, Dec. 2018). UPP and local partner ambulances provided the main emergency referrals service and PHC outreach in rural Raqqa. Trauma care needs remained high and chronic diseases (8% of tot cases) were also significant. Through beneficiaries' and local authorities consultations UPP assessment identified low awareness amongst returnee community in a high health and protection risk environment. Early marriage and IPV were widespread cited negative coping mechanisms. Gaps included barriers to equal access to health&protection services, especially for most vulnerable identified groups. DEIR-EZ-ZOR: Ongoing conflict in southern DeZ Gov. still provoked significant displacement, partly redirected to IDPs camps in the North or Hasakeh Gov. (Al Hol, Abu Khashab). Health care was a priority need in the whole gov. (REACH, Oct. 2018). In Dec. 10.000 IDPs from DeZ remained in Areesha camp (Hasakeh Gov.). Serving an average 250 patients/day, the UPP and local partner EPCC (established in Aug.2017) remained the only provider of emergency healthcare 24h, PHC and referrals to health & protection services. Assessed gaps included low awareness amongst IDP communities in camps with poor water/sanitation conditions; low awareness on RH and GBV. Socio-cultural barriers prevented equal access to health and protection for the most vulnerable (girls, children, elderly, PwDs, FHHs).In Abu Khashab camp, an informal settlement initially born as a transit IDP screening point, the population almost doubled in 1 month, between Nov.-Dec. 2018: from 2.500 in Aug. to 4.125 IDPs in Dec. According to NES Forum acknowledged UPP assessment, the main gap in the camp remained the absence of a permanent 24h health facility. Closest health facilities were ⪆45 mins. Basic emergency maternal service was identified a crucial need for approx. 200 pregnant women and 1.000 women of child-bearing age. IPV and early marriage were identified as widespread forms of GBV. Among the most vulnerable groups, FHHs, children under6 and out-of school adolescents were deemed most at risk. Lack of awareness of GBV was also a major concern.

Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will provide continuity to services established in 2017-18 in project locations, including the Emergency Primary Care Centres and the ambulance network. New activities identified by UPP needs assessment (Dec 2018) include the setup of basic clinic in Abu Khashab camp as well as additional ambulances. Capacity building in medical and protection modules will continue to be a key activity, targeting 200 local staff for current and future responses. As well as further efforts towards strengthening protection mainstreaming, a new set of activities are aimed at strengthening resilience in the affected population both in terms of health and protection through a community based approach. The result is that an expected 96.000 war affected persons in NES will have access to emergency primary care at EPCCs and through referrals, that protection is mainstreamed enhancing access to case management and that persons’ capacity for self protection is raised through community awareness campaigns. A1. Enhancement of the ambulance system and staffing: 4 new ambulances for intensive care will be procured to support the existing network and the referral pathway, increasing the emergency response capacity of the local partner to possible new conflict scenarios. A2. Setting up and continuous support of the EPCCs in providing Life-saving and Primary Health Care assistance: Raqqa and Areesha EPCCs will continue to provide Maternal care including Emergency and urgent delivery, paediatric care for children under 5 and Internal care; Level 1 Emergency trauma care; Life-assistance through ambulances and MMUs; Referral and transport to hospitals for stabilized cases. EPCCs will further develop (5) comprehensive programs of primary care, in line with best standards: 1) Emergency medicine, 2) Maternal, 3) Children, 4) Communicable diseases (IPC), 5) Chronic diseases. In Abu Khashab camp, the project will set up and provide full support to a new static emergency medical point offering: Emergency care (1 caravan); BEmONC services 24h (1 caravan); Primary care consultations 2 days/week. A3. Referral of patients through established pathway including transport and stabilization services: Ambulances may be deployed according to assessed needs as assistant medical mobile unit (MMU) providing basic services in rural areas and/or for referrals transporting patients to nearest hospitals or EPCCs. A4. Support to the emergency response capacity of referral hospitals: provision of medical equipment and medicines for trauma care and by deploying ambulances to the hospitals for referral. Support and equip the Emergency Departments of pre-selected public hospitals (Ain Al Arab, Ras El Ain, Menbij and Tel Tamer) in order to standardize quality of care. A5. Capacity Building in health and protection response: Local partner staff training will include modules in health and in protection (Communicable diseases, Maternal care, Child care, Chronic Diseases, BLS-ALS, Community health, GBV awareness, protection mainstreaming, Child protection, PFA, PSEA). A6. Community Health Outreach and Prevention: expanding health services in the community, reaching the most vulnerable groups while reinforcing local capacities and building the resilience of the war-affected population to future crises. The strategy foresees a community-based approach in services planning and provision and deployment of Community Health Workers (CHWs) at EPCCs adapted to each location. A7. Protection Prevention and Community Outreach: aimed at reinforcing protection mainstreaming on the field with Protection Prevention Officers, referrals and carry out information dissemination through sensitization campaigns and awareness raising sessions on protection. A8. Protection Mainstreaming and Referral services to protection case management actors: capacity building for local staff, implementation of feedback mecanims; referrals to specialized case management actors.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that a stable situation allows the EPCCs to be operative 24h/day and refer cases to hospitals in other areas of North East Syria. The emergency system has been built based on this primary assumption and proved to be effective in saving lives of people from Raqqa and in the rest of NES. Further, referrals have been effective due to the collaboration among civil and military actors, NGOs and private and public hospitals. Coordination is maintained through the NES Forum and OCHA, which perform continued advocacy with military actors on behalf of all the INGOs to assure accessibility and safety of humanitarian space in crisis areas. Protection referral mechanisms are not yet standardized in all action locations and depend on NES Forum Protection sub-working group's actors sufficient level of information sharing/coordination capacity. The main risks are the following:- Escalation of violence between SDF and GoT and new displacement waves (high risk);- Start of violence between GoS and SDF (medium risk);- Withdrawal of US/Coalition Forces may lead to destabilization in NES (high risk);- IS is not defeated and takes back some areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- Use of chemical weapons (medium risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk);- Cross-border INGOs relocate staff further west and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (medium risk);- EPCCs, referral hospitals and project staff become a target in the conflict (medium risk);- Demining operations are stopped in Raqqa and DEZ (medium risk). Mitigation strategy consists in setting up or keeping extremely flexible EPCCs in order to move them according to emerging war scenarios. All the equipment and staff may be deployed in other locations within few days (Raqqa city, Raqqa Governorate, Aleppo Governorate, Hassakeh Governorate, Deir-ez-Zor Governorate, other IDPs and refugees camps in NES) according to needs and if the scenario changes. Particular attention will be given to the commodity disposal, especially the ones included in the cold chain. Local partner is able to shift its support to facilities where is needed or use mobile response to follow population needs. In case of a serious deterioration of the situation in NES and of complete lack of accessibility for international staff, depending on the context, UPP will choose between a mixed strategy of direct and remote management (preferred), maintaining its field office with national/local staff under the supervision of senior management responsible for the Action from Dohuk office in KRI. In case UPP is obliged to close its Syria mission, the Action will be remotely managed by UPP from Dohuk office in KRI, and the activities will be carried out by the implementing partner.
