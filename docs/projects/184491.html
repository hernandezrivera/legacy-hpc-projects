Project Summary

GOAL Sudan takes a holistic approach to WASH in our areas of operation. Quality water supply, hygiene promotion, sanitation facilities, training and management, and environmental waste are all elements of our WASH program, which is implemented as part of a multi-sectoral response. Nutrition, WASH, and Health activities are all linked. In GOAL’s nutrition and health facilities, where existing water facilities are not adequate or available, GOAL will rehabilitate existing water points with hand pumpssource which are located near the GOAL supported facilities, to ensure they have access to safe water. In addition,
targeted water points source will contribute to improving access to sustainable and safe water in
selected villages where existing waterpoints water system are non-functional. Water committees responsible for the appropriate management of the water supply (and collection of water user fees for operations and maintenance) are trained on management of water systems, awareness on over-pumping and the importance of ground water recharge. In addition, the WASH committees established through this project will be empowered to play a key role in project monitoring, including identifying needs and service delivery gaps in WASH programming (reporting these findings to partners and local stakeholders), and supporting GOAL in household assessments, distribution monitoring and WASH monitoring. As well as providing valuable additional data, this approach has the aim of increasing community ownership and
sustainability by embedding management and monitoring within community structures. The community-level feedback and complaints mechanism offers an additional cross-check and source of information on project successes, challenges and gaps. In GOALsupported health (including nutrition) facilities, clinic staff are responsible to for proper the management of medical solid waste including biological waste. GOAL will construct incinerators in all GOAL supported health facilities in order to ensure proper disposal of medical waste these will be operated and managed by the clinic staff. All the clinic staff will be trained on the use and maintenance of incinerators. However, the training will particularly target the facility cleaning staff, guards, and in some clinics a selected staff member to ensure that they handle and put the waste into the incinerators without endangering their own health by making sure that they handle waste for disposal using the Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and that sharp boxes are not emptied into the incinerator but rather they are disposed into the incinerator together with their contents (sharps) to further reduce the
risk of needle pricks. GOAL currently covers the cost of fuel for running incinerators for supported facilities as part of running costs but will work out a way to sustainably cover this. In addition, during the project period GOAL will conduct universal precautions training for MAs, Pharmacists, MWs and vaccinators. In addition, our Nutrition Impact & Positive Practices (NIPP), School Health Club, Care Group, and other community education activities, all include hygiene promotion as a key topic to improve and address hygiene related community health concerns. In order to build capacity, increase ownership and clarify responsibility for the management and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, GOAL is in the process of
implementing the UNICEF WASH FIT framework for adoption across the health and nutrition facilities. WASH FIT is a: “practical guide that provides a description of a risk based, continuous improvement framework and associated tools for undertaking water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) improvements as part of wider quality improvements in health care facilities. WASH FIT extends beyond water quality to address sanitation, hygiene, health care waste and other aspects of environmental health and health care
facility management and staff empowerment.

1 - Needs

Access to safe WASH system and services at community level and institution (health facilities and schools) remain inadequate in the facing of growing demand. GOAL considers WASH needs on their own and as interlinked with health, nutrition, and FSL needs. The majority of the water infrastructure (hand pumps , water yards ) has not received adequate maintenance for number of years due to lack of funding and technical support from the government leaving 69% of the population have to resort to accessing water from medium to high risk water sources . Accordingly, contamination and insufficient water treatment is a rising concern. GOAL and inter-agency needs assessments from Q4 of 2018, as well as GOAL’s baseline data collection from Q3 2019, found critical WASH needs in Talodi, Habila and Rashad localities, with both the general population as well as health facilities in particular unable to access clean water, without adequate sanitation infrastructure and services, and using poor hygiene practices that can contribute to disease. In Talodi, the Water Supply Unit receives no funds to ensure proper operations and maintenance of the water systems. According to a GOAL assessment conducted in December 2019, 50% of the handpumps are non-functional forcing health facilities to fetch and purchase water from long distances, exponentially increasing the risk of contamination during transit. An inter-agency assessment found out that only 45% of Talodi’s population of over 42,000 (plus 30,000 gold miners working nearby) was covered by functional water sources. Much of the population still uses seasonal ponds dug to store water from the rainy season(hafirs) shared by human and livestock. In Habila, a similar situation remains and is compounded by the poor groundwater situation, where most boreholes can only yield adequate flow only for a small hand pump, yet only 58% of the pumps in the locality are functional. Otherwise the population and service providers rely on hafirs in Habila as well, where water requires filtering before it is safe to use. In Rashad, GOAL assessments found that 25 hand pumps and 10 water yards non-operational, which GOAL is beginning to address with existing funding, but the groundwater situation in Rashad also limits theyield of even the most precisely drilled boreholes. As indicated on the GOAL need assessment the sanitation coverage very low in both state and only 37 % of the assessed HHs have access to sanitation facilities at their homes. There are also many gaps in safe hygiene practices due to limited or lack of access to essential personal hygiene items. Hygiene and environmental health are critical components of integrated programs as well, to prevent spread of diseases, reduce vectors, promote good nutrition, and allow for proper IPC in health facilities. According to Kutum’s July 2019 MICS report only 23.7% of people surveyed in the targeted areas knew even three of the five critical times to wash hands, 34.9% of people reported washing their hands at least three critical times with no significant change from 2016. In all localities in South Kordofan open defecation is common due to low latrine coverage 60% for Kutum,69% for Habila, 68% for Rashad, 70% for Abukarshola and 70% for Talodi in general (but only 5% for IDPs in Talodi). As part of the routine WASH assessments during implementation of GOAL’s ongoing WASH projects, and the households observation. it severally pointed to gaps in effective observance of hygiene practices due lack of hygiene knowledge and limited or lack of essential hygiene promotion items including female/hygiene dignity kits. And also skills related to the changing conditions continue to be necessary, such as safe household-based water treatment and advice on re-using water, menstrual hygiene management, menstrual hygiene waste management, personal hygiene and environment hygiene for pregnant and lactating women, and safe disposal of children faeces.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Use of solar powered pumps reduces the need for fuel and thus emissions of greenhouse gasses. - Training water committees for rehabilitated water points allows for improved management of water sources, ensuring that the water sources are not over-used thus reducing the long-term sustainability of water resources - Clean-up campaigns will remove harmful solid waste from the communities to reduce potential environmental damage due to unmanaged rubbish and solid waste in the environmentFor any technical assessments jointly conducted with the government line ministries and departments, and or community representatives, possible environmental impacts of solid waste, contamination of water resources and related mitigation measures will be taken into consideration. All WASH-related infrastructure design and construction, including the planned technical assessments and engineering-feasibility study, will consider environmental issues such as use of environmentally-friendly technology – including solar-power systems. The constructed WASH facilities in health centers and schools will be designed to contribute to the safe removal of waste and minimize pollution in the surrounding environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks to assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partners are aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
