Project Summary

IRSS through the project –emergency WASH intervention to vulnerable community groups affected by the conflict (returnees, IDPs and host communities) in Central equatoria, eastern equatoria and Warrap states, intends to provide emergency WASH services to 100,000 vulnerable population in Central Equatoria (Kajokeji, Morobo,and Lainya) counties, Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta east, Kapoeta south and Kapoeta north) counties, Warrap state (Tonj east and Tonj north) counties. It is expected to improve access to safe, clean and equitable water supply gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene services for vulnerable women, girls, boys and men, elderly, people living with disabilities and people living with HIV/AIDs. Moreover, the program intervention will be providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene services. The AAP community approach will be used to achieve the project objectives that will address the most needed WASH and protection related issues of the vulnerable people and their resilience will be enhanced by addressing the main drivers of vulnerability.
The project will seek to improve timely and equitable access to clean and safe water supply, The project will be achieved through construction of new water sources/boreholes in the areas with critical needs, rehabilitation of existing nonfunctional water sources /boreholes in the communities, health facilities and schools.
153 defunct water points/boreholes will be rehabilitated and restored back, 03 water yards will be rehabilitated. 18 new water points/boreholes will be constructed, 02 surface dams (water pans) will be constructed in the most difficult area for drilling in Lopet locality of Kapoeta east county. 6,120 most vulnerable HHs will be supported with WASH NFIs (plastic jerry cans, washing basins, washing soaps, water storage buckets and water treatment tabs). 5,110 most vulnerable women of child bearing and girls of adolescent age will be supported with women dignity (MHM) kits that will improve their MHM status and protect their dignity during the key times. Behavioral change towards better hygiene practices will be enhanced for the different groups, communities, schools, health facilities and nutrition centers will be targeted with specific gender sensitive hygiene messages to increase their knowledges of hygiene management among different groups in the community. While delivering the most needed WASH services, resilience of the affected population will be enhanced, members from the communities will be trained as water management committees with 30% women representation that will effectively manage their respective water supply and sanitation facilities. The capacity of selected members from the affected population with 60% women representation will be enhanced and strengthened to carry out hygiene promotion activities all across the targeted villages. Community hand pump mechanics will be trained with 20% women representation where appropriate will have special roles of repairing and maintaining community water supply facilities in all the targeted villages.
The intervention is expected to reinforce and strengthen protection of the various groups affected by the conflict IRSS will conduct WASH based protection risk analysis and the intervention will be designed on the understanding of the local context in terms of socio-cultural dynamics to mitigate all risks to various groups women, girls and children and the marginalized of the affected group. Awareness on GBV, sexual exploitation and child abuse and exploitation to raise awareness and inform the affected community of all forms of GBV and sexual exploitation practices that might affect and be able to mitigate and prevent it. The intervention will ultimately contribute to improvement of protection related to WASH and recognition of the dignity of the affected people and ultimately reduce their vulnerability and enhance their resilience.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian situation in south Sudan remain challenging, across the country about 5.9 million south Sudanese people will still need clean and safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) support by the year 2020. These needs are closely link to the conflict (internal displacement), the economic down turn and that has reached its sixth year and still exert further strain on already limited WASH services. Moreover, the huge WASH need is related with natural disaster such as flooding, drought and other climatic factors. It is estimated that about 90% of the population experience limited access to basic safe sanitation hence practice open defecation. It is estimated that only 13% of the population has access to WASH NFIs (jerry cans, washing basins, soaps, water treatment tabs) according to WASH Cluster periodic report in 2018. The prevalence of cases of malnutrition in some of the locations are also linked to poor WASH conditions as exemplified in the diarrhea –malnutrition cycle prevalence in vulnerable populations. Women and girls are exposed to the risks of different forms of gender based violence, these includes harassment, sexual and other physical violence when accessing water or go to nearby bushes. Access to safe water, sanitation and appropriate hygiene remained key needs. June 2019 IRSS assessment report showed that women of child bearing age and adolescent girls does not have access to appropriate menstrual management (MHM) kits and dignified washing locations hence the MHM kits remains key need of the women and the girls. According to assessment result done by UNOCHA led Interagency in which IRSS has participated and IRSS independent assessments in some of the target counties (Kajokeji, Lainya, Morobo, Kapoeta east, south and north, Tonj east and Tonj north) only 30%-45% of water sources/boreholes are functional while over 46% and above of water sources /boreholes are nonfunctional in Kajokeji, Morobo and Lainya this is due to lack of access to spare parts and tools. This leaves a bigger populations without sustained access to safe and clean water supply this increase the burden of water collection for women, girls and small boys. The situation also leads to protection concerns such as rape, sexual exploitation abuse and other forms of GBV across the counties more worse in Kajokeji and Morobo counties in Central Equatoria state. The assessment also indicates the high need for access to spare parts for maintenance of the water sources and consistency of trained community based hand pump mechanics for all the targeted counties including the availability. According to information from Tonj water department 2019 the situation in Makuac and palal of Tonj east county is even worst in which over 65% of all water sources/boreholes are reported nonfunctional resulting to extreme water shortage especially during the dry seasons coupled with high prevalence of conflict over water sources including the need of water for livestock. The report has indicated that shortage of water in Tonji East County is more related to conflict which causes the displacement of hundreds of vulnerable communities in dry season. Thus the need for improving sustained access to safe, clean water supply remains a high priority for the target community. Similarly, IRSS assessment identified critical gaps in WASH services in the two payams of Jie and Mogos in Kapoeta east county caused by conflict that displaced hundreds of vulnerable people to Mogos payam.Different reports showed that sanitation situation remain very poor with about 7%-12%% of the population for Tonj east, Tonj north, Kapoeta east, Kapoeta south and Kapoeta north coupled with poor understanding related to the use of latrines. In Kajokeji, Morobo and Lainya is relatively better (about 20%-30% of the population is having at least uses some kind of traditional pit latrines). 2 - Activities

The project is intended to provide timely and equitable access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene to 100,000 vulnerable people affected by the conflict in the targeted areas which includes IDPs, returnees and host communities. The intervention will ensure:a) Provision of both temporary and sustainable gender sensitive WASH services provided so as to addresses the key and vital needs, public health and protection concerns through an AAP community approach. The program will be targeting the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross –sectoral evaluation and analysis to ensure that the different groups of the affected population have access to safe WASH services. This implies IRSS will undertake broader consultation with the various groups of the affected population –women, men, girls, boys and elderly. It will also ensure that the water supply facilities are safe, accessible and could be used by all members of the target community and similarly will ensure that sanitation facilities will provide services safely and accessible by all groups of the target community. IRSS will conduct risk analysis to ensure that the various groups are equally positively affected by the activities b) Ensure specific activities for GBV mitigation within the target areas will be based on protection assessment conducted by IRSS female staff with women, girls and children. This will guide on putting the WASH infrastructures in sites considered by women, girls and children as secure and agreed upon by respective groups. Borehole and sanitation facilities will be located after a GBV risk analysis is conducted to ensure that all water points and sanitation facilities provide easy and safe access and use of the facilities. Based on the needs identified IRSS will undertake the following activities:- Drilling of 18 new boreholes fitted with Indian MKII/III hand pumps ( Kapoeta east 02, Kapoeta south 04, Kapoeta north 04, Tonj east 4, Tonj north 04).- Construction of 02 water pans in most difficult areas in eastern equatoria (Kapoeta east 01, Kapoeta south 01).- Rehabilitation of 153 defunct boreholes (Kajokeji 30, Morobo 10, Lainya 10, Kapoeta east 15, Kapoeta south 02, Kapoeta north 26, Tonj east 30, Tonj north 30).- Rehabilitation of 03 defunct water yards (Kajokeji 01, Tonj east 02).- Rehabilitation of 10 blocks institutional latrines in health facilities and schools(Tonj east 04, Kajokeji 02, Tonj north 02, Kapoeta east 02)- Rehabilitation of 01 water pan in Lopet Kapoeta East County.- Training of 182 water management committee with 30% women representation this is for effective management of the respective water facilities( Kajokeji 31, Morobo 10,Lainya 10, Kapoeta East 18, Kapoeta South 06, Kapoeta North 34,Tonj East 38, Tonj North 34)- Training of 91 community hygiene promoters (Kajokeji 16, Morobo 05, Lainya 05, Kapoeta East 09, Kapoeta South 03, Kapoeta North 17, Tonj East 19, Tonj North 17).- Training 0f 182community hand pump mechanics (Kajokeji 30, Morobo 12,Lainya 11, Kapoeta East 17, Kapoeta South 06, Kapoeta North 34,Tonj East 38, Tonj North 34)- Distribution of 6,120 kit of WASH NFIs to 6,120 HHs.- 5,110 most vulnerable women of child bearing age and girls will be supported with women dignity kits.- 16 sessions (2 sessions per county) on GBV mitigation and prevention will be conducted across all targeted areas ,- Conduct GBV risk analysis in all target areas - 32 sessions (4 sessions per county) of hygiene promotion, awareness and campaigns will be conducted in public places, health facilities, nutrition centers and schools.- Conduct KAP survey in Tonj east and Tonj south.The project will focus on strengthening access to gender sensitive feedback and complaint mechanism in the project sites. 6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project can be partially linked with durable solutions , depending on the political situation in the country that will play a major role for the complete adequate safe protection environment to make it possible for durable solutions to be established , by creating enabling environment , execution of policies and better resource mobilization and allocation to Water , sanitation and hygiene sector through the ministry of physical planning and county department of rural water supply are key players to sustainable durable soultions . The proposed project locations in Kajokeji , Lainya and Yei counties portrays a mixed response towards the link to durable solutions. While Kapoeta east , Kapoeta south , Kapoeta north , Tonj east and Tonj north is a medium risk environment affected by common trend of related inter-communal conflicts ,drought , cattle raiding , water and pasture shortage and population movements in search for resources to meet both needs of human being as well as livestock . It is generally marginalised communities with strong cultural back grounds . The lessons learnt through the previous interventions and on-going projects gives an impression of average safety for stable and durable solutions related to WASH interventions . Kajokeji , Yei and Lainya has been a high risk area till 2019 . with the revitalized peace agreement in Sept-2018 might create positive safe environment , however if by default political insecurity increases this will affect safe environment for durable long term solutions in the area . Presence of armed groups in the area( Kajokeji, Lainya and Yei) that have not been part of the agreement on the revitalized peace agreement late 2018 portrays high risk of safety on the political environment . Hence basic WASH services should be handled by the state and county water supply departments and IRSS like any other partners will advocate for stability in order for the government to take its own responsibility .

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRW is member of CHS Alliance and has all AAP related policies, including PSEA and CP Safeguarding. IRW considers affected population as equal and active partners and they are included in all project phases. By established a functional Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) and reporting procedures at the field offices, all beneficiaries and community members are informed about purpose of CFMs and different options to submit their grievances/complaints. A safeguarding focal point (SFP) and complaint/feedback mechanism (CFM) focal person are available in each of the field offices, and community members have different forms for submitting their complaints and feedbacks – direct in person to SFP and CFM focal persons, via Accountability Committees that IRW establishes in all locations of operations, by phone, mail and in written form in CFM boxes. Under this project, ACs will be established and trained on community deemed vulnerability criteria. AC members will include women, men, PWD and elderly population from the communities, Water Management Committees (WMCs) and Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) trained on the purpose of complaint/feedback mechanisms, as well as how to collect information from the community and beneficiaries, and how to report through the established systems. The CFM focal person has an obligation to record complaint from each complainant on the IRW CFM form with the respect of the confidentiality of the complainant and to inform the complainant in 72 hr if possible, in appropriate, confidential and easy to understand way on decision and actions taken. All IRW staff, volunteers and partners are trained on PSEA and child protection safeguarding issues at the beginning of their assignments, and communities are continuously informed about PSEA and CP policies and how to report any breaches and access to services and support with full respect of their identity, privacy and confidentiality.
