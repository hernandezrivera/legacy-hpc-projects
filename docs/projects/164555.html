Project Summary

The goal of this project is the reduction of morbidity and mortality among the Syrian population in Idleb and Aleppo, by integrating preventive and curative nutritional services within UDER’s community-based program, with focus on appropriate under-five child feeding practices through household visits by UDER’s currently existing 120 CHWs, and by adding six Rapid Response Teams and six nutritionists linked to UDER’s PHC centers i nTermanin and Sarmada sub-districts, and to other actors' centers in Afrin and Azaz.
UDER’s community health project is currently providing community health services according to the standard guidelines developed by the Community Health Working Group (CHWG) in Gaziantep. The proposed project will be implemented with support from the Nutrition Cluster in Gaziantep Hub.
The project forms an important foundation for integrated primary health care and nutrition services to provide Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition CMAM throughout outreach and facility-based activities. The CHWs are already trained on CMAM program and will continue to conduct MUAC screening during household visits for each Pregnant and Lactating Woman PLWs, as well as for every under-five child. Detected cases of acute malnutrition will be referred to UDER's and other actors' centers, especially for those severely malnourished who demand inpatient care. Thus, nutritionists will be localized within the PHC centers besides the pre-existing GPs/Paediatricians to ensure comprehensive management for mild and moderate acute malnutrition cases.
The CH team will be trained on messaging IYCF and best nutritional practices through individual and group sessions, and will refer cases that require further intervention to facility-based staff.
The project is also aimed at fostering the health referral system in Atareb, Dana, Afrin and Azaz sub-districts to ensure continuity of care for the population in target areas, with a focus on IDP concentrations and remote rural communities, given the fact that Dana sub-district has received the largest IDPs influxes, as well as Afrin and Azaz which became of the main intended directions for IDps. These newly affected IDPs comprise the main community groups who will benefit from this project, with special attention to the most vulnerable people who can barely access static services, such as people with no civil documentation (who fear crossing military check-points), extremely poor people with no adequate money for transportation to access services, widowed and divorced women, and PLWs with disabilities or disabled children. For that reason, Rapid Response Teams RRTs will be established in order to respond to the newly arrived IDPs. Each RRT will deploy two CHWs, one Nutritionist, one PSW, one Nurse, plus one midwife and one GP if available. Each team will be supported by a driver and data entry, as well.
Cultural misconceptions and lack of trust with the current health system push patients in NW Syria to fail to adhere to treatment plans and optimal nutritional practices. The project aims at eliminating nutrition and health misconceptions and encourage the community to adhere to standard nutritional practices and prescribed treatments to ensure the best utilization of PHC and nutrition services. A well-targeted health education plan is a demand to raise community awareness about common mal and best practices for prevention of acute and chronic malnutrition. Nutrition interventions heavily rely on communities for identification of cases in need of follow-up care or support for adherence to treatment by the households.

4 - Needs

According to REACH, HNAP and CCCM since 1 Dec 2019 as of 2 Feb 2020, over 560,000 IDPs moved from southern and eastern Idleb towards areas closer to the Turkish boarders, where Dana, Afrin and Azaz sub-districts are among the most intended areas for IDP influxes. Over 80% of IDPs are women and children.Based on Nutrition Cluster data, the severity scale is 5 in Dana, with 742,513 total population estimation, of which; 59,401 PLWs and 148,503 children 6-59 months where 22,275 of them are with disability. That makes 207,904 PiNThe severity scale in Azaz is 4, with 251,839 total population estimation (13,599 PLWs, 38,783 children 6-59 months of which 5,817 are with disability). That makes 52,383 PiNWhile in Afrin it is 4, with 108,355 total population estimation (5,851 PLWs, 16,687 children 6-59 months, 22,537 PiN).UDER’s MUAC screening data shows around 60 cases of SAM and MAM discovered per month in each of Atareb, Sarmada and TermaninSMART survey findings in 2019 shows that malnutrition in PLWs increased at least five-fold from 2018 to 2019. While at least one out of eight children in Syria is chronically malnourished, this ratio is worse in areas affected by displacements, returns, and over-burdened communities including Idlib (1 out of 5 children), and Aleppo (1 out of 4 children).IYCF practices in Syria remain poor with artificial feeding and bottle-feeding identified as common but harmful practices. Only 28.5% of infants < 6 months are exclusively breastfed, and 26.5% of 6-24-month-old children have a minimum acceptable diet.IRC conducted an RNA in late December 2019 in 11 communities of Dana and Maaret Tamasrin sub-districts, which revealed that all participants confirmed limited movement. Similarly, patient exit interviews collected at UDER facilities show that transportation presents a significant challenge to accessing fixed facilities for vulnerable communities especially for elderlies, people with disabilities, women and young mothers.

5 - Implementation of activities

REA has already trained 120 CHWs on CMAM and MUAC screening. Each CH team is assigned to 250 households and responsible for conducting MUAC screening during household visits for every single pregnant or lactating woman, as well as every under-five child. The team maintains records of cases of malnutrition and follows up on their health and nutrition needs according to the standard SoP developed by the CH Working Group.In order to respond to the needs of new IDPs, UDER will establish six Rapid Response Teams. Each team will comprise of two CHWs, one Nutritionist, one PSW, and one Nurse, plus one midwife and one GP if available, supported by a driver and data entry. Each of those teams will be able to cover between 3,500 and 7,000 PLWs and children mainly for nutrition-related problems by screening, detection, education and referral. The intervention is aimed at being comprehensive as much as possible to include all psychological, physical and social aspects of covered communities.Nutritionists will be positioned at UDER’s PHCs to screen all children under 5 and PLWs present at the health facilities, and provide CMAM outpatient care treatment for malnourished cases. They will also supervise the work of the CHWs, conduct refresher training, and constantly improve IEC materials and messages delivered through individual and group educational sessions.Moreover, nutritionists will provide one-to-one counseling to mainstream best practices of IYCF among PLWs visiting the PHC centers or referred by CHWs.Data is collected and submitted securely by CHWs during HH visits through Kobo surveys using the tablets distributed to all of them. MEAL manager will analyze the data to feed in the Program Manager decisions.Besides TPM, beneficiary satisfaction surveys, random site visit and post-intervention evaluation will be conducted by UDER’s supervisors and team leaders. Data collected allows continuous monitoring on a daily basis for necessary corrective actions

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Children receiving nutrition services are over-exposed to the risk of harm by nutrition personnel, organization staff or related entitiesProbability: moderate. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- Child Safeguarding polices and systems are in place and regularly monitored for corrective action to be taken when needed;- Nutritionists will be provided regular psychosocial support services; 2- CHWs steal items from households.Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- Contract include a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct of that kind;- All staff sign and commit to REA’s Code of Conduct;- Enhance Complaint and Feedback mechanism;- Hot line for allegations of SEA misconducts is in place;- Regular surveys conducted by the MEAL team to monitor the conducted visits. The surveys include question about team members behaviors, misconducts and their respect of privacy; 3- Household data and privacy is exposed to leakage or destruction.Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- Record all beneficiary data on an online database through Kobo surveys and using tablets. This prevents data storage on hard or soft copies which are exposed to be stolen or destroyed. To enhance confidentiality, beneficiaries’ names are only accessed by the information team. Family records are coded anonymously before sharing with other team members.- All tablets are password protected and remotely managed by the information team in Turkey to allow immediate data deletion if the tablets were stolen or lost; 4- CHWs are harassed by the community or local authorities.Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- CH and RR team members are mainly recruited from the local community to make sure trust is exchanged between them and communities they serve. The project is backed by local health authorities and community leaders through ongoing communication and trust-building.- CH and RR teams have visible IDs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions include:- Permission for REA to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues.- Current conflict doesn't affect program activities.- Donor funds continue to be provided for NW Syria.- Community acceptance for this project continues.Risks and mitigation measures: There is a potential continuum of violence escalation in Idleb through a military offensive on southern Idleb, or fights among Organized Armed Groups as the Government of Turkey (GoT) leads an effort to eliminate sanctioned armed groups. REA’s chosen locations for this project (Atareb and Dana subdistricts) are located near to the Turkish border and away from potentially targeted locations. Continuous massive IDP movement to northern Idleb and Aleppo might put increased pressure on the health system in northern Syria. This requires maximizing the coverage of nutrition, primary and secondary healthcare systems. REA will ensure that the network of CHWs and RRTs continues to be in place to be ready to respond to potential waves of IDPs. Close communication will be maintained with Health Directorates, local councils and community leaders to ensure that our teams are covering the communities with the highest concentration of IDPs. Given that a functional mobile phone network does not exist in Idleb and Aleppo, REA will equip the CH team with communication devices (walkie talkies or local mobile phone subscriptions) to ensure that they can contact with the team leaders, provide information about their location, and receive immediate guidance from them and facility professionals as needed. Team Leaders will be primarily responsible for handling and reporting security incidents related to their teams, and they will receive support on safety and security measures, including proper walkie talkie communication practices.
