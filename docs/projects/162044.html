Project Summary

Under GBV Sub Sector, Caritas will conduct the Women and Girls’ Friendly Spaces (WGFS) project which aims to promote the protection, empowerment and resilience of Rohingya women and girls. Through this project, we are targeting 300+ women for regular attendance and participation in each WGFS. Targets specific to each camp will be finalised contingent upon additional funding as well as according to the needs and population figures in the camp. The project has four components: life skills and resilience programming; psychosocial support and case management; awareness and community-based protection; engaging men and boys in GBV prevention.

Needs

1. A high number of women and girls have been exposed to widespread and severe forms of sexual violence in Myanmar before and during their flight to Bangladesh. Following displacement, they continue to be at risk of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), including domestic violence, forced and early marriage, and sexual exploitation. Abuse and neglect inside the home have reportedly increased since the beginning of the influx, along with practices like polygamy. Response to trends of Intimate Partner Violence is rendered difficult by the lack of access to justice and the perceived impunity in the camp. The most urgent need to respond to this high risk of GBV for all women is quality and accessible GBV response services, which include quality survivor-centered case management services and psychosocial support services. 2. In the 5th SPP assessment conducted in 33 out of 34 refugee camps and settlements in Cox’s Bazar District from 25 June - 5 August, 2019, 35% of women and 43% of men reported that advice of about safety issues is one of interventions needed to improve the sense of security and safety in the camps. However, restriction of women and adolescent girls’ movements in camps and public spaces further hinder their ability to access entry points that provide information on GBV response, services, information and other possibilities of support. This hinders their recovery and healing. Based on findings from the MSNA and other sources, safety remains a key consideration for women and girls, with 50% reporting that they do not feel safe when accessing facilities such as latrines and bathing facilities. Therefore, women and girls’ safe spaces remain crucial entry points for them to come together and heal in a safe space as well as to receive vital information on topics related to protection, GBV prevention, early and forced marriage prevention and skills development. 3. Many women and men are not aware of the existing GBV services and the possibilities of support in the camp. The need for appropriate and widespread information on GBV, and prevention and response services is acute, through awareness sessions targeting different groups of the population. 4. A significantly high number of women and girls are illiterate, with few opportunities to learn as education opportunities for adolescents and adults, vocational training, and livelihoods, are restricted. The lack of opportunities for women increases their vulnerability, their exclusion of the community life, and adoption of negative coping mechanisms that are potentially harmful. There is a need for women to be engaged in life skills activities that build their resilience and decrease their vulnerability to foster social and economic empowerment. 5.. There is a pressing need to engage men and boys in an accountable manner to prevent and respond to GBV. The need for continuous and structured engagement with men and boys is important to ensure the sustainability of GBV programming as well as to mitigate backlash and resistance to gender transformative work that forms the core of GBV prevention activities. Men and boys in the community need to be sensitized on ways to become stronger and accountable allies. There’s a need for greater awareness and buy-in from men and boys as well as religious and community leaders on the importance of services for women and girls, including women and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS)/women friendly spaces (WFS).

Activities or outputs

"Under GBV Sub Sector, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will conduct the Women and Girls’ Friendly Spaces (WGFS) project which aims to promote the protection, empowerment and resilience of Rohingya women and girls. CB currently runs 5 WFS: Camp 1E, Camp 17, Camp 19, Camp 20 extension. The WGFS focuses on key areas as detailed below:1. Life skills and resilience programming: Women and girls will have an opportunity to attend life skills sessions to improve gain knowledge and skills in a wide range of areas, including sewing, tailoring, crafts making. This intervention seeks to empower Rohingya women and girls to take ownership of the WGFS as active participants and as co-facilitators of sessions – sharing their unique talents and using their voice to educate and support their own. 2. Structured PSS sessions and case management: With each WGFS staffed by a team consisting of PSS and GBV case workers, the WGFS will provide a safe and confidential entry point for individuals at risk and survivors of GBV to access services. In 2020, CB will roll-out structured PSS sessions with facilitators using No Strings International (NSI)’s film (in Rohingya language), theatre and puppetry as well as other creative arts approaches. The WGFS team will be using these materials in structured PSS sessions to cover topics such as normalizing emotions, stress management, positive parenting, identifying and reporting protections concerns (e.g. abuse, neglect, violence, child marriage). 3. Awareness raising and community-based protection: Awareness raising on issues such as early marriage, intimate partner violence, trafficking, etc will take place in the WGFS. Women and girls would be encouraged to propose their ideas and facilitate sessions themselves. 4. Engaging men and boys in GBV prevention: With our participation in the Male Engagement Task Team (METT), we will ensure men and boys are engaged in an accountable way through structured activities (awareness sessions outside of WGFS) to build allyship, sensitize them on the importance of WGFS and the benefits it brings to women and girls as well as to transform negative gender norms. 5. Internal mechanism to prevent and respond to PSEA: CB has a zero tolerance approach to SEA. CB has an existing national PSEA policy that applies to all its staff, vendors, consultants and volunteers. In 2020, CB will be conducting regular trainings for its senior management, sector managers and field staff on PSEA reporting and response. 6. Staff trainings, coaching and mentoring: The WGFS team have been trained on GBV Core Concepts, Inter-Agency GBV Case Management Guidelines, IASC GBV Guidelines, Caring for Child Survivors of Sexual Abuse, CP Principles and Policies, PFA, GBV Case Management for Men and Boys, CP Case Management Guidelines, IASC MHPSS Guidelines, PSEA, Child Safeguarding, and GBVIMS. With support from Caritas MHPSS Specialist and CRS Technical Advisor, the WGFS team will continue to receive structured coaching, supervision and mentoring, including a case management refresher every 3 months. 7. DRR: CB will continue its ongoing DRR awareness sessions on landslides, cyclone preparedness and monsoon floods in the WGFS. CB will also conduct quarterly DRR trainings for its GBV staff (incorporating this into safety planning and working closely with the GBV SS to advocate with the CiCs on the importance of retaining WGFS as a women and girls’ only space given the sensitivity and confidentiality surrounding case files).
