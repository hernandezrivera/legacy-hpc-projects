Project Summary

Through this project, Dorcas and the partner are willing to distribute hygiene kits to 5,000 households living in Aleppo in vulnerable conditions and in need of hygiene kits as prevention of Covid-19 pandemic.
Moreover, through this project, Dorcas will be conducting awareness sessions and delivering flyers related to messages of prevention for Covid-19. The distribution will be done once and will not be provided regularly, it is considered as a one off distribution for urgent intervention related to Covid-19, this modality was selected based on discussion with the interested donor. The cases will be identified through the 2 Community centers (CC) run by Dorcas under the umbrella of the Presbyterian church in Aleppo (Hanano and Hullok). The project was designed based on a multisectoral needs assessment (MSNA) conducted by Dorcas in October 2021. The selected cases will identified based on well-defined criteria using a scoring system. Selecting and distributing these hygiene items in an appropriate and effective manner is key to successfully implementing a Covid-19 prevention project. The selection will require identifying basic hygiene items as well as additional hygiene items that are determined through consultation with the affected population. It is necessary to coordinate the selection and distribution with other actors in order to ensure hygiene needs are met, duplication is avoided and gaps in the provision of items are addressed. Distribution will be accompanied with demonstrations or training on how to use unfamiliar items. Post-distribution monitoring will be done to assess the use of items, to determine the need for replenishment of items in future projects and to inform further selection and distribution of hygiene items. Deployment of community mobilisers and volunteers from within the affected population is a key factor for the success of the project.

4 - Needs

Dorcas targeted populations have lived the last years under the economic and lockdown stress due to Covid-19 lockdowns have had little to no support and have additionally suffered from the conflicts over past 10 years where some of them have their houses destroyed. According to HNO 2021 in Syria: Over-crowded sites continue to be major challenges in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, where the application of preventive, response and mitigation guidance is essential. COVID-19 presents a highly dynamic environment with a need for preparedness and response. Mainstreaming the COVID-19 response throughout all regular implementation activities, with a focus on ensuring appropriate kits and hand hygiene promotion and a systematic approach to address behavior change are crucial to mitigate risks. In terms of access to hygiene items the needs for specific groups are higher than for the rest of population. Families with young children have additional financial burden & are more likely to not be able to afford some items, like disposable diapers or shampoos for babies. Likewise, HHs staying in IDP sites with women in reproductive age (15-49) face more challenges in accessing hygiene items than the same group in out of IDP sites settings, HHs residing in more urban areas are less likely to face challenges with hygiene items affordability (20%), than those living in more rural communities / smaller towns (40%) or IDP sites (45%). (2021 SYRIA HNO WASH SECTOR)

5 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas and the partner are planning to deliver hygiene kits for 5,000 HH living in Aleppo and considered vulnerable, in addition, Dorcas will be providing awareness sessions for 200 individuals monthly on Covid-19 prevention. The project is expected to take place in 2022 and 2023. The selection will be made through vulnerability criteria and using a scoring system managed by the MEAL department. Early consultation with the affected population will take place to identify the basic and priority items that are appropriate and applicable. Dorcas/the partner will make sure to consult women as they are often most aware of their household needs. Plans will be made to provide training on the proper use of those hygiene items that are unfamiliar to the affected population but are required to protect them from key health risks including COvid-19, otherwise, items may be used. A distribution plan will be in order to ensure effective and efficient distribution. The distribution plan will set clear lines of responsibility between humanitarian actors in terms of ensuring that the proper type, quality and timeliness of the hygiene items is provided and in terms of post-distribution monitoring. To facilitate the distribution, the population will be provided with information on the timing of distribution, location, the content of hygiene kits, target groups within the population, and basic info on covid-19.Dorcas will conduct PDM to assess the importance of such activity

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project will continue the work that Dorcas has been doing through its communitycenters for several years. Dorcas’ community centers/partners are well-known &trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide amultitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like hygieneitems and food parcels. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas developsmany proposals and activities based on information received from its feedbacksystems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the center.Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established in the communitycenters to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activityimplementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listenedto as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in theimplementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations ofsexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protectedagainst sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas ensures beneficiaries havemultiple ways to provide feedback, including a hotline, text messages, emails, & boxestablished within the centers. Dorcas’ long term presence within the community alsoensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows arereached through its services.
