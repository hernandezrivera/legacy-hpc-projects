Project Summary

This project aims is to reduce economic vulnerabilities through the provision of emergency humanitarian assistance and livelihood/employment opportunities.
1. To ensure conflict-affected population is able to meet basic food needs without engaging in negative coping mechanisms
2. To provide livelihood/employment opportunities to conflict-affected population

Needs

The conflict has had a negative impact on Ukraine’s economy. Industrial and agricultural production in Donbass has significantly decreased since the beginning of the conflict. Likewise, unemployment has increased, decreasing household’s income while living costs have remained the same or become more expensive due to scarcity. The cumulative impact of the conﬂict over the past 2.5 years has eroded people’s coping capacity, pushing them deeper into vulnerabilities. According to the FSLC’s Humanitarian Needs Overview, out of 3.4 million people in need 1,6 million people are severely and moderately food insecure in eastern Ukraine, in addition to 37,000 IDPs residing in other oblasts, who also need food assistance. Their needs are different in sector, type and scale. 2016 saw a worrisome deterioration of the protection environment ensued from predicaments spanning from continuous ﬁghting, and protracted displacement, to suspension of social beneﬁts and pensions. In addition, socio-economic exclusion for people living in NGCA and along the ‘contact line’, disruptions/loss of income and livelihoods across much of the east, and higher cost of living trigger unsafe returns and have a knock-on effect on other aspects, including access to healthcare.Between 2013 and 2017 the percentage of the population living below the actual minimum subsistence level increased from 20% in 2013 to 74% in 2017 in Luhanska oblast and from 22% to 66% in Donetska oblast (“Analysis food security and socio-economic situation 29 march 2018”)1.Food Security Significant humanitarian needs remain unmet with some 558,000 people severely and moderately food insecure on NGCA (decreased from 800,000 people in 2017). Of them, around 103,000 people are severely food insecure across the NGCA. Overall, moderate and severe food insecurity was recorded at 17.4 per cent in October 2017 , while of these, some 3.2 per cent are severely food insecure (decreased in 2% compared to 5.2% in June 2017).Conflict has had a negative impact on employment security as almost one-fifth of active households lost employment since the conflict began. Some 45 per cent of the IDPs have difficulties in finding a new job, while youth unemployment rate is 21% and the level of unemployed youth IDPs is 42% compared to 35% of unemployed IDPs (with 68% of unemployed IDPs being women ).Forty-nine (49) percent of unemployed IDPs report their skills being irrelevant in the current economy as the main reason for their unemployment (REACH Initiative, Livelihoods Assessment, 2017) .The application of (negative) coping strategies due to lack of money to buy food has increased in NGCA to 87% (up from 40%), while in GCA the situation has become slightly better at 53% applying (down from 55%). In GCA, levels of emergency coping strategies remained the same as in the earlier analysis. In GCA, 37% of HHs interviewed (up from 27%) are borrowing money or have debts whereas in NGCA this percentage is 43% (this was not covered in 2016 MSNA). Most of them borrow money from family or friends.In 2016 women earned on average only 73.7% of men’s average monthly wage, which has not changed in nearly 20 years.[1] Such gender-based wage gaps create further vulnerability to poverty for women, especially female-headed households. It is estimated that 21% of female-headed households and female IDPs have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue more vulnerable in GCAs (FSL Cluster)Level of unemployed youth IDPs is 42% compared to 35% of unemployed IDPs[2] (with 68% of unemployed IDPs being women[3). A higher economic vulnerability increases the risks for women and girls to engage in negative coping strategies, including prostitution

Activities & outcomes

Activities1) Distribution of cash grants (contact line GCA) Targeted conflict affected HHs are better able to cover their basic needs through unconditional restricted cash transfers.- Assessment of locations, coordination with local authorities and other NGOs, registration of beneficiaries. - Selection of beneficiaries based on vulnerability criteria, baseline.- Selection of: financial provider for cash transfers- Sign frame work agreement with financial suppliers- Community meetings - beneficiary training on cash delivery mechanism.- Distribution of cash (one or several tranches)- Vendor’s payment and reconciliation.- Post distribution monitoring\endline, monitoring of proper cash utilization between tranche. SCI will try to implement cash grants for food program both in contact line GCA. For the last two years SCI has a lot of experiences implementing different types of cash programs in GCA through different financial suppliers. 2) Conducting training on transversal skills and in entrepreneurship (GCA) Targeted affected households will receive relevant skills and knowledge for employment opportunities through participation in training on transversal skills and in entrepreneurship. Providing micro-grants for successful businesses ( GCA and NGCA in-kind) Identification and pre-selection of beneficiaries for participating in trainings Frame work agreement with training providers  Business skill/plans, financial and other relevant trainings Selection of final beneficiaries based on submitted business-plans Baseline Distribution of cash\in-kind to beneficiaries (cash transfers can be done in several tranches with conditionality assessment of proper cash utilization between tranches) Post distribution monitoring in 1, 3, 6 months/endline SCI is implementing successfully such activities for the last two years in GCA and NGCA. Beneficiaries in GCA are receiving cash-based micro-grants through bank transfer, while NGCA program participants are receiving support with equipment, tools and materials due to constrains in bank system in NGCA. The Livelihoods Restoration activities allowed people to use and build on their existing skills and knowledge to generate income for their families. Prior to receiving the micro grant, beneficiaries are participating in business management trainings. Within the project duration Baseline and End line, surveys will be conducted in order to measure the impact of the programs and decrease of negative copying mechanisms. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted to assess the outcome of the activities, conditionality assessments will be conducted between cash transfers to ensure proper cash utilization SCI will use lesson-learned, PDM and other relevant information (assessments) to design the activities and programs to ensure the best quality of the activities is achieved. Also, the focus group discussions and personal interviews with potential beneficiaries and local authorities will be conducted to meet the particular needs of targeted population in targeted locations. On-going monitoring activities are in place thought out the whole implementation of the projects to ensure the quality of the projects and to update the activities based on the feedback from beneficiaries. SCI's hot-line is set up to receive complains and suggestions from beneficiaries. The information is used to update the activities and implementation in order to receive the best outcomes of the projects. The cash transfer amount which equals 910 UAH (+ 60 UAH bank fee for cash transfers) is the average between the recommendation of FSLC - 825 UAH (the value from WFP monitored food basket) and recommendation of CWG is 970 UAH/person/month. The total amount is calculated for 1566 HH (3 persons per HH) for two months assistance.

Indicators & targets

Objective 1.• 3000 of HHs=women, girls, boys and men in different age groups at all times can cover their food needs through the provision of cash grants (target: 3000 HH=10000 ben), GCA – 30%, NGCA – 70% (if feasible – cash, if not – in-kind) • 80% of money received by targeted HH is utilized to meet basic food needs• 80% of HHs reporting decrease in using of negative (stress) coping strategies related to food access Objective 2.Training on transversal skills and in entrepreneurship• 1000 of people particularly young people are benefiting from training, entrepreneurship, internship, etc.• 200 of HHs receiving grants utilize 90% of money received as specified in agreements • 70% of targeted HHs exhibit an improved level of income at the end of the project • 150 of targeted HHs who continue to run the business in 6 months, 1 year Indicators1) % of targeted households have increased capacity to cover their essential food needs and reducing negative coping strategies through provision of cash grants2) % of women and men are in different age groups satisfied (and equally) with the provided assistance and/or modality3) % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age and diversity) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible and participatory manner
