Project Summary

The 4 year-old conflict in the NWSW regions of Cameroon has provoked mass displacement of persons from their comfort zones, seeking shelter into other parts of Cameroon. The displaced population comprising mostly women, young girls, and children have undergone untold suffering, hardship, and neglect. These IDP women, young girls, and children are facing the oddity of war as they live in bushes, dilapidated structures, and go on for days without food. Pregnant women and nursing mothers having sleepless nights on bare floor and devoid of basic health care services. The plight of women, pregnant women, girls, and the elderly needs not to be overemphasized. Devoid of any form of economic, psychological, and health support, women and girls are forced to become breadwinners making them more vulnerable which accounts for a surge in cases of unwanted pregnancies. With this backdrop, 300 cases of pregnant women will be identified in the rural areas of the Center region; Bafia, Ebebda, Makenene, , Ntui, Obala and Littoral; Pouma, Njombe-Penja, Mbanga, Loum, Mombo and Melong. Statistics have proven that all these remote areas in the targeted zones are lacking in quality health care infrastructures as well as health personnel. Equally of note, these localities have registered an exponential influx of displaced persons from the conflict zones thus exposing them to other health hazards. These pregnant women some of them juvenile lack basic health ante-natal and post-natal training and services, which are a big risk not only to their lives but to the lives of the unborn. So far and with the influx of more IDPs into these localities, no appropriate health care policy has been elaborated to cushion these adversities.
A point in case at Ebebda was a 19 year-old by name Emilienne, an IDP from the NW region, diagnosed locally with complicated pregnancy. Shortly on arrival in Yaounde where she was referred, she got a still birth. Primary reasons for such still birth could be: poor feeding, poor and unhealthy living condition, financial hardship, lack of ante-natal services and training, lack of appropriate psychological preparedness and mindset, stress from distance covered from Ebebda to central hospital in Yaounde.
To avoid such wanton loss of lives, CEFONEV do advocate for a downstream health policy for all with special attention accorded women in line with UN Sustainable Development Goals 1, 2 and 3.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The mass displacement of IDP young women and girls into new rural communities has provoked other societal issues among which are economic hardship, high morbidity rate and a surge in pregnancy rate.To these are added; - high mother and infant mortality rate- Quasi neglect of these IDP pregnant women,- unhealthy lifestyle and poor living conditions,- Ignorance of ante-natal and post-natal care by these women in these communities, - Financial constraints by IDP women which has led to untold sufferings,- Lack of health infrastructure and lack of trained health personnel in these localities.To address the above mentioned concerns and challenges identified within these communities, this project will be carried out in collaboration with 2 gynecologists, supported by four mid-wives and ten nurses where the IDP pregnant women will be schooled on healthy practices. They will participate in health screening, vaccinated against diseases and medical checkups. Equally, provision of psycho-social and mental health support for these women will be carried out. There shall be distribution of delivery kits, first aid kits and food items to each of these women.There shall be training of local IDP women-relay-workers who will continue schooling the IDP women on healthy practices beyond the execution of the project.
