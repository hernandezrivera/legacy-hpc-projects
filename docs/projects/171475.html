Project Summary

MdM has 30 years of experience in implementing Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) projects across 45 countries and has been present in Palestine for over 20 years. In 2014, MdM ran a SRH program which provided Family Planning to the community of Al Bureij in Gaza strip. The intervention was successful but highlighted the widespread problem of unwanted pregnancies. An assessment conducted in 2018 by MdM, concluded that Gender Based Violence (GBV) related to unwanted pregnancy continues to be a significant health need in the West Bank (WB) and identified similar factors between GBV and unwanted pregnancies in Gaza. As our experience has identified the multitude of cultural, political and economic factors that affect a woman's right to make decisions regarding her reproductive health, there is a strong need for a rights-based approach to developing programs that decrease violence against Palestinian women and improve access to quality family planning, including emergency contraception.
Based on MdM´s experience in implementing SRH activities in the oPt alongside local partners and based on its continued involvement with the local community and operational networks, the proposed project intends to facilitate access to people and communities without, or excluded from, basic healthcare provisions and provide key components of women´s and SRH.
This will be achieved in the WB Area C trough mobile clinics with a partnership with Health Work Committees (HWCs) and referring patients who are in need to specialized care in Qalqilya governorate to Women´s Health Center of HWCs. The intervention will provide support for GBV survivors of all ages and gender, with transversal mental health support as well as a community-focused approach that reinforces the activities through awareness raising and empowerment activities. That will ensure a strong impact on a long-term perspective.
In Gaza strip, MdM will provide SRH and GBV services, and community awareness sessions in partnership with the Culture of Free and Tought Association (CFTA), which has a long experience and excellent reputation for implementing and advocating for better access to SRH. CFTA has also been a long-term partner of MdM.

1 - Needs

Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) is a fundamental component to aid, as it cuts through both humanitarian and development needs. In times of COVID-19 and throughout protracted conflicts, fear, violence and uncertainty, access to SRH deteriorates as well as the quality of practices, with women and children often and clearly being the most adversely affected but certainly not excluding adolescents and men, which suffer from known but frequently unreported consequences. Women’s autonomy rights remain a key barrier within Palestinian SRH due to a combination of cultural, economic and social factors, in addition to the pressures of occupation, and the lack of services specialized on the continuum of care during COVID-19 pandemic. In 2020 HNO in Palestine reports 1.9 million people (52.6% females) affected by the humanitarian consequences related to protection and displacement, 365 000 of them are facing GVB situations. Gathering data reported from the previous year by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics in the second quarter of 2019, 38% of Palestinian women in Gaza and 24% in the WB, reported psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic violence by their husbands during the preceding year; 11% of currently married or ever married women in Gaza, and 8% in WB experienced sexual violence by their husbands. There is no information collected for children regarding sexual violence in the previously cited report, however 3% of the youth (18-29 years) reported that they experienced sexual violence or sexual harassment when they were below the age of 18. People with Disabilities (PWD) experience additional threats that increase their vulnerability. There was a 52% increase of domestic violence against PWD in 2020 by family members and a lack of information about GBV prevention and response, including a 58% lack in how and where to access relevant assistance and services during emergency (Educaid- Rapid Assessment: Ensuring Disability Inclusion in COVID-19 Response-Palestine ). GBV within the general Palestinian context, suffers from a lack of regular, systematic and effective data collection and comprehensive management of services. When these services are available, occasionally from a range of different institutions, they are often fragmented, overlapping and/or contradicting. This is especially true in Gaza where access to health, especially for women, has witness almost a reversal over the last decade, as a result of ongoing blockade and a protracted humanitarian and economic crisis. Further, there is lack of standardized care within clinics, and non-universally accepted advice is given regarding contraception and their side effects. Women who lives or lived with GBV have less autonomy and control over their decisions and studies show that unwanted pregnancies are more common in households where violence is present. An external evaluation report written for MdM in 2014, highlighted that the most effective way to reduce the incidence of ‘less safe’ and ‘unsafe’ abortions, is to reduce the incidence of unintended pregnancies. One way to do this would be to include the provision of easy to access and free contraceptive services available to all women, including emergency contraceptives. Our current project with UNICEF, implementing training activities focused on capacity building and strengthening SRH services in Primary Health Care Centers in Gaza, has shown that this need is still very much existent. Qalqilya region is deprived of quality medical services and is considered one of the most vulnerable governorates with a population of 112,400, of which 47% are refugees. Qalqilya city has been completely enclosed since 2003 by the Israeli West Bank Barrier with a single entrance operated by an Israeli military camp which limits access to basic and emergency services including SRH and GBV. It is also a hotspot for clashes with 60% of male citizens being affected by ISF and settler violence.Gaza is also encircled by concrete and steel fences and a complete air and sea blockade, resulting in extremely limited admittance of aid and supplies. Gaza faces serious complications in an already vulnerable and affected system. Assessments by actors such as CARE during COVID 19 emergency, reports how women and men are limited in receiving health services within and outside their communities, women are doubly vulnerable by not being able to receive obstetric emergency services and adequate monitoring of pre- and post-natal complications.

2 - Activities

Taking into consideration the collateral impacts of Covid-19 on the already complex situation in the oPt, MdM is aiming at improving SRH for women living in oPt through a right-based and global approach aiming at the integration of services in SRH that addresses GBV, MHPSS and community awareness with mobile clinics and clinic centers within the WB and Gaza. All activities will be pre coordinated and agreed with the health cluster and MOH regarding geographical location for mobile clinics to ensure the highest geographical coverage and avoid duplication. In the WB, MdM will support HWCs in providing a Level-2 Mobile Clinic Program, as well as in providing various services: SRH, MHPSS, GBV, focusing on the Area C communities of Qalqilya governorate and referring patients that require specialized care to the Women’s Centre of HWCs in Qalqilya city. A mobile team includes 5 persons: 1 driver, 1 general physician, 1 specialized women’s health physician, 1 health educator and 1 social worker. This team will provide health services to women, men and children one day a week in the defined locations (10 locations including Arab Ar Ramadin, Far’ata, Wadi Ar Rash, Ras At Tira, Abu Fardeh, Al-Mudawar, Izbet Al-Tabib, Arab Ar Ramadin As Shamali, Falamya, Ezbet Ashqar and Isla). The HWCs Qalqilya center has been in place for more than 20 years, they are a relevant actors providing specialized care of Women’s health, including gynecology, ultrasound and emergency and is targeting the complete Qalqilya governorate within its SRH and GBV services, moreover this center will provide care to GBV survivors from Area A and B of Qalqilya governorate to address the high increase of GBV patients in need during this COVID 19 emergency. MdM’s intervention in Gaza will focus on strengthening the capacities and providing technical support to our partner CFTA at their women’s health center in Burije camp /Deir Balah governorate and their other centers in Khan Younis. This support aims at improving the quality of the care (SRH, GBV and MHPSS services) and education, with a focus on community awareness, outreach and advocacy towards changing attitudes among families and community members. Over the years, MdM has developed extensive experience in GBV and SRH issues integrating mental health as a transversal, crosscutting focus of all activities. MdM will follow the harmonize protocols proper for SGBV case management in Palestine. MdM and its partners will ensure close coordination and reporting with both health and protection cluster. The MC activities will be closed coordinated with the mobile clinic supported group pf the health cluster. Data collected within the project will feed the 4 Ws system both health and protection cluster. MdM and its partners will continue being and active member of the GBV supporting group and will reinforce the quality and access to a comprehensive GBV case management. Therefore, the project activities will be focused on: Health activities 1- Provision of Level 2 primary health care to vulnerable communities living in Area C of Qalqilya governorate through mobile clinic (close coordination with MC sub-working group and the MoH). 2- Provision of quality Sexual and Reproductive Health Consultations (FP, GBV, MHPSS) through mobile clinic and partners health centers. 3- To ensure the follow up of guidelines and developing materials regarding protocols for the SRHR services on SRH, FP (emergency contraception), MHPSS, and GBV, in coordination with relevant stakeholders and GBV AoR. 4- Provision of telemedicine services on specialized care (gynecologists, psychologist, Gp) during Covid-19 restrictions to ensure access to health. 5- To organize together with clinics, awareness and sensitization sessions on SRH, SRH rights, FP, MHPSS and GBV.6- Provision of community awareness campaigns on SRH, GBV, MHPSS, PSEA with CBOs and CSOs. 7- To lead an operational advocacy campaign on mainstreaming of SRH, gender and counter GBV, both at policy level and at local level addressing religious authorities and community leaders. 8- To train clinical staff about specific needs of PWDs. 9- To design and produce MEAL tools (special focus on SRH and GBV) and provide data reporting of GBV and SRH to health clusters, disaggregated to gender, age, PWDs and other relevant aspects. Protection Activities 1- To ensure the follow up of guidelines and protocols for the GBV and MHPSS services with a gender and rights approach. 2- To minimize multiple referral points and improve patient flow, gender-based violence survivors will be conducted directly to the specified room and triage will be avoided within the Women Health Center’s in Gaza and WB (Area C GBV survivors will be referred to HWCs Women’s Health Center in Qalqilya. 3- Provision of services reachable through phone for urgent psycho-social support, urgent social assistance (if necessary), and protection (if necessary) reachable 24/7 days. 4- To conduct effective prevention campaigns on GBV against women and young persons with disabilities, including gender inequality, discrimination based on both gender and disability. 5- To provide psycho-education to PWDs addressing healthy relationships in order to avoid, recognize, and report instances of exploitation, violence, and abuse. 6- To organize together with partners youth friendly space to talk about new masculinities and GBV. 7- To train clinic staff in charge of provision of services to GBV survivors upon quality medical care and MHPSS and protection 8- To design and produce MEAL tools (special focus on SRH and GBV) and provide data reporting of GBV and SRH protections clusters, disaggregated to gender, age, PWDs and other relevant aspects.9- To train local partners in PSEA policy and the proper complain mechanism for users.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place as complaint box located in the clinics or through phone call, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can follow-up and track the process. The complaint will be processed first by the MEAL officer who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular feedback / complaint and sensitive feedback. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to program design and implementation/service delivery, such as changes in location of provided service, working hours of related to MdM outreach program/service, waiting time for receiving the services, unavailability of certain service, inclusion of a certain service, etc. Sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse of authority by MdM staff, derogatory/immoral behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal abuse/physical assault, discrimination of beneficiaries based on race, gender, sexuality, creed or religion, harassment of beneficiary or any act that might harm MdM’s reputation, financial corruption, misuse/fraud of money or material, child abuse and neglect, counterfeiting, threats, blackmails. Adapting the response to the situation of COVID 19, patients will be informed that they can re-launch complaint mechanisms through social networks such as WhatsApp and Facebook. Based on that will take up the feedback to coordination level, if feedback is considered relevant according to MdM criteria, MdM will proceed with further actions, a review ethical committee will be formed (in which members of the community involved will take part) to analyze the case and make decisions. The user(s) who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be officially communicated through official mechanisms (letter). All complaints will be registered anonymously and treated following the internal MdM procedures.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management: Diagnosis, Implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation. Protection mainstreaming is a cross cutting theme for MdM programs and projects. MdM-F, HWCs and CFTA understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programs followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards regarding SRH, GBV and MHPSS. All organizations remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard, MdM-F, HWCs and CFTA are adopting several steps to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening their capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following UNICEF, MdM and Sphere guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO.All activities conducted by MdM F and its partners will ensure and monitor that health personnel involved in providing care follow risk reduction measures to prevent the spread of COVID 19 in the communities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MdM-F bases its intervention in ethical and humanitarian principles. This project aligns with the principality approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter. Ethical principles in health care include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneficence, the promotion of others’ wellbeing, commonly known as “do no harm,” and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles are a second source of values and norms that animate all MdM-F actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conflict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors
