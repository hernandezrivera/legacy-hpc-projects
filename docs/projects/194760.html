Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements gender-sensitive livelihoods early recovery initiatives targeting the most vulnerable households to enhance their resilience and self-sufficiency to the multi-faceted protracted crisis.
Action Against Hunger assessments confirm that the main livelihoods activities in rural areas are related to crop cultivation and livestock, in addition to employment as a daily wage earner in agricultural-related activities. However, the income generated by households is insufficient to cover the basic needs and households are implementing a number of food and livelihoods based coping strategies to survive. According to the MSNA 2022 conducted by the Early Recovery Sector, in Government of Syria (GoS) controlled areas, the inability to meet basic needs increased in 2022 compared to 2021: 77% of households reported that their income was insufficient to cover the cost of basic needs (up from 70% in 2021); female-headed households were double the male-headed households with regard to their inability to meet their basic needs; 92% of the surveyed households in 2022 have an income that is lower than or equal to the MEB; to cover costs of living, incomes would need to increase by 67% in GoS areas; households are adopting several coping mechanisms such as buying on credit (70%), borrowing (56%), and remittances (46%). Action Against Hunger will prioritize female-headed households who will receive vocational training on value-added processing of milk products in order to increase profits rather than relying on the sale of raw milk, in Aleppo and Hama. 100 women will be provided with vocational and skill building training (theoretical and practical) in food processing and preservation for the purpose of producing local and traditional market-based types of Syrian food. The training will include a session on the establishment and management of income-generating activities related to adding value to milk. A grant will be given to each woman in order to start her small business. The grant will be a conditional and restricted grant and will be split over two payments. The selected women will need to attend the vocational training in order to get the first disbursement. Once they require funds for the purchase of equipment, raw materials, or a source of energy, the second disbursement will be provided.

3 - Needs

According to HNO ERL Chapter Draft for 2022, livelihoods and electricity are consistently reported within the top three priority needs of households across the country. Households throughout Syria face a gap between incomes and expenditures on essential items, with 77% of households reporting that their income was insufficient to cover the cost of basic needs. In Government of Syria (GoS) controlled areas, 43% of households report a need for livelihoods support. The September 2022 WFP standard reference food basket increased by 40% compared to the price at the onset of the crisis in Ukraine, up 76% compared to a year earlier, reaching SYP 329,526 (USD 109 at the official exchange rate of SYP 3,015). Action Against Hunger assessments in 2022 confirm that a significant portion of households are not able to cover their basic needs, and face barriers to access to nutritious food groups such as meat, chicken and fruit. Households are applying many negative food- and livelihood-based coping strategies to cope with the lack of food availability and food access as a result of decreasing household purchasing power. These strategies include borrowing money or relying on help from friends and relatives, selling livestock or assets, consuming less expensive foods, removing boys from school in order to help with income generation activities for the household, and early marriage for girls, which increases GBV risks.

4 - Implementation of activities

Action Against Hunger will prioritize female-headed households in Hama and Aleppo who will receive vocational training on value-added processing of milk to produce products with a higher market value than simply selling raw milk. One hundred women will be provided with vocational and skill building training (theoretical and practical) in food processing and manufacturing for the purpose of producing local and traditional market-based types of Syrian food. A grant will be given to women in order to start their small businesses. The grant will be a conditional and restricted grant and will be split over two tranches, the women need to attend the vocational training in order to get the first tranche and later on they need to spend the money on purchasing the needed equipment, raw materials, and source of energy in order to receive the second tranche. The training will include a session on the establishment and management of potential income-generating activities related to production of products that provided an added value to raw milk.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Likelihood: medium; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: participatory approach in selecting the beneficiaries including women and marginalized community groups, accessible feedback and complaint mechanism in place and communities informed on it, AAH staff will address the complaint/feedback; information shared about the project and the selection criteria. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to SEA. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. AAH prohibits SEA by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; support awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy. All employees and representatives of AAH are trained on Safeguarding, Protection and PSEA and are signatories to AAH’s ethical policies and Code of Conduct, and are therefore obligated not to engage in sexually exploitative behaviour linking sexual favors with the receipt of aid, engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age or be involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH staff and communities AAH works with have access to safe and effective reporting systems. AAH will investigate allegations of SEA in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of substantive devaluation of the Syrian Pound; international bank transfers to Syria are not fluid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors with options defined. Risk of aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of suppliers, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk of data collection hampered. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: transparency, trust building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide compulsory training to staff members on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a Policy for PSEA with an implementation plan.
