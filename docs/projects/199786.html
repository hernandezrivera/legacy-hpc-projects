Project Summary

Since 2014, the crisis unfolding in Lac Province has been characterized by numerous challenging factors, such as insecurity, flooding, large-scale and continuous population movements (Internally Displaced Persons, returnees) as well as community clashes and socioeconomic-related tensions. Currently, there are more than 240,000 Internally Displaced Persons ( IDPs) within Lac Province (DTM Round 18), mainly due to the deterioration of security conditions: 99% of IDPs, 100% of returnees from abroad and 77% of former IDP retrunees indicated lack of security conditions as the reason for their displacement. These persons were forced to flee their homes suddenly, leaving behind assets, food and other belongings. In addition, as the majority of these displaced persons are mostly women and children, serious concerns have been raised about the risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV) and of violence directed specifically against children such as forced marriage, exploitation, and child labor in the crisis areas. Thus to reach these vulnerable groups IOM will: 1. Strengthen the availability of displacement data and trends in Lac province and 2. Improve living conditions of vulnerable displaced households in Lac Province through data-informed, Shelter and NFI activities.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

In the context of the Lac Province with continual displacement movements (over 80,000 displaced in 2022 alone according to DTM Emergency Tracking Tool), the availability of regular updates on displacement trends, numbers of displaced people, profiles, living conditions, needs and access to services is of utmost importance to keep informing efficient humanitarian responses based on reliable and updated information. In addition, rapid identification of beneficiaries and the monitoring of assistance delivery channels is still a challenge, as displaced populations regularly move between different locations for a multitude of reasons, including the search for humanitarian assistance. One of the consequences of these challenges is a high probability of duplicated assistance to same persons. Furthermore, emergency assistance has been delayed in some cases due to lack of adequate and timely beneficiary identification mechanisms. To tackle these challenges, in 2021, Chad’s Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) designed a biometric registration strategy to identify displaced persons via rapid biometric registration, which can be used in any mass displacement context. The strategy aims to quickly verify the status of displaced people (old or newly displaced), update the overall list of displaced people and ultimately facilitate emergency assistance. The implementation of this mechanism requires the possession of baseline information to regularly verify or update. Thus, WFP and IOM launched biometric registration in the sous-préfecture of Bol in November 2021 and were able to register 45,000 persons in 19 displacement sites. As of December 2022, in partnership with WFP, INSEED and CNARR has conducted the registration in 10 displacement sites in Baga Sola. Over the years, IOM has supported the Government of Chad in the provision of assistance to displaced populations but the worsening displacement situation in the country calls for renewed efforts to strengthen humanitarian assistance in the country, especially in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic – which has affected movement of goods and personnel – in addition to creating a public health emergency. In order to improve understanding of displacement dynamics, with the objective of providing lifesaving assistance that is tailored towards the specific needs of beneficiaries, IOM suggests the implementation of a two-fold response: 1) Strengthening of displacement data management to support decision-making, planification and direct assistance, through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM)’s Mobility tracking and continuation of the implementation of rapid biometric registration, and Emergency Tracking Tool and 2) Shelters and Non-Food Items (NFI) procurement and distribution for most vulnerable displaced people. IOM’s planned response is driven by comprehensive community-based assessments that engage community members and relevant local authorities at every stage of the planning process, to utilize existing resources available within the community.
