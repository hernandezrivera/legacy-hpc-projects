Project Summary

The project intends to provide integrated Protection, Child protection, FSL, Education, WASH, Environment & Energy, and NFI services to a total number of 234,806 South Sudanese, 5,000 Non-South Sudanese refugees and 24,771 host communities. The project will cover the needs of 87,775 refugees and 2,771 host community reside in Kostis ,Jebalain and ELsalam localities in White Nile; 41,618 refugees in Shiekan locality in North Kordofan; Abu Jubaiha and Leri localities in South Kordofan; and 5,000 Non- South Sudanese refugees and 2,000 host community in Wad Alhelew locality in Kassala state. Plan international seeks to provide timely emergency services to the mentioned refugees in the areas where it is already operational and providing refugee assistance. The project is intended to start on 01/01/2021 and end on 31/12/2021.

1 - Needs

The open-door policy adopted by the Government of Sudan has encouraged significant number of refugees to consider Sudan as a destination and transit point for refugees from several neighboring countries. The recent war in South Sudan compounded by food insecurity has forced at least 800,000 South Sudanese to flee to the neighboring Sudan, among them 260,000 reside in Kostis , Jebalain and ELsalam localities in White Nile state . In addition, a total number 81,929 refugees reside for years in camps in Wad Elhelew locality in Kassala state. While more than 41,000 refugees reside in South Kordofan and North Kordofan. The protection of refugee remains a serious issue as there is a significant number of new arrivals are not registered while children lack birth certificates. Plan international teams foresee the need for community-based protection interventions. The child protection concerns are alarming among the refugee’s community as there at least 7,500 UASC in White Nile alone. Child labor is quite common among refugees’ families as a mean for earning additional income. In North and South Kordofan where refugees are not residing in camps, children are more exposed to child labor particularly in farms, markets and domestic work. This further exacerbates children wellbeing and expose them to serious child protection issues. Moreover, there is an increased caseload 10,000 of children in need for psychosocial support, counselling, case management and referral to other services. GBV cases are estimated to be high particularly in Kasala in Eastern Sudan as human trafficking and smuggling is widely practiced while no official data is reported. Among South Sudanese refugees, girls are exposed to early marriage and pregnancy while social norms and stigma prevent case reporting. The Covid-19 pandemic, recent floods as well as the poor infrastructure has restricted refugees access to hygiene and sanitation services. A number of latrines were damaged by the floods in White Nile in refugees camps exposing them to disease outbreaks namely cholera and watery diarrhea. High latrine usage ratios remain a major challenge in White Nile State having the highest latrine usage ratio at 94 persons per latrine in Dabat Bosin compared to the desired number of 20 persons per latrine. Open defecation due to poor latrine coverage and poor hygiene practices remains an ongoing challenge in most targeted areas. Young women and girls access to MHM and other hygiene services is very limited as they can not afford the increasing costs. If not supported, their vulnerability will increase during the second wave of COVID-19. Access to quality education for refugee children has always been a concern in White Nile, Kordofan and Kassala. The school curriculum , language barrier and high education fees has largely impacted refugee children from formal education. The dropout rate among girls is higher due to protection issues and inaccessibility to sanitation facilities within schools. Early marriage among has been also reported in some case as a barrier for girls education specially among South Sudanese and Eritrean refugees. A total number of 21,381 children need to be supported to enroll or continue their primary and secondary education. In the mentioned areas , some of refugee schools or schools accommodate significant number of refugee children were partially or totally destroyed. In addition, the recent floods have largely impacted the refugee communities particularly in Kordofan states creating the needs for SNFI support to 6,000. The quality of refugee children education in refugee schools is poor and interrupted due to many factors including lack of school supplies, trained teachers and school meals.The planned response is in line with the Sector Response Strategy and Priorities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project realises and recognises the impact of refugee camp establishment and on the environment particularly on trees and other vegetation. The project will work the the local government line ministries and identify plus or minus 20 hectares of land on which there shall be planting of both indigenous and exotic trees as deemed fit by the community in consultations with the technocrats in the line ministries. Eforts will also be made to minimise some soil errosion effects that might be caused by the new large refugee settlement. There is a budget to provide the identified trees to be planted and a budget for labour to do the planting has also been put in place. The project will also work closesly with other projects working on climate change for a better informed approach.
