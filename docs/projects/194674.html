Project Summary

This intervention aims at contributing to the improved health and hygiene status of women, girls, men, boys, the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs) through timely and equitable access to safe, potable and adequate water in targeted communities in Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang counties.
The proposed WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with RI’s health , nutrition , protection and FSL programming. Women and girls are the primary users of WASH services, the intervention is designed to be gender-sensitive and prevent GBV. Activities will include provision of safe water supply; upgrading of existing boreholes to solar powered water supply system, pipe line extension from the existing solar powered water supply system and distribution of water purification chemical (PUR), (for those beneficiaries who are depending on river or surface water) , the rehabilitation of water points (boreholes) with secure location and access within the community and nutrition facilities with a focus on construction of flood resilient boreholes for which the apron and flood protective wall will be raised by at least 1m. The designs of the structures will be considered so that people with disabilities (PWDs-both male and female) as well as older people can easily access and collect water. Further, household latrine construction, WASH NFI distribution, and the training of Water Users Committees (WUCs) and pump mechanics will be carried out. RI will encourage selection and participation of female in the WUCs. Each of the WUCs will consist of at least 50% female membership. These WUCs will be responsible for ensuring that sustainable and viable operation and maintenance of these systems are in place at the community level. RI will ensure the quality of water through water quality testing which will be done before and after rehabilitation/construct on of water points.
For the sanitation component, the project plans the implementation of flood resilient communal and institutional ventilated improved pit latrines which will be equipped with hand washing facilities. The design of the structures will be done considering all the segments of the society especially PWDs and older people and ensure that they can access them easily without any barrier. The latrines will be gender inclusive and will have a lock on the doors for protection purposes. The location will also not be far from the beneficiaries’ home and will therefore be easily accessible by women and girls during day and evening time. The construction of latrines will be complemented by the delivery of hygiene promotion messaging on safe excreta disposal to improve the sanitation practices. RI hygiene promoters and trained home health promoters (HHPs) will conduct weekly monitoring visits to at community, nutrition and household level to assess the usage and cleanliness of the latrines and document any challenges or feedback from the household. The HHPs will disseminate information on the five essential hygiene and sanitation practices (fecal–oral route transmission or the ‘F-diagram’), Safe water handling, Safe excreta disposal, Handwashing and personal hygiene, Environmental hygiene and baby WASH messages. RI will continue using its internal hygiene promotion training module as well as the Care Group Manual to train HHPs for the Healthy Practices, Safe Communities Project.
To improve, the WASH condition of the caretakers of SAM/MAM which contributes reduction of malnutrition, WASH NFI kits will be distributed. The integrated approach will address the high levels of malnutrition by targeting the families of SAM and MAM children for WASH interventions and providing access to drinking water, thereby acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver of poor health and malnutrition. The WASH intervention in these three counties will be done carefully without creating any conflict among the beneficiaries.
Data will be disaggregated by sex and age wherever possible.

1 - Needs

An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 by lack of access to adequate safe water and sanitation. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states including Upper Nile. Despite replenishment of major waterways during the rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide. This dropped to 34% in rural areas. In Upper Nile, 33% of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water. Persons with disabilities (PWDs) are most vulnerable to this situation. Access to safe water as well as extremely low coverage of water infrastructure makes households rely on surface or unimproved water. In Upper Nile, 71% of households reported using rivers or puddles as their main water source (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022). The WASH needs in the targeted areas of Maiwut, Nassir and Ulang therefore remains high. According to the WASH cluster 2022 severity / Vulnerability mapping these three counties have been classified under WSC-4 with Maiwut county falling under priority one and Nasir and Ulang counties falling under priority two. The existing inter-communal conflict, regular flooding and mobility of the community have exacerbated the WASH needs of the communities. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2022, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera and AWD. According to the national WASH cluster strategy, the main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure. Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang counties share those underlying, common drivers. The low capacity of the community and poor management of the system contributes to the continuous damage of the WASH structures. The absence of government WASH department in these counties exacerbates the situation as this department is supposed to give technical support to the community WASH services. According to the Inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessments conducted in August, October and November 2021 in Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang counties, by agencies operating in these counties, the WASH needs are as follows: • Water points were flooded and contaminated; people are forced to drink and use contaminated river, swamp and stagnant water. • Limited water treatment practices including use of PUR. • The primary water collectors are women and girls have to walk long distance to fetch water and are exposed to protection risks (GBV, harassment). •Women and girls collecting water have to wait in long queues for long periods • Open defecation is prevalent where latrines are not functional, especially in rural areas, except in households where latrine construction was supported by INGOs. • Unsafe hygiene practices prevail as does lack of handwashing facilities in homes and institutions. Children are particularly prone to water borne diseases since they play in open areas and bathe in stagnant water. • sanitation facilities in health and schools are not functional as they lack regular maintenance, resulting in deteriorating sanitation and environmental health amongst patients, health workers and pupils. • Poor hygiene practices aggravate health issues and heighten the risk of disease outbreak (including cholera and typhoid). Frequent and recent flooding, displacement has resulted in lower food productivity affecting the nutritional status of communities, lack of access due to poor road conditions and conflict which prevents rehabilitation of existing water supply structures. • Lack of awareness, prevention and control measures for prevailing Covid-19.

2 - Project activities

RI has engaged the communities during needs assessment and will continue this engagement throughout the implementation to encourage community participation and sustainability of the program. RI will conduct assessments and baselines to assess current WASH needs and key trends in target communities and technical and sanitary surveys of targeted water points / sources for upgrading or rehabilitations. RI will also carry out distribution of famine prevention minimum package (WASH –NFIs kits) comprising buckets, PUR Sachets / Aqua tabs, soap and filter cloth to ensure safer water consumption and handling. Distribution of WASH NFI kits for caretakers of SAM/MAM children who are receiving treatment at the nutrition facilities. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated according to gender and age. Safety audits at distribution sites will be done in collaboration with the RI protection team.RI will upgrade existing boreholes to flood resilient solar powered water supply systems, pipe line extension and construction of water collection points with six faucets for the community and from the existing nearby solar powered water supply system to the nutrition facilities, identifying locations through consultation and risk analysis with women, girls, boys and men – 6 BHs, conduct safety audits and focus group discussions. Rehabilitation and repair of flood resilient boreholes at community and nutrition facility level. WUC and local pump mechanics training, regular monitoring and supervision. RI will encourage at least 50% female WUCs membership. These WUCs will be responsible for ensuring that sustainable and viable operation and maintenance of these systems are in place at the community level. Water quality testing will be done before and after rehabilitation of water points to ensure safe water provision, prevent WASH related diseases. RI will undertake distribution of water treatment chemical/PUR for SAM/MAM care takers.For the sanitation component, RI will construct flood resilient ventilated improved pit latrines at community nutrition and health facilities, each of 5 stances with a 3m depth pit lined with brick wall and super structure iron sheet equipped with permanent hand washing facilities and soap. The design of the structures will be done considering all the segments of the society especially PWDs and older people and ensure that they can access them easily without any barrier. RI will make special emphasis on WASH interventions for PWDs. At least 10% latrines constructed/ rehabilitated will ensure enough space for PWD to enter and use comfortably (providing enough space to enter in to the latrines with wheelchair). All elevated water and handwashing stations will have a ramp for easy access. RI will conduct house to house verification for HH latrine construction; PWD identified will be provided with seating arrangement or ramp arrangement for their latrines depend on the disabilities. The latrines will be gender inclusive and will have a lock on the doors for protection purposes. The location will also not be far from the beneficiaries’ home and will therefore be easily accessible by women and girls during day and evening time. Latrine construction will be complemented by the delivery of hygiene promotion messaging on safe excreta disposal to improve the sanitation practices. Trained HHPs will conduct weekly monitoring visits at community, nutrition and household level to assess the usage and cleanliness of the latrines and document any challenges or feedback from the household. The HHPs will disseminate information on the five essential hygiene and sanitation practices (fecal–oral route transmission or the ‘F-diagram’), Safe water handling, Safe excreta disposal, Handwashing and personal hygiene, Environmental hygiene and baby WASH messages. RI will continue using its internal hygiene promotion training module as well as the Care Group Manual to train HHPs for the Healthy Practices, Safe Communities Project.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality. They are encouraged to report to the nearby local authorities and staff. The project staff will organize FGD with those people with disabilities as well as those who are not literate to get their feedback. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. By using a variety of methods to receive feedback, RI will ensure that feedback from vulnerable and marginalized groups is captured. RI has standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites. IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the project leads through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI as a member of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) alliance is committed to the implementation of effective humanitarian response. Using an evidence based and participatory approach, RI will ensure that the response is appropriate and relevant. The project design is based on needs assessments and will ensure participation of communities. The program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation will therefore be informed by and be inclusive of those who they are designed for. This ensures that all voices within a community are heard. Specifically, RI will:1.Deliver an effective and timely response intervention. Internal kick-off meetings and community kick-off meetings will be held. Chiefs, project management committees, water user’s committees and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including water, sanitation and hygiene promotion activities and will be sensitized to the project activities including selection criteria, the targeted beneficiary rights and entitlements, expected outputs, beneficiary participation and key dates of the project interventions. During these sensitization sessions space will be given for feedback;2.Aim to strengthen the local capacities by leveraging on already existing community structures during implementation of the activities. RI will actively seek to engage all segments of the community including men, women, boys, girls and PWDs in the project planning, implementation and monitoring exercises. RI staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. All staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals.3.RI will implement a formal Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all interventions and have in place appropriate channels preferred by the community to encourage continuous information flow to and from stakeholders, including marginalized groups. 4.RI will coordinate with stakeholders to share relevant information on changing context or needs, avoid duplication of services and/or identify service delivery gaps and use of the existing referral pathways to maximize benefits for the affected persons. 5.RI will remain adaptable and factor in feedback into the implementation and will use lessons learned from RI’s previous and ongoing projects to continuously improve on service delivery. 6.RI will adhere to a “Do No Harm” (DNH) approach. The response will therefore continuously be monitored and RI will provide high standards of technical and management support in line with humanitarian response standards to ensure that all project activities are being implemented correctly thereby mitigating any negative impacts of our programming.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RI has a zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Staff have committed to uphold and adhere to RI’s policy on this. To ensure Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), RI staff will be advised to understand and demonstrate professionalism and impartiality. All staff will be required to understand and implement safeguarding and mandatory code of conducts. Additionally, RI will sensitize local leaders, community groups and targeted beneficiaries about safeguarding and the SEA reporting mechanism. RI's Safeguarding focal point in based in Juba.All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the targeted locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally, RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt the project design and implementation. RI will get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on the services they received through post distribution monitoring exercises, mid and end line evaluations. RI will then address feedback received and will adapt its programme to address any concerns or gaps highlighted. During monthly consultation meetings, RI will then provide feedback to the beneficiaries on any changes made to the project based on feedback received. Additionally, RI's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on RI's WASH project activities. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will implement WASH activities aimed at strengthening communities resilience to floods so as to reduce the vulnerability of communities to due to flood. The project plans the rehabilitation of flood resilient boreholes, construction of flood resilient latrines, dissemination of key hygiene promotion messages to create a strong and resilient community and advocates strengthening of early warning system during monthly WASH cluster monthly meeting to give clear information to the community before the flood comes and before flood affects the community.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

In collaboration with the existing protection team and partners, the project will strengthen safe and timely access to quality case management and psychosocial services as well as building the capacity of the protection team to provide these services.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver of poor health and malnutrition this intervention will through different durable and sustainable WASH activities address the needs of the targeted beneficiaries with the aim of strengthening their resilience to recurrent shocks such as floods. All targeted activities will be done in collaboration with other sectoral interventions including Food Security and Livelihoods for maximum synergy. RI in line with the Core Humanitarian Standards will work closely with communities leveraging on the local capacities while at the same time building further capacity to manage water infrastructures thereby enhancing community ownership in line with the sustainability agenda.
