Project Summary

This proposal is designed to complement Health Action Africas' (HAA) health projects with General Protection services in hard-to-reach areas facing severity IPC4&5. Assessments indicates, Canal Pigi County faced increased protection concerns exacerbated by multiple shocks; sub-national crime, floods, and restrictions on COVID 19.
The literature analysis of various assessment findings indicates that, there has been no humanitarian assistance for general protection services in Canal Pigi from 2020 to the present day, Feb 2021. This has increased the level of serious general protection service needs by all vulnerable groups under category 4 severity needs in canal Pigi.
Health Action Africa- HAA is a static health partner in Canal Pigi and thus designed this protection project to complement Health project to enrich programming to ensure the continuity of critical lifesaving programs, optimizing resource utilization, and increasing access to protection services. HAA will also broaden its General protection programs to the hard-to-reach areas in the Kapoeta North and Mundri East Counties.
Brief Context problem statement in the Counties
Since 2013, armed conflict, human rights violations, cattle raids, inter-communal violence, protection threats, security concerns, and natural disasters have continuously exacerbated patterns of severity and displacement of populations in Canal Pigi County. Over the years, people in this County have been persistently listed under IPC4&5. For instance, the Dec 2020 Integrated Food Security Phase classification placed Pigi Canal County under IPC4. The Jan 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)2 reported by OCHA similarly indicated that 103,82 people require humanitarian assistance in canal pigi
These dynamic shocks continued to drive general protection concerns among women, girls, men, and boys of all social classes. Women and girls are seriously affected and face numerous protection threats; Domestic violence, sexual exploitation outside the home due to their household role of finding alternative sources of livelihoods such as collecting wild foods outside settlements. Forced early marriages among young girls is high.
Vicious cycles of inter-communal violence caused mass victims, casualties to Men and Boys beside forced recruitment into armed groups, and retaliatory attacks
Market engagement assessments conducted in Greater Kapoeta North (Reach Feb 2020)2 indicates, increasing cases of gender-based violence and arranged forced marriages. Mundri East County experiences both armed conflict and fluvial floods since 2019. The Sept IRNA 2020 Mundri County3 indicates, that more than 12,450 households were displaced by floods. Perceptions of safety across the IDP settlements indicates high risks of women and girls to SGBV, increasing social distress, trauma as a result of loss of properties/capital assets, and lack of shelter.
Response Model
The project targets 100% overall general Protection (GP) populations and therefore intends to reached cumulative totals of 14034 beneficiaries aggregated as 3513 girls, 4039 boys, 3389 women, and 3094 men; equitably spread to Canal Pigi, Kapoeta North, and Mundri East Counties. A comprehensive county-level target is indicated in the log frame
HAA will dispense the services aligned to the broader general protection; cluster-specific objectives and associated activities. These programs will include; mobile and static GP assessments and monitoring of protection/safety; protection by presence and protective accompaniment; Comprehensive case management, psycho-social support (PSS), Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services, legal awareness, assistance, and counseling for persons of concern; Individual Protection Assistance, Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging; Community based protection preparedness and risk mitigation, Peace building and peaceful coexistence; CBI for durable solutions programming.

1 - Needs

The December 2020 Integrated Food Security Phase classification placed Pigi Canal County in Jonglei State under IPC4, and in Jan 2021, the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)2 reported by OCHA similarly indicated that 103,82 people are the populations in need of humanitarian assistance in this County. The HNO OCHA indicates, these Populations were severely affected by the sub national violence, floods and COVID 19 restrictions beside the economic down streaming. These complex shocks continued to drive general protection concerns across all the social groups’ women, girls, men and boys. The Literature review of IRNA Assessments, SMART surveys and HNO reports conducted in different intervals indicates that, Women and girls are highly affected and faces various protection threats; Domestic violence, forced teenage marriages and also risks of sexual violence outside the home due to their household role of finding alternative sources of livelihoods such as collecting wild foods outside of the settlements. With the increasing economic down streaming, forced teenage marriages have increased driven by the need to secure more resources such as cattle through bride price2. There are weak referrals /non -functional referrals protections systems for settlement of community social concerns including protection threats. In Canal Pigi, the Protection threats are characterized by rampant human rights violations, poor case management and absence of community based initiatives (CBI) for Peaceful coexistence3. The absence of functional CBIs has created case management vacuums as such Boys and men majorly faced effects of inter-communal violence with increase incidences of mass casualties, injuries, fierce revenge attacks from neighboring communities, adjacent clan subgroups. young Boys below 12-17years and men also face forceful recruitment into armed groups and or armed factions. Other cross cutting issues noted were increasing rape cases and Psycho -social distress across all social groups; Women Girls, Boys and Men. Cattle raiding continued to affect both men and young boys. These events are accompanied by Child abduction (IRNA Jan 4th 2021) Pigi Canal1. There is general lack of awareness/information dissemination in regard to protection service availability across the county. Communication network for safe referrals for protection concerns are not readily available. Market engagement assessments conducted in Greater Kapoeta North (Reach Feb 2020)2 indicates, increase cases of gender base violence, arranged forced marriages to mobilize resources for family upkeep. Human rights violations are thus rampant across the remote locations of Kapoeta North. Populations in Mundri East were faced with fluvial floods and as a result, more than 23450 HHs lost their shelter according to Sept IRNA 2020 Mundri East and Mundri West report. This triggered displacements across the floods affected Payams in the County. Perceptions of safety across the IDP settlements are low. Both IDPs and returnees Populations are grossly affected with psycho Social distress, trauma as a result of loss of properties/capital assets, lack of shelter and poor safe referrals of the protection concerns. In the remote locations, case management platforms are either weak or dysfunctional. HAA has designed this project to respond to these identified needs through Mobile and static protection assessments and monitoring, Protection by presence and protective accompaniment, Comprehensive case management, Psycho-social support (PSS); Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services, Legal awareness, assistance and counseling for persons of concern, Individual Protection Assistance , Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging, Community based protection preparedness and risk mitigation, Peace building and peaceful coexistence and CBI for durable solutions programming

2 - Project activities

The activities in this project will work under the General Protection sectoral cluster guidelines. HAA will therefore, use a mix of static outreach, and mobile modalities based on the context in each County and areas of interventions. The target beneficiaries of this project are 60% IDPs and 40% Host Communities because most of the protection threats are found in IDP settlementsThe key activities will be related to comprehensive case management and referrals, including CBI; mobile and static protection, monitoring and assessment in form of rapid assessments; psychosocial support services; legal awareness, assistance, and counseling; protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging; community based Protection(CBP) preparedness and protection risk mitigation; protection by presence; and peace-building and peaceful co-existence activities. The project team will embark on routine protection monitoring and assessment throughout the project period. They will actively trace and identify protection concerns at the community level. This process will be reinforced with the local social networks’ complementary to the standard practices to ensure that women and girls, men and boys with protection concerns are promptly reached, identified during the IPMT, and assisted including persons with disabilities or physically challenged persons There will be a variety of psycho-social support services tailored for these vulnerable groups, based on their contextual needs to ensure that women and men, girls and boys receive either individual, group, or specialized Psycho-social support (PSS) and other related services based on the context of the assessed need. HAA will incorporate recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills, and other activities that promote social harmony, to improve local skills and enhance local peace-building initiatives. HAA will deploy a range of techniques to create awareness on protection and prevention messaging. Social protection campaigns, broadcasting information on airwaves, community scorecard forums, and other essential platforms will facilitate prompt protection messaging to women and men, girls and boys promptly. HAA will also adopt the ‘’Boda Boda talk talk ‘’ model to create awareness and a local feedback mechanism adopted from the community engagement communication working group. HAA will support protection by presence activities and ensure women and men, girls and boys benefit from deterrent civilian patrols and protective presence in the counties' high-risk spots. HAA will adopt modules from the peace forums and conduct refresher training to local peace committees, pilot the model, and implement peace forums in the counties. Key modules for the refresher training will include conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peace-building. The peace committees will be tasked to conduct community-level awareness-creation activities across all the counties' social groups. This project is designed to contribute HAA health sector's outcomes by ensuring inclusive access to critical cross-sectoral services including, SGBV, Psycho-social support services (PSS), Mental Health, Clinical management of rape (CMR), referrals among other services that may arise based on needs. There will be regular protection monitoring and protection assessment at the community level to allow critical analysis to inform the do no harm and conflict-sensitive approaches to other sectoral programming responses. HAA will encourage community-based interventions to significantly increase security stability, including the view towards the recovery phase. The project will ensure protection mainstreaming and the centrality of protection during design, implementation monitoring, and reporting. HAA will also integrate legal assistance, and cash-based interventions as part of comprehensive case management incorporating multi-sectoral support

3 - Project indicators

Health Action Africa(HAA) will adopt the regulator protection cluster standard monitoring tools to ensure data collection and analysis are captured using harmonized tools for ease of interpretation, relevant stakeholders understanding and sharing and information dissemination. In the field levels, there will be routine protection monitoring and assessment throughout the project period. The project team will actively trace and identify protection concerns at the community level, document information gathered and submit the reports. Routine reports will be shared through various avenues, case specific information sharing, weekly or biweekly Protection cluster updates Monthly reports as well as the protection cluster 5Ws. There will be regular quarterly field visits by senior protection cadres from Juba office to give supportive supervision for protection activities, and on job mentorship. Every field visits shall be guided by the use of activity monitoring tools adopted from the protection cluster.The field monitoring team will generate reports and share with the stakeholders. HAA will also document the relevant reports, share the lessons learnt, challenges and proposed mitigation measures in consultation with the protection cluster and field level stakeholders and beneficiaries/communities. HAA will also participate in all planned assessments in the field locations document findings and share to the protection cluster. The project protection information management team will be actively engaged in the design and development of monitoring and evaluation tools alongside the protection cluster. We will adhere to the use the protection cluster common performance indicator collating commonly used data collection tools and making them available to all the stakeholders. Within the communities, the use of community score cards will be utilize to ensure communities actively participate in project designs , monitor progress and feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Health Action Africa-HAA have PSEA policies and code of Conduct. All staff are trained and practice this code of conduct and policies. We have also adopted the core humanitarian standards to government projects, staff and the beneficiaries. Using local forums, information campaigns are conducted to disseminate PSEA policies and messages to the Populations. Local channels including airwaves, community level mobile announcements, radio talk shows and other avenues are used to orient, disseminate PSEA information to the project implementation team and the beneficiaries. A free toll phone line, suggestion boxes, community score cards are the community tools used for collecting feedback from the communities. Base on context cases, information feedback is handled in manner in line with the do no harm humanitarian core standard principle.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Local tools are used for communicating feedback to the beneficiaries. These include visual aids, posters, and banners, feedback boxes, community announcements in the villages, social gathers among others. These tools are spread in community points accessible by all social groups including persons with disabilities. Local persons are trained to be used as resource persons to assist in giving interpretations to the communities. HAA has always translated messages into the local languages to ensure the right information is communicated and understood by the beneficiaries. In all the project areas, feedback is conducted at both community and stakeholders’ level to ensure correct information is disseminated and understood. HAA use the local resource persons to pass information to all social groups in the communities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There will be community level consultation through the local structures and authorities in the project areas during project designs, baseline assessments, implementation, monitoring and reporting using local mechanisms. At community level, the community score cards will be used to able the populations actively participate in discussion forums; FGDs, KIIs and interactive inquiries learning and documentation. There will be participatory action planning for protection activities, monitoring and reporting. A local complain redress and feedback mechanism will be adopted based on context. The community and the leaders will be involved in voluntary monitoring of the project through community surveillance activities, which align with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Policy on Protection in Humanitarian Action (2016). This involvement emphasizes the IASC's commitment to prioritize protection and contribute to protection outcomes in humanitarian action for safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, participation, and empowerment.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During project implementation, HAA always establish feedback desk, install toll free lines, select local committees for receiving complaints and also use local mechanisms for giving feedback to the communities. Based on context, HAA normally conduct baseline assessment, project launch, real time project reviews, quarterly reviews, post distribution monitoring, progress monitoring, end line and assessments as well as project evaluations. All these are collectively conducted in a participatory manner with the local Populations donors and HAA project staff. The reports of each activity mention above are published, disseminated and a follow up is made

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Various literatures of assessment reports conducted by different partners over the year’s sums that, the project areas have previous and current profile of violent armed conflict, sub national /inter-communal conflicts and revenge attacks, limited sub optimal social services, access constraints and protection threats since 2013. The recent reports with these data are; South Sudan HNO _OCHA Jan 2021; IRNA _Canal Pigi Jan 2021; Mundri _County-IRNA_ Sept 2020 and the Dec IPC _report -2020. Having these external uncertainties, HAA will use mobile and static services to reach the beneficiaries. The Populations in the hard to reach villages constraint by these shocks will be accessed by mobile services to ensure; access and utilization of the protection services while not exposing them to harm or surrounding threats. There will be active participation and engagement comprising; Community leaders, Communities themselves, persons with disabilities (PWD) and the stakeholders (NGOS, CSOs) operating in the specific identified Populations in the counties to involved in the in the identification and selection of Mobile protection service Points within the Communities. A criteria for identification of the service points will be jointly used with core concentration on safety, access, distant, acceptability, voluntary space, among others to ensure do no harm principles and rights based approached is adhered to. HAA will also conduct consultation with communities including PWDs to assessed their needs and agree on modality to respond to the various needs of all the social groups. HAA will also guide all these groups to develop a local community feedback mechanism using the community score cards and the Boda Boda Talk Talk Mode adopted from the Community Communication engagement (CCE) working group led by Internews to ensure local mechanisms for reporting and feedback are functional and effective. There will be active reporting of suspected ordinance and explosives to the stakeholders for effective management. A summary of Service package in the Protection mobile points will include; protection monitoring and assessment, including rapid assessments; psychosocial support services; legal awareness, assistance and counseling; protection awareness raising and prevention messaging; CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation; protection by presence; and peace building and peaceful co-existence activities. They will be active tracing and identification of protection concerns at community level. This process will be reinforced with the local social networks complimentary to the standard practices to ensure Women and girls, Men and Boys with protection concerns are timely identified during the IPMT There will be refresher trainings to the protection staff centered on the PSEA, Code of conduct and the core humanitarian standard to enhance knowledge and dispense awareness messaging on PSEA within the project environment and communities. There will also be routine awareness of the beneficiaries about PSEA to mitigate the Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. The HAA code of conduct , child protection policies and the PSEA policies will be used alongside standard tools from the protection cluster to mitigate the Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA.There will be coordinated referrals of beneficiaries to specialist service centers and engagement of the local authorities to ensure safety of beneficiaries while accessing services.
