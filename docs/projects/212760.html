Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. (HNO, Summary, 2024). Within the education sector, numbers indicate an increase in the number of PiN in 2024 to 917,778 in Idleb, and 1,312,862 in Aleppo who are in dire need for education services.
In alignment with the education cluster’s strategic objectives for 2024-5, Shafak aims to expand access to high-quality formal and non-formal education for a total of 115,594 individuals, comprising 113,823 (3-17 yrs. old) children (52,997boys; 60,826 girls, including 8,091 children with disabilities) and 1,771 education personnel (909 males, 862 females).
a) Formal education initiatives in 32 schools will target approximately 23,040 students (b/g and age disaggregated), basic education and high schooling, with monthly incentives provided to teachers and education personnel, alongside educational materials for students and teachers, and operational costs coverage such, as water trucking printing costs…etc. Renovation of damaged schools and creating safe temporary learning spaces, ensuring gender and disability sensitivity, will be prioritized. Transportation services will be provided for children with disabilities through cash modality, while professional development sessions on child-centered pedagogy and classroom management will be conducted monthly, addressing overcrowding and inclusivity. ECCE (Early Childhood Care and Education) intervention includes provision of ECD kits, daily school feeding programs, play activities, and psychosocial support for children aged 3-6.
b) Non-formal education efforts, facilitated through BTL campaigns, will reintegrate children aged 8-12 into inclusive education. Support will be extended to 48 Non-Formal Education centers, benefiting approximately 34,560 children (b/g and age disaggregated), offering modalities aligned with the NFE matrix and supplemented with psychosocial support sessions, to support transition to formal education.
c) Winterization support by supplying heating fuel to 208 schools for 113,823 students (b/g and age disaggregated) in severely affected sub-districts, alongside heaters, fuel tanks, and winter clothing for students. The project design will incorporate stakeholder input, including children, adolescents, older individuals, and persons with disabilities, ensuring tailored access to humanitarian services and information.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

To prevent child safeguarding incidents, training is provided on child safety, with policies developed and complaint mechanisms established. As for the risk of having some teachers receiving salaries from a third party or are forced to get part of their incentives deducted, confirmations are obtained from all education directorates and education actors that no teacher is permitted to get paid again from any other party.To reduce any possible risks of GBV if Wash facilities are not separated or there are no locks on the doors, female team staff will consult with women and girls on location of these latrines and ensure locks are on the door, with post-monitoring feedback forms and regular repairs of these facilities (if locks are broken). For the risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls, Shafak ensures a confidential complaint mechanism is in place, with staff signing on code of conduct, with proper training provided to schoolteachers and education personnel on PSEA to know how to report misconduct.
