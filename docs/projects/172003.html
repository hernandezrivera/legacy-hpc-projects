Project Summary

This project aims at addressing WASH-related needs of the conflict-affected population in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts in eastern Ukraine. Within the framework of the project NW will provide multipurpose assistance at different levels, including community, institutional as well as household level. The intervention will not only significantly contribute to the safe and qualitative service delivery to the conflict-affected communities, but will enhance their resilience in light of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic.
The project activities will contribute to the WASH Objectives set for 2021:
- Ensure immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision for conflict- and COVID-19-affected people;
- Provision of critical WASH-related supplies and information for the prevention of infectious diseases;
- Promote resilient, recovery and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, minimal levels of sanitation provision, and minimum hygiene standards in conflict- affected oblasts.
The project activities are designed to cover needs of all vulnerable categories of the affected population considering gender and age aspects. In particular, it refers to construction works in public institutions as well as distribution of hygiene kits to HHs.
The project intervention will contribute to address WASH needs of around 128,000 people of communities in Luhanska and Donetska oblasts on both sides of the LoC. For that purpose NW is going to attract 2.2 M USD in 2021 within the humanitarian response to the crisis in eastern Ukraine.

1 - Needs

Access to adequate water, an internationally recognized human right, is a precondition for the adequate standard of living and for the right to the highest attainable standard of health. WASH-related problems often create a risk of spreading water and sanitation-related diseases. Despite that, in the sixth year of the ongoing conflict, access to safe water remains a challenge, both in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts of Ukraine. According to the WASH Cluster, approximately 1.7 million still need help with access to water. This can be put down to numerous factors:- the ageing of water infrastructure which requires repairs; - access issues, either due to the presence of armed groups, landmines or unexploded ordnances coupled with the poor condition of the roads; - frequent water cuts which are contributing to the worsening of the situation;- inefficient water supply management. Similarly, to the situation related to access to water, the sanitary situation in the region demands proper assistance. Sewage networks and wastewater treatment facilities (often 70 years old) are breaking down, which creates a risk of water contamination. Lack of safe access to adequate water and poor sanitary conditions can be also observed on the institutional level. Schools and medical institutions (ambulatories and hospitals) located close to the LoC are of particular concern. According to the Rapid Assessment of 473 Health Facilities in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (GCA), conducted by REACH in April 2020, 29% experienced problems with water and 47% of primary health facilities relied on trucked water or water from wells.Furthermore, the WASH Cluster Needs Assessment Study 2019 has revealed that:51% of HHs in GCA needs support in WASH, similar to NGCA - 47%;31% had problems with water, 26% sanitation and 14% with hygiene issues;14% of HHs using centralized potable water as the main source, received water only by schedule (28% near the LoC); 54% of HH have a plot for agriculture of which 30% use piped water (potable or technical) and 30% of those HHs experienced shortages of water for watering. While eastern Ukraine remains the epicentre of critical humanitarian needs due to the armed conflict, 2020 has brought yet another threat to the region. The rapid spread of COVID-19 around the country has reached two oblasts in eastern Ukraine, and the number of cases is climbing daily. By estimates of the Ukraine COVID-19 Humanitarian Response Plan, some 3.4 million people in eastern Ukraine will need humanitarian assistance during 2021.
