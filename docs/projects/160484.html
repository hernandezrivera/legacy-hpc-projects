Project Summary

Faced by an estimated 7.5 million persons in dire need of humanitarian assistance across South Sudan, IOM plans to reach up to 116,489 most vulnerable individuals with shelter and NFI assistance (Shelter 10,799 NFIs 78,111 settlement project 6,000 and protracted 21,579). IOM will target the affected population who lack the capacity to anticipate and cope with conflict stresses. The project will provide support to targeted affected communities which includes newly displaced persons, IDPs in protracted displacement in Protection of Civilians (PoCs) and collective centers (CCs), IDPs living with host communities, returnees, and affected non-displaced host communities. The proposed activities align with Shelter Cluster Strategic Objective 1, 2 and 3 of 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP).
Since January 2019, IOM as the S-NFI cluster leading agency, was able to provide S-NFI assistance to 190,884 individuals across the country. In 2020, IOM will respond to 11 percent of the Cluster target caseload and provide shelter, NFI and settlement support to a total of 116,489 individuals across South Sudan through its mobile as well as static teams. IOM will implement inclusive community-based approaches to ensure that the needs of people with disabilities, men, women, boys and girls are considered throughout the project cycle. IOM will further improve its program quality by designing evidence-based intervention through improved monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning including rapid monitoring, PDMs, end-line evaluation and success stories; and implemented as appropriate respecting market capacity, financial infrastructure, beneficiary preferences, as well as principles of do no harm. Also, IOM plans to strengthen the coordination with cash partners as its capacity of Cash Working Group (CWG Co-lead).
IOM will expand its provision of Housing, Land and Property (HLP) services to vulnerable individuals who lack the capacity to secure land, settle ownership disputes, lack awareness and/ or are attempting to return or resettle after protracted displacement. Technical support to Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management (MHADM) will be provided in developing national and sub-national disaster management plans.
In line with the Cluster objective for durable solutions and rebuild lives through community-based shelter and NFI solutions, IOM aims to enhance resilience of the target population in conjunction with addressing their S-NFI needs. The project intends to replicate the settlement project model to other areas, including Rabkuna, Raja, Yambio and Renk with the plan to reach a total of 6,000 people (62% of the Cluster’s total settlement target). IOM will continue to expand its cash-based interventions (CBI) approach and plans to reach 48,245 (41%) individuals through CBI. IOM works in close coordination with solutions working group encourages area-based coordination for integrated multi-sectoral projects with all other sectors or partners on ground including CCCM and Protection. IOM will ensure service delivery improvement with all sectors by leveraging the contribution of additional partners in settlement and integration including coordination with CCCM. Through advocacy component and area based approach of the project IOM aims to ensure access of the affected communities to more sustainable livelihoods, health, education, nutrition, water supply and sanitation facilities, and protection against physical and violent threats.
IOM as Cluster lead agency will further enhance national and sub-national level coordination between government and partners, as well as continue to advocate for adequate funding to meet the S-NFI needs of affected people. The S-NFI Cluster will continue to invest and explore effective avenues to strengthen the capacity of both established and new cluster partners through technical support, training, joint responses and standardized data collection tools.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the country’s protracted crisis has left 7.5 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance. An estimated 4 million people remain displaced, by the humanitarian crisis: 1.8 million internally and approximately more than 2 million fled to neighboring countries. Cumulatively, more than 900,000 internally displaced people (IDPs) and refugees were reported to have spontaneously returned between 2017 and August 2019.In 2020, the S-NFI cluster estimates that 2.3 million of people will have limited or no access to adequate shelter and non-food items. The increase in the estimated number is indication of the deteriorating socio-economic condition of the country. Lack of shelter and non-food items continues to expose affected communities to health and protection risks, particularly women, children and persons with disabilities. Although the peace agreement signed in Sep 2018 improved access of the IDPs considerably, S-NFI supplies remain inaccessible in some of the areas due to low purchasing power, non-functional markets and insecurity. These are some the contributing factors which increases the vulnerabilities of IDPs and expose women and children to GBV risks, especially when they are required to travel long distances to meet their household S-NFI needs. The overall progress on the implementation of peace agreement has been modest in 2019. The government and opposition are expected to form a coalition government in November 2019 and begin the long recovery from the five-year civil war toward elections in 2022. The formation of coalition government may begin to increase level of confidence for durable solutions, including returns or local integration. S-NFI Cluster anticipates increase in the number of returns in 2020 which may lead to escalation in HLP issues. This will pose challenges of security of land tenure and may increase demand for legal aid services for arbitration of claims. Considering the anticipated increased return or local integration there is need for more multi-sectoral and area-based integrated programming. IOM’s S-NFI team focus on the below areas, the needs are the following: Western Bahr el Ghazal: In Jur River county, there are increased risks of inter-communal clashes between local farmers and cattle radars which may lead to new displacements. IDPs displaced in May 2019 are returning to their area of origin in Jur river and requires support in ensuring safe access to shelter and non-food items. The situation might worsen in 2020 due to floods and heavy rains as experienced in 2019 and associated food insecurity. Access to southern Wau is improving. However it is still not certain, with clashes resuming around contested areas of greater Baggari provoking displacement of people in bush areas. IOM anticipates an increase in the number of returnees in Wau and Raja areas due to local level peace talks and freedom of movement. Jonglei: Remains an area in need of humanitarian assistance, as periodic clashes continue. Also the environment is not conducive to large scale cultivation and livelihood activities. Recent floods have adversely affected many counties in the stats Upper Nile: Areas of tension between SPLA and IO remain in Pagak and Nasir. The same areas were badly hit by the recent floods in 2019 and has worsened the vulnerabilities of the affected population and their access to S-NFIs. Access to Tonga is opened by the river from Malakal, but the situation remains volatile. Unity: Central and Southern Unity counties are still contested areas between SPLA and SPLA-iO (Leer, Rubkuay, Thaker, Mayendit). Koch and Leer Counties continue to receive IDPs/Returnees, mostly from Rubkona, Khartoum and Guit. Most of the are sharing the same rakuba/tukul, and IDPs lack non-food items. Upon return, they are often not able to immediately find resources and assets to rebuild shelter as well as access livelihood activities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In collaboration with the local authorities, the project will target the local affected communities, internally displaced population and IDPs returnees. IOM also plans to use biometric registration to ensure that the beneficiaries are properly registered and recognized. The project will provide individuals, greater awareness and utilization of their rights, as guaranteed by the Constitution and by forthcoming national land legislation. Rights to land, housing and property are governed by both statutory and customary legal systems, and the project serves to educate community members about their rights and entitlements within this complex and overlapping system.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

IOM is committed to ensuring the incorporation of community perspective and indigenous knowledge in program design through community participation. All activities are based on a consultative need assessment/analysis process, where local communities share and prioritize their needs and way-forward. IOM’s intervention is underpinned with AAP and have expertise to ensure AAP is incorporated in overall intervention process. IOM has established complaint feedback mechanism and communities are encouraged to share their feedback on different component of the project. IOM incorporates the specific needs and views expressed by women and vulnerable parties (as identified in nationwide FGDs held with diverse populations living inside and outside of PoC sites), were fundamental to the determination of appropriate project activities and objectives. Each village committee has representation from different groups including, men, women, boys, girls and people with special needs and people from different ethnic groups. The committee members are elected by the local communities and community have direct access to them to share and participate project interventions

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

IOM understands that; for the longer-term impact and sustainability of the projects it is essential to work in collaboration and that the interventions are aligned with policies of government institutions. IOM’s SNFI team encourages community/people centered approaches and design the intervention in way to ensure that community participation is safeguarded in all phases of the projects. Under the settlement projects IOM forms village committees considering the existing community systems/structure and service development committees from local government. The village committees work alongside with the communities and with local available leadership structure. IOM works in collaboration with the local chief’s (local government representatives), RRC, municipality department, ministry of physical infrastructure and Parliamentarian committee on land and physical infrastructure

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

IOM is committed to saving lives and promoting community trust and resilience to strengthen their capacity to cope with challenges. IOMs interventions are fully aligned with the national action plan/framework for return, reintegration and relocation of displaced persons. IOMs interventions are aligning with the South Sudan’s national framework principles and contributing to assist the government in establishing conducive environment for safe and dignified volunteer return of the IDPs. IOM’s HLP interventions are aligned with national Systems of land allocation, community land management, dispute resolution (both customary and statutory) and records management/ issuance, both formal and customary.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

IOM works in close coordination with solutions working group at State level and encourages area-based coordination for integrated multi-sectoral projects. IOM successfully implemented the pilot settlement project in Wau, aiming to improve service delivery complementarity across all other sectors and ensure access to more sustainable livelihoods, health, education, nutrition, water supply and sanitation facilities, and protection against physical and violent threats. IOM promotes strong inter-cluster coordination and collaboration to bring together the Protection, GBV, CCCM, WASH, Food Security and Livelihood and Solutions Working Group. The Community Led Settlement projects are planned in phases to remain flexible, sustainable and relevant to the needs prioritized by targeted communities as they move towards recovery. The project is underpinned with different modalities, People Centered Risk Informed Approach for Resilience (PCRIAR) including in-kind support of materials, community participation and market approaches to keep up with changes on the ground. Conflict sensitivity, protection and prevention and mitigation of gender-based violence are mainstreamed throughout the intervention. The project further aims to contribute to foster dialogue and peaceful coexistence among different communities.IOM intends to expand this approach to areas capable of sustaining the stability required to conduct consultative, community-led needs prioritization. IOM conducts regular safety audits of program area and in collaboration with Protection Cluster. IOM also conducts risk assessment to ensure that the environment is sufficiently safe for the beneficiaries, staff and no harm is caused.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project serves to establish trust and reliance on people centered approaches, local governmental management systems, which will in turn be supported to serve claimants without outside assistance and build the confidence of the communities. Additionally, the establishment and empowerment of village committees fosters community inter-dependence and reliance on internal decision-making, developing sense of ownership and community’s capacity building, thereby reducing dependence or expectation on humanitarian assistance. The village committees will work independently and will develop linkages with government and other development partners. These village committees will continue to work beyond project period for the betterment of their communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project team ensures that interventions design is based on the proper consultation with the vulnerable groups of the community and meaningful access is safeguarded for the vulnerable individuals. The project establishes the complaint feedback mechanism considering cultural appropriateness of the Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM) and easy access. IOM will continue to use existing feedback mechanisms to ensure the vulnerable communities voices are heard and incorporated in the design of intervention. Prior to interventions, IOM teams ensure proper communication with communities (CWC) to confirm they are aware of their entitlements. IOM’s M&E team conducts rapid monitoring during all interventions. Apart from the direct observations and resources put in place during the interventions for the beneficiaries, Rapid monitoring also includes exit interview of beneficiaries, particularly identifying vulnerable individuals including women, elderly people and people with special needs. The M&E team seeks their feedback on overall process of the intervention and incorporate the findings and suggestions from the participants to improve quality of intervention. Post-distribution monitoring (PDMs): IOM M&E team also evaluate the targeting and beneficiaries’ selection process, including vulnerability criteria, needs analysis and prioritization of vulnerable individuals during the distribution process. The survey sample includes a certain percent of vulnerable individuals or households (5%) to collect their feedback on overall process. PDM monitors knowledge about CFM, if a complaint was lodged, progress on complaint and overall satisfaction with the CFM mechanism. The PDM data is further analyzed to assess the effectiveness of the overall process
