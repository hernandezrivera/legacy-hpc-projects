Project Summary

This project directly responds to ERL sector objectives 1, 2 and 3. Save the Children and partners will use a cross-sectorial area-based programming approach to support inclusive community recovery. Community selection will be based on a conflict-sensitivity analysis and evidence-based vulnerability criteria, with focus on communities with limited available services and high stressors such as communities of potential return or local integration for displaced persons, and will be coordinated with other actors though existing and new area-based coordination groups. The 2-year planning cycle enables a focus on community engagement, capacity strengthening and meaningful sustainability planning.
Based on a participatory process with women, men, boys and girls of different ages, abilities and backgrounds, the project will work with communities to prioritize their recovery needs and develop community action plans. The plans will be implemented based on a sequenced approach over two years, starting with most urgent priority services and needs. Activities with communities may include rehabilitation of community infrastructure such as WASH or illumination, improvement of solid waste management or recreational spaces. The projects will be implemented in collaboration with the communities using, whenever feasible, a Cash for Work modality. To support households with specific vulnerabilities and unable to benefit from CfW, CVA will be used. Moreover, vulnerable displaced and host community households, will be supported through a range of CVA, including MPCA, cash for food, cash for winterization, top-ups for PLWs, fresh food vouchers for PLWs, and other cash plus related interventions. On the medium-term, the project will also create sustainable livelihoods opportunities in targeted communities through two pathways: 1) supporting new and existing businesses through micro-grants, coaching and mentoring; and 2) providing vocational training that corresponds to labor market needs in order to support productive sectors and provide employable skills.
The project will also aim to enhance social cohesion through dedicated community activities that will build positive relations across dividing lines, as well though supporting communities in sustainable management of their resources and assets, and provide technical capacity strengthening to key local stakeholders. The project will have a strong emphasis on accountability to affected populations, and will aim to foster positive dialogue between the communities and local authorities.

3 - Needs

The impacts of economic downturn in Syria and the region and COVID-19 have further eroded the coping capacity of the Syrian households already stretched by a decade of conflict and displacement. End of 2019, 86% of Syrians lived below the poverty line, further increasing to around 90% in mid-2020 (HNO 2021). Access to livelihoods alone does not guarantee subsistence: decimated public services and infrastructure have made basic services inaccessible to many, and Syria’s ‘working poor’ struggle to meet daily needs with dignity. The conflict, massive-scale displacement and prolonged strain on resources within and between communities have largely destroyed the social fabric. Millions live in protracted displacement in Syria with little progress towards durable solutions. Inter- and intra-community conflicts are exasperated by IDPs returning or seeking to integrate in areas with limited services and resources. In late 2021, operational partners in NES reported an increase in failed returns and people re-entering camps in search for access to most basic services. Grievances are often played out in the perceived or real equity gaps in the allocation of services and access to resources, including employment opportunities (SC, 2020, Return to Al-Raqqa). The daily struggle to meet basic needs has led to an increased risk of harmful coping mechanisms across all population groups, putting those with existing intersectional protection risks in a cycle of deepening vulnerability (ibid).

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will include a combination of the following activities in targeted communities: - Gender and conflict sensitivity analysis; community consultations with specific focus on ensuring meaningful participation of all community members (including women, youth, PLWDs and IDPs/returnees, and use of specific age-appropriate approaches to ensure child participation) and development of sequenced community actions plans; - Rehabilitation of key community infrastructure and services (e.g. WASH, illumination, parks, street lighting, improvement of solid waste management, etc.) primarily through CfW and working with local stakeholders to implement sustainability plans; - Targeted CVA for vulnerable HHs; This will be coordinated on area level through the NES Durable Solutions TF, in addition to CWGs in NES and NWS.- Community-based protection activities and community-led activities to enhance social cohesion and positive community relations; - Establishing effective and accessible-to-all mechanisms to ensure accountability; and fostering positive dialogue between the communities and local authorities; - Support to creating sustainable livelihoods opportunities through support to new and existing businesses through micro-grants, coaching and mentoring; vocational training aligned labor market needs to support productive sectors and provide employability skills.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Exacerbating real or perceived inequalities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigations: Area selection based on evidence-based criteria and conflict sensitivity analysis; establishing a clear communication-with-communities strategy and a feedback mechanism; coordination on area-based response to ensure fair distribution of efforts. Risk 2: Excluding vulnerable communities from access to ER assistance. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigations: Community-level engagement informed by a gender and power analysis and analysis of local conflict dynamics; community-led design through an equitable process that supports participation of marginalised groups (e.g. women, youth, PLWDs, IDPs etc.) through dedicated consultations, affirmative action and gender transformative approach. Risk 3: Exacerbating local tensions. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigations: Dedicated accountability mechanisms; clear communication on the policies guiding SC and partners’ engagement with authorities; clear communication about the benefits of multi-actor engagement for sustainability. Risk 4: Exacerbating local power dynamics. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigations: Awareness raising to emphasize the temporary nature of CfW activities to avoid creating pull factors; daily rates aligned with sector guidelines and practice in the area of implementation; service rehabilitation based on community prioritisation and plans communicated to local technical authorities for eventual


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for SC to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: SC has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to SC and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk to SC or partner staff safety and security. Mitigations: continuous monitoring of conflict and context developments, including safety and security risks; ensuring that all activities are culturally appropriate and abiding by humanitarian principles. Risk of COVID-19 delaying or hampering project delivery. Mitigations: COVID-19 adaptations to all feasible programme modalities; use of PPE by all SC and partner staff; incorporating health safety messaging in all community activities. Risk of losing access to targeted communities due to change in political/conflict context. Mitigations: continuous monitoring of the conflict and context developments, including relative stability in criteria for area-selection. Risk of diversion of cash by the Money Transfer Agency (MTA). Mitigation: the Framework Agreement with the MTA, is vetted by an ATC check in UK and US platforms. Programme and MEAL staff are present during distributions to oversee that this is done in accordance with established procedures and also collect feedback from beneficiaries immediately after the distribution (exit surveys). The MTA presents consolidated distribution records using a standard SCI template after each distribution.
