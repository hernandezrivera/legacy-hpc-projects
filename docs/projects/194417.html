Project Summary

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview indicated at least 4.6 million children (0-17 years) (girls and boys), are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection. Protracted conflict compounded by prevalent cyclical waves of intercommunal violence, revenge killings, children and women abduction including forceful recruitments and cattle related fighting in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar, Rubkona, Aweil East and Pibor, continue to negatively impact vulnerable populations, particularly among girls and boys who have witnessed various atrocities and at risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety disorders. This has been coupled with loss of friends and family members further affecting mental health and emotional development of children, further exacerbated by the COVID-19 outbreak with only 2 percent of south Sudan’s having been fully vaccinated (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022).
These have been further exacerbated by recurrent yearly flooding extremes (2018, 2019, 2022 and 2022) that have affected at least 1 million people as at November 2022 (Flooding Situation Updates, 2022); with worse affected counties including Unity state and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. This coupled with unprecedented food insecurity levels; with children (boys and girls), men and women with disabilities facing greater challenges to access services. COVID 19 related economic pressures have further contributed to negative coping strategies including child labour and early and forced marriages (UNICEF, 2022; IPC November 2022) in addition to increasing cases of both forceful and voluntary recruitment of children, with recent reports in Unity (UNMISS, 2022).
Most vulnerable and displaced populations’ needs are further worsened by protection concerns including GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse, predominantly among girls and women with an uptick in cases of GBV cases in 2021; which are often under-reported due to stigma and lack of legal assistance. Women and girls continue to face increased risks of harassment, assault and sexual violence, early marriages with estimated that 52% of girls are married between the age of 15 and 18 (Child Rights Coalition, 2016), poor rule of law which continues to prevent perpetrators from being held accountable for violations.
Proposed project which will target 20,000 beneficiaries comprised of 2000 men, 2000 women, 8000 boys and 8000 girls including people with special needs/ disabilities. Proposed initiatives, which in line key Child Protection Sub Cluster objectives: Vulnerable and at risk girls, boys, men and women including children with disabilities have improved access to quality lifesaving child protection; and Reduce the suffering of boys , girls , women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk or experience violence , abuse ,exploitation and neglect including GBV through provision of specialized/lifesaving child protection and multi sectoral services.
Proposed action will respond to the critical child protection needs via child rights and child protection awareness, including strengthened psychosocial support, case management and strengthening community based child protection networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children and other protection concerns including recruitment and use of children in armed forces and groups, family tracing and re-unification, child protection response monitoring, support and referrals among others. These initiatives will ensure COVID 19 mitigation measures in line with the ministry of health/ WHO guidelines including advocacy for COVID vaccine uptake. The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict sensitive programming by upholding Do No Harm ethos.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable members who shall include people with special needs, illiterate, women and girls will have an equal opportunity in active participation throughout the project cycle. These will be engaged and be part of implementation committees including ensuring assistance is offered including priority given to people with disabilities who cannot walk and ensure services are provided at their residences or ensuring activities are implemented close to their homesteads.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly, illiterate); local community leaders, community based child protection networks/ members, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation. VSF Suisse field teams will also conduct weekly/monthly project management meetings to review and consider any issues emerging that may impact on the project implementation, in addition to mainstreaming of cross cutting issues such as gender, protection, HIV/AIDS and accountability etc. Men, women, boys and girls are consulted jointly and separately including making arrangements to engage people with special needs such as disabled. Sufficient information on the project, includingsharing relevant documents with the local leadership, local State/County ministries will be shared to enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at Activity/ distribution sites and appropriate contacts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In line with VSF Suisse PSEA policy, the organization will sensitize targeted communities on the principles of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and harassment including communities having key contact information they can confidentially report. Beneficiaries will as well be empowered through sensitizations and information dissemination including ensuring their meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

VSF Suisse will set up community engagement and feedback desks at targeted sites, which will be manned by male and female beneficiary representatives to collect feedback that shall be reviewed on a weekly basis including holding consultations and discussions on issues raised and way forward. These will then inform programming including relevant adjustments upon consultations with other key stakeholders including line ministries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Key risks such as insecurity and inter/ intra communal violence including congregation of beneficiaries during implementation, long distances covered especially by women/ girls to access services including PSEA; will be mitigated through conflict sensitive programming. VSF Suisse actions will ensure appropriate conflict sensitivity including dissemination of peace messaging to de-escalate tensions and working closely with key stakeholders on ground including relevant line ministries to ensure safety and access guarantees’. VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights, UXOs while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host, returnee and IDP communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided. The bulk of the program activities are planned to be delivered during dry periods, to ensure communities are not distracted from the core farming activities with potential to harm their productive livelihood engagements. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. VSF Suisse will in addition monitor security trends and ensure conflict sensitive programming and linkages with other security focal points such as UN Department of Safety and Security Contingency Plans for South Sudan, South Sudan NGO Forum Security Working G and short message alerts and coordination with UN OCHA and UN HAS emergency relocation and evacuation plans. As cases of COVID 19 continue to spread, VSF Suisse will ensure implementation adheres to key Ministry of Health/ WHO Guidelines including provision of protective gears among frontline staff, ensuring social distancing and messaging on key preventative efforts throughout implementation and COVID vaccine uptake messaging.
