Project Summary

The life-threatening situation characterized by insecurities and the dire need for basic necessities in North-Eastern Nigeria remain volatile and there’s therefore the need to plan and respond to protection concerns of Internally Displaced Persons, returnees, and host communities. Increased armed conflict attacks, particularly in some LGAs in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states, highlighted the risks of Women, Men, Boys, and Girls Including the elderly and Persons with Disabilities (PWD). The well-being of communities and that of the humanitarian workers was further threatened by the COVID-19 pandemic.
As a result of COVID-19, there was an increase in protection risks such as violence and unlawful arrest by security forces, land evictions, loss of means of livelihood and limited access to assistance including food and health care, increase in teenage pregnancies, Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), sexual assault, exploitative child labor and other protection concerns due to government declarations that included curfews, state lockdown, and movement restrictions. In addition, reports of COVID-19 related misinformation, rumors, and stigmatization resulted in other protection concerns such as tension within the communities. With the situation returning to a state of normalcy, the impact of COVID-19 is still being experienced by community members whose needs were exacerbated
The IRC’s approach is to promote protection and solutions leading to self-reliance of the affected community. The approach will be supported through Protection Case management, Targeted Individual Assistance, and linkages to specialized services in responding to identified protection cases. Other activities for prevention will include awareness-raising in communities and advocacy with the government on protection issues, protection monitoring, capacity building of stakeholders, protection mainstreaming, and information services through information desks and Sign Post. The IRC will upgrade information centers to an information counseling platform known as Sign Post that will ensure a two-way responsive feedback through an online portal and will support protection action groups and humanitarian workers to provide information to the community.
These activities shall ensure full and equal participation, inclusion, and access by marginalized groups.
Since community social norms which are viewed as appropriate exacerbate these protection concerns that encourage discrimination and inequality towards vulnerable groups, our intervention will ensure that women, men, boys’ and girls’ are safe at home, in the communities, and in schools and are they are able to access appropriate and quality services when they experience harm.

1 - Needs

The IRC will address the needs for a specialized response to protection risks that have been exacerbated as a result of COVID-19 in empowerment driven approach and ensure access to justice. The project will also address the need for information sharing on matters that regard human rights, domestic policies, key messages on countering risks associated with COVID-19 rumors, and myths. And also information on referrals to services and access to justice. There is also the need for the provision of civil documentation such as identification cards and birth certificates to enable access to services as well as to ease the movement of individuals across the different States. The project will also address the need for knowledge building of community protection groups on the different protection issues and how to respond to them in order to strengthen the community-based protection. There is a need for regular gathering of information about people’s needs and possible rights violations against them. This will help to influence and guide the planning and actions of Governments and humanitarian actors.The project will also advocate for durable solutions for IDPs including voluntary returns in safety and dignity as well as supported integration. There is also the need to reposition ourselves for emergency preparedness and response to flooding, epidemics/pandemics, forced displacements, and forced returns that affect the communities we already working in. The needs for access to services such as livelihood will be addressed by this project through collaboration and coordination with the different sectors concerned.

2 - Activities

1. Protection Case Management: The IRC will provide protection case management services to individuals that face heightened protection risks and cannot benefit from specialized child or GBV case management services. Our Specialized protection workers will offer individualized interventions to identified clients experiencing high or medium risk of further harm while those considered to be at a low risk will be supported through safe referrals to other services.2. Protection Monitoring: The IRC utilizes multiple modalities for protection monitoring in order to triangulate and validate information. Information will be gathered on access to basic services and possible violations of socio-economic rights such as food, health, water; COVID-19 protection risks; Protection risks associated with Rumors and Myths of COVID-19; land, property and housing rights especially in return areas; civil and political rights such as the right to life, security, freedom from torture, freedom of movement, free, informed and voluntary return; deprivation of liberty and access to fair judicial processes by both government and customary justice institutions including a focus on adherence to fundamental human rights principles particularly by customary justice institutions. 3. Protection mainstreaming: As a global leader in protection mainstreaming, the IRC will will integrate four key elements of protection mainstreaming: (1) meaningful access to assistance and services; (2) prioritising safety, dignity and doing no harm; (3) accountability and participation; and (4) empowerment of beneficiaries that guide all sectors in the COVID-19 front line as well as sector activities that target the communities.4. Targeted Individual Assistance: Targeted Individual assistance (TIA) which can be in form of cash or in-kind is provided once to enable most vulnerable individuals to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access and participation to services. It aims to build on rather than replace existing support mechanisms. The IRC will support in addressing immediate needs of beneficiaries that falls outside the scope of Child Protection and Women Protection and Empowerment program by accepting referrals from other sectors to enable extremely vulnerable individuals in situation where no other alternative response, assistance or support exists to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity.5. Community Based Protection: We recognize the communities as active agents of their own protection as well as stakeholders in protection approaches, the IRC will provide ongoing support, capacity development, mentoring and other assistance to our Protection Action Groups composed of IDPs, Returnees and Host Community. The support and assistance will help the groups identify protection and justice concerns particularly in relation to the actions of traditional justice mechanisms and protection risks associated with COVID-19 in their respective communities and find ways to address such concerns by using existing community resources, allies and local strategies to the greatest extent possible. Ultimately, this will increase proactivity of communities and reduce their reliance on external humanitarian agencies.6. Structured capacity strengthening of State and Non-State actors: Access to justice partners, ministries responsible for civil documentations, access to justice, health, SEMA, NEMA and Non-State actors that provide specialized services targeting the vulnerable community members such as PWDs, will be supported through training on the different legislations that enables a protective environment for the community.7. Upgraded Protection Information Counselling Center: The information counseling centers will offer a broad range of information relevant to IDPs and host communities, including how to access available services and general humanitarian trends developments,

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC is a global leader in protection mainstreaming. The IRC Protection team will integrate four key elements of protection mainstreaming: (1) meaningful access to assistance and services; (2) prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm; (3) accountability and participation; and (4) empowerment of beneficiaries. The strategy will be informed by findings from beneficiary assessments, a staff capacity assessment and safety audits and the feedback and accountability mechanism. Foundational capacity building activities for different sectors will be conducted to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including child safeguarding principles, and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC will sensitize the community on IRC’s accountability for quality services and on the ways they can provide confidential feedback, drawing on the IRC’s in-depth guidelines on client responsiveness. Using a variety of feedback mechanisms including hotlines, the IRC will continue to gather feedback on services. Protection mainstreaming reports comprised of focus group discussions, monitoring visits, questionnaires and key informant interview. Inputs from the Accountability Feedback Mechanism (AFM) and protection mainstreaming personnel.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate robust client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, older people and people with disabilities. These will include regular FGDs with men and women; and listening sessions, including sessions specifically with women and girls; suggestion boxes at project sites, including within women’s safe spaces and protection information centres; a toll-free hotline accessible to illiterate beneficiaries, with notices in local languages with the hotline number posted prominently at project sites, community dialogues and household interviews, which will reach beneficiaries served by mobile teams in hard-to-reach locations. The IRC will also conduct regular consultations with community leaders to get feedback from the community about project activities including and whether the feedback mechanism itself is safe, easy and accessible. Formal feedback mechanism will include the use of hotline opinion/complaint boxes in project areas. The IRC will ensure the feedback is confidential and will provide responses to the feedback that is received and use this feedback to modify its approach wherever appropriate. Moreover, monthly coordination and project review meetings will be held with stakeholders to discuss the project objectives, challenges, including feedback received from the communities as described above, and the way forward. In this way, the services and community activities remain responsive to the needs and concerns raised by the communities.
