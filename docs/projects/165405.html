Project Summary

As part of ALL TOGETHER Gender-based Violence Program (GBV) in 2020.
ALL TOGETHER plans to respond to the growing needs of conflict-affected and most
vulnerable families (women, adolescent girls, men, adolescent boys, and people with
disability) within northwest Syria by providing GBV services projects.
The projects' activities will respond to the Gender-Based Violent (GBV) survivors by
providing free GBV services within women and girls safe space (WGSS) Centers in afrin &
alraee&jarablus and Azaz One center in each area & MHPSS
Centers. The response services will target (women, adolescent girls, and women and girls
with disabilities) GBV survivors.
The WGSS centers will provide GBV case management, as well as multi protection
support activities, such as recreational activities, psychosocial support, life skills, and
razing awareness sessions for the center beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the centers will allocate specific activities to target young mothers (age
under 1 8). The activities will aim to raise the young mothers aware of GBV risks, RH, and
CP issues, by using young mother club curriculum developed by UNFPA.
Moreover, the GBV outreach’s teams will work on GBV prevention activities by conducting
raising awareness sessions and GBV campaigns for men, and adolescent boys, in the
community, to increase their role in preventing GBV in the community.
Also, the outreach teams will use the awareness raising toolkits; which developed by GBV
sub-cluster by targeting men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising sessions.
The GBV program teams will ensure in delivering the protection information to survivors
in
an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access, using
the GBV sub-cluster SOPs.

4 - Needs

Communities in northwest Syria continued to experience volatile and unpredictablesecurity situations and displacement. According to PMTF quarterly report April-Jun1 8,indicates that communities continue to experience challenges in accessing basic needsand services, and face a variety of, and often inter-linked protection risks. Also, theGBVfocused analysis of FGDs, as well as the KI interviews conducted in 201 7 for theHumanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), identified this to be a common issue, mainlyaffecting women and girls who are trying to access aid (Voices from Syria, 201 8).Moreover, the questionnaire (MSNA); where focus group discussions were conductedwith (men, women, adolescent boys and adolescent girls) has indicated that girls andboys of all ages still face violence in multiple life situations: at home, at school, at work,and in the community. Adolescence, in particular, is a time when exposure to new formsof violence increases. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detainedand recruited by parties to the conflict or to be involved in child labor, while girls areincreasingly at risk of being recruited and used by parties to the conflict in somelocations. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms ofgender-based violence, including sexual violence. As is the case globally, women andgirls are disproportionately affected by all forms of gender-based violence in Syria, inboth the public and private spheres. In addition, women who have experienced divorceand widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed tostigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. 1 - Risks: Some people and dominant groups in the work area do not accept the idea thatthere are particular centers for women and girls especially that our center is the first oneof its kind in the area. Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure: External awareness sessionsto introduce the center's activities. 2- Risk: Distinguishing between residents anddisplaced citizens Risk Level : Average /Medial Mitigation Measure : Full survey of thepercentage of the displaced people in the region and implementing external activities forthem and inviting them to the center. 3- Risk: Distinguishing between residents of the cityand the residents of the neighborhood areas Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure :Increase the external activities in the neighborhood villages more and more. 4-Risk :threats of kidnapping the workers in organizations that have been widely spread in theSyrian inside Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure : district of safety and security rules interms of mobility and not to declare the dates of mobility and not to paste any banners ofthe association logo on the cars. 5- Risk : The inability to meet the needs of all women inthe region that we work in. according to their needs and provide them the services theyneed. 6-Risk : Fear of the inability to keep the confidential and secret documents safe dueto the emergency security incidents. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one ofthe major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conflict in northernSyria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures:1 - Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordinationand continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure thesafety of staff and beneficiaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities and multiplelocations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensified shelling 5- Daily RiskAssessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowdingof beneficiaries during activities. B-Low Quality of Programming/ This includeslimitations on direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight datarelated to gender, age or protection issues etc. This is the setting for a decline in programquality unless remote management tools are accurately employed. Solution/ Mitigationmeasures: 1 - Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2-Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authoritiesto ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities andmultiple locations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensified shelling 5-Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding thecrowding of beneficiaries during activities. C: Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict / Thisrisk including that staff of organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besiegedin one area of control or operational assets are lost. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1 -Continue to gather information from range of sources. 2- Develop contingency andevacuation plan to work in alternative location. 3- Continuous efforts by the accessteams to gain new assurances
