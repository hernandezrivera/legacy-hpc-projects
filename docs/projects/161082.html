Project Summary

The project will provide support to the most vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, boys, youths, women, and men in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks in targeted areas of Raga, Wau, Baggari, Jur River and Pochalla counties of Western Bahr El Ghazal State and Jonglei State respectively.
The project will be provide the following key sub-thematic services to at risk and vulnerable children, youths, and community members in the targeted areas: provision of comprehensive case management (CPMS standards 18, 11, 13); provision of family tracing and reunification for UASC (CPMS standard 13); provision of quality age and gender appropriate psychosocial support (PSS) through community based interventions to extremely vulnerable girls, boys and their caregivers (CPMS standards 10, 15); establishing and supporting of referral mechanisms (CPMS standard 17); capacity building to frontline service providers and volunteers on Child Protection including PSS, case management and general CPiE (CPMS standards 2, 18); strengthening of community structures and mechanisms including community based child protection committees (CBCPC) and child protection desks (CPMS standard 16, 17); conduct child protection awareness-raising & prevention messaging and Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities; conduct child protection assessments and monitoring using the existing and recommended tools (CPMS standard 6).
Hold the Child will use both static and mobile (especially in hard to reach areas) responses to provide the above-outlined services to the targeted populations with support from the cluster and subcluster.

1 - Needs

Many children and youths remain at-risk to recruitment into armed groups, family separation and abduction, neglect, maiming and killing, sexual and economic exploitation, physical violence and psychological trauma and inadequate parent/guardian care or support; With over 20,457 (9,499 girls, 10,958 boys) have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing as per recent reports of 18th October 2019 out of this a total 8,590(3,957 girls, 4,633 boys) as active cases. To date, more than 1M children have been affected by psychosocial distress, more than 1.17 million children aged 3 to 18 years old have lost access to education due to conflict and displacement since December 2013 about 31 percent of schools open has suffered at least one or more attacks by armed actors. Over 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups in South Sudan.IPC_South Sudan_Key_Messages_August_2019; In the Greater Western Bahr El Ghazal region, an estimated 2.37 million people experienced Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August 2019 – approximately 60% of the population in the region. While increased access, both physical and among humanitarians, has supported a decline in the number of people experiencing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Western Bahr el Ghazal compared to the same time last year, continued incidents of inter-communal conflict, market disruptions and prolonged depletion of household assets continued to limit household food availability and coping capacity. REACH report on the situation overview of Greater Bahr El Ghazal, throughout March 2019, an escalation in cattle raiding and insecurity in Jur River County triggered forced displacement. The primary needs of the displaced have been reported as food, clean water, and health services. Some of these displaced populations traveled to Wau PoC sites and IDP collective centers in Wau. There have also been refugee returns reported (88% assessed settlements in WBeG in March). The IOM DTM found Dulu, of Raja County, had a large influx of South Sudanese refugees returning from Darfur, Sudan in late February 2019. Since July 2019, more than 908,000 people have been affected by unusually heavy rainfall and subsequent flooding, of whom 620,000 people (the majority being women and children) need immediate humanitarian assistance. UN OCHA South Sudan Situation Report of 30th October 2019. Floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. Some of the areas affected include Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Twic East, Pibor, Pochalla and Uror counties in Jonglei; among others within South Sudan. The heavy rains have inundated shelters, water sources, crops and crucial productive assets in many areas. They have also damaged essential service infrastructure like schools and health facilities, impacting women and children in particular. Thousands of families have temporarily relocated from the worst affected to safer areas. The flooding has increased the risk of diseases among the communities. The most commonly reported ones include acute watery diarrhea, respiratory infections, and malaria.Pochalla is a remote and marginalized region of Boma State, with little access to essential social services, as well as educational and economic opportunities. Children face grave protection concerns and there is a strong need for increased access to CPiE services. To reduce risk and build the capacity of children and communities to protect themselves from such threats, Hold the Child will continue to undertake Child Protection in Emergencies related interventions to at-risk children in Wau, Baggari, Jur River, Raga, and Pochalla. This project also aims to reduce household vulnerability and the risk of recruitment and use through vocational training and economic strengthening activities using the existing Youth center.

2 - Activities

• Provide family tracing and reunification services, including identification, registration, and documentation, interim care, family tracing, follow up visits and reunification, reintegration follow-ups and case closures for UASC. Target 500 children (250 girls and 250 boys).• Provide comprehensive case management services and referrals to extremely vulnerable children. Target 700 children (350 girls and 350 boys). These will include training and coaching of staff and volunteers on case management.• Provide community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers in extremely vulnerable situations and those facing multiple protection risks. Target 12,000 children (6,000 girls and 6,000 boys); 2,800 adults (1,900 females and 1,000 males).• Provide release and reintegration programmes for 250 children (150 boys and 100 other vulnerable girls) previously formally and informally released from armed forces.• Conduct awareness campaigns on the prevention of family separation, prevention of recruitment and re-recruitment of children. Target 9,000 adults (6,000 females and 3,000 males).• Support caregivers and parents to provide appropriate care and good parenting skills for affected children and mitigate risks of psychosocial distress and protection concerns for children in their households, communities, and schools. Target 7,000 adults (4,000 females and 3,000 males).• Building capacity of CP staff and community volunteers, community-based child protection structures, including government officers through coaching and monitoring in CPiE. Target 160 adults (80 females and 80 males)• Conduct peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities among youths and adults. Target 4,400 adults (2,200 females and 2,200 males)• Conduct monthly mobile and static protection assessments and monitoring for advocacy and inform the cluster on interventions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Hold the Child will help the affected communities claim their rights through information and documentation, and support efforts to strengthen respect for rights.Hold the Child will have a duty to advocate for the full respect of children’s rights and compliance with international law that supports a stronger protective environment. All children and other targeted beneficiaries should be able to access solutions (such as legal actions at local, national or international levels) and claim legal rights (such as inheritance or restitution) that may influence their ability to protect themselves and to claim other rights. Children should be supported to become informed and engaged citizens. Hold the Child will implement simple, accessible, child-friendly, anonymous programme feedback and reporting mechanisms at each site. Affected populations will/must be aware of how to use the mechanisms and what response they should expect when they raise a concern or report an incident. All general programme and Hold the Child’s feedback will be addressed promptly and shared with staff and associates in team meetings and performance reviews following the principles of confidentiality and need to know. A friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms such as suggestion boxes, one on one, a focus group discussions among others will be explored in all our target areas. Through regular field visits will enable to have open meetings with the communities of the affected populations, therefore the beneficiaries will be always be given a chance to express themselves safely. Reports will be collected from the community-based child protection members who are close with the beneficiaries hence strengthening the linkages of feedback and complaints.
