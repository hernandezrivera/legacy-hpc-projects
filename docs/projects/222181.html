Project Summary

More than 2.1million children 6-59 months and more than 1.1 million PLW women are acutely malnourished and in need of treatment between July 2024 -June 2025 in South Sudan (IPC Nov 2024).
This projects aims to reach 94,000 direct beneficiaries, targeting 65,000 children<5yrs and 29,000 women and caregivers in 6 Counties of Baliet, Panyikang, Lopalafon, Juba, Lainya, and Morobo. The beneficiaries comprise of children and adults, both male and female inclusive of persons with disability. The proposed target accounts for 37% of nutrition cluster targets for these locations.
Health Link intends to implement varies activities to achieve reduction in malnutrition through achievement of Nutrition Cluster Objectives (NCO) as below:
Cluster objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025.
To achieve the above objective, HLSS intends to:
• Establishment and training of Mother to mother support groups.
• Provision of MIYCN counselling services both at facility and community level.
• Training of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) on MIYCN/prevention of malnutrition
• Training of health workers on MIYCN/Prevention of malnutrition.
• Vitamin A supplementation and deworming of children aged 6-59 months.
• Training of pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWGs) and caregivers on Family MUAC to screen children for acute malnutrition.
Cluster objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations by the end of 2025
• Screening of boys and girls aged 6-59 months for MAM and SAM
• Screening of PBW who were for acute malnutrition
• Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months.
• Identification and referral of children with severe acute malnutrition with complications.
• Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months and PLWs.
• Training of health and nutrition and nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN with major focusing on case detection and referral for community health workers.
Cluster objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025.
Health link south Sudan with support the attainment of the above objective through:
• Participation in national nutrition surveys.
• Routine submission of NIS reports on to the national database.
• Routine surveillance and reporting of diseases of epidemiological importance.
Cluster objective 4: To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025:
• Screening and testing for malaria for all children with SAM.
• Training of Health and Nutrition workers on Malaria testing and treatment.
• Support establishment of kitchen gardens.
• Support national nutrition surveys and GBV safety Audits at service provision sites.
• Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community etc.
• Training of Health and nutrition workers on GBV, case identification and referral including how to respond to GBV disclosures safely and appropriately.
Provision of WASH kits and LLITN for all children admitted with SAM.
• Training of CNV and Nutrition workers on provision of ECD
These activities will be implemented in collaboration with County health officers and SMOH and implementing partners of Health, WASH, Protection, FLS and Education among others in accordance to MOH-SS guidelines

1. Needs

More than 2.1million children 6-59 months and more than 1.1 million PLW women people are in urgent need of humanitarian lifesaving nutrition assistance in South Sudan (IPC Nov 2024). The malnutrition situation in South Sudan is Critical with Overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels at 15.8%, that is above the 15% emergency threshold. (FSNM r26)In Central equatorial, GAM rates are estimated at 10.3% and 4.8% in Juba and lianya counties respectively which have improved from 9.6% and 15.3% reported in 2020. (FSNMS R24 & R25). In EES, GAM rates are reported to worsen from 12.5% to 15.3% in Lopa-lafon. (FSNMS R29,R28 ). Likewise, Upper Nile reported extremely critical GAM rates in the country rising from 11.25% and 10.5% in Dec 2019 to 17.1% and 20.5% in Panyikang and Baliet respectively, IPC (Nov 2024) classifies regions of Upper Nile and EES to be in critical phases of acute malnutrition while Central Equatoria state is classified at Alert level; expected to worsen if no interventions are commenced. Drivers of the acute malnutrition situation include: Inadequate child feeding practices: with Minimum Dietary Diversity was reported at 16.5 %, and Minimum Meal Frequency at 7.5%. High disease burden: One out of every two children reported illness in two weeks preceding the FSNMS Round 30 - with 18.3 % affected by diarrhea, 20.3% by acute respiratory infection (ARI), and 38 % by fever. Low program coverage, Limited access to sanitation facilities: 74 % of households at national level practice open defecation that is more detrimental in the flood affected areas and increases environmental contamination. Acute malnutrition and food insecurity with household resorting to relative coping strategies in IPC AFI Phase 3 (Crisis) or above. Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security, and FSL cluster sectoral analysis; Between December 2024 to March 2025, 6.1 million people (45 % of the population analyzed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). The high levels of food insecurity are driven by: Economic crisis-that is characterized by worsening currency depreciation, high food prices and declining household purchasing power, Conflict and insecurity: Forced displacement, interruptions in humanitarian aid delivery, and localized conflict and insecurity especially in the Baliet, Lopalafon, Budi county and in sporadic areas within Juba and Lianya counties. Climatic shocks: Floods and dry spells contribute to low agricultural production. Flooding was 10 % higher in 2024 compared to 2023 and the floods not only displaced people but also disrupted delivery of humanitarian support, and contributed to low access to health services in the face of increased disease burden driven poor sanitation practices thus increasing the risk of acute malnutrition. Low agricultural production: Unpredictable and extreme weather patterns, poor access to quality seeds and use of traditional farming methods negatively impacted agricultural production, resulting in increased humanitarian food assistance needs.To bridge the gap, HLSS intends to roll out a strategic nutrition response by establishing mobile teams to deliver nutrition services in 6 counties, targeting locations of: -wonduruba, Bungu and Ganji in Juba County, Kimba PHCU, Lokube PHCC and Geri PHCU in Morobo county, -Ryangnum, Benythiang, Baychan, Gelacel in Baliet County, Pakwa and nyibol in Panyikang County, - Kupera, mukaya and Lainya town in Lanya County where beneficiaries walk over 2 hours to seek services. This proposed nutrition project is meant integrate into ongoing projects run by health link in these locations.

2. Project activities

The proposed nutrition project will be integrated in to the already existing Health link south sudans’ health, nutrition, protection and CCCM projects programming in this locations. The following activities make up the project:• Community sensitization and awareness creation focusing on prevention and causes of malnutrition.• Conducting community dialogue meetings and focus group discussions.• Mass MUAC screening for all children under 5 including pregnant and lactating mothers in the community.• Micronutrient supplementation and Deworming through National immunization days, twice annually.• Conducting Radio talk shows on awareness and prevention of malnutrition.• Strengthening Family MUAC screening and formation of mother to mother support groups.• Identification and referral of children with malnutrition to OTP/TSFP sites.• Establishment of OTP/TSFP sites that will be used for management of malnourished children.• Treatment of Severe and Moderate acute malnutrition among children 5-59months and Pregnant and Lactating mother.• Conduct integrated outreach sessions to hard-to-reach areas and to displaced populations in Baliet County and any other location that may encounter disaster such as flooding etc. and home visits by CNVs.• On-job mentorship, coaching and Training of health and nutrition workers on MIYCN, CMAM and IMAM.• Conducting MIYCN/IYCF individual and group counselling sessions at facility and community level.• Screening and testing for malarial among all children with severe acute Malnutrition and those with medical complications admitted at the Stabilization Centre.• Support existing facilities in establishment of kitchen gardens and demonstration gardens with support of the community nutrition volunteers and mother support groups.• Conduct cooking demonstration sessions to beneficiaries both at facility and community level.• Screening, identification, referral and linkage of GBV survivor to GBV services.• Training of health and Nutrition workers on GBV case detection and referral.• Testing and treatment of malaria.• Training of nutrition and health workers on Malaria testing and management.• Support the national nutrition SMART or FSNMS country surveys in the locations of implementation.• Establish a community complaint feedback mechanism in the nutrition sites by placing suggestion boxes, establishing a help desk at each site, initiating community focus group discussions with the community members.• Continuous joint supportive supervisions and mentorships.• Provision of WASH kits to children attending services at nutrition sites.• Provision of ECD activities using locally available materials at nutrition sites• Community sensitization on hygiene and sanitation.• Maintain demonstration gardens at all health/nutrition facilities and community house hold levels. • Conduct exit interview and MIYCN barrier Surveys to inform decision making.
