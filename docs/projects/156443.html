Project Summary

The objective of UNHCR's project is to increase resilience and livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable people affected by the Syrian crisis. This is to be achieved by providing livelihoods support to IDPs and returnees as well as selected members of host communities across Syria. The project contributes to the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) Sector outcomes are as follows:
1) Sustainable livelihoods for households and communities provided.
2) Access to basic social infrastructure established and increased.
3) Access to basic productive and related marketing infrastructure increased.

Needs

The crisis in Syria has had a severe impact on livelihoods including on the incomes of a significant proportion of the population. There remains an urgent need for improved access to basic social and economic services as well as livelihood opportunities (including through income generating activities). Delivering improved livelihoods for UNHCR persons of concern in Syria is a complex challenge not least on account of the security situation, the difficulties that arise in relation to certain housing, land and property (HLP) issues as well as climatic-related impacts (e.g. drought) which have been exacerbated by damage to key agriculture- related infrastructure, including irrigation, in many rural areas. For example, a number of areas, including parts of Deir-E-Zor and Rural Damascus (e.g. East Ghouta), became accessible only recently accessible in 2018 - before which time they were largely inaccessible other than through emergency interventions. in 2018, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) residing outside their areas of origin remained very high (estimated at 6.1 million persons according to HRP 2018). In the same year, a number of refugees returned voluntarily from their countries of asylum and their numbers are expected to grow significantly in the future. According to UNHCR internal data, as at 29 December 2018 there are some 40,205 refugees, asylum seekers and IDPs living in camps in north-east Syria, where they continue to experience varying degrees of restriction on their movement and had limited opportunities to apply their full range of skills and capabilities. The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2018 emphasized the requirement for multiple livelihoods-related interventions ranging from creating short-term jobs through cash-for-work activities, to supporting entrepreneurship and small business as well as providing vocational training and start up kits. It also emphasized the importance of support for livestock and agriculture activities in rural communities where farmers and pastoralists are particularly vulnerable. The rehabilitation of basic infrastructure that is critical to boost local economic activity and thus livelihood activities was also deemed a priority. The number of female headed households and other vulnerable groups, such as persons with disability and youth at risk, has risen significantly. Financial hardship and lack of regular income has resulted in many families resorting to negative coping mechanisms to reduce their expenses or find ways to meet their needs, such as school dropout, child labour, child marriage, recruitment, survival sex and others. On the other hand, the hardship faced by heads of families to meet the needs, and changes of the roles within the family, have caused tension inside the home and, in many cases, resulted in domestic violence and family separation. Syria suffers from a high unemployment rate and increasing loss of livelihoods opportunities. The unemployment rate in Syria currently stands above 50%, a figure that rises significantly in the context of youth unemployment. The employment needs are huge among both skilled and unskilled IDPs.

Implementation of activities

The following six (6) main activities will be undertaken as part of the project:1) Provision of key ancillary goods and services to support farmers, fishers and others dependent on the natural resource base for their livelihoods; 2) Rehabilitation of key local and community level economic and social infrastructure such as bakeries, market places, vocational training classes/workshops, warehouses, irrigation channels; 3) Delivery of formal vocational and other skills training courses (outside community centres) to address gaps in knowledge and to enable adjustment to changing labour market and other conditions; 4) Provision of business advisory services (including 'counselling') regarding business start up processes, business management and pursuing business opportunities; 5) Conducting key socio economic surveys, livelihoods analysis and other assessments (e.g. value chain analysis) necessary to understand vulnerability and relevant coping strategies and livelihoods opportunities; 6) Supporting temporary employment schemes where there are no realistic livelihoods alternatives.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination and the possibility of tension arising from selection of beneficiaries of livelihoods interventions. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Needs assessment will be conducted to gather information about the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees to be targeted. Participatory assessments and community consultations will be applied in all stages of the programme cycle to enable beneficiaries to actively engage in the assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation of the project. Awareness raising on equal opportunities and respect for human rights will be part of each intervention. Indicators of tension will be monitored throughout project implementation to intervene in a timely manner as necessary.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Existing labour market opportunities limit the participation of youth, women and persons with disabilities resulting in lower remuneration and return to negative coping mechanisms. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Analyse access to market opportunities with an age, gender and diversity lens. Compare this information with protection needs assessments. Conduct market assessments and socio-economic field surveys to identify viable livelihoods interventions. Provide livelihoods counselling and maintain referral system with protection partners.
