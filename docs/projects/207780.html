Project Summary

"The proposed PRIDE Project will be implemented in the host communities of Ukhiya Upazila under Cox’s bazar District. The project will engage the host community to ensure the access to water for their agriculture production and daily uses through Drip irrigation and rainwater harvesting. The Project will promote rooftop rainwater harvesting at household levels as alternative water sources for drinking water, domestic use, homestead gardening etc. Harvesting rainwater will increase the sustainability of water supplies as well as reduce the climate change vulnerability. The project support households in the host community to set up for rain water harvesting along with technical supports.
In the summer season (mid-April to mid-May), there is a huge scarcity of irrigation water, especially for growing summer vegetables in some of the sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar . Water availability during that time is hardly sufficient for drinking purposes and is not adequate for irrigating summer crops. Therefore, channelize water for the irrigation system is essential to protect the production from the calamity. Therefore, the project will pilot and promote drip irrigation in the host community for crop production, improved and efficient irrigation support. The project will support host community households to access water for crop production trough piloting drip irrigation methods considering the climate-smart agriculture to improve the agriculture development and climate responsiveness. "

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"Yes, the PRIDE Project incorporates elements of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM), as well as environmental considerations and climate change mitigation measures. In terms of Emergency Response Capacity Building, the project focuses on enhancing capabilities for responding to hazards like cyclones, tornadoes, landslides, and floods. This involves comprehensive training for HELVETAS and partner staff, as well as Rohingya and local communities. Adaptive actions such as tree planting are emphasized, contributing to community resilience.In the realm of Livelihood Development and Skill Enhancement, the project integrates disaster risk reduction preparedness. Six livelihood designs incorporating climate-resilient agriculture management are developed, emphasizing the importance of building resilience within the communities. Initiatives include home gardens, income-generating activities, and skill development programs targeting youth with opportunities in various fields. This multifaceted approach not only enhances livelihoods but also prepares communities for potential disasters."
