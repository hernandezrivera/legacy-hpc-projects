Project Summary

The project will contribute to the Food Security Sector Strategic Objective # 3 The ability
of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental
threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and
shocks are pursued
The objective of the project is to increase food security resilience of vulnerable Palestinian through the provision of essential services and promotion of agricultural activities in
Qalqiliya governorate. The project will focus on vulnerable farmers and their communities by providing rehabilitation of their agricultural land including improved irrigation systems, enhancing access through rehabilitation of agricultural road, along with emergency
technical support; in order to increase agricultural yields.
The project also aims to address violations and other threats faced by Palestinian farmers, by increasing knowledge of referral pathway regarding any legal issue and confiscation of agricultural lands along with advocacy and localized awareness
campaigns. In order to do so, SIF will cooperate with NRC, through its partners ESDC, especially for awareness training on rights violation.
The choice of Qalqiliya governorate has been informed by the launching in September of the Strategic plan by the local authorities for this governorate identifying critical needs for intervention in agriculture and spelling out the main challenges.
The vision for the project is multiyear, of which the present project represent the first
phase. Agricultural technical support and activities does not provide income measurable
results in one single year due also to the start date of the project being 1st January 2020,
therefore SIF intend to continue monitoring the outcome of the present project over the
following year in addition to expand its intervention in neighbouring areas based on the
emergent and updated needs.

1 - Needs

Ministry of Agriculture’s strategy on agricultural development highlights the fertile quality of Qalqiliya’s terrain, the water availability and the temperate climate that allows for the production of tropical produce like guava, avocado, mango, that are accompanied with willingness from the agricultural sector to incorporate new and improved agricultural techniques. Agriculture in Qalqiliya governorate amounts in total to 86% rain fed agriculture and 14% irrigated agriculture (Summary document http://www.palestinecabinet.gov.ps/WebSite/Upload/Documents/GOVPLAN/3naqeed3.pdf. ). Although it is consideredا-ﺔﻄﺨﻟاﺎﻄﻘﻠﻟ-ﺔﻳﻮﻤﻨﺘﻟع-اﺰﻟراPdf with strategy ﺔﻴﻠﻴﻘﻠﻗ-ﺔﻈﻓﺎﺤﻣ-ﻲﻋ as one of the most important governorates in the West Bank in terms of agriculture, the governorate faces challenges that are political, relating to natural resources (land, water), technical facilities and expertise. Political challenges due to the fact that 20% of its area is Israeli settlements and 74% is area C, where the majority of agricultural land and available area for future urban development are located. Enhancing agricultural activities can strengthen farmers' resilience in their land as a safe way to meet the challenges of the wall and the nearby settlements. Total land area of the Qalqiliya governorate is 173 km2. The arable area is about 125,000 dunums of which only 70,000 dunums are under cultivation, and 25,000 dunums are in need for agricultural reclamation. This can be attributed to: the high costs of reclamation and rehabilitation of agricultural land; difficulties to work in lands next to the wall and near settlements, with threat of confiscation; lack of adequate agricultural roads, problem of wild boars in the area. Regarding irrigation, although Qalqiliya is located on the western groundwater basin, which accounts for 52% of the total water resources in the West Bank, Israel exploits about 80 percent of Palestinian groundwater in the West Bank (cf. Strategy). Qalqiliya governorate faces many challenges related to the water available for agriculture: lack of water resource in the Eastern part of the governorate; where most of the agricultural activities are rain fed; worn-out main water lines; use of diesel for most of the agricultural groundwater wells with its result on the water prices; limited water harvesting projects. Qalqiliya suffers with the rest of the West Bank of the phenomenon of an increase of women headed households. (www.pcbs.gov.ps/post.aspx?lang=en&ItemID=3503). According to the update of end 2018 of the PCBS 12% of the households in the West Bank are women headed households. Due to the restrictive Israeli occupation, some women have become widows or have lost their husbands to prison, while other men are struggling to find employment. Due to discrimination in formal workforces and society’s assumption that women should not be responsible for household finances, a large majority of women in the West Bank is technically unemployed although almost all of them are working in some way. Most women are responsible for unpaid work within the home, and 85 percent of unpaid women in Gaza and West Bank work in agriculture. Therefore, while many women contribute to the economy through the agriculture value chain, they are still unpaid or underpaid for their work. Despite this major contribution, an estimated 40 percent of rural Palestinian women at working age carry out unpaid work, which means that their production is not comprehensively covered in the Gross Domestic Product (GDP). This marginalizes their role in the production process. SIF is very much aware of this challenge as will work with the communities to increase acceptance of the work of women in agriculture and support their empowerment.

2 - Activities

The project mainly aims to increase food security resilience and access to agricultural lands of vulnerable population in the governorate of Qalqiliya. In order to do so, the activities will be based on 3 main results: 1. 540 dunum of land for 150 farmers (range of 3.6 dunum each) is rehabilitated in a comprehensive way 2. 10km of agricultural roads are rehabilitated to benefit 400 farmers 3. 200 individuals (98 females - 102 males) are taught how to provide protection to their communities in regards to legal rights specifically regarding land rights and referral procedures. Main activities per result Result 1 Comprehensive Rehabilitation of 540 dunum of land for 150 farmers’ households (690 individuals [339 females, 351 males]) aiming to also improve the quality of the agriculture through provision of emergency technical support to targeted farmers a) Comprehensive Rehabilitation of 100 dunums including 20 agricultural cisterns, building of terraces, and levelling for 20 farmers’ households (92 individuals [45 females, 47 males] b) Rehabilitation of irrigation carrying lines (4 KM of pipes and other spare parts); will benefit an estimate of 80 farmers’ households (for 240 dunums) - (368 individuals [181 females, 187 males] c) Emergency technical support for men and women farmers d) Collective fencing of agricultural lands for 200 dunums benefiting 50 farmers’ households - (230 individuals [113 females, 117 males]). SIF and ESDC will be identifying potential farmers’ households via household visits and interviews applying a socio-economic scoring selection. The head of the woman headed household will be given a care time survey to ensure the performance of the agricultural-related activities will not be a hindrance to their regular child caring and chores. Result 2 10km of Agricultural roads will be rehabilitated, benefitting 400 farmers’ households (1.840 individuals [904 females, 936 males]). Selection of the roads will be done through advertisement in the village council; application by farmers; identification of priority roads with technical visit and assessment of costs. Result 3 Awareness rising of the targeted communities in regards to the Israeli land’s regulation in area C. ESDC will coordinate with NRC who will start with provision of Legal awareness rising in targeted villages a) ESDC will follow up with the constitution of local technical committees (one per each target community with around 5 members) to be able to follow up activities on the ground. This will take place during the startup stakeholders’ meeting. The members will sign a MoU together with the ToR of the committees, to ensure transparency in the implementation. b) 10 awareness sessions, on lands rights violation and other legal right that will target 170 from community and 30 local committee members (88 females - 92 males). c) Production related video or photo exhibit to raise awareness on the land issues For all aspect of the project SIF will be providing in municipalities and or community centres boxes for suggestions or complaints about all beneficiaries through the project life. We will be indicating a NO HARM principle: When making program decisions, both organization will analyse and anticipate the range of implications these decisions could have on the protection of different groups and their environment. The participation of vulnerable groups in program planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation will help to ensure that these programs are responsive to protection concerns; Coordinate with relevant actors can substantially improve a program’s impact in terms of protection. In that regards SIF and ESDC will cooperate with NRC, already established intervention.

3 - Indicators

SIF will measure the success of the project through the following outcomes and outputs indicators: Overall Objective indicator: Indicator 1: # of vulnerable people living in “Area C of Qalqiliya” have improved agriculture livelihoods and increased access to income. Target: 2,730 individuals “1,344 females and 1,386 males”.For result 1, outputs indicators are: # of dunums become available for planting and thus more productive (target: 340) for 150 farmers’ households, 20% are farmer women headed households; # of Cubic meter of water capacity per cistern become available for supplementary irrigation in rain fed area (target: 1400) for 20 farmers’ households, 20% are farmer women-headed households); KM of irrigation carrying lines rehabilitated (target: 4) for 80 farmers’ households, 10% are farmer women-headed households; # of Cubic meter of water become available for irrigation in irrigated area during the six months of the dry season (target: 350); # of dunums of agricultural land protected through fencing (target: 200) for 50 farmers’ households, 10% are farmer women-headed households; Increasing the use of land and the farmers’ presence in 100 rehabilitated dunums. Outcome indicator is: 85% of the targeted farmers’ beneficiaries of the land rehabilitation and agricultural cisterns report increased occupation of their lands due to our interventions. For result 2, outputs indicators are: KM of agricultural road rehabilitated (target: 10) for 400 farmers’ households, 10% are farmer women-headed households; # of dunums become more accessible (target: 1200). For result 3, outputs indicators are # of protection session conducted (target: 10); # of attendees of the protection campaign sessions (target: 200- 100 out of them are women); # of local protection committees formulated and trained (target 6) 20% percent of the members are women. Outcome indicator is: 80% of awareness sessions attendees become aware of their legal land rights and referrals pathways.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

How does the project enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs? SIF beneficiaries’ selection methodology is a fair, transparent and accountable system with blueprinted procedures incorporated in the mission’s implementation of projects. A multi-faceted approach is applied considering the various levels of involvement for all stakeholders. The approach consists of · Kick-off meeting/discussion with local councils (3 phase discussion approach: we introduce SIF, we introduce the project and the guidelines for steering committee selection) · Steering committee selection and verification. · Public information sessions in the selected villages about SIF (4’Ws) open to the whole community, an announcement for submission of applications. · NRC risk assessment community information sessions. · Application submission process. · All applications are entered into a scientific and mathematical database system providing scoring for each beneficiary. · Field visits with technical and social aspects are conducted to validate the beneficiary selection. · Scoring system application list verification and review with the steering committee. · Publication of the list in the selected villages. · Response to feedback mechanism. · Final list for selected beneficiaries. Moreover, information dissemination through announcements; for transparency and credibility with communities; we announce through village councils' and others CBO’s Facebook pages and through mosques calls in addition to keeping the old method of the announcement (written announcement to be published in all public places). After finalizing the selection of beneficiaries the list will be announced and feedback from the community will be taken into consideration. SIF West Bank applies a beneficiary participatory approach to its projects in many phases of humanitarian activities such as assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. There are community discussions, presentations and awareness sessions, feedback mechanisms in addition to pre and post-assessments. Additionally, SIF West Bank’s community-based interventions are based on potential, promise, and performance. SIF will provide all the beneficiaries with the opportunity to be advised about the intervention, take feedback, have access to information concerning the project and organization and integrate them with key decisions made during the project implementation. Furthermore, committees help SIF to understand deeply the community needs by gathering information relevant to the design of the project's intervention in terms of geographical locations, and needs. As providing information and gathering feedback on the design is one of the main components of accountability to beneficiaries, public announcements will be revised with the presence of the project committee and published in key locations around the village to give equal chance to the community to apply.
