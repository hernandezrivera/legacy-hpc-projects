Project Summary

Lack of adequate WASH services remains the main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases impacting the health of growing children. Lack of access to water & adequate sanitation in schools is a contributing factor to poor school attendance. In historically drought-affected districts, lack of access to water for humans & livestock remains one of the leading causes of displacement, conflict and diseases. Anticipated climatic changes with the upcoming La- Nina that may lead to drought, water scarcity and poor hygiene practices may increase in water scarce areas. According to the JMCNA, 41 per cent of Somalis do not have access to regular & stable improved water source for drinking & only 66 per cent among them walk less than 15 minutes to reach their main drinking water source. 20 per cent of Somalis do not have enough water per day to cover basic needs. Over half buy water or trek to other villages, often over 30 minutes.
According to a KAP surveys conducted in Gedo, Puntland & Mogadishu at the beginning of 2020, Open defecation & using un-hygienic/un-clean facilities persists. Safe, lockable & well-lit latrines are unavailable. Communities have adopted negative coping mechanisms like sharing a latrine with more households by as many as 20 HHs than in normal conditions, borrowing money to access hygiene items/materials that has left families in dept impacting their daily lives and access to basic services. Supporting Women & girls with MHM materials as well as people with incontinence with hygiene materials/dignity kits is critical. Regarding hygiene in the wake of the COVID pandemic, false & misinformation, lack of access to right information, and limited knowledge on preventive measures has exacerbated the spread. The lack of commodities and increased cost of hygiene items may limit further access.
WASH response will focus on addressing life saving needs of COVID-19/disaster/people with disabilities &conflict affected groups with Rights deficits on lack of/inadequate access to clean & safe drinking water of appropriate quality & quantity, health risks resulting from poor hygiene & sanitation practices . Emergency water trucking will be considered as a life saving option.WASH facilities will be rehabilitated/ constructed in communities & institutions as part of durable solutions in areas where they are scarce. The facilities will adhere to meet community preferences, with gender & disability considerations included to reduce GBV concerns as well ensure leaving no one behind. Community consultative processes will be done to respond to the specific needs &concerns of PLWD. Gender-balanced WASH committees will be supported to maintain facilities. Solar-powered water systems will improve access & reduce costs whilst limiting environmental degradation. Participatory Hygiene promotion approaches will promote knowledge between hygiene & health. WASH installations will use CFW as appropriate to inject cash into communities and markets. Where feasible, restricted CVA (following market assessments) will allow choice of hygiene items to ensure access to preferred MHM/ incontinence items. Emergency guides/ tools for rapid onset crisis (WASH emergency stocks, Sphere, cash toolkit) will be adopted for implementation. The WASH response will coordinate with the WASH cluster to provide comprehensive services using nexus approaches, where appropriate, to promote resilience.
Application of CHS in implementation of WASH interventions ensuring response is appropriate and relevant, effective and timely, strengthens capacities and has DNH considerations, needs addressed are needs assessed that invoked participation of rights holders, provide room for complains and feedback works with other stakeholders in target location and through cluster mechanism to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication.
The project will target Dangorayo, Garowe , and Eyl districts in Puntland state & Daynille, Marka & Afgooye in Lower shabelle region.

Project Activities

According to JMCNA 2020, approximately 8.9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. Of this number Approximately, 4.6 million are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities The project will ensure safer access WASH services for men, women, boys and girls through; Community mobilization sessions, Mobile money transfer to 200HH beneficiaries for purchasing water from the vendors . Two phases for CFW @ 35 USD per HH to procure 7.5 barrels of water per month . The second phase will depend on how HH has spend allocated money to procure water for the first installment . The second phase will be released by HH which has found vouchers which indicated they had sold water from the registered water vendors , rehabilitation of 26 berkards, Rehabilitation/maintenance of 2 strategic borehole in Dangorayo, Rehabilitation of 3 spring wells and construction of 10 shallow wells in Eyl, solarization of two water systems in Garowe. The training of water committees and provision of operation and maintenance tools will also be undertaken in this location. Rehabilitation of water supply system, strengthening water committee and training water technicians in Daynille, Installation of new shallow wells and rehabilitation of old wells in Afgooye, training of hand pump technicians and training on water treatment in Afgooye and Marka. Construction and rehabilitation of the water systems will be done Through CFW modalities. Establishment of 2 scale waste management in IDPS camps by training them and provision of donkey trucker, sanitation tools and one month intensive for labors and Construction of kiosk fence and shed room in IDPS camps through skilled labout by near villages in Garowe On sanitation rehabilitation of 4 twin latrines for health facilities and 4 in schools and rehabilitation/construction of latrines80 latrines including provision of solar lamps will be undertaken in Garowe and Dangorayo. Construction of solid waste management pits will be done in Banadir and Nugaal. Hygiene promotion through distribution of 2000 hygiene /MHM kits will be undertaken through use of vouchers while hygiene messaging will adopt use of mass media including door to door visits. Ensure women, men, girls & boys have enough knowledge to protect themselves against health related risks by: Establish & train hygiene clubs in Garowe school; Men, women, boys and girls including people with disabilities will be consulted on their preferences to encourage use of facilities upon completion

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Evidence has shown that giving people cash is a superior and less expensive way to meet their needs. It provides households with flexibility in meeting their prioritized needs, helps to empower women through having more control over resources and also assist in stimulating local markets and livelihood opportunities. In the WASH programme CVAs will be used in ensuring access of communities to basic needs including meeting WASH outcomes on access to water supply, sanitation and hygiene. CFW interventions are also aimed at reducing dependency and strengthening community’s ability to be self-sufficient to some extent in supporting interventions that enhance their access to services. Cash Transfer Programming will be used as a delivery mechanism using local service providers and will support local supply of financial services. Cash for work activities will promote employment opportunities that will enhance household abilities to meet their basic needs. NCA will scale up and use of its Redrose platform to manage MBP/CVA interventions where possible. Use of mobile transfers will also be considered on case o case basis.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

NCA participated in the Joint market monitoring initiative that has largely informed this response in terms of what activities can be linked to CTP. Internal rapid market surveys have been undertaken to determine commodity prices but this is subject to change with the changing market trends resulting from the impact of COVID 19 and limited access to markets in some area as a result of floods/conflict etc

Mitigation Measures

Joint program planning, implementation is central to this intervention. The involvement of the communities strengthens their ability to manage the project and ensure that needs prioritized locally are responded to. Empowering/capacity development of the different committees will ensure increased management and sustained access to WASH services. Through the partnerships, the different and complementary roles of local authorities, committees and local population will be strengthened. This together with building the capacity of the committees will enhance community development in a manner that communities are responsible of their own needs. At the end of the project, community capacities and coping mechanisms are expected to be strengthened on different fronts, constructed/rehabilitated infrastructure will remain in place, and hopefully, capacities for peace and stability will be enhanced Include gender sensitivity analysis in all assessments; integrate gender sensitive perspectives into NCA's staffing policies and routines; include gender considerations in partner assessments - in terms of both partners' capacity and routines; engage all stakeholders in target intervention areas to ensure women's and girls’ inclusion in the initiative; include women and girls in a meaningful way into NCA and partners activities; establish necessary mechanisms to avoid increased exposure to discrimination or exclusion based on gender. NCA has a good standing with communities where we work. On the basis of this, NCA has a fair understanding of the current sensitivities surrounding ‘conflict prevention’ in Somalia. Recognizing these complexities and sensitivities, NCA has developed risk mitigation strategies that have been utilized to enable project implementation. First, NCA employs national staff and works with partners who understand the local context and are well known and respected by local authorities. NCA national staff can access locations that non-Somali nationals cannot access, hence ensuring continuous project implementation and monitoring even in challenging contexts NCA also works with locally based partners who are located within the communities we serve. Further, NCA also employs remote monitoring systems to follow up and monitor projects where physical access is a challenge. Second is a deliberate approach to project branding: selecting language that will explain the project fairly and accurately, in a way that will be acceptable to stakeholders. Third, is an approach of open, ongoing stakeholder engagement involving NCA/partners, local communities and government. Regular security updates will be sought from International NGO safety organization, NCA and partners’ security teams to ensure that staff are well informed on security situation for necessary precautions and arrangements. NCA also has a complains handling mechanism in place that will give beneficiaries the freedom to raise concerns they may have on the project. DO no Harm considerations are also put in place to ensure that no further harm is targeted on the already vulnerable groups being supported as part of our interventions

Please provide further explanation

The proposed plan will Build the resilience and livelihood of the displaced households this will lead to protection of environment, displaced persons are already engaged in different forms of income generating activities some of which may not be sustainable, for example charcoal burning, firewood collection etc. in order to ensure protection of environment, the displaced households must be supported to develop diversified and sustainable positive livelihoods that will contribute to improved resilience. the project will also construct bunds and terraces to conserve water and soil in the target areas. The project will work closely with the government and community structures. The proposed project will educate the stakeholders and community on DRR and how to integrate disaster risk reduction into livelihoods activities hence a step towards enhancing the capacity of both community and government representative. The training will also strengthen the capacities of the local community to mainstream community managed disaster risk reduction concepts, principles and practices in the planning and implementation of community development efforts The rehabilitated community assets after completion will be managed by the community and supervised/ rehabilitated by the local government. To ensure sustainability, the proposed plan will establish early warning and action centers in the community, the centers will be managed by community DRR committee. the role and responsibility of the community DRR committee will be to sensitize community of possible disaster and help community prepare on how to cope with the disaster. The rehabilitated/constructed communal assets will be handed back to community management committee with supervision of local government to ensure that the assets are accessed by all community groups (women, children and youths). the cash for work beneficiaries will be linked to the private sectors and CSO’s in the regions to access new construction job opportunities. NCA will engage rights holders and stakeholders at all levels and stages of project implementation to ensure their participation. During community mobilization and sensitization, NCA will provide information to the targeted communities about their entitlement /benefits. NCA will conduct consultative meetings with the relevant government officials and work closely with existing community leaders and representative bodies (Village Committees) especially in providing the criteria for beneficiary selection which will be translated into the local language.The project will also ensure gender sensitivity in all aspects of programming through ensuring that girls, boys, men and women have equal opportunities to participate in various platforms and their needs identified and responded to. This will minimize risks related to conflict arising as a result of non-inclusion or discrimination.
