Project Summary

This project aims to address the immediate and acute protection needs of vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority localities of El Fasher, Kebkabiya, El Geneina, Kereinik and Jebel Moon in West and North Darfur.
NP's teams will provide a protective presence in communities under direct threat of violence, implementing crosscutting GP, GBV and CP activities. They will maintain a protective presence at locations at which aid is delivered, and use this presence to facilitate access to other livesaving services through accompaniments and referrals, and awareness raising of ERW/UXO. NP will also support communities’ abilities to protect themselves, building their capacity to identify and respond to threats of violence.
The teams will also contribute to strengthening the protection environment through the ongoing identification and analysis of protection risks and violations. These analyses will be developed with and on behalf of communities under threat, who will be supported to take action to improve their own safety. NP works with protection committees which are representative of whole communities, and also with specially formed Women’s Protection Committees, who focus specifically on the protection risks focused by women.
Analyses and reports will also be shared with humanitarian partners to improve the overall understanding of the protection context and to support advocacy for increased humanitarian assistance for individuals or communities who are prevented from accessing services due to protection concerns. NP’s teams will also identify and report grave violations through the MRM system.
NP’s teams are embedded in the programming areas and are present 24/7 to address protection needs, resulting in community trust and acceptance that helps NP negotiate access to areas, and in doing so advocate and open up access to other organizations. It has the capacity to coordinate the deployment of mobile teams from Elfasher, Genina and Kerenic, that can be immediately deploy to where violent conflict happens or where there is no presence of protection actors to respond. NP’s forward leaning approach to protection means that it will focus on areas which other actors cannot access. Nomadic areas such as Jebel Moon locality and Sortony in Kabkabiya will be prioritized.
In light of the protection and GBV needs, NP will respond to direct physical violence through direct protection services including patrols, protective accompaniment or referral to services, while developing local community structures to address the root causes of (S)GBV in the long term through engagement of men and boys while creating Women/youth Protection Teams in targeted areas. NP will engage authorities, service providers and duty bearers in Survivor Centered Approaches to ensure that dignity, security, confidentiality, privacy, consent and choice are at the forefront of response to case management and handling of SGBV cases
NP’s conflict sensitivity approach ensures that all actors in conflict are included equally in program cycle. Through shuttle diplomacy and continued presence NP will develop capacities in all communities in conflict to ‘humanize the other.’ These interventions reduce the likelihood of local-level conflict and contribute to an improved protection environment.
The project targets a total of around 110,000 direct beneficiaries including women, men, boys and girls inclusive of those with characteristics which make them additionally vulnerable to protection violations.

1 - Needs

In West Darfur’s the humanitarian situation remains precarious, the region has experienced renewed patterns of violence against civilians in Kereneik, Jabal moon and the region’s capital El Geneina. Between January and September 2022, there were reported armed clashes and intercommunal conflicts displacing a total of 93,779 individuals in West Darfur, 14,733 in North Darfur. This resulted to injuries, deaths, destruction of property and displacement of populations with some individuals and households experiencing multiple attacks. NP community protection meetings in Torshana revealed that the nomadic communities fear accessing the market government and humanitarian services and facilities., in Kerenik for fear of harassment and intimidation by residents, whilst IDP communities face challenges accessing farmlands and areas of return due to fear of attacks from armed groups. The prevalence of GBV incidents persists around displacement sites and areas of return within West and North Darfur. Humanitarian assessment reports documented domestic violence, restriction of movement for girls and women, and sexual and physical violence targeted at women and girls in displacement sites when accessing farms and grass collection areas around the camps. NP community protection meetings held in Galala, revealed the same with women reporting Inter-communal clashes and the presence of armed groups increase the prevalence of GBV and the state of insecurity for IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable communities. Among some of the protection challenges identified is limited access to CMR for GBV survivors, and stigma attached to SGBV, an inadequate response and access to justice decrease the likelihood of survivors speaking up and reporting violations committed against them. Most of the hard-to-reach areas lack adequate GBV referral facilities , forcing them to take long trips to access primary health services and specialized care for survivors of GBV. In North Darfur, most communities have most communities have to travel to El Fasher while in West Darfur communities report travelling to El Geneina. The selected localities Kabkabiya, El fasher in North Darfur and Geneina, kereinik & Jebel Moon in West Darfur, have faced serious security and protection challenges mentioned above. General Protection: Throughout NP's assessments community leaders and members stated that both the nomadic community and farmer, IDP communities face security challenges when leaving their immediate areas. The process leading to signing of the local peace agreements was not participatory and lacking in adequate representation of local communities. GBV: Current agricultural season poses risks to women and girls carrying out their daily work, such as collecting firewood and fodder in unsafe areas around camps. Women and girls also reported SGBV as one of their main concerns in the 96 IDP gathering sites in El Geneina, which are often overcrowded and lacking in privacy. Collection sites are composed of female-headed households which increases their vulnerability. SGBV often goes underreported in the region because of social stigma and ineffective legal frameworks. Child Protection: NP's assessment In WD that while the school is functional, they are no WASH services. They urged NP to advocate for better WASH services. Child labor is a key protection concern. Children are at high risk of being forcibly recruited into armed groups. Many CFSs have become dysfunctional, and materials looted while previously existing child protection networks are non-existent, consequently there is a gap in responding to child protection concerns Inter-communal conflicts within the region, devastation by floods during the rainy season, and a general sense of insecurity around displacement sites and areas of return further affects the level of access to schools and other learning facilities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

All project activities are designed to protect civilians and reduce violence, and thus inherently prioritize their safety. NP’s principle of ensuring the “primacy of local actors” ensures that the beneficiaries remain the central participants in all of NP’s programming, and promotes their self-protection capacities. Activities such as creating safe spaces for conducting strategic/protective accompaniments for civilians to access authorities or security actors, are prime examples of this methodology. Such an approach prioritizes the dignity of beneficiaries, treating them as partners in implementation. The proposed programming is designed to enable equal and impartial access to services. Vulnerable subsets of the community, such as women having to travel long-distances, older persons or persons with disabilities who are less able to protect themselves, minority ethnic groups who can easily be overlooked in other types of aid are the starting point of specifically tailored activities. NP also engages with community leadership and advocates for the inclusion of the concerns of these groups, or when possible representatives of these groups to be included in leadership structures. The protection mainstreaming component of the program helps to support vulnerable groups in obtaining meaningful access to assistance and services provided by other actors. NP carries out in-depth context analysis and community consultations about the impact and potential effect of our work, prior to and throughout program implementation. Field staff are trained in protection mainstreaming. Furthermore, while NP will be directly involved in ensuring the centrality of protection at the distribution, the Team will also support humanitarian actors in protection mainstreaming, such as identifying and prioritizing vulnerable individuals, analyzing the areas where distribution sites are being set up for protection concerns, participating in negotiations with local authorities about distributions as well as providing technical assistance in the form of training and agreement making for inter-cluster response missions (i.e. helping partners think through the impact of and commitments to hiring casual laborers, introducing protection as a cross-cutting component, identifying the activities and indicators that might put communities at risk, etc.). Trainings will target national implementing staff and volunteers of agencies and local community structures to enhance knowledge and practical capacity in protection mainstreaming. Training curricula includes definitions of protection, international standards, including “do no harm” and principles of humanitarian aid, confidentiality, ensuring the safety and security of beneficiaries in all activities and a planning session for how agencies can improve the way protection is mainstreamed within agencies and local community structures.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NP has institutionalized accountability across all levels, ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources.Engagement with local communities is crucial to a successful programme. NP ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. NP considers it of paramount importance that target people have access to information about their entitlements, have access to NP Code of Conduct and can provide feedback on programs. During any community activities discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the Donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, NP our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organisation with feedback, requests for information, and grievances.NP ensures engagement with local authorities. Technical Agreements are established with relevant government during project set-up. During general protection, GBV & Child Protection interventions the relevant sector ministries will be invited and capacity to ensure feedback mechanism. NP is a member of Core Humanitarian Standards Alliance. A Community Response Mechanism will be established for all locations in order for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during monitoring, community engagement and patrolling, visibility information will be displayed as relevant. The CRM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and government, partners, and NP staff. Core principles of NP’s CRM are confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. NP’s Code of Conduct and a Safeguarding policy both of which explain how stakeholders can provide feedback. service referral mechanisms will be established within all project localities and project team participate in (or facilitate) national and or local protection sectors/clusters as a way to refer unmet needs or for advocacy purposes. Do No Harm and conflict sensitivity approaches are integrated in NP’s work across countries. Child Safeguarding policies, Discrimination Harassment and Sexual Misconduct policies are put in place and maintained across country projects. All NP’s projects are preceded by an extensive process of socialization and awareness raising . Long-term relationship building and living with communities remains key in maintaining respectful and appropriate communication with affected communities. NP has strict guidelines with regards to partnerships, as adherence to principles of non-partisanship and independence are essential for its protection and shuttle diplomacy work.
