Project Summary

Project summary
A large population is Kismayo, Somalia is comprised of vulnerable populations largely Internally Displaced People (IDPs), returnees who previously had fled the area due drought or violent conflicts, as well as poor host communities who have withstood harsh conditions from the weather and incessant conflicts in Somalia. Invariably, women and children have borne the brunt of this realities. This project seeks to alleviate the suffering of this populations by supporting their food security and livelihoods through various activities that will address various aspects of what is ailing the above described populations.
The overall goal of the project is: To contribute to improve the food security and livelihood of the most vulnerable Host Communities (HC), IDPs and returnees in Kismayo.
Beneficiary description:
1. IDP households: Somalia has experienced complex emergency and continued inter/intra and factional warfare since almost over two decades now. Throughout Somalia, an estimated 350,000 of the country's 7,000,000 inhabitants IDPs who, as a result of protracted conflict and insecurity, have experienced impoverishment, often combined with drought & other natural disasters, which induces mass population displacement. Additionally, Somali IDPs have lost most of their assets and sources of livelihood including livestock and land, and have little access to stable employment. Despite a gradual return of IDPs to their locations of origin, violence, food insecurity and water shortages continue to exacerbate the IDP situation.
2. Host households / communities: Kismayo hosts about 30,000 poor host community households. Among these poor host community are households from poor marginalized communities, households who lost their parents, especially their bread winners in armed conflicts, families who’s one or two of their family members have been disabled during conflicts and lived with these disabled family members for so long and a large number of HHs who never owned permanent shelter or land in Kismayo, mostly work as casual worker on the construction of house/ or as loaders in the port and live from hand to mouth in abject poverty which in turn becomes a cycle with no much to provide a good social foundation for their children.
3. Rural communities of pastoralists: The return / repatriation process of refugees from Dadaab in Kenya began in December 2014, after the tripartite agreement between the Government of Somalia, the Government of Kenya, and UNHCR. Since then, a large population returned to Jubaland, especially to Kismayo. It is estimated that over 54,409 individuals (11,624HHs) returned to Jubaland of which 52,148 individuals (11,300 HHs) returned to Kismayo. These returnees have been away from the country for long period of time and have to some extent been disconnected with local context; and most of them have no land in the country. This has exposed them to vulnerability due to lack of means to provide for their food and nutrition needs, shelter and other basic needs.

Project Activities

SO1. To contribute life saving support to the new IDPs by providing them self-selection food vouchers to enable then access food commodities. • 500 HHs (3,500 individuals/vulnerable persons) have improved their access to food by benefitting from food self-selection vouchers amounting $50/HH over the period of 6 months. SO2. To provide livelihood opportunities and build productive capacities for the most vulnerable host communities, IDPs and Returnees by providing them with entrepreneurial business skills training, business starter kits, and start-up grants. • 8 Business committees (40 persons) to be trained and facilitated with business starter kits and grants to start various small businesses. • 30 HH (210 individuals) have 5 live chickens, chicken shelter materials, and build chicken shelter through cash for work each. • The 30HH will also receive chicken food for 3 months SO3. To provide livelihood support to the most vulnerable elderly and disabled host communities and IDPs by providing them monthly cash grant.• Provision of unconditional cash transfer to 200 HHs (1,741 individuals) at the rate of $50 per household for a total period of six months; SO4: To provide livelihood support to most vulnerable women among the IDPs, host communities and returnees.• 50 women from Host and IDPS communities have receive entrepreneurial and business management and have received business starting grants of 300$ SO5. To improve the sanitation and hygiene conditions of vulnerable populations to reduce common hygiene-related diseases • Carry out hygiene promotion education and advocacy activities in the IDP camps and other affected settlements.
