Project Summary

SEMA will provide quality lifesaving secondary and trauma care in: Idleb Surgical Hospital, Wasim Hasino Hospital, and Ikhaa Hospital for patients suffering from conflict and non-conflict related injuries, as well as specialty care for management of chronic diseases, major and minor surgeries, intensive care, emergency services, RH and gynecological services, deliveries both normal and c-section, in addition to pediatric health care services and neonatal health services. SEMA prioritized these facilities due to their established services, high demand in their catchment population, and the urgent need to maintain service given limited donor resources.

Needs

The 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that over 13.1 million people need urgent humanitarian assistance inside Syria with 11.3 million requiring health assistance, 4.6 million nutrition assistance. Attacks targeting health facilities have resulted in fewer functioning facilities and healthcare workers, as many have been killed or fled the country. Women and girls, people with disabilities, older people, people with chronic diseases, children and infants, and pregnant and lactating women, suffer disproportionately from lack of access to quality healthcare, as do those in need of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Civil society organizations have stepped up inside Syria to address these problems, however, many lack the organizational and technical expertise to respond effectively.Given that each one of the project facilities is operating in a high need area, with a significant impact on enhancing the overall access to quality trauma, secondary, RH, and neonatal care services.To be more specific, all three locations (Idleb city - Kafr Takharim - Atmeh) are considered a destination for IDPs, with a dense population and a high need for health services. For instance, according to SEMA's most recent needs assessment the population of Idleb city is over 700,000 people, Kafr Takharim has also a population of 77,000 people, because it resides near the Syrian Turkish borders, just like Atmeh which has a population of 200,000 people, with a high density of IDPs and camps. All the mentioned communities suffer from a very difficult living situation with very limited resources of income, due to eight years of continuous conflict. More specific points that emphasize each hospital importance:Idleb Surgical Hospital: One of the biggest hospitals in NWS, and the biggest one in Idleb city. The hospital provides a wide variety of specialized health care services, in which some of these services are not or rarely available in the NWS (even before the crisis). That makes the hospital a destination for referrals from all surrounding countryside. Giving that the hospital serves almost 9,000 beneficiaries per month with high-quality SHC services, and the importance of having such specialized hospital in Idleb city; SEMA considered the facility to be as the most important health facility supported by SEMA. Wasim Hasino Hospital: The hospital provides high-quality SHC services including major and minor surgeries in a highly populated area with both IDPs and host communities. The area does not have an established facility that provides SHC services free of charge, hence the hospital receives more than 5,000 beneficiaries per month and that emphasizes the importance of the hospital in the intervention area. Ikhaa Hospital: The hospital provides MCH care and neonatal care services in a highly populated area, especially, with IDPs and camps. The importance of the hospital is being the referral destination of deliveries both normal and c-section, for a community with a high number of IDPs and camps, where people are in dire need to such services. The hospital also provides very important awareness-raising sessions on family planning for beneficiaries, and such activities have significant importance on raising the awareness of the targeted community.

Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. SEMA, in general, responds to such issues by upholding individual rights in accordance with the humanitarian principals. In that regard SEMA implements all of its activities, taking into consideration doing no harm to the target communities, and providing all services and activities to beneficiaries regardless of their gender, religion, sect, ethnic group, political or regional affiliation. In addition, SEMA works to improve gender equality in term of recruitment, considers protection mainstreaming in all of its projects, provides PSEA training for all staff, provides self-care sessions for staff in order to help them feel more comfortable when dealing with beneficiaries, and introduces staff on how to refer CP and GBV cases to active protection programmes whether implemented by SEMA or other active organisations. Specifically to this project, SEMA initiated a GBV case management team in Idleb Surgical Hospital to receive referral cases from all departments, especially, the RH department where detecting such cases is rather easy. Also, the staff capacity in Idleb Surgical Hospital was improved in regard to providing integrated health and protection programming; by conducting various training for the hospital staff on how to provide clinical care for GBV survivors and on how to detect and refer potential cases, as well as, protection mainstreaming, CMR, and SOPs for GBV case management. Also, in all the three facilitites SEMA always works on improving access to people with disabilities, and the most vulnerable groups. In addition, to upholding gender equality and humanitarian principles.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SEMA has its own procedures and strategy which aim to prevent humanitarian assistance diversion to members of the armed groups and maintain the humanitarian principles by using the security triangle (acceptance, protection and deterrence). SEMA is committed to “JOP protocol for engagement with conflict parties”. Also, all major stakeholders identified through stakeholder analysis who potentially can affect the project's implementation are consulted regularly and appropriate rapport/ relationship is maintained. SEMA has experience of safely delivering services in high-risk areas. Where risk Assessments are carried out for all operational areas to inform program design and allow appropriate budgeting to support mitigation measures. Over the past period, SEMA has worked on training and building the capacity of the staff in Liaison and Humanitarian Access Department, and a plan for broadening the department is in place. SOPs have been gradually prepared for all key procedures and they have been well understood by staff. These include the prohibition of the presence of firearms and the use of armed convoys. As well as, the Communication protocols including communication trees and incident reporting, in addition to, the securing of sensitive information. Safety management plans are developed for each location that includes the provision of basic safety equipment, the communication and logistic support required; Functioning communications equipment and at least one back-up method are maintained for all offices and operational locations. Contact with staff and all offices are maintained on a day-to-day basis through the use of appropriate communication technology. Training on these has been provided to the most staff. The risks are analyzed, and analyses are appropriately communicated and used to inform the operations department. Clear guidance and measures are in place to protect and secure sensitive and confidential information through both physical and electronic means. In case any emergency incident happens, SEMA staff inside Syria are required to comply with safety and security plan. The organization maintains and analyses an accurate and reliable flow of information relating to the context. In addition to that, all safety and security incidents are reported in writing to the designated authority and partners in a timely manner and followed up appropriately. A plan B for each operated facility was prepared, which is included in a comprehensive contingency plan of all SEMA’s offices and facilities. This includes as well, Hibernation plan, and relocation plan for each facility considering many possibilities might happen. A detailed plan exists of when and how and who will do the relocation if needed.
