Project Summary

The security situation in Libya remains volatile, affecting the main urban centers, with clashes between militia and tribes, absence of rule of law, smuggling and trafficking of all kinds, including migrants. Migrants, IDPs, returnees and host communities, people with special needs including people with mental health problems and rural communities are in severe need of healthcare services. Even those who enjoy some access to health services are not having the able opportunity to avail from a complete package of services in an integrated manner; one main shortcoming is the shortage of medical equipment and medicines. The proposed project will improve access to lifesaving and essential primary health care services among the vulnerable migrants, IDPs, returnees and host communities in Libya.

Needs

The country health system is fragile and fragmented, and the protracted crises hads weakened the technical and operational capacities of the health care delivery across the country. The health system that is not able to work at the barehas minimum capacity resultings in lack of access to critical primary and secondary medical healthcare. This significantly impacts the health of thepopulations people across Libya, with the most vulnerable population affected most severely at the extreme receiving end. Migrants, displaced persons, refugees, vulnerable groups, people with special needs including people with mental health problems and rural communities are in severe need of healthcare services. Even those who enjoy some access to health services are not having theable opportunity to avail from a complete package of services in an integrated manner; one main shortcoming is the shortage of medical equipment and medicines. The Service Availability and Readiness Assessment (SARA 2) revealed that, 17.5 per cent of hospitals, 20 per cent of Primary Healthcare (PHC) facilities, and 18 per cent of specialized health facilities have been partially damaged or completely destroyed. Only 41 per cent of hospitals have a comprehensive set of essential medicines available, while levels of availability at PHC facilities and medical stores are extremely low at 10 per cent and 13 per cent respectively. Antenatal care (ANC) availability and readiness is limited at a national level due to a lack of medicines, guidelines and trained staff. There are gaps in facilities that provide delivery services, particularly in Sirte, Wadi Al Haya and Ghat. Only 18 facilities offer family planning services in the entire country, and only eight offer counselling and testing for HIV. Overall the prevalence of sexually transmitted infections in Libya is unknown, and only 8 out of 22 districts have STI services available with only six PHC facilities and nine hospitals offering diagnosis and treatments. Overall, the capacity of the health workforce is in the public sector very limited. For example, facilities able to provide specialized healthcare services across the country have become particularly scarce and overstretched, primarily due to a shortage of specialised medical doctors. The lack of access to healthcare services can be a major challenge for women, who overall suffer from chronic diseases more than men and face greater challenges in accessing specialised services. For example, sSexual and reproductive health (SRH) services are weak across the country and only 4 per cent of all public health facilities are able to offer a diagnosis of cervical cancer. The availability of preventative and curative services for children under five is limited. Over one-third of municipalities cannot provide child healthcare to their constituents, and even where PHC facilities do offer services the package is very limited. People with disabilities, elderly people and people with chronic illnesses are among the most severely impacted by the deterioration of the public sector and resulting lack of access public healthcare, due to stark reality that the services available to them are grossly insufficient to match their critical needs. Moreover, spread of communicable and vaccine preventable diseases and at the same time the rising burden of non-communicable diseases (NCD) is one of the major challenges faced by most of the districts and municipalities. Weak disease surveillance, limited rapid response capacity and fragmented health system paint a grim picture on universal health coverage indicators in Libya. The country is currently facing an outbreak of Measles and Rubella. Spread of Tuberculosis and Leishmaniosis has been widely seen in some districts and municipalities. Migrants are held in extremely precarious conditions in detention. Sanitary facilities are insufficient, nutritious food is unavailable, and the lack of access to health create undue suffering among detainees.

Activities

1. Improve access to lifesaving and essential primary health care services through mobile outreach services and the provision of medical equipment and supplies to targeted primary and secondary health facilities providing services to vulnerable migrants, IDPs, returnees and host communities. The health care services to be provided will include the following: 1) Emergency and general clinical services; 2) Child health, including management of malnutrition; 3) Health Surveillance and management of communicable diseases; 4) Sexual and reproductive health, including maternal and new-born health, management of sexual violence and 5) Management of non-communicable diseases and referrals for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).2. Organize capacity building trainings for health care workers on the complete package of primary health care including case management of communicable diseases and specialized emergency medical services through deployment of the emergency medical teams. The emergency teams will also provide supplementary capacity to existing health workforce in the facilities enhance availability and quality of services3. Support the ongoing disease surveillance system through strengthening of the existing Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN) and Rapid Response Network (RRN) under the coordination of Emergency Operations Center (EOC). IOM will ensure early detection and response to disease outbreaks in the detention centers.4. Assist in establishing a functional referral system and linkages at different level of health care system including community, primary health care centers and secondary hospitals. IOM will support and manage the referral and transfer between the mobile outreach clinics in the IDP camps, in migrant-dense urban settings, and in detention, at reception facilities and nearest primary health care centres; and when needed between the primary health care centres and hospitals.5. Strengthen coordination mechanism among partners delivering health services to vulnerable and stranded migrants, IDPs, returnees, refugees, asylum seekers and other vulnerable populations throughout Libya. In its role as co-chair of the Migration Health Sub-Working Group (MH-SWG), IOM in collaboration with MOH will facilitate coordination of partners’ activities (eg. capacity building, service provision, referral mechanisms) to in order to avoid duplication and promote complementarity and synergies.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Indicate any study/assessment that has been used, if any

Protection needs are amongst the most commonly reported needs in Libya for all people affected by the crisis, whether Libyan or foreign nationals. Overarchingly, protection needs are primarily driven by exposure to conflict and violence, human rights abuses, contamination from explosive hazards in urban centres, and major challenges related to impediments to access critical services and essential goods and commodities. Militias and armed groups, often with linked to competing governments, carry out arbitrary arrest and detention, torture, unlawful killings, indiscriminate attacks, disappearances, seizure of property and forced displacement. In some instances, such as the siege on Benghazi, armed groups have also imposed restrictions on movements, trapping civilians in hostile and unsafe environments without access to basic necessities. (Human Rights Watch country analysis) In addition, IOM will utilize DTM’s Mobility Tracking with protection enhanced indicators to identify specific protection risks and needs of vulnerable groups. The findings from DTM will be considered throughout the project cycle including beneficiary selection, service modality selection, and delivery of assistance. The project will pay special attention to the most in need among the vulnerable population including unaccompanied migrant children, victims of trafficking, and survivors of gender-based violence. The project will work in close coordination with the Protection unit to ensure use of already established referral links and pathways between health and protection actors. Whenever cases with protection concerns are identified by the teams providing basic services, they will be referred to IOM’s Protection Team for further assistance or referral.
