Project Summary

The project aims at supporting the basic and emergency food needs of the vulnerable IDPs,
returnees, and HCs. In addition, the project will target newly displaced people. The project comprises the following components:
a) Distribution of regular Food Baskets to 3000 HHs over 8 rounds for Vulnerable host communities and IDPs residing in the community for more than 3 months.
b) Distribution of Ready to Eat Rations (RTERs) to 15000 newly IDP/returnee HHS
c) Distribution of cooked meals to 3,000 IDP individuals as a response of spike within 72 hours.
d) Distribution of 1,000 MT of flour to bakeries to produce bread at a subsidized price.
e) Restricted Food Vouchers 3000 with a value of 55 USD (Based on Food SMEB) over 8 rounds

4 - Needs

Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Food security exists when all people at all times have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food to meet their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life.” Some 70 per cent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 per cent of their expenditure. More than 37 per cent of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 per cent of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2020 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through life-saving, life-sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficits of the most vulnerable populations as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria. The agriculture sector accounts for up to 30 per cent of Syria’s GDP, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and resilience of Syrians. Source: HRP - FSS 2020
