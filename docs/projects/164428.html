Project Summary

This intervention takes a participatory, community-based approach to recovery programming, ensuring market-sensitive livelihoods support is provided to vulnerable
Syrian households. Increased income, assets and consumption hold the key to promoting an increase in individual economic well-being. Social well-being stems from human capital and positive social relations.
The provision of vocational training and on-the-job training or apprenticeships will be provided to youth and women, who will be offered a selection of enterprise and employment pathways. For those seeking to set up their own small/micro enterprise, a further package of business skills training and business grant will be provided based on their business proposition. Other vocational training graduates will be supported to seek employment with small-medium businesses within the community. All skills training will be identified based on a rigorous assessment of the labour market, identifying local sectors of economic growth or gaps in skills. Small and micro-business owners before the crisis whose business has been negatively affected by the conflict will be supported to relaunch their businesses through a combination of skills, business grants & coaching. Business trainings will be provided, particularly for those new to the labour market such as youth, women & people with functional limitations. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities will be ensured through community participation in selection of types of businesses needed in the community as well as beneficiary selection and monitoring project progress. Cash for Works schemes will be identified to provide a benefit to the entire community.
Inclusion of people with functional limitations will be ensured through community mapping and assessment of their specific needs, awareness-raising on the rights, case management and intensive coaching support. Participation of women will be supported through awareness and advocacy with their families, provision of female trainers and skills training for home-based businesses. Complementary services such as Mine Risk Education (MRE) and Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided based on community needs & vulnerability assessments.
The organization will be also providing technical support to 5 other livelihoods agencies within a consortium, across all livelihood intervention areas to ensuring that the design and implementation of all activities take an inclusive approach and that they provide people with disabilities with equitable access to different livelihood opportunities. This support also includes guidance on the meaningful engagement of older persons and women in activities. The organization will provide technical expertise on the delivery of livelihood activities in government-controlled areas, NWS and in NES with the objective of contributing to the restoration of livelihoods and economic links between groups and increased household income.

4 - Needs

According to the 2019 HNO, 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity and a further 6.5 million are food insecure. Unemployment rates are estimated at 55% by the Food Security Sector, but likely to be even higher in reconciled areas and amongst specific vulnerable groups. Food insecurity is mainly driven by a lack of economic access to food. More than 90% of households report spending over half of their income on food. Trends like hyperinflation, high food prices, and unemployment have left many Syrians unable to afford enough food to cover their needs and resorting to negative coping strategies. In October 2018, WFP and FAO reported that half of all households surveyed reported buying food on credit, especially among vulnerable households, 60% of IDPs, 57% female-headed and 54% leaving in rural areas. Women-headed households, older adults, and persons with disabilities face particular challenges accessing food, often stemming from protection risks, as threats to physical safety, or due to a lack of mobility. The situation is likely to further deteriorate and exacerbated harmful coping strategies taking in into consideration the 2019, continuing lower purchasing power, inflation, petrol shortages and declining incomes and the possibility of a large number of people return from abroad. A recent study conducted by UNDP estimates that a mere 51% of the pre-crisis workforce is now available inside Syria. A study confirmed by the Syrian Economic Sciences Society which estimated that about 830,000 skilled workers are needed in the six governorates of western Syria across several sectors: agriculture, industry, construction, trade, etc.. Meetings with job seekers indicated that they do not have the required educational and technical skills. Almost all male and female youth job seekers (95%) interviewed expressed their desire to access training in order to improve their skills to meet market needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1. Cash-for-Work (CFW) participants will be the mostly vulnerable households including at least 25% of women and 15% of people with functional limitations (15%) will be ensured. After discussion with the community cash for work schemes may lead to small infrastructure rehabilitation projects (implemented through cash for work) that might include water infrastructure, including irrigation systems and accessibility of marketplaces1.3 Provision of technical & vocational skills, life skills and business skills training will be offered to eligible applicants. Technical/ vocational skills trainings will be based on the LMA findings of sectors of economic growth and skills in demand in the local market. Vocational skills training (VT) will be certified where feasible, and include an apprenticeship or on-the- job training and establishing links to local employment opportunities. Young people and women will be prioritized for VT & apprenticeships. Life skills training will be provided for target groups who entering the work market, such as young people, women and people with functional limitations. Beneficiaries graduated from the VT and/or apprenticeship will have the opportunity to apply for the micro-business grant scheme.1.4 Labour Market Assessments (LMAs) will be conducted in each selected community in order to establish local-level economic growth sectors, new economic opportunities, and skills in greatest demand. This data will feed into a response options analysis for livelihoods programme design in line with the needs of the local labor market. LMAs will also identify specific barriers faced by women, men, young & older people and people with functional limitations in accessing employment (including self-employment) and make recommendations for addressing them. Provision of business skills training, grants & coaching for small & micro business recovery for re-establishing businesses will be offered to eligible applicants.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Loss of access to intervention areas: Livelihood programming will be prioritized in relatively stable areas, in communities with severity raking 3 or below. The organization continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects operations. The strategy includes the possibility of suspension/relocation of activities to safer areas. It also includes adapting the team’s work modalities to remote management of activities in the field or by implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and may be able to retain access for longer.- Acceptance of local community and authorities is maintained. the organization teams and partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community; A stakeholder analysis can be conducted and community leaders and local authorities are consulted from the project assessment and design phase to gain buy-in. - Unavailability of service providers: Market assessments are conducted to identify the potential service providers for livelihood interventions; identify and make arrangements with cash transfer agents or traders or Hawalas for cash delivery; identify potential TEVT and business service provider and build their capacity; capacity building of local partners to ensure delivery of services to the targeted participants.Intended beneficiaries are psycho-socially not ready to participate in productive activities. Potential community people that require psychosocial support can be identified during the vulnerability assessments; Basic PSS activities such as Psychological First Aid, group PSS sessions to be integrated into livelihood activities; Mental Health Service mapping if relevant to the area for referrals, Additional targeted PSS support may be provided to beneficiaries most in need.
