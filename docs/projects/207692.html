Project Summary

Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) provides a comprehensive legal aid approach designed to support individuals in navigating complex legal, administrative, and civil issues, every step of the way. It focuses on providing personalized assistance through tailored counselling and legal assistance. NRC will be implementing activities across 25 camps and 8 host community unions ensuring protection and gender mainstreaming. ICLA will work with the CiCs, UNHCR and other actors in camps to secure identity and documentation of Rohingya refugees through existing registration and documentation guidelines. Furthermore, through mobile clinics, ICLA provides its HLP services throughout all the camps. NRC will also work with Ukhiya and Teknaf local government authorities e.g. UNO office, UP office to host communities.
The project is designed to positively increase access to identity documentation including Family Attestation Certificate, Smart Card, birth, death, marriage, and divorce certificates, required for accessing essential services, and HLP including tenancy rights in the refugee camps and host communities, whilst also mitigating and addressing tensions between the Rohingya and local communities generated by HLP disputes. ICLA Bangladesh operates across several thematic areas, including access to legal identity documentation (LID) and Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights, addressing tenancy rights, rental disputes, evictions, ownership issues, and other HLP-related concerns. The approach involves assessment of each case, developing tailored plans that may include legal representation, advocacy, or mediation, and maintaining records to ensure effectiveness of support. ICLA case management stands out for its emphasis on empowering individuals with the knowledge and resources to address their specific barriers, promoting both immediate resolution and long-term resilience.
This objective will be achieved through the increased capacity of refugees and host communities to exercise their identity and HLP rights and improved access to collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms in communities. NRC will provide legal counselling and assistance based on individual needs to enable target beneficiaries to overcome legal, procedural, and documentation challenges, including referral to specialized case management actors as it may be required; combined with information dissemination and awareness-raising initiatives to empower target beneficiaries to claim their rights and access available justice mechanisms. Improvement of the relationships between refugees and host communities, and within the Rohingya community itself, will be achieved through the local leaders` capacity building in HLP dispute resolution.
These supports contributing to project participants’ access to civil justice and other forms of humanitarian assistance. For both civil documentation and HLP, ICLA’s using general information sessions to raise community awareness of rights and to generate interest and awareness of the need for civil documentation and tenure security. Information sessions typically last 45-60 minutes and are open to all camp residents and are also conducted for vulnerable host community members. To address the widespread issue of refugees paying mahjis and others to complete their written applications, ICLA will supporting by the field staff to review refugee applications, assist beneficiaries in gathering supporting paperwork and witnesses, completing the written application, and submitting the completed package to the CiC office. ICLA follows a step-by-step approach, extending beyond application submissions to the CiC. This includes information dissemination, counselling, hospital visits for birth registrations, and intricate advocacy for necessary permissions and validations for identity documentation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The proposed response is informed by analysis of needs and gaps identified in NRC`s Information, Counselling, Legal Assistance (ICLA) Programme`s field experience. Overall findings on identity documentation and HLP in the Refugee camps and host communities: Both refugee and host communities, women in particular, are not aware of the procedures for identity documents and the potential benefits of each document; Vital events (marriage, divorce, death, birth) registration practices vary between camps, with CiCs having varying capacities and understandings of this aspect of their roles.There is a lack of knowledge about how to obtain, correct, amend the “Smart Cards”, Family Attestation Cards, WFP Scope cards, and register vital events; Due to the low literacy level in general, and in Bangla language in particular, especially amongst women and elderly persons, most Rohingya are unable to complete the applications to CiC required to register marriage or divorce, or to amend the “Smart Card”, Family Attestation Card and WFP Scope Card. Local people living in rural / remote areas are having limited access to citizen services, including availing NID, marriage registration and inclusion in the updated voter list; With the resumption of birth registration for Bangladeshis living in Cox`s Bazar District, there is a lack of detailed information about how to apply for the birth certificate and/or how to check the birth certificate in the online Birth Registration Information System (BRIS), as well as on how to correct errors in the birth certificate; Rohingya face significant barriers to accessing formal justice system; There is an overall high level of reliance on informal justice. At the same time the quality of informal justice not aligning with basic human rights and principles, women’s interests not represented, lack of female representation in informal justice bodies; there are risk of corruption and power abuse; Many refugees, particularly those residing in the Teknaf camps, are engaged in tenancy relationships with host community landlords and face rent extortion and threats of forced eviction; On many occasions the land rented out to refugees and I/NGOs by local population is classified as a forest land, which is prohibited under the national legal framework for any form of settlement and development; Already existing tensions between Rohingya refugees and the host community have further increased because of the reduced means of livelihoods, as well as narrative that refugees are receiving most of the humanitarian aid. In the targeted areas ICLA will be using protection-sensitive vulnerability targeting. The individuals with highest risk of exposure to harm, limited access to services, and lowest ability to cope are among following categories: single women headed households, youth (female in particular), elderly and people with disabilities and limited mobility, and LGBTQI+ persons. As the finding of JMSNA and community consultation on adjustment to assistance in 2023, confirms elderlies, women and people with disabilities are identified as the most affected groups. Most of the households specially, women and those who have no income have to sell kinds to pay the rent monthly due to the fear of eviction from the landlord. As most of the Rohingya have no income so paying rent is extra burden for them. On the other hand, as they have no written document with the landlord, whenever, the Rohingya community extend any kind of portion, they have to pay money for that individual portion. NRC ICLA advocating the issue with the protection sector and CICs with camps. Though the formal agreement not possible between refugee and host community, NRC ICLA will continue informal lease agreement. Meanwhile, focusing on empowering the existing dispute resolution mechanism through collaborative dispute resolution training.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC work closely with the CiC office, site management, protection sector and protection focal points so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. NRC prioritizes on evidence-based programming, utilizing sector-related data collection, analysis, and advocacy to inform its programmatic actions. Also, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable the organization to understand the protection risks, needs and gaps in areas where it works. ICLA coordinates with protection sector, and its activities are reflected in the sectors information portal 4/5W. NRC is an advocate for integrated programming, already integrated programmes across its sectors (WASH, Shelter, education, and protection). Furthermore, NRC has been helping other sector (SCCCM, SMSD) mainstream HLP in their programmes through capacity building and technical support including conducting HLP due diligence for all constructions, land related interventions. Also, ICLA team coordinate with PERU teams in camps during any emergency to support on the registration, documentation, and other related services. To strengthen social cohesion, and peaceful co-existence, NRC dedicates up to 25% of its assistance to vulnerable host community members in compliance with operational FD-7 authorizations. NRC understands the importance of predictability, and continuity of its operations, and is fundraising for multiyear programmes. NRC’s current institutional donors have already expressed interest to continue funding, and managed to attract multiyear funding from FCDO which will continue until 2025. NRC is committed to ongoing fundraising efforts and engagement with funding partners to ensure the continuity of ICLA critical services. In addition to this, NRC has an exit strategy in place that includes transfer of materials, knowledge, and skill to local civil society organizations.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection Mainstreaming principles as it called in NRC safe and inclusive programming (SIP), NRC has the institutional capacity to ensure a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment throughout its program cycle starting from the design of project set-up to implementation and outcome monitoring. NRC will prioritize persons with disabilities, female learners, and gender-diverse groups, with a special emphasis on encouraging the participation of girls . The organization places a strong emphasis on protection, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, and accountability, with a specific focus on the most vulnerable populations.NRC regional Safe and inclusive advisor already trained ICLA staff training of trainers (ToT) in order to cascade the same level of knowledge and skills to all the staff, NRC designed tools and tipsheets with protection mainstreaming principles that will be used for assessment to identify the gaps, principles to be complied during implementation phase and sample questioner for outcome monitoring exercises, at the same time SiP action plan for the year 2024 will reinforce compliance with aforementioned four principles. in addition to this NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff and will engage the PSEA Network to train new camp volunteers at the beginning of each project. Furthermore, NRC has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) aimed at encouraging beneficiary feedback on interventions and modalities employed. Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (hotline for complaints and feedback) will be constantly upgraded in line with the NRC CFM Policy and the NRC CFM Handbook.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Context in Cox’s Bazar poses specific risks for women, girls, elderlies, persons with disabilities (PWD) and gender-diverse populations, who are facing more barriers in accessing services. Addressing these challenges requires meaningful participation of project participants. NRC understands the gender impacts, different needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls. To address the Legal Identity and HLP needs of the affected communities according to the age disaggregation, gender and disability marker, NRC targeted the individuals with a range of vulnerability criteria. These includes adult women, 18-59 years, Adult men, 18-59 years, and elderly men and women above 59 years, diverse gender individuals and differently abled persons. Although, ICLA mainly targets the men and women at adult range, children, girls and boys under 18 years old will be directly benefiting from our HLP and legal identity documents services as well, through securing the housing option and obtaining documents. Furthermore, Through integrated programming, ICLA provide basic CDR training for youth to reinforce the community led solution is present in community for resolving the disputes. Young men and women, as Volunteers from host and refugee community are engaged in implementation of activities to ensure equitable outreach and identification of gender barriers in different stages of project cycle management, acting as an enabling factor to highlight their role in finding solution within the community. NRC has child safeguarding and PSEA policy and staff and volunteers are trained, and refresher session will be organized prior to inception of interventions. ICLA use WGQ in its assessment and as an active member of referral pathway, ensures needs and vulnerability of elderlies, women, boys and girls, PWD is well captured and responded. This comprehensive approach underscores NRC's dedication to addressing barriers and promoting inclusive and equitable access to available services.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

NRC considers the input of beneficiaries (men, women, boys, girls) in all phases of the programming cycle and makes deliberate efforts to incorporate their views, concerns, and feedback throughout all programming. NRC accountability strategy for project implementation is based on transparency, community engagement, learning, evaluation, and feedback mechanisms. The following will be the key activities to assure beneficiary accountability during project implementation: a. Regularly conduct community and partner meetings to share basic project information about the project objectives; b. Community representatives and volunteers will be involved in project participants' selection, targeting, verification and distribution process; c. Strengthen feedback and complaints mechanisms across all projects and capitalize on the strengths of existing mechanisms. Moreover, by conducting regular outcome monitoring and project participants’ satisfaction surveys, feedback of community is utilized for feeding the program to make the necessary changes for steady improvement of response.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff and is engaged with the PSEA network for regular training for the staff and new camp volunteers at the beginning of each project. NRC has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) designed to encourage project participants’ feedback on interventions and modalities. The CFM also addresses reported cases of sexual abuse and PSEA, supported by a protection referral pathway to ensure appropriate case management. PSEA, is one of the key elements for NRC way training that regular staffs, volunteers and partners are sensitized. To effectively mainstream protection, NRC recognizes the importance of community engagement. Thus, communities will be empowered and capacitated to actively participate in ensuring that project participants have access to ICLA services in safe and secure environments in received services in a dignified manner. This holistic approach underscores NRC's commitment to creating protective spaces and fostering active involvement from the communities it serves.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC actively participates in the Disaster Risk Management Working Group (DRMWG) and remains committed to being an engaged contributor to interagency disaster preparedness efforts and emergency response initiatives, including protection emergency response unit (PERU). NRC's Emergency response team will play a key role in providing capacity-building training to both staff and volunteers, enhancing their preparedness and response capabilities. Furthermore, Environmental considerations are integral to NRC's approach, emphasizing the principles of reduce, reuse, and recycle. By incorporating these principles, NRC seeks to minimize its environmental footprint and contribute to sustainability in disaster response efforts such as using digital intake forms, employing environmentally friendly practices during implementation. NRC ICLA will be an active participant within inter-agency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone responses and in addressing any other challenges like fire incident. ICLA team will coordinate with PERU teams in camps during any emergency to support on the registration, documentation, and other related services. NRC's multifaceted strategy in disaster risk management underscores its dedication to collaborative efforts, environmental responsibility.
