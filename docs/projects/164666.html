Project Summary

The project aims to support the livelihoods and development of vulnerable crises- affected communities, while focusing on female and male youth by conducting early recovery and livelihoods activities under the following four components:
First, supporting Local Spatial Planning, Implementation and Livelihoods through forming community committees in three locations in Aleppo and Idleb, where these committees will be provided training and technical support on mapping of needs, project and spatial planning, resource mobilization and implementation of projects to maintain and develop and rehabilitate livelihoods related sites and infrastructure. Moreover, community prioritized sites for rehabilitation and support (two community initiatives per location) will be funded by POINT to be conducted.
Second, livelihoods and economy related Infrastructure Rehabilitation, where POINT will conduct further assessment to identify sites that needs rehabilitation and have a positive impact on the economy and livelihoods and will conduct a total of eight rehabilitation projects, these two components will reach directly the population of Dana in Idleb, Jarablus and Daret Azza of 420 thousand people who were affected by the ongoing war.
Third, Vocational and skills training to youth to cover skill gaps in targeted communities, after conducting needed labor market assessments, POINT will identify skill gaps to conduct vocational and skills training to 1200 female and male youth.
Fourth and last, Small-micro enterprise financial support - Livelihood Grant Support, where POINT will provide technical and financial support (though in-kind and cash grants to 400 small-micro enterprises) to support local markets. By conducting this holistic and inclusive support and including female, male and people with disabilities and injures, POINT aims to generate livelihoods opportunities and revive local markets and develop targeted communities in Northern and western rural Aleppo areas and in Northern Idleb area.
Beside the above mentioned activities, Point will be focusing on the rehabilitation of key access roads to the most-in-need IDPs sites and on the reparation of damaged drainage infrastructures outside camps that are essential to prevent calamities from threatening IDPs lives, by providing most vulnerable male and female IDPs, including destitute IDPs and PwDs, with enhanced access to livelihood opportunities against negative coping mechanisms, such as the exploitation of child labor, the project will improve access of humanitarian assistance to camps, improve movement and mobility for livelihoods opportunities and benefiting from other funds programming, in addition to income for vulnerable groups and protection cases that suffer from bad economic conditions. As such, the project will address the needs reported in section 1. and identified by recent, post- flood, vulnerability assessments as well as by the referred assessments on the living conditions of IDPs and host communities' members with a focus on the severity of the economic situation in which they find themselves.
• POINT will rehabilitate and reparation of the access routes to IDPs camps in strict coordination with CCCM partners to ensure complementarity with the rehabilitation of roads inside the camp
• POINT will work on reparation and rehabilitation of drainage systems outside the camps and adjacent to host communities in coordination with WASH partners to ensure inside-camps works can benefit from outside work rehabilitation and complementarity with other WASH intervention at the community level
• The identification of vulnerable HHs with a specific focus on severe economic vulnerability, including female-headed households and PwDs
• The access to employment opportunities through cash for work activities also related to the reparation and rehabilitation of roads and streets and drainage systems and to their ongoing maintenance

4 - Needs

Daret Izza community in Jabal samaan District in Western Rural Aleppo, is scored 4 on the inter sector severity scale (HNO 2019 data), and is home to over 31 thousands people in need. POINT identified the following needs. Lack of community engagement in spatial planning of the village, as the only body responsible for this is the local council, did not enable people of daret Azza to have any voice to community needs and most needed basic services. 35%of the total infrastructure is either partially or completely damaged due to the war, over the course of the past 7 years, a large skill gap has formed especially with the youth, which led to decreasing livelihoods and a low average income (80-140) USD per HH. Moreover, most basic services are lacking, the cleaning of the village public areas and extending to clean drinking water and electricity as the people are depending on their own scarce financial resources to access these services.POINT has conducted an assessment in Jarablus community during 2019, in Jarablus and A’zaz districts in Northern rural Aleppo, as it also scored 4 and 3 respectively on the inter sector severity scale, and collectively has over 37 thousand people in need, POINT identified the following needs: Damaged infrastructure (45%), the unemployment rate reached 45% among male youth and over 80% among females and lack of skills in different vocations. In Idleb, according to the RNA done by REACH and HNAP, 74% of the interviewees reported partial damage to roads/streets as a result of the flood, and 25 of the interviewees (approximately 14%) reported a complete damage. The geographical areas where the highest number of interviewees reported completely damaged roads and access roads are in Dana, Atareb, Ma'arrat An Nu'man and Badama sub-districts. Moreover, with regard to water-related infrastructures, 51% and 35% of the interviewees reported small drains and culvert/piped-culvert partially damaged, respectively.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. supporting Local Spatial Planning/ livelihoods and economy related Infrastructure Rehabilitation: through forming community committees in Western and northern Rural Aleppo and in northern Idleb. those committees will start and lead the initiatives to support the community and POINT will provides financial and logistic support those initiatives could vary as per the communities needs regarding the economical and infrastructure support like (markets rehabilitation, roads, health..) Point will monitor the impact of each activity in terms of community strengthening 2. Vocational and skills training to youth to cover skill gaps in targeted communities” in Jarablus, Daret Izza in Aleppo and Dana in northern Idleb. Under this component, POINT will conduct the following activities to cover skills gaps and increase the livelihoods opportunities in targeted communities, 1- Skills/Vocational trainings like (sewing or electricity, developing CVs,) 2-SME trainings. 3.Job fair to link workers with market 3. Small-micro enterprise financial support - Livelihood Grant Support” in Jarablus, Daret Izza, And Dana in northern Idleb. The project provides start-up grants for the groups who want to start (or re-start) their own business, or those who are skilled and experienced and only need resources to re-start/start a business. Recipients of business grants are selected based on selection criteria developed during the inception phase. Cash grants, or in-kind support are distributed directly by Point field team. Recipients will be monitored and followed up with as their business develops.4. rehabilitation of the access routes to IDPs camps, by rehabilitation 5 km of key access roads to the most-in-need IDPs sites and on the reparation of damaged drainage infrastructures outside camps. The modality will include CFW which will provide employment opportunities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Assumption: selected of beneficiaries have enough capacity to handle computer software that will limit their chances to benefit from project support Risks: beneficiaries not benefiting from the training program or have difficulties handling the online management system Likelihood: Medium, Risk: Medium Mitigation: selection criteria will be developed to ensure all participants are capable of handling basic knowledge of computer software and calculus to avoid this issue, moreover, Field partner staff will be available at the training center to provide technical assistance to beneficiaries at the time of training. Assumption: non-beneficiaries not complaining and influencing project negatively and project information and selection criteria are shared with the community and there is a clear feedback and complaint mechanism in place. Risk: Targeting a limited number of beneficiaries, could create problems in the targeted community between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries Likelihood: Medium, Risk: Medium Mitigation: Sharing the complaint and feedback communication lines with targeted community members and stakeholders. Sharing the selection criteria with the local community before starting with the implementation. Protection risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance grants among beneficiaries. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Point will share the developed selection criteria for participant and the grants mechanism including the proposed projects’ assessment criteria.
