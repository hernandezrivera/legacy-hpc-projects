Project Summary

Health Link South Sudan (HLSS) CCCM aims to reach 51,864 beneficiaries, in to three locations of IDP population sites with the population figures of Bor South 15,697, 7,106 Male, 8,591 Female Magwi 12,329, 3,944 Male, 8385 Female and Melut 33,838, 11,531 Male, 12,307 Female. The CCCM mobile response team will target five (05) formal IDP Sites in Melut these will include (Khor-Adar, Malek, Dingthoma 1 and II, and New Paluch IDPS population sites), eight (08) sites in Jonglei IDP sites particularly in Bor South (Anyidi, Jarweng, Malual Agur-baar, Pariak, Cueiker,Jam, Malau, Banchorot, Liel, Malek and Pan-pandiar) and Magwi county will include Lobone, Katire and Pageri).
HLSS will applied CCCM mobile response approach in all the three sites proposed for CCCM activities during implementation of including setting up and training camp management committees, and introduced community led initiatives and support and hand over the site management to the trained committee, conduct post response monitoring in all the three sites proposed for IDPs.
Conduct sites scooping assessment to profile (PSNs, special groups, life savings needs etc ) and establish information system in coordination with the RRC, Counties Commissioner and IDP leadership. Form site governance structure to conduct stakeholder’s awareness to establish the IDP population size, and enhance effective coordination and mobilization of humanitarian services among others.
HLSS CCCM mobile team shall set up leadership and representative governance structures; at the entire process involve members of the camp population as volunteers in specific tasks/projects; that identify the barriers, skills and capacities for men, women and other groups to support community representatives, Collect information about existing leadership structures in sites
HLSS CCCM team will Identify the information needs of different groups of camp population, that shall be develop a communication strategy to meet the information needs of a camp community, build a formal feedback mechanism; facilitate effective communication between camp population and service providers, in all the sites develop monitoring systems for addressed complaint, develop capacity monitoring tool conduct post capacity building assessment.
There will be awareness creation in all the three proposed sites and will targeted both IDPs, returnees and host communities, as part of the implementation strategy there will be repair and upgrade of facilities/infrastructure; engage community volunteers in hygiene promotion and maintenance with support of community volunteers, ensure that latrines are regularly emptied; Conduct regular camp cleaning exercises; supervise and help fix up broken taps, unblock the sewer systems; mobilize displaced persons in decision-making and daily upkeep of the camp; Maintaining fire breaks for fire prevention; Cleaning toilet and bathing facilities; Education promotion for children.
HLSS CCCM response team will develop a safety audit in in all the project sites to understand protection concern in all the sites and that includes information on related to movements and activities of women and girls outside the camp for water, firewood, market access, employment or livelihoods; Conduct physical Observations, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and FGD with the women’s group, adolescent boys and girls, women, men, youth and church leaders, teachers and other members of the community
HLSS will support the communication with community’s information campaigns, as well as capacity building training to the leaders on the leadership, conflict management, and gender based violence prevention, mine risk reduction, and fire preventive measure put in place, housing, land and properties to the affected population and host communities.

1 - Needs

The situation of South Sudan remain unpredictable for displacement population to return their home following 2013 and 2016 displacement by the arm conflict, hence the current political situation of South Sudan has created fear in to entire civil populations and including the current situation and the lack of political will by the warring parties in implementation of revitalized peace agreement in south Sudan, this situation has created huge challenges and inflicted and created human suffering and this couple with lack of protection services, Sexual Gender base violence (SGBV) being reported, lack of shelter/NFIs and medication, the most affected and the majority are women, children and PSNs across the country. In line with the CCCM cluster strategy to continuity of services for the Internally Displaced Populations (IDPs) living camp life setting, HLSS intends to ensure uninterrupted provision of essential services with support of CCCM mobiles response to save the lives of affected population, including addressing the major issues related to protection of concern in displacement of the population. According to the report provide by OCHA heavy rains have destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities, increased the risk of water-borne diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas, according to UNHCR. South Sudan’s IDP baseline was reset from 1.83 million individuals to 1.47 million after an 18-month-long exercise between OCHA and IOM to update and verify displacement data. According to the Integrated Food Security, Armed clashes were reported in several part of south south and this included Lainya, Central Equatoria; Torit, Eastern Equatoria; Maiwut, Upper Nile; and Tonj, Warrap, with civilian casualties, displacements and suspension of humanitarian activities. Cattle-raiding in Tonj South displaced thousands to Tonj North. An estimated 46,000 people were displaced in Maiwut (33,000) and Tonj South (13,000). During August, seasonal floods have affected more than 364,600 people in different locations including more than 31,600 people in Akobo, Pibor and Uror counties in Jonglei, these situation has made huge number of displace population in various parts of South Sudan and affect most women and children and some have settle in life settlement that require support and created crisis within the humanitarian services (Ref: Bi-weekly Humanitarian situation report, Issue Number 29, Reporting weeks 38 & 39, date 21 Sep-5/October, 2019).
