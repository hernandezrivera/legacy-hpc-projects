Project Summary

Protracted and new displacements of population have resulted in severe protection implications, particularly for women and girls who make up 53% of affected populations. Women and girls continue to be vulnerable to gender-based violence (GBV) and conflict related sexual violence (CRSV). The situation is further exacerbated by occasional conflicts in parts of the country, displacements and weak justice system resulting in the impunity for the perpetrators of GBV. Of the reported GBV cases, 96% of survivors are women and girls whilst 76% are IDPs. Female genital mutilation (FGM) persists with a prevalence of 98%; whilst the prevalence of child marriages remains high with 45% of women aged 20 to 24 married before the age of 18. Women in Somalia experience higher unemployment rates than men: 74% for women and 61% for men.
Young women encounter challenges every day. Apart from sharing their communities’ struggles over limited resources like water, land or jobs, many systematically face discrimination simply by virtue of being female. This includes physical and sexual violence, being kept out of school, disproportionate vulnerability to HIV infection and lack of access to property and other rights. Yet young women around the globe possess the collective power to change their lives, their communities and the world we live in. Just as they face daily challenges, young women are continually developing innovative, effective ways to improve their lives. By bringing together their wisdom and creativity, young women are leading change. Understanding that investing in young women’s leadership is essential to social change now and for the future.
Protection concerns also continue to persist regarding persons living with disabilities in Somalia. They are discriminated against by their families and the public, and usually do not receive any additional protection.
The human rights abuses they suffer include forced marriages, violence, rape and other forms of sexual
violence, forced evictions, lack of assistance and access to essential services.
This project is designed to support the development of young women skills and enable them to provide leadership skills on issues that concern them and further enable them to care, guide and protect the young girls. HAPEN will also target reducing number of cases of violence against women and girls in the project areas. Increase understanding of women on human rights and legal rights and how to take action against violence and discrimination. Focus will also be to support and increase number of women taking up entrepreneurial activities in-order to ensure sustainable livelihood.
The project will also prioritize the efforts to ensure that the voices and rights of women and girls with disabilities are also incorporated in the areas of leadership roles, economic empowerment, access to information as well as equality and non discrimination.

Project Activities

1. Community mobilization processes to inform the local leaders about the project followed by a field visit. This will be followed by awareness campaigns and meetings at the respective villages. During community mobilization phase, Hapen will enforce awareness to the community to emphasize and inform the public and specifically the local leaders of the inclusion of the women, girls,disabled and older persons in the project (15% of the targeted to be disabled women and girls) . 2.Arrange for trainings for the respective groups to include training workshops for local women leaders on human rights and legal rights, gender based violence, creating and building capacities for the available women committees in the villages to prevent and respond to protection concerns. Building capacities encourages cooperation among the communities hence accelerating the process of change and providing power to vulnerable individuals. During these trainings, emphasis will also be highlighted on the participation and representation of the women with disabilities. This is because all women with disabilities can also influence leadership roles, influence policies and advocate for change toward stronger prevention and response mechanism. Access to information and technology and social protection programmes will also be a key highlight in the trainings, this will enable the women and specifically the disabled to live independently and fully participate in society on an equal basis. It is expected that the trainings will enable them realize full and effective opportunities to participate in making the society safer for women,girls,elderly and the disabled. 3. Campaigns to encourage women to take part/participate in protection roles in identifying cases of abuse and coming up with a better community response to violation of women,girls,elderly and the disabled. The initiative will increase women's political participation and their engagement in local governance.4. Awareness to support women established LNGO's and support groups. This would form an entry point for women of different age groups, social economic and political backgrounds that would empower them begin to have voice to prevent and respond to protection concerns. 5. Set up safe spaces that provide regular, structured activities to help Women and Girls who have survive sexual and other form of violation to deal with related stress and negative cooping mechanism, and form and maintain relationships. 6. Awareness on reproductive health. topics to be covered will include: sexual education for young women and girls, awareness on breast cancer, lifestyle modifications and training on emergency/first aid kits. 7. Capacity building on family and personal budgeting. Women will be trained on concepts and new methods for family budgeting. They will be provided with innovative techniques and approaches that they can incorporate in their daily household budgeting. 8. Distribution of equipment and tools. Equipment's and tools in the form of grants will be distributed to women entrepreneurs who undertook the trainings and are willing to start their own business This is considered a way to encourage them expand their business at a lower initial cost. As a follow up action point, Happen will work with the local leaders in-order to try and enforce measures to ensure that cases of discrimination against women with disabilities are taken into consideration and victims receive appropriate actions .Happen will also monitor the project during implementation to ensure that all policies regarding persons with disabilities are included at local/ national levels. A follow-up mission will also be undertaken by project staff after project closure in-order to ascertain project impact to the community.

Mitigation Measures

1.Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services Hapen will ensure that the registration and distribution activities are undertaken at the field level where there is ease of mobility for all. During the awareness creation, communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure set standards of accountability are followed in order to meet the policy of zero tolerance. 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities.Hapen has an established vulnerability criteria methodology that will be utilized to ensure that all vulnerable women groups and girls are identified and targeted. Community awareness/campaign activities will be undertaken to ensure inclusion of the marginalized groups among the women so that they understand their rights in the project. The selected project committees will be representative of all groups within the community (ie. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) in order to ensure that they are diverse and not biased. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Hapen will organize awareness raising sessions so that people understand the means their can use to channel their complains and feedback. A mechanism for submitting feedback will be established. This will not require beneficiaries exposing themselves to project staff. Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Mechanisms for collecting feedback should use a combination of channels. This will include active channels, where client perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups), reactive channels, where clients have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines) and open channels where feedback will be solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. Consideration for a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors will also be undertaken in order to minimize confusion especially when one community is targeted by different partners.
