Project Summary

The aims of this intervention is in line with cluster objectives that is to provide timely equitable drinking, sanitation and hygiene services to the population affected by conflicts, diseases and malnutrition and food insecurity .Provision of WASH lifesaving intervention is very critical in the humanitarian space for IDPS,returnees and host communities.
The second most important objective is to provide the integration between different clusters that will mitigate WASH related diseases and GBV that include improve nutrition level by providing safe water drinking to children admitted with SAM/MAM treatment and provide them with minimum WASH package. This intervention is expected to reach a total of 163,516 affected population in Akobo,Nasir,Nyirol,Juba,Leer,Canal,Maiwut,Longchuk,Ulang,Koch,Uror respectively
The priorities of our intervention include WASH interventions at household and community levels for displaced population/host community in the areas with highest malnutrition rates (GAM) coupled with highest famine risks (IPC 4),WASH interventions at community level for displaced population/host community in the areas at highest risk of protections issues within GBV and WASH interventions at cholera etc. transmission contexts levels for displaced population/host community in the areas at highest risk/rate of cholera outbreaks/WASH-related diseases
Nile Hope will integrate all the four sectors that include WASH and Nutrition training and supervision for treating acute malnutrition, along with distribution of soaps, aqua tabs of minimum package. In OTPs and in the communities, screening for SAM combined with information, education and communication on hygiene promotion and household’s water treatment. Nile Hope WASH and health partners will focus on cholera preparedness and response in these hotpots areas that can contribute to the decrease of the transmission rate in case of cholera outbreak.
Women and girls are affected by WASH needs more than men and boys, as women and girls are the primary water collectors, as well as caretakers in the home. In South Sudan, the primary water collector is a female. Increasing safe water access, and thereby reducing collection time, can increase the time spent on livelihood activities. Where possible WASH interventions will seek to increase the access to basic WASH services taking into account safety considerations of water points and latrines can place women and girls in vulnerable locations.

Needs

According to latest Food Classification Phase report indicates that more than 6. Million people faced Crisis or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season of whom 47000 are already in Catastrophe or IPC phase 5 and .7 million are in emergency phase 4 and these include counties like Leer, Pigi/Canal where Nile Hope is already providing lifesaving intervention and the situation is not expected to improve given the level of food insecurity and malnutrion coupled with poor sanitation and hygiene. WASH services is very critical in improving the health, nutrition and protection and gender of women, children, boys, girls and all the vulnerable groups Lack of improved water sources, poor sanitation and hygiene expose billions of people, particularly children and the vulnerable, to a wide range of preventable diseases and are major contributors to the world’s morbidity and mortality. According to UNICEF and WHO a staggering 2.3 billion people do not have access to basic sanitation and 844 million people lack basic access to drinking water and South Sudan is not exception (WHO & UNICEF, 2017).As a result of this situation, around 5.7 million people will be in need of WASH services and majority women and children example AWD, waterborne. Malnutrition is the root cause of about 35% of all under-5 child deaths globally. It is estimated that 50% of these cases are associated with diarrhea or with repeated intestinal worm infections caused by unsafe drinking water and/or poor sanitation and hygiene (WHO Reports 2018)Diarrhea is an aggravating factor in malnutrition, as it reduces the body’s capacity to absorb nutrients (leaky bucket syndrome). In addition, malnourished children are more likely to contract diarrhea, as their systems are already weak, and the effect is cumulative.Intestinal nematode infections like Ascaris and hookworm infections claim nutrients for themselves so that they are not available to be taken up by the human body. This issue has an impact on the long-term and causes chronic malnutrition. In addition, faecal bacteria ingested in large quantities by young children living in unhygienic conditions can lead to permanent gut damage which leads to nutrient malabsorption and consequently to undernutrition and stunting – a phenomenon known as poor environmental awareness Greatest concern are counties where harvests are likely to be poor; access to humanitarian assistance may be limited for a section of the population; possible returns from within and outside of the country may cause further pressure on already scarce resources; insecurity, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods prevails. As a result, extreme levels of acute food insecurity are expected to persist in many areas. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance such access to equitable and timely provision of this critical lifesaving service The overall situation of acute malnutrition has slightly improved this year as compared to the same period last year with no county reporting extreme critical levels (GAM above 30%) of acute of malnutrition in 2018. A total of 31 counties in the former states of Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei reported ‘Critical’ (GAM (WHZ) 15.0 – 29.9%) levels of acute malnutrition while 20 counties in Lakes, Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile reported ‘Serious’ (GAM (WHZ) 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition. Most of the counties for which county-level data was available in Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria recorded ’Alert’ (GAM (WHZ) 5.0-9.9%) and ‘Acceptable’ (GAM (WHZ), <5.0%) levels of acute malnutrition. The level of acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to improve marginally between October and December 2018 due to the seasonal availability of local production,
