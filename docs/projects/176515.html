Project Summary

South Sudan protracted crises have left 7.5 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 despite the signature of the Revitalized peace agreement, and continues to face conflict, natural disasters and climate instability, food insecurity, and lack of basic services. As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in South Sudan will negatively impact an estimated 5.6 million people in 2021.
Since 2006, SI has become a key WASH actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency lifesaving response and providing integrated WASH services to crisis-affected populations in the areas thanks to its static presence in Juba County, Nyirol County, Fashoda County, Raja and Wau Counties. Since 2012 SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-affected populations all over the country. In 2021, SI will aim at improving timely, equitable and integrated access to WASH services to the conflict, epidemic and natural disaster affected populations in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Access to basic services including safe water and sanitation is limited across the country, especially in Upper Nile and Jonglei states. As per the assessments conducted by SI, there are very limited solutions in terms of water supply, especially in Upper Nile. Underground water presence is limited, leaving to the communities no alternative apart from surface water (80% in Fashoda, 100% in Pigi/Canal). In blocks and villages around Aburoc, 29% of the population did not have enough water for the whole year. In addition, SI has undertaken a hydrogeological study to identify a sustainable water supply solution, especially in Aburoc. The situation remains critical the majority of the dry season. In Nyirol county (Jonglei), the boreholes already targeted by SI in Nyirol for rehabilitation have limited sustainability due to the emergency nature of the repairs, and were strongly affected by 2019 and 2020 floods. The lack of spare parts, tools, and training for communities to conduct basic maintenance and repairs leads to minor issues rapidly becoming more serious and resulting in repeated breakdowns. Lack of maintenance around water points by communities and Water Management Committees (WMCs) leads to unsanitary conditions and possible contamination of the boreholes. Women are rarely members of WMCs, despite the fact that they are the persons primarily responsible for water collection and are most impacted when hand pumps break. The flooding hazards highlights that improvements to make boreholes more resilient to future flooding are required. In Raja county (Western Bahr el Ghazal), the access to safe water is very limited. 25% of households reported by KIs to take more than 30 minutes round-trip to collect water. On top of this risk, 39% of households reported by KIIs needed to access healthcare but were not able to in the six months prior to data collection. Limited access to medical services to the population in health risk can contribute to increased mortality within the county. The access to sanitation in concentrated areas of Fashoda and Nyirol is insufficient, even nil (0%) for the population in Pigi/Canal. The highest reports of open defecation were reported in the villages at 77%. Each communal latrine in Aburoc camp is currently used by 94 persons on average whereas the SPHERE standards recommends 50 users per latrine. KAP surveys conducted by SI found that in some areas of Nyirol county, 97% of households do not have access to latrines. In Thol payam, SI’s IRNA found that more than 75% of people living in the surrounding villages were estimated to use open defecation and 85% of survey respondents reported observing faeces in their community, including 60% near water sources. During the 2020 rainy season in Nyirol, the flooding covered many of the bush areas used for open defecation, spreading faecal contamination to homes and water sources, and forcing many people to defecate directly into the water. Those high rates of open defecation increase the risk of water borne diseases. The lack of hygiene items and services, the distance between homes and water points also increase the risk of GBV. Most people do not treat water especially at household level regardless of the source of collection. Hygiene promotion campaigns focusing on handwashing and hygiene in public areas are also required. The country is also severely prone to epidemic spread due to a critical lack of resources, inappropriate health infrastructures, medical equipment and personnel. Juba County is particularly exposed to COVID-19 disease as it has been the primary centre of the epidemic in addition to being the most congested city in the country Furthermore, the access to WaSH services is also jeopardized as this context can slow down the activities. Many water points have to be rehabilitated, and there is still a considerable sanitation gap with latrine facilities having more than 150 users per stance on average in Juba county.

2 - Project activities

SI 2021 strategy in terms of WASH will focus on three axes: 1) Short-term results to reduce mortality, morbidity and protection threats for the most vulnerable population in areas of WASH severity level 4&5; 2) at a medium term to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of WASH severity level 4&5; 3) building resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses on a longer term basis, and enable people to recover from crisis, seek solution to displacement.In the first axis, SI will implement the following activities:- WASH integrated with nutrition and health: building or rehabilitating WASH infrastructure: in nutrition and health centers- Hygiene promotion (HP) and IPC trainings at nutrition and health facilities, along with follow-up visits- Distribution of hygiene kit for SAM/MAM cases - Semi-permanent access to safe water: WASH NFI kits distribution, Water trucking, SWATs, Water points rehabilitation and chlorination, Communal water treatment- Establishment of early warning systems (epidemiological, WASH severity, IPC)For the second axis:- Access to safe water: Water points rehabilitation and chlorination, water point upgrading to serve more people, Communal water treatment;- Access to sanitation: communal latrines directly or through material provision, communal and school handwashing stations- HP: public sessions, school level, health awareness in communities, distribution of MHM kits.- Along with Protection partner, mitigate GBV impact through WASH Facilities construction or rehabilitation, reduce risk of violence when people access to WASH services after assessment, construction of latrines for the most vulnerable, specific kits distributions and trainings, consideration of MHM needs for girls in the design of latrines in schools.For the third axis, SI will implement the following activities to improve WASH Resilience: - Creation of new water infrastructure in a disaster resistant way: borehole drilling for public areas and institutions, connection of new tap stands to water sources, provide materials and trainings for rain water harvesting, construction of slow-sand water filters – through community consultations to choose the appropriate design and locations for the infrastructure to build/rehabilitate- Upgrading of existing water points for increased water production and disaster resistance in the community: solarization of public boreholes or institutions, upgrading existing parts in public or institutional boreholes;- Rehabilitation of existing water infrastructure: water yards, boreholes, damaged water points in institutions;- Improved waste management: systems setting, distribution and training for communities, waste management system creation in public in health or education institutions- Creation of WMC to maintain and manage water infrastructure. Increase woman and youth participation and empowerment, selection in WMCs, CRM groups etc.- Creation or rehabilitation of existing sanitation infrastructure: change design of latrines for drainable to adapt to urban context, construction of public latrines, CLTS- Community-based HP: design hygiene messaging and spread through Community Hygiene Promoters, and through WMC members- Access to water for irrigation: reservoirs/ponds construction, water runoff usage from boreholes- Drainage of livelihood infrastructures: markets, roads, crop storage areas- Training of pump mechanics as a livelihood opportunity- Design more sustainable approaches and permanent solutions in strategic locations showing an extreme and catastrophic WASH vulnerability, and regularly affected by flooding and/or food insecurity - Experiment and try to implement long term solutions in Aburoc permanent IDP site, such as shallow wells, rain water harvesting – borehole drilling being not possible- Participation to a collective WASH Infrastructures mapping.

3 - Project indicators

SI implements an extensive framework of tools for the monitoring and evaluation of the activities conducted. All the indicators are designed in order to closely monitor the meeting of the project objectives. KII and FGD will be conducted in the targeted areas at different stages of the project in order to assess the distribution modalities and adapt them to the targeted population needs. SI will carry out PDM surveys covering all the distributions during the project, to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness and relevance of input distribution, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues. A baseline Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) is done at the beginning of all the projects, to assess the KAP of the communities in each area and adapt the HP activities and messages to the needs and what the communities would be more sensitive to. An endline survey will also be conducted at the end of the projects to support the final reporting and assess level of indicators achievement, satisfaction of beneficiaries and lessons learnt of the project to be shared with the donor for further projects. FGD on AAP will be implemented at the beginning of each static or EPR intervention and at the end, to adapt activities in between and assess the level of AAP as per the beneficiaries’ perception following AAP framework policies (Annexes 15). SI will apply the following actions and tools to ensure that the objectives are reached and needs covered: Activity Progress Update (APU) tools and MEAL plan; Follow-up of the different assessments and interventions implemented by the project teams; Weekly update to state level Clusters on achievements and challenges; Monthly reporting to the Clusters through 5Ws and GBVIMS reports; Routine field visits by the Coordination teams. At SI coordination level, a program coordinator and a MEAL assistant will monitor the implementation, achievement of indicators, and quality of the program using the APU, and with support from SI HQ.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SI will do a market assessment before any project, tailored to the context and the area. SI can carry out rapid market assessments to complement longer term market assessments to determine if the market is functional enough to absorb cash assistance to vouchers and avoid create shortages in the market or inflation in the areas. This assessment will also try to identify vendors willing to accept the vouchers and the items that can be found on each market. SI is taking part of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative led by REACH every month in several areas of the country.SI will link with market information management systems to monitor the price of items.While implementing a project, SI will promote linkages between buyers and sellers, boosting local economy and business development around WASH.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher assistance in the WASH sector can be very useful for the following reasons:- To develop or reinforce markets (especially in urban areas – Juba and Raja): indeed, injecting cash and having people buying WASH items on the market will trigger traders to buy and sell more WASH items. It produces effects at medium/long term. - Try to disseminate habit for people of ownership and maintenance of WASH facilities. Indeed, making people pay for water points (through water vouchers for instance) and other WASH facilities instead of water trucking/carrier only, will strengthen the water points sustainability. People would be more involved in hygiene prevention and measures if they purchase their soap and water containers themselves. It will increase their autonomy and independence from humanitarian support.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the FGD about AAP with the population of the affected area, SI will start by presenting the principles and mechanisms for which SI are accountable. For the complaints made in terms of PSEA and CP policy, when CRM committees can be created, one man and one woman will be selected by the community as PSEA focal points. They will be considered focal points for the community. At every activity, awareness will be raised with regards to their availability to receive particularly sensitive feedback, especially regarding the behavior of the project staff. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line with the consortia manager and the PSEA focal point of each partner, will be provided. This will ensure, all PSEA and CP policy complaints reach the coordination offices without the program staff on the ground being aware. These complaints will be stored in a separate database compiled at the coordination level of each partner and following specific confidentiality SoPs. Complaints or issues not under the framework of this project will be referred to the other actors or clusters.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Ensuring all different groups of beneficiaries and communities have access to safe complaint and feedback mechanisms is one of the first steps in mainstreaming protection across the activities of the project. Each CRM committee is selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society and particularly women with specific needs, children, People living with Disabilities and individuals who are not literate have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. If the beneficiaries cannot write their feedback, they will be able to reach program staff and particularly community volunteers who will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. Whenever possible, distribution sites complaint boxes and desks will be set up, as well as a phone line in the areas where network is available. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To gather complaints and feedbacks on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), CRM committees will be selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society and particularly women, children and PWD have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. Program staff and particularly community volunteers will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. Additionally, offices and distribution sites complaint boxes and desks will be set up. A hotline is not relevant in the areas as phone network is absent. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism and by the community volunteers, and forward to the coordination at field and Juba levels. Each CRM committee will share on a regular basis directly with the Field Coordinator and SI coordination office the database that compiles all the complaints and feedback received as well as the measures taken at field level to be remediated. The community leaders and RRC will also be involved and sensitized to this topic, especially for very sensitive complaints that could concern staff members and sharing information to be handled with respect to confidentiality. SI provides accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and affected populations over information provision. SI will always include the most vulnerable in its messages (i.e. women, children, elderlies and disabled) and make sure the printed and Information Education and Communication materials are properly transmitted in the local dialect. The same materials will also include information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism so that beneficiaries can access to the mechanism in a safe and confidential way. FGD on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) will be implemented at the beginning and at the end of each intervention and to adapt activities in between and assess the level of AAP as per the beneficiaries’ perception following SI AAP framework policies.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Before the implementation of activities, SI will organize FGDs focused on the identification of beneficiaries’ perceived levels of vulnerability to define the selection criteria. The beneficiaries’ opinions will be crosschecked with findings of the assessments conducted at the beginning of the project. Moreover, SI Program Managers report quality checks based on beneficiarie’s feedback to the Coordination level on a monthly basis. Each project is analysed through this quality check and followed up in the internal monitoring tool (Activity Progress Update). Based on the findings, Coordination and field bases determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project and messages to the needs captured through feedback and complaints. Should any issue raised exceed the project’s scope, SI will advocate this situation to the relevant Clusters (at National, State and County level), local authorities and other humanitarian partners to tackle the issue and ensure onset needs can be covered.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

IPC 3, 4 and 5 situations are a trigger for EPR response. All the activities aim at giving access to basic services to vulnerable populations in these areas. SI will implement WASH activities, and doing that aims at increasing access to water and thus reducing the distance of communities from water points. That can help also people on movement, contribute to bringing access to services to displaced populations as well as in areas of return. SI will implement activities to improving access to water for agriculture and indirectly contributes to reducing food insecurity in areas of IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

SI will prioritize vulnerable households with protection risks, and provide them with integrated, multi-sectoral projects, between WASH and FSL, but also with Protection, Health and nutrition. The selection assessment database will comprise a scoring taking into account the levels of vulnerability, types of vulnerabilities, number of people in the households to select the targeted beneficiaries.For instance, the distribution of dignity kits will depend on vulnerability criteria during selection, answering specific needs of women, children, elderly, persons with disability. The distributions will be done as close as possible to the places where communities live to reduce distance to access humanitarian aid. SI will take into account protection issues for its selection of locations, such as rehabilitation of boreholes.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Access to safe water, latrines and hygiene promotion are three ways of fighting global acute malnutrition through reducing the prevalence of water borne diseases. Acute malnutrition and food security levels are a trigger for EPR response since WASH needs are linked to these stresses. The MAM children under 5 are one of the main vulnerability criteria for distribution (especially for WASH NFI, dignity kits). SI is prioritizing families with malnourished children under 5 for distributions of WASH NFI kits, along with a training on how to use them. By improving the quality of water the child is drinking, reducing risk of contamination due to absence of latrines, soap or bad application of hygiene measures, the level of malnutrition is likely to reduce. SI will also implement WASH activities, through building new WASH facilities or repairing, in nutrition or health centers that welcome global acute malnutrition cases.The main areas where SI is targeting GAM cases are the ones in severity levels 3 and 4, meaning Juba, Wau, Nyirol, Akobo, Pigi/Canal, Maiwut, Melut counties.
