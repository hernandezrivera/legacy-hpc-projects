Project Summary

In order to mitigate the protection-related risks associated with the high level of land contamination by CW, CM and IEDs in Syria, the Organization will implement its integrated two-tiered Humanitarian Mine Action programme:
1/ Risk prevention and local capacity building
the INGO will provide RE for most at-risk populations living and working in highly contaminated areas in Syria. the Organization's strategy is based on a composite intervention methodology, allowing the activity to remain flexible to changes in context and needs. Thus, based on context, access and needs analysis, the RE will be conducted either through direct RE sessions at households’ level or through public information. Direct RE sessions will allow the INGO to reach the most isolated people and to adapt the content and approach of the sessions to the target audience. Public information will raise awareness at a global scale and will be carried out under different modalities, decided according to the context and the resources available locally.
In addition, safety briefing will be proposed to humanitarian workers to spread RE knowledge within the humanitarian community and thus to promote safe behaviours and minimize the risk of accidents.
Also, ToT will be proposed to key members of communities (teachers, community leaders and EO survivors) so they become focal points and empowered to dispense safety messages to reduce their peers' vulnerabilities This activity aims to strengthen the skills of key local actors in order to ensure the appropriation of the messages disseminated and thus the sustainability of the activity.
Finally, the Organization will produce and externally disseminate evidence-based advocacy factsheets so as to mobilize and persuade local, national, regional and international stakeholders and denounce key humanitarian and protection issues for populations and humanitarian actors in Syria.
2/Clearance
The INGO will work towards the identification, marking and the safe disposal of explosive ordnances when safe to remove the immediate threat.
Through this Action, the Organization will also implement clearance activities in Raqqa Governorate, while maintaining the possibility of deploying Clearance teams to Deir Ezzor if the context allows. In this perspective, NTS activities will only be carried out in Deir Ezzor if demining in the area is confirmed so that it does not raise expectations and places people at greater risk.
3/ Victim Assistance
The organization will provide physical rehabilitation, prosthetics and orthotics as well as MHPSS services to EHA survivors and as well as other People with Injuries or Disabilities living in contaminated areas; the organization will also support access to other services through referrals.

Needs

Based on Mine Action Sector inputs for the HRP 2019, it is estimated that 10.2 million Syrians live in 1,980 communities reporting contamination by explosive hazards. These explosive hazards and the threats posed by unexploded ones represent high protection risks for populations in those areas, over 3 million children are directly exposed (HNO 2018). IDPs and returnees are especially vulnerable to explosive ordnances, due to their lack of knowledge in terms of types of devices, extent of contamination and safe behaviors. Likewise, children and adolescents (both male and female) are often unaware or misinformed on the risks posed by explosive ordnances, they do not know the most appropriate safe behaviors to adopt when dealing with them, and are at higher risk than adults due to their playfulness. Through community surveys (CS) and victim reports gathered in 2018, the organization identified direct correlations between the conflict history in an area, victims related to EO and whether victims had received RE prior to the accident or not. Furthermore, the organization noted a high level of risk behaviours among affected communities in the absence of knowledge and understanding of EO-related risks. In October 2018, REACH Initiative conducted a rapid assessment of various needs in North -East of Syria. It reported that 22% of the communities in Deir Ezzor are at risk of being exposed to unexploded ordnance or mines, while 4% are exposed to IEDs, SVBIEDs, and BBIEDs. In North-East Syria, based on 414 victim reports (conduction of reports started in April 2017), the organization found that at the time of the accident, 126 were farming or grazing their livestock, 83 were deliberately playing with the EO, and 10 deliberately moved the item to either bring it to a safer area or try to dispose of it. The remaining 195 victims reported various livelihood activities such as collecting scrap metal, travelling, hunting/fishing and household chores. Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Based on available data, 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. According to HNO 2019, 45% of persons injured - 65% of EHA survivors - are expected to sustain a permanent impairment. A survey done in 2018 by the Physical Rehabilitation and Disability Working Group in Idleb, Western Aleppo and Raqqa found that 33% of the people in these areas had a disability, which is double of the world average based on WHO estimates. In Ar-Raqqa governorate, prevalence rate of having one or more disabled member in the household was reported at 27%. Over half of the respondents with disabilities said that the Syrian conflict had exacerbated their difficulties, showing an increased vulnerability of persons with disabilities in conflict settings. The number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on the health sector, in particular, on the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and psychosocial support on the short and longer term. With a substantial number of victims being reported as heads of households, the entire families' ability to provide for their own can be compromised, leaving them in even more precarious situations. In addition to being a danger for populations, EOs are also hampering delivery of aid by humanitarian workers who run high risks by intervening in contaminated areas. These results show the importance of an integrated approach to humanitarian mine action in order to reduce risks as well as address the needs of victims of EO and other people with injuries or disabilities, in order to help them overcome challenges caused by the conflict.

Implementation of activities

Obj. 1: the protection of the population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers 1.3 Advocacy with duty bearers and key stakeholders to inform and enhance the response to protection risks. The organization will conduct thorough analysis on its internal data and on secondary data, share relevant data with other advocacy actors (in a confidential and secure way), and produce and disseminate two evidence-based advocacy factsheets highlighting protection concerns regarding the use of explosive weapons in populated areas to the national, regional and international community. Based on the network of relations built over the organization experience. The organization will facilitate and co-lead throughout the duration of the Action one closed-door advocacy workshop to address pressing and emerging issues confronting the humanitarian response in Syria. Obj. 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, IHRL and through quality principled assistance. 2.1 Capacity building activities targeting humanitarian actors and national/ local authorities to improve quality provision of protection services. Safety briefings: The organization will provide safety briefings for humanitarian staff in order to raise their awareness about the risks and consequences of mines, ERW and IEDs they could face when in the field, and because they are directly assisting the victims and survivors during their work. Objective 4: Reduce the impact of explosive hazards 4.1 Conduct risk education for at-risk groups Risk education sessions: A composite intervention methodology will be implemented, allowing the activity to remain flexible to changes in context and needs. Thus, based on context, access and needs analysis, RE will be conducted either through direct RE sessions at households’ level or through public information. 4.2 Conduct survey and explosive hazard removal: Community surveys constitute the first step of the Contamination Impact Survey (CIS). Field teams are deployed at community level in order to gather relevant information through questionnaires, about accidents and the victims of these accidents, and about presence of contamination. Conduction of accident and victim survey The simultaneous implementation of community surveys and risk education sessions will permit to gather the necessary and relevant information on accidents and victims. Following this, the field teams will be able to identify record and refer victims of landmines, ERW and IEDs to appropriate services including rehabilitation and MHPSS services, as a third step of contamination impact survey. It will lead to cross-checking and validating information gathered on accidents, victims and contamination. Identification of EO, suspected and confirmed hazardous areas and EO disposal Using the information collected through community survey and risk education sessions field officer are in charge of investigating areas declared as contaminated. The field officers will then proceed with the identification of the threat following rigorously the INGO standard operating procedures. The information is fed back to the Multi Task Teams (MTT) who will take over for the clearance 4.3 Provide Victim Assistance Services for EHA survivors, people with injuries or disabilities. The organization provides physical rehabilitation, prosthetics and MHPSS services to people leaving in contaminated areas of Syria through direct provision of services and support to existing health facilities as well as referral to appropriate services based on the needs identified. Beneficiaries can also be referred by RE or NTS teams, identified through outreach activities or referral by other organisations. (direct beneficiaries are mentioned here as well as in the Health HRP).

Protection Risk Analysis

Mine action activities (clearance in particular) are not considered as humanitarian activities by all the parties to the conflict and this poses additional risks both to the staff of the Organization and its partners and to their beneficiaries. In addition, some armed groups might want to control or manipulate mine action activities in order to gain a military advantage and/or harvest and reutilize explosive remnants of war. The provision of Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) call out service and Battle Area Clearance (BAC) will increase access to safe land and infrastructure and mitigate the threat posed by mine/IED/ERW presence and contamination. In areas of Syria with high intensity of conflict and frequent airstrikes, gathering large numbers of people in public places for large Risk Education (RE) sessions might put these people at risk of becoming targets. To ensure the safety of parties involved during RE sessions (beneficiaries, partners’ and the Organization staff): 1) the INGO's mine action activities are managed in a strictly confidential way; 2) The Organization does not conduct mine action activities in Syrian government controlled areas at the moment, in order to avoid unnecessary risk to partners, staff and beneficiaries; 3) clearance activities are conducted with explosive and/or non-explosive means, depending on context and areas 4) quality assurance of clearance activities is conducted by the Organization in an independent way; 5) risk education is conducted under different forms (home sessions, group sessions in public places, Training of community focal point , etc.) and the INGO chooses in each context the most appropriate form in order to reduce the risk exposure and maximize the positive effect for the beneficiaries; 6) targeted beneficiaries who are identified as facing housing property and land issues and/or risks will be referred to adapted organizations able to provide support 7) Beneficiaries identified with conditions requiring physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support will be referred to relevant sectors within the INGO to increase impacts.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Preconditions Access to the targeted areas is maintained in acceptable security conditions during the project duration; The terms and commitments of partnership are respected by the parties; The various stakeholders coordinate themselves; Acceptance by local population is granted to the Organization staff and partners; Relations with authorities allow the Organization to operate in the country during the course of the project. Assumptions and risks - Deterioration of the security situation - Risk of investigation and arrest for stakeholders involved in aid provision - Denegation of approval and support of the local authorities (Syrian and Jordanian) - Shift of the geopolitical situation - related to shift in power/new agreements (as de-escalation zones) - Logistic and fund transfer issues jeopardize the implementation of activities - Difficulties faced in the recruitment of high profile positions and of gender balanced team Mitigation measures - Deterioration of the security situation: the access to intervention areas is not maintained in acceptable security conditions – the INGO has developed an access strategy and will refer to it if needed - Risk of investigation and arrest for stakeholders involved in aid provision: In case of accident, the Organization activates its duty of care Policy. - Denegation of approval and support of the local authorities (Syrian, Jordanian and Lebanese): the INGO's teams are well-perceived on the ground by both the local communities and the authorities. - Shift of the geopolitical situation - related to shift in power/new agreements (as de-escalation zones): Shift of territory control that might lead to a loss of access. the Organization will refer to its access strategy. - Logistic and fund transfer issues jeopardize the implementation of activities: Fund transfer issues will be mitigated by assessing several transfer partners as well as by enhancing coordination with INGO working in this area. In light of the recent evolution of the context in NES, the Organization has hired in early January 2019 a contingency planner who will support the team in the field and in coordination to work on the definition of the most likely scenarios in order to anticipate the potential impacts on the projects and prepare the implementation of the contingency measures.
