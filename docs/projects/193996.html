Project Summary

Through this intervention, PUI will focus on two localities (Al Geneina and Kereneik) in West Darfur. PUI aims at targeting 4 locations; two gathering sites in Al Geneina town, one primary health care centre (PHCC) in Kereneik, and one Mother and Child Centre (MCC) in Geneina. The MCC is currently used by IDPs as a gathering site, but discussions are already ongoing with the SMoH about the alternative location for the MCC. This centre will serve as the main primary care service for mothers, particularly conducting maternity care, such as ante-natal care (ANC), post-natal care (PNC) and Family Planning while MSF supports delivery services in the hospital, ensuring continuity of primary-secondary care and higher impact of each level of sexual and reproductive health services.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs continue to grow across Sudan. The fragile situation, particularly in West Darfur continues to unravel, as the political, economic, and food insecurity crisis affects millions of people. About 931,000 people need humanitarian assistance in West Darfur State. Increase in internal displacement and intercommunal violence is expected to continue in 2022 with immediate, mid-term and long-term consequences on social cohesion, resources management, and economic resilience of the population. Increased sub-national conflict in Al Geneina, Jebel Moon, Kulbus and Kereneik localities since 2021 has led to the primary and secondary displacement of an estimated 250,000 people across the statealso man-made causes, particularly with mass displacement. These include haemorrhagic fever (Dengue fever), Measles and Hepatitis E, in addition to the effects of the Covid-19 pandemic and most recently, Monkey-poxIDP communities are left without any basic services support for health, nutrition, psychosocial support (PSS) and protection needs. Al Geneina Teaching Hospital is partially functional, with frequent strikes staged by the MoH staff due to low salaries and inadequate working conditions, making the provision of quality health care services even more challenging. More than 80% of Sudan’s localities lack specialized GBV services, such as Clinical Management of Rape and minimum psychosocial supportAs of September 2022, around 83,017 individuals are displaced in Al Geneina and its surrounding villages. Women represent 52% of these displaced people. At least 9,940 cases of additional vulnerabilities were identified. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) among children under five in Sudan remains a major public health concern: it is 13.6 per cent, with 64 localities having World Health Organisation (WHO) emergency levels of 15 per cent and above, of which five have catastrophic levels of 30 per cent and above. MSNA done by IOM mid-year 2022 show that 10.68 of households in West Darfur have at least one child was SAM and 11.98 with at least one child with MAM. The nutrition cluster found that in Geneina the prevalence of GAM among children under 5 years is 13% for 2022 and the SAM rate is 2.2%, which is also projected for 2023 (HNO 2023). Al Geneina town has some functioning health facilities but not enough to cover the needs of the host communities and the IDPs within the gathering sites. Due to the economic situation in Sudan and the reducing funding for humanitarian assistance, many IDP communities are left without any basic services support including health, nutrition and protection. This has particularly worsened during the rainy season with an expected overall increase in the burden of communicable diseases. Malaria has the highest consultation rate in health facilities across West Darfur, standing at 19% of total consultations, followed by diarrheal diseases (and gastrointestinal diseases) at 15%, and pneumonia at 4%. The lack of services is an exacerbating factor contributing to the worsening of the GBV situation. PUI conducted a MSNA in Geneina in September 2022, according to Focus Group Discussions and Key Informant Interviews it was stated that there are high numbers of GBV cases with extremely little or no support for survivors. GBV happens particularly to girls and women from the IDP community in the gathering sites. Focus group discussions and key informant interviews showed the continuous occurrence of this kind of violence, which particularly affects girls between the ages of 15 to 20, including but not limited to rape among others, with cases happening within and outside of the gathering sites. Referral pathways and case management are particularly weak, leading to an everlasting impact on girls and young women.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI activities revolve around an integrated approach, focusing on Protection as a cross-cutting theme. As per guidelines of the Global Protection Cluster, PUI will incorporate protection principle and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in all activities.The following elements will be taken into account:1. Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3. Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints4. Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rightsTo prevent causing harm and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct sessions on safety measures to adopt during the repairs and after the work. These will also be developed with the aim to reinforce the beneficiaries’ autonomy and capacity to protect themselves in case of endangerment. PUI will ensure creating a safe and dignified environment. PUI aims at identifying the most vulnerable individuals or groups when leading assessments and surveys, to be able to appropriately analyze their needs and feedbacks, and PUI will develop strong accountability mechanisms as well. PUI will develop efficient accountability mechanisms, with the local authorities and community leaders. PUI will apply the “Do No Harm” principle across all its activities. PUI will also ensure that the supported health facilities are safely accessible, particularly for the vulnerable groups. An entrance accessible for people with disability, and a safe waiting area will be dedicated to women. Across all its intervention at health care facility level, the health and nutrition staff will able to identify unattended cases in need of PSS and base protection assistance such as child protection related issues, GBV cases, and severely traumatic experiences. PUI will activate the identified and relevant referral system when necessary.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

PUI will ensure that the field staff share with the community PUI’s commitment to accountability to the affected population on issues such as staff recruitment and training, code of conduct, information sharing and transparency. Partners will be included in the monitoring and evaluation mechanisms. The beneficiary population will also be informed of the project implementation modalities and the selection procedure, if any, as mentioned above.PUI will set up a complaint and feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the views and complaints of a wide range of stakeholders (affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors) and take appropriate action.A complaint response mechanism (CRM) will be designed in line with the protection principle of Do No Harm, in consultation with partners and affected communities, taking also into account available internal resources (financial and human) and with the objective of gradual scaling up. PUI thus aims to guarantee the availability of appropriate Feedback/Complaints channels, while ensuring effective complaints handling, in order to avoid creating frustration or mistrust with regard to the proposed mechanisms.Depending on the context, this mechanism consist of:• Community Mobilizers, who would be an important messengers conveying feedbacks and complaints from the population, themselves being part of the community,• Complaint boxes, especially in health facilities,• Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees,• Focus group discussions led by PUI teams. Whatever the mechanism(s) chosen, PUI will:• Inform the population of the existence and functioning of the complaint and feedback mechanism. This information will be disseminated through several channels (i.e. through community relays, during surveys etc.) in order to ensure that the targeted community can make use of it as soon as the first activities are launched.• Inform the population in the intervention area of the anti-fraud, anti-corruption and PSEA principles
