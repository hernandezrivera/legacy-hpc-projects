Project Summary

The project is responding to humanitarian needs of drought, covid 19 and locust affected community in target locations of Puntland and Somaliland through cash injection to address the immediate food needs of the people. Cash transfer programming will be used to reach households that are in IPC 3 and above. While focusing on life-saving interventions, the project will also support recovery and resilience building of the people where possible.
Findings from FSNAU 2020 post-Gu seasonal food security and nutrition assessment conducted across Somalia during July and August 2020 indicate that Due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021.
Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly.
The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the Somalis’s economy is significant impacting on poor urban and IDP households, as well as pastoralists in northern Somalia, namely Puntland and Somaliland. External remittance flows from the Somali diaspora constitutes 1.4-2 billion USD every year and contribute 23-32 percent of Gross Domestic Product, according to the World Bank. However, after the onset of COVID-19, money vendors report that the volume of cash transactions declined approximately 30-40 percent from March to June 2020
Improving food availability through support to agricultural production in Awdal and Nugal and introducing it in Sool and Sanag regions of Somalia is seen as an entry point to diversify pastoral livelihoods of those two regions. This plan involves also supporting communities’ recovery and livelihoods diversification interventions through livestock support with vaccination and treatment and Improving Agricultural Production/Food Security.
Livestock production was severely affected by the drought leading to substantially reduced herds; which is the country’s prime investment especially in rural communities. The mains reasons leading to the death were water shortages and lack of pasture. In view of this situation the project will explore fodder production combined with campaigns on livestock treatment. These include promotion of carrying capacity for sustained production. These actions will be coupled with Cash for Work activities which will be used for communal asset development linked to crop and animal husbandry.
In times of shock and more critical food insecurity, Oxfam will utilize cash and voucher assistance to reach to the most affected households impacted by triple threats of drought, covid or locust to meet the immediate emergency needs of at risk communities. cash is the preferred option for Oxfam to reach to people in remote areas, it is faster, sacure and easy to cover at scale. cash and voucher assistance also helps people to have flexibility on their needs and decision, choice and have multiplier effect on the economy

Project Activities

1. cash and voucher assistance (CVA) linked to Locust crises and Covid impacted HHs in Somalia to be supported to have better access to live saving food in times of food insecure times based on FSNAU food security outlook: when households coping strategies are depleted and are facing risk of food insecurity. This activity to address the locust crises and covid impact in Somalia. This unrestricted cash transfers supports local markets and can enhance Household’s to meet their basic needs and ensure economic recovery, preparedness and resilience. The communities will be able to access immediate food needs to survive during drought peal time. 2. Cash for Work (CFW) will be rolled out for the construction and/or rehabilitation communal assets identified by Awdal, Sool and Sanag communities. Targeted assets will ensure access to strategic grazing and water resources, and improve access to income and purchasing power of CFW households3. Strengthening agriculture through input provision and training of farmers with a focus on drought resistant agriculture inputs such as seeds and assorted tools. Oxfam will train targeted farmers on post-harvest management. The training topics will include harvesting methods, field to store handling, establishment of standard crib, post-harvest pest control, shelling methods, and storage.4. Testing fodder production- growing and preservation of seeds: Oxfam will support communities to increase access to biomass for the livestock to minimize risk of livestock death associated with scarcity of food. This activity is aimed to boost the capacity of the pastoral communities who lack conservation and preservation ability of pasture. During long dry period, the target fodder producers will sell surplus fodder in the surrounding and make money as an income generation activity. 5. Livestock treatment campaigns will be held with Ministry of Livestock this will involve community messaging and mobilization as well as treatment to be undertaken across multiple regions of Somaliland and Puntland. Livestock experts in the ministry of Livestock and Fisheries will participate in the development of campaign messages and participate in the treatment, Oxfam will provide drugs and technical support alongside this.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfer is fast, secure and reaches bigger outreach in areas hard to reach areas. Somaliland and Puntland has strong markets supporting this intervention. people in target region of Somalia have access to telephone reliable network covering entire Somalia, functioning markets and communities are used to it since 2017. apart from this cash provide flexibility, choice, dignity and stimulates the market making it more favorable than in kind support

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Markets are essential for accessing food, livelihood inputs and income before, during and after a shock. EFSVL team in Somalia/Somaliland will Continue to advance our reputation as a leading voice in markets work. Mobile network coverage is excellent in Somaliland speeding up CTP, contributing quality and speed and scale of the program. markets are supportive allowing food and other necessary commodities availability to the beneficiaries- Emergency market mapping assessment will be done to review any changes in the market. Cash programming remains one of our central ways of supporting food security and livelihoods in emergency context and promote a cash first approach – improve flexibility in moving between programme modalities. Continue to be at the forefront of CTP.

Mitigation Measures

Gender and protection issues will be streamlined through different stages of the response, and teams will be supported to ensure that minimum standards of gender and protection are met. The sector will focus on the most vulnerable men, women and children affected by drought, conflict and disease outbreaks in the pastoralist areas and IDP settlements, in particular those who lack safe access to assistance such as women and child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, people with disability. Gender analysis and inclusive consultation with all community members informs the design, implementation and measurement of impact. Implementation will be by trained, gender balanced teams. Project participants targeting will be based on gender sensitive vulnerability criteria with payments made to female heads of household as key decision makers in management of household food consumption. Monitoring/evaluation will assess qualitative and quantitative sex-disaggregated information. Protection: The project acknowledges the centrality of Protection and adheres to the 4 core Sphere protection principles. Community engagement will follow the Acceptance Approach and activities will be informed by risk analysis with assistance provided across Communities based on need without discrimination in accordance with the Do No Harm principle. Mainstreaming protection is fundamental to all Oxfam’s humanitarian work. Oxfam ensures that measures are in place so that its programmes do not put the affected population at risk by carrying out ‘safe programmes’. It endeavours to reduce risks that community members face because of the situation that they are in and ensures that its interventions do not bring additional burden into the lives of the target beneficiaries. Oxfam will carry out activity risk analysis linked to project implementation at the beginning of the intervention. Potential risks associated with an activity will be analysed to be able to put mitigation measures in place. The risk analysis is a living document, because situations change, and risks appear which were not foreseen. The analysis is checked and updated regularly as need. Targeted women, men, boys and girls will actively participate during the project cycle and community feedback mechanism will be disseminated widely at the start of the project. Targeting beneficiaries will be based vulnerability considering gender differentiated needs and priorities the selection criteria will be discussed with the target women men boys and girls to ensure the fairness of the selection process. Monitoring/evaluation will assess qualitative and quantitative sex-disaggregated information. Safe Programming: This is cross-cutting approach throughout the country programme. Oxfam has trained the entire team and its partners in Somalia on Safe Programming. The project will use safe programming lens in its entirely. We have well established safeguarding focal person at country level and field levels. Oxfam provided safe programming training to partners and Oxfam twice since beginning of 2019
