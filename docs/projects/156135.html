Project Summary

The project will contribute to reduce the suffering of the 24,922 displaced persons (5268 men 5905 women 7049 boys 6700 girls) including 147 persons with disabilities in the winter. inhabiting the 9 camps in (Atareb, Azaz, Salqin, Janudiyeh, and Badama)sub-district in Idleb and Aleppo. Through improving the winter resistance mechanisms in camps " installation of external insulation for tents - plastic sheet" as well as enhance the resilience and facilitate IDPs' access to water and sanitation services camp residents through improving the technical situation for the road network inside camps and reducing the damage in winter by graveling camp roads for 9 camps, and excavate open channels (Raining drainage systems), filling the pits in addition to enhance the access of the elderly and the people with disabilities to services like"installing ramps and Removing barriers" and modification of WCs to adapt to needs of people with special needs and elderly ,as well as protection of camp-based internally displaced persons (IDPs) through the installation of solar lights, the establishment of volunteer fire networks "3 men in each camp" and provide them with training and necessary equipment, as well as promoting of camp- and tent-specific fire safety practices amongst all beneficiaries. Furthermore, Space of peace "SOP" will provide incentives for 45 IDPs to invlove them in IDPs site to work in this project.
All the activities will be according to HLP guidelines, ensure that the site where tents will be installed are properly graveled, and the CCCM Cluster guidelines on safety and security measures and camp management are applied.

Interventions are based on the main needs reported by CCCM during the recent flood response, Gravelling, tent leveling, tent inner and outer insulation and establishing drains to eliminate water from the site.
The proposed activities will ensure the resilience and safety of IDPs in future flood and deteriorated weath
These activities have been designed to address the life-saving needs of the camps' residents while at the same time incorporating protection-enhancing measures and establish camp-exit and decongestion programme by conducting community mobilization about the camps which are a temporary place in the crisis in addition to, conducting the assessments and data collection about the IDPs who have a house in the safe place "village or cities" and determine the percentage of house's damage then share this information with Shelter cluster and UN agency and any party who care about exit strategy of camps with this information to rehabilitate their houses to be a safe place to live.

Needs

According to the statistics of (2019 HNO,CCCM Cluster) in spite of the use of IDP sites, including camps, remains an option of last resort, however, the lack of other alt contributed to an increase by 16 per cent of newly displaced IDPs seeking shelter in such sites following significant incidences of displacement, also an estimated 34 per cent of the sites’ population, can't receive minimum food assistance on a monthly basis. Approximately 25 and 26 per cent of the population in sites, do not received adequate shelter and NFI assistance, respectively. Challenges also persist in sanitation, waste removal and water (15% of the population). A large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements. The proposed intervention has been designed based on feedback from beneficiaries gathered through multiple FGDs, endline reports, as well as a rapid assessment on winterization needs conducted by our (M&E) field staff This project targets both IDPs already residing in camps, and newly arrived IDPs ,SOP staff on the ground visited IDPs in the camps and collecte data on needs of IDPs through observation and discussions. With overcrowded tents and overcrowded community centres resulting in difficult access to water and hygiene facilities; the risk of infections are high for the most vulnerable population due to insalubrity.Other risks related to the lack of privacy are the breakdown of protection mechanisms. Females and girls are more exposed to SGBV when they are not able to access suitable WASH facilities. The graveling of the area of operations will offer access roads to the different parts of the camp, it will enhance freedom of movement in the camp, diminish the feeling of isolation of IDPs and it will address the mobility needs of persons with physical disabilities. Indeed, when rain turns the soil into mud, persons with physical disabilities are the most affected in their movements. Muddy soils and floods increase the risks of IDPs to contract infections due to the cold but also to the stagnation of water. During winter, the main challenge is to keep tents warm for that the tent should be insulated with weatherproofing The building of activities have been based on Space of peace team needs assessments and past experience. FGDs carried out by Space of peace's team in the last week of August found, the cold in the winter is a major concern amongst residents, in addition to fire protection.Camp residents also suffer from extreme cold in the winter, some of tents need to external insulation sheets to protect families from rains and snows. The camp residents can't buy heaters and fuel needed due to poverty. As FGDs have pointed out that the camps need to the installation of solar lights at public latrines and other communal spaces, the public latrines have an urgent need for lighting, Also, the lack of privacy and safety for women and girls in these settlement conditions increases their risk of gender-based violence (GBV). There are more needs for the sensitization and raise awareness for the camp's population, the work needs to take place from the camp population, and other influential community leaders who could play a significant role in responding, in addition to supporting prevention efforts through raising the awareness of the community and camps against different types of GBV. More importantly, services and facilities constructed require constant maintenance and to save it to meet the needs. In the absence of a governance, people and communities should be accountable for sustainability of services and facilities.Space of peace will work to contribute to a more sustainable, accountable and service-oriented governance structure for the area's IDP population
