Project Summary

Qatar Red Crescent Society will improve the access and utilization of health services for 33,216 women, men, boys, and girls living in the most crisis-affected populations in IDP settlements and the vulnerable urban and rural poor in Qardho, Balad, and Baidoa.
Proposed interventions contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2, and three. Qatar Red Crescent will seek to reduce excess morbidity and mortality as a result of climatic shocks and conflicts. Proposed activities will seek to contribute to improvement in the physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement among IDPs and non-displaced; including people with disabilities. Further, seek to improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis-affected population IDPs, and non-displaced population.
Static facilities and mobile outreach teams will be set up and strengthened through the provision of medical supplies and the improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be recruited and trained. Among the activities that QRC will include supplemental vaccination campaign to under 5 children (boys and girls) and Women of childbearing age (WCBA); Provision of antenatal and post-natal care to pregnant and lactating mothers, strengthening physical and mental healthcare system. Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting which boys, girls, PLW, WCBA, and men; Strengthening of a referral system between Primary Healthcare facilities, the community (remote villages) and the regional hospitals; Establishment of mobile clinics; Creating Disease Surveillance and Response plan; Training of health workers on clinical management of childhood illness, Basic Emergency Obstetric Care and disease surveillance and response. Training will be conducted for community health workers in​ integrated community case management on communicable diseases​ such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.

1. - Projects activities

Activities under cluster objective one: 1.Support early detection, investigation, and response to disease (e.g. outbreak) through Rapid response teams/RCCE, HCW training, monitoring (EWAR), and supplies. Setting up and training RRT, deployment, and providing back-up support through static facilities and mobile teams. Support scale-up of outbreak early warning and response (EWAR) and case management of epidemic-prone diseases. 2. Targeted [supplemental] vaccination campaigns. QRC will embed regular and periodical vaccination campaigns targeting children as well as women of childbearing age (WCBA) in the program. This will be through mobile medical services and static facilities staff as well as working with Community Health Care workers. Hard to reach areas will be given more emphasis. 3. Health awareness, social mobilization, education, and advocacy activities in disease control, health, and nutrition. Leverage capacity-built CHW workers to carry out health awareness to strengthen demand/uptake for health and nutrition care services. The CHW will be integrated within mobile health services as well as support static facilities. 4. Health Care Workers training: trauma, medical countermeasures (MCM), case management, emergency management. This activity will target critical health care workers. This activity will primarily target static health facilities. Areas include; just-in-time training (JITT) to learn how to complete their roles during various emergencies. Covers topics including chain of command, job action sheets, critical skills, and communication. 5. Infection Prevention and Control (IPC): Provider and patient safety (e.g. PPE, triage, hand-washing); Environmental health. The project will design mobile IPC stations and enhance existing static facilities in order to improve access to emergency and essential health services. Staff will be trained on environmental health and supplies support. 6. Health Protection and Surge: Medical logistics and contingency supplies. Working with district hospitals and health actors, support prepositioning of supplies in areas highlighted as prone to epidemic disease outbreaks through QRC run facilities or supporting existing hospitals. Proposed activities under cluster objective two: 1. Rights to Health Approach. Support accessibility facility elements for people living with disabilities (PLWD) and the elderly. Improvement of health facility infrastructure and ensuring health care workers are sensitive to PLWD/elderly health care needs and culture. This will include the provision of wheelchairs, ramps, side rails, and capacity building HCW on PLWD culture. 2. Support health facilities in reporting PwD as well PwD providing feedback on health services. Activities under cluster objective three: 1. Provision of life-saving primary care health care services, through fixed and mobile outreach services. Services include general OPD targeting all the population, under five and PLW. Primary health care services, consultations, secondary health care, and referral services. 2. Integrated Health and Nutrition services to enhance complimentary services and reduce the cost of the intervention. These will be done mostly through IERT. 3.Maternal and newborn health services. Build capacity on B/CEmNOC mobile and static facilities respectively. Train staff on and provision of pre- and post-natal services including CHW. WCBA reached through sexual and reproductive healthcare with a focus on hard to reach areas leveraging CHW.

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance.
4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.
4.3 - Exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements.
4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors.
4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area.
4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. - Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. -Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. QRCS will Identify central locations and bring services closer to vulnerable populations in IDPs and host communities. For example, QRCS will use mobile outreach teams to provide services to populations living further away from static facilities to reduce their risk of exposure to protection issues. -Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. -Ensure services are offered within time to ensure beneficiaries travel back safely. 2. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. -Design and clearly mark separate facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children. -In coordination with protection actors, train health staff to be able to identify and appropriately respond to victims of grave human rights violations, including rape and physical abuse, and set up referral networks and protocols to protect the privacy of victims. -Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with patients, health staff, and beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). -In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community health workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. -In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to jointly report incidents -Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them. -Set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions. -Promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at the nutrition centre, phone lines. 4. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. -Keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use - apply effective data protection measures. -Do not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). -Separate examination rooms from public spaces or the waiting area (eg. Establish dry walls or at least a curtain) -Establish an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided.
