Project Summary

Relief International considers the acute needs of the targeted localities and vulnerabilities of these people and will ensure increased household access to basic services and will help reduce vulnerability to diseases including the spread of COVID-19. The project will emphasize increasing access and coverage to vulnerable displaced population (Refugees, IDPs), returnees and host communities focusing on children U5 and PLW to increase resilience and reduce acute malnutrition in Blue Nile (Alr-series, Damazine, Bau, Geissan and Krmuk localities), North Darfur (Al-Malha, El-Fasher, Kebkabiya, Ailliet, Mellit, Kalamindo , Al-Tawisha and Sarfu-Omra) and Khartoum states ( Jabal Aulia and Sharq El Nile). This project is designed to provide a package of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) services through supporting the existing system by capacity building of health and nutrition staff, securing nutrition supplies, contribute to the running cost, and establishing linkages between SCs and the OTP sites. Emphasis will also be given to IYCF strategies, community-based support of IYCF through mother support groups, and IYCF in exceptionally difficult circumstances (IYCF-E) with focus on one-to-one IYCF counseling and support for mothers, promotion of breastfeeding and hygiene promotion. This program will build on existing community groups wherever possible and will be based on the mother support groups (MSG) model. This is a peer-based nutrition promotion program that aims to create individual and community behavior change in nutrition practices through dissemination of key nutrition messages. The proposed project will support resilience through capacity building of the local community. The project also will address the cross-cutting issues of gender the project mainstreaming protection services through enhancing accountability towards gender Based violence (GBV) through training of team leaders of MtMSGs of IYCF to integrate GBV in nutrition intervention
To complement nutrition and FSL interventions, Relief International will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to help households meet their basic needs during the lean season and reduce their dependence on external assistance. The MPCA will target mostly refugee and IDP women by providing cash assistance which will be used to provide basic COVID-19 protective materials such as sanitizers, face masks, soap and other hygiene materials which will improve their personal hygiene. The MPCA will also aim at proving cost for food which will reduce the tendency of the most vulnerable of refugees leaving their houses in search of food which significantly increases their chances of contaminations. Additionally, the MPCA will targeting families with children who have severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications at the Relief International-run outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) who have been referred to the stabilization center. This cash will enable them to buy children needs like clothes and food.

1 - Needs

Sudan has high level of acute and chronic malnutrition indicators which are both above the international ‘emergency’ thresholds. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is 13.6%, Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rates are also worryingly high at 2.2% which translates into half a million children suffering from SAM. About 35% of children under-fives were moderately or severely stunted (too short for their age (S3MII, December 2018). With other underlying causes mostly structural such as conflicts and protracted displacement, extreme inflation as a result of the current ongoing macroeconomic crisis that has caused the price for the average minimum expenditure to quadruple this year (2021) leaving most of the households among the both hosts, refugees and IDPs struggling to meet basic needs, leaving them highly vulnerable to food insecurity, communicable disease and malnutrition. The Sudan government hosts huge burden of refugee’s community clustered in various parts of the country who require much needed support. The inter-agency assessments of the refugees’ situation in Khartoum and in Blue Nile (December -2017- April 2022) have documented dire needs with most common issues facing them as limited access to quality nutritious food. Malnutrition is being driven by high food costs, poor healthcare and a lack of access to WASH facilities and services. In North Darfur, and Blue Nile, food insecurity and acute malnutrition continue to negatively impact nutrition outcomes. All targeted localities are classified as Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 3 - Crisis as of February 2021. FMoH most recent results of S3MII (December 2018) in North Darfur (El-Tawisha, Al-Malha, and El Fasher) showed high SAM and MAM rates of 9% and 60% respectively. Children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are disproportionately affected by malnutrition. Because of their high nutritional needs and vulnerability, children are at particular risk of stunting and even death without access to a diet that meets all their nutrient needs. Poor nutrition for pregnant women can cause mortality but can also impede fetal growth, resulting in low birthweight and increasing the risk that children’s growth will be stunted. North Darffur S3M II survey results indicated that about 7 out of ten (69.4%) mothers exclusively breastfed their child during the first six months, and 92.6% started breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth. Only a minimal proportion (6.3%) of caregivers have negative perceptions of colostrum. Approximately 10 -13% of children 0-5 months received water from birth while approx. 5 - 9% of infants by 4–5 months of age received solid or semi-solid foods too early than recommended. The early introduction of water and solid or semi-solid before six months was from the belief among some mothers that breast milk cannot quench a baby’s thirst and breastmilk is too dilute for the baby and needs complementing with other foods. Only 73.95% of children 12–23 months continue to breastfeed, implying that fewer children continue to breastfeed for two years and beyond. Dietary diversity among children 6-23 months was poor as just 17.8% received food from 5 or more food groups out of eight main food groups to meet the minimum dietary diversity standard. The counseling coverage in both states (North Darfur, BN and Khartoum) remains low at 37.8% average that calls for elaborate effort to scale up the awareness raising sessions. There is protection risks among the IDPs and refuges Women, boys, and girls have high exposure to violence and exploitation due to limited economic opportunities and lack of social safety nets. Between December 2017 and January 2021, UNAMID documented 192 victims of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). The impacts of SGBV are exacerbated and prolonged due to a lack of quality response services and reporting mechanisms.

2 - Activities/Outputs

IRI will target IDPs, Refugees and host communities in North Darfur, Blue Nile and Khartoum states by increased access to nutrition assistance for affected children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Integrating WASH, Health, and Nutrition activities the project will contribute to saving lives by reducing wasting and stunting levels caused by Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Key activities will involve: - SAM treatment for children U5 (without complication) in 57 OTPs (2 in Khartoum; 46 North Darfur and 9 in Blue Nile) targeting 9,262 girls and 8,214 boys aged 6-59 months. - Treatment SAM with complication treatment in 3 SCs targeting a total of 548 girls and 4860 boys- Referrals of SAM with complications referral to various SCs targeting 1034 girls and boys. - MAM treatment for children U5 targeting 24,197 girls and 21,458 boys - MAM Treatment in PLW targeting 8,992 women in all target locations. - Supporting the running cost for 3 SCs and food to a total of 1,034 caregivers - Rehabilitating/upgrading SC in Sarfu Umra locality hospital. - Carrying out the quarterly MUAC mass screenings/ referral and regular at household level) per locality - Vitamin A supplementation per child targeting a total of 68,723 girls and 60,944 boys - Micronutrient supplementation through MNPs (home fortification) of children aged 6-59 per child targeting a total of 18,138 girls and 16,084 boys- Provision of FBPM for under for 0-23mo per child targeting 10,468 girls and 9,284 boys - Provision of FBPM for per PLW targeting a total of 10,682 women- Conduct counselling of PLW and caregivers of children 0-23 months on IYCF targeting a total of 39,029 women- Training of health and nutrition workers on basic CMAM to a total of 250 participants for five days. - Train Health and nutrition workers for basic in-patient management of SAM (SC) to total of 25 staff for 7 days. - Training of Health and nutrition workers on basic IYCF to total of 250 participants for 5 days. - Training of Community health workers (CNVs) on CMAM and IYCF for 3 days targeting a total of 570 participants across all states of operation. - Conduct SMART survey in EL-Fasher locality to determine the current GAM prevalence.- Conduct regular and joint monitoring and supportive supervision of the nutrition interventions in operational areas. At least 4 quarterly joint visit and weekly supportive supervision - Train a total of 250 nutrition frontline workers on GBV referrals (and PSEA) and create awareness of GBV through nutrition services to a total of 39029 individuals- Conduct 4 community sensitization campaign of nutrition services, disseminate relevant materials regarding services in all 57 health facilities and establish the feedback and reporting mechanisms in all nutrition sites. - Provision of hygiene kits and to a total of 73,159 nutrition (SC, OTP and TSFP) beneficiaries (boys, girls and PLWs) - Support the promotion of WASH services at health facilities/CMAM sites (OTP/TSFP/SCs) and the provision of regular awareness raising at 57 nutrition sites - - Enroll vulnerable households with children and PLWs with acute malnutrition into FSL related interventions of cash-based interventions targeting a total of 17,476 HH during lean season- Link households with malnourished children with Kitchen gardens trainings and support with inputs resources to improve their food security in priority localities

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

RI will undertake waste management as an important element of environmental protection. Its purpose is to provide hygienic, efficient and economic solid waste storage, collection, transportation and treatment or disposal of waste without polluting the atmosphere, soil or water system in all the health facilities. Pharmaceutical waste resulting from routine medical distribution will be disposed of as per SMoH guidelines. RI will maintain strict adherence to waste disposal in all health facilities. All the medical waste that emanated from WFP/ UNICEF food distribution (empty sachets of RUSF, CSB++ and MNPs including the packaging empty cartons) will be disposed of in an environmental friendly incinerator constructed in all health facilities through other sources of RI funding. RI will ensure there is adequate dust bins in all distribution sites and staff will be trained on proper waste management. Any rehabilitation works will ensure minimal usage of materials that are not depleting the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Relief International uphold protection mainstreaming and GBV protection measures throughout all stages of the project cycle. For GBV risk reduction, The IASC Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Settings: will be a reference document for ensuring quality in planning and coordination.RI has consulted communities, government authorities, and other relevant partners in the design of this project to ensure accountability to the affected population. The consultation with communities has involved both women and men as well as children. The accountability mechanism explained in the accountability section will provide an opportunity for the affected population to raise their concerns and claim their rights. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive, and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions. Capacity building will include activities of risk reduction to ultimately increase the targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness, and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships and to improve all aspects of protection for children. RI will ensure that meaningful access to the targeted population will be needs-based and without any constraints such as discrimination, reduced mobility, or other limitations. Additionally, the social, cultural barriers that affect GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance will be addressed through awareness-raising sessions and involvement of local partners. RI will ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended adverse effects on the targeted population. Which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks by working closely with various stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to analyze the security situation and to avoid tensions between within target communities.RI recognizes the importance of the human rights of host communities, returnees, IDPs, and especially women, girls, and minority groups. During implementation, RI will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence, and abuse the rights of vulnerable populations. RI will reach out to communities who are unable to access the static facilities, to ensure that individuals afraid or unable to move are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

RI will ensure effective communication and coordination between all implementing partners to the best benefit of the affected persons. RI will sensitize all relevant stakeholders about the nutrition activities its undertaking while coordinating with relevant sectors of WASH, Health and Livelihood. All components of nutrition services available shall be clearly be displayed at the health facility translated in local language to sensitize the community. Actions indicated in this proposal are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through ongoing rapid need assessments, field visits, and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. RI has a zero-tolerance approach against all forms of abuse and exploitation. Our child safeguarding policy works to ensure the protection of children and families who come into contact with RI. RI safeguards children from deliberate or negligent actions that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury, and any other harm. The RI child safeguarding reporting procedures ensure cases are investigated and concluded within a specified period depending on the categorization of those cases. RI will take necessary disciplinary measures against any staff, including reporting to local law enforcement authorities. The Whistleblowing Policy allows a person to raise a concern directly with senior leaders if they feel unable to use usual reporting processes. All staff, volunteers, and partners that work with or on behalf of RI sign the policy before beneficiary engagement & regular training ensures employees/volunteers are provided with necessary information regarding child safeguarding issues. We have focal points/champions at each field office and area offices to coordinate all safeguarding matters.Health, Nutrition, and WASH response information will be shared timely with beneficiaries in the response area through COVID-19 sensitive means of communication. Feedback and complaints will be collected through meetings as far as the conditions allow, as well as through RI’s accountability phone number that can link response beneficiaries with RI, MoH, and other local partners. Feedback and complaints received will be transferred to relevant RI to be addressed, documented, and analyzed to contribute to the learning processes and decision-making regarding the action. In the event of COVID-19 and other pandemics, RI will modify its accountability systems to suit the steps recommended to combat the pandemic depending more on the accountability assigned phone number or limiting the number of community members attending any meetings for collection of feedback and complaints and keep the recommended distance between attendees. RI will display its hotline-reporting mechanism so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided to relevant staff in the organization. RI’s team will verify the information obtained
