Project Summary

This project seeks to reach at least 1600 Syrian IDPs and host community members to increase household income and their ability to meet ongoing basic needs. The program will focus on improved market linkages in relevant sectors including support services to facilitate these linkages such as access to training, business support services and appropriate financial services increasing the market-driven skills training and develop linkages with local private sector bodies to provide apprenticeship and employment opportunities. Orange currently operates several livelihoods centres in Azzaz (Aleppo governorate), Ma'aret An Numan and Sarmada (Idlib governorate) as well as vocational training centers across Aleppo and Idlib governorates, Syria. In response to community consultations, the centres have been providing training to build employability skills, language courses and productive asset (entrepreneurship) support, apprentices/internship (employment) support services. In order to respond to this need for livelihoods support, Orange committed to employing the principles of best practices for economic recovery with focusing on the medium to long-term economic recovery efforts that will lead to employment creation and inclusive economic growth.

Needs

The livelihood conditions of the Syrian population in northern Syria has been hugely affected by the ongoing violence mainly because of the economic situation, including the deflation of the Syrian pound against foreign currencies, the breakdown of the government subsidies, breakdown of supply routes and sources of raw materials and the closure of factories, and massive rise in the prices of fuel and raw materials. Overall, these have been as a result of the ongoing violence which also led to massive displacement of people to other areas and putting further pressure on available services within host communities. This in turn resulted in increasing competition for decreasing opportunities, especially in trade and labor. Furthermore, those displaced people did flee to other areas with little or no assets, and forced to sell assets, which have minimized their long-term coping abilities particularly in reduced income generating opportunities context like Idleb, Aleppo and Hama governorates. The ongoing conflict especially aerial bombardment and barrel bombing, not only caused significant loss of assets but also lead to the destruction of infrastructure including the destruction of public facilities (schools, hospitals, and public services buildings) as well as markets and income generating facilities like factories and down-scaling the cross-border trade. HNO highlights that main early recovery and livelihoods needs include: better access to markets; livelihoods; emergency employment; business revival; and sustainable income-generation opportunities. Rehabilitation of infrastructure such as quick repairs of electricity and water, jobs and employment opportunities, are also urgently required in many areas.

Implementation of activities

Objectives: to provide opportunities of income generation for displaced youth and women in Syria:• Livelihoods assessment of assets of target population, skills gap analysis to identify skills training needs, mapping the local labor markets for potential apprenticeship opportunities for youth and women, and understanding potential for job opportunity specially for women. A market research will be started in Syria as first steps of this process will include information on the labour market and on the potential local and external markets for goods and services. The analysis will allow subsequent programming to match training with the demand for skills in the labour market, so that trainees and apprentices will be able to gain sustainable employment or successfully pursue their own businesses. The analysis will include a range of sectors, including agriculture, industry, trade, and transportation. It will consider the demand for labour and existing services. • Adaptation of market-based education and training curricula such a business management, life skills training will be provided to at least 1400 women and youth (ages 18-25) as well as vulnerable host community members inside Syria helping them to improve non-technical skills, including business management, financial literacy, communication skills, self-confidence, self-management and goal-setting.• Vocational literacy courses for youth and women who may face reading, writing or numeracy barriers.• Design market-oriented business development services of Vocational skills training and apprenticeship programs in new sectors with likelihood to lead employment/self-employment in aligned with labor market demand. Technical, Vocational and Educational Training (TVET) prepares young people for productive participation in the labour market by providing the knowledge and skills required for occupations, as well as a specific pathway into the profession offering both classroom-based and hands-on training. Additionally, apprenticeships provide on-the-job training and job shadowing between an apprentice and a skilled artisan or tradesperson providing apprentices better understanding of the work environment, develop their skills, and will receive a small stipend. The project will work with local private businesses to demonstrate the value of apprenticeships and training support, which contribute to their own productivity and to local economic recovery.• Short-term employment opportunities, the livelihood centres will also offer opportunities for non-Skilled/Skills-based cash-for-work projects that allow youth and women to earn a consistent income for up to three months while also building skills for future employment. This will allow quick injections of cash into beneficiary families in exchange for work on projects that improve host communities and local infrastructure or providing services/good for people in need. Projects are decided and designed with community input and work will also include a skill building component.• Microenterprise and counselling of start-up grants to youth and women who have completed vocational trainings, apprenticeship programs and business training to launch their own business, including small home-based business. To improve the business management capacities and practices of stakeholders in the local business community, leading to stabilized income opportunities of small-scale business owners.

Protection Risk Analysis

- Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Also, lack of qualified services lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services.- Privacy worries along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women and girls to access livelihoods center.- Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on accessing the LVH center by women and girls.- Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment.- Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to the Livelihood center.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:•Orange is maintaining a very good relationship with the community, relevant stakeholders, NGOs operating in the area. •Orange has a bank of local trainers/project staff who have the experience delivering similar activities in the same s field.•Orange has access to the community, responds to their feedback and ensure any necessary corrective decision is made during the life cycle of the project. •Orange has been delivering similar programming, demonstrating its relevant experience in professional development and the provision of capacity development and integrated protection and livelihoods programming. •Orange is operating through policies, in line with donors’ rules and regulations including Human Resources, Finance, Procurement, Logistics, MEAL and project Management policies, through which Orange abides by donors’ and international standardized rules and regulations. •Availability of all the resources, financial, human, material resources inside Syria, which will facilitate the implementation of the project activities. Risks:•Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas: To ensure that the changing security situation does not affect the implementation of the project activities Orange will:-Ensure flexible context-informed approaches and mechanisms to deliver the project outputs/outcomes.-Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for staff and the projects beneficiaries and sufficient resources to enact those plans with provision of safety training to the project staff.-Assess where is the safest place to store supplies. If besiegement or road cut-offs seem likely, preposition stock, as appropriate/possible. -All critical documents will be saved as soft copies.•Insufficiently skilled staff due to difficulty of finding adequately skilled people inside Syria or unwillingness of people to travel to work within Syria.-As part of our project process potential we will provide evidence that we have, or can hire, sufficiently skilled project staff.•Unstable project costs due to an unstable Syrian Pound, or unstable costs due besiegement, poor market access/availability, etc.-Project budgets will take into account real costs and most likely changes in costs during the life of the project (i.e. price increases/decreases linked to the start/stop of besiegement blocked roads, etc.) -Flag early any budget issues related to price fluctuations. As a last resort project budgets may be realigned.•Delay or inability to deliver supplies in a timely manner due to border crossing closure or border regulations, insecurity on transit routes or near warehouses.-Procurement, pre-positioning and delivery plans will take into account possible delays in borders or transit.-Go to locally purchase project supplies/materials as long as supplies are available in the market.•Drop out from Center-Conduct awareness sessions to encourage the continuity and commitment of beneficiaries.
