Project Summary

BAHAR seeks to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the healthcare facilities,Outreaches,and RRTs,it is currently operating through screen all children under 5 years and PLW, selecting beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score),provide SAM treatment for children with complicated SAM using f75, F100(use only in Stabilization Center), and RTUF beside clinical management of common complications, provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW withMAM using Plumpy sup, provide the blanket supplementary feeding program for children 6–23 months and CVA for PLWs to increased access to improved diets and prevent them from deterioration nutritional statues, Conduct MIYCF awareness sessions and messages through MBA, community levels(outreach)on WHOrecommended,apply FMA for caregivers.
As well as BAHAR will conduct training to build capacity of frontline nutrition servicesproviders on IYCF and CMAM programs. BAHAR will procure about 10% of the necessary nutrition supplies for this project(buffer), with the remainder being supplied by UNICEF.
This project will target 8 subdistricts:Aleppo(Afrin, Bulbul, Jandairis, Ma'btali, Raju, Sharan, Sheikh El-Hadid), Idleb (Maaret Tamsrin)
the scope of services includes:2 Stabilization center within two materniy& pediatric Hospital(BAHAR & Almadina)1 MBA in Almadina hospital 1OTP, 1SFP in BAHAR hospital and 9RRTs(Outreaches)
the project will achieve effect cost through integration at various levels with other sectors: Health, Protection, FSL and will provide the appropriate nutrition services to all age and sex groups; boys and girls, women, men, with attention to people with special needs, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and HC, while ensuring that inclusion is mainstreamed throughout these activities to avoid exclusion of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities. The targeted number of children under 5years is 36036 and 15444 PLWs, these number planned basedon SMART Survey, that refectthe situation in the targeted locations.
Implementation of activities: the Project data is collected on a monthly basis from the field, and reviewed by technical and administrative staff to ensure quality control, adherence to the project proposal and sent as 4Ws for Nutrition Cluster by monthly basis, also BAHAR follows the financial andadministrative guidelinesfor financial control.
BAHAR's M&E team will monitor the project' activities based on the agreed log-frame, Adetailed monitoring plan will be prepared according to the final version of the log-frame,BAHAR will ensure its MEAL plan to be implemented fully compliant with ‘Do No Harm’principles. The MEAL team will be trained on protection-sensitive interview skills and approaches to ensure a dignity of the beneficiaries is maintained. The MEAL plan will utilize the stratified sampling method to collect sex and gender-segregated data, ensuring equal participation of women and youth, who are often under-represented in the Syria response.
Gender and age-appropriate survey tools and methods developed, and MEAL team will ensure a balanced representation of genders among its staff to ensure access to both genders. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to provide honest feedback throughout the project, including negative feedback, to enhance project performance and to gather lessons learned for future planning. Their feedback will be incorporated during review exercises
The M&E team will implement the following plan:
1-Field visits: to observe the overall progress of the project and compare the work-planwith exact implementation.2-Monthly reports: To check programmatic/financial progress, identify obstacles3-Patient Exit Interviews via patients,parents and companion feedbacks. (Monthly)4-Receiving and reviewing all complaints with project focal point through the WhatsAppcomplaint line and the complaints box.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to HNO 2024,16.7 million people are in need, including 5.5 million displaced individuals and 5.7 million requiring urgent nutrition assistance. Compared to 2023, there has been a significant rise in the number of people requiring nutritional support, particularly among pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls, and children under 5 years old. the number of children in need of preventive interventions has surged by 12% from the previous year (from 812,858 in 2023 to 933,177 in 2024)Integration with other sectors: Presence of BAHAR WASH, Health, Protection, Nutrition FSL, services in target locations will play vital role in malnutrition cases and achieve effect cost through integration at various levels with other sectors.for example, Diarrhea is one of the top five major causes of child malnutrition and death, emphasizes the need for a comprehensive intervention strategy in partnership with the WASH, Health, Food Security, and Protection sectors. . M&E:-Monitoring, Evaluation, and Accountability through BAHAR MEAL team, including baseline, end line, and PDM. Where data is collected from the field and reviewed by technical staff to make achievement control and sent as 4Ws for Nutrition Cluster by monthly basis BAHAR's M&E team will monitor these project activities ensure its MEAL plan to be implemented a fully the details as mentioned in project summery.Protection Risk Analysis:1.Risk of exploitation of beneficiaries by service staff: Likelihood: L Impact: H Mitigation Measures: Carefully select staff with high behavioral qualities. 2.Drastic changes in the context of the Syrian conflict, such as extensive changes in the front lines, leading to mass expulsions and/or access restrictions Likelihood: L, Management: Provision of emergency plans e.g. through the deployment of personnel with RRTs in the event of a sudden IDP influx Constant and accurate context analysis and situation assessment by the security focal point of the project partner BAHAR 3. Risk of GBV on service staff during home visits: Likelihood: M Impact: H Mitigation Measures: service staff selected from targeted communities, staff trained in first-line support and GBV identification. 4. Risk of discrimination in service provision: Likelihood: L Impact: H Mitigation Measures: Ensure project design includes accessibility for vulnerable groups, implement effective community-based complaints mechanisms, and adhere to humanitarian principles and a "Do No Harm" strategy.Regard PSEA: Considering the high vulnerability of the project beneficiaries, BAHAR provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per facility. In addition, the services will be provided on a free-of-charge basis for all those in need regardless of their individual gender groupings.
