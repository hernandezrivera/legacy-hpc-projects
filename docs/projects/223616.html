Project Summary

Integrated Women and Youths Empowerment Centre (IWAYEC) is implementing a targeted intervention to enhance access to inclusive, quality education for 2,440 children—75% of whom are girls—in three communities of Askira/Uba Local Government Area (LGA), Borno State. This initiative aims to address the persistent educational challenges stemming from conflict, displacement, and systemic educational disparities in the region. With over 148,000 internally displaced children in Borno State alone, the lack of safe, accessible, and resource-equipped educational environments remains a critical barrier to achieving universal education. This project leverages a multi-faceted approach to bridge these gaps by establishing temporary learning spaces, equipping schools with libraries, distributing essential educational materials, and engaging communities to prioritize and sustain education for all children.
The project will set up temporary learning spaces in communities where school infrastructure has been destroyed or rendered unusable due to conflict. These spaces will include semi-permanent structures, furnished classrooms, and gender-sensitive WASH facilities to ensure safe and inclusive environments. To enhance learning outcomes, libraries will be established and stocked with age-appropriate textbooks, storybooks, and reference materials aimed at improving literacy and fostering a love for reading. School kits comprising stationery, uniforms, backpacks, and other necessary supplies will be distributed to all children. These resources will alleviate the financial burden on families, ensuring that vulnerable children, especially girls, can access and stay in school. The school kits will be gender-responsive to address specific needs, such as including menstrual hygiene products for adolescent girls.
To strengthen the overall educational ecosystem, the project will integrate teacher training on conflict-sensitive education, inclusive teaching practices, and psychosocial support (PSS). These capacity-building sessions will enable teachers to address the complex needs of children affected by displacement and trauma. Complementary to these efforts, literacy initiatives such as reading weeks and competitions will be conducted to improve students' engagement, foster a culture of learning, and reduce dropout rates, especially for girls who are disproportionately affected by barriers like early marriage and domestic responsibilities. Advocacy campaigns targeting parents, community leaders, and local authorities will focus on promoting education for all children while addressing societal norms and gender-based biases that hinder girls’ education.
This project aligns with strategic objectives to provide safe, inclusive education in emergencies and enhance the capacity of government institutions and communities to deliver sustainable education solutions. By creating resilient learning environments and fostering community ownership, the project seeks to transform the lives of conflict-affected children in Askira/Uba LGA. Beyond addressing immediate educational needs, it establishes the foundation for long-term educational resilience, empowering children with the tools and opportunities to rebuild their futures. The integration of material support, teacher training, and community advocacy ensures a comprehensive and sustainable approach to improving educational access and quality for children impacted by conflict.

1 - Needs

Conflict in Borno State has had a devastating impact on the education system, with 875,000 people in need of educational support, and 540,000 children facing significant barriers to accessing education. Of these, 148,000 are internally displaced children (IDPs) in camps or host communities, many of whom have been deprived of formal education for years. Askira/Uba LGA is especially affected, where schools are either destroyed or repurposed, and children in both IDP camps and host communities face overcrowded classrooms, lack of learning materials, and deteriorating infrastructure.1. Access to Safe Learning SpacesConflict has destroyed much of the educational infrastructure in Askira/Uba, leaving 62% of schools in dire need of rehabilitation. Many existing schools are overcrowded, with pupil-to-teacher ratios exceeding 1:100, which hampers effective teaching. This project will address these gaps by constructing safe, semi-permanent learning spaces, designed with child-friendly and gender-sensitive features, including WASH facilities. These spaces will offer a secure environment for children to learn and thrive.2. Educational Materials and School SuppliesA significant portion of children in Askira/Uba—70%—lack the basic educational materials required for school, such as textbooks, exercise books, and writing tools. This problem is exacerbated by the limited financial capacity of families, with an average of only ₦250 ($0.32) spent monthly on education. As a result, children often do not attend school due to a lack of materials. This project will provide school kits to 2,440 children, ensuring they have access to the necessary tools to engage in education.3. Gender-Sensitive Education InterventionsGirls face additional barriers to education, including security threats, early marriage, and gender-based violence (GBV). In Borno, only 35% of girls are enrolled in school, compared to 52% of boys. Moreover, 24% of GBV survivors in Borno are children, many of whom are school-aged girls. The project will address these issues by distributing menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits, creating safe spaces for girls, and launching advocacy campaigns to promote girls’ education and combat harmful gender norms.4. Teacher Capacity and Psychosocial SupportTeachers in Askira/Uba often lack the skills and training necessary to address the specific needs of children affected by conflict. Over 85% of teachers have not received training in psychosocial support (PSS) or child protection, which are crucial in a post-conflict environment. This project will provide specialized training for teachers on conflict-sensitive education, PSS, and child protection, empowering them to better support the emotional and educational needs of their students.5. Community Engagement and AdvocacyCommunity attitudes toward education, particularly for girls, remain a significant challenge. 60% of parents in Askira/Uba lack awareness of the long-term benefits of education, with many pushing their children, especially girls, into early marriage or domestic labor instead of schooling. This project will engage communities through advocacy campaigns, community dialogues, and collaboration with religious and traditional leaders to raise awareness about the importance of education and encourage school enrollment and retention.6. Rebuilding Educational ResilienceDue to the ongoing conflict, many children in Askira/Uba have missed over a year of schooling, further exacerbated by flooding that damaged 12 schools in late 2023. To address this, the project will introduce remedial education programs and literacy activities for children who have missed significant learning, alongside the establishment of libraries stocked with diverse reading materials to encourage self-directed learning and foster a culture of education.By addressing these critical needs, this project aims to provide 2,440 children in Askira/Uba with access to inclusive, quality education.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The Humanitarian-Development-Peacebuilding (HDP) package is a comprehensive approach designed to address the interconnected challenges faced by conflict-affected communities. Its goal is to foster sustainable development by simultaneously addressing humanitarian needs, promoting long-term development, and supporting peacebuilding efforts. The HDP approach recognizes that these three pillars—humanitarian action, development programming, and peacebuilding—are deeply interconnected and must be addressed together to achieve sustainable solutions in fragile contexts.Components of the HDP Package1. Humanitarian Assistance: Providing urgent, lifesaving support to populations in crisis, including food, shelter, healthcare, and protection. This component focuses on alleviating the immediate needs of vulnerable populations, particularly in conflict zones.2. Development Programs: Long-term interventions designed to address the root causes of poverty, inequality, and underdevelopment. These programs focus on sustainable solutions, including education, livelihoods, and infrastructure development.3. Peacebuilding Initiatives: Efforts to prevent violent conflict, promote social cohesion, and build resilient communities. This includes peace dialogues, conflict resolution, and community-driven peacebuilding activities.Key Actors in the HDP Package• Humanitarian Organizations: UN agencies (e.g., UNHCR, WFP, UNFPA), NGOs, and local organizations that provide emergency aid and protection.• Development Partners: UNDP, World Bank, and other development agencies working on long-term recovery, infrastructure, and economic development.• Peacebuilding Organizations: Local peacebuilding groups, international actors such as the UN Peacebuilding Support Office, and governments involved in conflict mediation and reconciliation efforts.Interaction of the Project with the HDP PackageThis project aligns with the HDP package by addressing both the immediate educational needs of children affected by conflict (humanitarian response) and supporting the long-term development of educational infrastructure (development component). In addition, the project fosters social cohesion and peacebuilding by promoting inclusive education, particularly for girls, and engaging communities to address barriers to education and participation.1. Humanitarian Assistance:The project’s activities, such as the distribution of school kits and the establishment of makeshift classrooms, provide immediate relief to children affected by the conflict in Askira/Uba and Kaga LGAs. This aligns with humanitarian goals to alleviate the immediate educational needs of displaced children.2. Development Programs:The establishment of libraries, teacher training, and advocacy campaigns contribute to the long-term development of education infrastructure, empowering communities to improve educational access and outcomes. By focusing on educational retention and quality, the project supports the broader development goals of the HDP package.3. Peacebuilding:By providing children with education in a safe and inclusive environment, the project indirectly contributes to peacebuilding efforts. Educating children, especially girls, creates opportunities for social cohesion and the prevention of future conflict. The project also fosters community involvement, which strengthens local peacebuilding initiatives and enhances the capacity of communities to address conflicts non-violently.Collaboration with Key Actors:The project will collaborate with key actors within the HDP framework, including humanitarian agencies like UNICEF and UNHCR, development partners such as UNDP, and local peacebuilding organizations. These actors will help ensure that the project addresses not only the immediate needs of the children but also contributes to long-term development and peacebuilding goals in Borno State.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming in this project, we will implement the following modalities:1. Gender-Sensitive ProgrammingAll project activities, including the establishment of libraries, makeshift classrooms, and distribution of school kits, will be designed with a focus on the unique needs of both boys and girls. Gender-sensitive spaces will be created, ensuring safe and inclusive learning environments, with segregated facilities when necessary to protect the privacy and dignity of girls.2. Child Protection and Psychosocial SupportTeachers and community members will receive training on child protection, positive discipline, and psychosocial support to ensure the safety and well-being of children. This will help in addressing any emotional and psychological needs arising from the impact of conflict and displacement.3. Community Engagement and AwarenessFour community awareness campaigns will be conducted to promote the importance of education, raise awareness on protection issues, and encourage the safe enrollment of girls in schools. These campaigns will specifically address cultural barriers and promote positive community attitudes toward girls' education and child protection.4. Feedback MechanismsTo ensure accountability and child protection, the project will establish feedback mechanisms such as community dialogue meetings, suggestion boxes, and confidential reporting channels. These will allow children, teachers, and community members to voice concerns and ensure the project is responsive to the needs of the affected populations.5. Monitoring and EvaluationProtection-related outcomes will be monitored through regular assessments to track the safety and well-being of children, with a particular focus on girls. This will help ensure that protection issues are identified and addressed promptly, and adjustments are made to the project as necessary.These modalities will ensure that the project’s interventions not only meet educational goals but also protect and empower vulnerable children, particularly girls, in a holistic and sustainable manner.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, Integrated Women and Youths Empowerment Centre (IWAYEC) will implement a variety of inclusive, accessible, and safe feedback mechanisms:1. Community Feedback Sessions: Regular, structured feedback sessions will be organized in accessible and safe locations, ensuring representation from marginalized groups, particularly women, children, persons with disabilities, and survivors of gender-based violence. These sessions will be facilitated by trained IWAYEC staff to ensure a welcoming environment for open discussions.2. Focus Group Discussions (FGDs): FGDs will be held with vulnerable groups (e.g., women, girls, persons with disabilities) to gather detailed feedback on the project's effectiveness and inclusivity. These discussions will provide a safe space for sharing concerns and suggestions.3. Hotlines and SMS Feedback Channels: IWAYEC will establish a confidential feedback hotline and SMS service, enabling community members to share concerns remotely, ensuring accessibility for individuals who may be unable to attend physical sessions.4. Surveys and Questionnaires: Digital and paper-based surveys will be distributed to assess the quality, impact, and inclusivity of project interventions. These surveys will be designed to gather anonymous feedback from the most vulnerable populations.5. Complaint Boxes: Complaint boxes will be placed in community spaces (e.g., schools, health centers) to allow individuals to submit anonymous feedback confidentially.6. Disaggregation of Data: Feedback will be disaggregated by gender, age, disability status, and vulnerability factors to ensure the voices of specific groups, such as women, girls, and persons with disabilities, are adequately captured.7. Community-Based Feedback Committees: Feedback committees consisting of community representatives will review collected feedback and suggest project adjustments based on community input.
