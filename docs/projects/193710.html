Project Summary

Through this project, QRCS aims at supporting primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention services for breast cancer patients in the Gaza Strip, in partnership with PMRS. QRCS and PMRS will coordinate with the Ministry of Health and the Palestinian Turkish Friendship Hospital in all of the project phases. QRCS and PMRS will conduct community awareness activities to benefit 43215 beneficiaries (both women and men). In addition, QRCS will provide the MoH and TPFH with cancer drugs for 350 breast cancer patients to cover their needs for a year. Also, PMRS will conduct diagnostic tests for 300 breast cancer patients. Furthermore, QRCS will provide MoH with 30 breast prostheses for 30 women who had mastectomies.

The proposed intervention is in line with strategic objective 2 and is aligned with Cluster Objective 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPT. In addition, the proposed intervention is in line with SDG3: Good health and well-being and meets SDG3, target 4: Reduce mortality from non-communicable diseases and promote mental health.
The project activities are built on the public health model of prevention that aims at providing comprehensive services to prevent risk, manage diseases, and reduce the impact of diseases.
QRCS and PMRS will conduct community awareness activities that aim at raising awareness about screening and early detection among women and men. The community awareness activities will target both women and men in the five governorates. They will be tailored to fit all of the community groups by using visual media tools, audio media tools, printed materials, and direct communication with the beneficiaries. Furthermore, QRCS and PMRS will consider the different educational levels of the beneficiaries and the gender-sensitive language, in addition to reinforcing a violence-free environment. The total number of beneficiaries of the community awareness activities is 43215 (21410 are women, 19525 are men, and 2280 are PwD).
QRCS will supply MoH and TPFH with cancer medications from the registered drug list including chemotherapy, targeted therapy, hormones, and analgesia for the benefit of 350 breast cancer patients (all are women). Moreover, PMRS will conduct diagnostic tests for 300 breast cancer patients (all are women) who will be referred from MoH and TPFH for diagnostic investigations. Those activities are expected to mitigate the severe shortage of drugs and diagnostic tests and decrease the referral rate for treatment abroad.
As part of "building back better" for breast cancer patients, QRCS will procure 30 breast prostheses for 30 women who underwent mastectomies in order to improve their psychological status and quality of life accordingly. This activity is directly related to the mental health of women subjected to additional psychosocial sufferings and aims at reducing negative emotions (helplessness, hopelessness, low mood) and improving their social status.
Responding to any unexpected event (emerging disease, re-emerging diseases, escalation on Gaza), QRCS and PMRS will prepare a contingency plan that includes a clear risk assessment and management framework. Regarding community awareness activities, QRCS and PMRS will use online methods (social media, online awareness sessions…). In addition, QRCS will coordinate with international and national bodies to ensure the delivery of the drugs, breast prostheses, and diagnostic tests without delay.

1 - Needs

The healthcare system of the Gaza Strip faces a real challenge to provide essential healthcare services in light of a catastrophic humanitarian situation that is driven by limited resources and 16 years of access restrictions. According to MSNA 2022, 89.6% of Gaza households reported a member of their household having a healthcare need requiring care, and 99.8% of these households reported that they face barriers to care. The most commonly reported barrier to care was the cost of services being too high (experienced by 76.8% of households), followed by households who reported that treatment was not available (22.1%) and who reported medicines were not available (19.4%). In addition, HNO 2022 estimated the total number of people in need of health assistance is 1.5 million; 51% of them are male, while 49% are female. According to the Health Annual Report- MoH 2022, cancer is the third leading cause of death among the population, represented by 12.5% of total deaths. The incidence of cancer increased from 89/100000 in 2016 to 91.3/100000 in 2021. In addition, MoH reported 1952 new cases diagnosed with cancer in 2021, and 610 new deaths in the same year. Breast cancer is the most common type of cancer in Gaza contributing to 18.6% of all cancers in both women and men and 34.3% of cancers among women. In 2021, MoH reported 363 new cases of breast cancer with an incidence rate of 17/100000 compared to 300 new cases in 2018. Interestingly, 16% of the newly discovered cases were women younger than 40-year-old, and 36% of them were younger than 50-year-old. Moreover, breast cancer deaths represent 13% of all cancer deaths according to MoH statistics in October 2022. HNO 2023 estimated the number of people in need of cancer management to be 13,800 patients; 2567 are breast cancer patients. Given the high incidence of breast cancer in Gaza, it is vital to make stronger focus on early detection of breast cancer, which would include but not be limited to mammography screening, and raising awareness among the public -both women and men- on the importance of early examination, as recommended by the United Nations Population Fund (2016).Breast cancer patients face several challenges that add additional suffering to cancer patients' hard life. Those challenges include the severe shortage of drugs and medicines that causes discontinuation of treatment protocols and increases the medical complications among them. The pharmaceutical report that was released in August 2022 revealed that 28 items out of 63 (44%) are zero-stock and 5 items (7.9%) are sufficient only for less than 3 months at the central drug store which threatens the life of cancer patients directly. Moreover, cancer services are not fully available in the Gaza Strip, so MoH refers breast cancer patients for treatment abroad either for diagnostic tests, radiotherapy, PET scan, chemotherapy, or other unavailable treatment modalities. In 2021, a total number of 5327 cancer patients were referred abroad representing 29% of total referred cases (17,982). Gaza patients -in general- face hard challenges when referred abroad due to movement restrictions, permits denial, and low affordability which increases their vulnerability. Furthermore, breast cancer patients face social challenges due to gender inequalities that subject them to emotional, physical, and psychological violence and denial of their rights and resources. Also, women who suffer from breast cancer face social stigma which limits their accessibility to the already limited accessibility, thus worsening their health status, psychosocial status, and quality of life accordingly. Specifically, women who had mastectomy surgery suffer severely from low-confidence, hopelessness, helplessness, lack of support from partners, and inferiority feeling which make their lives even worse. This intervention responds to the Health Cluster Objective no.1: “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”, which links to the Second Strategic Objective: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under international humanitarian law".

2 - Activities

Through this project, QRCS aims at supporting primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention services for breast cancer patients in the Gaza Strip, in partnership with PMRS. The project activities are built on the public health model of prevention that aims at providing comprehensive services to prevent risk, manage diseases, and reduce the impact of diseases. Starting from primary prevention, QRCS and PMRS will conduct community awareness activities that include 30 awareness sessions in the five governorates of the Gaza Strip. The awareness sessions will target both women and men from the marginalized areas of the five governorates in order to contribute to raising the awareness of the whole community. In addition, QRCS and PMRS will produce 1 social media infographic promotion that focuses on the importance of screening and early detection and reducing the stigma. The infographic will be distributed through social media platforms to reach out to a wide range of audience. In addition, QRCS and PMRS will produce 3 radio spots and 2 radio sessions that aim at raising knowledge about the early detection and screening and reinforcing resilience and positive coping mechanisms. Furthermore, QRCS and PMRS will conduct a campaign in the breast cancer month- Pink October that includes raising awareness about screening and early detection, reducing the stigma toward breast cancer patients, and creating a supportive environment. Moreover, PMRS will produce and distribute 5000 brochures that clarify the risk factors, the importance of early detection and screening, the positive coping mechanisms, and the self-examination manner. PMRS will distribute the brochures in public areas, primary healthcare centers, PMRS healthcare centers, and other centers. Furthermore, PMRS will conduct an orientation day for the oncologists and hematologists in the MoH in order to introduce the project activities to them especially the referral for free diagnostic tests at PMRS. The community awareness activities will be tailored to fit all population groups across the five governorates by using audio, visual, and printed media tools. In addition, the awareness sessions will be conducted in CBOs that are fully adapted for people with disabilities. Responding to any unexpected event (emerging disease, re-emerging diseases, escalation in Gaza), QRCS and PMRS will prepare a contingency plan that includes a clear risk assessment and management framework. Regarding community awareness activities, QRCS and PMRS will use online methods (social media, online awareness sessions…). Regarding the secondary preventive activities, QRCS will supply the MoH with drugs and medicines that are part of breast cancer management protocols. Those drugs include vital chemotherapy medications, hormones, targeted therapy, biological drugs, and analgesia of the most needed categories. The drugs and medicines are expected to benefit 350 of breast cancer patients over a year. Drugs and medicines will be distributed based on the management protocols of each patient regardless of their beliefs, backgrounds, and social class. This activity will contribute to alleviating the shortage of medicines in the MoH in the Gaza Strip. In addition, PMRS will conduct diagnostic tests for 300 breast cancer patients that are not available in the MoH. Those interventions will mitigate the financial burden on breast cancer patients and their families by providing cancer medicines locally, saving them the hassle and costs of traveling to receive treatment abroad. As part of the emergency contingency plan, QRCS will ensure the supply of medications and diagnostic tests through coordination with international and national organizations without delay. The tertiary preventive activities are considered as part of "building back better" for the survivors of breast cancer who underwent breast removal surgery. QRCS will provide the MoH with 30 breast prostheses for 30 breast cancer patients who had mastectomies and are candidates for breast prostheses. Although this could be considered a cosmetic intervention, it is very vital for the life of women who are subjected to the negative psychosocial consequences of breast cancer. The aforementioned activities will be implemented in full coordination with the Ministry of Health and the Palestinian Turkish Friendship Hospital, starting from holding introductory meetings, preparing the cancer medication list and specifications of the breast prostheses, participating in the tender process, receiving the medications and the breast prostheses, distribution of medications to the targeted patients and following up and ensuring the delivery of service to the beneficiaries.It is worth mentioning that QRCS has a well-experienced staff and its own procurement system that is intended to facilitate work and speed up the delivery of the supplies. QRCS takes into consideration the quality of supplies, duration for delivery, and prices adopting international integrity and transparency standards. In addition, QRCS has an extended program that has been going on for years to support cancer services in the Gaza Strip with the coordination of MoH. Furthermore, PMRS has a health promotion department that is well-skilled and has extended expertise in community awareness activities considering the use of different and adapted media tools to respond to the needs of people with disabilities.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

QRCS and PMRS adopt an open-door policy that focuses on open communication between the organization and the beneficiaries. As breast cancer patients are considered vulnerable due to their health and psychosocial status, they tend not to report their feedback directly, so QRCS and PMRS try to use active and passive reporting methods. As part of the passive methods, QRCS and PMRS use complaint boxes and generic emails in order to assure a proper address of the beneficiaries' concerns. Regarding the active data collection, QRCS and PMRS conduct field visits and individual interviews with the beneficiaries and health workers in order to go deeper into their perspectives and concerns. The field visits and individual interviews are used for two main purposes, the first one aims at informing breast cancer patients and healthcare workers about their rights to speak out, the importance of their voices, the feedback mechanisms, and how the organizations can respond. The second one aims at collecting data about the beneficiaries' concerns, complaints, recommendations, and suggestions. Moreover, QRCS and PMRS plan to use a satisfaction survey in order to collect quantitative data. QRCS and PMRS document the feedback from the beneficiaries according to organizations' templates and refer them to the related personnel to deal with. QRCS and PMRS deal with any feedback according to the internal accountability policy ensuring proper addressing of the feedback in a timely, ethical, dignity-saving and humanitarian manner. In addition, the beneficiaries' feedback represents a core part of the project evaluation, and QRCS and PMRS take into account the client's feedback in future project designs. In emergency settings, QRCS and PMRS will continue collecting feedback and responding to them via using alternative online methods including (generic email, and online meetings….).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

QRCS and PMRS have developed organizational policies- based on international and local protocols- to assure the maximum benefits and the minimum harm to the beneficiaries. Those policies emphasize on the safety of the beneficiaries as a top priority including the safe geographical location, adapted and free-violence environment, and minimum threats. QRCS and PMRS follow strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed and reject items that don’t match the technical criteria and may cause any harm to the beneficiaries. All medications that will be provided through this project are registered in MoH lists, stated in the local guidelines and protocols, with assured effectiveness and are already used in hospitals. No new medications will be introduced, so no harm from the unknown effects of drugs can occur. The awareness activities will be conducted in well-known CBOs in the five governorates in order to decrease the possible transportation risk, especially for PwDs and women. In addition, QRCS and PMRS highly consider the adaptability and the cultural accessibility to respond to the cultural backgrounds of the beneficiaries. The working staff includes both women and men and is trained in using gender-sensitive language. Furthermore, there are clear lines for privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff, and there are clear rules for data collection that include; not collecting unneeded information nor sharing information with any except the working staff. If any risk to the beneficiaries is shown, QRCS and PMRS will act in a timely manner to protect their safety and dignity.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

QRCS and PMRS are independent organizations that are not affected by commercial or political powers. QRCS follows the seven standards of the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent in humanitarian action (IFRC) to assure the equitable distribution of services to the vulnerable population regardless of any other considerations such as gender, age, religion, etc. At the start of the project, QRCS and PMRS will discuss the possible barriers to equity, and they will prepare a contingency plan to mitigate those barriers and assure equal opportunities. The contingency plan will be followed at all phases of the project alongside the M&E plan. In addition, QRCS and PMRS try to adapt the project activities to respond to the special needs of vulnerable people in order to maximize equity. Regarding the awareness activities, QRCS and PMRS will conduct the awareness activities in the five governorates using different media tools; the visual tool, the audio tool, the printed materials, and direct communication with the beneficiaries. In addition, QRCS and PMRS will use several tools of feedback mechanisms to assure equitable opportunities to report any concern. The feedback tools will include verbal tools, written tools, social media tools, and direct communication with the beneficiaries. In addition, QRCS and PMRS will provide the medications and the diagnostic tests to all women targeted in the project regardless of their social status, disability status, or beliefs. To assure equitable access to medicines and diagnostic tests, QRCS will check the lists of beneficiaries received from MoH and PMRS, and QRCS will respond to any inequity concerns from the beneficiaries in a timely effective manner. Furthermore, during the tender process, QRCS will ensure a transparent process according to its guideline without leaning to any commercial power.
