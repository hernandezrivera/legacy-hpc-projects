Project Summary

Under GBV Sub-Sector, World Vision is applying in this JRP 2024 as an appealing partner without any implementing partner for Improving & strengthening the protection environment against Gender-based Violence (GBV) at refugee camp and host community. This project will provide survivor-centered GBV support services to GBV survivors and those at high risk of GBV in Rohingya refugee Camp-13 (Block-A, B, G), 15 (Block-B, Sub Block-B2), 19 (Block A & B) and the host community, Pallongkhali Union, next to Camp-19. Along with this, the project intends to strengthen the protection environment against GBV by disseminating GBV basic knowledge and developing community leaders, including religious leaders, and those who show the potential to become leaders, being trained as key actors in GBV prevention and response.
JRP 2024 World Vision is focusing on the objective of “enhance the access to survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing, and mitigating GBV risks, and supporting survivors of GBV in the Rohingya refugee/FDMN camps and targeted areas in host communities”, World Vision not only focused on GBV response but also focused on GBV risk minimize, sustainability and transformation by following the GBV minimum standard. To achieve the mention objective bellow activities will be providing:
GBV Case management- World Vision will provide Case management survivor-centered service that will focus survivors individual needs in accordance with a global standard guideline provided by Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) & GBV sub-sector's guidelines.Through GBV facilities GBV Case Management & referral service, access to life saving services like clinical Management of Rape (CMR) etc. will be providing to survivor including unaccompanied & separated & children at risk. Awareness raising & risk mitigation services will be in place to reduce forced marriage, trafficking, sexual violence, mental health service.
GBV Awareness Raising & Risk mitigation- World Vision will use include promoting gender equality and women's empowerment, providing education and training on consent and healthy relationships, implementing laws and policies that protect survivors and hold perpetrators accountable, and ensuring access to support services for survivors. Risk mitigation also will be involving in addressing the root causes of GBV, such as Risk Mapping by conducting FGD with Rohingya and host community people, gender inequality, social norms that condone violence, and lack of economic opportunities for women.
Capacity building on GBV response and Prevention-Training and monthly follow up meeting is part of volunteer capacity building. World Vision also will focus the staffs capacity building training for the project staff, like- COH, COC, GBV Case Management, GBV basic training, GBVIMS+primero, PSS & PFA training, life skill curriculum, GBV Case Management including child & male survivor, LGBTQI, MHPSS etc. training also will be ensured. Capacity building training for non-protection staff, mediation, dispute resolution mechanism, local & community representative, health, legal, CiC/Govt. officials, Women lead organizations etc. on protection mainstreaming will be ensured.
Social Behavioral change to transform social norms- The Community Leader have conscious about violence against women, dowry, child protection and GBV. They alerted the surrounding people about GBV, Human trafficking, early marriage, violence against women etc. World Vision will promote Women Leader in this program to pave way for sustainability with moving transformation. Structural & non-structural prevention activities like CoC, EMAP, Girl Shine, Male Engagement, SASA together! Will be providing for social behavior change.
Moreover, multiple events will be organized during the 16 days of activism, int. Mother’s Day, Int. Father’s Day, Int. Women Day, Youth day, Suicide prevention Day etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya living in the camp of Cox’s Bazar District are exposed to severe protection threats on a quotidian basis which includes violence, physical hazards, and exploitation. With higher rate of anti-social behavior, violence in Rohingya Refugee context female, adolescent girls & people with disability are in higher risk & negatively impacted. Especially girls (age 12 to 18) become exposed to child marriage, teenage pregnancy, mental health problem, IPV (age 20 to 24), sexual abuse, exploitation and violence. According to GBVIMS factsheet Q3 2023, number of reported incidents of GBV has increased in Q3 compared to Q2 2023, 0.7% survivors are living with disabilities, 79% of the survivors are Refugee & 21% are Host. Among all types of GBV, physical assault was the most (49.1%) reported type, followed by psychological & emotional abuse 22.7% Lack of awareness, practical social norms, child marriage, lack of services, gender unfairness restrict women's rights and opportunities. Women with disability are often considered weak, worthless and subhuman by the societies- because of that they face heightened risk of domestic and sexual violence.A correlation has been seen between protection risks and the level and quality of access to basic services, in particular for women, adolescent girls, older persons, pregnant women, children and persons with disabilities based on community consultations. Moreover, refugees have limited information on protection-related assistance and services. The dissemination of this information is usually focused on male-dominated community leadership while the other communities have less knowledge about the available resources like reporting incidents or complaints, or where to seek assistance. Lack of knowledge of this information is more prevalent among those who are more isolated by sociocultural factors, limited mobility and gender discrimination issues against women and girls. These people are marginalized from camp governance and decision-making, as well as in community economic and social life. Gender-based violence (GBV) constitutes one of the main protection risks within the refugee community and a severe human rights violation. Women and girls have been constantly experiencing sexual violence since the prosecution started in Myanmar and are still being exposed to it in the camps. While in displacement, they continue to be at risk of GBV, particularly intimate partner violence, forced marriage and polygamy as well as trafficking and sexual exploitation. There are challenges in meeting minimum standards to mitigate the risk of gender-based violence across sectors, meaning that women and girls feel unsafe in their communities and in accessing essential services. Involved in organized human trafficking, drug trafficking using girls, and sexual exploitation, gun fire, unrest situation etc. along with GBV incidents are occurring between the refugee and host communities.

World Vision GBV project will be focusing on case management services as well as focused on self-care, prevention, awareness, Girl shine and life skill for adolescent that meet individual needs. World Vision also mainstreaming with child protection case management and MHPSS. World vision is the child focused agency the valued child protection related services. In the time of providing services World Vision will be careful to prioritize individual need, safety, confidentiality, respect and response. Ensuring age and disability is the mainstream intervention in WVB Rohingya response programming. Key activities will be: 1. Assessing (Screening and identifying) age-disaggregated persons with disabilities using Washington Group Questions. 2. Providing need-based support services for persons (learners) with disabilities (12% as per ISCG). 3. Providing training on PWD for staff, volunteers, Self Help Group (SHG). 4. Conducting individual service delivery planning and sessions for person with disabilities. 5. Ensuring gender-friendly and accessible IEC materials. 6. Creating opportunities for inclusive recreational activities for girls & women with and without disabilities. 7. Providing home-based service support as per the needs of the projected populations. 8. Ensuring community groups/committee including PWD. 9. Ensuring WGSS with age (12-18, 19-59, 60+), & PWD inclusive facilities including pathway, WASH, sitting arrangement, learning tools & materials, etc. 10. Ensuring age and disability disaggregated reporting. Regardless all age groups (children, adolescent girls & boys, men & women and elder including PWD) will be covered by the World Vision Bangladesh accordingly.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

World Vision has the Humanitarian Program Accountability Framework and Accountability Operating Guideline aligning with global standards covering CHS, IASC, etc. Following organizational policy each year WVB conducts an annual multi-sector need assessment (MSNA) and accountability & safeguarding assessment where AAP needs and recommended actions are captured. Projected people's needs will be given priority without any discrimination or bias. A detailed AAP plan and progress tracker will be followed over the project duration. Standard Operating Procedures and tools on AAP will be developed and agreed with all key stakeholders. Key activities will be 1. Strengthening the capacity of all staff, volunteers, and community leaders on AAP, 2. Selecting beneficiaries inclusively following agreed criteria through engaging community peoples, 3. Providing project & AAP related and asked information among community peoples, 4. Consultation meetings with all relevant stakeholders including beneficiaries will be conducted, 5. Promoting community peoples’ participation in implementation, monitoring & maintenance through volunteerism, community platforms like women watch group, community protection group etc. 6. Establishing community preferred feedback & complaint channels (feedback box, help desk, face-to-face interviews in the community, hotline, etc.) including feedback registers. 7. Conducting learning workshops with all relevant stakeholders, 8. Conduct joint monitoring, 9. Providing community-friendly IEC, BCC materials focused on beneficiaries’ entitlement, project activities, beneficiary criteria, free assistance facility, feedback & complaint mechanism, frequently asked & answer tool, etc. 10. Disseminating AAP-related messages, 11. Continuous monitoring and improvement of AAP activities.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

WV has the PSEA policy and membership of the PSEA network. WV BRCR has a PSEA focal and it has a protection and integrity hotline globally & managed by a third party. The project will promote an environment free from discrimination, harassment, and exploitation; uphold the principles of gender equality and non-discrimination in all project activities; and work closely with community leaders, women's groups, and local authorities to prevent and address any PSEA concerns. Key actions will be as- 1. The hiring department will check all necessaries related to safeguarding in the recruitment process, 2. All staff, community facilitators, vendors, CMC members, and partners have been capacitated with training or orientation on PSEA, 3. Develop and disseminate PSEA awareness materials (such as posters, flyers, and videos) including key PSEA messages, 4. Appropriate and available Complaint Feedback Mechanisms will be established and functional, 5. Appropriate referral pathways and referral issues will be coordinated efficiently. According to PSEA policy framework of WVB there are three levels of PSEA incidents and there stated response protocol clearly. WV Bangladesh Entities must investigate and respond to reports of child or adult beneficiary abuse in WV Bangladesh programs in accordance with local law.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WVB has a disaster preparedness and response plan for the Rohingya Response Program. WVB has also three cyclone shelters in Cox’s Bazar. Already WVB has implemented DRR projected activities in both Teknaf & Ukhiya upazillas covering both Camp & host communities partnering with WFP. The key activity under this education sector will be orientation for projected community peoples including Facilitators & community groups’ members on Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) issues. The project will conduct a brief environmental examination (BEE) for each intervention before execution. The project will arrange awareness and campaign programs, and day observation to make the community aware of disaster response and reduction and prepare the mitigation strategies. IEC materials and messaging will be also ensured aiming to increase awareness of DRR. The project will provide dignity kits among the affected people following needs.
