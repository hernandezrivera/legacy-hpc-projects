Project Summary

The project aims to reach the mostly vulnerable group of people including Pregnant and Lactating Women and Children Under the age of Five with rapid nutrition and nutrition sensitive lifesaving interventions of early detection, referral, and treatment of malnourished throughout preventative and curative activities of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, Infant and Young Children Feeding in Emergencies, Integrated Management of Childhood illnesses, in a close linkage to the nearby Health and Nutrition centers providing Targeted Supplementary Food Program, Outpatient Therapeutic Program, and Inpatient Care services to the Acutely Malnourished cases. Moreover, a tightened involvement of awareness raising of maternal and children nutrition and health will be scaled up by the dissemination of integrated key messages of WaSH, Health education, COVID-19, and optimal feeding during the first 1000 days of child's life throughout the deployed Nutrition Rapid Response Teams that will be operational in the Districts of Idleb, Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, A'zaz, Jarablus, Al Bab, and Jebel Samaan.
BINAA Organization for Development will support the Nutrition Programs throughout the deployment of 45 Rapid Response Teams in 7 Districts, 13 Sub-Districts, 107 Communities, and 1077 Camps from the high-level severity areas. Additionally, 7 Mobile OTP centers in the targeted Districts will be accompanying the RRTs for the treatment of Acutely Malnourished cases.
425,716 individuals are targeted to be reached through the response, 274,669 of them are females including the pregnant, lactating women and girls, and 151,047 of them are males. Almost 6,195 PWD are targeted among the CU-5 and PLWGs. In the selected areas.

4 - Needs

4.9 Million girls and boys under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating womenare at risk of undernutrition and in need of comprehensive curative and preventivenutrition services to continue during 2021. An estimated 141,540 children aged 6-59 months face an elevated risk of morbidity and mortality attributed to acute malnutrition, out of which 37,863 children are under the direct risk of death because of severe acute malnutrition and additional 103,677 moderately malnourished children are at risk of co-morbidities and development of severe acute malnutrition if they are not treated appropriately.And almost 600,000 children might lose their future learning and developmental potentials because of chronic malnutrition where chronic malnutrition was a problem inSyria even before the crisis and increased rates have been observed recently. Women andchildren living in hard-to-reach areas, overburdened communities, and those ‘one themove’ deprived of basic services are in urgent need of nutrition interventions to ensurethat their nutritional status remains stable and improves. Considering aggravating factorssuch as COVID-19 pandemic, the ongoing displacement, anticipated returns, change in control, challenging accessto nutrition/health services and food, high food prices, diminishing employmentopportunities and low service coverage of nutrition partners, are forecasting an increase inundernutrition cases especially in hard-to-reach areas, where the severity is classified with high levels.

5 - Implementation of activities

BINAA Organization for Development planning to strengthen maternal and child nutrition services for PLW and CU-5 with IYCF-E services, Micronutrient supplementation throughout the activities of:• Community based 1-2-1 counselling on optimal IYFC-E practices.• Community involvement of the supporting the adherence to criteria of BMS distribution.• Blanket distribution to MMNs tablets to PLW and MNPs to the children 6-59 months.• Blanket distribution of preventative LNS to the children 6-36 months.• Distribution of HEBs and emergency rations of BP-5 biscuits to the newly displaced mothers and children 6-59 months.Additionally, BINAA Organization for Development will improve the access to the curative services of Acutely Malnourished cases throughout:• Early identification and referral of Acutely Malnourished cases.• Provision of community-based treatment of the Moderate to severe cases without complications under the medical supervision.• Facilitate the transportation to the severe cases with complication to the inpatient care centers with a prior coordination with the SCs and OTP centers.Finally, BINAA Organization for Development will build the capacity of the staff working in nutrition programs for IYCF-E protocols, the simplified guidelines of CMAM, Identification and referral of danger signs, PSEA, and social violence, Personal Protection of COVID-19 outbreak.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Syria is the country where the most major attacks took place during the last 2 years. And Community health workers in humanitarian aid face a unique constellation of risks regarding both potential perpetrators of violence and the context/location of attacks. Moreover, the risks of non-renewal of UN resolution in Syria becomes very critical due to the dependency of humanitarian assistance in NWS on the border of Bab Al Hawa where this assistance is supplied almost entirely via this operation,The outreach nutrition activities are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical thatNutrition service providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory,quality services. Mitigating measures: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package. -Specialized nutrition and outreach health staff should be trained on providing first-line support. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting nutrition and health services and clinical management with social services, and privacy of the survivor. -Presence of female staff increase access of women and girls to Nutrition services.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: is to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aidproviders come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community.Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The Nutrition sector and Community Nutirtion workers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both onorganizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about theorganization.
