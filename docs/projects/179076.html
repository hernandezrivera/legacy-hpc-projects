Project Summary

Given the humanitarian needs in Syria, EPDC is planning to conduct a project with a total budget of 922,015 USD (1 USD = 2500 SYP according to the Central Bank of Syria) for a period of 12 months from January 1st to December 31st, 2021. The proposed response will be in Rural Damascus in the following locations: Arbin, Az-Zabdani, Ma’loula, and Sidnaya. The proposed intervention will response to the needs enlisted under the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) in line with priorities of protection, environment, and cross cutting issues. This project will provide livelihoods and support to vulnerable communities in Rural Damascus governorate to improve their ability to respond to shocks and stressors in the future. EPDC will provide agricultural Voucher, training and technical support to Medium-Sized for 700 Farmers, and support for 400 households through training of project components, environment analysis, market study and technical study trainings and distribution of MSMEs income-generating equipment (livestock). The intervention will respond in total to the estimated 1,100 households/ 6,600 individuals (1,583 W, 1,057 M, 2,376 G, 1,584 B) through two major activities detailed as follows: 1. distribution of agricultural input (seeds) using voucher modality (indicator 5.1) to 700 households/ 4200 individuals (1,008 W, 672 M, 1,512 G, 1,008 B); and 2. Agricultural income generating using MSMEs modality (indicator11.1) to 400 households/ 2400 (575 W, 385 M, 864 G, 576 B).
The beneficiaries will be selected according to: (1)Vulnerability criteria (Elderly headed HH- Female headed HH- Child headed HH with a poor nutritional status- at least one PLW in HH- >5 members in HH- at least one PWD, or chronic disease in HH-Displaced <6 months; (2) Selection criteria:
(i) Voucher: 50m2 – 500 m2 of owned land (according to SO2/SO3-B2)- not receiving any agricultural or kitchen garden assistance in this agricultural calendar season- having regular access to water for irrigation, or close to, their agricultural land- experience in agricultural work and producing crops in the past seasons- agriculture as the main source of income of the household- having access to sufficient, usable agricultural land for at least 2 agricultural seasons.
(ii) IGA: Existing business:
- Intended use of the grant according to their submitted action plan- Facing financial difficulties in expanding their business- The owner has no other regular source of income than the business.
Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), communication and participation and complaints/feedback mechanisms will be inclusive in the project cycle. Responses to Food Security and Agriculture is aligned with the HNO overview 2021, as well as with updates shared by the relevant Whole of Syria (WoS) Clusters and Working groups, encouraging revitalization of Syria’s social, economic and environmental space, while simultaneously mainstreaming key cross-cutting issues (human rights, conflict sensitivity and Do-No-Harm (DNH), women’s rights and gender equality, climate and environment and anti-corruption).
EPDC also foster this two-way communication with beneficiaries and local populations through: extensive and systematic community sensitization at the start of activities and throughout implementation, regular monitoring of the project implementation through both the MEAL and Program teams, and CRM. EPDC will ensure transparency with the targeted community in the provided services especially regarding the selection criteria and project objectives.
EPDC also will report on the progress of the activities under this project to the relevant sector, i.e., FSA sector and Cash Working Group (CWG), using the 5Ws tool and will participate effectively in the coordination meetings to ensure high coordination with other humanitarian actors and will share lesson learnt and experience. It is worth mentioning that EPDC has experience in the mentioned activities and locations given its good reputation and easy access.

4 - Needs

The crisis has been compounded by both the COVD-19 pandemic and the deterioration of the socio-economic situation, which saw the rapid devaluation of the Syrian currency during the summer of 2020 and early 2021. This has had significant impacts on all communities, increasing households’ vulnerabilities, and limiting access to basic items and services. According to HNO 2021, at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure. The instable economic situation has frustration effects on the southern and central governorates with queues to the basic and moving needs, e.g., bread, fuel. Agriculture is considered of the main income generation sources in Syria. However, this sector was frustrated as due to war remnants in the fields, seasonal fires, high cost and limited availability of seeds and related equipment, and deterioration of livelihoods.The people in the proposed locations lost some/most of their livelihoods and were burdened to secure their immediate needs i.e., shelter, water, and food, after the situation was settled in the areas. With the prices increase by 236% (HNO 2021), people were unable to plant their lands or develop their livelihoods. According to HNO 2021, the food situation among IDPs, vulnerable resident population and returnees are expected to remain poor throughout 2021 due to the degradation of livelihood and socioeconomic situation. Thus, the targeted community are in need of comprehensive approach to address their food insecurity through providing support to farmers via voucher (seeds) and income generating activity via MSMEs (livestock).The proposed locations were selected according to the severity score of the FSA sector (: Arbin, Ma’loula, and Sidnaya=3/Az-Zabdani=4).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project intends to help HHs secure their basic food needs and could sell them for profit, constituting an economic support. Also, it allows for more availability of agricultural products in the targeted areas. EPDC will distribute conditional value vouchers as it provides more choices to BNFs whilst supporting local market. The vouchers are designed according to EPDC previous experience and similar projects in Rural Damascus and other governorates. 5-6 kinds of vegetables seedlings/seeds according to the agricultural calendar, 10 kg of fertilizer, water tank and irrigation pipes. EPDC will conduct a needs and market assessment to determine the functionality of local markets, and determine where the vouchers for seeds will be redeemed. Farmers will also participate in trainings to increase their knowledge and expertise on climate-smart and sustainable agricultural practices.EPDC will support existing with IGA equal to (1000 USD). The grants will support BNFs ability to develop their businesses and increase their sustainability. A needs and value chain assessment in targeted market will be conducted, interested applicants will submit their application containing information on nature of their business. EPDC team will visit the applicants to confirm the accuracy of the information submitted to track the best use of what was distributed. A training will be conducted for BNFs on project management topics. They will review their original application and develop an action plan. IGA (livestock) will be distributed in single batch upon completion of training and submission of final workplan reviewed by EPDC team. BNFs receive IGA support through inkind support because purchase of livestock needs technical and vet experience.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Security level does not deteriorate to an extent that prevents EPDC teams from conducting the activities in the field- No major change of the current balance between parties at conflict leads to major upheavals in the targeted areas- No major security incident targeting EPDC personnel or other I/NNGOs or civilians- Field access remains granted by Syrian authorities- No major rise in the exchange rate and prices of materials remain stable- Procurement for vouchers done at the right timesRisks:- Planned activities pose environmental/ health risks to staff, stakeholders, or targeted communities and beneficiaries including the unintentional spread of COVID-19- Value of the Syrian Pound deteriorates to a level that activities cannot be implemented within the planned budget- Potential corruption or fraud- Planned activities pose negative impact on land and biodiversityMitigation Measures:- All activities will be planned and monitored closely in coordination with the local community to ensure no harm to beneficiaries. All EPDC staff and beneficiaries use adequate PPE, practice social distancing and instructions at the beginning of the trainings on COVID-19.- Maintain strict controls and application of Anti-Fraud policy and procurement and finance procedures.- Seeds and livestock are selected according to a needs assessment and market assessment and in coordination with the related official body and in line with FSA sector guidelines.- Procurement process ensures fair and transparent competition (Tender).- Start date of the project must be three months minimum before one of the two agricultural seasons (summer/winter).
