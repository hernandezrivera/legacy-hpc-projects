Project Summary

TF seeks to improve access to safe, lifesaving and appropriate S/NFIs to returnees, newly IDPs, and affected non displaced persons. The project will equip beneficiaries with skills and knowledge on disaster risk reduction to enhance their resilience to cope with new shocks. TF will also support affected communities with durable S/NFIs solutions to rebuild their lives including training women and girls on making Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES). Vulnerable youth will be trained on making bricks, beds, chairs and tables using local materials. S/NFIs Solutions training will be complemented with environment awareness to mitigate negative effects of climate change. The project will target Torit, Rumbek Center, Kajokeji, Yei, Morobo and Lainya, Magwi, Yirol West and Cueibet for GIK and CBI. GIK will target locations with new displacements and no functional markets while CBI will focus on locations experiencing spontaneous returnees, increased number of affected non-displaced people and have functional markets. For CBI, TF will work jointly with SPEDP, PAH and LCED. A total of 25000 (18000 F) beneficiaries will be reached with GIK and CBI intervention. For GIK, a total of 22000 beneficiaries are targeted as follows: Rumbek Center (2000), Torit (1900), Kajokeji (4500), Yei (2500), Morobo (2100), Lainya (3000), Magwi (3000), and Cueibet (3000). For CBI intervention, a total of 3000 beneficiaries are targeted as follows: Rumbek Centre (700), Yei (600), Torit (900), Magwi (400), and Cueibet (400). Totals for each county being as follows: Torit: 2800(1800 F); Rumbek Centre: 2700(2200 F) Kajokeji: 4500 (3000 F); Yei: 3100 (2000 F); Morobo: 2100 (1500 F); Lainya: 3000 (2300 F); Magwi: 3400 (2500 F) and Cueibet: 3400 (2700 F). Due to high level and concentration of S/NFIs needs in these locations, TF project staffs will dedicate 8 weeks in each location with the communities to better understand their pressing S/NFIs needs and coping mechanisms, jointly craft alternative S/NFIs durable solutions, train community based S/NFIs Committees on resilience-enhancing messaging such as Housing Land and Property (HLP). The project prioritizes safety, dignity, inclusive participation and encouraging complaints and feedback. A key focus for this intervention is AAP through CFM embedded in project activities. CFM will be established upon contact with the affected community and during implementation process to receive, acknowledge and address complaints. Protection is mainstreamed through HLP Messaging, safety audits, sharing GBV information and referrals, training women and girls on FES and engaging protection partners while implementing CBI activities to mitigate potential violence as a result of CBI.TF will liaise with the SFPs and NFIs partners to coordinate the response, share experience and ideas for better and cost effective response. To integrate the response, TF already has funding support for FSL, Wash and Protection for Yei, Cueibet and Kajokeji. S/NFIs staffs will participate in Joint Missions with Nutrition partners (ARC) in Kajokeji to deliver malaria messaging, distribute mosquito nets to mothers with children under 5 years and conduct life skills training at Nutrition outreach and stabilization centres. S/NFIs staffs will work with FSL partners in Kajokeji, Lainya, Magwi, Rumbek Centre, Torit, Cueibet and Yei to introduce the concept of FES and Briquettes to farmers and fishermen groups and to mitigate misuse of mosquito nets (for fishing and fencing gardens). In locations at risk of Malaria, diarrhoea, Pneumonia, Skin Infections, Ebola and HIV and AIDS, TF staffs will work closely with health partners to deliver lifesaving messages. S/NFIs intervention will be coordinated through SFPs based in Juba, Rumbek and Torit. To ensure an integrated response, TF will share information, coordinate and participate in joint missions with CCCM partners in Kajokeji.

1 - Needs

The OCHA situation report for August 2019 estimate that South Sudan has over 1.9 Million IDPs; 7.2 Million people in need of humanitarian help and 2.5 Million refugees in camps in neighbouring Countries. The HNO 2020 draft report estimates 2,442,716 individuals require S/NFIs assistance in 2020. Most affected population include returnees, newly IDPs and affected non IDPs residing in formal, informal and hard to reach displacement settings or host communities in Torit, Rumbek Center, Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya, Morobo, and Magwi Counties. The affected person either left their S/NFIs assets behind as they run to seek safety and protection. What they left behind was either looted, burnt or destroyed. According to 2019 HNO report, the listed counties already had a population of 123700 individuals (Cueibet: 4500; Rumbek Center: 1400; Kajokeji: 27500; Lainya: 7300; Morobo: 3900; Yei: 35000; Magwi: 34300 and Torit: 9800) in need of S/NFIs needs. These S/NFIs are amplified by food insecurity. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classiﬁcation (IPC) report, 305,000 individuals are expected to remain in IPC3 in Central Equatoria in Septembers to December 2019. In the same state, food security situation is expected to worsen in the ﬁrst quarter of 2020 with 345000 individuals projected as IPC3 and 35,000 persons in IPC4. Kajokeji, Yei, Morobo, and Lainya counties are expected to face IPC 3 or worse food insecurity in the first quarter of 2019. In Eastern Equatorial State, 320,000 individuals are estimated to remain in IPC3 while 60,000 persons in IPC 4 between September and December 2019. Torit and Magwi Counties are classified in IPC3 during the same period with Magwi County classified as AMN IPC4 (emergency acute malnutrition). In the months of January to April 2020, Magwi and Torit counties will be in IPC3 food insecure or worse. During the same period, Magwi County will slide to Emergency AMN IPC4. In Lake States, Rumbek Center and Cueibet Counties are projected to remain in IPC3 or worse food insecurity in the last quarter of 2019 and ﬁrst quarter of 2020. Due to food insecurity, households are expected to dedicate the little money they have to buying food rather than meeting S/NFIs needs. Regarding displacements, a recent joint mission by TF, VSF-Germany and CCOSS in Cueibet County noted 5425 IDPs residing in North Malou and Langdit Payams. More displacement were reported in Tiaptiap, Pagor, and Duony Ngap due to localized fighting, flooding and inflow from Warrap State. In Morobo County, an Inter-Agency Fact Finding Mission conducted in October 2019 noted 5397 individuals in Morobo and considered in need of S/NFIs. The County has no functional market and was inaccessible during the first half of 2019 due to insecurity. More returnees are expected in the county in the last quarter of 2019 and throughout 2020 if current security remain stable. According to IRNA (Kajokeji 2019), UNHCR and DTM returnee tracking reports for the month of July 2019, increasing cases of returnees have occurred with Lainya recording 949 cases, Torit 340, Magwi 60,676 and Yei 8640 and Kajokeji 14742. According to OCHA seasonal flooding snapshot report for the month of October 2019, 600,000 to 800,000 individuals are affected by flooding in 29 counties including Cueibet and Rumbek Center Counties. Degradation and loss of S/NFI items as a result flooding will create more S/NFIs needs in the last quarter of 2019 and into 2020.The high water level in flooded areas is constraining affected populations from accessing forests to harvest naturally grown shelter materials. Also, malaria and pneumonia are on the increase due to increased mosquito breeding and elevated levels of coldness. Taken together, flooding, increased inflow of returnees, food insecurity, and displacements are factors necessitating regular if not increased provision of S/NFIs to affected persons in Torit, Rumbek Center, Lainya, Kajokeji, Yei, Morobo, Cueibet, and Magwi Counties.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The guiding principle of the NFIs intervention is “Do no harm’’ and it prioritizes the protection, safety, dignity and rights of beneﬁciaries while delivering services and assistance. Distribution of inputs and trainings will be provided in adequate scale, within safe and easy to reach known affected Returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and vulnerable host communities according to needs and risks devoid of any form of discrimination based on age, gender, ethnic allegiance, or religion. The NFIs project ensures maximum inclusion and will seek to build the capacity and assist affected individuals to claim their rights. Also, activities proposed will abide by principle of humanitarian protection by ensuring women, girls and children are protected from all forms of sexual gender based violence and exploitation. Platforms such as open community dialogues, complaint and feedback mechanisms, community consultations, Focus Group Discussions and meetings are embedded in the project to ensure community members views, suggestions, ideas and concerns are received, acknowledged and attended to on time to safeguard their rights and entitlements

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The NFIs intervention is designed based on information obtained from returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and Vulnerable host communities during IRNA, NFIs Cluster and Multiagency assessments. Also, the project refers to data developed through Integrated Phase Classiﬁcation for 2019-2020, Acute Malnutrition Integrated Phase Classiﬁcation reports and analysis. Assessments and Reports noted are referenced in the NFIs project to justify the intervention and were compiled after extensive Interviews, Focus Group Discussions, Observation and household visits to obtain information from affected persons. Titi Foundation has reached a minimum of 88 individuals (33 Females) while designing the NFIs projects. Their needs, priorities, concerns and suggestions are embedded in the project activities. As such, the project is a product of assessed, expressed and prioritized needs of the affected communities and will ably meet the speciﬁc needs of the affected populations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Due to years of conﬂict, Government and Community Structures and Networks collapsed in targeted locations. However, as a coping mechanisms, communities in target locations have come up with their own systems of addressing challenges they face as well as in disaster risk reduction. In locations with or without Governance and community structures, the NFIs project assumes participatory approach seeking to involve community members in project activities at every stage to generate project buy in, legitimacy, acceptability, sustainability, accountability and to allow the community members to contribute their talent, ideas and other resources to complement the intervention. Coordinating project activities with the local authorities, community leadership diminishes chances of overlap or duplication of effort. Also, coordination allows the project also to beneﬁt from technical knowledge and capacities at the community and government agencies. The NFI project staffs will actively participate in meetings, ﬁeld visits and assessments organised by the Local Authorities, Community Leaders and chiefs throughout the period of the intervention. Also, the intervention will seek to develop existing referral pathway for effective referrals of GBV cases Project Staffs may encounter while implementing the project. Increased participation of community members through existing networks in the community allows for strengthening community networks and systems for better response to risks and disasters.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The Proposed NFIs intervention is an emergency response informed by pressing needs currently faced by Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and vulnerable host communities. Whereas the aim is to meet immediate NFIs and Shelter needs of the affected communities, the project will seek to engage target communities on durable shelter and NFIs solutions. For example, Titi Foundation has already piloted resilient shelters in Kajokeji by establishing an initial 28 shelters made of clay bricks and iron sheets. The Intervention involved community participation where community members made bricks, laid the bricks for them to receive some construction inputs. In the next twelve months, Titi Foundation anticipates to establish another 150 Shelters through community participation approach. As such, as immediate S/NFIs needs are being met, Titi Foundation will continue to explore and work on long term shelter solutions for affected communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Whereas project locations were immensely affected by the conﬂict that erupted in 2013 and escalated in 2016, they have enjoyed relative calm after the signing of Revitalized Peace Agreement in September 2018. Accessibility to locations and beneﬁciaries is by road and by air with the local authorities allowing unhindered access to beneﬁciaries and delivery of humanitarian aid. Assessments conducted in the locations by the Protection Cluster have noted improved security with no much risk posed to humanitarian agencies and supplies. Noting that security situation is fragile across South Sudan, Titi Foundation will conduct safety audits in target locations to map existing and potential risks and possible impact on project activities. The ﬁndings of the audit report will be embedded in project activities and used to advocate for protection of staffs, beneﬁciaries, project supplies and assets. Further, Titi Foundation will rely on protection cluster, FSL Cluster and UNOCHA for security updates. Local authorities will be informed of staff’s movement, project activities well in advance in order for them to provide security. To alleviate resource driven conﬂict during project implementation, the NFIS project will ensure protection for environment with special attention being placed on avoiding depletion of natural resources i.e. ensuring trees are not cut, avoiding conducting project activities next to watering points, animal migration corridors, and religious and cultural shrines.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The NFIs project will seek to equip beneficiaries with skills and knowledge they can utilize to come up with their own shelter and NFIs solutions. In the proposed intervention, Women and Girls will be trained on how to make Fuel Efficient Stoves and Briquettes (Funding already secured to train 125 Trainer of Trainers in Kajokeji on FES and Briquettes from Diakonie Katastrophilfe. Each TOT is expected to train a minimum of 5HHs on moulding and making of Briquettes). On the other hand, through the same Diakonie support, 150 Youth will be trained on how to make bricks for shelters, beds, chairs and tables using local materials. Youth, women and girls TOTs trained through Diakonie Support will support this S/NFIs project by training households on how they can sustainably utilize local resources for durable shelter and non-food solutions. Trained TOTs and community members will generate a pool of men and women equipped with skills and knowledge they will utilize long after the project has come to an end. Rather than just distribute aid, the Integrated S/NFIs response seeks to build the capacity of targeted beneﬁciaries meaning they can utilize the skills by their own to help themselves come up with own shelter and NFIs solutions. Titi Foundation will also continue to fund raise to support NFIs activities in targeted locations during and after the 12 months’ period through Diakonie Katastrophilfe and other agencies who are already willing to continue supporting NFIs interventions. Finally, by building the resilience of community members, the intervention will strengthen the resilience of households’ thus expanding household coping mechanism.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

61% of displaced persons in South Sudan conﬂict are women and girls. Negative gendered norms, safety concerns and domestic chores can exclude women and girls from accessing humanitarian assistance. To ensure equal participation of women and girls in this project, project consultations and planning meetings will be conducted in locations that women and girls can easily and safely access and during time of day they can attend. Women and girls will be engaged in project monitoring to embed their views and concerns in project activities. To mitigate occurrence of GBV, Titi Foundation will deploy a protection oﬃcer who will provide guidance to project staffs on existing and potential GBV risks to ensure activities do not pose additional risks to women and girls. To mitigate exposure to violence during registration and distribution exercises, FSL staffs will conduct consultation with the community members and leaders to provide information on the project including selection criteria, number of beneﬁciaries per location, distribution dates and locations. Also, registration and distribution exercises will be conducted in collaboration with community leaders, local authorities, beneﬁciaries’ representatives to ensure fairness. To support impartial and conﬂict sensitive intervention, community leaders, members and local authorities will be engaged in decision making regarding the project. All complaints received on the project will be safely, impartially and swiftly acted on with complainants being provided with feedback within the shortest time possible. Prior to commencing project activities, a safety audit will be conducted in each target location to map existing or potential protection concerns posed to beneﬁciaries, project staffs and NFIs supplies.The Safety Audit report will be utilized by project staffs, community leaders and members in deciding registration, distribution and training sites, where beneﬁciaries can access easily and safely.
