Project Summary

IRC is intending to improve the livelihoods status of vulnerable host communities through integrated livelihoods supports. The project will reach out vulnerable host communities with unconditional cash grants, farm and non-farm inputs aiming to increase capacity on food consumption and production. The project will address through double assets approach where homestead gardening will be established to all targeted beneficiaries and another one asset will be provided based on their need and suitability. The project will also establish 4 vegetables nursery cum farmers HuB where the owner will produce soil touch free, high quality vegetables sapling as well as work as one stop service center for the neighboring farmers to get solution on modern farming. The benefits and progress will be tracked through regular montiring and data will be collected through program reports , studies and evaluation.
Considering the resilience, the HHs will be brought under orientation DRR basics as well as climate smart agriculture. Social cohesion is one of the components and youth group will be targeted in the processes where they will be brought under groups and mobilize in social cohesion activities.
The community people will be engaged in consultation, complain & feedback mechanism, and learning events for program design, implementation and evaluation processes.
The project also provide orientation on DRR and PSEA to all targeted HHs. The project will serve 5794 HHs of Whykong, Baharchara, Haldiapalong and Ratna Palong union.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox's Bazar is one of the poorest districts of Bangladsh with highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as cyclones, monsoons etc. GoB states that 17% of people live below poverty line across Cox’s Bazar on average and 57% of poor individuals do not receive safety net support [GoB, December 2019] and according to CARE and World Bank, 33% of the population in the district live below the poverty line [ACAPS, September 2020] . Prior to the start of COVID-19 response, more than 370,000 people were reportedly living under the poverty line with an 400,000 poor or vulnerable anticipated to have been impacted as a result of COVID-19 economic repercussions (COVID-19 JRP, April 2020). The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihoods, exacerbating existing poverty in the host communities (ISCG 04/2020; WFP 04/2020;WFP 05/2020; BBS, 2011). Shortages and unavailability of agricultural inputs due to supply chain disruptions and movement restrictions amid the lockdown, lead to a hike in cost of inputs and shortages of labour [FAO, June 2020] . 93% of households reported diminished income as an impact of COVID-19 [MSNA, August 2020] and almost 7 out of 10 host community households relied on less preferred food [WFP, August 2020]. 42% HHs have been reported to go into debt for buying food for the family (J-MSNA, July-August 2020).
