Project Summary

According to the 2020 Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS), an estimated 10.6 million people in North East Nigeria remain in need of lifesaving interventions to survive, including access to basic services. Of this figure, 7.8 million people are expected to be targeted for various interventions. The protracted conflict, a consequence of a decade of armed insurgency, has made the population vulnerable, particularly those living in IDP camps whose survival is closely linked to food assistance programs, WASH services, health and nutrition interventions provided by humanitarian partners to meet their daily basic needs.
Despite tremendous efforts by partners to prevent and reduce malnutrition with interventions in the area, the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates for North East Nigeria, over 1.5 million people needed nutrition interventions in 2020, a number with the potential to increase, largely exacerbated by the ongoing insecurity and displacement in North East Nigeria.
From the HNO report, there are 613, 909 people in need of nutritional support in Borno State. Of this figure, 73% are female, 56% children and 15% people with disability. Talking of which, 32% are IDPs, 15% are returnees and 53% are in the host communities.
Women and children are the most affected and those living at the reception centers are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival at the reception sites. There is urgent need to design nutrition intervention to target this group especially that they are in communities that are hard to reach and with limited options for meeting their nutritional requirements.
In addition, farming which provides the main sources of nutritional requirements for women and children has been hampered, partly due to insecurity in these hard to reach communities and limited access to more staple food items to such locations.
According to an analysis conducted jointly with the Food Security Sector, factoring projected food security degradation, COVID-19 impact and seasonality, it is expected that the number of children with acute malnutrition will progressively increase and peak in the 3rd quarter of 2021 (20% in quarter 1, 24% in quarter 2, 30% in quarter 3 and 26% in quarter 4.
Also, there is lack of services due to the lack of skilled health workers who were displaced or are not willing or able to work in areas of insecurity. In these circumstances, it is necessary to maintain the delivery of the lifesaving nutrition services to ensure timely identification, treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition to avoid preventable deaths through the support of INGOs.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic has directly and indirectly, impact the nutritional status of the population, especially children and women who are most vulnerable, as follows: COVID-19 infection may result in malnutrition to the infected persons due to the increased physiological needs in terms of energy, protein and micronutrients requirements; Malnourished children and women are more likely to contract COVID-19 due to their compromised immune system and further complicating their nutritional status and treatment regime; Mother/caregivers infected with COVID-19 may be too sick to properly take care of children including optimal breastfeeding resulting in malnutrition; Households affected by COVID-19 may divert resources to COVID-19 treatment resulting in reduced access to nutritious and appropriate health care with the children and women most affected, resulting in malnutrition. Besides, the members of the household may not be able to work to provide adequate nutritious foods for optimal nutritional status; Closure of all land borders leading to a disruption of the food supply that, in turn, will increase food prices and exacerbate food insecurity; In the hard to reach areas like Gwoza, there are 6829 vulnerable groups needing nutritional support, in Monguno, there are over 7005 in need of nutritional support with accessible areas like Maiduguri and Jere having the highest numbers for nutritional support due to the large population of IDPs in camps and in the communities.The most vulnerable population sub-group includes children less than five years, and pregnant and lactating women. Those in IDP camps are more vulnerable due to the congestion in the camps. In an already degraded context of weak health systems, expanded food insecurity, massive population displacement, limited access to WASH infrastructures, and restricted humanitarian access, the COVID-19 pandemic is a significant threat to the nutrition service delivery including the following: Compromised quality of care for acute malnutrition treatment if hospitals and health staff are overwhelmed leading to increased mortality.According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 with SAM and 514,526 with MAM. The ongoing response is focused on the adaptation of nutrition interventions in the context of COVID-19, including the following: Scaling up of the Mother/Family MUAC approach whereby mother/ caregivers self-screen and refer therefore reducing exposure for both targeted community and health workers; a vital intervention to ensure identification of acutely malnourished children is ongoing, modifying the treatment of acute malnutrition protocols to minimal or no-touch approaches, training of nutrition service providers, including health workers and community health workers in COVID-19 infection prevention and control (IPC) strategies, and putting adequate IPC measures at all nutrition service provision sites. GHIV Africa will carry out her interventions to target the vulnerable groups in the hard to reach communities to meet their nutritional requirements but also target those in the easy to access areas but are in dire need of nutritional support.

2 - Activities

GHIV Africa intends to undertake the following activities to contribute in reducing the gaps identified:• Stakeholders mapping and engagement• Community entry/diagnosis and Identification and selection of beneficiaries.• Behavioral Change Communication awareness creation on IYCF• Capacity building of community volunteers and supplies of MUAC tapes to scale-up the Mother/Family MUAC in the hard to reach communities.• Training of women and girls on the preparation of locally sourced nutrition supplements in the form of high nutrient dense complementary foods for use on severe acute malnutrition for children in the communities. • Formation of IYCF women champions groups for monitoring and sustainability of program in the communities.
