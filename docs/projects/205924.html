Project Summary

In the year 2024, the health cluster has set a goal to extend essential lifesaving health services to 3.2 million individuals, as outlined in the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2023. The Southern Sudan Healthcare Organization (SSHCO) is dedicated to delivering high-quality healthcare to those affected by crises, addressing, and preventing health threats, and enhancing the health system at both the Maar Primary Health Care Center (PHCC) in Twic East County, Jonglei State (JS), and the Mongalla Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) Camp in Juba County, Central Equatoria State (CES).
The strategic plan of SSHCO will prioritize 1) delivering crucial life-saving health services and kits, 2) implementing integrated responses through collaboration with the Health Cluster, National Ministry of Health, State Ministry of Health, and County Health Departments, 3) offering high-quality health services to individuals impacted by health events, 4) enhancing sexual reproductive health services, 5) providing assistance to health facilities and community-based systems, and 6) reinforcing the disease surveillance and reporting system.
In Maar, SSHCO will continue to provide essential healthcare services to the people of Twic East County by operating out of the Maar PHCC. In 2023, the total population of Twic East County is estimated at 51269 (Health Cluster, Pin and Target, 2023). 1. Needs

SSHCO plans to meet the need for essential primary healthcare services in Maar and Mongalla. Twic East County, (JS) and Juba County, (CES) are both listed as ‘in extreme need’ based on the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview-2023. Based on previous data collected by the SSHCO, the SSHCO sees around 1200 patients a month in Maar PHCC. Additionally, each month, the SSHCO sees an average of 388 patients under the age of 5 and an average of 706 patients aged 5 and older, in Mangalla. These numbers show the number of patients who rely on the healthcare services of SSHCO each month for essential life-saving services. South Sudan has had an increase of over 1,606,193 IDPs since 2021, making the needs even greater for the area. Beyond healthcare, there is an extreme risk of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in CES and Twic East County in JS according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview. There has been a great deal of flooding, causing increased issues for people with regard to gaining access to food or WASH, which is essential for living. The Humanitarian Needs Overview lists food security as a major priority this year, with over a million more people in the region labeled as food insecure with more increases to come. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, Jonglei State was listed to be one of the States in the area that is above the 15% emergency threshold for global acute malnutrition (GAM). Additionally, South Sudan has some of the highest mortality rates for under 5 populations anywhere in the world. Some of the major causes of mortality and morbidity are malaria, acute watery diarrhea, and acute respiratory infection, which all are set to increase over 2024. Maternal health in South Sudan has also become an increasing issue, with rising rates of mortality and morbidity(1,223 deaths per 100,000 live births). Though efforts have been made to improve maternal health through the Boma Health Initiative, there remains an extremely high maternal death rate and high infant mortality with a high number of births taking place outside of health centers (90.7 deaths/1,000 live births). There is a lack of healthcare workers to address maternal and infant health issues, causing increased morbidity and mortality for preventable issues. There are also increasing issues for gender-based violence (GBV) in the Humanitarian Needs Overview, as with increased flooding there is less land to inhabit and more safety concerns for women, children, and persons with disabilities.

2. Project activities

The primary operational focuses for the Southern Sudan Healthcare Organization (SSHCO) at Mongalla IDP Camp and Maar PHCC include:1. Provision of vital life-saving health services and kits.2. Implementation of integrated responses in coordination with the Health Cluster, National Ministry of Health, State Ministry of Health, and County Health Departments.3. Delivery of high-quality health services to individuals affected by health events.4. Improvement of sexual reproductive health services.5. Support to health facilities and community-based systems.6. Strengthening of the disease surveillance and reporting system. Medical: The SSHCO will provide primary healthcare services in Mongalla and Maar. This organization intends for Maar to be the primary care healthcare center, as a static center, serving people with essential healthcare. As well, the project intends to have mobile health units weekly to travel to the areas of Wuchung, Dhiam Dhiam, and Jonglei island along the Nile to help those who cannot travel to Maar. SSHCO plans to increase its staffing to better collect and report on diseases occurring to better prevent and treat them. We hope to increase the number of midwives and traditional birth attendants to better provide prenatal, birthing assistance, and postnatal care within the communities. We also hope to utilize this even more and the additional staff to assist in mental health support for women who experience gender-based violence (GBV). Moreover, SSHCO plans to distribute clinical management of rape (CMR) kits for women who have undergone GBV to integrate clinical GBV health services not offered to people anywhere else. SSHCO is also working to reduce morbidity and mortality for infants through the provision of basic immunizations at birth. Also, iron sources and supplements will be given to women suffering from severe anemia and/or breastfeeding mothers to support their needs. This organization plans to better identify and treat the most vulnerable populations, with a high emphasis on those with disabilities, to reduce the impact of physical and emotional stresses on their lives. With a stronger process for feedback, the organization plans to strengthen itself in the community. Non-medical: Beyond the provision of medical care, the staff distributes both food items and non-food items (NFI). With the growing need for nutritional assistance, SSHCO seeks to meet this by providing infant and child food items to combat the SAM outlined clearly in the Humanitarian Needs Overview. The staff will distribute key vitamins and minerals noted in the WHO Care for SAM guidelines as most essential to children and infants. SSHCO will also promote safe infant practices and work through community engagement to reduce the spread of seasonal infections to create a more resilient population from the stresses they are undergoing with recent floods. The SSHCO will continue to hold training for the staff for the prevention of COVID-19 and other communicable diseases. Also, the SSHCO clinical staff also intends to hold training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to better protect the most vulnerable population in the area. Further training on various topics will be held throughout the year with flexibility for topics that arise (The Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI), Traditional Birth Attendants, etc.).
