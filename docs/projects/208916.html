Project Summary

Sudan’s humanitarian crisis reached over nine months and the situation has converted into a catastrophe, leading to further displacement and accumulation of additional vulnerable people in unfavorable conditions. This has increased the severity of the people’s suffering, including potential risk to Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment, following the attack on Gazera state. The conflict continues to force 6,055,749 individuals to flee in search of safety and internally displaced observed in 6,355 locations across all of Sudan’s 18 states. The displacements have increased demands for a humanitarian workforce, the majority of whom might be vulnerable to SEAAH or could be potential perpetrators. This also exposes the affected population to greater risks of SEAH as meeting their needs could be met with transactional sex or other forms of misconduct. Sudan is the leading contributor to malaria in the Eastern Mediterranean Region accounting for 54%(WMR2022). 1,145,053 malaria cases and 141 deaths were reported in 2023 despite the interruption of health services and accessibility. As of the Epi week no 1/2024, a total of 7,459 dengue fever cases, an outbreak circulated in 48 localities from 10 states. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities have lost access to essential WASH services. Only 28.2% of households have access to these services, while one in three households practices open defecation. These factors pose a high risk of outbreaks of water-borne diseases, such as cholera, highlighting the urgent need to improve WASH services in healthcare facilities and locations where IDPs and refugees gather. Cholera continues to spread with a total of 9724 cases reported with 269 deaths and a case fatality rate of 2.77 in 11 states. The displaced population and the host communities in main towns require sustainable coverage by core WASH and vector control interventions and vigilant vector and Water quality surveillance. Preventative WASH activities have been suspended in ten states and the remaining eight states, the displacement of MoH staff, and the lack of money to pay government employees and/or run operations with high population density and increased risk factors of WASH-related diseases. With this project, WHO with its Sub offices in the field will ensure the protection of crisis-affected populations from exposure to WASH hazards by managing all risk factors by supporting (1)entomological surveillance, (2)Water quality monitoring, (3)vector control Interventions, and (4)community mobilization to apply effective measures for their protection against WASH-related diseases. (5)Chemical control of aquatic and adult vectors, Capacity development, technical tools, community mobilization and awareness to understand and report sexual misconduct and inappropriate behavior of any form by anyone, and active surveys will also be conducted to up-date/map risks of vector-borne diseases in affected areas and IDP settlements with a focus on the invasive vectors. (6)Support WASH in Health Facilities Adopting the WASH FIT will be widely implemented especially in treatment/isolation centers in outbreak-affected areas, in addition to (7)environmental cleaning and good housekeeping of selected health facilities in crisis hot zones and referral facilities to apply good infection prevention and control practices to protect injured people. (8)Training of 760 environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH and partners, and community volunteers on multiple environmental health (WASH) packages. with a total cost of 11.4M USD targeting 4.7M individuals, in. WHO will work closely with partners to improve water quality activities. Beneficiaries’ involvement will be ensured through supporting community water safety plans, and community-based vector surveillance and control.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO is one of the lead agencies that takes the protection of the affected population, the work-force (irrespective of contract types), partners, contractors and suppliers seriously. For example, WHO has a zero-tolerance policy with clear accountability mechanisms on protecting and re-sponding to SEAH. WHO is also part of the protection cluster and the GBV Sub-cluster. From the design stages of the actions, WHO will ensure all interventions fully reflects the 6 core principles of SEAH with clear actions on both prevention and response, including referrals for protection related services. All risk assessments with surely have variables and indicators on protection, particularly related to SEAH and findings will be fully integrated into the plans and budgets of WASH. Interventions will be delivered with ease of and safe access by the targeted population without a fear of SEAH and other forms of misconductWHO will also enhance the ownership of the community for project activities raising the aware-ness of the communities during sessions and training to address the socialcultural barriers that affect GBV and SEAH survivors from accessing services and assistance. Activities of risk reduc-tion to ultimately increase the targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness, and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships and to improve all aspects of protection for children. The project will additionally contribute to achieving sustainable social development through, strength-ening local partners on both the national and states level. WHO will also apply the standard measures for the containment of vector control remedies to ensure and prioritize safety and digni-ty and avoid causing harm. Efforts will be made to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unanticipated or unintentional negative effects on the targeted population, which could potentially expose them to further physical and risks. By design, the project targeted vulnerable communities that requires protection intervention such as IDPs and the women population. The project also adheres to WHO PSEA standards.

Community feedback and thoughts will be compiled, analyzed, and sorted out to apply the needed corrective measures, for the feedback related to deviation from the activities, WHO will provide pieces of training and orientation to accelerate the technical knowledge for appropriate implementation of the standard activities. Other feedback related to the knowledge, or community beliefs will be addressed through community awareness raising. Accountability to affected people at all levels is a core value of WHO work. Upon the commencement of this project and following the discussions during the planning phase; WHO will sensitize the community about the project objectives and activities and the importance of community involvement in implementation and feedback.
