Project Summary

To empower communities and strengthen gender equality by ensuring that vulnerable people in the Far North Region have access holistic support, including gender-based violence services and community-based inclusion initiatives.
In the Far North region, physical violence and risks of exploitation, including incidents of GBV such as early and forced marriages and early pregnancies due to the persistence of harmful traditional and cultural practices, are among the main protection risks identified in the Minawao camp, as well as in other localities hosting IDPs and returnees. In the Logone-and-Chari division, rape and sexual assault (on both girls and boys) are also significant risks. Other types of violence, such as survival sex, are also overlooked. Of the 2,920 cases of GBV reported in 2020 in the Far North region, 98% of the survivors were women and girls and 15% were children. However, it is not always possible to have comprehensive statistics on incidents of GBV due to a culture of silence, as well as community or religious management of incidents. Many women and girls are economically inactive and therefore are exposed to all forms of exploitation and abuse. Boys who have no income-generating activities and no professional training use drugs and are identified as perpetrators of GBV. Despite previous interventions by humanitarian actors including IMC with BPRM funding, notably interventions focused community awareness raising on GBV prevention, risk identification, there are major gaps that need to be addressed. In addition, concerning community engagement and mobilization, the participation of the population and the inclusion of women, men , girls and boys in the humanitarian program cycle is still low despite past interventions focused on community engagement. This hinders the opportunities available for the crisis-affected population to find durable solutions to their problems and self-reliance
To address the above mentioned issues, the project intends to achieve the following in terms of impact over the next five years:
 By 2024, procedures for accessing GBV services for persons of concern in all target locations will be put in place or updated and relevant staff will be trained. GBV referral pathways will be established where they do not exist and/or updated to facilitate access to safe and timely GBV services. GBV staff, MINPROFF, CBOs and volunteers will have increased capacity through trainings on GBV case management and psychosocial support, including referrals.
 By 2025, IMC UK will ensure the transition process of GBV and community mobilization services and will work hand in hand with MINPROFF and MINAS and the respective Districts and Municipalities to help them manage project activities in target locations in the Far North.
 By 2026, people of concern in the target locations will have access to improved GBV and protection services in an equitable and sustainable manner. Gaps in key socio-economic indicators will be reduced, reflecting greater gender equality and the empowerment of youth, women and girls, and other vulnerable groups, including persons with specific needs (PSN).

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2015, IMC has been involved in the fight against GBV in the Far North especially in Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Sava and Logone and Chari. In 2018 and 2019 with UNHCR support in Minawao camp, IMC has been supporting interventions focused on building the capacity of service providers to provide protection services and referrals in accordance with the guiding principles. The results obtained through these activities has improved the ownership of the community in GBV and Child Protection services and the involvement of stakeholders in interventions related to GBV and protection. From August 2018 to July 2021 with PRM support, IMC provided psychosocial support through individualized case management and referral to related security, medical and legal services to 401 survivors in Minawao camp. To promote behavior change and community participation in prevention interventions, IMC established and strengthened protection committees made up of community structures such as women's committees, husband's school and gender clubs. This strategy enabled the community to actively participate in the promotion of a safe and protective environment for women and girls which contributed in reducing harmful effects of cultural practices. IMC also identified and strengthened the capacity of existing community-based organizations (CBOs) in psychological first aid, referral systems and the management of WGSS. These CBOs also received operating material and equipment consisting of office and awareness materials and are currently receiving coaching on referrals and village, savings, and loan associations (VSLA) activities for their empowerment. This strategy created a link between the community and the supervision of MINPROFF to facilitate the connection between the survivors and the services providers. Given the turnover of MINPROFF staff and service providers, in the various intervention areas, since 2017, IMC has furthermore conducted regular training on holistic care, gender mainstreaming as well as basic concepts and guiding principles of GBV. The coordination of GBV interventions was supported through coordination meetings and joint supervision where challenges and mitigation strategies for improving survivor satisfaction and sustained the intervention were evaluated. IMC UK has a strong capacity to raise funds from other donors to complement UNHCR funds, has gained acceptance from all stakeholders, including other implementing partners, representatives of line ministries, administrative authorities, and has a strong knowledge of the context. IMC UK has extensive experience in implementing activities in Cameroon in general and the Far North in particular. Similarly, IMC UK has gained the trust of the population benefiting from its services provided in all intervention sites. IMC UK has been present in the Eastern region since 2008, and in the Far North since 2010, and has the capacity to respond quickly and in a timely manner to sudden humanitarian emergencies, including IMC UK’s response to the Goura humanitarian crisis following the massive influx of Nigerian asylum seekers fleeing attacks of violence in Rann, Nigeria in February 2019 and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and 2021. With the support of its donors, such as ECHO, BPRM, START, AFENET, CDC, BHA, UNFPA and UNHCR, IMC UK is currently implementing health, nutrition and protection services in five regions: Far North, East, Adamaoua, North and North West. The success of International Medical Corps UK depends vitally on its ability to manage and safeguard UNCHR resources, both governmental and private, that are entrusted to it. A sound financial management and control structure and the judicious use of these funds are essential to achieving this goal. IMC UK's financial management and accounting system tracks all revenues and expenditures by project, segregates costs by project and expenditure category, and accounts for eligible costs.
