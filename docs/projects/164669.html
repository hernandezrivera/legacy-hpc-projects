Project Summary

EPDC project is to contribute towards the estimated 11.06 million people in need1. In Syria, the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020 indicates that there are 6.1 million internally displaced people and living throughout Syria. While 2018 proved to be somewhat positive in terms of returns, whereby 1.4 million Syrians have been identified as spontaneous returns2, this has only increased the protection needs of Syrians. The crisis affected the health situation as many health facilities are damaged or partially damaged and the functional ones are many times hard to reach. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable and exposed to GBV from a multiplicity of ways ranging from “…direct exposure to hostilities, displacement, conditions in sites/collective shelters, protracted displacement and returns to destroyed and impoverished areas.”3 Besides, many of the southern areas including Daraa and Rural Damascus are unserved raising the probability of waterborne diseases and infections. Accordingly, the project will establish 4 mobile clinics, 2 in Daraa and 2 in Rural Damascus that will provide PHC services and reproductive health in addition to MHPSS individual sessions. In each area, there will be two mobile clinics, one to provide reproductive health and one for the PHC services and both individual MHPSS sessions will be conducted for GBV survivors. Each mobile clinic will contain 1 doctor, 1 MHPSS specialist, and 2 nurses. The health staff will receive health training on health-related topics. In the implementation phase, it will be a high level in coordination between both clinics in each area (the reproductive health and the consultation one) for example if a pregnant woman who is being treated in reproductive health clinic suffer from another disease it will be referred directly to the second clinic. Regarding mental health, there will be PSS specialists in each clinic. EPDC will ensure the proper application of CRM mechanism as it will be informed with the targeted community along with hotline, WhatsApp number, Facebook page, complaints box and field monitor to keep track of the services provided and response to the complaints effectively. The project will be monitored throughout the project through field visits, checklists of the beneficiaries, staff, work methodology, and tools and equipment of the mobile clinics, pre and post-test of the health personnel benefiting from the health training along with observation of the training process.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to health care due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources and imposed access constraints. These latter constraints constitute a barrier to health care in hard-to-reach areas and areas with high severity scores, including those that recently witnessed changes of control. People most at risk include people living with chronic diseases, children who are not vaccinated, pregnant women and neonates who have no access to life-saving obstetrics care, and women with poor access to essential mental health and reproductive health. The provision of health services continues to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict. 85 total attacks on health care personnel and facilities1 that impacted the provided health services with 17% partially functioning facilities and 2% non-functioning facilities2. The number of people who are in need of health assistance in Syria is estimated to be 11.06 million, where women make up 5.54 million of the PiN (People in Need). Vulnerable groups include 4.81 million people under 18 and 5.83 million people above 18 as at this age vulnerability to non-communicable diseases becomes highest. Syria is home to at least 3.07 million disabled people. The crisis resulted in psychological and mental effects on the people, especially the directly affected population. According to the HNO 2020, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are critical as the long period of the crisis has increased the prevalence of mental health conditions above the WHO global prevalence of 15 percent. Although the need for MHPSS is crucial, there are not many experienced health workers and it is still stigmatized. Accordingly, mobile clinics are needed to reach people in remote areas and integrate the MHPSS services with other health-related services. 1 OCHA HNO 20202 HeRAMS Annual Report 20193 OCHA HNO 2020

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The protection risk: GBV survivors may arrive at the mobile clinics in need of health care for GBV-related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, but may not directly disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. The likelihood is high. The impact is medium. The mitigation measures: In order to facilitate the required care, survivors will have safe, stigma-free access to the mobile clinic, and health providers will be equipped to offer survivor-centered, rights-based, non-discriminatory, trauma-sensitive first line support and health services and provide referrals to specialized GBV support services. The risk: The health workers will come into contact with vulnerable people and MHPSS will have knowledge of the GBV survivors cases and information and might exploit these information. The likelihood is low. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: The healthcare providers will strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk: Worsening of the security situation in the region and in the project targeted areas. The likelihood is medium. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Trust-building actions and developing relationships with municipalities and district authorities.The risk: Monitoring activities hampered by the escalation of conflict in the area of implementation. The likelihood is medium. The impact is medium. The mitigation measures: Regular security monitoring will be undertaken by EPDC to analyze context. The risk: Risk of increased fragmentation of care. The likelihood is low. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: EPDC will avoid potential tensions by a provision of services based on needs and not based on status. The selection criteria will be transparent, and the beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized.The risk: Devaluation of currency/fluctuation in the exchange rate. The likelihood is high. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: A security margin has been included in the budget cost to mitigate currency fluctuation and if needed a budget revision. The risk: Delays in the procurement of equipment and supplies. The likelihood is medium. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: EPDC will follow strict protocols and procedures to purchase the equipment thanks to clear communication on the humanitarian nature of the project.
