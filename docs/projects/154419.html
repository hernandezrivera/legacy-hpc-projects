Needs

Mali is one of the poorest countries in the world, with over half the population living on less than US$ 1.25 a day. Mali’s food security has been rocked in recent years by recurring disasters including erratic rainfall, drought, and a military coup that triggered a political and security crisis. The number of food insecure people is 55 percent higher compared to the 2017 lean season, and the highest since 2012, with as many as 1.6 million people in urgent need and more than 4.3 million people expected to be affected by the lean season. Besides erratic rainfall and drought, which create pockets of decreased cereal production and pastures, persistent insecurity in northern and central Mali has also had a direct impact on normal agricultural and pastoral livelihoods. In a context characterized by rapid population growth, the absence of sufficient diversification of employment opportunities away from agricultural and pastoralism means continued reliance on less and less available productive land to meet food consumption needs. This indeed fuels tensions and conflicts. Access to food is also problematic, with a steep rise in the price of food items, agricultural inputs and animal feed in some areas at peak demand periods because of inefficient supply chains that are further negatively impacted by conflict. During the lean season the food stock of vulnerable households are completely depleted and the food prices are very high. Based on the Household economy analysis, the very poor and the poor are only able to cover 3 to 5 months of their basic food needs. The rest of the time they use negative coping strategies to satisfy their basic needs. The objective of the intervention is to build their capacity in food production, increase the food availability at household level throughout the year, prevent the use of negative coping strategies and develop self reliance. NRC is also curenlty carrying out a comprehensive assessment in targeted area in Menaka and Gao on livelihoods and food security needs for the population affected by the crisis but specifically long term IDPs and nomadic/pastoralist communities and a pilot will be defined based on the results. NRC has been active in Mali since 2013, providing education, shelter, food security & livelihoods, and information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) to respond to the needs of vulnerable displaced-affected Malians (IDPs), as well as the most vulnerable people among host communities, returnees and repatriated. Since 2016, we also have a dedicated emergency response mechanism (RRM) that supports people forced to suddenly flee their homes with food and non-food items. In Mali, NRC also runs a pilot humanitarian mediation project to reduce violence and improve protection of civilians. NRC FS&L activities are designed to respond to the needs of vulnerable people affected by conflict by addressing the intermediate and long-term food security challenges through early recovery responses. To do this, we focus on supporting income-generating opportunities for vulnerable households, for example through setting up community gardening activities to facilitate the economic recovery of the target households, strengthening their food production skills and allowing them to produce enough food to cover for both their food needs and non-food related expenditures. We also work on setting up village savings and loans associations to reinforce beneficiaries’ livelihoods.

Activities

This project will have a focus on strengthening the capacity of the target groups in terms of livelihood income diversification and employability linked with start-up capital to enable them to engage in diversifying their livelihoods opportunities. NRC will also develop climate smart agriculture through establishment of community garden with dry season farming facilities, livestock support in pastoral areas, and capacity building related to basic nutrition practices (diet diversification, food hygiene and good child feeding practices using locally available foods), in collaboration with local partners and other international NGOs. To ensure long term household food availability and accessibility, this project will also support either the establishment of new or the maintenance of cereal banks, village saving and loan groups (VSLA) and Income generating activities (IGA). Based on multi-sector needs and vulnerability assessments and analyses, assistance and support will be provided through combined NRC’s core competencies responses to ensure Livelihood Provisioning, Livelihood Protection & Livelihood Enhancement that will take place conjointly to build community resilience to future shocks and ensure durable solutions. As for instance, the ICLA (Information Counselling and Legal Assistance) team will support the livelihood activities through conflict prevention and management but also in securing and delineating agricultural lands documentation, in addition to community awareness on Legal Identity and Housing Land and Property Rights. All the project is defined based on women needs and main challenges in accessing land or IGA and will be the main target of this project.
