Project Summary

The Government of Bangladesh, with support from UNHCR and other UN agencies, provides infrastructure and essential services to Rohingya refugees/FDMNs on Bhasan Char. A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed in October 2021 established a joint framework for protection and humanitarian efforts aligned with government priorities. This includes access to shelter, food, health, water, sanitation, learning in the Myanmar Curriculum in the Myanmar language, as well as the ability to engage in livelihoods, capacity-building activities, and skills development commensurate with opportunities available in Rakhine State in Myanmar
By 2024, 37,000 Rohingya refugees had voluntarily relocated to Bhasan Char, with ongoing joint registration and documentation efforts. The humanitarian response aims to complement the government’s leadership, ensuring equitable standards and services for refugees both on Bhasan Char and in Cox’s Bazar camps. Voluntary relocation and need-based movement between the two sites continue.
The humanitarian community also supports disaster risk reduction and emergency preparedness, while promoting sustainability and self-reliance through education, skills training, and livelihoods.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The 2024 Bhasan Char Needs Assessment (BCNA) identified Protection, Healthcare, Shelter, Food, and Education as the most reported needs. Health services, particularly mental health, and psychosocial support (MHPSS) are limited, with significant gaps in maternal and reproductive care. Protection risks for women and children remain high, especially in public spaces like latrines and bathing areas, where inadequate nighttime lighting causes safety concerns—60% of households reported such issues. WASH facilities need improvement, particularly for persons with disabilities. Although school enrollment is strong, more attention is needed for older children. Youth face barriers to education, vocational training, and community participation, with 84% excluded from planning and 69% from leadership roles due to gender constraints and lack of skills.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Inter-agency activities will aim to prevent and minimize any negative effects of interventions that may increase vulnerabilities to physical and psychosocial risks. Efforts will focus on ensuring equitable access to assistance and services, with special attention to vulnerable groups facing barriers. Mechanisms will be established to enable refugees to assess interventions, raise concerns, and strengthen self-protection capacities. These will support access to rights, including shelter, food, water, sanitation, health, and education. This will be ensured by supporting different sectors when conducting regular needs assessments and participatory discussions with the beneficiaries to ensure that the services provided are tailored to their changing needs; also supporting regular monitoring & evaluation of the quality of the services provided.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The inter-agency response has taken into account the varying capacities, needs, and protection risks faced by refugee women, men, girls, and boys targeted in the program. These considerations have been integrated into inter-agency assessments, planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and evaluation. Disability inclusion will be systematically mainstreamed across interventions. Activities will continue to facilitate access to services and assistance for diverse individuals, including children, adolescents, persons with disabilities, and older persons.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Inter-agency actions related to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) include the protection hotline, participatory assessments, feedback boxes, public information boards, community meetings, interviews, counseling sessions, and home visits. AAP efforts also encompass adherence to the Code of Conduct and a Zero-Tolerance Policy for Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment. Training and awareness initiatives are extended to community members, equipping them with the knowledge to identify and safely report SEA.Additionally, as part of the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM), the Centralized Automated Information Processing Platform (CAIPP) is being rolled out in Bhasan Char, further strengthening AAP efforts.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is a core commitment of the UN Secretary-General and the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). In line with this commitment, the Bhasan Char Inter-Agency Coordination has pledged to mainstream PSEA across all Sector activities. All partners are required to prevent, mitigate, and respond to SEA cases, with inter-agency coordination playing a key facilitating role. As a minimum standard, all Sector members and partners operating on Bhasan Char must complete mandatory PSEA training. In addition, the Bhasan Char PSEA network offers ongoing technical support, capacity-building initiatives, and resources to enhance SEA prevention and response mechanisms.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Bhasan Char faces multiple hazards, including tropical storms, cyclones, flooding, destructive winds, lightning, and fire. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) measures are integrated into sectoral responses.The SMS Sector, in collaboration with the Government and key stakeholders, strengthens emergency preparedness through continuous monitoring, drills, simulations, and the prepositioning of relief items. Volunteers under the Cyclone Preparedness Program (CPP) and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) initiatives are trained and mobilized to support community communication during disasters.Partners are working to enhance the resilience of refugees to climate and environmental risks by expanding access to sustainable lighting and connectivity. Key efforts include:Maintaining and expanding solarization in refugee houses and WASH facilities,Enhancing electrification of community facilities while optimizing energy use,Upgrading the 1 MW solar plant and electrical distribution network,Advocating for the connection of essential services to the solar grid,Strengthening stand-alone solar power solutions.To promote environmental sustainability, partners are reducing greenhouse gas emissions, supporting renewable energy solutions (hybrid solar and biogas), and encouraging bicycle use as an alternative to vehicles.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project has incorporated emergency preparedness and response measures to enhance readiness and resilience in crises. These measures include:Business Continuity Plan (BCP): Ensuring the uninterrupted operation of essential services during disruptions and maintaining access to critical resources.Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs): Establishing clear guidelines for timely and coordinated responses to emergencies such as floods and cyclones.Capacity Building and Community Resilience: Training and equipping community volunteers for emergency response while implementing community-based preparedness initiatives.By strengthening local capacity, these efforts empower refugees to manage risks independently, fostering greater resilience on Bhasan Char.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will adopt a holistic approach that recognizes the role of adolescents and youth in fostering a protective environment and promoting gender equality. Adolescents will be engaged through clubs and participatory initiatives, enabling them to identify safe and unsafe spaces and understand the underlying factors. The project will also implement activities focused on building a safer community through GBV and SEA risk mitigation and awareness efforts. Partners recognize that one in three refugees is young, with 56% of Bhasan Char's population being children. In response, they will provide GBV information, life skills, and opportunities to support young people—particularly adolescent girls—in reaching their full potential.Young people will have access to youth-responsive safe spaces, empowering them through life skills initiatives and strengthening their community engagement. They will actively participate in decision-making processes that impact their lives, contributing to the advancement of human rights and key development issues such as health, education, and employment.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

With global priorities shifting, humanitarian funding is decreasing while needs continue to grow. As a result, self-reliance and resilience are central to livelihood initiatives aimed at reducing refugee dependency. All planned activities and interventions are designed to strengthen refugees' ability to sustain themselves economically. These initiatives are not only viable but also structured for long-term sustainability, enabling participants to generate income and maintain their livelihoods beyond humanitarian aid.
