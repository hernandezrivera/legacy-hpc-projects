1 - Needs

The protection environment for refugees and asylum-seekers remains in need of improvement, with significant gaps in relevant national policy and legal frameworks for the protection for persons of concern. While UNHCR continues to leverage on the strategic locations of its offices in Sudan to ensure delivery of quality and effective protection response, insecurity and inaccessibility factors that affect access to refugee hosting areas for biometric registration remains a concern. The capacity of GoS and other relevant institutions to effectively discharge their responsibilities stemming from the national legal and policy framework is in need of improvement, as processes are of insufficient quality, are not fully accessible to all, and are frequently slow and unpredictable. Progress towards ambitious GCR pledges is not yet seriously underway in key areas, preventing persons of concern in Sudan from enjoying the rights to which they are entitled under international law, domestic provisions, and according to state pledges. Improvements in key areas of intervention including registration, RSD, child protection, GBV, legal assistance and refugee law and policy and COR capacity will contribute to an environment where duty bearers are empowered to effectively fulfil their obligations and , refugees, asylum seekers and stateless persons enjoy their rights without discrimination. UNHCR Sudan aims to sustain a strong protection environment with effective responses to protection risks, including through empowerment of communities to mitigate protection risks, strengthen their resilience and engagement in peaceful coexistence with host populations. Action will be taken to empower UNHCR staff, government, partners, and diverse stakeholders to reach these objectives. UNHCR Sudan will prioritize education, GBV, child protection, mixed migration, peaceful coexistence with host communities, access to justice and interagency coordination between UNHCR and relevant stakeholders. This impact statement aims at realizing rights-based and equal access to services and sustainable solutions for PoC to have an improved quality of life and security regardless of their nationality or where they live. UNHCR, working closely with others, will pursue a path to drive a change towards the realization of and enjoyment of rights by refugees, asylum seekers and other POCs. The casual path to change will include the below interventions:If (1) policies and national systems promote protection, non-discrimination between refugee groups, are gender-responsive and age-appropriate; if (2) quality protection systems and solutions services are equitable and accessible to all; if (3) all stakeholders are supported and their capacity built to perform critical protection tasks related to protection and to mixed migration issues – THEN – (1) Core protection services are accessible and effective for all persons of concern, and (2) National systems are AGD inclusive, functional, accessible, and effective providing equitable access to quality and responsive protection and solutions services, and (3) the needs and rights of persons of concern are appropriately reflected in the policies, systems and services of the country.

2 - Activities/Outputs

GoS capacity to effectively conduct continuous registration and documentation of refugees, including the provision of refugee identity cards will continue to be supported. UNHCR support to the GoS to strengthen civil documentation and legal identity for PoCs will be enhanced to prevent and reduce the risks of statelessness as well as advocacy conducted for the GoS’ adhesion to the 1954 and 1961 Statelessness related conventions.UNHCR key areas of intervention will include improved access to adequate shelter and housing, NFIs, quality education for boys and girls, health and nutrition services, WASH, access to safe and renewable energy services among others. In furtherance of the global CBI policy, the operation will also continue to explore and expand use of CBI as a programmatic response modality for delivering assistance to populations of concern.The operation will maintain focus on coordination, collaboration with other entities to deliver quality, AGD sensitive and evidence- based programming informed by monitoring and needs assessments, and will aim at addressing priority needs, mitigating protection risks and reducing vulnerabilities.UNHCR will strengthen efforts to achieve the inclusion of PoCs in national systems and development programs through advocacy, capacity building of national institutions, coordination, inclusive partnerships, and joint and/or complementary programming. As such, UNHCR will continue to provide support to the MoE to ensure the gradual inclusion of refugee children in the national education system. Similarly, the inclusion of refugees in national preventive and curative health & nutrition health systems will be improved and strengthened. Capacity building of wide range of stakeholders on protection mainstreaming to support integration of protection principles in humanitarian and development activities will be conducted. Finally, the contingency Plan will remain in place and updated to respond to emergencies.As part of its theory of change, and in a bid to ensure delivery of its mandate and responsibilities, UNHCR will work as a catalyst and advocate for the inclusion of Refugees, Asylum Seekers and hosting communities in the overall Govt of Sudan plans, as well as Humanitarian and development response mechanisms. UNHCR will work with stakeholders in a system wide approach to humanitarian response thorough developmental approach that in return will support the peace building and stability. UNHCR overall aim and contribution will be geared towards building a Humanitarian- Development-Peace nexus-oriented programming with its PoCs integrated at all stages of the cycle, from inception to implementation and closure. UNHCR will provide technical and financial support as well as act as catalyst of the system approach that will gradual limit its direct financial support and implementation role to bear minimum. As a priority intervention, while continuing with its care and maintenance activities for the course of 2022-23, UNHCR will introduce and integrate a developmental approach to its programme in Eastern Sudan. Bringing on board various humanitarian and development actors, a “refugee hosting area” project for the duration of 2022-23 will lead to a comprehensive, system approach to HDP-N programming that will go forward into 2024-25. The overall goal of finding a wide range of solutions for the PoCs by end 2025, with technical support and capacity building.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability toaffected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particularneeds of such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design,implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensurethe following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2)equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms,with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that willfeed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation,participation and community empowerment. The sector will work with partners tostrengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abusethrough awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacityas Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such ashousing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs.HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG toharmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durableshelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providingICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm andmainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.
