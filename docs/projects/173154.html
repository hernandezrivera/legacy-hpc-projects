Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food and livelihoods security of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar through the implementation of multi-sectoral interventions. Refugees and host community will receive agricultural support that includes the provision of agricultural inputs and capacity building. Improved vegetable seeds, farm tools to enhance agricultural cultivation, training on improved agronomic practices and vermicompost promotion will be provided to selected beneficiaries. The home garden initiative will be implemented through a demonstration garden approach where selected farmers will be used as a training ground to introduce best practices. Farmers who reside along the coastal areas who are affected by salinity will be targeted for the saline resistant crop production where saline tolerant seeds will be provided. Beneficiaries who do not have diversified means for their livelihoods will be targeted and receive support in income generating activities. This activity will provide particular emphasis to women, elderly and persons with disability. Businesses will be selected based on compatibility to these group of beneficiaries. SI will provide business management training, professional mentorship, and start-up either in cash or in-kind form. The business development initiative will include portable skill development for refugees who aspire to enrol in vocational skill development. The project will provide support to strengthen the DRR community structures at Union and Ward level through the development of initiative that build the capacity of Disaster Management Committees through capacity building, supporting their monthly and quarterly meetings, and facilitation of community risk assessments. People who rely on daily labour for their livelihoods will be targeted for the cash for Work initiatives where the primary focus is to develop community mitigation schemes in the targeted areas. The project activities will be implemented in line with Cluster and sector recommendations to ensure standardized programming while avoiding duplication. The project support will be provided with an analysis of the gender and age requirements of different groups. Differential needs will be identified and assistance will be tailored accordingly. Though the project has a standardized assistance package for different activities, special emphasis will be given to women when it comes to income-generating activities. Female-headed households will be prioritised for agricultural assistance while HH who host vulnerable groups (children, disabled, old people and others) for the cash for work initiatives if there is an able person in the HH for labour activities. The project will also be considered the key questions as indicated in the RGA- “In the Shadows of the Pandemic: The Gendered Impact of COVID-19 on Rohingya and Host Communities October 2020”, SI will ensure the project considers the key priorities in the food security sector in regards to the economic consequences of COVID-19 and will provide tailored IGA assistance to different gender groups. Age and gender disaggregated data will be gathered and reported on throughout the project period.

1 - Identified Needs

Almost three years after the violence in Myanmar and massive influx of Rohingya in Cox’s Bazar, the situation is at a corner point, as basic needs of the refugee population rely only on humanitarian assistance, and both refugees and host communities lack sustainable solutions to cover their needs and increase their self-reliance. The JMSNA (2020) indicated a significant drop in the food consumption score and increase negative coping strategies to access food for their household. In Cox’s Bazar district, the rate of Household Food Insecurity Access Scale is 34.1% for moderately food insecure households while severe food insecurity represents 46.9%. The lack of access to livelihoods opportunity for refugees has a direct impact on food security, and poor nutrition outcomes among children. Rohingya refugees face multiple challenges related to livelihoods and markets opportunities. Though markets are expanding, the prices increase jeopardise abilities of communities in fulfilling their basic needs. The refugee influx has affected the labour market for host communities as the daily wage rate is reduced because of labour availability while price of basic commodities increased. On top of the impact of COVID-19, these has adversely affected the income level of host communities. Sustainable livelihoods are difficult to maintain for refugees and host communities when they face frequent health and natural disasters. Bangladesh is one of the countries with highest vulnerability to climate change and disasters. According to the World Bank (2018), more than 80% of the population is exposed to floods, earthquakes, and droughts while more than 70% is exposed to cyclones. As a result of climate change, disaster vulnerability is increasing with time, despite efforts made by the government. Therefore, building of the resilience, individually and collectively, allows to anticipate, cope, and mitigate the consequences of disasters is of paramount importance. The current outbreak of COVID-19 is the most recent disaster faced by Bangladesh in this regard. It must be considered in link with its direct and indirect consequences on the living conditions of refugees and host communities. Women and girls are more affected as they are adopting negative coping strategies while men dominate the decision-making power at household level. The effect of disaster is differential based on gender, age, disability, and social status. With this understanding in mind, SI will conduct a rapid assessment to identify the differential needs of different social groups with due consideration of gender, age, and disability. In line with the findings, different strategies will be implemented to address the differential needs of men and women, elderly and person with disability. At least 50% of the project beneficiaries will be women while 50% are men with the age category above 18 years. The SI IGA market analyses identified different businesses that can easily be operated by women, elderly and people with disability. Therefore, the assessment recommendation will be utilized in the project implementation. SI will ensure the participation of different social groups in the design during need assessment, designing of tailor-made programmes, implementation, monitoring and evaluation which includes the need for programme adaptations. SI ensures participation of project beneficiaries in need assessment, analysis, and project modification. This flexible approach will empower project participants to have a voice in the need identification and on deciding the targeting parameters which is in line with the project purpose. Feedback will be collected to ensure recommendations are considered to inform programme decisions. SI has a complaint response mechanism SOP which defines how complaints are collected, processed, and responded to complainants.
