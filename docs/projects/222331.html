Project Summary

The Rescue Mission under this project, proposes integrated WASH interventions and GBV mainstreaming through implementing interventions through Rapid Respond Mechanism and Static response to priority 1 and 2 counties of Jonglei State ( Pibor, Fangak, Duk and Bor South etc), this intervention aims to contribute in reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality through integrated programming in Nutrition Centres/OTP sites, increase access to safe and basic WASH services to reduce WASH related GBV in the selected priority areas of Pibor, Fangak, Bor South and Duk Counties including response to prevention of cholera. The targeted areas are prone to floods, water scarcity due to extended dryness, disease outbreak, displacement of people due conflict and the Sudanese returnees. The project ensures beneficiaries have safe access to quality basic, gender responsive services including safe water and sanitation facilities through Repair and rehabilitation of 53 nonfunctional water points with cluster approved flood-resilient design (50 Hand pumps and 3 water yards), Construction of 6 new boreholes in Pibor county to increase access to safe drinking water at a secure location.
To ensure sustainability, TERM will provide hand pump repair tool kits as well as conduct refresher training to water user committees (WUCs) at all repaired bores and newly installed water points in order to build their capacity on basic maintenance and management of their water points as well as train Pump mechanics
In locations affected by or prone to seasonal floods or using unprotected water sources, TERM will provide water purifiers like PUR and Aqua tabs to promote water treatment. TERM will train community hygiene promoters who will carry out Hygiene promotion awareness, standard kits paired with hygiene promotion shall be provided to SAM children/caretakers to reduce vulnerability to Acute Watery Diarrhea, cholera and promotion of good sanitation and hygiene practices.
Construction and rehabilitation of institutional latrines in collaboration and coordination with health and nutrition actors, TERM plans to construct/rehabilitate 25 stances of gender segregated permanent institutional latrines stances grouped into appropriate blocks. Hand washing facilities shall be provided to each constructed latrine facilities to support hand washing.
Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of reproductive age group accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions. TERM will ensure ownership and sustainability of the response through involvement of the targeted community throughout the life cycle of the intervention. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, through accountability to affected populations (AAP), working with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities (PWD), pregnant and lactating women (PLW) incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations
To mitigate against GBV risks, TERM will involve women and other at-risk groups in the design and construction of the latrines and conduct a GBV risk analysis/safety audit to ensure access and safe usage of the facilities. The proposed intervention will reach a target population of 25,235 vulnerable Individuals (Host 7400, IDP’s 9013, Returnees 8,822, People with disabilities 250).
The project will directly contribute toward achievement of the WASH Cluster specific objectives; CO1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition. CO2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation. CO4: Community member’s exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure and thereby to the overall humanitarian response plan.

1. Needs

Access to safe drinking water and Sanitation facilities continues to be challenging, and water-borne diseases have continued to spread due to lack of clean water, poor sanitation and hygiene practices, Humanitarian situation in South Sudan is kept worsening, the 2024 HNRP indicates that approximately 5.6 million people require WASH assistance in 2025 in South Sudan, the data also indicates that 28 counties including Pibor, Fangak, Bor South and Duk have extreme WASH conditions often coinciding with high malnutrition rates. A significant portion of the South Sudan population (41%) lacks access to improved water sources and 37% need to travel 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing particular hardship to persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. Access to sanitation is even more limited with prevalent open defecation, 88 per cent of the population lacks access to improved latrines, according to FSNMS 2023. The compounding challenges of displacement, insecurity, flooding, economic difficulties and influx of returnees from Sudan further strain the already limited resources. According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART surveys' findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in South Sudan remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. The situation further attributed and worsened due to multiple shocks and unaddressed root causes of need of endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges, and economic and climatic shocks. All these challenges impacted the vulnerable people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards, and coping mechanisms, exacerbated by marginalization of women, youth, and persons with disabilities. The WASH cluster 2025, HNRP briefing note revealed that 5.01 million people are in need WASH services where cluster targeted 2.25 million people in priority 1, 2 and part of priority 3 counties. South Sudan, Floods Snapshot (UNOCHA, 1 November 2024) reported that about 1.4 million people are impacted by floods in 43 counties. Most affected states are Jonglie, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Warrap, Lakes and Upper Nile states with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising about half of the affected population. In locations that are currently hosting displaced individuals, the overwhelming demand has often led to water points being stretched beyond capacity, leaving many without sufficient access to drinking water. This dire situation worsens with each new wave of displacement, as additional individuals find themselves with no access to any water points at all. Insecurity remains a pervasive challenge, further complicating the efforts to improve water access. Moreover, acute food insecurity is driving millions of South Sudanese into precarious livelihoods, leading to deteriorating health outcomes across the nation. According to the Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HNRP) for 2024, it is estimated that approximately 8.9 million people in South Sudan currently require humanitarian assistance.On 20th October 2024, South Sudan Health Ministry declared cholera outbreak that has since spread to other regions like Rubkona, Canal Pigi and Fangak necessitating an urgent need for humanitarian assistance to protect the lives of those affected by this crisis to convert the spread of the outbreak. The intertwined issues of poor access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services, coupled with alarming levels of food insecurity, are significantly undermining the health and well-being of vulnerable populations, particularly children, women, and the elderly, in regions such as Pibor, Fangak and Duk. This situation has resulted in a worrying prevalence of malnutrition and various waterborne diseases, including acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and skin and eye infections related to inadequate water handling.
