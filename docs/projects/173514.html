Project Summary

IOM will expand its Mental Health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) programme to a more systematic and comprehensive approach with engagement of community volunteers and health promoters. Access to quality reproductive health care services remains a challenge. IOM intends to enhance quality access to comprehensive services through the integration of MHPSS with Primary Health Care (PHC). IOM will also continue working with the health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, capacity building of health and mental health professionals, health information management, and emergency referral and support system. As part of the health promotional and preventive activities, IOM will partner with local NGOs to implement health outreach through Health Promoters and community volunteers. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and will continue to support the State Ministries of Health.
IOM partnered with the University of Maiduguri Teaching Hospital (UMTH) in setting-up and operating a 24-hospital bed and 2-ICU (intensive car eunit) bed isolation center for humanitarian workers in north-east Nigeria. The center also operates a COVID-19 laboratory that will primarily address the PCR (polymerise chain reaction) testing of humanitarian workers. IOM aims to continue the partnership with UMTH in operating the isolation center and COVID-19 laboratory for humanitarian workers in north-east Nigeria.
In coordination with the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), Federal Ministry of Health and related departments, World Health Organization and the UN Medical Services; IOM will leverage its health clinic infrastructure and capacity to contribute to the government’s effort to respond to COVID-19. IOM will continue to support the provision of COVID-19 testing services in Borno, FCT (Abuja) and Lagos. Activities include sample collection and testing for the UN staff, staff of International Non-Governmental Organizations (INGOs), diplomatic community and migrants using existing UMTH facilities.

1 - Needs

Across three states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) in north-east Nigeria, around 10.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance including life-saving essential health care services (OCHA 2020). In addition to the ongoing humanitarian emergency, the COVID-19 pandemic poses a significant social and economic threat to Nigeria, with a potentially devastating effect for the most vulnerable population who have endured a decade of conflict.The protracted crisis in north-east Nigeria has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, which has contributed to the destruction of health facilities and an incapacitated healthcare sector. 42 per cent of healthcare facilities in the BAY states are non-functional due to the conflict. Additionally, quality of care is unstable due to the volatile security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. An increase in quality service provision, enhanced technical capacity of the healthcare providers and the establishment of a robust monitoring mechanism are thus noted as critical issues by the agencies responding to the crisis. Furthermore, gaps in referral services have been strongly flagged by Health Sector partners currently providing the responses at PHC in camps and host communities, as a key need speciﬁcally the inability of patients to receive critically needed health care nor timely access emergency care in hospitals. Health facilities were targeted during the conflict and as a result many health facilities across BAY states are still non-functional or partially functional while those that are functioning with minimum capacity to deliver standard health care packages. In hard-to-reach areas / new operations areas, communities face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including lack of medical staff, unavailability of drugs and supplies, security barriers, transportation issues and damaged/destroyed health facilities. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines. The disruption in supply chain of medicines and medical supplies has compounded the problem. The dire living conditions of these IDPs together with the often-poor water and sanitation conditions contribute to increased health risks and higher vulnerability to the spread of outbreak prone communicable diseases, such as cholera. IDP returnees in newly accessible areas or IDPs living in “Satellite camps” have reportedly limited or even no access to health care services. In Borno State, the vast majority of public servants have not yet returned to conflict-affected areas, in part because they remain inhospitable. There is an urgent need to expand the provision of integrated basic PHC services, to prevent further deterioration of the health status of affected populations. Health services also need to be made available and accessible at the different levels of care, supported by a well-functioning referral mechanism, at and between the community, primary and secondary/higher care levels. While the Ministry of Health has made efforts in integrating the mental health through PHC, there is a need to further this approach to ensure increased access to mental health services. Gender-based violence (GBV), including sexual violence, is widespread in the region due to the ongoing conﬂict, insecurity, living conditions in the IDP camps and informal settlements, also inadequate WASH facilities, lack of safe access to ﬁrewood, and restrictions on freedom of movement in and out of camps. Furthermore, it is equally important to strengthen the referral system to address gaps in sexual and reproductive health including medical care of GBV survivors and MHPSS interventions through appropriate referral support.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Establish three Mobile Health Teams providing an integrated standard PHC service package, including referrals to secondary care. Activity 2: Identify and train local community volunteers on health promotion and community-based health and mental health interventions. Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to control of disease outbreaks and other public health events. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipment of 10 selected public health facilities to resume their function to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Conduct training for PHC workers with the use of the WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme Intervention Guide (mhGAP-IG), Psychological First Aid (PFA), and staff self-care using already trained trainers. Activity 6: Strengthen referral and provision of specialized Mental Health services at tertiary and secondary health facilities (e.g. Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital, State Specialist Hospital). Activity 7: Conduct an assessment on access of Mental Health services in PHC. Activity 8: Conduct awareness campaigns to increase concern, informing the targeted groups and attempts to change their behaviours promoting mental health and psychosocial well-being. Activity 9: Support the coordination of the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Sub Working Group (MHPSS SWG) through capacity building activities and provisions of technical support in close coordination with the State Ministry of HealthActivity 10: COVID Isolation facilities providing psychosocial support and training health personal on the isolation to prevent mental health problems due to isolation and tackle stigma/discrimination at community level.Activity 11: Continue the partnership with UMTH in operating the isolation center and COVID-19 laboratory for humanitarian workers in north-east NigeriaActivity 12: Provision of COVID-19 testing services for humanitarian workers, including sample collection and testing for the UN staff, staff of International Non-Governmental Organizations (INGOs), diplomatic community and migrants using existing UMTH facilities Protection, gender and MHPSS approach will be mainstreamed throughout all health interventions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM is conducting regular and continuous Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training to staff, partners/stakeholders, and beneficiaries (IDPs) to uphold the value of zero tolerance to PSEA as adopted by IOM and on protection principles and how to mainstream such protection principles in all sectors of humanitarian interventions. In addition, to continuously strengthening feedback mechanism through promoting active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and speciﬁc vulnerabilities, including those of women and girls, youth, elderly, and persons living with disabilities, among others.Additionally, the psychosocial approach is also tackling main threats related to stigma and discrimination to promote psychosocial wellbeing and social inclusion. IOM, through its Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit, operates an active Community Base Feedback and Complain Mechanism in most of the displacement sites in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States and this will ensure collection of essential feedback from the targeted populations, especially those related to sexual violence and related concerns. Focus Group Discussion sessions will IDPs, their representatives, Persons With Speciﬁc Needs such as persons living with disabilities, persons with mental health conditions, child headed, households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process In the provision of medical care to humanitarian workers who tested positive for COVID-19, the project shall ensure that due care be given to humanitarian workers who are more vulnerable than the others due to their pre-existing condition such as illness, age, pregnancy, etc. at the time when they test positive. As the need arises, humanitarian workers confined at the isolation center may also be linked to a welfare counselor to support the patient psychosocially through the process of treatment and recuperation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM, through its CCCM Unit, maintains active Community Based-eedback and Complain Mechanism in most of the displacement sites in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States, which will ensure effective collection of feedback from the targeted population, analysis and assimilation of findings to inform new intervention or adopt changes to ongoing support to meet assessed needs of the affected populations. Regular and continuous assessment of needs and concerns will be ensured through focus group discussions sessions with the targeted populations to ensure that services provided meet the peculiar needs and concerns of the targeted populations. Perception surveys will be regularly conducted to gauges the impact of the project to the targeted populations. Leveraging on its experience in collecting feedback from service end users through the humanitarian hubs customer satisfaction survey, IOM shall put in place a similar client feedback collection mechanism at the isolation center and COVID-19 laboratory, analyze the feedback gathered and make necessary adjustments in the service processes to better address the needs of the humanitarian workers served by the isolation center and the COVID-19 laboratory, taking into consideration their vulnerability and protection needs.
