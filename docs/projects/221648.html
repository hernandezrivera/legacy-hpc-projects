Project Summary

The Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon are facing an acute humanitarian crisis exacerbated by the ongoing political conflict that began in 2016. This turmoil has led to the displacement of 583,112 internally displaced persons (IDPs), the return of 459,605 individuals (former IDPs and refugees), and the presence of 65,224 Cameroonian refugees in Nigeria (UNHCR, March 2024). Additionally, the situation is compounded by recurring climate-related emergencies, heightening the demand for critical Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services.
A Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted in August/September 2023 across these regions identified education and WASH as the second and third most urgent community needs. Preliminary findings from the 2024 WASH Specific Needs Assessment (WSNA) indicate a concerning increase in open defecation within IDP settlements due to limited or nonexistent WASH resources. This deficiency severely impacts the social and economic well-being of IDPs and host communities, revealing significant protection concerns, increased vulnerabilities, and school dropout rates among adolescent girls.
The absence of adequate WASH infrastructure, particularly in schools and learning environments, poses a formidable barrier to children's education. Climatic events such as floods have further damaged existing WASH facilities, disrupted educational services, displaced families, and heightened the risk of cholera outbreaks. The lack of reliable WASH services in schools and child-friendly spaces, coupled with food insecurity and poor disaster preparedness, continues to challenge community resilience. Many children must travel long distances to collect drinking water, a situation mirrored across host communities where more than 40% of the urban and rural population lacks access to improved water sources, exposing them to economic and environmental risks. The WSNA reports that 70% of surveyed individuals spend over 30 minutes on round trips to unprotected, often unsafe water points, many of which have become worn out or abandoned due to overuse and neglect.
The WASH situation in the Southwest region remains dire, marked by frequent cholera outbreaks and flood disasters. Over 48% of the population relies on unimproved sanitation facilities, with about 25% practicing open defecation. Hygiene promoters from EPDA at UNICEF-supported Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) have observed that these conditions significantly hinder children's educational experiences and weaken community resilience to climate shocks and disease outbreaks.
To bolster social and climate resilience within affected communities and enhance educational environments, it is crucial to implement integrated solutions. These include rehabilitating and constructing WASH facilities, establishing climate resilient education hubs in existing school clubs, and developing local early warning alert systems. Such measures will ensure the safety of children, promote climate education, and provide gender-sensitive protection and WASH support, alongside unconditional cash assistance. These initiatives aim to engage host communities, IDPs, children, and adolescents in various activities that reinforce social and climate resilience, enhance learning opportunities, and improve participation. By supporting activities in protection, WASH, climate education, and capacity-building programs, communities can strengthen their resilience and improve children’s self-confidence, social connections, and overall well-being.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon are experiencing an acute and multifaceted humanitarian crisis driven by prolonged political conflict since 2016. This crisis has resulted in the significant displacement of 583,112 internally displaced persons (IDPs), the return of 459,605 former IDPs and refugees, and 65,224 Cameroonian refugees residing in Nigeria (UNHCR, March 2024). The compounded impact of climate-related emergencies has further intensified the urgent need for Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services.Recent assessments, including the 2023 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) and preliminary findings from the 2024 WASH Specific Needs Assessment (WSNA), underscore the critical nature of WASH, climate and education needs within these regions. The absence of adequate WASH infrastructure, particularly in schools and IDP settlements, has led to an alarming increase in open defecation and compromised the well-being of vulnerable populations. These deficits exacerbate protection concerns, elevate risks of disease outbreaks such as cholera, and contribute to higher school dropout rates, particularly among adolescent girls.Flood damage, poor disaster preparedness, and the overuse or abandonment of water points force many children and community members to travel long distances for unsafe water. With over 40% of the affected population lacking access to improved water sources, the broader community remains exposed to economic and environmental risks.This situation, observed by EPDA hygiene promoters in UNICEF-supported Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS), severely disrupts children's educational experiences and weakens community resilience to climate and health crises.To address these urgent challenges, comprehensive solutions are needed, including the rehabilitation and construction of WASH facilities, the introduction of climate resilient education initiatives, the development of early warning systems through trainings and community engagements especially in flood prone areas, and the protection of degraded and fragile landscapes close to human settlements in the host communities. Such measures will promote child safety and support educational continuity and foster social and climate resilience. The provision of gender-sensitive protection, targeted WASH interventions, and cash assistance will empower IDPs, host communities, and vulnerable children to participate actively, boosting their social stability, emotional well-being, and overall resilience. To note, the climate-resilient education program shall be engaging, age-appropriate, and action-oriented. It should aim to build foundational knowledge, skills, and values that promote environmental stewardship, adaptation, and resilience to climate change. It will focus on equipping children in emergency settings with fundamental knowledge and practical skills to promote safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) practices while fostering climate resilience. The program is designed to be simple, interactive, and culturally adaptable to various emergency contexts. the following modules shall be facilitated in school climate education clubs: Climate Change and Emergencies, Safe Water Practices, Sanitation and Waste Management, Hygiene Practices, Water Conservation and Climate Resilience, Preventing Waterborne Diseases, Emergency WASH Preparedness, Community Engagement and Advocacy.
