Project Summary

Protection needs remain the one of the most important priorities in South Sudan. In Jonglei and Unity State, protection concerns including GBV continue remain a high risk for vulnerable mainly women and girls. Cultural norms, traditional gender roles, regional conflict, militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding, and revenge killings are some of the factors contributing to this plague. There is Urgent needs of Gender based violence prevention, mitigation, and comprehensive response.
Through this project INTERSOS aims to strengthen the prevention and response to GBV risks in in some of the counties with the GBV catastrophic severity (Ayod, Pibor, Rubkona and Mayendi) and extreme severity (Nyirol). The intervention will seek to address the immediate needs of GBV survivors and women at risk through establishment of women and girl friendly spaces where women can be empowered through life skills acquisition trainings and access to services and opportunities, Case management services and Psychosocial support services. To promote durable solutions and challenging negative social norms a strong component of GBV prevention has also been foreseen in the form of awareness through a community approach.

1 - Needs

8.9 million people are in need in South Sudan in 2022, this is 200 000 more than 2021, with 5.6 million in needs of protection, among them 53% of women and 58% of children. (HNO, 2022).In South Sudan, women and girls have been experienced Gender based violence for a long period now. Studies shows that majority of women and girls have reported being survivors of SGBV, a quarter of them being less than 18 years. Moreover, almost half of the Gender based violence incidents reported were Intimate partner Violence (IPV), other type on GBV experienced by women were perpetrated by people that they don’t know, this can occur during clashes, raids or displacement. Gender inequality, unequal power relationship as well as harmful gender norms are among the reason nurturing this issue.In addition to that, Conflict, as well as natural disaster has exacerbated food insecurity and displacement – an estimated 2 million internally displaced people- which has worsened the protection situation including Gender-Based violence. In addition to worsen physical and mental wellbeing of affected populations, people are turning into negative coping mechanism such as early and force marriage, IPV, child labor and sex for survivor. Jonglei and Unity state are two of the counties with the most concerns in humanitarian needs. Pibor, Ayod, Nyirol in Jonglei State and Rubkona and Mayendit in Unity are some of the worst affected area with: 57 thousand people are in extreme needs and 57 thousand are in catastrophic need in Pibor, 29.1 thousand are in extreme needs and 19.4 thousand are in catastrophic needs in Ayod, 21.3 thousand are in extreme needs and 14.2 thousand in catastrophic needs in Nyirol, and in Unity State 51.3 thousands people are in extreme needs and 34.2 thousand in catastrophic needs in Rubkona and 10.6 thousands are in extreme needs and 7.1 thousand are in catastrophic needs in Mayendit. In addition to food insecurity, the states are also the most affected by conflict, including revenge killingsand intercommunal fights. In many clashes, women and girls are the most at risk ofviolence, South Sudan is not an exception. In a protection monitoring done in Jonglei in2021, data collected show an imported degree of protection concerns affecting mainly women and girls. In Pibor, Lankein and Ayod, participants reported that parents tend to marry their children at a young age. Moreover, in these locations all respondents highlighted sexual violence as one of the most common protection risks. Concerning Unity state, in data collected in FSNMS+, households reported that girls are at risk of 65.5% and GBV and sexual exploitation 63.1%. In Mayendit, through a protection project implemented in Mayendit in 2020-2021, INTERSOS team identified 340 GBV cases with115 being girls.According to data collected by INTERSOS during protection monitoring and needs assessments, all respondents still believe that Gender-Based violence is culturally acceptable. Women, girls, boys, and men still at risk of Gender-Based violence as protection risks remain high and according to HNO 2022, the situation in 2022 will most likely deteriorate as vulnerabilities of people continue to grow.

2 - Project activities

Activities will be implemented in 5 locations: Pibor, Ayod and Nyirol in Jongeli State and Manyandit and Rubkona in Unity Sate. Following different assessment that highlighted the increased of GBV risk for women, girls, men and boys, and the gaps in response services, INTERSOS will tailored its response in order to respond to the urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and specialized GBV response services. 1. INTERSOS case workers will provide comprehensive case management for Gender based violence survivors and women at risk of GBV. In order to have an effective referral system, INTERSOS will regularly conduct a service mapping and update the referral pathway. Identification and referral will be done by INTERSOS protection staff, INTERSOS other projects’ staff and referrals from other agencies and self-referral. The service mapping will also contribute to identify existing services and gaps in order to know how to address the gaps with community members and other actors. After identification, caseworkers will register, assess, and create a tailored action plan with the beneficiary to meet his/her physical and psychological needs; after the implementation of the case plan, which will be done through internal or external referrals to provide the relevant services, the caseworker will follow up on the implementation of the action plan and will close the case according to case management SOPs. 2. INTERSOS will establish and/or strengthen Women and girls’ friendly space in safe and accessible locations for all beneficiaries to attend. Women, girls, and other community members will participate in choosing the space. The safe spaces will be equipped to host structured PSS activities as well as unstructured activities for women, girls, boys and men, and the safe space will have a separate, safe and confidential room to hold Case management sessions with survivors and women at risk. 3. INTERSOS will provide both structured and unstructured psychosocial support including individual counselling. Structured psychosocial support activities will be conducted by both trained PSS facilitators and case workers and will be done according to INTERSOS’ psychosocial well-being toolkit. INTERSOS will measure perception of increased resilience of those who participate in structured PSS activities through already developed and used pre/posttests. Results will be compiled by the Protection technical person with assistance from INTERSOS MEAL team. 4. INTERSOS will conduct capacity building including training, facilitating access to information, and coaching to protection teams and other frontliners. Subjects will be including protection principles, GBV case management approaches and guidelines, PSS and PFA interventions, and protection information management (PIM). The aim of this equipped frontliner and other community members with skills and knowledge that will helps them to provide effective protection services. 5. INTERSOS shall provide dignity kits for girls and women at reproductive age in all the target location combined with dignity kit utilization awareness sessions during the distribution. 6. INTERSOS will contribute in prevention and mitigation of Gender-Based violence and contribute in transforming negative social norms into positive ones through conducting awareness activities in the communities of Pibor, Ayod, Lankein, Rubkona and Mayendit. This will participate in creating a protective and safe environment for women and girls. The awareness will be done through mass awareness session, small group session (including tea conversation and discussion with community leaders), and door-to-door information sharing. Awareness will be raised on gender roles and human rights with a strong focus on the rights of women and girls. In addition, information will be share on sexual exploitation, abuse and the reporting mechanism available in the community.

3 - Project indicators

Measuring the impact of the Gender-based violence intervention will be done by a MEAL plan developed to measure both outcome and outputs of the project in a quantitative and qualitative way. The measure of outcome result will include the collection of a baseline, to have a base against which to measure the success of the intervention. Additionally, indicators measuring overall satisfaction of beneficiary will be measured on the regular basis through PDM exercise and quality surveys performed quarterly in all locations. The MEAL plan will include the following indicators: 1. # of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through case management. 2. # of women and girls who accessed services in WGFS. 3. # of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities,). 4. # of women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness-raising activities (GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting). 5. # of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV guiding principles and response services(Case mgt, PSS etc). 6. % of conflict-affected vulnerable individuals receiving group-based PSS who report perception of increased resiliency. 7. Number of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits (disaggregated by age) as part of addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation measure.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff will held information sessions with community members and community protection committees on the ground and add to a pre-established database all feedback received from community members and the beneficiaries will the measures and addressed.. The database will be shared with INTERSOS coordination that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, complaint will be collected through different channel including complaint boxes and PSEA focal points and will be share with the PSEA referent at coordination level. Each complaint and concern will be assessed and investigate if needed and a feedback will be provided to the person that raised the complaint.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

INTERSOS will conduct regular information sessions and focus group discussions with community members to share information about the project and receive feedback from the community. Different groups from the community will be represented including people living with disabilities, women, and girls. In addition to that, INTERSOS will work closely with community-based committees representing all groups from the community. Moreover, during door-to-door awareness raising, the team will share relevant information about the project and will collect feedback from community members. From information received from the community, internal meetings will be organized to understand the community feedback on existing services and additional needs identified by those who closely work with members of the community. This will inform any changes or adaptation to the intervention in regard to delivery.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

INTERSOS is committed to prevent acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by INTERSOS employees and related personal. INTERSOS PSEA policy commits INTERSOS to achieve full implementation of the six core principles related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse adopted in 2002. All INTERSOS staff and volunteers sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct and undergo a PSEA mandatory training as part of the onboarding process. In addition, recurrent trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. INTERSOS will also ensure a confidential reporting and mechanisms of any cases regarding SEA. For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, a focal point will be selected to collect complaints, he/she will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA referent in mission in Juba.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will oversee reaching all the community using door to door and information sessions and FGD to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality survey questions will be included in the PDM exercises done after every distribution. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly women, children and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this mean.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. - The risk is medium: INTERSOS will involve the camp leaders and local authorities in identifying the most vulnerable persons based on the criteria set and registration done by INTERSOS local staffs (Hygiene promotors). Distribution of Hygiene kits, dignity kits, solar laps and other supplies to the registered beneficiaries will be done in an orderly manner, during the day and in a safe place proximity to the beneficiaries’ residences. Door to door distribution will be considered to the most vulnerable e.g disables and persons stigmatized due to GBV incidence.Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Staff will have to sign the code of conduct when signing the contract and before activities start all teams will receive PSEA and Child protection training. Additionally, specific PSEA complaint mechanisms will be put in place through the PSEA focal points within the CRM committee to ensure complaints reach Juba and the internal audit as early as possible.Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures: in each CRM it will be ensured at least on representative of each marginalized group is represented in the committee to guarantee feedback from these vulnerable groups is received. Additionally, during PDM exercises, a percentage of the sample interviewed will have to come from every vulnerable group.Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. Mitigation measures: Child might not access standard complaint mechanisms including suggestion box and or address their complaints to the CRM committee. Each staff in CFS and case workers will be trained to address any complaint, additionally child friendly quality survey mechanisms will be set up to encourage children to provide feedback on a regular basis.Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. Mitigation measures: To ensure external stakeholders don’t interfere with the selection of beneficiaries, a strong awareness component will be established through which it will be explained only INTERSOS team can assess vulnerability of the different households and therefore nobody that hasn’t been assessed by internal staff can be considered eligible for assistance. To guarantee a transparent process after the vulnerability assessment, a selection committee including INTERSOS staff, members of the community and local authorities will be established to finalize the selection following the specific prioritization criteria established in advance.
