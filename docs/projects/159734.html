Project Summary

The project targets 10 primary schools of learners 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls), 50 teachers (25 male, 25 female) 20 CECs (10 male and 10 female) namely ‛Eel Munye, Ayuub, ‛Ali Banaadir, Buulo Mareer, Gaywaarow, KM50, Shalanbood, Tima ‛Adde, Busleey Daauud and Bakaal Primary schools in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts of Lower Shabelle region affected by crisis.
AYUUB in response to its recent KM50 Inter-Agency Rapid Assessment September 2019 and Marka Education Needs Assessment Report 06_10_2019 identified some of the needs of the conflict affected learners in the larger parts of Lower Shabelle and has proposed this integrated project Education along with strong child protection activities with the purpose of retaining learners in existing schools and enroll new IDPs at school in mitigating the case of high school dropout rate and all kinds of child protection violations. The project will strive to achieve the outcome of enhancing quality learning opportunities by ensuring emergency affected children have equal access to education, are engaged in life-saving activities that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion through; provision of quality Teaching and Learning Materials, school feeding, improving WASH facilities, Provision of safe drinking water, improve physical and mental growth of learners through child protection and Teacher incentives.
Protection aspects will provide protective learning environments for most vulnerable learners through; Training of 50 teachers (25 female) and 20 CECs (10 female) on child protection issues including Psycho-social Support (PSS) and disaster risk reduction (DRR) in 10 targeted schools, In addition, this project will consider CTC and community dialogue consultation and social mobilization as an entry point to influence the community for child protection aspects. AYUUB will conduct protection mapping to assess available protection service providers so children with protection concerns are referred to get access to protection and medical services.
The project will train 50 teachers on pedagogy, lesson planning and child centered approaches including child safeguarding while 20 CECs will be included with teachers in the training on child protections and management of school resources to better address child protection and Education concerns brought about by the effects of the 2017 drought in the schools that AYUUB has been supporting with the help of volunteer teachers all of whom were once learners in the same schools and now giving back to the community where they were brought up. This will also act as entry points to address all child protection issues starting from the school sensitization, training, psychosocial support to the referral point.
The project is designed to reach IDPs children of school going ages by Providing immediate child protection for the most vulnerable children through awareness creation and support to the child to child clubs with recreational materials and Increase resilience through the provision of basic protection services and support to school-based resilience mechanisms that will contribute to the sustainability of children’s well-being and build the capacities of teachers and community to better respond to child protection issues.
By implementing this work in such a manner, AYUUB NGO is ensuring to not only build up the emergency, capacity and child protection and education opportunities available to vulnerable groups in Lower Shabelle, but to improve the child protection response capacity, coordination and cooperation between the different groups and administrations within the region to promote child protection and Education rights.

Project Activities

Overall Objective: To ensure retention of learners where they live through protective and lifesaving education activities that integrate fully with protection to achieve quality education for most vulnerable learners affected by conflict in Lower Shabelle.Outcome 1: 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) crisis affected children of school going ages in humanitarian emergencies have increased access to quality education in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts in Lower Shabelle region. Output 1.1: Improved access to quality education for children in emergency through provision of Temporary Learning Spaces, Teaching and Learning Materials and incentives to help retain 1981 learners (1010 boys and 971 girls) in school. Activity 1.1.1: construct 1 Temporary Learning Space with 4 classrooms in KM50 to serve 160 learners (88 girls and 72 boys) in Merka district.Activity 1.1.2: Provide Education Teaching and Learning Materials to the 10 Schools serving 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) learners. (6 Exercise books 100pgs, 4 Eraser, 1 Pencil,1 Sharpener, 2 biro pen, 1 ruler, 1 school bag per child distributed once).Activity 1.1.3: Provide regular monthly incentives for 9 months to 50 (25 female) teachers; 100 USD/month/Teacher and $150/month/Head Teacher in 10 primary schools in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts. Outcome 2: Ensure 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) vulnerable emergency affected children have improved quality education with integrated essential services and life-saving messages. Output 2.1: 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) Children have improved quality education with integrated essential services and life-saving messages in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts. Activity 2.1.1: support 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) learners through wet feeding to 10 schools affected by conflict. Each child will receive porridge and cup of tea for breakfast at 10:00 am and will served Rice with pulses for 5 days per week (Sat-Wed) as lunch for 4 months.Activity 2.1.2: Provide water to 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) through water boozers to 10 constructed water tanks in 10 schools (7 liters per child/day as per SPHERE standard, 20,000 liters of water/school/week) for 4 months.Activity 2.1.3: Construct 4 latrines in 4 schools (two in Merka, and two in Qoryoley with hand washing facilities) to serve 120 learners (60 girls) and sensitize children on hygiene promotion and maintenance of WASH facilities. Outcome 3:Contribute to Sustainable Development Goals SDGs by reducing humanitarian needs, risks and vulnerabilities through integrated Child Protection approaches that promote child safeguarding in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts. Output 3.1: 1981 (1010 boys and 971 girls) children are safely accessing schools and their protection risks are mitigated through community sensitization, distribution of sanitary pads and trainings targeting teachers and CECs on child safeguarding and school safety. Activity 3.1.1: Train 50 teachers on pedagogy, lesson planning and child centered approaches (child safeguarding) and identify 20 CP focal points from among 50 teachers.Activity 3.1.2: Identification and referral of 200 children (100 girls) in the 10 schools with protection concerns for appropriate support.Activity 3.1.3: Facilitate recreational and Psychosocial support activities in 10 schools targeted in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts through distribution of recreational materials to CtC clubs.Activity 3.1.4: Conduct 4 Community sensitization sessions on Child Protection services and importance of child safeguarding in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey and Wanlaweyne districts.Activity 3.1.5: Provide sanitary pads to 580 adolescent girls in 10 schools for 9 months.Activity 3.1.6: Conduct a Mapping of existing child protection service in Marka, Kurtunwarrey, Qoryooleey & Wanlaweyne districts for establishment of referral.

Please provide further explanation

The protection environment in Marka is sufficiently safe and stable to enable durable solutions. This is why AYUUB is proposing activities linked to Somalia governments' SDGs especially for long term interventions aimed at alleviating poverty through skill training and provision of basic services. These interventions have also been aligned to national objectives for the education sector (ESSP). The proposed activities are also sustainable and upon completion will be handed over to the communities through the CECs. The challenges of displacement on the affected communities has been incorporated into the programme design.
