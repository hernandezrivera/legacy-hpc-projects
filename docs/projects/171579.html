Project Summary

This project is designed to provide emergency and lifesaving nutrition services to 21984 vulnerable persons living in Balcad, cadale, Johwar and Mahaday districts of Middle Shabelle. The intervention will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under the age of 5 years (CU5) amongst the host communities and IDPs in the Balcad district where Wocca is nutrition clusters primary rationalized partner; in Adale and Johwar where we are the secondary and tertiary rationalized partner and already operating 4 fixed facilities and also extend our services to Mahaday where nutrition services have become elusive over the years. Wocca will operate 4 fixed/static OTPs and 4 mobile OTPs targeting 20 locations chosen on the basis of vulnerability and absence of nutrition interventions in the 4 districts. The static facilities will be established in Balcad town, Haji Cali, Johwar and Cadale town locations of Balcad, Cadale and Johwar districts respectively while the 4 outreach teams will serve 20 identified locations in the 4 districts. WOCCA will engage UNICEF for backstopping during training of the project staff, community health workers (CHWs) and mothers for the “mothers MUAC approach”, on nutrition screening and case identification, Integrated management of acute malnutrition(IMAM) (using new IMAM guidelines), Infant and young child feeding education (IYCF-E) and Nutrition, health and hygiene promotion (NHHP).The trained staff shall be deployed to the targeted locations, mobile and static OTPs where together with the trained mothers will screen CU5s and PLWs. CU5s with SAM conditions and no medical complications will be enrolled to the static and mobile OTPs for treatment while the cases with medical complications will be referred by the mobile clinics and CHWs to the SC in Waberi star village managed by WOCCA for nutrition and medical rehabilitation. CU5s and PLWs with MAM conditions will be referred to WOCCA’s TSFP programme funded by WFP. CHWs and Hygiene promoters will be trained on NHHP, they will disseminate hygiene promotion messages to the communities to prevent outbreak and spread of communicable diseases like AWD/cholera. Mothers will be counseled individually and in groups by project staff and CHWs on appropriate maternal infant and young child feeding as part of efforts to prevent malnutrition. The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements to PLW and CU5 through the static and mobile facilities. UNICEF has been supporting WOCCA with nutrition supplies and technical guidance on previous nutrition projects within the targeted locations. Therefore, in this project WOCCA will still engage UNICEF to provide nutrition supplies and technical support for management of SAM cases. We will upgrade the fixed nutrition centers to provide basic health services to affected population by providing access to treatment of common childhood diseases and Health care support to PLW (including ANC and PNC) and EPI. Efforts to improve vaccination will be integrated into fixed and mobile service delivery mechanisms. Protection mainstreaming principles will be incorporated in the capacity building of the nutrition staff and CHWs. They will work closely with WOCCAs protection team on GBV prevention. WOCCA will also ensure that feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into organizations’ strategies, M&E, staff inductions and training. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on how to use the complaint/feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. To enhance effective coordination, WOCCA will provide monthly activity updates with partners including Nutrition, Health and protection cluster both regional and Nairobi level, Other agencies working in the field who are doing other sector projects in the project sites will also be updated to avoid duplication and promote effective and efficient utilization humanitarian funds

Project Activities

Two objectives are envisaged in this project. They include:(1). Provide equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases: (2). Strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micro-nutrient interventions.Towards achievement of this objectives, the following activities will be undertaken.(1). Training on effective acute Malnutrition management for project staff and community health workers (CHWS). (2). Community mobilization and sensitization. (Awareness events within the community with key persons - government leaders, village elders, religious leaders, women leaders and the general community, on malnutrition causes, the misconceptions about nutrition, prevention mechanisms and the services offered by the project). (3). Community screening and referral through “mothers MUAC approach” crosschecked by project staff (screeners)” for identification of acutely malnourished children and PLWs. Mothers will be trained on MUAC measurement and analysis and provided with MUAC tapes, they will work under the supervision of project screeners to screen children under five (CU5), boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and those identified as acutely Malnourished will be referred to the appropriate component (i.e. Outpatient Therapeutic Programme, OTP or Stabilization Centre, SC)(4). Treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children 0-59 months old through Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (Outpatient care) and/or Stabilization Center (Inpatient care) done through distributions of Ready to Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTFs) and routine medicines; medical and nutritional monitoring of the patient. (5). Treatment of moderately malnourished pregnant and lactating women through referral to targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP). (6). Treatment of moderately malnourished boys and girls under five years of age through referral to targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP). (7). Provision of De-worming tablets and vitamin A supplements to boys & girls. (8). Provision of Multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women and Iron supplementation for pregnant and lactating women.(9). Organize and Conduct IYCF training for staff, IYCF counsellors and community-health workers (CHWs) (10). Conduct counseling sessions for pregnant and lactating women and carers accessing both fixed and outreach centers, on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices on biweekly basis. Individual counseling sessions, group sessions and promotion sessions will be held.(11). Training of staff, CHWs and hygiene promoters on Nutrition, Health and Hygiene promotion (NHHP).(12). Conduct monthly Nutrition, Health and hygiene promotion sessions targeting 3664 IDP households representatives exploring but not limited to the following topics:- Food group and food sources, Maternal nutrition in pregnancy /lactation, Breastfeeding, Complimentary feeding, Diversified diet, Feeding the sick/malnourished children, Handling fecal matter/contamination chain (F- diagram), Hand washing with soap or ash, Food hygiene, Personal hygiene, Water treatment, Danger signs (Health seeking behaviour ), AWD/ Diarrhoea or cholera management (ORS & Zinc), Polio spread and prevention, Malaria prevention (ITN), Immunization, Micronutrient supplementation, Deworming, How to identify children with oedema and how to take MUAC, Health education and plumpynuts. Gender and Gender based violence issues. Existing referral mechanisms in (child)protection.

Mitigation Measures

Promote equitable and impartial access for all: - 1. Collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming2. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups.3. Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups.4. Ensure that beneficiaries know of available health services and assistance where/how to obtain it. Communicate information through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels (eg. health community workers, door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms).Ensure accountability to affected populations1. Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them.2. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg., feedback box at health centre, phone lines.3. Ensure all health staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.4. Health staff and health committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and all staff and members should receive protection mainstreaming training.Prioritise safety & dignity and avoid causing harm1. Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe.2. Ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private:3. Design and clearly mark separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children.4. In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospitals eg. use as a weapons depot, filtering patients in search of wounded armed group/forces members, preventing passage of wounded seeking health care, use of hospital as military operational centre.
