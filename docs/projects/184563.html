Project Summary

The project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable IDP families in Afgooye and Banadir to mitigate risks faced through Shelter/NFI interventions. It is expected that a total of 12,000 IDP(Elderly 600 men,1000 women, 4000 boys 1000 girls 3000, disabled 2,200 and non disabled 200) in Afgooye and Banadir will benefit from the distribution of emergency shelter kits and non-food items. Provision of NFI kits and ESKs will target the newly displaced persons and protracted IDPs with shelter structures that barely protect their dignity and privacy. The intervention is aimed at improving the living conditions of the IDPs by speeding up their recovery from damages caused by displacements.
2000 NFI kits and 2000 ESKs will be distributed through voucher and in kind respectively to 2000 IDP households. The "in kind" modality is influenced by SSWC's past experience with CBI where beneficiaries did not construct ESKs and channeled the money to other purposes. A market assessment will be conducted prior to the purchase of the items to ascertain their market prices. The beneficiary selection criteria will consider newly displaced persons, female headed households, child-headed households, the elderly in the community and persons with disabilities to ensure the most vulnerable groups are targeted.
To ensure sustainability, the ESKs will be established on lands with relatively long land agreements (three years and above). This will be ascertained through solicitation of written agreements from IDP site leaders and the subsequent confirmation of their validity from respective local authorities. ESK construction will be keen on COVID-19 and basic site planning aspects such as distance between ESKs, roads, fire breaks, drainage and distance from toilets.
Due to COVD-19, SSWC will use hotline numbers for CFRM. The anticipated data to be collected include among others both compliments and complaints: the quality of the ESKs and NFIs, fairness of beneficiary selection processes, shortness of items distributed, delays and other feedback. This will ensure that community participation and AAP is at the center of the project.
Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and Post construction monitoring (PCM) will be conducted after every distribution and construction respectively. The monitoring exercises will provide beneficiary feedback on the effectiveness of the distribution. 70% of the beneficiaries will take part of the exercise to ensure proper representation and that exact feelings are recorded about the processes and quality of distribution. The PDM/PCMs will also help to know the beneficiary satisfaction of the items distributed, identify the needs and feelings of the community better and inform on the effectiveness of the activities and achievements of targets while providing guidance towards success.
The activities are geared towards improving the living conditions of the newly displaced IDPs and protracted IDPs living in deplorable conditions hence contributing to the achievement of the shelter cluster’s objectives.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points of NFI kits and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. SSWC has a protection project this will leverage the shelter project and help reduce the risks of GBV to beneficiaries. During the project implementation the Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents may be identified . SSWC will ensure that all selected beneficiaries are aware of all the mapped out referral services through its field monitors. Te beneficiaries will also be encouraged to listen to radio programs sponsored by SSWC which highlight GBV issues and inform people on the available referral services in Somalia . The Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms may arise. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints by calling the free toll number 2091 with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at distribution site, phone lines , setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. SSWC will also provide clear information to distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance.
