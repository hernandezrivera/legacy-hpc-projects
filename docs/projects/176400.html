Project Summary

Bor South, Magwi, Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties are among other counties in the country where WASH needs have been worsening, aggravated an upsurge in COVID 19, unprecedented floods with vulnerabilities affecting particularly IDP, displaced, returnee and host populations, including girls, women, men and boys who continue to face violations of their rights including killings; and aggravated sexual /gender based violence particularly among women and girls (IPC December 2020; Protection Trends 2020; REACH Reports 2020).
Displaced women and girls continue to be dis-proportionally affected by protection related WASH issues, with GBV (be it rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced/child marriage, or psychological/emotional abuse-including abductions) occurring due to risks associated with insufficient and unsafe access to water, hygiene and sanitation facilities. This is in addition to high malnutrition which is further aggravated by unprecedented food insecurity levels with at least 5.82 million people projected to face IPC Phase 3/worse levels of food insecurity; projected to rise to 7.24 million people (IPC December 2020). This is in addition to high prevalence of WASH related diseases such as acute watery diarrhoea, respiratory diseases, eye infections often associated with poor access to WASH (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2020).
Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) is proposing to target 24,000 beneficiaries made up of 4000 men, 4000 women, 8000 girls and 8000 boys with a WASH integrated response, in line with the 2021 HRP strategy in addition to responding to most vulnerable populations with acute needs. CARD has been working with GBV, Nutrition, Education, Health – to mitigate and respond to Malnutrition, Public Health risks, such as COVID 19 and AWD outbreaks.
CARD will also ensure appropriate mitigation GBV measures through its WASH interventions and referrals where applicable. The project will in addition address protection especially among women and girls who face GBV, a key protection concern in an environment where undertaking daily survival
tasks while they walk for long distances in search for water, provision of MHM hygiene kits among girls and female youth/women of menstruating age including those in schools. In addition, the project will address high levels of malnutrition and IPC through WASH and Nutrition integrated programming for famine prevention by targeting SAM/MAM children and their families at household/ OTP facility levels.
CARD has been operating in the targeted counties for the past seven years with established office presence in Bor, Mundri, Mvolo and Magwi. CARD has competent staff, logistics and community goodwill, in addition to having good relations with the host communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries etc. These strong networks coupled with established local networks will be engaged and mobilized to rapidly implement the proposed activities.
In addition, CARD will ensure involvement of beneficiaries in planning, implementation, and monitoring. CARD takes a “do no harm” approach, by employing community-based protection measures that aim at understanding humanitarian issues from the community’s perspective and contributing to the development of risk reduction strategies with and for the community. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring AAP, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account Gender Based Violence mitigation measures.

1 - Needs

Most vulnerable populations in Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states continue to endure multiple shocks that have continued to erode and undermine access to basic life support services including water access, hygiene, sanitation and livelihoods with consequent in poor food and nutrition security outcomes. A combination of COVID 19 pandemic, flooding, poor harvests, confliict with recent escalation of hostilities in the Equatorias, economic meltdown, have conspired to drive food and nutrition insecurity to unprecedented levels with at least 5.82 million people projected to face IPC Phase 3/worse levels of food insecurity; projected to rise to 7.24 million people (IPC December 2020). This is in addition to high prevalence of WASH related diseases such as acute watery diarrhoea, respiratory diseases, eye infections often associated with poor access to WASH (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2020; South Sudan Floods Reports, 2020). Most vulnerable populations residing in these region cited insecurity as a primary concern particularly in Bor South which has witnessed unprecedented flooding extremes with at least 65,000 IDPs residing in Jalle, Bangachorot, Jam , Liil, Mabior biol and Yolthar among others in addition to recent inter-communal fighting at border areas (REACH Situation Overview: Jonglei, 2020). The current flooding extremes has led to submerging of basic life support amenities including boreholes, latrines, farms and disrupted livelihood activities and unprecedented levels of food insecurity, projected to deteriorate further into the lean season in 2021 (IPC Report, December 2020). Moreover, recent flooding in the Equatorias, though not as substantial as in Bor South, has had consequent resultant destruction of basic life support amenities including water points and submerging of latrines, houses, health centres further negatively impacting the already vulnerable populations whose needs have immensely increased in addition to heightened COVID 19 spread. There is an increased heightened risk of diseases among the affected populations with county authorities reporting increased incidences of acute watery diarrhoea, malaria and respiratory infections (RRC Situation Report, Oct 2020; REACH Updates, 2020). As needs are worsening, the most vulnerable and displaced populations, including girls and boyscontinue to face violations of their rights including sexual /gender based violence. Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) has remained a major protection concern for women/girls in Bor South and Equatorias with SGBV reported as the main protection concern for women by the highest proportion of assessed settlements in (46%), remaining largely unchanged from previous months (Protection Trends, 2020). CARD has been operating in the targeted counties since 2014 and has just successfully accomplished WASH SSHF projects in Magwi and Bor South, and currently implementing FAO funded actions in Western Equatoria that will complement proposed actions in 2021. CARD has competent staff, logistics and community goodwill; good relations with communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county line ministries. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account GBV mitigation measures.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD will ensure it holds community consultations and engagement in regard to PSEA including sharing key details of beneficiary rights and reporting channels, which shall be confidential, either through community chosen PSEA personell and PSEA feedback desks including direct phone contacts of Juba offices for confidential reporting. CARD staff and associated personnel have signed a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on their rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account. These will be supported at their areas of residence/ homes including sharing of key contacts and focal points for one on one support. AAP through two-way feedback by engaging and giving vulnerable people an opportunity to feedback on implementation and CARD ensuring their views are tacked and they are provided with relevant information.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project including throughout implementation in addition to formation of implementation committees to spearhead beneficiary messaging on the project including collection of feedback and complaints that will inform implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration. This will also include referalls through other agencies in case of cross sectoral support.
