Project Summary

This projects aims to provide integrated multi-sectoral assistance to 6,839 disaster affected households (41,035 people) including displaced and host communities in Somalia. For people recovering from drought, attention will be placed on provision of recovery and sustainable solutions to build their resilience while under-served long term displaced households will be supported with durable solutions related to reintegration. Disaster affected populations will be supported through life-saving emergency interventions. To achieve this the project will focus on mitigating protection risks through a three-prong integrated approach consisting of Food security and livelihoods, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Shelter and House Land and Property assistance. In line with the Somalia Resilience Recovery Framework, long-term displaced populations in prioritized locations in Bosaaso, Garowe, Galkacyo, Qardho, Kismayo, Baidoa and Dollow will be provided with durable solutions to basic services where land is availed by the government. Livelihood and Food security (LFS) intervention will aim at improving access to food through cash-based emergency support in the short term so as to enable people in Emergency and Crisis (IPC3 and 4) deal with the impending situation and mitigate the negative coping strategies. The LFS will support targeted populations especially in hard to reach areas to meet their basic food and non-food needs through provision of emergency and safety net support. This will be achieved through provision of Unconditional Cash Transfers (UCTs), Conditional Cash Transfers where appropriate, and companion programming through awareness raising for good nutrition through promoting diet diversity and safe food preparation, and hygiene practices. All cash transfer programmes (CTPs) will take into account seasonality including agricultural calendar and fluctuating market prices, in order to maximize positive effects of the cash injection in communities and on the market. Livelihood and Food security interventions will reach 17,340 individuals (2890 hhs). The same beneficiaries supported through LFS in addition to others will also receive WASH services.
Life-saving WASH interventions will temporary provide safe water through direct delivery or restricted cash to enable beneficiaries buy their own water depending on context and market functionality. Provision of hygiene kits through cash or in-kind, household water treatment and/or point of use water treatment to 400 hhs, construction of 100 simple design latrines, latrine emptying (50 latrines) and safe fecal sludge disposal and hygiene promotion will be delivered. Schools and health facilities will be provided with adequate water and sanitation facilities including private space for menstrual hygiene (40 institutional latrines) while hygiene promotion will be through group practicing on good hygiene behavior. All latrine will be installed with handwashing equipment and community sensitized to use soap for handwashing. Women and girls will be involved in identification of sites for facilities to ensure their safety and security is taken into account. Latrines will be gender separated, provided with lockable doors and handwashing equipment. Solar street lights (5 posts) will be provided near water points and latrines to improve the security and safety of women and girls especially in crowded IDP settlements. Long term solutions will include construction and or rehabilitation of water sources including 2 boreholes (1 new) with infrastructure and hybrid energy, 5 shallow wells and 1 river water intake system with water treatment and extension of piped water network. New borehole and river water intake systems construction will be preceded by hydrogeological survey and hydrological studies undertaken by competent staff or consultants. Design of structure will take environmental issues into consideration to ensure safe abstraction. Low scale Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) will be undertaken for new water sources to define and implement environmental mitigation measures for negative impacts. A total of 25,000 people (4,167 households) will be reached with WASH services where some will also receive livelihood and food assistance, shelter and information and legal services.
Shelter: The project will focus on improving the physical protection and dignity of IDPs, refugee returnees and poor host communities across Somalia through the provision of locally purchased Non Food Items (NFIs to 3000 hhs) and Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs to 2000 hhs) through cash/voucher/in-kind modalities, construction of Semi-permanent and Permanent Shelters through community driven or owner driven approaches, settlement (re)planning training to reduce congestion and overcrowding, conducting Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) exercises for Shelters and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercises for NFIs & ESKs and participation in infrastructure mapping exercises led by Shelter cluster or any other partner. NRC will target total 5345 households (50% female-headed) through different Shelter interventions throughout the year 2019. NRC staff will ensure that the provided cash has been utilized for intended purpose by coordinating with the beneficiaries themselves and conduct follow ups with the support of the community leaders, local authorities and the relevant clusters. NRC will also support the displaced households through provision of Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance to promote security of tenure.
Design of infrastructure across all sectors will take into consideration the needs of special groups in the community including the disabled, old, children to ensure they have equitable access to the services. Design of latrines will take into account the needs of special groups such as the physically challenged hence depending on needs some will have a smooth ramp, handle and or side rail bars, toilet seat and adequate in size. To enhance sustainability, women and men will be involved in identification of permanent solutions, design and implementation of the activities. Community structures including water committees, Community Hygiene promoters will comprise of both men and women from all community groups including the minority and the marginalized for equal representation who will be trained on management of the facilities provided. For borehole water supplies, Private Public Partnerships (PPP) management system will be adopted for effective management and possible expansion of systems in future. NRC understands the context in the area of operation and will ensure protection issues that include exclusion of the minority and marginalized clans, SRGBV, extortion as well as barriers caused by gate keepers are addressed throughout all the components of this integrated programme. Beneficiaries will be provided with information on NRC’s complaints, feedback and response mechanisms that is managed by an independent M&E unit within the organization. NRC will work with identified local partners especially in hard to reach areas. Our partners will receive on-the-job training especially on enhancing operational systems within their organization, conducting credible assessments, proposal development and reporting.

Needs

Populations affected by severe drought and by floods in April/May 2018 and the IDPs living in overcrowded settlements remain in dire need of support with basic serviced including food, water, sanitation facilities, hygiene education, shelter and information and legal assistance. High level of acute malnutrition persists especially among the IDPs due to structural underlying causes including inadequate access to food, insufficient health services, lack of safe water and an unhealthy environment. A joint assessment (WASH Cluster, May ‘18), indicates that majority of IDPs in Baidoa are using unsafe water, while 70% of latrines are damaged and or filled up. Joint assessment in L/Juba estimates 200,000 people in rural areas of Kismayo and Afmadow districts were affected by floods and displaced. Flash floods affected latrines structures especially in Dhobley IDP camps. An estimated 830,000 people were affected by riverine and flash flooding in southern and central states, with nearly 290,000 of them temporarily displaced (HRP 2018, OCHA). Awdal and Wooqoyi Galbeed regions suffered from the effects of cyclone Sagar. A joint needs assessment (Concern, NRC, DRC, SC) conducted in May 2018 indicated priority needs were food, water, NFIs, shelter, medical care, and reconstruction of infrastructure and farmlands. Results indicate 14,938 HHs were severely affected by the cyclone. According to UNCHR-NRC eviction Trend analysis, over 204,000 people were forcibly evicted this year, nearly double the same period last year (Jan to July) and surpassing the total evicted for all of 2017. 86% of all the evictions this year have taken place in Mogadishu at 73% and Baidoa at 13%. Clan tensions and border dispute between Puntland and Somaliland at the eastern regions especially in Dararweyne, El-Afweyn districts and Tukaraq area escalated in the recent past. Main challenges arising include shortage of shelter, food, water and sanitation especially among IDPs.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Project will contribute the durable solutions efforts particularly in Kismayo, Garowe, Galkayo, Bossaso, Baidoa, Dhusamareeb and Burao where the government has shown keen interest and provided land to settle the many displaced people. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many durable solutions achieved. NRC contributing to this objective, will construct permanent shelters and infrastructures in these locations through proper settlement planning. Furthermore, through construction of semi-permanent (transitional) Shelters and rehabilitation of Caarish Shelters, shelter projects will focus on enabling communities to withstand at least 2 subsequent weak rainy seasons without major external intervention or wide-scale displacement, while building towards the achievement of durable solutions for displacement affected population. Part of the LFS activities will be focusing on improving and or establishing productive communities’ assets. These productive assets will be inclusive of establishment and or rehabilitation of farm to market roads, water harvesting structures and or small irrigation channels. This is envisaged that all of these assets will contribute in increased production, efficiency and better income of the communities. Secondly the project will also focus on improved safety net programs; therefore this is strongly linked with the durable solutions. In the target locations, the IDP are protracted and have therefore intermingled with the host community over time and established positive cohesion. Project contributes significantly to the Resilience Recover Framework and the National Development plan Infrastructure will be designed taking into account environmental sustainability and Water systems serving large population will preferably be managed through private public partnerships to enhance functional sustainability thus long term solution. Construction of latrines through the community driven approach, CLTS, leads to immediate ownership and sustainability of the facilities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

- Risk: Under or non-representation of minority or marginalized communities in consultative meetings or amongst Community structures. - Likelihood: High; - Impact: high - Mitigation Measures: • Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all • Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among households clusters. • Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be outside the settlements. • Condition implementation to on equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepers - Risk: Forced Evictions and Government unwillingness to provide land for sustainable infrastructure development for the displaced - Likelihood: high - Impact: High - Mitigation measures: • Align project to government’s resilience recovery agenda to gain support and sustainability for project • Advocate for legal rights of the IDPs in relation to the due process to be followed to relocate with dignity • Sensitize and support the government on developing policies and enforcing laws on land to protect the displaced

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

- Risk: gender-based violence and discrimination against women and girls as they access facilities Likelihood: High - Impact: Very high - Mitigation: - Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal facilities considering their security and safety • Ensure the location and access routes to facilities and distribution points are safe. • Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement - Install lights near communal WASH facilities • Where possible avoid central distribution on NFIs, Hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneficiary to purchase items at own convenient time • Construct gender separated latrines, lockable from inside, and have lighting • Increase number of water points to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness – • Avoid community tensions, by providing the host community with access to basic services where they are experiencing similar needs for services and facilities. • Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of basic services • Risk: Rigid infrastructure that is difficult to be used by persons with reduced mobility (eg. persons with physical disability, older persons) • Likelihood: medium • Impact: medium • Mitigation: - Make infrastructure adaptions to structures to make them accessible to this category of people (proportion of latrines with ramp and side rails, handlebars, toilet seats, • Ensure that beneficiaries know about the programme and how to obtain items, where and how much they cost

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

- Risk: limited access to affected communities and or destruction of infrastructure through armed conflict - Likelihood: Low - Impact: High - Mitigation measures: • Most areas are accessible to NRC staff and there hasn’t been cases of vandalism of infrastructure. However, in armed conflict, this remains to be a risk. • Conduct security risk assessment in target locations and follow security Standard Operation Procedures to ensure staff and beneficiaries remain safe • Conduct in depth consultations to understand factors that could pose security risk to beneficiaries, e.g unwanted visibility on facilities • For extremely hard to reach areas that are inaccessible to internationals, implement project through local partners with supervision by NRC’s national staff hailing from the target locations
