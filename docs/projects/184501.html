Project Summary

This health project will provide emergency life-saving health care services to humanitarian crisis affected persons with focus on children, women, newborn and vulnerable populations including internally displaced, elderly, those with disabilities, refugees, and returnees for a period of 12 months in 38 Districts in 12 priority regions of Somalia (Bakool, Banadir, Bay, Galgadud, Gedo, Hiran, Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle Middle Shabelle, Sool and Sanaag). UNICEF will continue to provide emergency healthcare in all regions including the ones targeted by the Damal Caafimaad until when the project starts implementation.
UNICEF aims to reach 931,316 direct beneficiaries, which is 20 per cent of the 4.7 million people in need targeted by health cluster with life-saving interventions through its implementing partners. An estimated 230,410 Girls, 210,847 Boys, 284,819 Women and 205,240 men will be provided with first outpatient consultation, 186,264 (96,857 Girls, 89,407 boys) children under-5 year to be vaccinated against measles, 30,733 pregnant women to be assisted by skilled birth attendance and 797 (438 women and 359 Men) Health care workers including Community health workers will be trained on Infection, Prevention and Control (IPC), especially important in the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic.
This project is designed to decrease morbidity and mortality for the main diseases among the most vulnerable groups, interventions are derived from core Somalia Essential Package of health services (EPHS 2020) and will be delivered through fixed sites and outreaches. These interventions include; Treatment of common childhood illnesses, routine and supplementary immunization services, reproductive, maternal and newborn healthcare (Antenatal Care, delivery, and Postnatal care to pregnant, lactating women and infants) with referral of complicated cases to comprehensive emergency obstetric and neonatal care. To increase demand for care and promote health practices and behaviors among vulnerable communities, other activities will increase community awareness by disseminating life-saving health messages for the prevention the outbreak of diseases such as AWD/Cholera, measles and COVID-19, promotion of health services utilization as well as enhancing community surveillance to improve disease detection for early action. In addition, the project aims to improve quality of services through capacity building of health staff and CHWs.
Strategic prepositioning of emergency essential medical supplies, kits, and equipment in UNICEF’s regional warehouses for timely response to emergency situations. The project will address the continued and imminent risk of a large outbreak of COVID-19 in conflict-affected areas, where access to the population is challenging and primary health care services limited by shifting gears and strengthening emergency health and other social services, and work with government and other partners to curb the effects of the pandemic in Somalia. Skills of frontline health care workers will be built on case management of common illnesses, GBV survivors and clinical management of rape, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), disease surveillance. Additionally, capacity development will be provided for Infection Prevention and control will focus on both medical and non-medical cadre and in the context of COVID-19 to improve triage and handwashing activities in supported HFs. Community health workers will be capacitated to provide key messages on outbreaks and referral services available and support disease surveillance activities in the communities. Health interventions to be provided will be done in a consultative manner through use of partners with presence in the area and Civil societies and community development committees which cuts across women, men and youth groups. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

To ensure accountability to affected persons, UNICEF and partners’ monitoring teams will continue to ensure appropriate community-based feedback and complaints handling mechanisms are functional, and sensitization of the communities done on the same. The mechanisms are designed to be inclusive and sensitive to the local community providing a platform for the active involvement of beneficiaries. The project will incorporate the DO NO HARM principle by making sure that there are complaints mechanism and immediate feedback and further enlists community support through the community-based participation in order to avoid conflicts on the project. This will include use of available feedback platforms including toll free number, sharing feedback during Focus group discussions and community dialogue forums. These feedback mechanisms will help identify and respond to beneficiary/stakeholders' feedback and complaints. UNICEF and partners will consistently review the nature of feedback received from the hotline and refer to relevant technical person for appropriate response/action while program team will address any concerns raised during dialogues and FGD sessions with community members. UNICEF and partners will ensure health facilities are disability friendly area. Structures will be slightly altered to accommodate the disabled. Disabled patients will be among those given priority. According to the latest REACH report on hard to reach areas, children with disability are those with the greatest challenges when it comes to accessing health services. UNICEF and partners will ensure communities are mobilised to bring disabled children out to receive health services. Additionally, use of mobile teams will work to bring services closer to the population so that parents of children with disabilities do not have to bear the burden of transporting them very far to access services. During the project implementation, UNICEF and partners will engage the District and regional Health Management Teams (DHMTs) to conduct quarterly joint supportive supervision visits to monitor the service delivery quality, identify capacity gaps and provide recommendations. Additionally, health facility committees will be involved in monthly community feedback meetings, which will provide an avenue for complaints and compliments to be raised, and a joint action plan for corrective actions instituted with clear roles and responsibilities for each party. The information gathered here will be documented and used as lessons learned to improve the health activities in the future. The organization will conduct a discussion with the community and the various stakeholders in the choice of the relevant infrastructure and will do sensitization on the project objectives, indicators, and all critical aspects in order to inform the community and the intended beneficiaries. To avoid risk of land disputes affecting where health facilities will be built, supported or operationalized, proper mapping and consultation with community and respective district, state or federal level Ministry of Health leadership will be engaged to determine suitability and based on need.
