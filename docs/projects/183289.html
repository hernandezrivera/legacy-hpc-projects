Project Summary

The proposed intervention sets to alleviate the vulnerable status of migrants in Lebanon, due to the ongoing socio-economic crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic. Through this project, Caritas is set to provide access and support the most vulnerable migrant workers and eliminate all protection risks, in order to decrease negative coping mechanisms. Particular focus will be provided to live-out migrants and migrant women at the retention center.
Migrant domestic workers are not protected by the country's labor law, but instead work under the sponsorship system (Kafala). Many migrants, mostly women, report exploitative working conditions including long working hours, deprivation of rest days, denial of pay or imposed deductions, deprivation of food & proper accommodation, verbal & physical abuse, & denial of access to health care. Migrants are also under the risk of trafficking, assault and deportation should they leave their employer. Many migrants find themselves unable to cover their most basic needs in the absence of an effective social safety net in Lebanon. As such, Caritas Lebanon will aim to tackle different objectives of the ERP 2021 Migrant chapter.
Under objective 1, CL will provide vulnerable migrants with protection services, ensuring that they are protected from abuse, exploitation, and marginalization. As a result, the protection team will provide case management support and assistance to migrants who are unable to access services due to a lack of documentation, fear of arrest, stigma, mobility/communication issues, and other factors, as a result, 120 migrants will receive case management support, 100 will receive legal services which includes counselling and representation, 250 migrants will receive focused group MHPSS and awareness and 400 migrants in the GS detention centers will receive legal counselling and representation. Migrants in detention centers need additional support because what is supposed to be a short stay is being prolonged due to understaffing at the center, the absence of certain embassies or consulates in Lebanon, lack of reliable cooperation, language barriers preventing communication between GS personnel and detainees etc.
Under objective 2, CL will provide a lifesaving assistance to migrants in vulnerable situations. Multi-purpose cash assistance will be provided for a variety of purposes, primarily to assist struggling migrants with housing rental payments, but also to address unanticipated, essential, and abrupt requirements of migrants and ensuring humanitarian gaps are filled. As a result, 950 live-out migrants will be given a one-time multipurpose cash aid to help them meet some of their basic needs and eliminate risks of homelessness, while 20 survivors of serious abuse will be directed to Caritas safe shelters for a temporary stay. Residents in the shelter typically remain between three to six months before moving on, depending on the complexity of each case and the level of assistance they receive from their embassies or consulates.
Under objective 3, CL will collaborate with the International Organization for Migration (IOM) and other partners to support the voluntary humanitarian return of migrants, including live-outs and survivors, to their countries of origin upon safe shelter departure. As a result, 20 migrants who want to return home will be given financial support to pay any fees they may incur while going, such as plane tickets, luggage, and COVID-19 testing, among other things.
For this intervention, priority will be provided to migrant women as most of the live-out are women and are highly vulnerable to abuse, such as Gender-Based Violence, and exploitation, including human trafficking and forced labour. As such, all targets of this intervention will support at least 60% of women for each activity

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The Human Rights Based Approach falls within CL’s general mission and vision to ensure all beneficiaries’ basic human rights are endorsed and protected. Taking into consideration women and girls whose protection is of the upmost importance; CL through its programme will ensure that they receive access to lifesaving services that improves their physical and mental wellbeing, as well as it allows them to live in dignity. Using the 4-Agency Protection Mainstreaming approach, Caritas identifies key protection mainstreaming needs and gaps and prioritizes actions on this basis. These include a regular gap analysis, and a robust complaints mechanism with standard operating procedures to deal with both sensitive and non-sensitive complaints. Caritas Lebanon adopts its Protection Strategy to guide all protection programming - including GBV and protection activities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability of affected populations is ensured throughout all phases of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project design is based on continuous assessments at field level that analyze the changing needs of affected populations and the relevance of ongoing and potential activities. During implementation, CL team ensures that beneficiaries have the tools to be able to express their concerns and suggestions for improvement. CL has established comment boxes in its Centres for beneficiaries to provide feedback - positive, negative, and suggestive - on activities and services provided. Additionally, the Centre Manager is responsible for developing and encouraging a culture of accountability and feedback in the shelters through participatory approaches.
