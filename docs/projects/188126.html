Project Summary

The organization will implement its project in four governorates in Northeast Syria to fill the existing and protentional gaps in the health system to enable the facilities to respond to the beneficiaries’ health care needs; as health services rely on international support due to the limited financial capacity of health committees, reduction of humanitarian aid by donors leading to suspension of support to services in NES. To support needed humanitarian services our NGO will implement integrated health and mental health programs that will include the provision of primary, secondary, specialized health care services, including sexual reproductive health. These will be provided through our support to four hospitals and three PHCs in Aleppo (Ain al Arab and Manbij), Ar Raqqa (Raqqa City, Al Qahtaniyeh), Deir ez Zor (Kesrah, Jazrat Bu Hamayid) and Al Hassekeh (Al-Malikiyah and Tal Baydar). The services will include the provision of health consultations, major and minor surgeries, patients’ referrals between primary to secondary facilities to ensure the benefices are receiving comprehensive care when it’s required through supporting ambulances. Specialized services will include lifesaving hemodialysis through the Kidney center/department, isolation and treatment for COVID-19 patients at COVID-19 center, in addition to the treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, including locally endemic diseases and health education. SRH services will include comprehensive sexual reproductive health services, which will include the following: health education, adolescent sexual reproductive health (ASRH) services, ANC, PNC, family planning, and sexually transmitted infection (STI) treatment. as well as within close coordination with WHO will provide vaccination services for children under five. The organization will offer MH services to the population affected by different mental health conditions. These services will form part of compressive MHPSS program and will offer a wide range of services that will prevent, assess, and treat MNS disorders and promote psychosocial resiliency for the affected population in NES. The services will include mhGAP consultations, which will be linked with comprehensive case management services, psychoeducation, community outreach services, mhGAP detection and referral, capacity building, and structured group support. There will also be upscaling evidence-based interventions through training of trainers and providers, who will implement them to beneficiaries to reduce mild and moderate mental conditions. The project will integrate MH services into primary healthcare facilities with an intention of better accessibility of services to all community members regardless of age, gender or impairment and other vulnerable populations.

4 - Needs

According to the Northeast Syria Needs and Response Overview (March 2021) there are an estimated 1.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in NES. The assessment also highlighted that NES hospitals are under extreme stress, while a significant investment is needed to build an effective hospital system providing essential primary and secondary health services. NES continues to experience shortages of essential shortages for the treatment and management of non-communicable diseases, pediatric medicines, also, increased COVID-19 cases in NES, overburdened health facilities, and a shortage of health personnel contributed to the crisis. An estimated one in 10 Syrians is expected to have a mild to a moderate mental health condition, while 1 in 30 has a severe condition. Prolonged exposure to conflict increases the prevalence of mental health conditions, yet stigma and shortages of trained health workers are barriers to treatment. Due to the Syrian conflict, the Syrian people have experienced major known risk factors for poor mental health, including exposure to traumatic events, forced displacement and ongoing stressors such as unemployment, impoverishment, social dislocation, and loss of social support Available data indicates that half of all Syrian children display symptoms of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and a quarter face intellectual and developmental challenges.

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO will support primary, secondary, and specialized services through all departments in Kobane Hospital and COVID-19 center (isolations and ICU wards), 9 departments at Menbej Hospital in Aleppo,12 departments in Raqqa hospital, Maternity and pediatric departments at Maternity Hospital in Al Raqqa, 6 departments at Kesrah Hospital Deir Ez Zoor, Al-Malikiyah hospital. Primary health care services through 3 PHCs (Tal Badyer in Al Hassekeh, Jazrat Bu Hamayid in Deir Ez Zoor and Al Qahtaniyeh in Raqqa) The support will include provision of primary, secondary, specialized health care services through the facilities, including kidney sessions, surgeries, and comprehensive SRH. The NGO will support the facilities with monthly salaries to the staff, staff supervision, capacity building, provision of pharmaceuticals, medical consumables including PPE and equipment, non-medical equipment such as furniture, and generators and PDWs morbidities across the facilities such as wheelchairs, accessing ramps on the facilities building entrances, and priority in consultation facilities minor renovation, and facilities running costs. The NGO will support mhGAP consultations including diagnosing and treating patients with different MNS conditions, psychoeducation, community-based outreach activities, community sessions training on mhGAP HIG, detection, and referral, common mental health disorders, stigma reduction, self-care, case management, and basic counseling skills.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WGSS will be staffed exclusively by women, making it easier for women and girls to attend the centers without any reservations. Reporting GBV incidents will be encouraged by building an atmosphere of trust and safety in the centers by delivering regular women and girl-focused activities and integrating GBV services in the WGSS *Risk of sexual exploitation: The NES context has a high degree of poverty that make clients resort to commercial sex, sex for food and other related abuses to secure the needs. This may have serious ramifications to programming. Mitigation: MH staff will receive PSEA training and will know how and when to report suspected cases of exploitation, through employing an equal number of male and female staff, so that beneficiaries will have a choice of the gender of case manager-community mobilizer that they feel more comfortable receiving services from. *Risk of violence/harm toward staff: Clients may have mental disturbances that can potentially lead to abuse to the staff, this can happen as well due to lack of income to most mental patients who due to their mental status can’t be able to secure meaningful employment Mitigation: Staff will work in pairs in the community providing emergency outreach and community activities and events will be participated in by pairs and/or full teams. Each MH staff member will have a mobile phone with them also and check in with their team leader regularly.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO is conducting several mandatory training courses for the staff newly joined on the Code of Conduct (CoC, signed by all staff) and ethics including the SEA and PSEA policy. The training provides/raises awareness among staff by enforcing mechanisms to address complaints about suspected PSEA. The Organization obliges its staff members to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse. Through the developed policies the NGO is ensuring the staff and beneficiaries at all levels and locations are protected.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

*Risk of Border closure due to weather or political, military or security situation, the risk is mitigated through have the flexibility of intended departure dates and further travel from the region, market survey for domestic procurement and look at longer-term program needs to consider larger deliveries and higher storage to take opportunities to travel when in an ad hoc/ sporadic situation. *Risk of worsening COVID-19 cases, the risk is mitigated through placing a remote implementation approach shall be adopted to ensure that MH clients are appropriately supported in cases where the facilities are suspended.*Risk of Government of Syria takeover or embedding in the health facilities supported by NGO, the risk is mitigated through Emplace red lines and closely following the GOS actions and discussing its implications with the health committee. The NGO will turn to the donor and seek approval regarding the continuation/adaptation of the project. *Risk of security disruption escalation of internal conflicts, forcing cessation of service delivery the risk is mitigated through, the risk is mitigated by placing a contingency such disruptions, where MH beneficiaries will be provided some additional stock of medication if deemed safe by the psychiatrist or mhGAP Doctor. Change of military/political control of geographic locations where the NGO implements programs; the risks a mitigated through maintaining strong lines of communication with global and regional security.
