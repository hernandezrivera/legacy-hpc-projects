Project Summary

Recurrent emergencies have highlighted the need for community-based response and prevention of Gender Based Violence and Child Protection Issues and to sustain strengthening the capacities of community structures resourceful towards improved protection environment for women, girls, boys and men.
The project aims to sustain gains made by SEDHURO Child Protection and GBV programs in the promotion of rights, care and protection of children in Gedo and Lower Jubba regions of Jubbaland State through a multi-sectorial resilience programming encompassing Social Norms, GBV, FGM, IDTR and strengthening community child protection structures. The project enhances local knowledge by promoting dialogue on social norms issues including harmful traditional practices, women and children’s rights which are enshrined in the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) and Somali constitution.
The program places family at the center of child protection and hence unaccompanied and separated children will be provided with services aimed at reunifying them with their parents or customary caregivers as quickly as possible and as informed by the “best interest of the child”.
The project will incorporate a plan for capacity building notably strengthening community-level capacity to prevent/mitigate family separation, GBV and Harmful practices.
The project will build synergy with other protection programs and cross-sector partners to ensure children, including unaccompanied and separated, will have their lives protected and for them to grow and develop healthily, including with access to the resources necessary to achieve their maximum potential. Holistic and comprehensive support to children’s welfare will therefore be pursued through establishing referral pathways, establishing/reactivating coordination platforms and creating a space for community contribution. Among other partners, SEDHURO will coordinate ICRC, Intersos, HILWA, CEDA and WRRS who may be working in UASC’s “next destination” to ensure cross-regions reunification is possible. The project will benefit from community structures including CPC, religious leaders and women groups. SEDHURO is running CFS in Dollow, Luq, Bardera and Kismayo in partnership with UNICEF and SHF. SEDHURO has also established one-stop GBV centre in Gulwade IDP camps in Kismayo. This project will build on and sustain those activities.
The project aims to achieve the following:
1.0 Address acute protection needs of women, men, girls and boys, stemming from violence, including GBV, as well as coercion, and abuse in Gedo and Lower Juba.
1.1 Enhance the protection of unaccompanied and separated children affected by emergencies including conflict and natural disaster in Gedo and Lower Juba, through strengthening and harmonising case management of UASC.
1.1.1 SEDHURO will carry out identification urgently as part of a coordinated and strategic programme response. Inform staff in all sectors, local authorities, community-based organizations including CPCs and CPAs, community and religious leaders, and camp managers of the need to identify UASC and refer them to SEDHURO for assistance. SEDHURO will identify 600 separated and unaccompanied children through out the project period.
1.1.2. SEDHURO will reunify 500 UASC with their families ensuring the child’s genuine participation, organizing transportation, and ensuring formal handover of the child to the responsible family member. Further post-reunification follow-up will be conducted to ensure the child has successfully reintegrated into his/her family. If the child has additional protection concerns, SEDHURO will refer the child to appropriate service provider. If tracing is positive, SEDHURO will verify all family relationships and both the child’s and family’s willingness to be reunited.
1.1.3 SEDHURO will involve already well-trained foster mothers to provide family care to UASC considering the best interest of the child. Children will only placed there as a last resort i.e. when immediate reunification is not possible, and when placed under foster care, SEDHURO will plan ways to keep children safe, and facilitate their return to, or contact with, their families. Through the project period, SEDHURO will provide 100 UASC with family-based care.
1.1.3 SEDHURO will provide training to 300 social workers, HW, Case workers drawn from community members (50% female) in knowledge/ skills related to separation, family tracing and reunification and case management, communication skills, ability to complete forms to a high standard, familiarity with data confidentiality requirements, and training and competency in working with and interviewing children.
2.0 Improve access to GBV service delivery for Girls, boys, women and men subjected to GBV in Gedo and Lower Juba
2.1 Provide PSS to 1000 GBV survivors. The PSS workers will support survivors to overcome trauma and assume positive roles in their communities. Provide age-sensitive dignity kits to 300 GBV survivors and at-risk individuals. The project will provide transportation assistance and logistical support for 300 GBV survivors from hard-to-reach areas to service centers. Provide emergency and life-saving medical care and support including PEP treatment administered within 72 hours after rape for 200 survivors.
2.3 Establish two alternative interim-care centers in Dollow and Kismayo providing full board for a total of 80 at risk/GBV survivors
2.4 Provide legal aid services for 50 GBV survivors. The project will coordinate with Jubaland line ministries, JRA, and community groups.
2.5 Train 150 districts authorities, village, religious and IDP leaders in principles of child protection, human rights and GBV as well as on the role of community towards prevention of child protection (sphere standards)
2.6 SEDHURO will carry out massive awareness campaigns through outreach activities, broadcast over local FM stations, dialogue sessions reaching out 120,00 community members on prevention of GBV and family separation.
2.7 provide structured PSS through CFS in six districts in Gedo and Lower Juba. The project, instead of reinventing the wheel, will build on existing CFS established by SEDHURO in previous/on-going programs. SEDHURO is running four CFSs in Kismayo, Dollow, Luq and Bardera supported by SHF and UNICEF. To avoid disruption of normal learning sessions, as children naturally prefer sports over being confined in class rooms, SEDHURO will coordinate with educators to ensure Child Friendly Space should only be facilitated during break times or after school hours to avoid disruption of normal learning sessions at schools . CFS can continue all-day long during weekends. SEDHURO will also carry out outdoor recreational facilities to ensure active engagement with excluded children. SEDHURO will provide capacity building to CFS facilitators and provide adequate supervision related to life skills and psychosocial support. SEDHURO will further use CFS to link to other services for community. For example, when caregivers gather in and near the CFS, SEDHURO will raise awareness on services available, identification of child protection concerns, referral pathways for services as well prevention strategies.

Needs

The pre-2018 drought crisis and the March 2019 through June 2019 flooding emergencies combined to aggravate the existing vulnerabilities of Gedo and Lower Juba communities, disproportionately affecting women and girls, exposing them to violence and abuse. More than 30,000 people, mostly from the agro-pastoral communities who had nothing left with them, have settled in the IDP camps in Kismayo, Doolow and Afmadow districts. Making the total IDP in the Jubaland State more than 200,000 people (according to Jubaland IDP agency(JRIA). UNHCR PRMN reported that 9,533 people arrived in Doolow, Kismaayo, Dhobley and Luuq districts in October and November 2017. Three new settlements, one in Afmadow and two in Kismayo have since been added to the more than 80 IDP settlements in Jubaland regions. This has also been partially aggravated by the military operation against AS North of Kismayo. According to people who fled the AS held areas, AS started force recruiting children and cited one of the reasons for their displacement. SEDHURO, through its partnership with UNICEF, DRC, NRC and SHF, observed an upsurge in gender based violence (GBV) targeting young girls in camps and homes the first quarter of 2017. SEDHURO recorded 766 GBV incidents in Jubaland state from December 2017 to date. Children as young as five and eight years of age are doing domestic work and shine shoes in markets, and the numbers continue to increase. Sexual violence was reported to be on the increase due to the state of the camps/settlements which lack adequate lighting, and diminishing resources for families especially minority clans and children with disabilities.Widespread displacement has further caused separation of families and communities, resulting the breakdown of community support systems and protection mechanisms. Families also suffered from separation as the men return to their farms while children and mothers are left in the IDP settlements. SEDHURO identified 842 UASC in Jubaland in 2018.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will sustain/reactivate community structures instrumental to the prevention of and response to Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection issues in Gedo and Lower Juba. Through the creation/reactivating and building the capacities of Child Protection Committees (CPCs) drawn from pre-existing indigenous structures like women and youth groups in nine in Jubaland state, SEDHURO will put in place functional community-led prevention of and response to GBV and Family separation. Because they are rooted in the community, Community Based mechanisms have the potential to sustain protection intervention after the project has exited.The project will greatly increase local knowledge concerning protection issues. Training of local authorities, community elders, IDP leaders, gate keepers, women leaders and youth on prevention and response to GBV and rights of children as well as training on how to care for survivors of sexual abuse will help mobilize community support and involvement in the project. Community sensitization, creating awareness and capacity building activities will result in increased community knowledge on how to prevent, reduce and respond to SGBV and family separation in their community.The project, while establishing linkages with other similar programs including SEDHURO's, will continue to carry out dialogue sessions and outreach programs that will gradually trigger behavioral change in treatment of women and children. Through its awareness/outreach and media campaigns, SEDHURO will challenge and change social norms that condone violence by using men and boys as champions of change. Community sensitization and project awareness will influence a push for change in attitudes toward SGBV and UASC issues at grassroots. The organization will also reintegrate UASC with primary care givers to enable them realize their potential and reduce their exposure to exploitation. In partnership with UNICEF, SEDHURO has provided 70 GBV survivors in Beledhawo with employability skills. NFE and vocational training has improved the skills and literacy levels of 70 women SGBV survivors in Beled Hawo district. Beneficiaries learned basic reading and writing skills as well as business skills in the traditional livelihood sector of making alindi (traditional somali garment) and tie and dye skills. Beneficiaries have credited the course as one that have helped them to overcome SGBV associated trauma by interacting with other survivors who are ready to rebuild and better their lives. Some beneficiaries have jump-started their own income generating activities through the provision of start-up kits. CFSs which provide structured PSS will be transitioned into learning spaces and will be handed over to the community.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The upsurge in displacement has increased protection concerns and exacerbated existing vulnerabilities, particularly among women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities (HRP, December 2017). GBVims indicates that 76% of GBV incidents occured in IDP camps. The breakdown of family and social structures which protect children and women from violence, abuse and exploitation in communities continues to put women and children at risk of violence. In 2018, SEDHURO identified 255 survivors subjected to GBV within IDPs in Gedo and L.Juba due to the state of the camps/settlements which lack adequate lighting, and diminishing resources for families especially minority clans and children with disabilities. According to HRP 2018, IDPs particularly women and children face distinct protection concerns including family separation and limitations on freedom of movementExploitative relationships between IDPs, landowners and informal IDP camp managers (‘gatekeepers’) are recurrent, affecting, for example, 2551 individuals in Kismayo forcibly evicted in 2017. This also affects IDPs’ access to assistance, protection and information, and increasing vulnerability and risk of forced eviction (NRC Eviction Tracker 2017).SEDHURO’s PRMN project noted displaced members faced serious protection problems including lose of property, sexual and gender based violence, forced recruitment of children and family separation en route to final destination. More than 80% of displaced member are women and children, who face sexual abuse for lack of male protection, when they leave settlements to collect firewood, fetch water or use distant or badly lit latrines.The project will include IDPs in the decision-making processes. CPCs will be drawn from IDP leadership. Massive awareness campaigns towards prevention of GBV and CP will be conducted in IDP settlements. CFS will be constructed where children of IDPs including those living with disability can access.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

GBVWG data reveals a gruesome 96% of GBV survivors are women and girls. SEDHURO records of GBV incidents echo with the national statistics. In partnership with UNICEF and SHF, on improving protection of children affected by drought and other emergencies in Gedo and Lower Juba, SEDHURO identified 766 GBV incidents, of which 92% were women and girls. Women and girls are also exposed to harmful traditional practices such as forced early marriages and FGM/C, which increase in situations of forced displacement. A UNICEF study found that the practice of FGM is almost universal in Somalia with 98% prevalence rate and no significant changes in FGM/C prevalence can be observed. Early marriage has become more common since the start of the 2017 drought as families seek to access dowry as an additional income source (Joint Partner Assessment, 2018).Loss of traditional societal role experienced by some men, combined with their perceived emasculation in response to women increasingly taking on the role as breadwinner, contributes to increase rates of domestic violence. The increased proportion of women working outside the home has also resulted in their greater vulnerability to exploitation. SEDHURO-UNICEF partnership has noted although there has been a general increase in awareness about rape and sexual violence, there remains a reluctance to talk about gender-based violence. For instance, UNICEF reported that 76% of women 15–49 years old consider a husband to be justified in hitting or beating his wife. The project will strive for disaggregated data to identify special needs according to age and gender. Provision of material/services will be appropriate to gender and age with female staff attending to female survivors. Outreach recreational services will be delivered to children who cant access CFS.50% of gender make-up will be observed throughout. Age-and-gender appropriate messaging will be designed to enhance access to information.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In the areas that remain under AlShabab’s control including the whole of Middle Juba regions, Jamame in L.Juba region, and outlying rural villages in all towns, women and girls live under the constant threat of sexual and gender-based violence. According to Human Rights Watch, the militant group has abducted women and girls, and forced them to marry fighters or work as slaves. Women in Gedo and L.Juba regions continue to be subject to high levels of conflict-related sexual violence and domestic violence. Police and militia members, sometimes, have been found to be perpetrators of rape against women. Women and girls living in Internally Displaced Person (IDP) camps are particularly vulnerable to rape, early and forced marriage, and recent reports have documented a pattern of sexual exploitation.Women and girls who have been abused often undergo prolonged psychological pain and rejection by their families and communities. According to HRP 2018, IDPs particularly women and children face distinct protection concerns including family separation and limitations on freedom of movement.Whilst most incidents of violence against women go unreported, there is a culture of impunity surrounding sexual and domestic violence in Somalia. Customary approaches to dealing with violence against women typically involve making “arrangements” between the clans of the victim and the rapist. According to the United Nations Human Rights Council, rape or domestic violence is treated as civil dispute, often resolved through either the payment of money or a forced marriage between the victim and the perpetrator.The project will provide legal aid support and comprehensive GBV response including timely medical assistances. Survivors found to be facing further risk will be placed in safe houses. SEDHURO will work with local authorities to ensure minority groups and other vulnerable groups are protected.
