End Date

31/12/2024

Project Summary

This response is planned to facilitate 18,586 affected people of whom 16,846 (7,581 girls, 379 girls with disabilities and 9,265 boys, 463 boys with disabilities) are emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) in 80 schools (including primary, Secondary, ALP and TVET in Upper Nile (Nasir and Maiwut), Eastern Equatoria (Budi, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta South) and Central Equatoria (Terekeka) states. Also, 880 PTAs (484 male, 396 female), 800 teachers (640 male, 160 female) and 60 (48 male and 12 female) will be trained, engaged and supported through the implementation of the following activities;
Establish 20 and rehabilitate 20 learning spaces in line with INEE standards and SS Education Cluster Strategy, establish 20 blocks (40 stances) and rehabilitate 15 blocks (30 stances) of gender segregated and disability inclusive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards and 20 changing rooms for girls. To improve access to education for girls and children with disabilities and other marginalized groups, the intervention will provide 3000 adolescent girls with dignity kits, conduct 80 awareness raising with community leaders on the importance of education to girls and children with disabilities, distribute hygiene kits to 4500 boys and girls and assistive devices to 1500 children with disabilities, establish 80 child friendly reporting mechanisms and train 478 teachers and education officials in child safeguarding, GBV, CP, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support. Also, 6000 children will be provided school meals in collaboration with WFP and ADRA will facilitate the establishment of 40 school gardens. 478 teachers will also be trained in PSS to provide PSS to at least 2160 children. learners will be provided with learning and recreational kits, 478 teachers will be provided with teaching materials, incentives and training on EIE, subject matter and pedagogical skills. In order to strengthen coordination and response of local education staff, 65 school management committees, PTAs and education officials will be trained in school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).

1. Needs

South Sudan still suffers from the accumulated years of conflict and instability that started in 2013. It has also been a victim of years of annual flooding, inter-communal violence, drought causing displacement, deaths and food insecurity. The Sudanese crisis that started in April 2023 has led to influx of refugee returnees, Sudanese refugees and Third Country Nationals who have been flooding South Sudan in hundreds of thousands. This situation has exacerbated an already dire humanitarian situation. ADRA South Sudan conducted a needs assessment to understand the humanitarian concerns and develop appropriate response. The assessment was conducted in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Warrap, Central and Western Equatoria revealing the following gaps. It was established that about 80% responses reported that there are school going age children who are out of school and don’t attend schooling. Among the reasons mentioned why children were out of school were; 55% Lack parental support for education of their children, parents have low attitude to education of the children, 15% mentioned of early and forced marriage as cultural influences in the communities, 30% Cultural practices of child labor; keeping children to look after animals, female kept at home doing house chores, poverty and lack of financial support to some children like orphans, and peer influences. 45% of the schools have fairly adequate learning space /structures and in good conditions. 25% of the structures were damaged but still usable, while 30% of the structures of the schools were reported to be either damaged or under trees classes. 65% of the survey targeted schools were reported to have latrines, but not enough for the learners, the children share latrines boys and girls which is not meeting the sphere standard of 1:30 girls and 1: 50 boys. While the remaining 35% were reported to have inadequate or no latrines. The survey established that teacher’s qualification still remained low; 38% teachers with secondary certificate, 25% teachers hold primary certificate, 27% hold Diploma certificates, while those qualified with Degrees were only about 10% of the teachers reached during the survey. Teachers’ salary is very low because of high inflation rate in the country. The assessment revealed that 30% of the learners and teachers were reported to have inadequate or no learning materials in the schools; the government paid less than 50% of the teachers and education partners paid about 36%. 80% of the schools surveyed have limited play materials such as netball, football, and skipping ropes. There was limited support to children with specific needs or disabilities in the schools. Some childrenin Kapoeta are mentioned supported with sign language lessons, while the rest had no much supportor attentions meanwhile that is not the case for Terekeka, Maiwut and Nasir counties. Most of the schools 70% do not have school gardens. According to the IPC report 2023, 37.1% of the population of Eastern Equatoria are in IPC phase 3 or more, 56.1% of UNS in 3 or more and47.0% in IPC 3 or more in CES. In Upper Nile, 70% of the targeted schools are not providing school Meals. In Terekeka, 40% reported schools were provided with school meals, 60% reported that there was no school meals provided to the schools. There was high need expressed by the stakeholders for support to be provided to school feeding program in the schools, particularly in Nasir and Maiwut counties of Upper Nile. The common disasters reported were flood, drought and heavy winds, fire, fighting/communal conflict and diseases. The main protection threats commonly reported in the communities of Kapoeta included; rape case (50%) responses, inter- communal violence (32 %) responses, exploitation by adults (36%), forced marriages and gender-based violence (86%) responses.

2. Project activities

The proposed project will work in 3 states and (Upper Nile, Eastern and Central Equatoria and 6 counties (Nasir, Maiwut, Budi, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South and Terekeka) and target 18,586 affected people (school children=16,846, PTAs=880, teachers=800 and education officials=60 in 80 schools (including primary, secondary, ALP and TEVT). It will strive to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 16,846 school children (boys=9,265, girls=7,581, boys with disabilities= 463, girls with disabilities=379) emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys). It will further more improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments and finally strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities by undertaking the following key activities: Establish 20 disability inclusive Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) in line with INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019, rehabilitate 20 disability inclusive TLS/schools , establish 20 blocks (40 stances) and rehabilitate 15 blocks (30 stances) of gender sensitive and disability inclusive latrines according to INEE and sphere standards. Further more, 20 changing rooms will be established for girls and dignity kits will be provided to 3000 adolescent girls. ADRA will also provide hygiene to 4500 boys and girls. Meanwhile assistive devices will be provided to 1500 children with disabilities. Also, 6000 children will be provided school meals in collaboration with WFP and ADRA will facilitate the establishment of 40 school gardens. 478 teachers will also be trained in PSS to provide PSS to at least 2160 children. learners will be provided with learning and recreational kits, 478 teachers will be provided with teaching materials, incentives and training on EIE, subject matter and pedagogical skills. In order to strengthen coordination and response of local education staff, 60 school management committees, PTAs and education officials will be trained in school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
