Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect food production capacities and income sources in locations that are most at risk due to the chronic electricity deficit in the Gaza Strip. The rationale of this project is to target areas most impacted by the increasing electricity deficit in order to protect current levels of food and agricultural production, livelihoods and food security by providing vulnerable agricultural producers in the Gaza Strip with a consistent, clean and renewable source of energy to guarantee sufficient electricity for essential food production.
This intervention responds to the first need under the Agri Livelihoods section in the Food Security Sector Response Plan, which indicates the need for interventions that prioritize responses to the electricity shortage in the Gaza Strip. The intervention falls under Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP 2018-2022 as it invests in solar energy sources to ensure that targeted farmers have a consistent electricity supply as and when required in order to cope with the protracted crisis.
As an emergency intervention, this project aims to minimize damages and losses to domestic crop production in the current season and to enable normal levels of cultivation in subsequent seasons. In doing so, this project will protect agriculture based livelihoods, food security and domestic food production that are at risk given the chronic electricity deficit and will enable normal levels of cultivation if electricity supply to the Gaza Strip is further reduced.

Needs

Gaza’s farmers have been impacted considerably by the ongoing chronic electricity deficit, particularly in the southeast of the Gaza Strip where a significant proportion of local agricultural produce is sourced. Given the need for approximately 10 hours of electricity per day for the irrigation of crops, farmers had barely been managing to sustain agricultural production on eight hours of electricity per day. With electricity supply down to a mere three to four hours per day and the possibility of further reductions , Gaza’s farmers are faced with the choice of either substantially reducing the scale of agricultural production or investing in costly diesel generators with which to pump the required amounts of irrigation water.According to Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) estimates, the cost of one cubic metre of irrigation water has increased by up to 40 percent since Gaza’s sole power plant ceased operations in April 2017, before resuming at very limited capacity, with fears that the cost of irrigation water will increase further should the supply of electricity decrease further. Moreover, according to the MoA, up to 60 percent of agricultural land across the Gaza Strip remains at risk of being set aside due to the lack of electricity with which to pump water for irrigation. The overall impact of the increasing electricity deficit, in terms of the extent of damages and losses to Gaza’s agriculture sector, has the potential not only to reverse the gains made by reconstruction and recovery efforts since the end of the 2014 conflict but also to further jeopardize agriculture based livelihoods, to exhaust the coping mechanisms of farming households and, consequently, lead to a sizeable reduction in local agricultural production which will in turn have a negative impact on food security levels in the Gaza Strip. Moreover, according to the findings of a field assessment conducted by FAO in 2018 in response to Gaza’s deepening electricity crisis, farming communities in the Gaza Strip reported that input costs for planting had increased significantly as a result of the chronic electricity deficit. Increased water scarcity has further impacted the quality of crops for the summer season and has negatively affected prices in local markets as well as the competitiveness of Gazan produce in the West Bank and Israel. Women farmers are most impacted as they bear ever increasing levels of responsibility both inside and outside the home. According to the Gender Alert Report (2015) by UN Women and OCHA, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered to be “helpers” regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Data from PCBS indicate that 18.2% of economically active women work in agriculture and constitute 30% of agricultural employees in Gaza, thereby masking biases that exist with regard to recognizing the contribution of rural women in agricultural labour including as unpaid family workers in this sector. As a result, investment in renewable energy sources has emerged as key to protecting Gaza’s increasingly vulnerable agriculture sector.

Activities

FAO will provide time critical, in kind and technical support to targeted households working in the agriculture and livestock sectors through provision of solar energy systems to enable food producers to resume normal levels of food production.With the overall aim of protecting food production and productivity, FAO will target locations across the Gaza Strip where food production is most at risk due to the chronic electricity deficit. Women headed households, women’s cooperatives and households whose female members are actively engaged in agriculture related activities in support of household income will be prioritized during the community targeting and beneficiary selection processes. In close consultation with the MoA, the areas most impacted by the increasing electricity deficit and currently most at risk have been identified as: East Rafah Governorate West Rafah Governorate East Khan Yunis Governorate East Deir al Balah Governorate North Gaza Governorate Gaza Governorate All project activities will be coordinated with the MoA for technical matters. Operational coordination will also take place with the Agriculture Working Group of the Food Security Sector which includes all local and international organizations involved in Gaza’s agriculture sector. All project activities have been designed in consultation with the communities to be targeted by this project in order to respond to the needs expressed by the targeted communities. Prior to project implementation, in each of the targeted communities Local Committees will be formed to participate in the beneficiary selection process, to provide support during distribution of inputs, and to assist the FAO project team with follow up activities. The selection of the local NGO partner will be according to FAO global rules and regulations. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. Project impact will be assessed by comparing the results of a baseline and end line survey. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific time frames including: preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post distribution mounting as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant.. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the Food Security Sector to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives.
