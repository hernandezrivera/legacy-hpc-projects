Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict, and food insecurity. Prolonged insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable women and girls, including survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). With the protracted conflict and the deteriorating economic situation in the country, women and girls continue to face significant risks of GBV including sexual violence, intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harmful traditional practices like child marriage. More recently, the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 has led to an influx of people fleeing the country. This crisis has affected South Sudan significantly, with more than 700,000 fleeing from Sudan into South Sudan, about 49% of the new arrivals are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18. Many of the returnees and refugees to South Sudan are highly vulnerable and arriving in critically underdeveloped areas placing increased pressure on already strained resources. As per the results of the latest Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) analysis, 46% of the total population, or 5.83 million people, are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, with 1.64 million in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Additionally, 35,000 people are classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in specific areas. Specifically in Kapoeta North and South 45% of the population were classified in phase 3 and above with 10% in phase 4 during the analysis period. It is expected that this will increase to 50% with 15% in phase 4 in projection 1 (Oct 2023 – Mar 2024), and further to 55% with 20% in phase 4 in projection 2 (Apr – Jun 2024) Societal norms, values, practices and gender related power imbalances are key drivers of the various forms of GBV women and girls face, even not related to conflict. The nutrition situation in Kapoeta North and South has been a concern as per nutrition information working group (NIWG) priority counties, and the latest SMART survey conducted in April 2023 showed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 24.7%, which is above the emergency threshold of 15%. There have been developments since then, including the influx of Sudanese refugees and South Sudanese returnees, which have created an information gap. Inter-sectoral assessments conducted by humanitarian partners in flood-affected areas Eastern Equatoria state indicated lack of protection assistance, including dignity kits for displaced women and girls of reproductive age. The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services coupled with shrinking humanitarian funding, highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized GBV response services. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, psychosocial support, safety and security, livelihood, economic support, and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practices, and the Community Cares program, integration of GBV actions into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are high priority areas for interventions.
On this note, Lulu Care is proposing to scale up and promote GBViE response, prevention, and risk mitigation programming to address the new and emerging needs arising from various crisis in Eastern Equatoria. Lulu Care is well placed to respond to the urgent needs of the vulnerable affected population in the target locations and will jointly plan with local authorities and communities for the timely delivery of integrated Emergency GBV prevention and response assistance. Special focus will be placed on community participation, especially women and girls, and both with host communities and returnees in the process. Lulu Care will continue regularly to report implementation of GBV interventions on 5Ws report and resources mobilized on the FTS.

1. Needs

According to the HNO 2024, An estimated 9 million people, including refugees in South Sudan, are experiencing critical needs through 2024. This means that some people will experience needs that humanitarians cannot respond to. An estimated 7.1 million people will require food assistance during the lean season from April to July 2024, as projected through the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). Children remain at critical risk of malnutrition, and the IPC projection is that1.6 million children aged 6 to 59 months are at risk of acute malnutrition, of whom 480,000 are at risk of severe acute malnutrition. The impact of the Sudan crisis resulted in the arrival of vulnerable population that require assistance, increasing market costs and further stretching people’s coping capacities. Women and girls remain at risk of gender-based violence (GBV) as they try to undertake their daily activities. Violence, displacement and high levels of deprivation is felt in some parts of the country. People in South Sudan suffer the consequences of development deficit characterized by chronic poverty, lack of livelihoods and limited access to basic social services. South Sudan is one the countries badly affected by climate change. Dry spell and flooding contribute to people’s food insecurity situation.GBV is widespread, particularly affecting women and girls due to entrenched societal norms and practices that violate their rights. Intimate partner violence and conflict-related sexual violence are prevalent due to the prolonged nature of the conflict. A national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022 revealed that 58 per cent of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months. There is an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation and scale-up of response services in 25 priority counties including. According to the 2023 FSNMS+ analysis, cultural barriers reportedly include male control over decisions around income generating activities and over income earned. Perceptions that females without male support (such as those heading households and widows) should not engage in certain service sector activities has also been reported. The UNICEF-(MRM) points out that the risk of child marriage remains constant due to conflict and the country’s economic situation and harmful social norms . The quadrant of insecurities, loss of livelihood, changes in gender roles and lack of quality GBV services have culminated into increased incidences of GBV. Sexual violence remains high. Lulu Care GBV helpline 2023 report (January-December) indicates that 42% of the received calls are domestic violence cases, fueled by poverty and its related consequence. 34% of such women have sustained injuries due to the violence. Furthermore, women and girls continue to face extreme levels of GBV much of which goes under reported especially in remote locations, with limited availability of lifesaving GBV response services to survivors there has been loss of lives. Majority of survivors of GBV are often not aware of available services and they are afraid of seeking help. In Tonj South the inter-communal fights have displaced communities, majority of whom are women, children and the elderly, including persons with specific needs. According to the FGDs conducted by Lulu Care in Kapoeta there has been continued vulnerability of women to rape and harassment during the collecting of firewood in far bushes. All women that responded stated that they do not report attacks and incidents inflicted. With dire need for supporting survivors there is need to strengthen remote responses to effect the remote GBV service delivery predominately emotional support and case management) over a technology platform (i.e. hotline) PWDs continue to face multiple challenges including accessibility and attitudinal barriers that hinder their participation in social, economic and political life.
