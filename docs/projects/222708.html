Project Summary

The populations in South Sudan continue to face an increased risk of explosive hazards in many parts suspected of being contaminated with landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW). The HNRP 2025 indicate that, Renk county and Abyei Administrative area has the highest levels of contamination exposing inhabitants of the area into high risk of maiming, injuries and death by explosive Ordinance. The 2025 IMSMA estimates that; 114 mined areas measuring 5,315,105 square metres, including; 69 CHAs measuring 2,988,573 square metres and 45 SHAs measuring 2,326,532 square metres remained un-cleared. which prompted NMAA, UNMAS and partners in South Sudan to seek 2024-2026 country strategic plan extension for clearance of 4,589,000 square metres; including; 1,374,000 square metres, 1,638,000 square metres and 1,577,000 square metres by 2026 respectively.
The extended strategic plan of action for South Sudan has therefore, faced numerous challenges due to funding shortfall for mine action operation countrywide which is intended to meet the deadline stipulated in the plan and to declare a country free of mines and Explosive Ordinance by 2026.
The trend continues exposing innocent civilians into high risk while exercising their livelihood activities including collecting water from the water points or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, farming and receiving health care, among other essential services. It further prevents humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas.
As such the main objective of this project is to ensure provision of safe environment free from hazard by clearing the contaminated land, raising awareness and promoting behavioral change, including public information dissemination, education and training at community levels, schools, churches, radio talk-show, awareness on safety and prevention messages and handing over the cleared land that’s free from Exploded hazards to the community for use.
The project is aimed at clearing explosive debris of war to pave way for development and social activities, Rehabilitation and reconstruction which targets refugee’s population, elderly people, IDPs, returnees, host community who have a lot of potential in socio-economic activities for improving their livelihoods.
The project will be tailored to over 14,700 people who will benefit from EORE activities and about 98,000m2 of land will be cleared and officially release to the community for further usage by December 2025.

1. Needs

The Republic of South Sudan gained independence on 9th July 2011 after 50 years of civil war with North Sudan which granted the conflicting parties to signed a comprehensive peace agreement(CPA) through compromise. The two decades of civil war has resulted into high Land Contamination which presents Significant risks to human security manifested by lack of adequate food production, crippling socio-economic activities such as farming, fishing, trading, and animals’ production, render large scale of land unproductive which affects the provision of mainstreaming services including health, education, Nutrition, FSL and Agroforestry projects due to the presence of landmines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) amongst other explosive ordinance used by all parties to the conflict, including Small Arms and Light Weapons (SALW) continue to impact communities while exercising their livelihood activities.The 2025 HNRP estimated that, 373,799 people most of whom are boys, girls, men and women are in need of EORE services and over 2,627,943m2 of land is expected to be cleared to allow resettlement of refugee, IDPs, Returnee and the Host population.Its well noted that, South Sudan, with particular emphasis on Renk County and Abyei Administrative Region is witnessing large influx of refugees, returnees, IDPs and the host population most of whom are less-informed about the risk posted by mines and Explosive Ordinance(EO) only to be expose unto being maimed, killed, threatened, prevention of communities from accessing or receiving humanitarian aid assistance and development and reconstruction is stalled due to the threats posted by Exploded Ordinance, death and daily occurrence of incidences and injuries of local population as a result of the presence of Explosive Ordinance Country-wide.The PRMS report indicated that incidences of Mine/EO are on the rise: according to the most recent Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) figure out that; In every 7 people killed (4 men and 3 children) and 21 injured (11 men, 1 woman and 9 children) as a result of contamination which poses an immediate risk of death or injury on the local populations within the targeted project locations.Under this project, OSIL intend to deploy two (2) EOD teams and two(2) EORE teams to Renk County and Abyei Administrative Area to provide clearance and Risk Education(RE) services with the purpose to grand access to safe environment, safe agricultural & Livelihood practices, continues infrastructural development and to pave way for humanitarian interventions and responses in Support of the refugees, IDPs, Returnees and the host population in the targeted project locations.The project will be tailored to over 14,700 people most of whom are boys, girls, women, men and people living with disabilities who will benefit from EORE activities and about 98,000m2 of land will be cleared from explosive debris of war and officially release to the community for further usage and to pave way for developmental projects, social activities, Rehabilitation and reconstruction by December 2025. The project targeted beneficiaries includes; refugee’s population, elderly people, IDPs, Migrants, returnees and the host community who have a lot of potential in socio-economic activities to improving their livelihoods and economic well-being.

2. Project activities

1- Conduct Survey and clearance of land from explosive hazards2- Liaison with the community during project implementation and land release processes.3- Conduct awareness raising sessions on key EORE messages in schools and the community4- Conduct training to the staff of humanitarian partners with operational presence in the project location. 5- Conduct community-based EORE training to caregivers of mine/ERWs victims, community leaders, local authority leader, women and youth groups 6- Organize training to teachers/PTAs/SMCs on EORE messages to impart knowledge to the children at school and their families in both locations. 7- Provide training to adolescent peer educators on EORE messaging to support in delivery of risk education safety messages in schools and community. 8- Community Dialogues (Community Meetings). These will be general community meetings where ERW threat, risk-taking behavior, and target groups will be informed, 9- Community Awareness -raising on EORE, this will be done daily for five days in a week targeting communities in locations covered by the project. 10- Drama Sensitization will be based on pre-rehearsed skits with key EORE messages to target populations depending on the kind of risk and behavior being targeted for change.11- Public Information Dissemination, will be part of the integrated EORE package to reinforce the strategies mentioned.12- Formalizing the set-up of a data collection process and system at the county headquarter level to integrate EORE-related data (i.e. victims and survivors data) into the classic IMSMA forms and share with NMAA and UNMAS database.13- Data Collection: OSIL will collect Hazardous area and accident information which will be handed to UNMAS, NMAA for internal planning and prioritization. 14- Teachers' and Peer to Peer training as part of integrating EORE into School Curriculum 15- Safer Community Plan’ component, which aims to assist in the development of community-based plans and activities in communities in the project locations so as to promote a community and people-centered approach to safety.16- identification and marking of the finding, and sharing the reports with UNMASS and NMAA.17- Conduct regular IQA/EQA during project implementation18- Ensure accountability and reporting of the project 19- To regularly conduct Protection Risk Monitoring(PRMS) and reporting
