1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phases 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.As per Integrated Food Security Phase Classification report published on 18th of November 2024 and its projection for April – July 2025, 57.2% of the population will be in phase 3 or above, who need food assistance. When reviewing this by State, the two States seriously affected because of the combined effect of flood and communal conflict are Unity State, affected 71.6% and Jonglai State, affected 62.4%. Considering the resources scarcity and other partners contribution, from Unity States, Mayom and Pariang Counties are targeted, who has got 81,000 and 75,000 people in need respectively and from Jonglai State, Bor South and Pibor Counties are targeted, who has 88,000 and 200,000 people in need.The main drivers of severity of food insecurity are floods, dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In South Sudan, 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry. Climate induced shocks (floods events, localized dry spells and conflicts for grazing areas and farming) have increased violent conflicts among agropastoral communities and have led to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the agricultural productivity. Among the most affected people are child headed households, persons with disabilities, older people and women headed households. South Sudan ranks amongst the top ten countries in the world most vulnerable to climate impact, including droughts and flooding. Flooding has been a major driver of displacement followed by conflicts.

2. Project activities

Project activitiesThe project will have an entry activity which include conducting project inception meeting in all four-project operation area involving key stakeholders, facilitating consultation, mobilization and sensitization of stakeholders at Payam level and consequently doing beneficiary selection and verification with the wider community. Result 1, Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs.Provide MPCA to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 13,475 vulnerable households (8685 female & 5790 male), who are in IPC 4 & above including men, women, boys, girls of different ages and people with and without disabilities.1.1 Sensitization sessions on proper utilization of MPCA, all the targeted individuals will receive one day orientation on how to receive and use the MPCA. This will be done by both this project staffs together with the County Agricultural department. 1.2 Provision of MPCA for targeted 13,475 households in IPC-4+. Even though, still quick market and risk assessment will be done before this project action, as per JMMI information of October publication from REACH, all this project operation areas market are functional and allows for partner to plan and implement any CVA programs. This project considers MBE estimation of $165 for this month considering 30% increase from the previous JMMI MBE rate. With this consideration this project planned to distribute $83 /household per month for two consecutive months to quickly fix the food shortage for the most vulnerable households. This unconditional cash transfer with cash is to fix their immediate needs. Just to get the right feedback on the cash distribution amount, time and site, the project will conduct PDM in two – three weeks period after completing the cash distribution.Result 2: Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ prioritiesWith respect to integrating Protection, this project will facilitate and conduct safety audit so as to determine the right place and time for any inputs/cash distribution and to decide the venue for any of the planned training in such a way to avoid any harm to women and girls. With overall aim of ensuring Accountability to Affected People (AAP), this project facilitate formation and training of GRM Committee within each operational Counties and Payam so as to receive timely complain and give proper feedback as part of accountability measures on top of ensuring active engagement, consultation and participation of all project beneficiaries.Result 3: Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased and resilience-building opportunities are optimized.With overall aim of reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs support, 5,000 individuals from host communities and returnees will have an improved ability to absorb shocks with various resilience building interventions. MPCA given for 5000 individuals to rehabilitate dikes for flood protection; With overall aim of reducing the impact of flood in such flood affected area, this project will facilitate the construction of flood protection structure after doing quick field assessment and consultation with concerned community key leaders. For this flood protection structure construction, the project will use MPCA as module and will pay 5000 people for one month and everyone will get $ 83/month. The project will also try to mobilize more community contribution on top of this cash payment to maximize the impact and ensure ownership. 3.6 Facilitate establishment and support of VSLAiE; Village Saving and Loan Association in Emergency module will be used. The VSLA in Emergencies (VSLAiE) model combines VSLAs and cash and voucher assistance to improve.N
