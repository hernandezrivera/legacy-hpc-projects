Project Summary

This project aims to monthly distribute 3000 food baskets in Atma camps which are located in Idleb, Harim, Harim, Harim.
This project has a two-fold objective, as follows:
1. Reduce hunger by providing access to food to the population affected by the crisis especially people living inside camps in Dana sub-district to have access to life-saving food to meet their basic food needs, all year round.
2. Prevent malnutrition the targeted children, aged 6-23 months, receive specialized nutritious foods to prevent acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies.

Needs

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 5,898,489 million people will be in need of urgent humanitarian assistance in Syria. In Harim, Dana (population 821,640), there is a total of 266,199 people in need. The food consumption score breakdown is as follows: 8.6% poor; 26.2% borderline; 65.2% acceptable. Early 2018, the Camp Coordination and Camp Management Cluster (CCCM) tracked over 385,000 displaced people who arrived at the central and northern parts of Idleb Governorate (some may be secondary displacements) as a result of the GoS offensive on areas east and south Idleb and northern Hama, some of them have returned to their communities and others are still displaced. This ongoing crisis resulted in destruction and potential explosive hazard contamination of civilian infrastructure, including schools, medical facilities, local markets, housing, IDP settlements, and have disrupted humanitarian activities. In the coming period, significant displacements are expected in Idleb and surrounding areas. This will cause an increased need for a multi-sectoral response to mass displacements and immediate needs of host communities and IDPs, especially emergency services.North-western Syria has received several influxes of internally displaced people (IDPs) from different parts of the country, exacerbating already existing high levels of needs. Between December 2017 and February 2018, 385,000 people were displaced due to violence in the southern and eastern parts of Idleb governorate. Subsequently, between March and July 2018, there was an additional influx of IDPs (129,000 people) from areas that witnessed the implementation of local agreements to north-western Syria.Of note, the recent waves of displacements led to an increase in the percentage of IDPs residing in IDP camps/settlements from 23 per cent in late 2017 to 35 per cent in June 2018 “Allocation Strategy Paper, Turkey Humanitarian Fund for Syria, 2018 2nd Standard Allocation”. IDPs residing in IDP settlements are often more vulnerable during winter. According to the latest monthly IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix, there are 313 registered IDPs sites hosting 437,747 IDPs in northwest Syria. In Idleb, there are 276 sites with 302,547 IDPs. The majority of the IDP sites are by running beyond their absorption capacity at 400 per cent. The recent large waves of displacements put immense pressure on the already overpopulated sites and created additional housing land and property considerations.Eight years into the crisis, the main triggers of food insecurity in Syria remain insecurity, financial and physical access to food, and depletion of livelihoods. The first six months of 2018 saw massive changes in context related to displacements and returnees, shifting lines of control, end of besiegement in many places, as well as new offensives. These changes had major impact on food security and agriculture/livelihoods. In 2018 there were successive waves of displacements occurred within and into northwest Syria. Additionally, local level agreements resulted in over 129,000 people being evacuated from Eastern Ghouta, Rural Homs, Damascus to areas in northwest Syria, adding additional burden unto the overstretched resources of the host communities. Moreover, the demand for and price of food and non-food items for IDPs and residents also increased in September. WFP and other INGOs tried to alleviate the suffering for people either if they are HH or IDPs through distributing regular food rations, lipid based nutrient supplements – medium quantity (LNS-MQ), and ready to eat rations. According to “Syria IDP flow and returnee data - October 2018” Idleb has received 1,780,011 individuals since January 2016 and in 2018 Idleb received 610,337 individuals coming from several cities but mainly from; Homs, Daraa and Damascus.

Protection Risk Analysis

-Some people and dominant groups in the work area do not accept the idea of participation of women in project activities:Mitigation: External awareness sessions to the community on women's rights and the importance of their participation in the project.B- Abuse of women or exploitation:Mitigation: Strict measures and training of the field staff on protection topics in addition to taking measures such as providing transportation for women beneficiaries to attend training in addition to the permanent presence of women from the field staff during the distribution of vouchers in and activating the mechanisms of reporting and investigation if necessary (hotline).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

One of the possible risks during the implementation of any project is the intervention of military factions or civilian entities in humanitarian work.As part of the management of this risk: The Safety and Security department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects, and building a trust relationship with the local councils and community leaders by clarifying for the local authorities the organization and project in the area and signing a MOUs at the beginning of each project.The S&S department is committed to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality, and it strictly follows the JOPs principles, and work hard to prevent any kind of intervention by the controlling armed groups or any other de facto authority.In a complex conflict environment as Syria, all changes in conflict are expected, Including armed clashes or military advances of SDF, for exampleAs part of the risk management, The S&S department follows any developments in the project area, conducts a daily risk assessment of the area and informs the administration of any possible scenarios and how to deal with them, including evacuation and contingency plans and training the field staff on all expected scenarios.The safety of field staff comes first, especially with regard to the risks to human workers from arrests, kidnappings or even direct targeting.The management of risks related to the humanitarian worker begins by training humanitarian workers to commitment to the humanitarian principles, especially neutrality towards the parties to the conflict, in addition to specialized training in the person security and staff movement (movement clearance) in addition to effective communication during movement and the provision of all security and safety equipment.The safety of employees begins with the selection of their workplaces. Therefore, the Safety and Security Department is careful to choose the centers that achieve highest levels of personal safetyThe Security and Safety department coordinate with the security and safety assistants at the project site to select work places that meet the following safety and security standards- The choice of center away from military crowding - Provide security and safety materials in the center (extinguishers - first aid - means of communication ---)- Preparation of emergency exits.- check list of safety and security standards within the center.- Considering the proximity of the centers to emergency health center.Information security is very important, especially with regard to the risk of losing financial documents. Therefore, the Security and Safety department manages this risk by training the field staff on information security and signing them on the security policies in addition to daily sending documents (copy financial -copy program) to project manager, and coordinating with the Logistics department to send the original documents monthly to the organization's off
