Project Summary

The project is in line with HRP 2021 objective 1 and 2 and also with cluster/sector specific objectives 1, 2 & 3, Besides, it aligns with the cluster strategy to ‘Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5’. This project will improve access to safe, quality and inclusive protective education services to 12,000 emergency and COVID-19 affected children (boys, girls and youths) in Fangak, Ayod and Nasir Counties by 2021. The integrated education project by ADA in hard-to-reach areas aims to ensure that children (boys and girls) have access to quality and inclusive education and those already in schools are retained in Nasir County (Upper Nile State), Fangak and Ayod Counties (Jonglei State). The following interventions are envisaged: establishing safe learning and recreational facilities in 12 locations – 4 in each County; teacher training to enhance the teaching/learning function supported in schools; providing monthly stipends to 84 volunteer teachers - 28 teachers per County; campaign for education, encouraging children, especially girls and people with disability to return to school; and provision of school feeding programs to enhance attendance and retention of children at schools. Response priorities will include gender-segregated WASH facilities with much concentration in COVID-19 prevention measures, inclusion of GBV counselling rooms in the schools, provision of learning, teaching and WASH materials, capacity building of School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs), life-skills and recreational opportunities to support the recovery of children and to promote their overall well-being. Equal access to inclusive education will be prioritized regardless of gender, disability or ethnic background. Through introduction of school gender-sensitive WASH programme, this project will have established strong WASH facilities in schools. The project will seek to support prevention of COVID-19 through rehabilitation/construction of hand-washing facilities and latrines, provision of soap, face masks, sensitizers and maintenance of social distancing. Carry out messaging on coronavirus signs and symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention to children and their parents.
The project will seek to address one of the top causes of school drop-outs (School feeding) that will help increase and maintain school enrolment and attendance, and reduce risks of death, disease and cognitive underdevelopment. School feeding will also improve protection of- in-school and out of school children by keeping them away from potential recruitment into armed groups, child labor, early marriage, exploitation, sexual violence and abduction while on their way to schools and back home. This will help to reduce morbidity and absenteeism, support retention in schools and children to help sensitize their communities. Crisis-affected children will be engaged in basic education and recreational activities in order to create an enabling environment for their critical development and positive engagement with the community. Youths will catch up on primary education through accelerated learning programmes (ALP) to improve their literacy and numeracy, technical and vocational training, and life skills. The project will seek to include physically and mentally challenged children; girls and boys vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups, and child labour; youths; adolescent heads of households; young mothers; and children from different ethnic groups. Teachers, SMCs and PTAs will be trained on life skills and referral pathways to enhance the protective aspect of education in emergency (EiE) intervention. The project will include early childhood development program across schools in the project sites.

1 - Needs

The general humanitarian situation in Fangak County is critical due to the flood which affected all villages in most of 2020. Despite support being rendered by partners in the area, there is vast unmet needs of the community. The flooding disrupted people’s livelihoods thereby creating a dire humanitarian situation ADA runs two schools in Fangak, but these are closed because the surrounding areas are flooded. According to IRNA (2nd and 5th February 20221) reports in Old and new Fangak clearly indicates that IDPS and returnees who settled along the White Nile were being displaced by flooding. It adds that education and other humanitarian needs in New Fangak County require urgent intervention. The local area authority and assessment team indicated that two PPayams (Manejang and Phom) were the worst affected by the flood, and an estimated population of 48,103 individuals (7,944 households) are reported to be directly affected, with15% believed to be children under five years of age. Payam. In the second half of 2020, heavy rains resulted in massive flooding and also caused the water level in rivers to rise to unprecedented levels even during the dry season and this affected Old Fangak, Paguir, Mareang and Keew Payams. Local authorities had estimated the number of affected and displaced households to be 7,357 comprised of 44,472 individuals. The breakdown estimates of population affected can be found in an Inter-Agency Spot-Check Assessment Report to Floods Affected Communities in Northern Part of Jonglei State Fangak County. On 7th to 17th August 2020, an Inter-Agency Assessment was conducted at Ayod County of Jonglei State. The information collected was based on direct interviews and focus group discussions with affected community members and IDPs, local chiefs, local governmental authority representatives in Ayod County’s eight Payams (Pajiek, Korwai, Kuachdeng, Ayod Town, Padek, Wau, Mogok and Pagil). Most of the displaced persons (IDPs), according to the local authorities, moved to higher grounds while some settled in Eastern Canal (Ayod Town and surroundings). Within 4th – 8th Sept, 2020 many villages along Gilo, Korwakow and Sobat Rivers were flooded due to outburst of the rivers coupled with heavy rain falls. The populations were either wholly or partly displaced within the same village or to the other nearby villages. In total, six Payams, 29 villages, 15,107 Individuals, 3,914 HHs were affected by flooding out of 56,405 catchment population. Education priority needs and recommendation's for all locations: There is need for humanitarian intervention in order to assist the school children with learning materials including psychosocial supporting order for them to withstand the emergency stress. Most of the children interviewed reported losing their school materials. These children need to be provided with school learning materials/aid in order to mitigate the high dropout among school-age children within the affected communities. There is need to provide incentives to teachers, and rehabilitate schools destroyed by floods. There is also need to construct WASH facilities segregated by gender boys and girls

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ADA through project front line staff and community volunteers will provide awareness and sensitization in the community on Protection against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA). ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will train and guide the front-line staff while carrying out the activities. A strong referral pathway will be built where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies.ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through awareness raising and also provide information on who to report to and how to report preparators. ADA will map out PSEA partners and establish safe referral pathways to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including staff dismissal and taking legal steps against preparators. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee will be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The front-line staff will be reaching out to the beneficiaries who are unable to reach to the ADA field offices or complaint desks as part of their daily outreach activities. This will help those with disabilities and others who cannot easily access the feedback and complaint desk to express their feedback on the services provided by ADA in the community. The outreach staff will fill for them and what they recommend to be done to at least satisfy their basic needs. A good practice that ADA has used in the past is to ensure that people with disabilities, different vulnerabilities, girls, elderly etc. are represented in the monitoring teams and specifically gather info from their own category. Special desks, lines, suggestion boxes that suit their needs are provided. Oftentimes, special people who can translate or have special ways of rapport with the various categories are identified to be representatives in a feedback loop on project matters (including complaints). These methods will be employed to ensure the project feedback mechanism is representative and inclusive of the various categories of beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedbacks: design, implement, collect data and analyses; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop. In this project: 1. ADA will use available procedures in previous projects to adapt to the feedback needs of the current project, by involving beneficiaries on how best they would like their feedbacks to be recorded, analysed, and handled in a manner that is satisfactory to them. This will be area-specific and handled with utmost confidentiality and practicality. The modalities used by ADA include suggestion boxes, phone calls, use of SMS, face-to-face meetings, focus group discussions, project implementation committee appointees, complaints and complements forms, and questionnaires. Beneficiaries make their choice and this informs the course of action. 2. At the implementation level, ADA will agree with the beneficiaries on how to participate in collecting/gather complaints and complements realistically and in a timely, realistic manner, meeting the needs of beneficiaries in specific locations (Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir) 3. ADA will agree with beneficiaries on how data collected (complaints and compliments) will be analysed, by whom and at what frequency to provide needed feedback. This stage includes concrete action points on matters raised and possible redress or way forward 4. ADA will agree in advance on how the actions generated can be shared internally or externally to create the needed change or course of action that was desired by beneficiaries. At this stage, the communities are informed of necessary changes, by when and how. ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. The action points will provide feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, strengthening the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Positive feedback motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct in beneficiary communities. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold ADA and other decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.
