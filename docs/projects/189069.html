Project Summary

The Climate Change Vulnerability Index for South Sudan is estimated to be high being ranked among the five most affected countries in the world. The country’s climate change is characterized by extreme with localized droughts, torrential rains and seasonal flooding.
The main goal of this WASH project is to improve wellbeing of people affected by disaster and chronically exposed to lack of basic water and sanitation services in the targeted counties. Despite of a typically widespread and severe flooding due to climate change affecting large population of people, the project will contribute to reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality and provide safe, equitable access to water and sanitation basic services to achieve.
The Country is regularly affected by predictable natural disaster such as flood and period of dry spell. The intensity of flooding dramatically increased in last year, affecting more than a million people with unprecedented flooding with severe damages caused to infrastructures, including water and sanitation facilities. Consequences of flooding includes increased risk of WASH-related diseases outbreaks, reduce access to water and sanitation facilities.

Flooding affected different parts of the country, leaving the people more vulnerable with increased humanitarian needs including WASH needs. The living standard and well being of estimated 5.9 million people were negatively impacted by inadequate access to clean water, sanitation and poor hygiene practices last year. Whilst a rough trend analysis anticipates possible number of people in need estimated 6.1 million people’s living standard and their well being will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation and hygiene.
The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding increased displacement also placed an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites.
Displacement, COVID I9 restriction and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of water points.
Insecurity is also a factor that has affected water access. Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points both rural and in urban settings where women feel unsafe while collecting water.
The response will mitigate WASH related GBV with high risk of protections issues by enhancing safety audits at site level to identify GBV concerns and implement mitigation measures where women shall be given equal opportunities in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation.
The project will respond to the protection crisis GBV by providing services to survivors of GBV and dedicate services for women with protection concerns. The project will ensure WASH facilities are safe, gender appropriate and maintained in secure locations as discussed with affected groups most focus on women, girls and persons with disabilities.
Based on FSNMS analysis, overall 80% of households did not access to sanitation facilities with most households practicing open defecation. The floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is need for the communities to move to resilience, reconstruct or repair sanitation facilities.
Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in the focused locations. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases which should be mitigated.
The project will target 36000 beneficiaries Women 8640, Girls 9360, Men 8280 and Boys 9720 to undertake different roles and activities of the project. Community approach and vulnerability criteria will be used in beneficiary selection. .
