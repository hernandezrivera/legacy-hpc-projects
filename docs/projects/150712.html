Project Summary

The project will focus on monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations in Area C of the West Bank, specifically communities in Jordan Valley, Tubas and the South Hebron Hills. Through a joint collaboration between EAPPI and Bimkom. Bimkom will focus on violations regarding planning of the targeted communities. The documentations and reporting of these violations will be used to provide protection tools and mechanisms to these vulnerable communities and increase their resilience. The joint cooperation between the protection actors and DCA/NCA will contribute to a comprehensive and effective response in the targeted areas to improve their protection and thus their resilience. The response will ensure that residents and their supporters have full knowledge of the exact legal status of their buildings, understand the implications of the situation, and are presented with possible avenues of action to prevent demolition and displacement.

Needs

Palestinian communities in Area C are denied access to proper planning which would provide them with permits to build legally, infrastructure and services. Planning is completely controlled by the Israeli Civil Administration (ICA), and is often used as a tool to limit Palestinian development, coerce or force displacement, and to take control of the land for Israeli interests. Palestinian residents have no representation in the planning bodies and little recourse to stop harmful plans. Among the most vulnerable of the communities are those of the Mobile Pastoralists (MPs, these are herders/ Bedouin) who live in small villages/hamlets and rely on herding and seasonal movement to maintain their livelihood and lifestyle. Their villages are not recognized and therefore are unplanned, with little or no infrastructure (running water, sewerage, electricity, paved roads etc). With no possibility to build or develop legally, almost all structures are threatened with demolition, with many already served demolition orders and many already demolished. As seen recently in the case of Al-Khan al-Ahmar, the threat of demolition for a whole village, and being forcefully relocated is real. Some 10,000 residents of Area C are at heightened risk of displacement , most of them MP communities.Demolitions and displacement, or even the threat thereof, cause hardship and HR/IHL violations as well as a severe psychological burden for all members of the affected communities. Bimkom research has shown that coerced/forced transfer has harmed the women’s lives in two ways: they are traditionally responsible for the management of flocks, so their contribution to their families and their status has been adversely affected when this role is lost due to displacement to areas that do not support herding. Additionally, changes in construction and building patterns in the new villages, which are characterized by high density, are not consistent with their social norms of privacy and the protection of women from exposure to men. Women, as the main keepers of the home, and carers for children, are also dis-proportionally affected by home demolitions which can rob them of their sphere of influence.

Activities

Bimkom's actions in this project will both monitor and document violations and also provide legal/planning aid to 11 of the targeted communities that are most vulnerable to forcible transfer and displacement. The communities have been chosen on the basis of immediacy of threats: the number of demolition orders which are not covered by protective actions, as a percentage of all village construction , as well their willingness to work with Bimkom etc. BIMKOM will add to their database information on these threatened targeted communities , which includes for each village: data on demolition orders; actual demolitions; status of the land (whether it is considered privately owned, or State land, in a firing zone or archaeological site; near to settlements, military installations or other Israeli interests etc.); status of basic infrastructure and planning; brief history; community needs; along with maps and photographs. Data will be collated from official sources, stakeholders and the beneficiaries (both women and men). Community engagement will be carried out through consultations with residents in each village to learn about threats and needs; BIMKOM will further bring conclusions and analysis of gathered data back to the communities for their feedback before finalizing our documents for each. Through this process, the beneficiaries will gain awareness of the exact status of the threats, as well as possible avenues for actions. Bimkom's analysis of the data looks at the village as a whole rather than each demolition order alone. This analysis will provide directions for possible protection interventions. For each community, BIMKOM will prepare a paper summarizing conclusions and recommendations; and an additional document of lessons learned and overall recommendations relating to all communities.This data will be made available to a variety of stakeholders:• The beneficiaries, the lawyers, local and international NGOs, the protection cluster and others supporting the communities. This is a remedial and responsive intervention which provides stakeholders with information that can be used in village-wide advocacy and protective actions to improve the resilience of the communities and raise awareness of access to legal/planning assistance or at least freeze demolitions and transfer threats. • Local duty bearers and decision makers, and international policy shapers (particularly foreign diplomats). This is an environment-building activity that will encourage Israeli authorities to respect their obligations, and foreign actors to place pressure for such respect.In addition, when and if necessary, Bimkom will carry out responsive actions to provide legal/planning assistance to communities facing imminent threats. This is likely to continue beyond the time-frame of the project.
