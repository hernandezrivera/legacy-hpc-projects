Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the living conditions and wellbeing of vulnerable displaced and host communities who are affected by conflict and other natural disasters including drought and floods in Bay (Baidoa) Bakool (Hudur) and Lower Juba (Kismayu) of Somalia. This will be through emergency WASH interventions to benefit 85,000 Individuals (20,400 men, 28,900 female, 17,000 boys, 18,700 girls). This will contribute to the HRP 2022 strategic objectives by delivering life-saving WASH assistance to reduce the acute need among the vulnerable IDPs and communities; reducing protection risks against women, children and vulnerable groups accessing WASH services; and providing and restoring access to safe water and adequate sanitation services in the targeted communities. Prioritized activities include: 1) emergency water trucking for vulnerable communities to access water, 2) enhancing sanitation services through the construction of deslugible gender-segregated and disability-friendly communal/ shared latrines in congested IDP camps and host communities, 3)Delivering basic sanitation by enhancing COVID-19 response, construction of handwashing stations and pipeline water networks, establishing solar lamps for increased safety when accessing WASH services, hygiene promotion and awareness, and enhancing of menstrual health and hygiene for girls by distributing hygiene kits, 4) increasing emergency safe water supply through rehabilitation and/ or construction of shallow wells, boreholes (including solar-powered to lower the cost of groundwater abstraction), rehabilitation/ construction of storage tanks and communal taps, and strengthening the capacity of WASH committees in managing water sources and to ensure water safety and quality. Community hygiene promoters will also receive training to undertake house-to-house hygiene education.
The proposed intervention is based on the background of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) in 2022. An estimated 6.4 million people in Somalia need humanitarian assistance, with 40 percent needing emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 3.5 million will need safe drinking water, 5.1 million sanitation services, and 6.4 million hygiene supplies. 16 percent of these are females, children for 64 percent, and the elderly for 4 percent. This presents an increase of 43 percent from 2021, in which 4.6 million people were estimated to need WASH services across Somalia. Rural areas and hard-to-reach districts have shown a significant increase in the number of people in need compared to last year this is also attributed to increasing vulnerability due to limited access to water and sanitation among rural areas and IDP sites. According to the JMCNA 2021, the population in need of WASH services in 2022 includes approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. Populations in the target locations and especially those in crowded IDP settlements that lack water and sanitation facilities are at high risk of water-related disease outbreaks, Cholera/AWD, and COVID-19. There is an urgent need to support them through increasing access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene. The provision of water will also prevent further displacements and conflict over water resources.
Mercy Corps will ensure community participation in targeting. In general, Mercy Corps will ensure that, as WASH activities are implemented, the contextual issues of different needs of men, women, boys and girls, taking into account age, gender, and diversity (AGD) factors, including social background and disability. To ensure that the aged population and PLDs have good access to water and sanitation services, MC will consider them during the design stage. Moreover, accountability to the affected population will be ensured through the community feedback mechanism and Mercy Corps Policies.
