Project Summary

WVI is committed to enhancing the livelihoods of both host community members and vulnerable Rohingya refugees through an initiative known as Unified Skills Development Initiatives. This comprehensive project intends to reach out to 2500 vulnerable individuals from camp communities and 600 households among the host areas. It focuses on building capacity and empowering young entrepreneurs with diverse skills.
The skills development programs will cover a wide range of areas, including:
Basic Literacy and Numeracy Training: The participants will be facilitated with the basic literacy course by following the Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) programme from the sector to improve their better understanding of the training packages.
E-commerce Management and ICT training: Training individuals in the basics of e-commerce to help them establish and manage online businesses effectively alongside basic ICT training on Microsoft office packages.
Agro-fishery Quality Improvement: Guiding on improving the quality of agro-fishery products, thereby increasing their market value and sustainability.
Nursery Development: Offering support and expertise in establishing nurseries, which can provide income while promoting local agriculture.
Farmers’ Associations: Organizing local farmers into associations that focus on the cultivation of moringa, papaya, and other highly nutritious plant that can enhance food security and income.
Tailoring/sewing/knitting: Teaching skills in sewing and garment-making, which can create employment opportunities for
Auto-rickshaw Maintenance: Providing technical training on the upkeep and repair of auto-rickshaws, a
Solar Panel Maintenance: Training individuals in maintaining solar energy systems, and promoting sustainability while providing essential skills for employment.
Stove Maintenance: Offering education on maintaining and repairing stoves, which is critical for ensuring safe cooking practices within households.
Masonry and Bamboo Carpentry: These traditional skills will be promoted to create job opportunities and empower local artisans.
Mobile Maintenance: Training individuals in fixing and maintaining mobile devices, which have become integral to everyday life and business operations.
Conditional Cash Grants and Input Support: Providing financial assistance and necessary materials to support the initiatives and enhance livelihood opportunities.
To enhance program effectiveness, the project will establish learning centers in refugee camps and nearby host communities for education, skill-building, and resource sharing. Regular monitoring will be implemented through data collection to ensure accountability.
The initiative will educate households on disaster risk reduction (DRR) and climate-smart agriculture, helping communities prepare for environmental challenges. It will also focus on building social cohesion by engaging youth and women in organized groups to facilitate discussions, share resources, and strengthen community ties.
Gender equality and the inclusion of persons with disabilities are key priorities, with protective measures against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) incorporated throughout. Women will comprise 60% of the beneficiaries, ensuring their active participation in development and promoting gender equity. Overall, this initiative aims to improve livelihoods, promote social cohesion, and foster a more inclusive and resilient community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the REVA-7, approximately 95% of Rohingya households in Bangladesh are categorized as moderately to highly vulnerable, remaining entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. The assessment revealed that 70% of Rohingya households experienced poor to borderline food conditions due to ration cuts implemented in March 2023 amidst funding crises. Without continued humanitarian aid, simulations indicate that 83% of these households would fall below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) line. This scenario underscores the extreme reliance on external support for basic sustenance and survival among Rohingya refugees.The skills development situation for Rohingya refugees is critical. The JMSNA-2024 identified that limited access to regular income and livelihood opportunities in the camps results in structural barriers that hinder economic self-sufficiency. The 2024 JRP also emphasizes that addressing skills development and educational initiatives is essential, particularly since over 50% of refugees are under 18 years old and lack adequate opportunities for education and skills-building. Importantly, the average education level remains distressingly low, which perpetuates the cycle of poverty and reduces employability.The REVA-7 highlights that economic vulnerability has visibly increased, with many households resorting to negative coping strategies. Relying heavily on less preferred or less expensive food has become a common practice, indicating urgent food insecurity. Therefore, integrating skills development programs that enhance employability and income-generating opportunities is critical. Notably, programs aimed at vocational training are vital, as they could empower Rohingya women, youth, and persons with disabilities, who are often the most affected by economic marginalization. The JRP pushes for the creation of livelihood opportunities to enhance self-reliance among Rohingya refugees. The plan seeks approximately $852.4 million to address the dual needs of Rohingya refugees and the local host communities, focusing on food, health care, education, and skills development. Any intervention must include collaboration with international and local NGOs to build capacity for sustainable economic activities, addressing the immediate needs and facilitating long-term solutions for integration and capacity building.Ultimately, the evidence from these assessments conveys a clear message: ongoing and sustained support is essential for the future stability and resilience of Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar. Without strategic investments in education, skills development, and livelihood initiatives, the dependency cycle on humanitarian aid will likely perpetuate. Emphasizing a multifaceted approach that includes educational programs tailored to the needs of this population, particularly women and youth, will be critical in building their self-sufficiency and helping them reclaim agency over their lives.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project is designed to significantly enhance environmental protection and strengthen disaster risk reduction (DRR) efforts in targeted areas that are particularly vulnerable to climate change and natural disasters. By implementing a variety of climate-resilient interventions, the project aims to create a safer, healthier environment for both refugee and host communities.One key component of the project involves improving communication facilities to ensure that essential information can be disseminated quickly and effectively. This is crucial in climate-vulnerable and disaster-prone areas, where timely communication can save lives and enhance community preparedness.To foster environmental sustainability, the project will promote climate-resilient homestead gardening techniques. These techniques will not only improve food security but also increase the resilience of local agricultural practices to changing climatic conditions. Furthermore, cash-for-work initiatives, such as waste management and tree plantation programs, will provide employment opportunities while simultaneously contributing to community well-being and environmental health.In addition, the project will establish landslide and early warning systems tailored specifically for both Rohingya and host communities. These systems are vital for developing effective contingency plans that can be activated in the event of a disaster. The project will focus on preparing communities in advance by establishing disaster risk reduction preparedness plans and enhancing disaster response capabilities. Community awareness is essential to this initiative. The project will engage local communities through educational campaigns and outreach programs, fostering collaboration among government agencies, civil society organizations, and development partners to improve disaster preparedness and promote sustainable practices.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

For each rapid response, BRCR follows its Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) as well as reports from ISCG and RRRC or other nexus assessment findings. WV BRCR collaborates with the government and various organizations to facilitate a quick recovery in both host and camp communities. Our strategies align with the Rohingya response initiatives of like-minded development partners, such as the 2022 Humanitarian Implementation Plan (HIP). The Rapid Response strategy prioritizes life-saving needs based on the severity of the disaster and available resources. Field-level implementation focuses on the most urgent needs, guided by insights from RNA and RRRC, ensuring that our responses are manageable. In accordance with the WV BRCR strategy and recent emergency experiences, we have developed a Rapid Response Plan that is periodically reviewed to ensure continuity of operations. An Emergency Rapid Response Team (ERRT) has been established, led by a Senior Manager of PQGE.Each year, WV BRCR implements emergency programs that include the distribution of High Energy Biscuits and Hot Meals. We operate a multipurpose center featuring a mega kitchen with 65 burners, capable of preparing a significant number of hot meals each day. Annually, we coordinate with the World Food Programme (WFP) to provide and distribute hot meals.In the host community, we deliver dry food rations to the most affected populations following disasters each year. Additionally, WV BRCR provides non-food item (NFI) support for emergency responses and undertakes repairs and renovations of certain structures in the camp locations.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project aims to implement various initiatives designed to enhance livelihood and skill development for Rohingya adults. The focus will be on portable skills training in response to the global funding crisis that necessitates a safe repatriation. The project will prioritize market-driven portable skills and will establish market linkages based on these skills.Beneficiaries will also receive basic literacy and numeracy training, alongside adult learning opportunities to further their skill development. Within the camps, the project will emphasize market-driven approaches, including knitting, sewing, tailoring, hairdressing/barbering, beauty services, baking, handicraft production, and so on.Participants will have the opportunity to trade their products in informal markets within the camps, and the project will assist them in becoming home-based entrepreneurs. This initiative will empower beneficiaries to earn a living while enhancing their skill sets.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project aims to implement various initiatives that enhance livelihood and skill development for the people in host communities. The focus will be on regular agricultural activities, along with contemporary market-driven approaches such as information and communication technology (ICT), machine repair, plumbing, waste management, and more. Community members will also be provided with entrepreneurial ideas to improve their income opportunities. Additionally, participants from the host communities will have access to basic literacy courses. Private partnerships will be established in various ways to connect local markets with the participants. This initiative will empower beneficiaries to earn a living while developing their skill sets. All the interventions will be synchronized with the development plans of different governments and relevant stakeholders.
