Project Summary

This LoC-Din project titled “WASH Support to address COVID-19 in informal camps in Maiduguri,” will complement efforts of the government and WAH partners in containing the spread of the Corona Virus disease (COVID-19) among IDPs in informal settings in MMC and Jere LGAs who are already suffering from the impact of prolonged armed conflict in the area. LoC-Din will set up a project implementation team made up of Project Coordinator, Finance Manager, M&E Officer, project team leads and community volunteers who will work as a team to effectively manage the project to deliver on proposed activities and targets. The project will install hand washing points with sanitizers in 14 informal IDP Camps and host communities in MMC and Jere LGAs. This will ensure frequent hand washing practices are observed in project locations. The project will engage communities to frequently raise awareness and communicate risks to affected locations. It will carry our regular inspection of WASH facilities and undertake minor repairs where necessary to improve on sanitation and basic hygiene in project locations. The project will help to disseminate widely key WASH and Health messages transcribed in local languages and produced in printed and audio materials. LoC-Din team will coordinate with the WASH sector, government agencies, education and health partners to ensure the right messages get to affected population and at the right time.
The project will mainstream gender and protection throughout its life cycle. The humanitarian principle will be applied through out to ensure assistance is rendered based on vulnerability and needs, but with the involvement and participation of all relevant stakeholders and community members to give room for sustainability. The project will take care to address all cross-cutting issues of GBV, child protection, marginalisation, physical disabilities, mental health and psycho-social support, sanitation and hygiene, among others. The project will establish physical presence in selected locations to engage frequently with persons of concern, holding informal dialogues, and noting issues, challenges and concerns that may not be revealed by more formal mechanisms.
In this project, Jere and MMC are targeted in Borno State because they are the two LGAs identified as “Very high” risk locations in terms of COVID-19 importation and spread in the State according to the final draft of the HRP COVID19 2020 Addendum. Their “very high” risk ratings are associated with their proximity to the International Airport, their strategic locations serving as hubs of international humanitarian workers from affected countries, hosts to crowded IDPs and their weak health systems.
LoC-Din through this project, is targeting 14 informal IDP camps with at least 5000 IDPs who are women, men, boys, girls and children including the elderly, minority groups and people with physical disabilities.
The proposed activities by LoC-Din will be monitored directly by LoC-Din project office in Maiduguri with technical supports from the WASH sector. LoC-Din's Maiduguri office will provide direct financial management and oversight of the stated activities. Weekly internal reporting will be provided by WASH technical field officers and volunteers and will be technically reviewed by the CCCM Project Manager based in Maiduguri. Over the course of implementation, field visits will be conducted daily in order to monitor the project activities against the target stated within the proposal. Internal reporting, monitoring and evaluation will take note of all constraints or impediments to activities in order to undertake a regular evaluation of project goals and implementing strategies. Project updates will be uploaded on the UN GMS and FTS online platforms from time to time. A final narrative and financial report will be produced at the end of the project in compliance with donor standards and requirements.

1 - Needs

The Nigerian North Eastern region has witnessed over 10 years of armed conflicts caused by Boko Haram insurgentswith devastating effect on the people of the region. Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states are mostly affected. Over 8.2 million people have been displaced with over 7.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance according to the 2019 UN Humanitarian Needs Assessment. Borno State remains the epicenter of this crisis with 80% of affected populations residing in the state. In Borno alone, about 1.3 million people have been displaced out of which 265,782 are in Maiduguri, the State capital. With renewed waves of displacement ongoing, the number of new arrivals and informal camps are on the rise with increased overcrowdings. Women and adolescent girls have increasing Gender Based Violence issues to contend with by the day due to lack of segregated WASH facilities. Most informal camps in these LGAs have very poor sanitation and hygiene practices and near absence or poor health and WASH facilities which have all combined to aggravate health and protection risks faced by IDPs in these areas especially women and children. Since the virus is spread through droplets from infected persons and washing of hands with soaps and alcohol based liquids, steady water supply in project area is very key. This project will take advantage of the sunny weather of the area to install 2 motorized solar boreholes in most vulnerable camps or host communities to enable access to water for frequent hand washing practices. The corona virus disease which appeared in December 2019 in China has spread rapidly around the world and has been found to be highly contagious. At-risk countries like Nigeria without proper medical and WASH facilities are mostly devastated as cases have been on the increase in March and April, with cases and casualties already reported in Borno State. IDPs in informal camps, particularly in Jere and MMC, more than before, require regularly awareness and frequent hand washing and basic hygiene practices. Displaced populations in informal sectors have the weakest health and WASH infrastructures and services, and are most vulnerable due to the crowded and unhealthy physical environment they live in. While the government is taking measures to slow the continued spread of COVID-19 in the country, LoC-Din will join other WASH partners to complement government efforts in informal IDP camps and host communities against the spread of the disease in Jere and MMC, Borno State through WASH supports. Confinement resulting from lockdowns constitutes a significant challenge to women’s economic needs, especially those that rely on the informal sector and humanitarian assistance for their livelihoods. This project will target 500 most vulnerable females (who are breadwinners of their families, caregivers and foster parents of unaccompanied children, survivors of GBV and domestic violence, and those whose sources of livelihoods have been affected as a result of lockdowns and restrictions of movements) for small conditional cash based intervention. This intervention will be assessed monthly at designated vendor points by beneficiaries through voucher for the period of 7 months. The intervention will support basic hygiene and food needs of selected IDPs. Shortage of information and poor awareness raising is a concern which this project seeks to address through the pillars of Community engagement and risks communication for COVID-19 preparedness and prevention. IEC materials like banners, posters, hand bills and fliers will be posted strategically in project locations.

2 - Activities

The project will train and deploy community Volunteers to provide essential COVID-19 related services like basic sanitation and hygiene promotions and practices in all project locations. They will support awareness raisings and risks communication efforts through community engagement in project locations. The project will provide basic protective equipment for community volunteers including other frontline workers. The project will undertake community engagement to scale up risks communication and awareness on COVID-19 prevention. LoC-Din will work closely with WASH, education and health partners to ensure the right messages reach target population at the right time. We will also work closely with Translators Without Borders (TWB) to ensure all messages developed in English are correctly transcribed in major local languages (Hausa and Kanuri). The project will coordinate with government at the state and LGAs levels including land owners and community leaders to discuss access to land for wash facilities like drilling of new boreholes. LoC-Din will work closely with CCCM partners to identify basic water, sanitation and hygiene needs in project locations. Already developed IEC materials in form of posters, banners, handbills and fliers including audio messages will be reproduced and disseminated widely across all project locations. To ensure basic sanitation and COVID-19 recommended hygiene protocols are observed and practice in all project locations, hand washing points and sanitizers will be provided and installed at strategic entry points in all camps to ensure people wash and sanitize their hands regularly to protect against the spread of the disease. Regular inspection of health and WASH facilities will also be carried out in all locations. The project will identify 500 most vulnerable females and support them with small Cash Based intervention. This will be done through the voucher system where beneficiaries will be given voucher to access food stuff and basic hygiene kits monthly at a designated vendor points for the period of 7months covering the project duration. This intervention will alleviate their sufferings and reduce the hostilities that come with engaging with vulnerable IDPs without direct food interventions.
