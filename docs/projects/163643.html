Project Summary

This project seeks to strengthen the protective environment for vulnerable conflict-affected persons in Libya through multi-purpose cash assistance. Without access to hard currency due to the liquidity crisis, a decline in purchasing power due to inflation, and low or no income available to meet their basic needs, vulnerable households suffer the most from the effects of the protracted crisis, increasing their reliance on negative coping mechanisms and exposure to protection threats. Therefore, DRC will provide multi-purpose, unrestricted, and unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to the most vulnerable households to meet their basic needs empowering household choice on the response most suitable for them, while respecting their dignity and reducing their reliance on negative coping strategies such assistance will be delivered in areas where markets are functioning, accessible, and safe delivery of cash will be possible. The eligibility for the multi-purpose cash assistance will be determined by the vulnerability of the households based on the vulnerability score card endorsed by the Cash and Market Working Group and Protection sector in December 2019.

1 - Needs

Libya has been adversely affected by conflict and protracted political crisis since 2011. According to the2020 HNO, Out of 6.7 million people in Libya, 1.8 (26%) million people have been identified as people affected. About 50% of the affected population around 897,000 are identified as people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 342,000 are identified as acutely in need of humanitarian assistance including 102,600 children. The need for humanitarian assistance is due to instability, conflict and insecurity, the absence of rule of law, disrespect of IHL by armed actors, a deteriorating public sector and a dysfunctional economy. The HNO 2020 identified that the ongoing conflict and economic crisis has impacted people's ability to secure their wellbeing and livelihoods, resulting in the most vulnerable increasingly relying on negative coping mechanisms to cover basic needs. The shortage in cash is considered as one of the key factors which prevent access to basic goods and services, including protection. Moreover, without access to cash, a decline in purchasing power due to inflation, and low or no income available to meet their basic needs, vulnerable households suffer the most from the effects of the protracted crisis, increasing their reliance on negative coping mechanisms which increases their vulnerability. According to the 2019 MSNA, 77% of surveyed households adopted stress strategies (spent savings, purchased food on credit, selling non-productive household assets and reducing expenditures on essential non-food items), while 74% adopted crisis strategies (took an additional job, borrowed money, sold productive household assets or means of transport and reduced expenses on health and medicine). Further, DRC's 2019 protection monitoring data among IDPs showed that the most common copingstrategies adopted were spending savings to meet basic needs, borrowing money and delaying rent, allof which are categorized as signatures of "crisis" levels of coping by that same index.The new wave of violence that erupted in April 2019, resulted in new displacements of more than 128,000people, an unconfirmed number of civilian casualties, damage to infrastructure while thousands remaintrapped in the conflict-affected areas. The majority of the newly displaced people are staying with family orin private accommodation, while thousands are sheltered in at least 25 collective centers set up by localauthorities. This conflict further exacerbates the vulnerability of populations already exposed to significant risks especially in Western Libya, which has the highest concentration of people in need following the ongoing conflict. For example, IDPs displaced since 2011, may now be facing secondary or tertiary displacement, while existing IDPs are forced to return prematurely to their regions of origin (such as the remote mountain areas of Al Jabal al Gharbi) where basic services, homes and infrastructure have not yet been restored. These mountain communities such as Alawiniya have experienced a spike in spontaneous returns (above 300 households to the town of Alawiniya alone), with families arriving from Tripoli to live in their burnt-out homes. DRC protection monitors that these families are living in shelter conditions lacking safety and dignity due to the lack of alternatives. Assessments and references: - HNO 2020 executive summary- Protection Sector Joint Rapid Assessment in Tripoli in May 2019- DRC Protection Monitoring Report Overview June – Dec 2019- DRC Protection Needs Assessment Azzawiya, Sabrata, Yefren and Zintan July – Sept 2018- DRC Rapid Protection Assessment in West Libya May – June 2019- DRC Protection Monitoring Dashboard March – April 2019- DRC Derna IDP Protection Monitoring Report 2019- Libya Multi-Sector Needs Assessment 2019

2 - Activities

Identification of the 187 most vulnerable households will be through the formal referral pathways established by the authorities for Libyans in the West to obtain information on the most vulnerable households, however, this could be substituted or complemented with information obtained by DRC field teams or self-referrals. DRC will conduct interviews with the households either by phone or home visits, the interview questionnaire integrates the socio-economic criteria endorsed by the CMWG and the protection sector in December 2019. The selection to receive MPCA will be determined based on the CMWG vulnerability scorecard. Following the selection, DRC will conduct a verification exercise on a sample group to determine the accuracy of the eligibility of the pool of the selected households and thereafter provide multi-purpose cash assistance in three transfers to the most vulnerable households. The cash transfers will be performed in a timely and orderly manner, following strict control mechanisms and full capacity to track the use of funds. The transfer value for each household will be aligned with the value endorsed by the CMWG in Dec 2019. Payment instructions to the FSP will be initiated following the validation of the final list of selected recipients. Upon final selection, each household will be assigned a specific DRC code which will be the only data shared with the FSP. The distribution will be organized in a safe and secure manner. The FSP online portal allows a daily tracking of all spending by beneficiaries. PDM will be conducted four weeks after the distribution. DRC will continue to coordinate as a member of the CMWG with other actors to avoid duplications. DRC will use an FSP with a positive experience in cash transfers. The FSP tendering process was launched in December 2019.Should the situation deteriorate, DRC will adapt/ expand their programming to one-off blanket distribution of MPCA to newly displaced.

5 - Mitigation measures

1. DRC has established security management plans and standard operating procedures including the contingency plan for hibernation/evacuation. In the event of a major incident, the Incident Management Team will be activated to address urgent situations. Security assessments are regularly undertaken in program locations to manage risk and mitigate potential threats. In case of direct threats to staff and beneficiaries, activities would immediately be suspended.2. DRC will carry out stakeholder analysis and develop an engagement plan to clarify the extent of its activities and seek permission before operating. Working in a collaborative manner with others could potentially limit risk and exposure directly to the agencies to not pose unnecessary risks to operations.3. DRC Libya adheres to the DRC North Africa data protection protocol that was developed to ensure the effective protection of confidential and sensitive data and information, in adherence to humanitarian and protection principles and to applicable legal standards. Additionally, each household recipient of the cash assistance is linked with a unique DRC code which is used as an identifier and only this code is shared with the financial service provider. 4. The current financial service provider does not request ID from beneficiaries to access their services. DRC ensures from a protection lense that during the distribution all beneficiaries receive adequate knowledge and information on how to use the delivery mechanism (i.e. prepaid card) and a simplified user manual is available and is handed out to each beneficiary. 5. DRC has Complaints and feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries with every beneficiary receiving a Complaint card containing the dedicated phone number and email for this purpose and are also informed how and when to use it. Deactivation of the delivery mechanism could be conducted in a swift and timely manner by the DRC cash team.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

DRC has accountability mechanisms to ensure beneficiaries are able to provide feedback (both positive and negative) on the implementation of the action. DRC will adhere to CHS Standards and has already in place a specific Complaints/Feedback Response system. The information collected will be used to adapt appropriate approaches and to improve the level of safe programming. Through comprehensive feedback loops in the program design, DRC will ensure adaptive programming, which is responsive to the dynamic context and complex needs of IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable groups. DRC's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) unit carries out a series of monitoring activities during project implementation. The team conducts regular assessments and interviews, as appropriate, to ascertain a detailed understanding of the impact of the interventions, including identifying areas of improvement during the project period and future interventions. The proposed activities are based on the needs identified by the communities during multiple assessments(Joint Protection Sector Assessment, and DRC's 2018 Protection Assessment), and protection monitoringin the target locations (see assessments above).DRC requires informed consent from beneficiaries at multiple stages of the intervention, includingprotection monitoring interview, inclusion in the cash activities, referral, and any post-distribution monitoring exercises. This empowers the beneficiary to make informed choices on whether to opt-out or to continue.DRC’s cash assistance activities solicit community feedback on safety, accessibility, access to information,transfer modality and amount, which DRC uses to inform programming. According to the July 2019 PDM survey conducted among cash beneficiaries, 98% of respondents felt very comfortable using prepaid cards, showing the method of transfer was simple, accessible and quickly adopted; while, 98% stated feeling safe in the way the cash assistance was handed to them. This positive feedback was against a background of the modality being relatively new as during the May 2019 exit survey, 84% of respondents said they had never used pre-paid cards before. DRC maintains a beneficiary feedback mechanism via phone and email through which beneficiaries report their concerns or needs. The monitoring and evaluation (MEAL) unit receive and records all incoming feedback, which is analyzed, and appropriate action taken including project redesign.DRC's feedback mechanism will connect to the Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) that the Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) will implement in 2020
