Project Summary

1) Support water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. 2) Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people. 3) Improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria. CARE’s proposed intervention will target Hasakah and Deir-ez-Zor (DEZ) governorate in Northeast Syria.
CARE response will focus on immediate emergency life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems (water supply and sewer networks, wastewater treatment, solid waste management) and WASH in institutions, such as schools and healthcare facilities, ensuring coordinated and cohesive response.

4 - Needs

CARE MSNA Nov-Jan 22 DZ: Water: Primary source of water (93%) in assessed communities reported as water trucking from private vendors. Same for IDPs in settlements surveyed. Access to safe water an ongoing issue in DZ with 95% of respondents reporting issues with safe water access. 74% of HHs reported insufficient water to cover drinking needs more than twice in past 7 days. Most common coping strategy reported as drinking from unsafe water sources. 89% of HHs reported having insufficient water to cover HHs needs in week prior. 58% of IDPs surveyed report insufficient water for HH needs. Main coping mechanisms include reduced bathing & washing clothes (43%,47% resp.) For PLWD distances to water points are an observable issue as mobility equip difficult to access.Latrines: 56% HHs had latrines inside dwelling that were functioning within past month. 10% forced to openly defecate & use communal latrines. Only 15% of IDPs had private latrines. None with handwashing stations beside their latrine. 100% believed the latrines were unsafe for females. WM: Majority of environmental issues in communities were increase of vectors alongside rubbish accumulation. Results supported by information from informal IDP settlements. IDPs lack sewage and waste water disposal options. > 70% disposing of waste water outside their tent or on street. Hygiene Promotion: 56% of respondents received info on hygiene in past 3 months. Higher than average report likely due to COVID info campaign.

5 - Implementation of activities

In coordination with the resp. stokeholds CARE will identify population in need of WASH support at community & HH level. CARE will target 60,000 individuals in Y1 and 50,000 in Y2. CARE will establish community committees at small scale water sources to increase community ownership. CARE will conduct technical assessment for WASH infrastructure (i.e. functionality and maintenance needs). Staff trained on community mobilization/sensitization, CRM, DNH & implementation approaches. At field level, local council and comin orientated on implementation plans, modalities, registration & CRM. CARE built on the learnings from previous or existing programming and selected modalities through consultations with the target groups, whilst also considering their feasibility, appropriateness, acceptance and contributions towards the overall project objective. Activities include: • Emergency water trucking• Full and light rehabilitations of water stations• Rehabilitation and extension of water networks• Providing consumables for water stations• Rehabilitation an upgrading of sewer systems• Construction, rehabilitation and upgrading of sanitation facilities• Solarization of water sources• Rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities in healthcare Facilities• Hygiene promotion activities and distribution of IEC materials• Distribution of soap, hygiene kits and covid-19 hygiene kits• Installation of handwashing stations

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Most pertinent Protection risks (as outlined on PRA) associated with 1. emergency water - quality, quantity, waiting times, accessorily & GBV. 2. emergency sanitation - accessibility & GBV. 3. SWM - unreliability of services, UXO/ERW & risk associated with garbage disposal. 4 Support to water supplies systems - equity, power dynamics, UXO/ERW & construction risks. Potential protection risks related to the implementation of the WASH related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring and those risks have been identified. CARE will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women, adolescent girls, children, PWD, female-headed households, to greater protection risks, particularly GBV. WASH staff trained on key GBV concepts & referral pathways provided by Protection team. Women, adolescent girls and PWD will be consulted during project cycle to facilitate feedback. High level of explosive hazard contamination close to water and sanitation infrastructure, or contamination of accumulated garbage/rubbles, which potentially put humanitarian staff in danger during WASH assessments, rehabilitation works, and operation/maintenance will be assessed. Where relevant, WASH staff will be trained on explosive hazard risk mitigation, while risk education sessions & awareness-raising materials for beneficiaries will be integrated as where possible into WASH programming.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals.Mitigation 1: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with chemicals wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors. Risk 2: Unequal distribution of services, inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Prevention/ Mitigation 2: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care. Risk 3: Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distributionPrevention/ Mitigation: 3 Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of water collection points remains at minimum distance as outlined in country policy document as to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with extra work load in particular;
