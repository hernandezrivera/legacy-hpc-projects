Project Summary

IOM will implement a range of a CwC activities under its Site Management unit to ensure programming is accountable to the affected populations of the Cox Bazar's Rohingya refugee response. Programming includes the operation of over 30 Feedback and Information Centers (FIC) across 18 camps of IOM Area of Responsibility (AoR), and regular community engagement and awareness sessions. A dedicated team of CwC technical specialists will produce consultations to inform the strategic planning of the response and develop the capacity of concerned partners. Gender, DRR and AAP principles are mainstreamed throughout all activities and overseen by a dedicated CwC technical team. Data is sex & age disaggregated & SADD populations receive tailored programs. IOM CwC also routinely participates in regular DRR information campaigns, early warning, and response efforts. The affected population is included and consulted at all stages of program design and development.
FICs (Help Desk (non-permanent/mobile) operation & Help Desk (permanent/static) operation) – IOM operates, along with partners, FICs and routinely receives feedback in all of the camps under its AoR. IOM also collects feedback through door-to-door outreach activities. IOM helped the sector develop standards for Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) operations that will be implemented in all IOM service points in 2021.

Communication and Engagement (Awareness raising/sensitization (block/sub-block/general), Community Mobilizers with Community level committees (existing/pre-formed), & Conduct Video Screening session) – IOM holds routine meetings with different camp-level committees and leaders, including imams, women, girls, boys and men. These meetings serve to disseminate and share key information related to camp operations, waste management, health, WASH and emergency preparedness for monsoons and cyclones. During these meetings, feedback is also gathered from the community and streamlined into IOM’s new CFM system for beneficiary referral to relevant partners and services.
Communication Resource (Audio/Podcast/narrowcast development/dissemination) – Under IOM's Transition and Recovery Division (TRD), this project seeks to incorporate conflict-sensitive media engagement trainings with the aim of fostering social cohesion and peaceful coexistence between Rohingya refugees and host community populations. This will be done by strengthening media capacity in regard to documenting and framing different aspects of the crisis in a conflict-sensitive manner. The project will also contribute towards the production of media content for both refugee & host community populations, including information & multimedia campaigns, photography and art. This aspect of the project will focus on imparting conflict-sensitive writing practices through workshops and cross-community field visits.
Assessment, Research and Studies – IOM retains a dedicated team of 26 Rohingya volunteers trained in qualitative and quantitative data collection from within the response. IOM will continue to strategically partner with organizations & researchers to produce assessments & reports that highlight the various needs of the community and improve upon a deeper understanding of Rohingya people & culture.

Capacity Development (Capacity development Training for agencies/sectors/WGs(Volunteer) & Capacity development Training for community members) – IOM will continue to offer capacity development of both its own staff & that of partners with particular attention being paid to staff engaged in CFM systems & operations. A complete retraining of all IOM & partner staff engaged in CFM works is planned for 2021. Other trainings include comprehensive training for Rohingya volunteers to improve their facilitation & data collection skills. IOM will also conduct a comprehensive training of journalists within Cox's Bazar to promote ethical and objective reporting practices, which will lead to greater social cohesion.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya populations living in the camps of Cox’s Bazar have a range of information needs related to both their current situation and future durable solutions. IOM’s CwC team has continued to help reveal systemic shortfalls of the response to help the humanitarian community effectively engage beneficiaries and build their trust, as documented in the COVID-19 Explained series. Many of these issues have been exacerbated by the pandemic and there is widespread acknowledgement within the Sector to adapt community engagement practices and approaches to better ensure accountability to affected populations across the response. There is also a continued lack of understanding and focus on achieving specific learning outcomes of outreach and awareness raising activities, with many Rohingya in the camps being unable to fully comprehend all key messages related to disaster preparedness, health, and WASH-related messaging. Rohingya are in need of better engagement strategies to accurately ensure that important lifesaving messages are conveyed in detail and comprehended by affected persons. There are a range of challenges related to the achievement of these objectives, from coordination between actors, failure to test messaging prior to dissemination with different sex and age groups within the Rohingya population, and weak facilitation skills of outreach volunteers and staff. IOM’s internal assessment highlights the continued need for tailored engagement and CwC interventions based on participatory approaches to ensure beneficiary inclusion in all aspects of the delivery of humanitarian relief. There is also a lack of detailed social research on cultural communication practices, community structures, and religious traditions to inform the wider response and ensure and inform consultations and community engagement programs. Consultations with different Rohingya stakeholders is greatly hampered by a lack of representational structures, and consultations need to be conducted with respect to the unique needs and perspectives of refugees based on their gender, age, ability, and place of origin. The humanitarian response for the Rohingya involves a range of different actors working across multiple sectors in order to provide a variety of services; many of whom routinely collect community feedback and conduct consultations. However, agencies and sectors have yet to harmonize these approaches. As a result, referral of community feedback and response is not systematized and responses to the community are weaker as a result. Despite strong commitments to accountability to affected populations within the Grand Bargain, there are no harmonized or established operational principals surrounding the handling and response to community feedback between agencies.
