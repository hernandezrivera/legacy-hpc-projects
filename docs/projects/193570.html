Project Summary

As per conclusions by the MSNA in the West Bank, at the level of MHPSS, 11.9% of households surveyed reported that at least one household member showed signs of psychosocial distress or trauma; 28.9% reported that at least one child under 18 showed MHPSS disorders and 87.8% reported that at least one adult household member showed signs of MHPSS distress. Besides, at the level of protection against sexual exploitation, 3.9% of households (168 households) reported women being at risk of sexual harassment or sexual violence; 6.1% of households (233 households) reported girls being at risk of sexual harassment or sexual violence and 3.9% of households (150 households) reported boys being at risk of sexual harassment or sexual violence. Finally, at the level of employment and living conditions, the MSNA concluded that 22.1% of households surveyed in Area C and 11.8% in H2 suffer from unemployment, in addition to 23.7% reported obstacles for female members to find a job.
Against those findings, PSCCW and Qader for Community Development ( Qader) will contribute towards the mitigation of the MHPSS risks through delivering interventions for attaining improved psychosocial wellbeing and resilience, and enhanced awareness through group and individual psychosocial counseling and awareness sessions. They will also address the risks of sexual exploitation through interventions aimed at Mitigated exposure to risks through awareness campaigns, and handling SGBV cases through legal consultations, legal representation, referral and psychosocial counseling. In addition, partners will address the unemployment issue through attaining improved livelihoods and employment opportunities through economic empowerment interventions.
The targeted locations are: Bethlehem (Tqou', Alwalaja, Irtas, al-Rashaydeh and Battir); Hebron (Masafer Yatta, Beit Ummar and Arroub); Jordan Valley (Karbala, Bardala and Ein al-Beida); Qalqilia (Habla and North Azzun) and Salfit (Qarawet Bani Hassan and Hares).
In those locations, the project will target Women heading households, male and female youth (18-25 YO), Male and female children (6- 18 YO), male and female adults, noting that 14% of the targeted beneficiaries are PwDs.

HNO indicated that :-14% of all households reported having at least one household member with physical and or cognitive difficulty , 33% cannot access at least one basic services.- As a result of the increasing high poverty food insecurity and unemployment rates , women and girls in particular are turning to negative coping mechanisms in response to increasing GBV prevalence and severity (HNO-2022)- 28% of households report lack of awareness and access to MHPSS in case of GBV ( HNO-2022) , Children in West Bank threat of violence , threats of settlers have negative impact on the mental wellbeing of children and can contribute to negative coping mechanisms such as “ school drop out , child labor . 25% of the households with school-aged children interviewed as part of MSNA reported that psychosocial support for children was not available at schools . 2 - Activities

In order to address the defined needs, the following activities will be carried out: 1- Promoted MHPSS: It is estimated that adult females benefiting from this component will be 44%, versus 8% adult men, 30% child females and 20% child males. Of those, 14% will be PwDs.This component includes:a- Group Counseling for male and female Children survivors of political violence: a total of 450 children (270 girls and 180 boys, including 63 CwDs) will be engaged in a total of 30 groups, each group engaging 15 participants and receiving 6 sessions. b- Individual Counseling for Children survivors of political violence: a total of 30 children (15 girls and 15 boys, including 4 CwDs) will engage in individual counseling; each receiving 12 sessions. c- Group Counseling for GBV Survivors: a total of 120 male and female children and adults survivors of GBV (36 women, 6 men, 60 girls and 24 boys, including 17PwDs) will be engaged in 15 groups, each engaging 15 participants and delivered over 4 sessions. d- Individual Counseling for GBV Survivors: a total of 30 GBV survivors (12 women, 2 men, 10 girls and 6 boys, including 4 PwDs), will engage in individual counseling; each receiving 12 sessions. e- Referral: A total of 75 male and female children and adults (15 women, 30 boys and 30 girls, including 11 PwDs) will be referred through PSCCW referral mechanism which is aligned with the “Minimum standards for children protection in humanitarian action ) mainly Principle 2: Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance which focus on reaching children who are most in need of protection , this will include consultation with children and their families and communities .The referral mechanisms of children who are in need for multiservice done through :- To fill the child protection rapid assessment form-this fill by the therapist with the child or the care givers.- To fill the Best Interest /comprehensive assessment form this form cover all of the details about the child.- To fill the child protection plan.- Closure and termination – the final stage of women with referred children f- Caregivers' Group Psychosocial Support and Awareness: A total of 300 caregivers of the targeted beneficiaries (270 women and 30 men) will engage in 30 groups; each engaging 10 and delivered over 4 sessions. g- MHPSS/Child Protection Community Awareness Groups: in order to promote community awareness on issues relevant to child protection, a total of 300 community members (240 females and 60 males, including 42 PwDs) will get engaged in a total of 15 groups, each engaging 20 participants and delivered over 4 sessions. 2- Promoted living conditions: It is estimated that adult females benefiting from this component will be 77%, versus 23% adult men. Of those, 14 % will be PwDs. This component will include vocational empowerment and engagement in income-generating activities: a- Vocational training for male and female youth survivors of political violence: a total of 15 youth (+18) (8 females, and 7 males, including 2 PwDs) will receive vocational training to promote their preparedness to engage in employment opportunities. b- Engagement in income-generating activities: the same previous beneficiaries will either receive self-employment projects or get cash for work. c- Vocational training for women survivors of GBV: 15 women, including 2 with disabilities will receive vocational training to promote their employability options. d- Engagement in an income-generating activity: the same previous beneficiaries will either receive self-employment projects of cash for work to promote their economic independence and hence stop violence against them. 3- Legal Aid: It is estimated that adult females benefiting from this component will be 83%, versus 17% adult men. Of those, 14% will be PwDs. This entire component is delivered by PSCCW.This intervention targets women and girls survivors of GBV in order to receive: a- legal consultation: PSCCW, through its lawyers, will offer 45 women and girls survivors of GBV (6 are WwDs) with legal consultation. b- Legal representation: it is estimated that 8 of them (1 are WwDs) will receive legal representation before courts. c- Legal community awareness: a total of 300 community members (240 females and 60 males, 42 will be WwDs)) will be divided into 15 awareness groups, each engaging 20 and delivered over 4 sessions to promote their awareness on legal themes to help end GBV and violence against women and girls in their communities.

3 - Indicators

Qualitive and quantitative indicators are designed to measure progress against outputs: In order to measure to which extent MHPSS was promoted, the indicator will be: By the end of the project, 75% of beneficiaries engaged in structured psychosocial counseling showed less trauma symptoms including stress and anxiety (pre-post intervention scales). In order to measure the extent to which awareness was promoted; a pre/post awareness survey will be used to measure progress towards the achievement of the following indicator: By the end of the project, 80% of participants engaged in awareness sessions show promoted levels of awareness of protection themes. In order to measure the extent to which economic empowerment was achieved; the following quantitative indicator is used: By the end of the project, 60 males and females survivors of violence including GBV are engaged in income-generating activities and promoted their employability options. In order to measure the extent to which women survivors of GBV have had access to legal services, the following quantitative indicator will be used: By the end of the project, 53 women survivors of GBV had access to legal aid including consultations and representation. In order to measure the extent to which service providers were empowered to enable offer quality services, the following qualitative indicator will be used: By the end of the project, 80% of practitioners engaged in capacity building report improved knowledge and skills and are willing to put their new gains into practice. For this indicate a pre/post scale will be used. Standard Indicators include: For SO1: - No. of people in need receiving protection prevention and mitigation services (child protection, MHPSS, legal aid). - No. of children and caregivers affected by conflict related violence and violence of households benefiting from child protection interventions (level 2). For SO2: - No. of most vulnerable people in oPt accessing quality lifesaving health interventions by type of services including primary healthcare, nutrition, child health, sexual and reproductive health, including SGBV, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), healthcare for the elderly and rehabilitation services for people with disabilities (sex, age and disability disaggregated indicator). M&E:During the preparation of the project, the guidance for Gender Mainstreaming in HRP 2023 OPT was taken into account . On the other hand, PSCCW has its own monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and validated means of verification to assess program impact on the wellbeing of participating women , men , girls , and boys , and their satisfaction. PSCCW staff will collect data on a regular basis as services are being delivered and activities implemented, using customized tools for the project, including questionnaires, case studies, focus groups, surveys, success stories, and testimonies. For each indicator in the logical framework, sources and means of verifications are specified, and decisions regarding representative sampling and monitoring tools to be developed during project start-up phase , the selected sample will take into consideration GEM distribution . PSCCW will monitor the progress toward the results of the action, through field visits and quarterly monitoring reports from the our partner Qader, as agreed in our partnership agreement. In addition data will collected according to the data collection timeline of the intervention, and will be delivered for analysis to reflect success , achievements ,difficulties , or negative effects on target beneficiaries . the results of the Analysis will be shared with the donors, CBOs and partners to benefit from learned lessons in improving the project performance.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is mainly targeting acutely vulnerable groups who are alienated and marginalized; women, children and PwDs. Therefore it is fully making aid accessible an originally alienated group suffering impartial access to services, opportunities and rights. Within the same targeted groups, the project is even ensuring equal access to aid for females, especially those with disabilities as this group specifically encounter compound vulnerabilities.
