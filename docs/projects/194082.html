Project Summary


The project will serve both South Sudanese Refugees in White Nile Camps, and Khartoum, as well as Ethiopian Refugees in the Blue Nile. The project will respond to the emerging needs especially as a result of the violent conflict that broke out in April, 2023.
The project aims at providing lifesaving multisectoral assistance to vulnerable refugees in Blue Nile and White Nile Camps. Activities (along with selected modalities) will aim at also promoting resilience and enhance the protection environment in the camp. For SSR, ADRA will target all nine camps in Aljabaleen and Alsalam localities in White Nile State. ADRA will continue responding to WASH, FSL, Protection, GBV and Education needs of the communities and will also add Shelter through different modalities to meet the needs. In Khartoum, ADRA will primarily focus on MPCT as well as protection monitoring and referrals. The program will also target SSR fleeing Khartoum and entransit.
FSL activities will primarily focus on new arrivals, and provide emergeny half-rations to those not receiving assistance through WFP. In Blue Nile, ADRA will work with it's local partner CAFA and SUDO in providing refugees in Wad Almahi locality with integrated WASH and Protection assistance. The project will target both in camp and out of camp populations, with programing that emphasizes the " Do No Harm" approach through involvement of host community.

1 - Needs

According to UNHCR there are an estimated 1.15 Refugees hosted in Sudan, out of which 53 % are children, 52 % are adult and 5 % elderly. The largest group of refugees is from South Sudan, with an estimated 814, 000 SSR in the Country, out of which 52 % are females. From January to August 2022, more than 30,000 South Sudanese crossed into Sudan, mainly into White Nile State. Approximately 38, per cent of refugees in Sudan live in camps, while about 50% of the refugees in live in Khartoum and White Nile State. White Nile camps, which were set up almost 8 years back, continue to host some of the largest SSR populations. Currently the camps host over 250,000 SSR according to the latest data by UNHCR. The needs for all populations remains high especially with no clear solution in sight and the difficulty in the accessing income opportunities for many of those refugees. According to an assessment that was conducted by ADRA nearly 90% of households were food insecure and resorted negative copying strategies in order to meet their food needs. According to an MSNA conducted by NRC in the camps, only five per cent of households that were surveyed could meet all of their needs with their current income. Protection issues were also reported, especially when accessing different essential needs, mainly water and firewood. As per the MSNA nearly 90 % of females faced physical harassment when accessing water. Ethiopian Refugees also continued to arrive, but to a lesser extent in Eastern Sudan and Blue Nile State. As of August 2022, almost 60,000 Ethiopian refugees have crossed into Sudan since the start of the crisis in Ethiopia. As the conflict in Ethiopia continues the amount and severity of the needs in the Blue Nile camps increases. According to the needs assessment conducted in 2022, the main needs include WASH, FSL and protection. WASH needs are cross cutting both for Refugees and host communities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The main activities to be implemented are as follows:Education:1- Construction and rehabilitation of classrooms. This will include the planning and the construction of the classrooms. 2- Distribution of school uniforms: ADRA has an established workshop with equipment for sewing uniforms through a cash for work program. The project will serve a duel approach, whereby providing a source of income to refugees within the camp. 3- Provision of classroom furniture and supplies: This includes desks, blackboards, refurbishment of teacher offices. 4- Training of teachers in teaching methods. 5- Awareness on education for communities. FSL:1- Provision of cash for food. ADRA will provide a cash amount to cover the minimum expenditure basket that includes the costs. 2- Training of households on VSLA. 3- Provision of livelihood training. Shelter: 1- Provision of shelter subsidies.2- Provision of cash for shelter. 3- Provision of Emergency Shelter Kit. 4- Provision of NFI kit. 5- Rehabilitation of sites. WASH: 1- Rehabilitation/Construction of water points: ADRA will conduct an overall assessment on the water points in the camps. Water points will be constructed, especially in the new camps. Those currently non-functional will be rehabilitated. Water supplied through the water points will be tested and chlorinated. Locations for the water points will be conducted in consultation with the community to ensure that the water points are located in easy to access area, especially safe for females. Community Management Groups will also be established and trained on water management, and maintenance. 2- Provision of hygiene items: The project will work on providing essential hygiene NFI kits to the most vulnerable households. 3- Dissemination of hygiene messages and Behaviour Change Communication training. Protection: 1- Development of protection advocacy plan. 2- Protection monitoring. 3- Development of service directory and referral. 4- Provision of Protection counselling and referral. 5- Provision of Individual Cash Assistance. 6- Capacity building to duty bearers and service providers.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The environmental impact of the project will be adequately assessed. Communities will be involved in decision making process and in identifying more environmental friendly solutions in order to promote more ownership towards the environment. For instance when installing water tanks in camps and jerry cans, ADRA will work on providing efficient and sustainable taps to avoid water leakage and replacement of taps. As in the fuel efficient stoves, ADRA will work in promoting more efficient technologies as well as tailored approach that would help in having refugees and communities acceptance those technologies.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is a core dimension that the project addresses by ensuring safe, equitable, and equal access by communities to different services. ADRA will mainstream protection principles across the different interventions. Hygiene and protection messages touch on sensitive topics and provide an opportunity for what are typically gender-segregated groups to discuss sensitive topics of relevance when dealing with activities such as fetching water, which is often disproportionately practiced by women, and which at times take women out of the immediate community, risking exposure to GBV.

7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Protection is not only mainstreamed into all activities but it's also takes a focus in the project. ADRA will ensure that communities are involved across the different stages of the project from development to closing. At the same time, topics of hygiene, education, and protection will be presented in a way to maintain and enhance the dignity as well as benefit the health of the target population. Topics of MGM will be represented in key hygiene messages, as they play an important role in allowing women and girls to participate fully in daily routines without impediment due to lack of supplies, safe spaces, and knowledge of MHM practices and without encountering discrimination that can accompany menstruation.Even though there is very limited available data and information related to conflict issues and trends among IDPs and the host community, a good understanding of the context, including key conflict dynamics and key drivers of conflict, has informed the design of the program strategy and approaches. This understanding has been developed through field presence, work, and interactions with the communities. Experience from ADRA’s current program on community-based protection (funded by the Government of Canada) has provided evidence of main dividers and triggers of tensions/conflict as well as connectors have benefitted the design of the program. During implementation, RAWA aims to “do no harm,” by not exacerbating existing tensions. Conflict sensitivity will be used to ensure a fair distribution of services and inputs, and a meaningful representation and participation of different groups. The project has taken into consideration the power dynamics and potential conflict between men and women, IDPs and host communities as well as internal conflicts within the factions and host communities. Activities and approaches will be tailored to ensure cultural sensitivity and responsiveness to the specific needs of each group, for instance, messages in different indigenous languages and dialects. In addition, RAWA will promote opportunities for jointly implemented activities bringing together members of the two targeted communities. This is expected to enhance mutual understanding and positive interactions among different groups and individuals. RAWA will monitor and evaluate both unintended and intended impacts of the program on the conflict, tensions, as well as power dynamics. Particular attention will be given to monitoring potential backlashes related to tensions over new individuals and groups gaining new authority and independence and others fearing to lose their role and authority.Disaggregation of data on indicators by ethnical/tribal belonging as well as other factors such as age, gender, and disability status will help to ensure that all groups are represented and heard and that specific conflict sensitivity concerns are detected timely. Techniques and methodologies for data collection will also be conflict sensitive.
