Project Summary

Mercy-USA is seeking to ensure the continued operation of these health facilities through sustained staffing, provision of medical supplies, data management, and reporting, finance, governance, training, monitoring, and oversight of program quality, and infrastructure maintenance support.
These facilities will provide obstetric and gynecological services, treatment for diarrhea, respiratory tract infections and skin conditions for boys and girls under five, treatment for chronic conditions including hypertension, asthma, cardiac ailments and psychosocial

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, The number of people who require health assistance in Syria is estimated to be 12.2 million.Vulnerable groups include 3.38 million women of reproductive age, 1.33 million children under 5 years old, and 500 000 people aged over 60 who are vulnerable to no communicable diseases; only 54% of Syria’s PHC and hospitals (HeRAMS Q2 2021) are functional, a lack of adequate funding will result in further collapse of the health system and increased morbidity and mortality rates among vulnerable groups in Syria. Moreover, underfunding will further hinder health partners’ capacity to deliver and maintain essential health services during ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and will undermine public trust and collective action to protect health. In Syria, disability is estimated at 25 percent of the population, 3.05 million. According to the reposts (HeRAMS); The functioning health facilities have been assessed according to whether the health facility exists prior to the crisis or established after the crisis. 79% of health facilities were newly established, while 21% of health facilities existed prior to the crisis in 2011. (Kelly PHC is one of the crucial health facility exists prior to the crisis providing comprehensive primary health care). The project will be for two years 2022-2023 and aims to support (88,328 / 42,064 Female -46,264 Male) out of them (41,100 IDPs 21,880 Male 19,220 Female) - 7533 Pregnant and lactating women.

5 - Implementation of activities

Mercy-USA will be responsible for implementing the program in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders Mercy-USA will provide high-quality essential health services: trauma services, reproductive health, child health and nutrition services, mental health, diagnostic services, and specialized care for persons with disabilities, those with communicable and non-communicable diseases, and children with severe acute malnutrition with complications. MercyUSA ensures effectively and efficiently delivers comprehensive quality health services that are equitable, accessible, and sustainable, ensuring the timely delivery of cost-effective, integrated, and standardized health services tailored to meet the priority health issues faced by most of the population. The quality and accountability will be monitoring by Mercy-USA to ensure meet the standards; furthermore, Mercy-USA will be responsible for all program and relevant financial report to the donor and the cluster ( 4Ws ) and other related reports. 30 doctors and 60 nurses and 10 Midwives will be trained on infection prevention and control protocol IPC for three days. The program will be monitored and managed by the program coordinator, who will work directly from the Turkey office with the field's staff to ensure the program is being correctly implemented. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection /Risk/Threat: Gender-Based Violence GBV Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating Measure: - Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Trained specialized health staff on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Recruit female staff in health facilities will increase access of women and girls to health services. Monitoring: Case Management / Reports Resources Required: Train the specialized health staff Protection /Risk/Threat: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating Measure: -Mercy-USA maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards SEA in accordance with internationally-established protocols and standards for SEA protection. Mercy-USA staff, interns and volunteers are trained in SEA protection, child safeguarding with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA and child protection concern


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumption: No confirmed cases of COVID19•Risk: COVID19 pandemic spread•Likelihood: high •Impact: high•Mitigation: 1- Two tents were set up outside the facility building to conduct triage for all incoming patients. We also provided masks, gloves, and hygiene products and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) equipment for the facility2- Applying IPC protocols inside the facility and referring any suspected cases according to the case definition 3- Disseminating a message to the community through outreach workers to avoid visiting the health facility unless there is an urgent need4- All patients with chronic diseases to have their medications for two months in advance in order to minimize visits to the health facility6- All other needed measures will be applied according to the cluster recommendation Assumption: People have access to health facilities, despite restrictions of movementRisk: Access to areas of intervention are restricted due to the high level of conflict, change of controlling entity Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation: 1- In the event of major macro-level conflict-related threats, Mercy-USA will take appropriate action that is commensurate with the scale of the threat.
