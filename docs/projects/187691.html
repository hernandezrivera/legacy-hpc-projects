Project Summary

The proposed project aims at restoring rural and urban livelihoods for conflict-affected communities across Northern Syria. ACTED will provide a variety of support modalities in targeted communities, including short-term income opportunities through Cash-for-Work schemes; sustainable restoration of basic services and value chain through the repair, installation and rehabilitation of key assets including productive assets, such as irrigation canals, water stations, roads and schools; livelihoods support and market recovery through grants and training to small-to-mid-scale businesses as well as farmers and livestock holders. ACTED will continue its Area-Based Approach in six communities across Northern Syria, providing multi-sectoral assistance and building the capacity of local stakeholders toward community-driven response plans focused on early recovery and resilience-building. ACTED aims to reach a total of 621,151 individuals through this project in 2022. By fostering early recovery across areas of intervention where needs are acute, ACTED will also contribute to limiting pull factors into IDP camps.
With regards to road rehabilitation, ACTED will also conduct rehabilitation works on certain roads connecting IDP camps in Northwest Syria, ensuring safe and continued access to these camps by residents, service providers, and relief organizations. With regards to grant support to businesses, ACTED will deliver small-scale grants ranging from 500 to 1,000 USD to micro-businesses as well as small-scale agri-businesses, while larger, transformational businesses will receive on average 6,210 USD.
For cash-based approaches, ACTED will coordinate through the relevant Cluster / Working Group with other aid providers operating in its areas of implementation, so as to ensure harmonization in the value of the assistance distributed, thereby avoiding creating tensions or undesirable pull factors. ACTED will systematically conduct assessments and consult local stakeholders prior to implementation. ACTED will apply this approach for cash-for-work schemes, ensuring the relevant of proposed activities to local needs and avoiding the disruption of the local labor market, as well as for business support schemes, ensuring that supported businesses are connected to local, functioning markets with an existing demand. In all areas where cash-based modalities are rolled out, ACTED will avoid creating tensions at the community and households levels through community engagement in the form of representative Focus Group Discussions ahead of implementation.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, 2021 was marked by a continued economic downturn, with compounding effects of Covid-19 restrictions, continued conflict, currency collapse and inflation, as well as drought in the Northeast. These factors bore a heavy toll on livelihood opportunities, market systems, and food security. According to WFP’s Market Price Watch Bulletins, between July 2020 and August 2021, the unofficial exchange rate to the US dollar of the SYP increased by 48%; the average SYP cost of the WFP standard food basket doubled; the cost of a subsidized bread batch quadrupled; and the unofficial cost a liter of diesel nearly quintupled. At the same time, livelihood opportunities decreased, which negatively impacted purchasing power, making essential items for households and businesses largely unaffordable. While in IDP camps access to markets remains a major concern, limiting the opportunities for cash, voucher, and market-based assistance, off-camp areas general enjoy favorable access to markets and more limited market disruption, with the core issue being the affordability of goods and the lack of income.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at strengthening the early recovery and enhance resilience of conflict-affected populations across Northern Syria through the restoration of livelihoods and of basic services in targeted communities. ACTED and its implementing partners will look to achieve this aim through the provision of immediate income opportunities for highly vulnerable households through Cash-for-Work schemes; the support to subsistence and transformational businesses, as well as small-scale and/or drought-affected farmers and livestock holders through business development grants and vocational training; the provision of agricultural inputs (seeds) to foster food production towards improved self-sufficiency at household level; the rehabilitation of key infrastructure and assets providing crucial services, such as water stations, roads, schools, irrigation stations, bakeries, irrigation ponds, etc. With regards to longer-term resilience-building and recovery, ACTED will continue to roll out its Area-Based Approach across several targeted communities, in which capacity-building of residents, civil society organizations, and technical departments goes hand in hand with the conception and implementation of community-driven recovery plans to respond to needs identified by the communities themselves. Lastly, this project will have significant synergies with other sectors that make up ACTED’s multi-sectoral response in Syria, including most notably Food Security and WASH.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation (unlikely, high impact). Mitigation: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies.Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse (unlikely, high impact). Mitigation: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise complaints and give feedback on ACTED’s work, as well as contractors’, and to relay any arising issue.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the program and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, program and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and KI network in order to prevent tensions, regular dialogue ensured with relevant authorities• Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement and ACTED’s analysis of all offers led by Regional office. Evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and Regional Capital. Preparation of bid analysis, and full documentation is then done by Capital.• HLP risks: ACTED systematically conduct housing land and property assessment and due diligence processes so as to ensure that activities conducted in/on private areas/assets have proper, documented authorizations, particularly for rehabilitation activities.• Aid diversion risks by OAGs: in case of interference of OAGs, ACTED will suspend, re-direct, or stop its activities as needed, and regularly re-assess.• Market distortion and volatility risks (cash-based modalities): ACTED conducts repeated, stringent market assessments, focusing on functionality, accessibility, and safety; as well as engagement with local communities to ensure cash modalities are deployed where local communities are receptive. Based on repeated assessments and in coordination with other aid providers, ACTED will adapt assistance amounts as needed, and will remain ready to revert to in-kind modality where volatile markets become no longer able to sustain a cash-based intervention.
