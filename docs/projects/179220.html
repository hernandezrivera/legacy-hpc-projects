Project Summary

In North West of Syria (NWS), deteriorating economic conditions remain a challenge for vulnerable communities and households in accessing safe and equitable WASH services, notably for those that have to purchase services like water from private water trucking vendors. 74% of the off-camp HHs using water trucking as the main water supply modality spend more than 5% of HH’s income on purchasing water only, while the combined costs for water and sanitation services should not exceed 5% of a household’s income. The overall estimated average expenditures on WASH services and supplies (water, hygiene items, garbage removal and desludging) make up to 15% of HHs’ income in some governorates and could be much higher at the individual HH-level. Depreciation of Syrian currency erodes households’ purchasing power and subsequently household spending capacity on WASH services, supplies and consumables.
Through this project, ATAA will be reaching 140,316 beneficiaries (57%female-42%male)- (56% under18 - 44% above18) in:
1- Idleb, Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin: Maaret Tamsrin, and Kelly camps.
2. Idleb, Harim, Dana camps.
3- Aleppo, Al Bab, Suran.
4- Aleppo, A'zaz, Kafr Kalbein.
ATAA, through implementing this project, aims to secure sustainable and sufficient clean water for IDPs and host communities in the communities and surrounding IDPs camps by establishing a sustainable drinking water system and connect it to the main water stations, and support the operation cost for the water stations, the water system will be connected to the chlorination unit, the outlet of the chlorination unit will connect to the (ground, or high) main water tanks, and ATAA will connect this main water tanks to the main water networks.
ATAA will establish water units in the targeted locations and provide them with O&M training, and work closely with the local councils to establish the tariff system for sustainability of the water system, and MoU will be signed with the local councils.
By operating the water stations, the drinkable water will reach IDPs, and Host in communities and camps, and the operation cost will be decreased by providing PV solar system based on the required operating hours in each location.
The providing of drinkable water through the water network will provide good conditions to operate the sanitation system in the targeted locations, which will make the need to rehabilitation all damaged lines is critical and required, ATAA will rehabilitate the sanitation systems.
In the camps, ATAA will connect the camps to the main and closely sanitation system.
This project will ensure protecting project beneficiaries from the spread of diseases by providing garbage bins and renting garbage trucks to collect garbage daily, as well as improve hygienic behavior by providing Hygiene kits to all families, in addition, conduct hygiene promotion sessions about COVID-19 pandemic and hygiene awareness messages.
It is worth to mention during the implementation of the project, if the targeted locations receive more IDPs, ATAA will target the newly IDPs with all project activities, and the project activities will help to decrease the disturbances that emerged between host and IDPs.
ATAA will conduct FGDs with targeted BNFs and especially with PWDs, as well as Local councils and other stakeholders to inform them about the goal, outcomes, and, activities of the project.
Brochures and banners will be used to share information with the communities.
ATAA will set a community-based mechanism to make sure that community members can submit their complaints and feedback. Feedback tracker will be prepared on a monthly basis.
Specific monitoring activities: rehabilitate water networks and chlorination in the communities and camps, confirming the distribution of H.Ks through onsite monitoring, monitoring report, and confirming beneficiary verification for their access to cleaned and chlorinated water, feedback, and satisfaction.

4 - Needs

According to OCHA syria Situation Report No. 26, 2021, Despite the WASH Cluster’s efforts to phase out costly and short-term activities such as water trucking 70 % of IDPs in camps in northwest Syria continue to rely on WASH partners to provide trucked water on a daily basis. Wash Cluster shared the final document for general state of coordination and conducted a briefing session with partners. Access to safe water through piped systems remains a challenge, with only 125 (65%) out of 209 systems in Idleb and 136 (56%) out of 241 systems in Aleppo currently functioning. Meanwhile, 15 percent of the stations in Aleppo and 16 percent of the stations in Idleb are running without being chlorinated. ATAA between 20-25 Feb/2021 conducted a primary need assessment in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, Kelly and its surrounding camps, Dana camps, Zardana Mashehad, Suran, and Kafr Kalbein. there is a huge need to increase the water supply and manage drinking water safety. There is an increasing need for desludging. As the displacement is ongoing and displaced populations are prioritizing communities to settle in, there is a need to increase the operational support to existing piped water systems. 70% out of whole residents in both communities and camps don’t have access to both sewage and water system using instead sewage pits. Due to the high price of hygiene items and water (3.5SYR/L), whole residents can’t afford to buy their basic needs of water and there is a need for distributing hygiene kits and conducting hygiene promotion. Waste collectors should be recruited, bins should be provided and solid waste trucks should be assigned and ensure there is daily waste transport.

5 - Implementation of activities

ATAA will conduct an infrastructure assessment for the water network and prepare estimated costs and quantities. ATAA will extend the water networks, and support visits to the working on a daily basis in addition to final inspection for the networks and start pumping water through water networks (35L/Person/Day - WASH cluster standard).ATAA will rehabilitate and install 14000 M (110-75mm) polyethylene pipes to rehabilitate the old network and as a new network, and connect it with the existing main pipe (This network will be in the communities and will connect the camps with the communities water network to provide drinkable water daily and to dispense with the purchase of water trucks). ATAA will rehabilitate and install sewage systems network, the process of drilling and filling, and installing 9000M (1.5 length - diameter 0.3-0.4 M) diameter of new and sub-network. ATAA will cover the sewage pits with reinforced concrete and provide an iron top cover to the sewage pits, provide and install asphalt for the top covers. (This network will be in the communities and will connect the camps with the communities sewage network to cancel the septic pits in the camps and prevent the spread of diseases).Reducing operating costs, by providing PV solar systems to the water stations, as well as provide monthly running costs during the first 2 months to cover some of the operation costs. ATAA will conduct hygiene awareness sessions in the targeted locations and distribution 25000 Hygiene kits. Conducting the PSEA/GBV awareness-raising sessions to the beneficiaries. ATAA will work on re-activate the SWM in the targeted location throughout distribution garbage bins (1 bin 100L for 10 HHs) and collecting garbage on a daily basis.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: A member of the ATAA team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between ATAA and the affected community is damaged. ATAA face severe reputational damage and loss of funding. Mitigation: All staff who join ATAA Project are going to a thorough and transparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign ATAA PSEA and CSG Policies ATAA have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to protect all Education/WASH and Protection beneficiaries. ATAA’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what ATAA will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation or violence. All community centers/schools and mobile units’ staff will sign ATAA CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries
