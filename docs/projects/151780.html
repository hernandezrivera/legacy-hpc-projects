Project Summary

After over nine years of on-going conflict in North-East Nigeria, intense fighting between the Nigerian army and Boko Haram has resulted in the widespread contamination of the area by explosive hazards. The presence of those explosive devices is affecting the population and humanitarian actors, restricting the freedom of movement, access to villages, key infrastructures and arable land. With the beginning of movement of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) returning to their areas of origin, it is critical to confirm the level of contamination and target areas of intervention for clearance operations.
The data available on contamination is currently limited and incomplete. The analysis of the contamination and its impact on the different groups of population, including IDPs and returnees, is therefore very difficult. The aassessment of the level of contamination by asking IDPs arriving from conflict affected areas can help get a broad picture of the perceived or estimated level of contamination. . Results from the multi-sectoral needs analysis conducted by the United Nations in North-east Nigeria between June and August 2018, indicated that a majority of IDPs reported the presence of explosive hazards as a barrier to access land for agricultural activities in their areas of origin. Whether this threat is perceived or confirmed, it needs to be verified and, if confirmed, addressed through clearance of explosive hazards in order to allow people to return to their land.
The current capacity to address the contamination is limited. The Nigerian army and police have a few explosive ordnance disposal teams and there is no dedicated national mine action coordination entity.
In order to best address the needs of the affected population and humanitarian workers this project aims to provide civilians and humanitarian operators with safe access to areas of origins, areas of operations, key infrastructures and agricultural land by conducting survey and clearance operations of the conflict affected aread. UNMAS will concentrate on three main tasks:
1) Deploy three multi-tasks teams to conduct survey and clearance activities in conflict affected areas of Borno State as well as Yobe and Adamawa States should the need be confirmed;
2) Establish and maintain an information management system to register, analyse data and coordinate a prioritized response;
3) Support the development of a dedicated national capacity to coordinate mine action operations;
The project will be conducted in close cooperation with the Nigerian army to ensure the security of the operators and in coordination with the mine action and humanitarian operators in order to deploy teams to priority areas and avoid overlapping with other operators.

Needs

North-east Nigeria is contaminated by a wide range of explosive hazards that did not explode or have been abandoned. Although the full scope and scale of contamination is unknown, reports indicate that as much as 20 percent of munitions that were fired did not explode on impact, resulting in high volumes of unexploded ordnance contaminating communities and, together with abandoned ammunition, provide a readily available source of explosives to construct improvised explosive devices (IEDs). IEDs have been used extensively by Boko Haram, severely affecting the population and posing a threat to the humanitarian community since they are as indiscriminating as landmines and will remain active over time, if not cleared. These devices have been laid on roads, in fields, and also in villages. Open source data indicates that there have been 1251 casualties from explosive hazards in 2018 as a result of 211 incidents . It is believed that these figures represent only a portion of the actual number of incidents and victims, given that large areas of Borno State are inaccessible and there is no central data collection system. The lack of access is exacerbating food insecurity and will continue to do so until mine action activities can be undertaken. Many IDPs in Borno State, and Nigerian refugees in Cameroon, indicated their willingness to return to their places of origin when essential conditions are in place. Minimal conditions include inter alia, that they must be protected from explosive hazards. Returnees and humanitarian staff will be increasingly exposed to explosive hazards as people go back to their homes and humanitarian operations increase. Expansion of humanitarian mine action operations is an urgent priority to enable safe returns, freedom of movement for the delivery of humanitarian assistance, and access to key infrastructures such as schools, hospitals and water to conduct early recovery activities. While UNMAS is the global coordinator for the Inter-Agency Coordination Group on Mine Action and can coordinate the mine action response with the UN Country Team and Humanitarian Country Team in North-east Nigeria, there is a need to establish a national mine action body to coordinate the response with all the Federal and State authorities involved in humanitarian response, security, recovery and development activities. The complete clearance of the three States will take time and will require the establishment of a national entity with the technical expertise to develop national mine action standards, prioritization, tasking and oversight of operators; and an effective communication mechanism with the defence and security forces on the ground.

Activities

UNMAS is building up its response, in coordination with the national authorities, the humanitarian, development and mine action operators and in line with existing key strategic documents at country level such as: The Centrality of Protection Strategy; the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria; the Borno State Return Strategy as well as the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus. In its three areas of intervention, UNMAS will conduct the following activities: 1) Survey and clearance operations:UNMAS will recruit, train, equip and, pending access by the Nigerian authorities, deploy and mentor three multi-tasks teams (MTTs). The teams will first assess and clear explosive contamination in secure areas of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States and, as access increases, broaden their areas of intervention. While the contamination is estimated to be more important in Borno state, suspicions of contamination in Yobe and Adamawa need to be verified and managed if confirmed.As part of the Borno State Return Strategy, the multi-task teams will stand available to deploy and assess the newly accessible areas, before the return of the displaced communities. UNMAS will seek to establish gender-balanced multi-task teams will be composed of people from conflict-affected communities. By providing training and employment to those communities, UNMAS would contribute to socio-economic recovery of the local population. 2) Establish and maintain an information management system:The progress of the technical survey and clearance operations will be compiled into UNMAS Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) in order to map the land release process, which is the process of applying all reasonable effort to identify or better define confirmed hazardous areas and remove all suspicion of explosive hazards through non-technical survey, technical survey and clearance using an evidence based and documented approach.This information will then be analyzed and will inform the mine action and humanitarian operators. 3) Support the development of a dedicated national capacity to coordinate mine action operations;UNMAS, as the global mine action area of responsibility lead under the protection cluster, will re-initiate the meetings of the Mine Action Sub-Working Group (MASWG) in North-east Nigeria, with a focus on facilitating and prioritizing safe humanitarian access and return of the population displaced by the conflict. In addition, in order to ensure national ownership and continuity in the mine action response, UNMAS will also support the development of a national civilian mine action coordination entity. UNMAS will provide capacity building and technical advice and support the drafting of National Mine Action Standards (NMAS) to support the management, regulation and coordination of a national response to the contamination of explosive hazards. Given that women, girls, boys and men should have equal access to and participate in mine action programmes as beneficiaries, employees and decision makers, the project will promote an age and gender-sensitive approach to ensure that the activities implemented fully take into consideration the needs of the diverse groups in the areas targeted by the project. This will ensure that humanitarian mine action programming benefits all people affected equally and avoids putting some at greater risk.
