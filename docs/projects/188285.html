Project Summary

Given the fact that the challenging economic situation across Syria compounds humanitarian needs. the growth of cost of living and income stagnates have scaled the financial hardship of the vulnerable groups to seek the essential services, off these the essential health care.
This project aims at providing comprehensive health care services to affected people ( men, women, boys, girls ) from different communities (IDPs, returnees and host community) in Damascus and Rural Damascus. Activities in Damascus will target IDPs and the overburdened community. Damascus is considered as home to thousands of IDPs and functions as key referral sites for secondary care, thereby placing heavy loads
on the health system. Rural Damascus is an area where returnees movement is high and where health facilities are insufficient.
This project aims at supporting the vulnerable people in Damascus and Rural Damascus with essential health care services through 4 static clinics ( in Damascus, Maliha, Kafr batna, and Daraiyya) and 10 mobile teams in Arbin, Qudsaiya, Harasta, Alsashabiyeh, Ghizlaniyeh, Zabdani, Dimas, Ein Elfijeh, Madaya, Alhajar Alaswad) where the severity scale is 3. health services will prioritize children under 5 years of age, women of reproductive age (15 years to 49 years), people living with disability, and persons over 50 years of age with NCDs and/or elderly over 65.
AlTAmayoz is currently providing health care services in Damascus under a partnership with UNHCR, but UNHCR is following an exit strategy during 2022 so AlTAmayoz is planning to seek other fund resources for the rest of 2022 and for the year 2023 and to expand the provision of services since the number of registered families in alTAmayoz exceeded 30,000, 70% of them are IDPs with a high poverty level and suffering from sever harsh living conditions. AlTamayoz also plans to establish 3 new static centers in Rural Damascus in Maliha, Kafr batna, and Daraiyya where it has access and deliver some inkind support to people, and proposed to implement another kind of services including protection, food and education in an attempt to provide complimentary service to people in need, this will be done in coordination with DoH and other actors in those areas.
According to AlTamayoz presence in many locations in Rural Damascus, and due to the high need for health services observed and reported, AlTamayoz is planning to run 10 mobile teams in the above-mentioned areas in Rural Damascus noting that Altamayoz has very good experience in running mobile teams as it provided health care services through this modality to IDPs sites and to returnese during East Ghota response in 2018 under a grant from WHO, and is currently providing RH services in partnership with UNFPA through 2 mobile teams that are integrated with GBV services.
Activities in all locations will include medical consultations in the field of internal medicine, pediatrics, gynecology, provision of medicines, NCD care, Nutrition services, MHPSS, GBV integration, awareness-raising, and will support the provision of assistive medical devices and referrals for secondary health care services (surgical operations and diagnostic procedures) through a well-controlled referral system that has been approved by several UN agencies.
People with disability will be prioritized and will be additionally supported by MHPSS and assistive medical devices
Activities implementation will assure avoiding duplication as there is a strong database used for validation, crosscutting and statistics. ALTamayoz will also coordinate with DoH, SARC, and other actors in Rural Damascus to assure services complementarity and avoid duplication.
Activities will be monitored through periodically FGDs with beneficiaries, Monitoring mechanism will include pre and post assessments of several activities: (awareness-raising, referrals, provision of assistive medical devices..), calculating beneficiaries disaggregated by age and gender, field visits.

4 - Needs

As per HNO draft 2022: The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies1 and chronic challenges2 which affect the availability3 and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths4, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems due to low levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventive public health measures, and emerging variants which, together, strain attempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic.At the same time, increases in poverty across the country which have already resulted in economically-driven displacement may also increase and worsen determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes.12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected16, and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years). Just over 500,000 older people will require inclusive health services, as well as those with early onset non-communicable diseases (NCDs) which are estimated to account for 45 per cent of all mortality in Syria17. Youth and young persons, which comprise 1.98 million of the health sector PiN, are also reporting increasing stress and mental health concerns.
