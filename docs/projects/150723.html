Project Summary

The current Great March of Return and the long-lasting blockade imposed on Palestinians in the Gaza Strip are hitting the already bleeding wound of Gaza. This horrible situation adversely impacted all aspects of life including education. This proposed project aims to reduce vulnerability among Gaza’s Students by improvement of primary education through strengthening access to education services and to upgrade the capacity of the target area school counsellors in the field of PSS. The project will intervene at the most vulnerable, underprivileged, borderline and poor areas (northern area of North governorate and eastern area of Gaza governorate). The benefited children will be the most vulnerable, primary school children. Moreover, the project will help students’ families by providing school uniform, school bags and stationaries. Providing PSS sessions for the children and refer them to specialized program. On the other hand, the project will upgrade the school counsellors in the field of psychosocial support.

Needs

The Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) declares that “all girls and boys in the world are entitled to the right to survival; the right to development (including the right to be educated); the right to protection from all forms of abuse, neglect, and exploitation; and the right to participation in matters that affect their lives and that prepare children to take on increasing roles of responsibility as they mature” (Johnstone, 2011). Johnstone, C. (2011). Inclusive Education and Child-Friendly Schools. Miske Witt & Associates Inc Throughout the Gaza Strip, access to education continues to be compromised due to the recurrent military incursions and conflicts. The long-lasting blockade imposed on Gaza restrict movement of people and goods, and have affected all 480,013 basic, secondary and kindergarten students and teachers. In addition to the destruction of education infrastructure, the increased mortalities of students impact the psychosocial wellbeing of children and their teachers, in addition to experiencing difficulties in reading and writing. Some 63% of those run by the Ministry of Education already operate on a double or triple shift system (OCHA, 2018). OCHA (2018). Humanitarian Needs Overview Increasing power blackouts, siege, recurrent conflicts and destruction of education infrastructure continues to impact the psychosocial wellbeing of children and teachers. About 70% of UNRWA schools and 63% of those run by the Ministry of Education already operate on a double or triple shift system. This approach has reduced instruction time to about four hours a day, limiting the time available to reinforce learning, support slow learners, remedial education programs, and hindering co- and extracurricular activities. Also, electricity blackouts restrict students’ study time and ability to concentrate and learn and negatively impacts their right and ability to access education at school and at home.OCHA (2018). Humanitarian Needs Overview Due to lack of access to updated and recent information on state of education in Gaza, YEC has currently conducted primary needs assessment. The method of need assessment includes key informants and schools’ directors. The interviewed decision makers were Deputy Minister of Ministry of Education (MoE) (Dr. Ziad Thabet), Director of studies and information at MoE (Ashraf Saleh), and Head of Documentation Department (Amal Hamada). The assessment was revealed that: The number of primary schools at Eastern Gaza is 14 which include 9340 students; while the number of primary schools at Beit Lahia in North governorate is 5 which include 3370 students. The number of school counsellors at Eastern Gaza is 27 (10 male and 17 females); while the number of school counsellors in Beit Lahia is 21 (9 male and 12 female) (MoE, 2018). MoE (2018). Studies and Information Department Schools counsellors have received training in: body and mind, and school mental health (depression, autism, attention deficit hyperkinetic disorders, anxiety in children, behavior disorders, and schizophrenia). However, they need training in: counselling by play, family counselling, dealing with educational problems related to psychological status of the child (Education Directorate in North Governorate, 2018). Education Directorate in North Governorate (2018). Personal Communication with Director of Planning
