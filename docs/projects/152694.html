Project Summary

In response to the critical malnutrition and food security situation across South Sudan, Action Against Hunger will continues its program for treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition in Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei States. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projections for Sep 2018 - Mar 2019, despite planned humanitarian food assistance and limited relief in the end of 2018, an expected 5.2 million (49% of the population) will be left severely food insecure (IPC 3, 4 & 5). Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season, depleting households’ food stocks from their own production. Our SMART surveys, conducted in all major counties of Action Against Hunger’s interventions (sub Gogrial East), show GAM rates above emergency threshold of 15%: Fangak GAM 16.1% and SAM prevalence 2.5%; Gogrial West GAM 26.6% with 5.4% SAM and Aweil East at 21% GAM and 4.9% SAM prevalence. This reality is greatly aggravated by limited availability of food in markets, poor diets, poor harvests, economic crisis, ongoing physical insecurity, poor hygiene and sanitation, and lack of services. The program will directly target 14,769 children under 5 and 6,664 pregnant and lactating women in treatment of malnutrition at 13 of the organization’s existing 35 nutrition sites (OTP/TSFP). 8,680 total caregivers will be sensitized on MIYCN topics. Our static intervention is complemented by our emergency response teams, who provide malnutrition surveillance, treatment and prevention services and related capacity building in areas of high need, as indicated by the Nutrition Information Working Group. Under this program’s treatment component, children with SAM and MAM and PLW with MAM will be treated through Outpatient Therapeutic Programs and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs. Children with SAM and medical complications will be referred for treatment at the organization’s Stabilization Centers in Alek and Malualkon. At nutrition sites, caregivers will receive sensitization and counselling on MIYCN and other health related topics. Caregivers will also receive various crucial gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items. Community volunteers will conduct mass screening at household level to ensure early detection and timely referral of cases of acute malnutrition. During mass screening, community volunteers will also provide valuable sensitization on nutrition and hygiene/sanitation practices, as well as counselling for those affected by malnutrition. Under the community component, the organization will conduct community mobilization activities, such as the training of Community Leaders and holding of mass awareness campaigns (large gatherings, radio spots) around MIYCN topics. Mother-to-Mother Support Groups are formed to inform mothers directly of MIYCN and MUAC measurement (the latter an initiative being piloted for the organization in South Sudan in 2019). These groups will also conduct cooking demonstrations and kitchen gardening activities in order to improve the food security and nutrition statuses of households. Male staff will conduct Male Group Discussions, which will sensitize men on women’s issues and MIYCN, enabling women’s improved access to nutrition services and activities.

Needs

South Sudan is affected by a protracted humanitarian crisis that peaked in mid-2016. Since the beginning of conflict in 2013, an estimated over 4 million people fled their homes (over half reside as refugees in neighbouring countries). According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projections for Sep 2018 - Mar 2019, despite planned humanitarian food assistance and limited relief in the end of 2018, an expected 5.2 million (49% of the population) will be left severely food insecure (IPC 3, 4 & 5). This is due partly to an early lean season, which will deplete households’ food stocks from self-production. Moreover, returns from within and outside of the country may cause extreme pressure on already scarce resources. Through March 2019, Aweil East County will remain in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), with Critical levels of Acute Malnutrition through the end of 2018. Protracted conflict, displacement, poor access to services, disease outbreaks, inadequate diet, low sanitation coverage and poor hygiene practices are the key drivers of acute malnutrition across South Sudan. Despite efforts of partners, the food security and nutrition situation remains critical in the states such as Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG), Warrap, and Joneglei States. Although these areas were not directly affected by conflict, food supply has been disrupted due to closure of the border with Sudan and insecurity in other areas of South Sudan, which impact supply routes and limit the availability of food in the markets. In Fangak, perennial heavy rains cause extensive flooding, destroying harvests.This trend will be difficult to reverse in the coming years, hence requiring concerted efforts of humanitarian and development partners within HRP framework. Our SMART surveys, conducted in all major counties of Action Against Hunger’s interventions (sub Gogrial East), show GAM rates above emergency threshold of 15%: Fangak GAM 16.1% and SAM prevalence 2.5%; Gogrial West GAM 26.6% with 5.4% SAM and Aweil East at 21% GAM and 4.9% SAM prevalence. Our Sept. 2018 Gender Analysis in Gogrial West found that a lack of education/literacy, lack of access to productive resources and health care, and climatic and conflict shocks result in women’s and girls’ disadvantaged position and use of precarious coping strategies. Having little control over one’s sexuality, pressure on mothers to bear additional children puts added stress on household food and nutrition security and limits the ability to breastfeed. Men and boys lack adequate access to resources and knowledge on reproductive health and malnutrition, often resulting in men’s restricting women’s care practices and movement to nutrition services. Women must gather home construction materials, fetch water and firewood, bear and care for children, and do HH chores. Men look after cattle, and share cultivation and construction duties with women. Although men are primarily responsible for partaking in paid labor, most women in Gogrial West partake in paid employment/livelihoods activities, usually due to their husbands’ suffering from mental illness or forced recruitment into armed forces, creating an additional burden on their workload. GBV often results from men finding it difficult to cope with unemployment and inability to provide for families. When present, men have control over all HH income, including that generated by women; however, women must cater for all HH expenses (including food) based on the allocation the man provides. Thus, a woman’s lack of decision making power and control over assets combined with her responsibility for household affairs during times of severe physical insecurity poses a significant threat to household food security and nutrition status: a situation which reflects the vicious cycle of gender inequality, conflict, poverty, and malnutrition.

Activities

Action Against Hunger will continue the high-quality delivery of nutrition services in the catchment areas of its 2 SCs and 13 of its 35 nutrition sites for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition in compliance with the national CMAM guidelines, with focus on a preventive approach. This program also encompasses the organization’s emergency response teams, who provide malnutrition surveillance, treatment and prevention services in areas of high need. Collaboration with other health/nutrition actors will be sought and capacity building of key health/nutrition staff will be ensured. Additionally, this program will be closely aligned with the organization’s WaSH program, which ensures the equitable access of families of SAM and MAM cases to water and sanitation practices, supplies and services. Children under 5 with severe and moderate acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating women with moderate acute malnutrition will be enrolled and treated in the CMAM program through our nutrition sites in Aweil East, Northern Bahr El Gazal and one site in Aweil Center, Northern Bahr el Ghazal; Gogrial West and Gogrial East, Warrap; and Fangak, Jonglei. Children with SAM and medical complication will receive therapeutic treatment in Stabilization Centers (SC). Children with SAM and no medical complications will be treated in an Outpatient Therapeutic Program at nutrition sites. Children with MAM and pregnant and lactating women will receive treatment in a Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program at nutrition sites. The organization integrates child friendly spaces and ECD stimulation into its Stabilization Centers. Community access to key nutrition interventions will be increased, with special focus on MIYCN, MtMSGs, Male Group Discussions and sensitization activities to prevent under nutrition in children under 5 and PLWs. Community volunteers and mothers will provide regular MUAC screening of children (6-59m) and PLWs at community level for early case detection and timely referral. The organization will lead sensitization sessions for mothers/caregivers of children under 5 and PLWs at SC/OTP/TSFP sites, as well as at community level on Nutrition, MIYCN, WASH in Nutrition and promotion of basic health services. The organization will form Mother to Mother Support Groups with a focus on training of Lead Mothers on MIYCN facilitating techniques within the catchment areas. Action Against Hunger will provide training to staff and community volunteers on CMAM, MIYCN, GBV, and the use of Nutrition/WASH/Health key messages. Action Against Hunger will conduct regular monitoring of the nutrition situation in the project area implemented and the information will be used for appropriate actions at the project level. Our MEAL teams will conduct SMART surveys in the project areas on regular intervals to assess the nutrition situation and take appropriate steps/measures. SQUEAC assessments will also be completed to determine the access and coverage of services. The nutrition component will also encourage participation of men as change agents. Men and women both must be involved in this process, acknowledging their respective roles and needs, fostering mutual awareness and partnership. Male Group Discussions, especially including fathers, will sensitize men on MIYCN and women’s issues – Led by male staff, ACF will pilot Male Group Discussions in 2019, meant to initiate a dialogue around women’s issues (esp. SRHR, MIYCN, GBV) and promote gender equality through encouraging mutual support between genders. This will increase male awareness on nutrition interventions, proven to enable women’s participation. (e.g. women often need the consent of men to participate in activities outside home). The above activities are included in our emergency response component, which is also complemented by nutrition-sensitive multi-sector interventions (e.g. WaSH, FSL).

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This is done through community participation and engagement as part of the community component under the preventive approach. Community leaders (teachers, religious leaders, authorities), caregivers of children 0-59 months, PLW and men who are counselled by the community volunteers, will be involved in the assessment of the community volunteer network. This will help to identify strengths and weaknesses of the community activity and inform Action Against Hunger's community strategy in order to improve the integration between sectors and increase the impact and efficiency of community activities. This also ensures accountability to the community and good governance of the project. Community mobilization activities, such as mass awareness campaigns, Community Volunteer household visits, and Mother-to-Mother Support Groups, sensitize target communities on the intervention being delivered and provide counselling on how to access nutrition services. Action Against Hunger establishes a feedback/complaints mechanism for each of its projects, to encourage feedback from the community and keep accountable and transparent towards the people we serve .

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All messages delivered to the community are context specific and tailored to the specific gender and age groups. Sensitization messages are tailored for specific groups within the community. For example, mothers are sensitized on mother, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) as they oversee the household food security and nutrition status. Inherent to MIYCN, messages are specific to children's nutrition needs and mother's nutrition needs. Men are sensitized on women's issues and MIYCN in order to enable and support the involvement of women and children in nutrition activities and improve the sustainability of the action.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. It sets the standards of conduct and behaviour required for the creation of an equitable workplace and accountability to beneficiaries. All staff are required to sign the PSEA Policy, and trainings on PSEA and the policy are ongoing across all levels of the organization.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Participants from a wide range of communities and gender and age groups participate in mass sensitization and awareness campaigns conducted by the organization. For example, during such campaigns, women and men are involved in the selection of safe distribution points and nutrition sites to reduce associated risks (distance, location, facility). Additionally, the organization consults state-level Ministries of Health and authorities to ensure synergies, awareness of the intervention, and capacity building for these bodies.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Action Against Hunger is committed to ensuring that its interventions are timely and appropriate to meet the needs of its beneficiaries throughout its implementing sectors in South Sudan. To ensure that Action Against Hunger is Accountable to its beneficiaries and the community it serves it has developed a feedback mechanism that can be integrated into current program activities to solicit feedback from beneficiaries and first-line implementers.The three groups that Action Against Hunger is interested in soliciting feedback from are:1. First-line program implementers (including volunteers and field staff)2. The local communities where our programs operate3. The direct beneficiaries of our programsSince first line implementers are the ones working directly with beneficiaries on a day to day basis they can often provide the most useful readily available information regarding the challenges beneficiaries face as well as how programs are functioning, what is difficult, and what needs to be changed.A total of nine (9) Feedback Mechanism tools have been developed for program staff to use at the field level. Out of the nine mechanisms five have been identified as “minimum standards,” which should be implemented at the program site level. 1. Monthly staff meetings between first-line implementers & field office staff, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries at the site level, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate, 5. Fraud Hotline Number.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This will be done through community management committees, comprised of representatives from the target communities/villages, will be formed to facilitate CV selection and implementation of project activities in a transparent manner. Local hearing committees will also be responsible for receiving complaints and addressing them or passing them on to Action Against Hunger where and when these cannot be resolved at the village/community level. Action Against Hunger field staff will always be available to address complaints on the spot.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Action Against Hunger in South Sudan conducts Safety Audits and Gender Analyses in all of its areas of intervention in order to assess protection risks associated with its nutrition program. For example, a recent safety audit identified waiting time at nutrition sites as protection risks for women caregivers and children accessing those services. In 2019, Action Against Hunger will assess and pilot different tools and methods in order to mitigate this waiting time, such as looking at the flow of service delivery within the actual site operations.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Action Against Hunger routinely conducts SQUEAC surveys in all areas of interventions. Coverage rates for both SAM and MAM are assessed and any barriers to access identified. For example, recent SQUEAC surveys identified a lack of awareness on the severity and symptoms of MAM as a barrier to women and children's access to nutrition services. Sensitization and awareness activities have been adjusted to place specific emphasis on MAM. Also, the organization will pilot Mother MUAC as a means for mothers to be able to identify both SAM and MAM in the children of their household and community.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To achieve the involvement of men and women in activities and mitigate GBV, Community Volunteers will engage communities through awareness activities at household level. Mass sensitization campaigns will also be organized at community level to raise awareness on MIYCN and women’s issues. Women and men will be involved in the selection of safe distribution points and nutrition sites to reduce the associated risks (distance, location, facility). Against Hunger will also lead Male Group Discussions in order to ensure men’s awareness of caregiving and women’s issues and support to woman community members’ participation in project activities and safely challenge gender inequities. The project staff will be informed about and trained on how to use GBV referral pathways in the project locations. All Action Against Hunger staff are bound to our Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. It sets the standards of conduct and behavior required for the creation of an equitable workplace and accountability to beneficiaries. Additionally, all staff sign and agree to the Action Against Hunger Code of Conduct, which establishes the organization’s zero tolerance approach to harm, discrimination, and/or stigmatization of other staff, partners, and beneficiaries. Action Against Hunger plans to capitalize on increased recruitment of women at nutrition sites and field offices and create opportunities for skills acquisition. Adequate gender-segregated and lockable latrines, shaded waiting areas, and a clean water supply are present at each of Action Against Hunger’s nutrition sites. Patient flow is designed and managed to mitigate the waiting time of nutrition site patients to two to three hours for an average size case load. While mothers wait for treatment, staff will engage them in sensitization activities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Iterative SMART Surveys, SQUEAC surveys, feedback mechanisms, discussions community leaders, post-distribution monitoring, and reports from Community Volunteers assess the level of access both targeted and untargeted communities have to health and nutrition services.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Action Against Hunger in South Sudan conducts Safety Audits and Gender Analyses in order to assess protection risks associated with its nutrition program. Safety risks related to GBV and physical insecurity are identified and measured in all areas of intervention. The Safety Audit also proposes solutions for such safety risks, such as the training of staff on GBV, improvement of flow at nutrition sites to mitigate waiting time, and increased awareness raising among both gender groups to enable safe mobility of women and children to nutrition sites.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Through our feedback mechanisms, the organization assess the satisfaction of beneficiaries with services delivered and invites their feedback regarding the project’s respect of beneficiaries’ dignity. The organization specifically ensures that identified dignity measures, such as menstrual hygiene kits and reduced queuing time, are maintained throughout the project through its monitoring and surveillance activities.
