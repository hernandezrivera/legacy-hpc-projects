Project Summary

In the face of increasing food insecurity as stated above, initiatives to address immediate food needs of the most vulnerable and food insecure population are paramount in saving lives and protecting assets of the most vulnerable. To address the food needs defined above, Lifeline Gedo (LLG) will target the 3,722 most vulnerable individuals (1,648 IDP and 2,074 non-IDP (host) households including people with disability) to meet their food needs and reduce consumption gaps through life-saving cash assistance and agriculture inputs for Jilal and Xagaa seasons. The project will reach IDP and host households in two districts of Belet-Xaawo and Ceel-Waaq in Gedo. Each targeted household in Ceel-Waaq will be provided with Six months of cash assistance via Mobile Money Transfer following the Cash Working Group's recommended transfer value in Gedo region for food and other basic needs at the cost 85 USD per month. Most vulnerable host households in Belet-Xaawo district will be assisted with agriculture input through in-kind distribution at an estimated cost of 243 USD per household. The agricultural input includes provision of drought resistant seeds, provision of land preparation through tractor hours, irrigation service, farm tools and training of good agricultural practices.
These households are facing wide consumption gaps, due to successive below-average agricultural and pastoral yields, and lack of casual labour income opportunities in pre-urban and IDP communities. This is contributing to a dire situation, especially in the areas of household food security. Food assistance through multipurpose cash assistance for Ceel-Waaq and provision of agricultural inputs for Belet-Xaawo will improve beneficiary household’s access to food, while preventing households from resorting to negative coping mechanisms which erode long-term resilience and in partnership with Concern Worldwide LLG has submitted MPCA project in Belet-Xaawo and Baardheere district. Community and stakeholder engagement, gender sensitive vulnerability and eligibility criteria for beneficiary selection and emphasis on participation of men, women, boys and girls of different age and other at-risk groups will improve gender equality and women’s empowerment among target population under proposed project.
To target the settlements, LLG will conduct stakeholder consultations and key informants’ interview at both target districts. Village Relief Committees (will also be sensitized on the different needs of vulnerable population groups, gender and age as well as receiving gender a vulnerable group sensitization. LLG’s independent Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) unit will regularly monitor the project – including project progress and implementation processes. In addition, MEAL team will also conduct telephonic verification with sample size of beneficiary households on monthly basis after every top up exercise for multipurpose cash assistance and distribution agricultural inputs.
LLG puts a strong focus on coordination with other humanitarian actors. LLG IS a member of Food Security Cluster (FSC), Somalia NGO Consortium and other Clusters at national and sub-national level. LLG also actively participates in formal and informal coordinating structures across Somalia and Nairobi, including FSNAU, SWALIM, WASH, CCCM, Health and Nutrition Clusters where programmes align.
LLG, both independently and through FSL and Sub-National Cash Working Groups (SNCWG), takes a proactive role in engaging other cash actors in Somalia, particularly in encouraging them to engage in sharing forecast location data. LLG works closely within the Sub-National Cash Working Groups (SNCWG)) and food security cluster (FSC), as well as bi-laterally with other aid agencies to ensure no other NGOs work in the target location on similar programming. LLG will also report monthly updates to FSC's 3W matrix.

1. - Projects activities

1. Stakeholder and Community engagement to disseminate information about project - including objectives, scope, roles and responsibilities and vulnerability and eligibility criteria of beneficiaries. This is to ensure continuity of stakeholders and community engagement started from project assessment stage and enhance during the remaining project cycle. LLG will engage in meeting with regional, district and local authorities, participate in coordination forums at different level, share information with other actors, meeting with local leaders and elders, conduct community meeting and encourage participation of vulnerable people in meetings to facilitate participation of stakeholders and community. 2. Village Relief Committee (VRC) mobilized and orientation: existing VRCs are mobilized or if needed new are set up by facilitating community engagement. In 3 target districts in Gedo project staff will train members in the selection process, vulnerability criteria and responsibilities of the VRC. 3. Beneficiary household selection: beneficiaries selection follows community-based approach. VRCs identify the most vulnerable members of their communities using a validated selection criteria following orientation. Priority is given to female-headed households and special needs groups, e.g. household headed by older people, households with members with disabilities or chronic illness, household with malnourished child, household with many children - all acknowledged to be vulnerable with difficulties in accessing food needs. Vulnerability criteria include a combination of: a) characteristic of the head of household linked to ability to access food (ex. older, ill, disability) b) Poverty of household c) Reliance on negative coping mechanisms d) Other criteria tailored to meet the context of the community. 4. Beneficiary household verification: the list of beneficiary households is announced to the community, giving them an opportunity to provide feedback and recommendations on list, or lodge objections through LLG’ Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM). To minimize the risk of spreading COVID-19, large gatherings are not encouraged thus the list of beneficiaries is posted on notice boards in accessible locations if appropriate in the target location. Safety and security considerations as well as community comfort to publicize the names on notice boards ensure we do no harm. The list is revised where needed based on community feedback. Project staff conduct verification through an Open Data Kit (ODK)-based verification survey with a sample of households to ensure they meet vulnerability criteria. The final list is announced to the community and approved by the VRC. 5. Beneficiary household registration. 6. Baseline: Survey is conducted prior to project implementation with a representative sample of households randomly selected from the target households number. The baseline values from the survey will form the key benchmarks against which achievements made by the project will be assessed. 7. Provision of improved access to food response and seasonal livelihood inputs through cash assistance to increase their purchasing power to immediate access of food to 245 households of IDPS in Ceel-Waaq and 375 households of non IDPs (host communities) for the provision of agricultural inputs in Belet-Xaawo. Cash assistance is provided based on the Cash Working Group Somalia's monthly transfer value recommendation. Currently recommended monthly transfer value per household for food assistance is 85$ and an estimated cost of 243 USD for agricultural inputs that includes seed provision of drought resistant type, land preparation, irrigation services and farm tools per household for in kind distribution. 8. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) is undertaken using survey forms aligned to Cash Working Group Monitoring & Evaluation Workstream guidance. PDMs are conducted with a representative random sample of beneficiary households.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Assistance proposed under this project is an opportunity to integrate, synergies and leverage on LLG's ongoing Resilience, Health and Nutrition programmes in the proposed areas as well as proposal submitted under FSL envelope to WFP 2022. Through this project, LLG will provide cash assistance to 82 IDP and 700 host households in Ceel-Waaq and 894 IDP and 1,874 host households in Baardheere and Belet-Xaawo districts will be assisted with agriculture inputs for Gu and deyr seasons in 2022. LLG is well placed to deliver cash and in-kind assistance in all districts, having implementing the similar nature of interventions under its ongoing programmes. In line with cluster priorities, this project aims to provide immediate access to food for populations primarily IPC3+ following the beneficiaries vulnerability and eligibility criteria described in the proposal. There will be a particular focus on female-headed households and households who have already exhausted their coping strategies and for whom access to food is critical and who are experiencing increased vulnerabilities as a result of seasonality of their livelihoods – namely families with a large number of young children. To ensure effectiveness and timeliness, agriculture inputs for Gu and deyr season in Baardheere and Belet-Xaawo districts must be provided latest by third or fourth week of March and September 2022. LLG is implementing similar activities in the region. Thus our existing capacities and establishment is vital to ensure HRP funded agriculture inputs for Gu and Deyr seasons are provided in timely manner in Baardheere and Belet-Xaawo districts. Similarly based on season calendar January, February, March and August, September and October and to some extent November are lean periods when vulnerable households are facing food crisis. LLG’s presence in target districts and its ongoing support to Health and Nutrition through mobile outreaches and community engagement will be crucial factor to identify and support most vulnerable families in need of food support. LLG’s established standard procedures on Cash Transfer Steps adapted from CWG guidelines and Somali Cash Consortium and its existing agreements with Mobile Money Transfer service provider will enable HRP fund to provide food support in timely and effective manner.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Lifeline Gedo regularly refers to joint market monitoring and CWG dashboard shared by FSC and CWG. In addition, Lifeline Gedo and community committees continuously monitor the market at the district and below district level to respond to any changes. Lifeline Gedo under BRCiS Consortium also monitors changes in market price and functioning on monthly basis under Early Warning Early Action work system conducted across Baardheere and Belet-Xaawo where BRCiS is currently under implementation. Markets in proposed three districts in this project have remained functional and there is adequate infrastructure to enable this modality to work effectively.Markets in the proposed districts that are targeted in the project are functioning. Joint Market and Supply Chain Update of week 10 to 17 September 2021 indicated little variation in price and availability of basic items like food, fuel etc. in the markets in the proposed district. Similarly, currency exchange rate fluctuation is not reported significantly to affect purchasing power. Markets are accessible – both in rural and urban areas as well as IDP and non-IDP population. Recent market assessments have not reported any major issues of cross-border trades and availability of basic items. Experience so far has not indicated that prices are inflated due to cash transfer programming in communities.Food prices, supply, and market conditions will be monitored monthly to determine if price and supply changes could impact beneficiaries’ ability to use provided assistance to meet their food and other basic needs. Monitoring includes FSNAU/FEWSNET initiatives (e.g. monthly market data update), CWG market updates and WFP and REACH price/market monitoring.The main assumption for the project is that there will be price stability over the project period and inflation will remain reasonable not to significantly erode the value and affect the household’s ability to meet their food needs. The project will continuously monitor market conditions and adapt appropriate mitigation measures to significant commodities, particularly food price fluctuations. In addition, the security continues to prevail and project teams have access to the proposed project areas and beneficiaries. Lifeline Gedo will closely coordinate with CWG and FSC - if assistance transfer value needs to be adjusted to achieve the intended results.The markets for farm inputs exist in major towns and either through the in-kind or voucher system, the seeds will be accessible to farmers. The strategy would be to supply inputs, especially that farmers are familiar with and the quantities that will be supplied are not high as to cause market prices to rise. Lifeline Gedo will procure the inputs and issue vouchers to farmers well ahead of the season to avoid prices increases that usually occur closet to the planting season.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, Minimum Inter-agency Standards for Protection Mainstreaming and Do No Harm principles, the project will ensure accountability and minimize harm to all beneficiaries at all stages of the programme cycle. Further, LLG has a Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), LLG Code of Conduct and Safeguarding policies - which all LLG employees, partners, contractors, and consultants must sign up to during their contract of employment and engagement. The policies set out and clearly articulates the standards of behavior expected including to ensure Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) when interacting with programme participants in the locations where LLG implements programmes. Staff and other contractors are regularly sensitized on these safeguarding policies. LLG commits to deliver relief food assistance with a consistently high level of safety, accountability, accessibility, and participation, right from its first interactions with the community through to the end of the intervention.At the onset of engagement with communities and stakeholders, LLG will conduct sensitization sessions on its fully functional CRM while also re-confirming that they can access the available feedback channels used. Posters with the channels and information on how to lodge complaints will be mounted in strategic locations in the community. In line with the handling procedures, complaints categorized as sensitive are channeled to the Programme Director per practice, to ensure they are addressed appropriately. Protection issues from beneficiaries will also be monitored regularly as part of the PDM processes as well as the regular tracking and updating of the protection risk assessment/analysis in the project locations. LLG uses available mobile phone company provided technology to send short messages and voice messages to target beneficiary households about entitlement before the transfer of assistance and after transfer informing about transfer, its purpose, source of assistance, CRM information and zero tolerance to corruption and fraud. LLG will tailor its CRM dissemination material – e.g. printed posters, leaflet, SMS content etc. and in-line with proposed project and its contractual obligation to comply with WFP policies and guidelines. In the target areas, LLG has adopted conflict sensitive programming. LLG continuously assesses the potential impact of its programme in creating or resolving conflicts. LLG takes consideration of conflicts linked to selection of project location, resource transfers, operational activities like selection of vendors, hiring of staff or enumerators, visibility material, lack of interaction with local authorities etc. LLG has integrated various conflict sensitivity approaches into the project in order to adhere ‘Do No Harm’ principles. This includes but not limited to, extensive interaction with clan elders and linking with existing conflict mitigation systems, well balanced hiring of staff, communication chains, responsive CRM mechanism, coordination and interaction with local authorities, clan sensitivity, robust procurement and sourcing policies and procedures, community and stakeholder engagement throughout project cycle etc. LLG Programme Director charged with the responsibility to ensure adequate application of protection considerations in the project will work closely with the programme team on protection and safeguarding to provide technical support to the implementation team.
