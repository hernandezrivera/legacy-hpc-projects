Project Summary

Fighting and Recycling of violence in southern unity has caused severe mental distress to internally displaced persons and host community in the proposed project locations leaving children and women vulnerable to sexual abuses and psychological distress, livelihoods disrupted, separation of children, child recruitment and households looted.
Despite efforts made by the warring parties to silencing the guns, however, human right violations are still being perpetrated by the same parties and most of violation and abuses went unreported especially in the hard-to-reach areas. The aims of this project are to prevent and mitigate protection risks by working together with community-based
protection mechanism and protection monitoring in hard to reach areas targeting 3500 girls, 3000 boys, 5000 women, and 3200 men.
Gender-based violence (GBV) remains under-reported due to barriers to reporting. Through MHA ongoing protection monitoring
project in Dablual and Tochriak, 12 GBV related incidence including the death of two underage girls were recorded. That indicates that the level of GBV is high amongst the community in southern Unity especially in hard-to-reach areas. women and girls being exposed to rape forced marriages and other forms of GBV due to the fact that displacement makes women and girls particularly vulnerable to exploitation and abuse due to their social and economic status. Women have been reported exposure to the sexual assault between several locations, Mayendit-Mirnyal and between Leah-Biehpadeah when it comes to accessing services and assistance. They face similar risks when
walking long distances to collect water and firewood. According to the report and individual/group focus discussion during MHA Assessment to Leah as mentioned above, MHA wanted women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support, link them with services providers and dissemination of life-saving
messages through awareness raising/community mobilization. MHA as protection partner implementing protection monitoring project under HRP 2018, we have conducted an assessment between19-21 of Sept 2018 in Leah and surrounding villages and interviewed a good number boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People from Leah had to walk a distance of 3 hrs either to Padeah or to Bieh in order to access humanitarian assistance. Following were discussed to have been the major challenges.
1. Most vulnerable IDPs and host community have very little chance or no chance at all to express their need if they are not reached by humanitarian agencies in the hard-to-reach villages/Island, this is due to accessibility issue where they are not able to reach the services and humanitarian assistance.
2.
Protection monitoring and assessment to identify the basic need and protection related concern amongst IDPs is lacking.
3. Awareness raising to sensitize the community on harmful practices, negative coping mechanism, their rights, their responsibilities within the community, where they should access services and humanitarian assistance is lacking due limited partners accessing hard-to-reach locations due to insecurity and the distances.
4. There is also a need to strengthen the community based-protection networks because communities are the primary support mechanism for women, men, girls, and boys affected by the conflict. Through our ongoing protection monitoring project, MHA is working with the community-based protection networks in Dablual and Tochriak building on existing community structures and networks because they are also best placed for identifying vulnerable children, GBV survivors and can greatly reduce the risk of exploitation, violence, and abuse of children.

Activities

1. To minimize exposure to further risks, MHA aims to advocate on behave of the beneficiaries in the hard-to-reach areas/proposed project locations so that humanitarian assistance and other services can be delivered to their locations instead of long-distance walk to access services and humanitarian assistance. 2. To conduct weekly, protection monitoring and assessment within the targeted locations to identify the trend, gaps and related protection and basic needs and the information and finding will be shared with partners on the ground and protection cluster for a further decision to be made for possible intervention. The current four teams of two will be increased with an additional two teams of 2 to have better coverage for three locations.3. Community mobilization and awareness raising on harmful practices such child early marriage, negative coping mechanism targetting common public places, like churches, schools local authority and community leaders.4. Dissemination of lives saving messages, where beneficiaries can access services, humanitarian assistance, their rights, and their responsibilities within the community especially for community leaders and local authorities. 5. Establish or support the existing community-based protection mechanism, train them and support them so that they will be able to help in identification of the protection-related concerns and to be able to cope up with protection related risks that are affecting them. 6. Construction of the 3 IDPS leaders meeting places in Bieh, Leah and 7. To link the beneficiaries with the services providers in variousneed and address the protection need of individual or group. 8. Conduct six capacity building training for Community leaders/community Based-Protection Network in Leah,Bieh, and Tay/Gandor in Mayendit, Leer, and Koch counties. The training will cover basic human right, prevention of GBV, forced/early child marriage and conflict mitigation between IDPs and the host community. 9. Conduct monthly advocacy meeting with IDPs and host community Leaders to convey the advocacy messages and to get feedback from thecommunity on the project activities and share the planned activities to increase their participation in the project implementation. 10. To conduct services mapping and coordinates with other protection agencies on the ground to avoid duplication of services.11. MHA will engage and advocate for women, adolescents boys and girls to be included in the leadership of area land board committee in the target areas.12.Conduct training for local authority, customary/traditional leaders that involved in the traditional/rule of law.13. Promote registration of HLP right for women and group at risk.14. Awareness raising on how they should claim their rights and facilitate free legal assistance.15. Arrange and encourage broad-based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men16. Introduce a complain mechanism within the community and in the organization level where HPL related concern can be reported. MHA has assessed the level of risk on how the services can be delivered to the beneficiaries in each location. There was no significant risk that can prevent project activities because this is going to be a field-based intervention where the staff will be locally recruited and are able to access these areas fress because they are part of the communities. on another hand, MHA will include the advocacy for the services to deliver where they are needed so that beneficiaries are not subjected to further risk when accessing services from far distribution points. The management has already established a good relationship with local authorities where MHA has no issues in relation to access to the project sites.
