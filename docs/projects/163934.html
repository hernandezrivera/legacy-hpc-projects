Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its CCCM intervention based on the analysis of humanitarian needs (MSNA) of affected population in NW Syrian, need severity for CCCM assistance, and HNO 2020 key messages, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and CCCM Cluster. The project aims to support provision of humanitarian assistance to IDPs in NW Syria, targeting 6,000 INDV (3,100 M, 2,900 F), through strengthen the basic infrastructure, coordinate multi-sectoral assistance, improve the management quality and accountability, and develop exit strategies. The project activities fall into two main components:
** Establishing a new IDP site
BO will identify the land in coordination with all relevant stakeholders, obtain the land agreement with considering HLP due-diligence of the CCCM cluster guidelines, and prepare the site plan, engineering layouts, and BoQs and SoWs. BO will use contractor modality to prepare the site land (including levelling, graveling the internal pathways, demarcation, installation of external fence, establishing rain drainage system, and installation of the solar lightning), also will establish the camp’s key administrative and critical humanitarian infrastructure (including firebreaks & fire points, admin offices, distribution points, warehouse, and safe spaces for women and children … etc.). In addition to that, BO will conduct Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities including repair of lighting, fence and other camp facilities, and graveling of communal areas and roads, especially after heavy rains, cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities, provision of fire extinguishers and fuel for running generators and other maintenance cost.
BO will coordinate with humanitarian actors and develop MoUs to provide multi-sector assistance for the established IDP site including Shelter & NFI, WASH, food, health services, protection, and education, with applying protection mainstreaming activities such as lighting the key facilities, provide access for PwD to wash facilities ... etc. As needed, identification in gaps in services, and advocacy for improved service delivery, especially on their adherence to sphere standards and equal access for all, particularly for vulnerable groups (i.e. PwD, female-headed HHs, elderly, women and children).
** Improve the management quality and accountability, and develop exit strategies
BO will conduct wide consultation with IDPs living in the site to establish the camp management committees, and will assist in developing their ToRs. The committees will receive capacity building trainings to be responsible for carrying out site management activities that include reception, registration, data collection, vulnerability mapping, community engagement, maintenance of gender-balanced and inclusive camp governance, regular information sharing, and AAP mechanism. Also, BO will assist in formation of Rescue Teams and provide them with training on Firefighting, First Aid, and Rescue Principles in case of emergency (i.e. flood, ... etc.).
Throughout the project, BO will select the target IDP community based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with CCCM Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria. Principles of “Do No Harm” and “Accountability to Affected People” are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.

4 - Needs

The number of IDPs residing in IDP sites has increased by 28 per cent from 2018 to 2019 while households in IDP sites are entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance while services and infrastructure in these sites are overstretched and in need of repair. Population movements peaked in May due to the escalation of hostilities in Idleb and surrounding areas in the northwest. Of the 316,638 movements tracked in May, 262,826 movements or 83 per cent of all movements were displacements to Idleb Governorate. An additional 45,755 population movements to Aleppo Governorate were reported the same month. Given the increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites in north-west Syria, severe overcrowding was reported as a growing concern.739,462 of IDPs live in 426 IDP sites in the north-west, with women and children representing 79 per cent of the population in these sites. IDP sites of last resort in Atma, Azaz, Deir-Hassan, Karama, and Sarmada sub-districts alone host 416,938 IDPs or almost half of the IDPs residing in last resort sites. Overcrowding in these sites has resulted in many protection risks, lack of privacy, and increased risk of GBV. Over concentration of people has put essential services and infrastructure under strain leading to a lack of or limited access to latrines, bathing spaces, and other common facilities’ usage. IDP sites along the Turkish-Syria border in Idleb are prone to flooding. Inadequate shelter and essential services in these sites may lead to risks of communicable diseases. Acute winterization needs remain high for people in IDP sites in the north-west and north-east Syria from October to March when temperatures drop, requiring a large-scale, timely response to address immediate needs for winter clothing, blankets, insulation, and fuel. Displaced persons in IDP sites may experience a variety of protection risks including concerns related to freedom of movement.

Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators.
