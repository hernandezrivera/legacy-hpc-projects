Project Summary

The project aims to assist refugees in acquiring transferable, and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp contexts, which would support sustainable return and integration (in Myanmar) when conditions permit; while enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives in the camps, including through engagement in multi-sectorial camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities, and community works, with the utilisation of acquired competencies. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees, pending durable solutions. The other initiative will enhance the engagement of refugee women in productive activities inside the camps. These women will produce female hygiene materials for camp consumption. This will be achieved by strengthening the quality of final products, including through improving production processes and upgrading current production centres. The project has a component to create opportunities for the host community women.
The project will develop occupation-based skills with standard curricula recognisable across the world for 8,000 refugee youths (18-24). Another 1,000 will get non-standardised skills training to enhance their existing skills. The project will also develop 10 standard training curricula packages to implement in the camps.
The project will create production-based daily wage employment opportunities for 3,000 refugees, 2,000 in different sectors as a volunteer, and 2000 refugee youths will be supported in self-employment. 500 women from the host community will get a chance to earn money by doing artisanal products.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The baseline camp needs assessment 2022 highlights that 6.07% of the Rohingya youth have vocational training before the exile. Around 23.72% of refugee youths have received non-standardised skills training inside the camps since they arrived in Bangladesh, and 30.9% of the Rohingya youth have received education while living in the Refugee camps. Moreover, the result also highlighted that, among the females, the major set of skill training requested includes industrial sewing machine operation (26.5%), nursing (8.6%), health-community health worker (6.4%), health-midwifery (4.3%), agricultural production (3.8%), banking and nutrition (3.8%), etc. In contrast, for male refugee population, whose interests are for occupanios of community health worker (17.4%), masonry (14.7%), machine mechanics (13.6%), solar electrician and system maintenance (10.6%), electrician-house wiring (10%), plumber (8.1%), etc. The challenges of women's participation in productive engagement were reflected through three broad aspects such as biasness among women groups due to low self-esteem, stereotype threat often caused by lack of prior information, and lack of motivation for skills training and development associated with a low level of education. The skills development training solutions, whatever their extent would be, need to be offered to the targeted women as 'a group' considering their 'in-group' biasness (neighbour influences neighbour) and by mobilising the female groups for enabling the sustainable solutions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will target three categories of beneficiaries: i) Refugees with limited basic skills, including but not limited to literacy and numeracy skills, and ii) Refugees who have some level of knowledge and skill capacity within certain job categories which enable them to work as low skilled or semi-skilled labourers. The Project will also support advocacy for transferable skills development training opportunities for the Rohingya refugee youth and adolescents. With its inclusive character, the project focuses on the enrolment of refugees, particularly women, girls and persons with disabilities, in training and learning opportunities based on their skill levels, needs and interests and linking training graduates with volunteering opportunities in the camps. It is also focused on expanding women’s participation in non-traditional livelihoods through targeted entrepreneurship and skills development. The skills development project aims to assist refugees in acquiring transferable, and portable skills relevant to the Rakhine State of Myanmar that would support sustainable return and integration when conditions permit; while enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives during their stay in Bangladesh, including through engagement in multi-sectorial camp-based activities, livelihood activities, and community works, with the utilisation of acquired competencies.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will comply with the protection principles by ensuring their participation throughout the project cycle. The project will ensure equal participation of diverse groups, including persons with disabilities, and adhering to the principle of "Do No Harm".

3.2 - Gender

In the proposed Project and as indicated in the previous sections, women will constitute no less than 50 percent of the total number of beneficiaries for all planned activities. This will be done by assessing the challenges women are facing in participating in the skills and livelihood programmes and benefiting from them. Specific measures will be adopted to overcome these challenges, continuously monitor the extent to which women are participating in and benefitting from project activities, and adjust the strategy as needed when the goal of 50 percent is not achieved. At the practical levels, measures, including expanding women’s participation in non-traditional livelihoods through targeted entrepreneurship and skills development will be adopted.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will target refugees above 18 years old, as there are paid volunteering opportunities after successfully completing the training. The project has an obligation not to include the child in the productive engagement.

3.4 - Diversity

Among the target participants, at least 50% of them will be women and 1% will be persons with disabilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will adopt a community-based approach which incorporates the principles of age, gender and diversity mainstreaming in their work with refugees in order to ensure accountability to affected populations; in other words, through consultation and participation throughout the programme cycle, refugee communities in each camp should be engaged meaningfully and substantively in all aspects of programmes that affect them and play a leading role in catalysing change and enhancing community resilience.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The overall environmental and climate change impact, the Project will reduce the ecological footprint through economic empowerment, especially for women. The project will integrate the guiding principles of UNHCR’s Environmental Guidelines in alignment with the national legal framework (The Forest Act, 1927; Wildlife (Conservation and Security) Act, 2012) and Energy and Environment Working Group guidelines. All environmental and climate action activities will be implemented under the umbrella of UNHCR within or beyond the project scope in a comprehensive complementary approach. The focus will be to embed the principle of ecological restoration and reduce the risk of climatic hazards.
