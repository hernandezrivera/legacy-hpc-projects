Project Summary

This project is aimed at improving the living standard of persons of concern (Internally Displaced persons, Returnees' and vulnerable host community members) in target locations across Adamawa and Borno States, through food security and livelihood support. The project seeks to restore human dignity and promote avenues for an end to the plight of forcibly uprooted people, through improved access to food and sustainable means of livelihoods. The project outcome is to foster self-reliance, sustainable and durable return through a 4-pronged approach. First, support agriculture and farming initiatives, including livestock production and empowerment; second, enhance self-employment through grants for income generating activities; third, provide unconditional Cash transfers for food assistance to the most vulnerable persons of concerns PoCs to ensure immediate food availability for households, and fourth, promote vocational training - literacy, and entrepreneurship, for unemployed youths. While livelihoods in Adamawa and Borno States remain particularly affected by the twin factor of conflict and COVID-19 pandemic, improvements in security and access have improved market conditions and enabled the resumption of livelihoods, especially crop and livestock production, fisheries and aquaculture in some accessible locations. Host communities, IDPs and returnees here are able to engage in farming by accessing land through rental arrangements and communal land holding managed by traditional leaders (Bulama and lawan). According to the IOM DTM XXXII, 98% of IDPs in Borno State have access to livelihood activities in camps and camp-like settings, such as daily labourer (30%), petty trade (27%), farming (23%), collection of fuelwood (17%), agro-pastoralism (1%) and fishing (1%). In less accessible LGAs, populations are able to plant in a “green belt zone” a secured 2-5 km area in the periphery of settlements. However, albeit the above situation, access to food remains a great deal of concern to IDPs and returnees, and some cases, the host community members whose food consumption has been stretched beyond limits This has increase and even worsened the degrading coping strategies in these locations, as women, especially girls between the ages of 15 - 35 sell themselves in exchange for daily food. Young boys between the ages of 10 - 19 years are not left out in the coping strategy for survival, as they engage in dehumanizing and over stressed daily labor for survival. The aged who are not strong enough to engage in daily struggle end up being starved for lack of food. Given the dire need for household food, this intervention seeks to employ the cash transfer programme, to provide cash for food to most vulnerable households in target location. This way, the livelihoods support would magnify the impact of the project as PoCs will have the needed energy (due to food availability) to engage in income generating activities for household income sustainability. The first group of Persons of Concern for this project will be Internally Displaced Persons in camps and camp-like settings; Returnees and those living with host communities. The project will be implemented in 6 Local Government Areas in Adamawa State: Michika, Madagali, Yola (North and South), Mubi (North and South) and 3 Local Government Areas in Borno State: Gwoza, MMC and Jere. This project will be targeting 11,000 Household across the 9 LGAs of Adamawa and Borno State.

1 - Needs

The decade long insurgency affecting the Northeast States of Nigeria has continued to create negative impacts on the populations in so many ways. One of the impacts, is the displacement of larger number of people, many of whom are still taking shelters, across several internally displaced camps (IDP Camps), both formal and informal, in the three states. According to a recent study conducted jointly by the united nation’s women agency (UNWOMEN), CARE International and OXFAM, revealed that, even before the outbreak of COVID-19 North-east Nigeria has been devastated by decade long conflict and has been described as one of the worst humanitarian crises in the world. Across the three affected BAY states, there is an estimated figure of 7.9 million people that require humanitarian assistance in 2020 out of a total of 13 million people. That is, one in every two people will need help to stay alive. This represents an increase of 11 % compared to 2019. The factors responsible for the increase in number of displaced persons are mainly due to upsurge in violence and insecurity in the year 2020. The assessment further revealed that, over 80% of the IDPs are in Borno state, the epicenter of the crises. The majority of the IDPs are living within host communities putting an additional burden on the already overstretched resources of those communities. It is estimated that over 79-per cent of people living in Borno will require humanitarian assistance in 2020.About 80 percent of the population in the three states of northeast Nigeria depend on farming, fisheries and livestock production and trade for their livelihoods and food security. Most of this conflict-affected population have lost their livelihood assets and therefore, the means of livelihoods as well. Food insecurity and low levels of food consumption in the North East states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe can be attributed to depleted household stocks and poor access to markets, high prices of staple food, low income and extreme coping strategies. Furthermore, across the BAY states, access to productive services such as farming has been severely hampered by a twin factor of COVID-19 and insurgency, due to lockdowns and movement restrictions imposed by governments. Production of food crops has remained at an all-time low level. The low level of supply of food commodities in markets has led to souring prices of stable food items threatening an already fragile food security situation in the region. Agriculture is the mainstay of 80% of the population in Nigeria and it accounts for 40% of the total gross domestic product (GDP). However, the insurgency has negatively impacted on the ability of conflict affected population to undertake agricultural activities. Limited access to land and the closure of trade routes, levies imposed by the insurgents and the military restrictions have negatively impacted farming, disrupted markets and other income generating opportunities. Caritas Nigeria aims at targeting about 11,000 households (equiv. to 66,000 people), in accessible communities across Borno and Adamawa. The intervention strategy will be based on specific needs, type and number of beneficiaries, access to water and security. Caritas Nigeria will support agriculture and farming initiatives, including livestock production and empowerment through provision of agricultural inputs (seeds and vegetables and fertilizer) for planting, and provision of small ruminant livestock for rearing. Caritas Nigeria will also enhance self-employment through provision of grants for income generating activities; households with existing businesses (petty trade) will be identified and trained for proper business management. Caritas Nigeria also aims to provide unconditional Cash for food assistance to the most vulnerable persons of concerns PoCs, and finally, promote vocational training, literacy, and entrepreneurship, for unemployed youths. 2 - Activities

1. Production Kits & Inputs for Agriculture - 2000 PoCs will receive agricultural production inputs for the 2021 rainy season farming, this will be achieved through the formation of farmers group where every PoC targeted will join a group of 10 to 20 persons in their various communities. It will also be a means of sharing farming ideas and readily adopting improved agricultural practices. Caritas Nigeria shall source for certified seeds vendors to procure improved varieties of seeds for crops selected by the PoCs for distribution at the start of the farming season. The farmers will be trained on nutrition sensitive agriculture, so that their farm outputs would contribute to the eradication of acute malnutrition in target locations. There will be training on good agronomic practices, perma and home gardening as well as organic farming techniques, processing and storage techniques. PoCs will be encouraged to adopt these farming practices as a sustainable ecosystem friendly way to farm more profitably contribute to building a vibrant and robust agro-economy within target communities.2. Provision of Small Livestock - To strengthen and build the resilience of communities from the loss of livelihood assets, 2000 PoCs will receive livestock of their choice to rear for livelihoods. Veterinary care will be provided for the livestock to reduce incidence of livestock disease and death. The farmers will be trained on the appropriate livestock management and feed production for sustainability. This will also increase their livelihood asset of which they can sell in the time of need and also improve household nutrition and rebuild livelihood of vulnerable communities.3. Unconditional Cash Transfer (Vulnerable people in need have access to lifesaving food)-Caritas Nigeria will target 2000 Households with food assistance response using cash based transfer programme. We will encourage the community leadership to set up Community Project Implementation Committee (CPIC) to select the most vulnerable households amongst the community. We will conduct a rapid verification of the proposed households to confirm their vulnerability using vulnerability index developed by Caritas Nigeria and other vulnerability criteria that the community wish to come up with before registering the households in the kobo platform. For the immediate relief response (3- 6 months) the priority will be to stabilize the food and nutrition situation in the targeted areas where indications of severe emergency conditions and severe acute malnutrition threaten mortality. While meeting life-saving needs, important emphasis will be placed on refining geographic and household targeting, developing multi-purpose mobile cash-based transfer platforms, strengthening post-distribution monitoring and feedback mechanisms, and building the knowledge base around food security, nutrition, markets, and protection in order to track changes in vulnerability. As the acute food insecurity and malnutrition is stabilized, we will work with the government and partners to transition to livelihoods-based approaches after the lean season. The preferred modality for the livelihood response will be cash-based transfers, which will in turn stimulate local markets and trade.4. Cash for Business Start-up-2000 PoCs will be targeted across the 6 LGAs to receive a cash grant to start-up a new business or boost their existing business. For the purpose of transparency and accountability and transfer of risk, Caritas Nigeria will engage the services of a financial institution to carry out the task across the 9 LGAs of intervention.5. Provision of Necessary Non-Food Items (NFIs) in line with the humanitarian principles1000 HHs will receive energy saving stoves to reduce the incidences of tree cutting for fuel which contribute negatively to climate change.6. Capacity Building - 2000 PoCs will be trained on different skill sets such as soap making, bags/beads making, tailoring, shoe making and carpentry.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Caritas Nigeria will use a multi-sectoral approach to address the needs of refugees, displaced persons and returnees, in order to provide holistic services which will ensure the wellbeing of all beneficiaries. Caritas Nigeria and JDPC, through the project conducted a community sensitization on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and feedback and complaint mechanism. This was carried out in all the implementation communities of Adamawa state. On prevention against sexual and exploitation and abuse, the communities were sensitized on the various forms of sexual abuse and exploitation. How they can be exploited and abused by project team as well as the community mobilisers working for them from the community. It is important to note, that the project is being implemented in an environment where there is significant power discrepancies and deeply rooted inequalities, including gender inequality which can give rise to sexual misconduct. For this reason, Caritas Nigeria works systematically to identify and reduce risks including risks of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment in all its operations by ensuring that all its staff sign its code of conduct that prohibits, among other issues, sexual exploitations and abuse.Members of the community are also sensitized on the feedback and complaint mechanism established to better ways of implementing program activities without doing/causing harm to them. This will be achieved through various strategies, some key strategies for health & nutrition intervention will include: Community mobilization, sensitization, awareness raising and education using various platforms including already established extensive church structures and systems; Capacity building and system strengthening for healthcare workers in the diagnosis and management of GBV; Direct service provision such as HIV testing and counseling services and community diagnosis and referral of identified non communicable disease to nearest health facilities; Referral and support services; Food demonstration to improve nutritional status of beneficiaries. Given the peculiarities of the SGBV project, several platforms have been identified to provide SGBV interventions as best practices. These will form Prevention and Response components; Stakeholder engagement, awareness creation on SGBV using community platforms to implement Gender Norms Interventions (GNI). Post GBV care services will be provided to identified SGBV survivors as part of the clinical and non-clinical response services to SGBV. Caritas Nigeria will establish Community-based Protection Committees that will support restructuring when necessary, to ensure active involvement of women, young people and marginalized groups. The strategies highlighted to carry out gender activities within already existing platform where GNI cohort coordinators/champions will be selected and trained to provide community Cohort Sessions in selected communities with high prevalence of SGBV cases includes; Cohort (homogeneous groups) sessions that include gender-based issues will be discussed. A total of 10 meetings of one hour over the 1-year period will be counted for reached individuals. To respond to SGBV cases identified and reported, SGBV Champions will be identified and trained to provide psychosocial first aid and refer for Post GBV care First aid services provided in health camps located in the communities and link survivors for other post GBV care services mapped out in the communities that provide clinical and non-clinical services not provided in health posts.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Caritas Nigeria will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are safe, trusted, streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. Specific issues raised by affected individuals regarding violations and/or physical abuse including sexual exploitation and abuse that may have human rights and legal, psychological or other implications will not only be documented, but also acted on. Caritas Nigeria will continue to make use of its systems of the use of suggestion boxes, hot lines (07080601152; 07058890234), stakeholders email address (stakeholdersfeedback@ccfng.org), use of community project implementation committee and community mobilisers.The Community Implementation Committees (CPICs) and the various support groups shall designate one or two members with a clear mandate to animate the complaints/feedback of affected individuals to Caritas Nigeria. We will also institute a quarterly beneficiary satisfactory survey to get feedback from the beneficiaries with regards to the program, as well as take their inputs to ensure a better programming subsequently A monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning framework will be established to ensure that the beneficiaries participate fully in the monitoring the implementation of the project. This plan will keep track of activities through regular communication with field staff, weekly management meetings and monthly project review meetings. Appropriate tools for gathering information at community level will be designed with input from the CPICs and beneficiaries. Gathering of monitoring data for the project will be done by diocesan Caritas staff and partners. Information on project progress will be shared, adjustments made as required and lessons learnt quickly put into use.
