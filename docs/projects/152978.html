Project Summary

According to the UNICEF-WHO 2017 Joint Monitoring Programme, over 61 percent of the population of South Sudan practiced open defecation and the few existing sanitation facilities are mostly in disrepair. High open defecation rates are exacerbated by the internal displacement of over 1.94 million people since December 2013. The worsening economic crisis has negatively affected the cost of delivering WASH services. Institutional capacity is fragmented and communities that are unable to afford clean water have reverted back to using contaminated water sources. The shortage of fuel has increased the cost for hiring water trucks and this service is vital in areas such as Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites and refugee camps.
WASH infrastructure has been deliberately targeted by armed actors during the conflict, leaving most of the water infrastructure in conflict-affected areas destroyed. Internal displacement puts a strain on existing water sources and increases WASH needs. The deteriorating economic situation has made it difficult for the population to afford safe water, as in most locations, the price per liter has doubled while outside Juba the price has tripled compared to late 2016. With congested populations, both in crowded PoC sites and urban areas, water-borne diseases spread rapidly. The ongoing cholera outbreak, which has spread to new areas, is both a consequence and a cause of unmet WASH needs. High malnutrition rates are also linked to poor WASH conditions, as exemplified in the diarrhea-malnutrition cycle prevalent in vulnerable populations.
Recent surveys show that more than 30 percent of people in South Sudan do not have access to safe water supplies; only 13 percent have access to adequate sanitation facilities, making this one of the lowest service coverage statistics in the world. Only 45 percent of South Sudan’s 3,349 basic primary schools have access to safe water and a mere 17 percent have adequate sanitary latrines for both girls and boys. South Sudan remains host to 98 percent of the world’s remaining Guinea worm cases, despite a reduction in caseload from more than 20,500 in 2006 to below 1,000 in 2011. Concerted efforts are being spearheaded by UNICEF to achieve eradication of this debilitating diseases by providing safe and clean water to affected communities. The overall situation of acute malnutrition has slightly improved this year as compared to the same period last year with no counties reporting extreme critical levels (GAM above 30%) of acute of malnutrition in 2018.
In 2018, World Vision International South Sudan was able to provide clean and safe drinking water to 217,770 people who received at least the minimum 20 liters of safe drinking water per person every day through surface water treatment systems (water treatment plants and Small scale water treatment system) and ground water (boreholes). WVSS also constructed 4,239 shared household latrines and reached 193,110 people with hygiene promotion messages. In schools and health facilities, WVSS continues to provide WASH interventions. These activities were implemented in Central Equatorial, Western Equatorial, Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bhar El Ghazal and Warrap states.
In 2019, WVSS intends to continue providing emergencies WASH interventions to the same states mentioned above for the following reasons :
1. The increased movement of the IDPs due to multiple armed conflicts is putting a strain on existed WASH resources for both the IDPs and host communities. Thus, there is a need to construct additional WASH facilities and rehabilitated existing facilities.
2. The drought has left communities with very limited options for WASH facilities support, particularly water supply. There is a needs for adequate and sustainable water sources and supply systems to ensure that communities have enough support to overcome environmental risks and challenges, as well as build their resilience to respond to future challenges.

Needs

In 2019, World Vision South Sudan will prioritize an integrated approach to WASH interventions in health facilities, nutrition facilities and schools in the former Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bhar El Ghazal, Upper Nile, and Western Equatorial States. In line with the WASH cluster strategy and through an integrated approach with health and nutrition projects, WVSS will service new and unreached displaced populations by improving quality, equality and needs based access to WASH services for IDPs, through responsive and effectively engagement with priority populations by addressing protection gaps and building resilience, and by promoting relevant community participation. WVSS will work in collaboration with the WASH cluster and will also ensure that its WASH interventions leverage on other sectors, mainly the health cluster, nutrition cluster, GBV sub- cluster and the protection cluster. In schools, education and WASH partners will collaboratively provide safe water points and structural construction guidance for gender appropriate toilets and handwashing facilities, and train children as handwashing ambassadors for disease prevention. WVSS will support WASH activities in health facilities, nutrition facilities and schools. Hygiene promotion will be an important area of focus for WVSS and it will be done in communities, health facilities, nutrition sites and the schools. Depending on the movement of displaced populations as a result of armed or inter clan conflicts, WVSS will reach the beneficiaries through rapid response mechanisms.

Activities

As per the WASH cluster strategy, during the 2019 humanitarian response plan, World Vision South Sudan will provide the following emergency WASH activities to IDPs and host communities : - Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations - Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.- Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services - Ensure gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers - Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis - Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis- Provide safe drinking water and nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM-Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community -Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level
