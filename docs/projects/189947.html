Project Summary

UNFPA will continue promoting, coordinating and providing lifesaving integrated GBV services following the survivor-centered approach to address GBV in line with IASC (Interagency Minimum Standard) for emergency programming including all guidelines and measurement for COVID response and prevention. The Women and Girls’ Friendly Spaces (WGFS), selected One Stop Centers (OSC), and government service entry points will be offering integrated SRH-GBV services that includes GBV case management, mental health and psychosocial support and services, clinical management of rape, family planning services and counseling, treatment of sexually transmitted infections and emergency referrals to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls. GBV risk mitigation and prevention activities will be ensured by engaging community based approach where men and boys will be heavily engaged in line with IASC principles and guidelines. Life skill programmes will be in place and through an interagency partnership and referral mechanism context based livelihood and self-reliance programmes will be onboard. Social cohesion and community mobilization will be supported through a number of approach. As women and girls continue to face protection risks, advocacy and communication for change will be implemented to guarantee their dignity and safety. Accountability towards community will be prioritized to safeguarding of beneficiaries and as co-chair of the PSEA Network at national level UNFPA will continue to lead the PSEA Network, and work with PSEA Network in the regions, to ensure a robust implementation of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse reporting and response mechanisms at all levels in the project sides.
Under the leadership of UNFPA, the GBV AOR will enhance engagement with other sectors, national and regional government for integration of GBV prevention, mitigation and response programming for quality services of GBV survivors using IASC guidelines. The AoR will expand comprehensive GBV prevention and response programmes for women, and girls, focusing on case management and multi-sectoral referral systems. Capacities will be enhanced on the GBV minimum standard, GBV handbook, IASC GBV Guidelines to sector leads, government stakeholders, and implementing partners fostering effective coordination in planning, implementing and monitoring essential GBV risk mitigation actions in all humanitarian response and services. This will be supported with regular community based GBV risk monitoring and joint safety audits. The AOR will deliberately target disability inclusion in GBViE response.

A regular coordination meeting with service providers of GBV will be conducted to understand the challenges of referrals and services provided in place and also to accelerate response with proper support through coordination mechanism. The capacity of the Community Based Group, Women Leaders, Women Lead Organization will be strengthen and they will be consulted through into safety audits, community feedback sessions etc. the project will engage a number of local organizations in implementation of the porgrammes.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will facilitate regular community consultation to receive feedback on service provision. In the safe spaces a general satisfaction survey will be done, while the individual satisfaction will also be conducted on special service provision, for instance for GBV case management and MHPSS services. At community level the client satisfaction survey will also be ensured quarterly. The staff will be trained on using the satisfaction survey and it will be disaggregated on age, gender. The feedback will eb collected by the staff who are not directly part of the service provision to make sure the neutrality. As all staff will be trained don GBV guiding principles the information will be collected through a safe and confidential manner. The community will be trained don PSEA and information on reporting mechanism for PSEA will be available on the service sides. UNFPA staff will be monitoring the project sides and meet the participants directly and will collect on the spot feedback from community. The reports will be compiled and shared with implementing partners to improve the quality of services
