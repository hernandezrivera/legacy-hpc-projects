Project Summary

Despite a significant reduction in conflict in recent years, overall needs have grown considerably due to a severe economic crisis, which has been further exacerbated by COVID-19. It is estimated that 9.3 million people including 5.4 million children need humanitarian assistance in 2021. International support towards the transitional government has not yet translated into substantive financial and policy support to contribute to a progressive reversal of the situation. As a result, humanitarian needs continued to grow, driven by underlying factors including the economic crisis, food insecurity, flooding, disease outbreaks and intercommunal violence. All these factors have resulted in prolonged displacement in addition to recent fighting in Jabal Marra between different factions of armed groups and intercommunal conflict exacerbated by the deteriorating economic conditions and other political factors . Sudan has and is still receiving additional numbers of refugees from South Sudan, Congo, CAR, Eritrea and recently more than 45000 Newly displaced Ethiopian refugees. The country has also continued to experience rains and floods emergencies and witnessed the worst floods ever in 2020 where around 900,000 people have been affected in all the 18 states of the country. Moreover, endemic diseases such as Malaria, cholera, dengue fever and chikungunya, other novel diseases are also emerging including some which have not been reported in over 10 years. The people in need in Sudan will be the highest over the past decade. Protection of children from violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect within such complex emergencies is challenged by many factors that child protection partners are working to address. Children affected by conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks and other emergencies are more prone to all types of violations to their rights including their rights to basic services. Many have been separated from their families due to sudden displacement and become vulnerable to further violations including recruitment and association with armed forces/groups. Others who witnessed violence and atrocities during conflict are psychologically affected and need psychosocial support services to avoid further deterioration to trauma and mental health problems that needs specialized services which is not available in almost all conflict affected areas. Displaced children and adolescents specially adolescents girls are subject to GBV because of the breakdown of the protective family and community environment during displacement and conflict. Many children in the states affected by armed conflict are victims of UXOs and some of them lost their lives specially in the newly accessible areas in Blue Nile, south Kordofan and Jabal Marra area in Darfur. The six grave violations against children by armed forces/groups is wide spread in the conflict affected areas and with UNAMID withdrawal new approaches to monitor and report on grave violations including community based approaches to response to these grave violations against children are needed basically strengthening formal and informal child protection mechanisms and ensure that they complement each other. Under the strategic objective one of HRP, child protection emergency response to newly displaced people due to intercommunal conflict, flooding, and pockets of violence in Jebel Marra areas as well as in the newly accessible areas in South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states will be provision of lifesaving child protection services such as provision of PSS, FTR services, prevention and response to GBV against children and adolescents, UXO risk education, monitoring , reporting and responding to grave child rights violations, engagement with communities to end grave violations, provide reintegration services to children released from armed forces/groups and other vulnerable children and emergency preparedness in cases of intercommunal conflict, floods and disease outbreaks.

1 - Needs

People in the Sudan remain extremely vulnerable to emergencies. Deepening poverty iscausing widespread malnutrition; lapses in the rule of law are allowing ethnic tensions to eruptinto violence; flooding and disease outbreaks are destroying lives and livelihoods; andeducation interruptions in 2019 and 2020 have left 13 million children out of school. COVID-19 and related mitigation measures are further complicating these existing challenges.These hardships are threatening people's lives, denying children – particularly girls – access toan education, creating serious protection risks and giving rise to violations and abuse,including gender-based violence. Past conflicts in the Sudan and neighbouring countrieshave left thousands of people internally displaced or living as refugees and without basicsupport, in or out of camps.While the recent steps taken towards securing peace have opened the possibility ofhumanitarian assistance in previously inaccessible areas in Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile,humanitarian needs remain high: 9.3 million people, including 5.4 million children, will requirehumanitarian assistance in 2021. In addition to the violence, flooding and disease outbreaks,populations are affected by the economic turmoil that has undermined livelihoods andincreased vulnerabilities across the country. The Sudan’s food and nutritional security are eroding,deepening a crisis already affecting 14 per cent of the population.People are affected by regular outbreaks of diseases such as malaria,dengue, chikungunya, measles, polio, cholera and, since March 2020, COVID-19. Poorinfrastructure, meager resourcing and humble capacities have stretched the fragile healthsystem. Rural people are particularly affected due to heightened economic vulnerability andfood and nutrition insecurity in addition to the negative impact of armed conflict in the conflict affected areas. As of October 2020, 1.9 million people, including 1.6 million children, are displaced due toconflict and ethnic violence in Darfur, Kordofan and Eastern states. While peace agreementsmark political progress, deep communal tensions can quickly ignite into deadly violence. TheSudan also hosts 1.1 million refugees fleeing strife from surrounding countries. Over 813,000of these refugees are from South Sudan, including 560,000 living outside of camps in host communities. This difficult situation has resulted in an increased violence against children. As a result there are huge needs to ensure that affected children and their families will receive the needed MHPSS to avoid further mental health problems. The sudden outbreaks of fighting has resulted in many children being separated form their families and their are in need for FTR and alternative care arrangements. The displacement and breakdown of family and community protective environment have lead to that many GBV cases were reported among the displaced population including children and adolescents. Many other children and adolescents victims of grave child rights violations are also reported such as abduction recruitment and risks of UXOs. Children released form armed forces/groups and other vulnerable children have little opportunities and are at risks of further violations to their rights unless their needs for reintegration with theri families and communities and civilian life is addressed.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The activities include provission of MHPSS services to children and their families, provision of FTR and alternative care services, prevention and response to GBV, UXOs risk education, prevention and reponse to recruitment of children and reingration of children released from armed forces/groups and establihsment of CBCPNs to engage with communities on prvention and reponse to grave child rights violation. UNICEF has long experience in working with the government and civil society partners on all the emergency issues faced by Sudan. UNICEF's response depend mainly on these partnerships and the child protection system that have established and supported during the past 15 years. So, FTR is already established with clear SOPs that identifies the roles and responsibilities of different partners. Social workers in all the states affected by conflict are well rained on FTR issues and the alternative care procedures. For MHPSS, UNICEF in collaboration with the NCCW has been working during the past years on the design and production of a PSS manual for children and adolescents affected by conflict. This manual has been approved and used widely in providing PSS to children and adolescents by trained social workers in all the states affected by conflict. UNICEF has also long experience in reintegration of children released from armed forces/groups. In collaboration with DDRC, a training guide for social workers on working with children recruited or associated with armed forces/groups was designed and used in the reintegration of more than 2500 children after the signing of the CPA. More than 100 social workers in the states affected by conflict have received training on how to do case management for children released form armed forces/groups. The same applies to UXOs where a community based MRE manual has been designed and currently used by community volunteers and social workers to provide risk education to affected communities. UNICEF has also supported NMAC and National Center for Curricula to develop a curricula for MRE for school children and has been used before in Kassala, Red Sea, AlGadarif, Blue Nile and South Krdofan states.
