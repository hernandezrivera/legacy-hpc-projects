Project Summary

HCO with more than 12 years of experience in providing protection-centric lifesaving interventions for persons of concern (returnees, asylum seekers, IDPs), it current partnership with UNHCR and UNICEF and NRC in the region will provide for continued service provisions during 2025.
Working with technical oversights from protection Cluster, Child Protection AoR, CMTF/UASC working Group, GBV Sub Cluster, EORE Working Group, MHPSS Working Group and the State Working Groups to deliver protection will address identified needs among targeted locations: Case management services to children including cash-based initiatives; Provision of family tracing and reunification for Unaccompanied and Separated children; Community-based mental health and psycho-social support to children, adolescents and caregivers; Conducting awareness-raising for women, men, girls and boys on GBV risks and available response services and timely reporting; specialized GBV response services and strengthening of local referral pathways; Protection monitoring (cross-boarder), key messages, and targeted durable solutions ie legal aid, civil documentation (including land housing and property), social reintegration and EORE is high risk locations.
HCO will ensure effective community feedback and complaint handling mechanisms and robust PSEA and social inclusion approaches.

1. Needs

Since independence and after signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. over 2.3 million displaced persons throughout South Sudan with conflict and natural disasters being the leading drivers of displacement (Protection Cluster Report June, 2024). Atypical seasonal changes (climate change) characterized with prolonged droughts or flooding, along with the loss of livestock and likelihoods, inter-communal violence, cattle raids, killings, and land conflicts among other causes of displacement, food insecurity and unspeakable sufferings for children and women at high risks of sexual violence, harassment, and domestic violence. Refugee and returnees new arrivals through long desperate journeys from Sudan often with nothing face the blunt of the suffering. According to South Sudan Country Refugee Response Plan (SSCRRP) 16 Oct 2024, the country hosts over 680,000 refugees and asylum - asylum seekers, predominantly from Sudan; and some 150,000 refugee (asylum seekers) and 192,000 returnee arrivals are anticipated in 2025. Our experiences with transit center in Abyei and border monitoring in both Abyei and Raja has identified complexities of needs including psychological distress, health, civil documentation, land, housing, food and employment among other critical services in areas of final and/or interim settlement. WBeG has received 60,945 returnees especially in Raja and Wau (urban), Abyei 167 refugees and asylum seekers and 7,000 in NBeG and Bor 55,350. 51,643 of IDPs due to fighting in WBeG, 7,500 in Abyei and 102,000 NBeG and Bor. It noted that all these areas still habour UXOs. Over 45,599 cases (21,507 girls and 24,092 boys) as unaccompanied, separated or missing and at-risk most vulnerable children have been registered as per to UASC report for caseload summary of 28th November 2024 Their suffering may worsen in 2025 should there be no responses . Due to the above needs and protracted protection issues, there is a need for continued Humanitarian Response in the targeted counties in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

Through this emergency funding Hold the Child will implement the project through the following outlined activities: a) CP Emergency activities Provide case management services to children (also through cash-based initiatives). Target 800 children (400 girls, 400 boys); Provide family tracing and reunification for Unaccompanied and Separated children who have received family tracing and reunification services. Target 150 children (75 girls, 75 boys); Provide community-based mental health and psychosocial support to children, adolescents and caregivers. Target 15,000 individuals; 10,000 children (5000 girls, 5000 boys); 5,000 adults (2,500 females, 2,500 males); Establish, strengthen and update Child Protection referral pathways. Target 5 referral pathways; Train (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff) on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). Target 200 adults (100 females, 100 males); b) GBV Emergency activities Conduct awareness-raising activities for women, men, girls and boys on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting. Target 15,000 individuals; 10,000 children (5,000 girls, 5,000 boys); 5,000 adults (2,500 females, 2,500 males); Support vulnerable women and girls with cash and voucher assistance. Target 200 (100 girls, 100 women); Distribute dignity kits to women and adolescent girls. Target 2,000 (1,000 girls, 1,000 women); Support individuals with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, PSS & WGFS programming). Target 250 individuals; Develop and strengthen functional GBV referral pathways in areas of operation. Target 5 referral pathways; Train non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response. Target 30 adults (15 females, 15 males); c) GP Emergency activities Monitor people upon arrival to SSD. Target 20,000 individuals. Conduct protection awareness-raising sessions for individuals with protection concerns or specific needs. Target 15,000 individuals; Identify and refer individuals with protection concerns or specific needs, through static or mobile means. Target 4,000 individuals; Conduct protection interventions intended for durable solutions (other than CP, HLP and GBV) including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and other protection support that can also be cash-based. Target 3,500 individuals; d) Regular EORE activities; Conduct EORE for affected individuals. Target 8,000 individuals; 5,000 children and 3,000 adults.
