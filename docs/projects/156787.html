Project Summary

IOM will contribute to the two objectives of the Shelter/NFI sector which are:
1) Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
2) Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
The focus will be on the provision of core and essential NFIs, provision of seasonal supplementary NFIs and shelter (including repair/rehabilitation of unfinished buildings, shelter support for returnees), in addition to training of stakeholders while ensuring mainstreamed monitoring activities and implementation through a protection lens. The overall target number of beneficiaries is 498,145 individuals reached with NFIs and 18,000 individuals supported with Shelter solutions.

Needs

Almost eight years into the crisis, substantial assistance is still needed especially in geographical areas that do not have proper access to markets, mainly northwest Syria. According to the HNO 2019, there are 4.4 million people in need of NFI, a 6.05% decrease from 2018, but the number of individuals reporting a critical severity level has increased in Idleb from 400,000 in 2018 to 411,000 in 2019. Therefore, NFI assistance is still needed in Syria, but a strategic geographical focus is more appropriate to the 2019 response so that to address the people with the highest level of need. Access to markets remains challenging mainly in Aleppo and Idleb sub-districts which could be due to the volatile and unstable environment with unexpected political shifts. In addition, according to HNAP, in 2018 around 1 million IDPs have been displaced to Idleb and around 870,000 IDPs displaced to Aleppo. Therefore, IOM will seek to ensure that contingency stocks are in place to address any emergency needs in case of sudden displacements in 2019. IOM has established partnerships with implementing partners in prepositioning stocks in strategic geographical locations within Syria. Also, IOM has an advantage in supplying a large number of NFIs through the rigorous procurement process already in place, and the competitive advantage to supply NFIs at a low cost due to the large quantities purchased. Furthermore, according to the Syria Shelter and NFI Assessment conducted by REACH in August 2018, the highest number of reported unaffordable items for IDPs are solar panels, batteries and clothes, therefore IOM NFI packages will continue to be flexible and tailored to the needs of vulnerable man, women, girls and boys. Winter clothes will also be one of the key seasonal NFI assistance that IOM will provide. According to 2019 HNO, 2.1 million people in need are in 1st and 2nd priority winter NFI locations, therefore with substantial assessments and targeting IOM will provide winter assistance to the most people in need based on the cluster approved vulnerability criteria with a specific focus on female and youth-headed households, households with high level of dependent minors, disabled and elderly so that to reduce negative coping strategies. A gender-sensitive approach will be applied taking into consideration the specific needs of each category to ensure participation, meaningful access and protection concerns are addressed during the life cycle of the projects. In addition, according to the OCHA Northeast Syria Situation Report No. 30, one of the main challenges in the cross-border operation from Turkey is the policy environment and scalability. Concerns are still directed at the space provided for humanitarian actors to operate in a politically sensitive environment that has been exacerbated by unexpected shifts of local authorities and interventions in humanitarian operations which hinders the effectiveness of assistance being delivered. Also, with scaling up of operations for NFI assistance, IOM is keen to also scale up the capacities of the implementing partners not only at management level in Turkey but also at the field level in Syria. According to HNO 2019, across Syria, there are over 4.7 million people in need of shelter assistance. The overall shelter absorption capacity in northwest Syria has been dramatically depleted since the end of 2017 and further compounded by the continued displacement in 2018. Due to the limited shelter supply and large numbers of IDPs, northwest Syria is experiencing severe overcrowding conditions. According to HNAP (Demographic Household Survey - Economic & Shelter report), 13.1% of total population in Idleb Governorate, and more than 8% in Aleppo Governorate are living in ‘critical shelters’: unfinished/abandoned buildings, self-settled camps, organized camps, formal collective centres or informal collective centres.

Implementation of activities

Through direct and continuous coordination and partnerships with Implementing Partners (IPs), SNFI Cluster and other stakeholders, IOM will be implementing the provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps), provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, winter-specific shelter upgrades) and training of stakeholders on resilience and quality oriented shelter/NFI skills and capacities. IOM will assess the needs of affected population through the targeting of IDPs, returnees and host communities by using the already established network of IPs, resources from different active cluster members and own IOM assessments conducted by third-party monitoring. In addition, IOM will establish partnerships with cluster members either through the already established SNFI Cluster contingency stock mechanism or through the selection of IPs that have been evaluated by a transparent and objective process. IOM’s rigorous M&E programming will ensure the accountability of the partnerships established to donors and beneficiaries. The basis will be the log frames that will be designed to direct monitoring activities that IOM conducts through third-party monitoring approaches and include, beneficiary selection based on vulnerability scoring, distribution and shipment, commodity tracking tool, post-distribution monitoring surveys and focus group discussions. Moreover, a continuously functioning hotline registering complaints and feedback on the assistance treated in a confidential and transparent is applied. In addition, IOM will develop training modules that include technical knowledge related to SNFI distributions, information management skills and tools, coordination guidelines, protection mainstreaming and GBV sensitization. For emergency rehabilitation, IOM will deploy Mobile Shelter Repair Teams to identify locations of displaced populations in need of shelter assistance with nearby building stock with non-structural damages that can either be upgraded or repaired. This will include unfinished buildings where the owners can be contacted and appropriate rent free agreements are identified. In coordination with CCCM teams, households will be targeted from differing situations including congested collective centres and camps, as well as those newly displaced or without shelter. Secondary displacement will be avoided unless the affected people express a desire to move and living conditions threaten health security, privacy and dignity. For returns and reconstruction, mobile teams will also identify both those owning and renting houses which are in need of repair. Repair processes will empower host communities’ contributions to IDP shelter needs. In line with HRP beneficiary prioritization, this response will aim to support: 1) People living in hard-to-reach areas, or in newly accessible areas, 2) IDPs living in last resort sites, collective centres, and/or living in open areas, 3) Newly-displaced populations, 4) Spontaneous/self-organized returnees, 5) Over-burdened communities. Shelter repair teams will conduct technical safety assessments on targeted shelters, verify HLP due to diligence requirements, and profile beneficiaries in cooperation with protection unit to determine priority and eligibility for the assistance based on the predetermined vulnerability. Repairs and upgrades will also contribute directly to improving WASH and housing infrastructure as part and parcel of the Shelter response. Projects will be monitored by IOM's M&E unit and contracted engineers. SOPs will be agreed with implementing partners and validation will be conducted at each project stage including location selection, selection of buildings for repair/rehabilitation, beneficiary selection, vendor selection, during construction and as PDM. This will be fed back to project staff and adjustments made as required.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

For NFIs: 1. Security risks that hinder access to target locations are beyond the project’s control. Assumptions are that access is still secured for cross-border shipments and within Syria. Mitigation measures include contingency plans, and pre-positioning capacities within Syria, in addition to context analysis through active participation in OCHA access working group and IOM’s internal mechanisms of context analysis. 2. Risk of not having beneficiaries participate in the project’s activities are also beyond IOM’s control. Assumptions are that beneficiaries are still willing and able to participate in the project. Mitigation measures are through the open communication lines available between stakeholders that will allow continuous flow of information in a transparent and confidential way when needed. In addition, IOM will ensure beneficiary participation at all stages of the project, involving all gender and age groups (women, men, boys and girls) as well as community members with specific vulnerabilities such as PWDs. The participation activities include needs assessments and post-distribution monitoring through interviews, focus group discussion and surveys, consolations on vulnerability criteria, consolations on the design of non-food item kits, participation in the distribution through crowd control and sensitization activities. For Shelter: Risks include general insecurity loss of access to project areas or local markets due to armed conflict. Armed actors may seek to influence project activities, or try to take credit for the activities. These factors are beyond the project's control. As above, the assumption is that access remains possible throughout project areas. Mitigation measures include regular communication with implementing partners to report any incidents of interference by groups. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will conduct prioritization exercises to determine alternative locations in coordination with the SNFI cluster and project donors. IOM will guide the implementing partner to present community mobilizing initiatives that will upgrade the sense of ownership and responsibility of maintenance of the implemented projects, reducing the risk of non-engagement of beneficiaries in project design and implementation.
