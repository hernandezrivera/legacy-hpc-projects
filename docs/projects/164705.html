Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 11.06 million, of which 4.6 million are in acute need. The above figure of popele in needincludes 6.1 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 341,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 480,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities.
More than 70% of the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria. Overall, 7.9 million of people are estimated to be food insecure (including 0.57 million severely food insecure) and 1.9 million people at risk of food insecurity.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 97,500 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 585,000 people) in Syria. About 15% of the targeted householders will be selected from those who receive food support to help them shift from depending on food assistance to producing their own food and enhancing their self sufficiency. The objective of the proposed action is to protect and enhance the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers and IDPs through the restoration of critical irrigation infrastructures to increase household food production and security. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Critical small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken.
• Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders strengthened.
A training programme on enhancing irrigation management, water use efficiency in small-scale irrigation, agricultural land management will be based on FAO standards and approaches. It is expected that stakeholders and farmers will join this training.
This project is expected to contribute to increasing access to irrigation water for crisis-affected farmers and their families in the targeted areas through the light rehabilitation of critical small scale irrigation infrastructure. The targeted areas will be selected based on rapid analysis based on the scale of needs and following the HNO needs mapping. The cultivation and other agricultural practices, including livestock and animal feed production, will consequently benefit from the water supply plans as an emergency response to crisis. The proposed intervention will strengthen household food production capacities and knowledge and address immediate food security and nutrition needs through increasing local availability and supply of food. This may in turn promote flourishing of agricultural based livelihoods in the rural community, which would contribute to social cohesion, peace and stability. The project seeks to utilize intensive labour in order to maximize community participation and inject cash into the local community.

4 - Needs

According to the 2020 HNO, 11.06 million Syrians will require humanitarian aid in the coming year, of which 7.9 million are food insecure. Before the crisis, small scale irrigation in Syria was done mainly using electric pumps powered by the grid or diesel engine-powered pumps. With the lack of electricity from the grid, farmers have been coping either by switching to diesel-operated pumps or buying small diesel generators that they use to produce electricity for their homes and to run electric water pumps. The reduced availability and high cost of diesel has forced farmers to reduce their use of diesel for pumping water.Farmers’ livelihoods and stability are greatly compromised by so many challenges that continue to place more pressure on their lives due to high costs of agricultural inputs, low availability of water for irrigation, disrupted economy, increased diesel prices and unsecured roads. Given the high cost of irrigation, damage to pumping stations, irrigation structures and equipment, unreliability of electricity and the poor availability of fuel, many farmers have abandoned irrigation and turned to predominantly rain fed production, which is prone to climate-related risks. Light rehabilitation of critical infrastructures to support crop production –especially wheat (the major staple) – has become an urgent concern that calls for immediate actions to save the lives and livelihoods of farmers. The top priority actions are to support farmers to get access to water for irrigation through light rehabilitation of critical small-scale irrigation infrastructure to support household-level food production to improve food security and nutrition. The light rehabilitation of critical damaged irrigation infrastructures and supporting small-scale irrigation projects while introducing efficient irrigation systems in affected areas of the country will be key to strengthening the resilience of farmers, IDPs, returnees and host communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Output 1: Critical small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken. Activities 1) Conduct field missions with the MoWR, GCWR, and MAAR to the main accessible irrigation projects in the targeted Governorates and assess the irrigation infrastructure damage and needs. 2) Agree with counterparts on a priority list for urgent interventions to rehabilitate critical irrigation infrastructures and introduce efficient irrigation systems.3) Development of detailed beneficiary selection criteria in consultation with key partners and stakeholders, including gender needs analysis; participatory identification of neediest and most vulnerable groups;4) Formalize, finalize agreement with pre selected implementing partner. 5) Rehabilitate the prioritized irrigation infrastructures.6) Monitor and evaluate the project activities in a timely and effective manner Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders strengthened. Activities:1) Prepare training programme and capacity building materials.2) Select beneficiaries upon developed criteria, giving priority to women-headed households and youth.3) Conduct a short training programme on good agricultural practices.4) Conduct a short, practical field training on project-relevant features, including: Practical support of installation and operation of drip irrigation systems and solar panels and practical support for operating and maintaining of small-scale irrigation systems.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiary selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example: IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish a community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement is supported. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible friction between the two groups. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways to express their concerns and grievances.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2.Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities, conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3.Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: likely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework, diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders) through more coordination with the hub in Damascus.4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas, and establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities.5.Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the period/area concerned. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Assistance is balanced and not directed only to one specific group, Identification of beneficiaries is done in partnership with local communities including IDPs and host communities.
