Project Summary

The ongoing escalation of conflict in various parts of Syria is causing a continuous high influx of IDP in various safer locations in Northern Syria. Majority of these IDP are living in the settlements, makeshift camps or formal camps. The existing water and sanitation system in Northern Syria are overstretched. With the huge influx of IDP maintaining existing WASH facilities and upgrading to cope with the increasing demands of at risk population is urgently required. This will ensure that people can full fill their basic water, sanitation and hygiene needs and risk of diseases and environmental degradation is minimized. Through the proposed project, IR Syria aims to support those who are in need for wash assistance. 88,000 people will benefit from improved access to water and sanitation facilities. The project is primarily designed based on Humanitarian Need overview 2019. The project will support the formal and informal settlements as well as well host communities with core WASH services.
The activities included in this proposal are in line with the WASH cluster objective which is support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria and deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to most vulnerable people. IRW prioritizes WASH support interventions for the conflict affected population in the target location to enhance beneficiaries’ resilience in coping with ongoing crisis through the provision of access to clean water as well as supporting them in cleaning the wash facilities in a clean and healthy environment. Below will be the intended activities to achieve the project objectives:
1. Adequate quality and quantity of water: Water supply will ensure through water trucking. IR will sign an agreement with a contractor to supply water to the targeted camps on daily basis (23 L per person will be provided on daily basis). This water will be sufficient to cover the drinking, bathing and household need of the individuals. If required by the targeted families, IR will also distribute Jerry cans. This will be contributed by IR.
2. Water testing and treatment (chlorination): IR team will conduct water testing and chlorination before it is available for use by the beneficiaries. This will help to eliminate the risk of using contaminated water.
3. Waste removal service (solid waste management): Through a service provider (contractor) IR will ensure to collect the garbage from the camps and deport it to the agreed damp sites on daily basis. This will improve the overall environment and decrease the risk of diseases spread. IR will also place garbage bins
4. Operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities: Sanitation facilities (latrines and bathing areas) will be gender segregated and will have protection measures this will be considered during the regular maintenance of the facilities (lockable doors, lighting of paths to facilities).

Needs

Despite some investment in water treatment systems and provision of water disinfectants the main WASH need across Syria remains access to sufficient quantities of safe water. Up to 55 per cent of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Results of free chlorine (FRC) testing are even more concerning and indicates that countrywide only 40 per cent of water received from networks have a sufficient level of FRC at the point of use (below both national drinking water and emergency standards of min. 0.2 mg/l). Average water consumption during 2018 amounted to an estimated 71 liters per capita per day. However, communities purchasing water exclusively from commercial water trucks received on average only 57 liters per capita per day, with at least 10 per cent of them receiving less than 30 liters per capita per day. Areas well served by free water from networks use far more than the average of 71 liters per day. Families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10 per cent of their income on water, with 3 per cent of the population spending more than 20 per cent of their income to purchase water and in extreme cases as high as 40 per cent (Areesha). The situation is even more difficult for people living in informal settlements, where families can spend more than 50 per cent of their incomes on water. Regarding sanitation, more than 99 per cent of the surveyed population has access to a functioning toilet. Nevertheless, this figure may give a false impression that the sanitation situation in Syria is satisfactory, while at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are either not functional or only partially functional leading to significant community-level health risks. Recent estimates indicate that only 9 per cent of the population is still served by functional wastewater treatment plants. Dysfunctionality of existing wastewater treatment plants and sewage networks contributing to the discharge of raw sewage, will continue to have a significant impact on water sources, soil and the overall water resource management. The WASH Household assessment indicates that at least 10 per cent of garbage is left in public areas, potentially contributing to the increase in leishmaniosis cases observed since the beginning of 2018 in some areas. The situation seems to be particularly dire for some areas with a high returns ratio, with 35 per cent of returnees reporting leaving garbage in the public areas. IDPs living in IDP sites are amongst the most vulnerable groups in Syria. In the northwest the scale of displacements to IDP sites witnessed since the beginning of 2018, has contributed to a deterioration of already overused WASH facilities and increased the need to scale-up operations and enhance the focus around more sustainable solutions (connection to existing water networks and/or establishment of simplified water and sewer networks). Access to WASH services has been identified as the number one priority by displaced people in 30 per cent of camps and collective centers, the number two priority in 40 per cent and the number three priority in 30 per cent. Results of the camp WASH assessment conducted both in northwest and northeast Syria show that in IDP camps and collective centers 16 per cent of IDPs spend more than 20 per cent of their income to purchase water, with this figure rising to over 50 per cent of income in some cases and also contributing to increase recourse to harmful coping strategies. Sanitation needs are also considerably higher among displaced people living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns linked with substandard conditions of sanitation facilities frequently reported.

Not enough water distribution points available so people have to wait for extended periods to get water, fights and harassment could occur; inequality in distribution. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk of theft of NFIs and chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups inside Syria.( IR will promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).2. Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. (MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.)3. Closure of Turkish border will hinder the access of some material and staff, we will look for other alternative to get the material from the Syrian market or coordinate with the Turkish authority to deliver the items through humanitarian border
