Project Summary

As the Anglophone crisis continues to deteriorate, more population are being displaced leading to an increasing population in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the recently conducted Cadre Harmonized Assessment (October 2019), an estimated 968,000 people in the NWSW regions are identified to be food insecure and considered to worsen as current assistance provided are at small scale compared to the fast-increasing needs. The current people in need identified under the physical and mental well-being humanitarian consequence is significantly higher than the 330,000 from last year which is mainly due to the deteriorating security situation with weeks of imposed lockdowns, lack of access to farmlands, population displacements and locations being inaccessible. The security situation remains fragile in the South West region and the IDPs population group in the bushes and inaccessible areas remain the prioritized group of people to receive assistance. Because host families especially those in relatively safe areas have been receiving frequent influx due to displacements, the host community population are also becoming more vulnerable. Markets are either non-functional or overstretched in some areas leaving in-kind assistance as the option in these areas however; cash/vouchers are being implemented in small scale.
The project aims at improving food security and nutritional status, in particular for most IDPs, through increased farm food production and promotion of personal livelihood diversification, based on community action and facilitated by relevant local stakeholders and civil society actors. As a result the project will base on providing emergency food assistance to improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations as well as providing self-reliance training on improved food crop, fishery and livestock production among IDPs and host communities. The project will also strive to enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds production for maize and cassava transformation facilitated by training and mentoring of selected groups and IDP families.
The project targets an estimated 18,000 IDPs particularly women, girls, the elderly and the poorest of the poor IDP families, living in Ndian, Fako, and Kupe Manenguba Divisions, South West Region of Cameroon.
Complain feedback mechanisms shall be integrated for as part of the review and planning processes as well as the monitoring and evaluation to fast track performance and success.
We will mainstream protection issues especially gender based violence as well as WASH and health related responses and referrals.

Besoins / Needs

Our project will be implemented in a way which respects communities and protects their well-being and safety – by identifying activities or behavior which are causing harm and taking appropriate action. This will be the basis for our integration of a simple complaint and feed back mechanism such as to enure uhholding of the integrity of our intervention such identifying situations in which assistance may be diverted for personal or political benefit in order to take appropriate action. Our complaint and feed back mechanism is aimed at ensuring transparency and accountability as well as improving project implemntation. Gender equality and women’s voice shall be highly supported – by identifying what is working and not working for women, men, boys and girls and providing opportunities for all to benefit through the project.Trust with community members is built and maintained – facilitating implementation and creating a solid relationship with the community upon which to intervene at a deeper level in the future.Actual and potential cases of sexual harassment, exploitation abuse shall also be identified and addressed. A feedback and complaint mechanism will also trigger action, influence decision-making at the appropriate level in the organization and/or prompt a referral to other relevant stakeholders if necessary and appropriate; provide a response back to the feedback or complaint provider and if appropriate, the wider community.
