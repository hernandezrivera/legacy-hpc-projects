Project Summary

This intervention aims to contribute to PC objective #1 (Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations) and PC objective #2 (Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence) through undertaking the following eligible activities:
(1) Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence, including grave violations against children and against ARA communities, in the Gaza Strip;
(2) Evidence generation and advocacy on the impact of violations on children including grave violations;
(3) Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers;
(4) Legal aid to support access to freedom of movement for patients in need of medical care outside Gaza; and
(5) Awareness raising on accessing legal aid in context of IHL/IHRL violations, including freedom of movement.
The project team will:
 Monitor and document over 1,000 cases of IHL/IHRL violations, including grave violation against children, in the Gaza Strip;
 Issue over 25 press releases addressing protection concerns and IHL/IHRL violations;
 Issue 1 report on children and armed conflicts;
 Issue 1 report on IHL/IHRL violations;
 Issue 12 monthly statistical updates on grave violation against children;
 Isuue12 monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL against civilian population;
 Answer over 50 request of sharing information from humanitarian community;
 Conduct 10 briefing to members of the international delegations in Gaza;
 Engage with UN HRC, SPs, and CoI ( including through submission of 1 written and 2 oral statements to HRC, 2 urgent appeals/communications to SPs, and provision of evidence to CoI)
 Provide legal representation to 350 patients (149 women, 149 men, and 52 children) delayed/ denied exit permit to secure to access hospitals outside Gaza; and
 Implement legal awareness sessions targeting 250 farmers, fishers, rural women and staff members of CBOs working in the ARA received legal awareness sessions on IHL/IHRL.
Through high-quality monitoring and documentation, legal counselling and interventions, and raising awareness of at-risk communities, the project strive to strengthen resilience of the most vulnerable people in need. Monitoring and documentation data as well as outcomes of legal interventions will inform advocacy messaging vis-à-vis international community with the aim of prompting high-level statements (by HC/UN/EU member states) voicing protection concerns brought to focus under this project.

1 - Needs

Another round of fighting engulfed the Gaza Strip in May 2021 that was marked by heavy bombardments, high rate of child fatalities, and large-scale damage to the costal enclave’s infrastructure. Over the course of eleven days of military escalation not seen in Gaza since 2014, Israel launched hundreds of airstrikes targeting civilian objects, including populated houses and residential complexes, as well as industrial and commercial entities, governmental offices, livestock farms and farmlands, banks, mosques, schools, and water, sanitation, and electricity infrastructure, and thousands of square meters of paved roads. In unprecedented tactic, long stretches of paved roads in Gaza City were heavily bombarded, meters away from residential buildings, allegedly aiming at crumbling Hamas’ underground tunnels. This approach led to one of deadliest nights of this escalation, in the early hours of 16 May, when bombardment of the main road leading to Gaza’s central hospital (Shifa Hospital) brought down multiple inhabited residential buildings killing at least 42 Palestinians, including 10 children and 16 women. This incident along with other recorded incidents over the course of the 11-day offensive raised concerns over Israel’s adherence to the International Humanitarian Law during military action, particularly principles of distinction, proportionality and necessity. The documentation carried out by Al Mezan and partner human rights organizations after cessation of hostilities indicate that 240 Palestinians were killed by Israeli forces over the course of the 11-day escalation. Of those, 151 were civilians (including 59 children and 38 women), 46 were combatants, and 43 were affiliated with Palestinian armed groups but were not partaking in hostilities when they were killed. Further, at least 634 children and 401 women were injured, over 1,500 housing units were destroyed and over 6,300 were damaged. 110 livestock and poultry farms, nine water wells, and over 4,200 dunums of cultivated land sustained damages. In the weeks following cessation of hostilities, Israel maintained additional punitive measures that had been imposed at the outset of the escalation whereby entry of fuel to Gaza’s power plan was blocked, leaving residents of Gaza to contend with six hours of electricity supply per day and also forcing wastewater treatment facilities to operate at limited capacity, resulting in daily dumping untreated or partially treated wastewater to the sea. The additional punitive measures also include additional restrictions on movement of people, including medical patients referred by the Palestinian Ministry of Health to medical facilities in the West Bank and Israel for medical care unavailable in Gaza. In the weeks following the escalation, Israel limited processing of exit-permit applications to life-saving, urgent cases and cancer patients before expanding the scope back to pre-May 2021 status in later months. Data published by the World Health Organization covering the first eight months of 2021 indicate that over a third (35%) of submitted patient requests for exit permits were either rejected or remained pending by the date of patient’s hospital appointment. This amounts to an average of 400 patients, including children, missing their medical appointments and treatment outside Gaza every month. Further, over half (58%) of patient companions’ applications (typically parents or other first-degree relatives applying to accompany children or severely ill patients) were not approved. Patients have no options but to book another appointment and re-apply for a permit, and their only accessible recourse is to resort to human rights organizations, including Al Mezan Center, to represent them before Israeli administrative bodies. Over the past three years, Al Mezan represented hundreds of referral patients each year who sought legal counseling after their application were declined or remained pending; Al Mezan subsequent interventions before Israeli administrative bodies secured exit permits for referral patients in 40% of represented cases. Access to effective legal remedies remains a challenge for Palestinian victims of ISF attacks in the oPt. During the course of the Great March of Return demonstrations, Al Mezan submitted over 80 criminal complaints to the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG), requesting criminal investigation into killing and injury of civilians (including killing of 19 children, and injury of 24 children and 3 women Injured). At the time of writing, 70 cases reaming pending (including 16 pertaining to killing of children and 25 pertaining to maiming of children and women), with only seven actively under investigation. Al Mezan lawyers will continue to follow-up on pending cases with the MAG office in 2021 and follow-up with the Israeli Attorney General office on a number of appeals against MAG’s decision to close certain cases without/after investigation without pressing any charges against perpetrators. In the face of this situation, Al Mezan continues to prioritize improving the protection against human rights and IHL violations in Gaza through high-quality monitoring and documentation, legal counselling and interventions, and raising awareness of at-risk communities to strength resilience and involve more people in challenging the serious patterns of human rights violations. Further, monitoring and documentation data as well as outcomes of legal interventions will inform advocacy messaging vis-à-vis international community to highlight the lack of genuine accountability.

2 - Activities

Activities under PC objectives #1:(1) Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence, including grave violations against children and against ARA communities, in the Gaza Strip. Fieldworkers will fully document (i.e. collected relevant data using questionnaire forms, as well as testimonies and supporting evidence) on all cases of fatalities, cases involving maiming of children and women, attacks against hospitals and schools, arrests and detentions, and damage to civilian properties. (2) Documentation data will feed into the database for analysis of violation trends and will be utilized to draft press releases, reports, factsheets, monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations and violations against children. The produced materials will be disseminated to over 700 professionals, media organizations, and NGOs through existing mailing list, and will also be shared via Al Mezan’s website and social media platforms. Data will also be used to answers requests for sharing information received on weekly basis from humanitarian actors working in oPt. (3) Field information on IHL/IHRL violations will also shape and feed into advocacy activities, including submissions and communications to UN mechanisms, particularly the Human Rights Council, the UN Special Procedures, and the UN HRC permanent Commission of Inquiry. Locally, representatives of Al Mezan will meet in Gaza with members of the international community to brief them on violation trends and protection concerns. Activities under PC objective #2: (4) Lawyers will continue to follow-up with the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG) and provide any requested material concerning 75 pending complaints submitted on behalf of casualties of the Great March of Return requesting criminal investigation, and appeal closed cases to the Israeli Attorney General. (5) Legal representation before Israeli administrative bodies for patients delayed/denied exit permits to access treatment outside Gaza. In case of child patients, intervention will also aim to secure exit permits for the child’s companion without whom the child cannot travel to their designated hospital. PwDs in need of legal services will be visited at home by the lawyer to provide legal counseling and sign documents relevant to legal intervention. (6) Awareness raising sessions for ARA communities, particularly farmers and fishers, on protection of civilians in armed conflicts, legal mechanisms to seek protection and redress, and available legal aid and how to access it. Supplementary activities to ensure staff and beneficiaries are both aware of the Center’s policies on Child Safeguarding and PSEA: (7) Al Mezan’s policies on Child Safeguarding (CSG) and PSEA are finalized and shared with staff prior to the start of 2022, followed by training of all staff members on said policies, including reporting mechanisms. (8) Signed copies of both CSG and PSEA policies by every staff member are enclosed to their contracts and deemed legally binding. (9) beneficiaries are made aware of PSEA reporting mechanisms through awareness-raising material (containing contact details of the PSEA focal point) posted regularly on the Center’s social media account, with a reach of over 10 thousand followers. Potential risks and mitigation measures:Social Risk: low engagement from victims and/or their families with fieldwork team conducting after-the-fact documentation. Mitigation measures: Al Mezan has decades of experience in documentation violations and our fieldworkers/researcher are well known within their communities, rendering risk of low engagement minimal. We will mitigate the risk of low engagement from target communities by increase its reach out efforts and put more focus on discussing the aims and expected outcomes of our work, highlight its vital contribution to protection of civilian and fighting impunity. Risk to staff well-being: particularly those working directly with victims of human rights violations. Mitigation measures: Implementing partners took necessary steps to adopting measures to address staff well-being and psychological issues resulting from constant exposure to crises and trauma. This includes sessions delivered by mental health on stress management techniques. COVID-19 risk mitigation measures:Visible notice posted at the entrances of Al Mezan’s branches instructing all visiting beneficiaries to wear face masks and avoid physical contact with staff. In addition, hand sanitizers have been placed around the office and at the reception for visiting beneficiaries to use. Similarly, staff are instructed to abide by the same rules. When feasible, beneficiaries are encouraged to send supporting evidence needed for legal interventions via email or WhatApp instead of handing-in hard copies at our offices. Participants in awareness sessions will adhere to safe distances when attending and, when possible, will be held in open-air spaces. Sanitizers and face mask will be made available to all participants. HRP and FTS:Al Mezan will report on relevant indicators to HRP periodic reporting and will also disclose financial contribution towards the proposed intervention on the FTS.

Monitoring and Evaluation: The M&E plan will include the project outputs, the related indicators, data collection method and tools, roles and responsibilities, and frequency of data collection, analysis, and reporting. Progress and results-based monitoring will be carried out at different levels and by applying different monitoring tools as follows: M&E of monitoring and documentation of IHL/IHRL violations:Head of Fieldwork Units will be in constant contact with fieldworkers out in the field. An electronic database will serve as the primary tool to track progress of documentation of violations, and to ensure all relevant incidents are fully documented. Initially, monitored/reported incidents of violations are compiled on daily basis in an electronic record that contains preliminary data. Fieldworkers are deployed to verify and document the reported incidents using designated questionnaire forms; their documentation is fed into the human rights violations database. At the end of each week, the record of monitored/reported incidents is compared to the record of documented incidents, any identified gaps are reported by the data entry officer and database administrator to the fieldwork coordinator ahead of his/her weekly meeting with fieldworkers to devise a plan to remedy any issues hampering timely documentation of violations. They also plan their deployment plan for the coming days based on lesson learned and trends of violations identified over the course of the previous week. M&E of legal aid:In case of provision of assistance and interventions to referral patients and their companions, an electronic database is utilized to keep track of the beneficiaries, their due hospital appointments, and the result of the intervention. Each beneficiary is assigned a record that contains all the communications and measures taken vis-à-vis the duty bearers, including scanned copies of said communications. Records relevant to this action are clearly designated as such in the database to avoid overlap and duplication of service with other projects. M&E of information sharing, publication and dissemination:Requests of sharing information received from humanitarian community in the oPt are recorded and processed by data entry officer, with answered requests earmarked as such on daily basis. Publication are drafted by researcher and reviewed by coordinator/program director before publication. Efficiency of dissemination of publications is evaluated by information/database officer by reviewing stats relevant to views and interactions on social media, clicks on webpages, re-publication of statements and reports on media outlets’ websites, and articles quoting or citing project’s published material. M&E of advocacy activities:Evaluation of interaction with UN special procedure is gauged through regular monitoring of relevant SP websites for issued statements and reports that reflect concerns raised by implementing organizations through communications/urgent appeals to SPs. Additionally, SP communications to Israel on issues and concerns raised by implementing organizations as well as the State’s response are regularly recorded from the web-portal (https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/). Assessment of advocacy vis-a-vis HRC is gauged by reviewing adopted resolutions at the end of each regular session which are relevant to the oPt and adopts messaging and asks put forth by civil society active in the oPt, including Palestinian human rights organizations. M&E of community capacity building:Training activities utilize oral and written evaluations/feedback after training, and pre-training and post-training tests to precisely measures learning, whereas awareness-raising sessions will use satisfaction evaluation at of each session, and in cases of illiterate groups, a verbal evaluation of satisfaction will be conducted. Program Evaluation: Al Mezan organizes at least one evaluation session annually (in January) whereby a gender balanced group of beneficiaries is invited to participate in focus group discussions to elicit their feedback for Al Mezan’s annual evaluation process. Al Mezan’s training staff is responsible for collection of data with support from internal focal points, including the fundraising officer and project coordinator. Data is analyzed and used for learning, planning and reporting.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Given the fact that affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation, their involvement starts at an early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Further, victims of violations are referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to discuss their legal options. Legal services are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle. The recipients of legal services are informed of possible ramifications of the legal actions, and it will be made clear that the final decisions to initiate any legal action is theirs to take. Al Mezan's code of conduct, read and signed by all staff members, sets clear guidelines for service providers on dealing with beneficiaries, particularly children who in case of being the precipitant of any service must be accompanied by their legal guardian at all times.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Monitoring of IHL/IHRL violations will encompass all affected communities in the Gaza Strip (children, women, men) while documentation work will cover all cases of killings and priorities documenting cases of injuries for these groups in the following order: children; women; PwDs; medical personnel; and journalists (during documentation of injuries, cases with severe to moderate injuries will take precedence over cases with below-moderate injuries).During monitoring and documentation action, victims who are identified by fieldworkers as potential beneficiaries of legal intervention are refereed to lawyers. In circumstances were victim's injures or condition (PWD) hinders his/her ability to reach Al Mezan's offices, lawyers will arrange a home visit to provide legal aid, take affidavits, and sign power of attorney.Information, statistics and trend analyses based on monitoring and documentations work are shared with the public and stakeholders on regular basis through implementing partner's websites, social media accounts and mailing lists. Printed materials are disseminated free of charge and accessible for all those interested at implementing partners’ multiple offices in the Gaza Strip.
