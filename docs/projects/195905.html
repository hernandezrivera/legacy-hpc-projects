Project Summary

BRAC Child Protection (CP) sector has been implementing child protection interventions since 2017 in refugee camps and host communities across Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar. The programme contributes to strengthening community-based child protection mechanisms in close collaboration with the community people. BRAC will operate these activities in 11 camps and 5 unions of the host community area.
Toward 2023, BRAC’s Child Protection sector aims to strengthen the child protection system and services across the camps and host communities. The programme will ensure the continuation of existing services, e.g. unstructured psychosocial support (PSS), structured PSS, case management services, adolescent support activities e.g. life skills-based sessions, set-up adolescent clubs, parenting session, community-based child protection mechanism, e.g. Community-based child protection committee (CBCPC), awareness message, early childhood development (ECD) activities, social cohesion activities, and maintain sectoral coordination with a priority focus on integrated services for children with disabilities. In every component of the CP programme, children with disabilities will be supported with the services mentioned above. Referral support for children and adolescents, including children with disabilities, will be strengthened to ensure timely support. The programme will take the initiative to ensure all the facilities are safe from disaster. An emergency response team with improved knowledge will be established to respond to any emergency. BRAC will continue strong coordination with the child protection sub-sector (CPSS), case management technical working group (CMTWG), and CPSS localisation working group by attending the meeting and workshops and providing technical support where it is required. BRAC CP will also coordinate with the protection working group (PWG), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) Working Group, Age & disability working group, ECD Working Group and Youth Working Group through active participation and contribution as well as with the government, e.g. department of social service. BRAC will make sectoral inputs to the situation report, 5W report, and need-based report.
BRAC committedly applies it’s Safeguarding Policy, CP Code of Conduct and ensuring the staff's safeguarding, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) related cases. The minimum standard for child protection in humanitarian action (CPMS) guidelines will be followed while implementing the interventions to ensure “Do no Harm”. The programme will engage the adolescents in various activities through skills development and dialogue within the community among a range of activities; BRAC intends to promote empowerment in the communities that it works.
The Child Protection programme will actively consider the emergency preparedness and disaster risk reduction (DRR) issues during the monsoon season and develop contingency plans whereby emergency child and caregiver information centres are operational during disasters. In case of any child or adolescent needs special care during an emergency or crisis, one-to-one counselling at these centres will be available. Regular assessment-based precautions will be taken to address the DRR issues. The complaint feedback response mechanism (CFRM) will be in general focus for all the child protection interventions to ensure accountability to affected populations (AAP) developed by CPSS.
BRAC has considered the most vulnerable population in this programme and prioritised safety, dignity and meaningful access and participation of children adolescent and community to ensure the integrity of the programme. The programme will target 148,793 (58% female) children, adolescents, community members, and stakeholders. Though the programme will not have any cash programming, but will deploy community volunteers along with staff to ensure quality services and these volunteers will get remuneration.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Among 945,953 Rohingya people living in Cox’s Bazar and Bhasan Char, 52% are children who are mostly dependent on humanitarian support (UNHCR-Govt. Population factsheet September 2022). Children’s concerns about their safety in the camps have been affecting their access to basic services and encouraged unhealthy coping techniques since the beginning of the influx. Children living in Cox’s Bazar (camps and host communities) are still facing diversified protection risks and challenges that reflect their different vulnerabilities, needs, strengths, and capacities depending on a number of intersecting factors, such as age, gender, disability, and other relevant diversity factors. According to the “Bangladesh: Child Protection Assessment - Rohingya Refugee Response” report conducted in May 2020 (Source: Protection Cluster, REACH and UNHCR, May 2020), many child protection concerns have existed in camps and host communities including Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%) and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse of the child (23%). Mental health and psychosocial distress is also important child protection concern in this setting. Unaccompanied and separated children need special attention as they are more prone to different forms of protection concerns Rohingya households reported that at least one child is engaged in child labour. This is also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children are involved in hazardous work. COVID-19 affected the child protection scenario differently. Covid-19 and the accompanying containment and risk mitigation measures reduced children’s exposure to risks outside the camps but increased their exposure to the risks inherent in daily life in the camps. The ensuing economic hardship for households contributed to the increase in child marriage for girls, child labour, and child trafficking (Protection Cluster 27/01/2022). The scenario is almost the same for the host communities as well. The situation of the camps is known to generate anxiety, aggression, depression and various sorts of psychosocial stress which need to be addressed and treated properly through a more comprehensive and structured Mental Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS). Younger children below 4 years are out of service and there is a lack of sufficient ECD programmes for the children. On the other hand, the limited scope of pre-vocational skills-related activities for adolescents has also exacerbated this problem. Social cohesion needs to be ensured to decrease different forms of tension between the host community and the Rohingya people. The programme will, therefore, adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18 years old children and adolescents and above 18 years (both boys and girls in different camps and host communities) will be reached with the services as well. A holistic approach will be applied to provide services e.g. case management, structured psycho-social support, early childhood development, life skills, pre-vocational skills and service provision for children with disabilities. It will also engage community people and other important stakeholders to strengthen the overall child protection mechanism.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Through ISCG, the Child Protection Sub-sector (CPSS) has already initiated the rationalisation process. To comply with the rationalisation process, BRAC has been adopting an integration approach so that child protection services can be enhanced with the multi-sectoral camp-based activities. BRAC has already received the allocation of camp through rationalisation process where BRAC will be working in 11 camps. In this regard, BRAC will adopt integrated protection services including an early childhood development programme. Referring to the Rationalisation principle (principle 3), a holistic approach will be taken to ensure all child protection services to children and adolescents. BRAC has a solid fundraising team to work for funding opportunities and as a result, BRAC has confirmed funds from other non-UN donors which will be utilised to continue the service.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The Child Protection programme will closely work with the general protection and Gender-based Violence (GBV) to ensure the relevant services for the children. GBV-related cases received under the Child Protection programme will be referred to the respective sectors (e.g., MHPSS, GBV). BRAC Child Protection sector will also provide orientation to the Community-based child protection committee (CBCPC) on GBV. BRAC will also adopt protection principle while implementation of the programme. As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended adverse effects of any programme.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC recognises that women, girls, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are disproportionately at risk and most vulnerable during a crisis and need particular interventions directed towards them. Through the programme, BRAC aims to reach 58% of female of total target. Under the Child Protection programme, gender-responsive services and structures will be established to address children’s and women's gender-based protection concerns, considering a survivor-centric approach (safety, confidentiality, respect, and non-discrimination). These activities will sensitise them to express their ideas and to be conscious of their rights. GBV prevention and gender responsiveness are likely to create an understanding of equal participation and fair distribution of benefits among men and women. Available services and information will be ensured in an equal and gender-responsive manner.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

During the programme designing phase, as well as the monitoring phase, BRAC carefully checks that the gender, age, and disability elements are incorporated. As stated in its Safeguarding Policy, BRAC protects all its employees, participants, and beneficiaries of its programmes and projects, including humanitarian ones, against abuse, that is, against sexual harassment, bullying, discrimination, intimidation and violence, neglect, and exploitation. As a part of the commitment to safeguarding, BRAC will ensure that all staff receive training and are aware of organisational expectations and policies. The programme has been designed for integrated service for children and adolescents aged up to 18 years. But, from 19-24 years, youth will also be part of this programme for social cohesion and skills training. BRAC will continue a closed coordination and participation in Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) as well as with CPSS Disability Steering Group.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC always believes in diversity in the project or workplace. A wide variety of activities will be conducted with the support of a diversified group of people. BRAC will remain actively engaged in ensuring the equal participation and fair distribution of humanitarian assistance without contributing to discrimination, abuse, neglect or exploitation of any population group, with a specific focus on gender equality and disability inclusion. Therefore, BRAC emphasises Gender and Sexual Orientation diversity in its programme design. The Child Protection programme will be composed of diversified communities, e.g. Rohingya communities, host communities, and ethnic communities. Both girls and boys children, adolescents, youth, and adults, including Persons with disability, will have access to avail the services equally.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

While designing humanitarian projects, BRAC considers UNHCR’s AAP framework- i) participation and inclusion; ii) communication and transparency; iii) feedback and response; and iv) organisational learning and adaptation. In the designing phase, BRAC’ Child Protection team will ensure community participation through consultations. BRAC has a specific Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to address specific needs of the Rohingya refugees. It will collect and analyze gender, age, and disability-disaggregated data, and conduct participatory gender analysis to understand various needs, capacities, barriers, and aspirations. Child Protection team will regularly analyses the feedback/trends/unresolved issues and links with respective sectors for feedback referral. Based on the community needs, interventions, planning and priority of the programme can be modified. Following the BRAC’s guidelines (with technical support from CPSS), feedback and complaints will be addressed.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC has an Emergency Operation Centre and Emergency Response Team with representatives from all sectors to respond in an integrated way in emergencies. During environmental emergencies and man-made or natural hazards, all sectors jointly work in the affected areas by conducting primary assessments. The DRR sector provides essential Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training to our programme staff, including community volunteers, to respond effectively to an emergency crisis. Person with disabilities will be identified and ensured they are evacuated through the ERT if there is any issues raised during the disaster. Hence, the Child Protection programme will take guidance from the response team. On the other hand, BRAC child protection sector will take care of environment planting tees in the child protection facilties. Children and adolescent will also be oriented on environment and climate change which can have an understanding to protect the environment.
