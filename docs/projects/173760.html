Project Summary

This proposal aims to meet the urgent health needs in the increasingly unstable southwest region of Cameroon, through the provision of emergency primary health care (PHC) services integrating MHCP to populations affected by the crisis and particularly children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)
Through this project, AAH aims to consolidate the achievements of previous projects by taking into account experiences to adapt the approach. A mobile clinic will be deployed to provide health, nutrition and mental health services to those affected by the conflict in Meme's division. The reduction of our intervention mainly to the only division aims to concentrate our efforts in this division for more impact on the beneficiaries. AAH will also integrate into its intervention support for functional private structures and the implementation of a community approach such as ICCM + with the aim of strengthening local capacities and thus hope to perpetuate the activities of the project. The targeted division is not fixed. The mobile clinic can be deployed on other sites to better meet the needs according to the changing context.
The proposed package will be improved with the integration of an MHPSS component to take care of the most distressed and vulnerable people with a particular focus on women and children.
The package of care will also include medical care for rape victims, especially women and children, and referral to appropriate facilities for those whose post-rape condition requires further in-depth care, including, for example, social reintegration.
This project will target all people affected by the crisis: men, women, PLWs, children aged 0-5 and above, adolescents, IDPs, host populations, people living with a disability, the elderly, health staff from targeted private health centers and community actors
AAH will target Kumba, Mbonge, Muyuka, Buea and Konye health districts.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In this context, access to affordable healthcare has steadily shrunk. The flight of qualified medical personnel from conflict-ravaged areas coupled with the reduction in physical access to more remote locations led to the progressive closure of primary healthcare facilities. The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic in early 2020 has further compounded the situation. In addition, the ongoing conflict is also likely to have a detrimental effect not only on children’s psychological conditions, but also on their caregivers’ mental wellbeing. Violence and displacementq impact social fabric and weaken resilience at individual, family and community levels. Mapping and referrals mechanisms with specific actors will be enhanced to cover specific needs. AAH teams will offer psychological support groups for people in psychological distress distress. The approach will take into account the local culture and context. Gender exclusive group can be offered for participants to feel more comfortable. The number of participants will be limited for our group activities to ensure appropriate COVID-19 measures.Given the specific target groups of AAH’s activities, the existing mobile clinic teams comprise mostly of female staff to ensure potential beneficiaries feel comfortable accessing medical services provided in the field, first and foremost sexual and reproductive health services. A gender-sensitive approach will also inform as much as possible the recruitment of community health volunteers and community mobilizers in the early stage of the project. Recruited volunteers and mobilizers will then be inducted to the AAH Code of Conduct and PSEA Policy, placing great emphasis on the non-discriminatory and non-exploitative nature of aid.Specific attention will be paid to young people accompanying children and our teams will ensure access to people living a disability. Awareness-raising on IYCF and care practices will also incorporate messages promoting gender equality, while MHCP activities and community mobilizers’ health sensitization will include GBV and PLWDs themes.
