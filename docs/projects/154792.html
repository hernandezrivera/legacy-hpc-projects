Project Summary

The proposal presents the UNHCR concept note on the Provision of Shelter and Non Food Items to vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host Community families in Iraq. The proposal considers the humanitarian needs in shelter and NFI recently assessed in the country by the IOM-DTM and MCNA VI. It is also built on the UNHCR operation plan for 2019 and the SNFI cluster HRP target for 2019 (347,750 for shelter; 513,900 for NFIs). The UNHCR intervention will be strongly based on vulnerability considerations to make sure that the Vulnerable IDPs living in camps and the highly vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members living outside camps will receive due priority attention.
The concrete activities outlined under the proposal relate to the provision of emergency and transitional shelters, maintenance of camp infrastructures, provision of cash for shelter to repair damaged shelters (WDS) and distributions of NFIs to vulnerable families. UNHCR will continue to use its strong assessment and monitoring tools to guaranty full accountability to beneficiaries up to the humanitarian recommended standards. UNHCR will work with strategic partners including the Government institutions (MoDM, JCCs, etc.) to ensure a smooth handover and nationalization of the IDP/Returnee assistance as the emergency gets protracted.

Needs

Since early 2014, Iraq has experienced its most violent years in decades. In 2018, it is estimated that 2 million IDPs, approximatively 511,000 of these IDPs are hosted across 133 camps. The large number of IDPs has increased pressures on local services, including schools, water, and sanitation. The end of military operations in December 2017 created improvements in the security of affected governorates and enabled the return of 800,000 people in 2018, leaving some 1.5 million people in displacement, to date, who will continue to be in need of protection, and assistance. The main cited obstacles of return are the insecurity, widespread destruction and limited availability of services in the area of return, Housing, Land and Property (HLP) and documentation issues, on-going communal tensions and the lack of governmental funds for compensation claims. During the year 2018, several assessments of the situation on the ground, including analysis of the recent REACH multi-cluster needs assessment and IOM data, has indicated that there is still a great need for humanitarian assistance. Large numbers of IDPs remain in Ninewa (589,578), Dohuk (349,776), Kirkuk (120,798) and Salah al-Din (163,812), with tens of thousands returning to these governorates.Regarding the shelter and NFIs aspects, the scale, severity of needs remain significant until the end of 2018. According to MCNA 4 (Reach assessment), of the IDPs who do not intend to return to areas of origin within the next year, 47 percent cite damage to property as the primary reason to remain in displacement. So far, the post-crisis response has been focused on supporting essential services, private housing, especially in urban locations (e.g., Mosul) to the detriment of other affected regions, including rural areas, where poverty rates are higher. It is also clear that certain groups living on camps (new arrivals, vulnerable IDPs) and outside camps (highly vulnerable IDPs) may need NFI distribution throughout the year. In addition to the analysis above, the SNFI cluster revealed that only 10% of the target (191,463 people) are reached by shelter intervention and that 55% of target (1, 029, 253 people) are reached by NFIs intervention as of 31 October 2018, leaving the vast majority of those who have not been reached to remain in need. Based on the IOM-DTM and MCNA VI data, the SNFI cluster has determined a total people in need at 2,093,926 people, with an overall target of 861,650 (Shelter: 347,750, NFIs: 513,900) for 2019. The target is broken down into 530,150 IDPs, 324,000 returnees and 7,500 host community members. Winter assistance will still be needed for the most vulnerable IDPs leaving in camps, and highly vulnerable IDPs living outside camps and the scale of this assistance will be determined in line with the overall winter needs analysis of the cluster.

Activities

The UNHCR programme for 2019 aims to address these needs in parallel with the agency ‘’responsible disengagement’’ with the IDP population group in the years to come while supporting pore engagement of the Government counterparts. The UNHCR Shelter Programme in 2019 will be strongly based on vulnerability and aligned with the strategic objectives of the SNFI cluster approved by the Shelter Cluster SAG. In particular, for the specific shelter repair project, UNHCR will ask its partners to use the socio-economic vulnerability assessment tool of the Cash Working Group in non-camp settings.UNHCR will focus on the following activities: Camps settings- For Vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps and new arrivals: Provision of tents, replacement of tents and upgrading tent plots, distribution of NFIs (replenishment) and winter top-up for IDPs already living in camps. The replenishment will be necessary due to damage or destroyed due to prolonged use.- For all camp population: Maintenance or upgrading of camp infrastructures (including electricity grid, roads, drainage channels, fences, and other infrastructure). The care and maintenance of camps will also touch the IDPs living in collective centers.Non-camp settings - For highly vulnerable IDPs out of camps, unwilling to return: Distribution of NFI including basic NFI in kind or through cash-based interventions.- For highly vulnerable returnees, host community and non-displaced affected by the conflict: Distributions of NFI in kind or through cash-based interventions. - For highly vulnerable IDPs out of camps living in critical shelter conditions: emergency shelter repair such as the provision of SOKs will be provided to them.- For highly vulnerable returnees living in War Damaged Shelters: cash-for shelter for category 2 and 3, or provision of low-cost transitional shelter (RHUs) for category 3 and 4. - Winter top up in cash will be envisaged for out of camp IDP population. Due to foreseen financial gaps, winter assistance will not be provided to returnee families in 2019. Cash-for-shelter (CFS) is a particularly strong tool in urban settings, where there are viable market and banking systems already in place, which is the case in many cities in Iraq. Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection concerns include forced return, evictions, limited access to services and marginalization targeting some minorities (Like the Yezidis). It is expected that the security situation will remain volatile with extremist groups attempting destabilization. IDPs returning to the places of origin may face secondary displacement due to security instability and lack of resources. Security threats cover other aspects like contamination of areas IED/ERW, which might hinder the shelter activities. Locations in which shelter wish to intervene are not cleared of explosive remnants of war (ERW).One of the biggest risks that most IDPs and returnee families may face with is the Socio-economic vulnerability, if adequate assistance is not provided to them, and forcing them to remain in substandard living conditions. These vulnerable groups include female-headed households, families with low income and persons with disabilities, those with reduced mobility living in informal settlements and unfinished, abandoned or damaged buildings. Another protection risk may be related to Housing-Land and Property issues (HLP). HLP issues exist and include tenure discrimination leading to inequitable assistance; loss of legal documentation; access to land for shelter, land and property conflicts; forced evictions; secondary occupation.

Mitigation Measures

UNHCR leads the National Shelter cluster and ensures that all its shelter interventions follow the Due Diligence Guidelines developed and ensures the “Do No Harm” principles. UNHCR will continue to mainstream vulnerable and marginalized groups’ issues and integrate the HLP concerns in the early planning phase of projects, and appropriate referral did timely. In cash for shelter repair projects, UNHCR and partners will seek to understand the housing situation through the damage assessment guidelines led down by the S-NFI cluster. Construction site health and safety rules will be signed by the contractors to ensure the safety of the workforce where possible. Construction site risk assessments will be done to identify risks and strive to mitigate them through careful planning in awareness by putting female target groups at the fore front of the awareness campaigns. Where possible, field teams will include male and female members to ensure participation and leadership opportunities for women, also to have opportunities to reach all beneficiaries by removing the cultural barriers. If necessary, removal and disposal of debris as well as clearance of unexploded devices will be completed in collaboration with the Government to ensure that the plots are cleared before any project activities are commenced. The limited humanitarian and development assistance to cover the needs might hinder an adequate humanitarian response. With some multi-year grants concluding in 2018, the UNHCR operation will proactively engage with donors to underscore the necessity of continued support, including looking to non-traditional sources of funding to reduce the Socio-economic vulnerability of the target group.

How is your project accountable to affected population

UNHCR strives to be accountable to those whom we assist. UNHCR has various tools to monitor the accountability vis-à-vis population of concern. The accountability framework provides a simple checkbox format to gauge progress towards four main equality objectives: AGDM, protection of women and girls, protection of children and, response to and prevention of SGBV. UNHCR is also committed to the Accountability to affected populations (AAP). In particular, mainstreaming SGBV will be dining in the programming and response of NFI and Shelter interventions Beneficiary Communication, Feedback Complaint and Response Mechanism developed will also be used continuously to improve the shelter and NFIs response. UNHCR will continue to closely coordinate with IDP Call canter to ensure UNHCR’s beneficiaries reported issues, difficulties, challenges are addressed and appropriate referral made. For the shelter technical implementation, UNHCR will strengthen partners to systematically report achievements, using the agreed tools (activity info, UN-Habitat housing reporting tool) for more transparency and accountability to the affected population. Compliance spot checks will be used to ensure that designs meet the shelter cluster standards. The Post distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys will be pursued with beneficiaries to provide real-time information on the effectiveness of the assistance about beneficiary satisfaction, registration, and distribution process. As a protection agency, UNHCR will continue to comply with the Centrality of Protection and the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability. It will also encourage partners to do so. UNHCR will continue to place the protection at the center of the shelter and NFIs response and mainstream protection principles with the aim to ensure that the protective impact of response is maximized. In practical term, bearing in minds that the primary responsibility to protect people in such situations lies with the State, UNHCR will emphasize on identifying who is at risk, how and why to take into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and People With Disabilities (PWDs).
