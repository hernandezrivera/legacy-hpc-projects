Project Summary

This project covers WASH humanitarian needs of 140,000 conflict-affected population who have returned to their places of origin while living in humanitarian situation. The main objectives of the project are:
• Support efforts to improve access to safe drinking water benefiting 100,000 people;
• Reaching 90,000 returnees through provision of safe access to sanitation;
• Approximately 140,000 individuals receive critical WASH-related information to prevent child illness, especially diarrhea;
• Provision/ rehabilitation of the WASH facilities in 30 (thirty) schools and six health facilities in the focus areas.

Needs

WASH response in any humanitarian emergency forms part of life saving interventions and due to the dynamic nature of the complex emergency in merged districts, WASH interventions are expected to remain a priority area of response throughout 2019. In 2017-18 most of the displaced population returned to their places of origin in different areas of the merged districts. Due to continued insurgency the water supply systems were not maintained and as such became nonfunctional. With the return of IDPs to their places of origin, the provision of water and sanitation remained a challenge. Thus, making the population particularly the children and women more vulnerable to water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases. Recently, a vulnerability assessment has been conducted in merged districts with the following WASH related findings:• Only 52% of households have access to drinking water from a protected source like hand pump, protected well or tap water• Thirty three percent (33%) of the population don’t have access to latrines and practicing open defecation.• Only fifty eight percent (58%) of boy’s schools having functional toilets • Safe drinking water at school is available to only 56% of boys’ schools and 53% of girls’ schools. As per secondary information collected from government counterparts and other stakeholders, in certain areas of South Waziristan and North Waziristan, most of Government and community owned Water Supply Schemes (WSS) are still nonfunctional which need immediate rehabilitation. The returnees are fetching water from rivers and streams. Access to sanitation facilities is very limited. Open defecation is being practiced very commonly. As result of non-availability of sanitation facilities and the existing local norms, men use to defecate in open. Lack of hygiene related awareness and knowledge was also reported. Due to prolonged militancy in the area, the existing WASH facilities in schools and health facilities are also damaged and nonfunctional and require immediate rehabilitation to ensure school attendance and essential healthcare.

Activities

• Rehabilitation of non-functional drinking water systems, both public and community owned and operated; • Provision of hand pumps, extension of any existing supply system where required. • Support provision of safe water and sanitation in schools, basic health units and communal facilities; • Support community-based hygiene promotion using multiple communication methods targeting men, women and children. All activities will be designed and carried out with due regard to gender, DRR, vulnerability, cultural sensitivities & practices and environmental considerations.
