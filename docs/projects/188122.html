Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements food security interventions targeting the most vulnerable households with a focus on those female-headed, with pregnant and lactating women, with children under 5, and with disabled to enhance their nutritional status.
The agricultural livelihoods of vulnerable smallholder farmers and livestock breeders have been significantly impacted by more than 10 years of armed conflict in Syria. This has been compounded by the current unprecedented hyperinflation. Furthermore, the recent drought that has led to a notable decrease (in some cases zero harvests) of crops, which has resulted in an increase in food insecurity and adoption of negative coping strategies. AAH will target vulnerable smallholder farmers and livestock breeders to increase their self-resilience by supporting their agricultural livelihoods in 2022 and 2023 through distributing basic agricultural inputs and the provision of vocational training. The distribution will include high-quality cereal and legume seeds as requested by the communities with a crucial focus on rainfed, drought-tolerant varieties. The quantity of seed distributed will be sufficient to plant 10 donums of land in the winter planting season as per the recommendation of the Food Security and Agriculture Sector (200 kg of seed barley for cereal farmers and 200 kg of lentils for farmers planting legumes). Balanced slow-release fertilizer (100 kg of slow-release fertilizer) will also be included in the distribution. Farmers who planted wheat using government subsidized seed, and who have access to irrigation water through the public water network, will be supported with a distribution of maize seed planted after harvest of the wheat in summer. The quantity of seed distributed will be sufficient to plant 5 donums of land in the summer planting season (10 kg of seed maize). Balanced slow-release fertilizer will also be included in the distribution. The distribution of maize seed will allow wheat farmers to cultivate an additional crop on the lands where they have access to a source of irrigation. The high cost of fuel and diesel increases the already significant financial stress faced by vulnerable farmers. Therefore, AAH will support the farmers with a cash transfer equivalent to the cost of cultivation (mainly ploughing) plus an additional percentage to account for inflation. Livestock breeders have also been affected by the 10 years of armed conflict in Syria, compounded by the current unprecedented drought affecting the availability of grazing lands. This has forced the livestock breeders to purchase expensive fodder to feed their animals. These breeders will be supported with high protein pellets at the time of peak non-availability of pasture and/or during the critical periods of animal breeding. The amount of fodder will be in the line with the recommendation from the Food Security and Agriculture sector (1.5 kg of fodder per each head for a period of two months). All the supported smallholder farmers and livestock breeders will receive an orientation session about basic principles related to cereal/legume and livestock husbandry at the time of the agricultural input distribution. The quantities of inputs to distribute will be periodically revised to cater for inflation and specific needs. Training on smart agriculture crop/livestock production and water management will subsequently be conducted to these beneficiaries. The training will be provided by AAH technical staff in addition to technical staff from the Arab Center for Studies in Dry Lands and Arid Zone (ACSAD) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform (MAAR) extension officers. Distribution of home gardening kits will be provided to women headed households. Households receiving home garden support should have access to sufficient area to plant the vegetables and a source of water. Proposed interventions align with the FSA Sectors' recommendations.

4 - Needs

In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. The levels of acute food insecurity are expected to increase in Syria in 2022, due largely to the drought-like conditions. The estimated PiN in Aleppo is 2.3 M, in Hama 1.1 M, in Dar’a 0.7 M and in Rural Damascus 1.8 M. AAH’s assessments in the proposed locations confirmed that smallholder farmers and livestock breeders require access to high-quality agricultural inputs including seeds, fertilizers nutritious animal feed and financial support to restore their agricultural-based activities, in addition to capacity building in context-specific climate-related activities. However, these inputs are expensive and in many cases beyond their financial capacity. Both farmers and livestock breeders have been affected by the current, unprecedented drought and the poor harvest of the last planting season 2020/2021. This has led households to be unable to maintain their productive assets thus unable to continue with their main livelihoods activities. A significant proportion of the farmers were unable to plant their lands in the planting season 2021/2022 and hence implemented a number of negative coping strategies. Livestock breeders sold part of their flocks as pasture was not available thus having to purchase expensive fodder. Many smallholder farmers and livestock breeders have sold all their livelihood assets and are no longer in the production cycle.

5 - Implementation of activities

AAH will select the most vulnerable farmers through community committees with a set of vulnerability criteria with agricultural inputs. Vulnerable households headed by women who have access to water and 250 m2 of land will be supported with home gardening kits. AAH will support smallholder farmers with high-quality cereal and legume seeds to restore their main livelihood activities and return them to the production cycle. AAH will distribute high quality seeds of cereals (barley) and legume (lentil) at the recommended seeding rate by FAS. Smallholder legume farmers will receive balanced slow-release fertilizers for 10 cultivated donums. Cereal farmers who planted the subsidized wheat seeds and have access to water will be supported with 10 kg of high-quality maize seeds. Cash will be distributed to farmers to cover the cost of land cultivation. High protein pellets - mainly for sheep at breeding period or for milking sheep – will be distributed to smallholder livestock breeders. On-site, technical support will be disseminated. A proportion of smallholder farmers and livestock breeders will be trained on Smart Agriculture Crop/livestock Production and Water management. AAH will pilot and develop research on nature-based solutions for agriculture with low impact on the environment. A PDM report will be prepared. Outputs are measured against defined indicators to quantify the impact through baseline/endline surveys. An accessible feedback and complaint mechanism is rolled out.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: selecting beneficiaries will increase the tension within the community. Likelihood: medium; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: participatory approach in selecting the beneficiaries including women and marginalised community groups, accessible feedback and complaint mechanism in place and communities informed on it, AAH staff will address the complaint/feedback; information shared about the project and the selection criteria. Risk: beneficiary data protection is compromised. Likelihood: low; Impact: medium. AAH has comprehensive measures to protect beneficiary data, advocacy and coordination with local stakeholders to avoid breach in data protection. Risk : young adults at the age of military service will face problems to accessing the checkpoints: Likelihood: medium; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: Action Against Hunger will conduct the distribution within the targeted villages or select a site as much as possible a close site to the villages with a lower number of checkpoints to be passed. A transport allowance will be paid. Risk: beneficiaries will face security incidences during activity implementation. Likelihood: low; Impact: High Mitigation measure: follow S&S SOPs select locations and dates with communities and authorities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behaviour or linking sexual favour with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.
