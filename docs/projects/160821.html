Project Summary

Despite the R-ARCSS, 6.45 million people are currently at crisis level or worse of food insecurity. By December 2019 it is anticipated that 50.5% of the population will be at Crisis or Emergency Levels of food insecurity (IPC Aug 2019). The high levels of food insecurity are due to prolonged conflict severely disrupting food production and livelihoods opportunities, erratic weather patterns and deeply rooted poverty. Macroeconomic decline and high inflation have severely decreased households’ purchasing power, with vulnerable households reliant on markets to purchase food commodities. Beginning of June 2019, heavy rains , resulting in massing floods and destruction of infrastructure (schools, water and sanitation), cropps and shelters in NBeG and Unity (OCHA, August 2019). Guit is currently at Crisis level of food security, and will remain at this level through May to July 2019. cThere is also the potential for returnees into areas that have seen some stabilisation recently such as NBeG and some areas of Unity who will be reliant on humanitarian assistance to reestablish their livelihoods as they have lost productive assets during the conflict.
While the programme will not directly support FSL Cluster objective 1, Concern will coordinate with WFP who will partner with other organisations to identify completary activities such as supporting food production and improving nutrition practices. Concern will also partner with WFP to implement cash for assets in Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG where beneficiaries will receive cash transfers to meet needs during the hunger period for constructing community prioritise assets. The response will support achieving the FSL Cluster objectives 2 and 3. For objective 2, Concern will support vulnerable IDP, returnee and host community households with emergency access to agricultural and fishing inputs to improve food production and access to nutritious foods. In Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG, Concern will continue to provide access to seeds through voucher fairs where beneficiaries will have more choice to purchase seeds according to their preferences. Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers will be supported to train vulnerable farmers on Climate Smart Agriculture, biopesticide production and post-harvest handling. Concern will support dry season vegetable production to support income generation opportunities and access to nutritious foods. In Juba and Bentiu PoC, Concern will build on its approach of nutrition gardens to support improving dietary diversity through training lead mothers on sack gardening where space is limited in PoCs and they will train Mother-to-Mother Support Groups. Focus will be on households with members in OTP and TSFP. In Guit, Concern having supported vegetable gardens to improve access to nutritious foods will expand its FSL interventions considering the gaps in FSL support and most of the population being dependent on food assistance. Vegetable production and fishing will be supported to provide income generation opportunities and increase dietary diversity.
The response will contribute to cluster objective 3 through seed fairs for providing emergency access to seeds under objective 2 where local markets will be supported in NBeG. Although fragile, the relatively stable security situation in NBeG allows for Concern’s emergency programmes to be linked with integrated longer-term resilience programmes. These programmes focus on adapting to climate change through promotion of climate smart agriculture and natural resource management; and addressing high morbidity and malnutrition through increasing access to water and sanitation infrastructure and improving nutrition, health and hygiene practices. In Unity, beneficiaries of this response will also be targeted by Concern’s Nutrition and WASH programmes and in Juba PoC, Concern will coordinate with WASH parnters to ensure Concern’s FSL beneficiaries are targeted.

1 - Needs

6.45 million people are currently at crisis level or worse of food insecurity. By December 2019 it is anticipated that 50.5% of the population will be at Crisis or Emergency Levels of food insecurity (IPC Aug 2019). High levels of food insecurity are due to prolonged conflict severely disrupting food production and livelihoods opportunities, erratic weather patterns and poverty. Macroeconomic decline and high inflation have decreased households’ purchasing power, with vulnerable households reliant on markets for food commodities. Although NBeG has not been severely affected by the ongoing conflict, high food insecurity remains due to lack of livelihood opportunities, erratic weather patterns with delayed start of rainy season and subsequent heavy rains and flooding. Most household have limited access for quality and nutritious food increase households falling into the Crisis or Emergency level of food insecurity. In August 2019 560,000 people were at Crisis level or worse of food insecurity, and this is expected to remain the same in the coming months (IPC Aug 2019). NBeG has experienced the indirect impacts of conflict with displacement from other locations and disruption of the flow of food commodities. Rainfall was below average from June to August 2019 in NBeG, resulting in prolonged dry periods that delayed planting and resulted in poor crop performance, while flooding in June destroyed arable land (IRNA, Aug 2019). By the end of the rainy season most cropland have been destroyed by flooding and insect pests and diseases particularly Fall Army Worms (IRNA, Aug 2019). The GAM rate was at 12.7% and SAM rate at 2.5% in Aweil West and the GAM rate was 12.6% and SAM rate at 1.7% in Aweil North in 2019 (Concern SMART surveys 2019). While this is a decrease from 2017 where in Aweil West the GAM rate was 15.2% and SAM rate was 3.6% and in Aweil North, the GAM rate was 14.5% and SAM rate was 4.8%, acute malnutrition is likely to increase in 2020. In Unity, lack of livelihood opportunities and food production, limited humanitarian access due to the conflict have resulted in high food insecurity. In September 2019, 660,000 people were at Crisis levels or worse of food insecurity, and this is projected to rise to 685,000 by April 2020, even in the presence of food assistance. In Unity State, inside and outside Bentiu PoC, the protracted crises has prevented populations engaging in livelihood activities. Guit is currently at Crisis levels of food security, and will remain at this level through May-July 2019. Dietary diversity is poor with a Minimum Acceptable Diet at 5.6% in Bentiu PoC and 1.3% in Guit while the Minimum Dietary Diversity is at 32.1% in Bentiu PoC and 5.2% in Guit (Concern SMART surveys Mar 2019). Concern SMART surveys found that the GAM rate was at 14.7% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate at 1.9% in Bentiu PoC and the GAM rate was 11.2% and SAM rate at 1.7% in Aweil North . In Bentiu PoC and Guit, GAM and SAM rates decreased when compared to 2017. In 2017 the GAM rate was at 16.4% and SAM rate at 3.1% in Bentiu PoC, the GAM rate was 13.9% and SAM rate was 2.9% in Guit. However, these are rates for after the post-harvest period and are likely to have declined as the hunger period progressed in 2019. In the level of food insecurity in expected to rise in Guit to Crisis levels by the hunger period in 2020 (IPC Aug 2020).In Juba PoC there is limited access to diverse and quality food with minimial dietary diversity and this is caused by a lack of livelihood opportunities and food production making households dependent on food aid. Since November 2018, the general food ration size has been reduced to 70% in Juba PoC. 17.1% mothers only fed their children from 4 or food groups (minimum dietary diversity) which although shows slight improvement from December 2017 survey which was 15.4%, still remains low due to limited access to diverse food groups.

2 - Activities

CO1: Concern will support achieving this objective under a separate partnership with WFP for GFDs in Aweil North and Cash for Assets in Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG while other partners will implement responses contributing to this objective in Unity and Juba PoC.CO2: In Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern will support with ememrgency access to main season and vegetable crop seeds and fishing inputs. Households will be identified based on community-defined criteria including as having disabled, elderly or chronically ill in a household, household with 3 or more children under 5, households with a member in OTP/TSFP and female headed households. Crops and tool preferences will be discussed with beneficiaries. Main season crops including cereals and legumes will be provide through Input Trade Fairs where beneficiaries will be provided with vouchers to purchase seeds from traders. Prior to fairs, a market assessment will be conducted to determine availability, price and quality of seeds, if traders have capacity to meet increased demand and identify potential positive or negative impacts of the project on markets. Households will be supported with vegetable production in dry season to provide income and improve dietary diversity. In Guit, Concern will support vegetable production to improvee access to nutritious foods in both rainy and dry season. In Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit, FSL activities will be integrated with nutrition activities implemented by Concern. MtMSGs will be supported through the promotion of nutrition gardens in PoCs and vegetable gardens outside of the PoCs. Technical training on good agriculture practices, including conservation agriculture, post-harvest management, integrated pest management, fall armyworm control, water conservation/irrigation techniques, nursery and seedling production ) will be conducted throught the training of trainer approach. Cooking demonstration will be conducted with beneficiaries of vegetable production including food preparation and hygiene. Fishing is currently an underutilised source of income and nutritious food despite its potential to improve food security and livelihoods outcomes. In Aweil West, Aweil North and Guit Concern aims to support vulnerable household to improve fish post-harvest handling. Targeted households provided with fishing kits (hooks, nets, fishing line and other tackle) to improve food security and support livelihoods. Prior to the provision of these fishing gears needs assessment will be carried-out to determine preference and suitability of preferred gears. CO3: While fragile, the stability in NBeG allows Concern to consider linking emergency FSL interventions with longer-term programmes. Climate change including longer lean seasons and intensified rainfall and floods have resulted in loss of crops and increase the likeliness for production to be destroy by warms. Concern’s resilience programmes focus on promoting climate smart agriculture such as conservation agriculture, use of fast maturing crop varieties and natural resource management. Concern, with its well-tracked experience will implement seed fairs (using vouchers) in Aweil West and North to support and encourage local food production and hence, strengthen their ability to absorb shocks. Vulnerable households will be trained on VSLA appoaches, marketing and value addition of farm produce including groundnut and sesame milling. This FSL response will be integrated with Health, WASH and Nutrition to reduce the impacts of illness, poor hygiene, health and nutrition practices and gender inequalities on malnutrition. In NBeG, beneficiaries of this project will be targeted for Concern’s resilience programmes specifically those who have household members in OTP/TSFP. In Juba PoC and Unity, although the context does not permit longer-term development, integration with WASH and Nutrition will contribute to addressing causes of morbidity and malnutrition.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Concern has adheres to the Red Cross Movement’s 7 humanitarian principles which include impartiality, neutrality and humanity and will target beneficiaries only based on need. Concern will collaborate with Protetion partners to refer protection cases and to ensure their rights are adhered to in both South Sudanese and international law.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Concern has been working in Guit since 2016 and has established relations with local leaders and communities that have been affected by IDPs locating into their communities. In Guit, many communities have been forced to host IDPs and returnees placing a strain on households in these communities to provide assistance where they have themselves limited resources. Concern will target vulnerable IDP and returnee househeolds with assistance that will reduce the need for host communities to provide support.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This response prioritises the training of trainer approach to ensure sustainability and skills are provide to the communities that will be used after the response. Concern has had a long relationship with the County Agricultural Departments (CAD) in Aweil West and Aweil North and has established relations with the CAD in Guit when conducting assessments for this programme. CADs have supported Concern in conducting trainings with Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers. Community Agricultural Extension Workers and lead farmers then train beneficiaries on Climate Smart Agriculture, biopesticide production and post-harvest handling. Lead mothers will train Mother-to-Mother Support Groups in vegetable production to improve dietary diversity. For programme implementation, Concern will collaborate with community and camp leadership for introducing the programme, senstisations, targeting and monitoring activities. In NBeG, the response is linked to Concern’s resilience programme where beneficiaries will be provide with assistance as a form of social proteciton to prevent them from resorting to negative coping strategies such as selling assets gained during the resilience programme.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The programme prioritises life-saving and essential humanitarian assistance to provide emergency access to agricultural inputs for food production. However, activities under CO3 such as Concern’s resilience interventions will focus on contributing to learnings in PfRR where Concern is a lead agency in NBeG and the Resilience Exchange Network in the NGO Forum.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

NBeG has been relatively stable and has not had significant displacements although there are possibilities of returnees arriving. Guit has seen improvements in security although localised insecurity persists particularly with interncommunal tensions and cattle raiding. Concern will ensure that the programme will not impact on intercommunal tensions through close collaboration with communities such as ensuring entitlements to assistance are clearly communicated and the most vulnerable are prioritised. Concern will intgegrate conflict sensitivity including consultations with community leaders to understand social dynamics and design the programme so it does not have a negative impact on conflict dynamics such as ensuring selection criteria and entitlements are clearly communicated. Most of Concern’s staff are locally hired and have an understanding of conflict and social dynamics. Concern will collaborate with Protection partners and community leaders to ensure HLP is integrated for IDPs and returnees and host communities to ensure land access for food production.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In Unity and Juba PoC, the current context means that lifesaving and essential humanitarian needs are prioritised. However Concern is committed to supporting communities in these locations long-term and as security improves, Concern will focus on recovery and resilience. The programme will include trainings on Climate Smart Agriculture such as conservation agriculture. Beneficiaries will have gained skills that can be used beyond the programme that can suppor resilient crop production systems that can tolerate conditions such as prolonged dry periods. In NBeG, where the situation is more stable, Concern already implements an integrated resilience programme where beneficiaries of this response will be targeted to improve their capacities to cope with shocks and disasters.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

During targeting, vulnerable households with PSNs will be prioritised for targeting and their specific needs will be identified during assessments. PDMs will identify impacts of the response on PSNs’ food security. During assessments, equal participation of females and males will be ensured so specific needs will be considered, such as women’s and men’s roles in food production and preference for inputs. Targeting will be facilitated by communities and those involved will comprise of equal representation of women and men. Concern will ensure that distributions and fairs consider caregivers’ repsonsibilities of malnourished children. Monitoring activities will collect gender-disaggregated data to identify impacts on improving food and livelihood security for women, men, girls and boys and determine if inputs were appropriate for women and men. Separate female and male FGDs will allow for women and men to freely to express their opinions on the programme and will inform future programme design. Concern will assess security and triangulate information to ensure risks are minsimised. During needs assessments, safety and protection concerns will be identified so the programme can be designed so as not to place them at risk. Prior to distributions and fairs, Concern will consult with communities to identify distribution and fair sites to ensure they are in safe locations such as churches, schools and health facilities and close to beneficiaries to minimise travel times. Concern will conduct sensitisations on risk reduction measures before distributions and fairs such as travelling to sites in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Dates of distributions and fairs will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulating. Help desks will be used to identify and refer protection cases to Protection partners and to adapt the programme to minimise further risks.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Concern has a CRM in Aweil West, Aweil North and Guit where beneficiaries can submit complaints through approaching Concern staff directly, in writing or telephone and then complaints are assessed by an independent committee who accept or reject complaints and make recommendatiosn for action by Programme Managers. Feedbacks and complaints can also be submitted to community committees. Help desks are available at distributions for registering complaints. In the PoCs, camp managers are responsible for the CRM and Concern will refer any complaints to them. PDMs will be conducted following distributions with female and male beneficiaries so the can provide feedback on the programme activities and satisfaction levels. Information will be collected from women, men and specific groups such as PSNs. Separate FGDs conducted during PDMs will allow for women and men to have a safe space to share their opinions and any protection or safety concerns they had during the programme. Following seed faits, Concern will collect information on satisfaction levels of traders who participated in the fair process and market survesy will allow for identification of any positive or negative impacts of fairs on the local economy. Evaluations will be conducted with women, men, PSNs, traders and community leaders to assess the impact on meeting food security needs.
