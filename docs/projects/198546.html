Project Summary

This project seeks to improve the protection, dignity and wellbeing of UNHCR's People of Concern (internally displaced persons and returnees) and enhance the protection environment in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states, through the development and implementation of community-based Protection Monitoring activities, Protection by presence and Monitoring of return in targeted affected zones. With full involvement and meaningful participation of community based protection structures such as Protection Action Groups, and following protection principles such as the rights-based approach, Age-Gender Diversity Mainstreaming and Community-based approach the project will be conducted by way of Protection & Return monitoring missions for data collection, analysis and dissemination to humanitarian actors through UNHCR of protection risks and incidents, human rights violations and weaknesses in the protection environment carrying a potential of threatening the safety, integrity and wellbeing of persons of concern. Sensitization and awareness-raising activities, vulnerability profiling/screening, in-kind assistance to affected communities, including distributions of Core-relief items, assessments, outreach sessions, focus group discussions and other intensive protection activities will be undertaken as needs arise. Through the Child Protection project, children’s rights and protection will be supported in Borno state. Project interventions will include running Child Friendly Spaces where a range of activities, including case management, psychosocial support, life skills activities, will be provided. Community-based child protection structures will be strengthened, and material assistance provided to children at risk. Awareness raising and sensitization will be used to promulgate key children’s rights and protection messages to communities and duty bearers.

1 - Needs

The prevailing security situation in the BAY states remains volatile, characterized by non-state armed groups (NSAG) attacks, the killing and abduction of civilians, destruction of property and widespread human rights abuses. The ongoing military counterinsurgency operations have also restricted the affected populations' freedom of movement. Given the pervasive insecurity, killings, abductions, illegal vehicular checkpoints, complex suicide attacks using improvised explosive devices (IEDs), incidents of detonation of unexploded ordnances (UXO) and explosive remnants of war (ERW), widespread human rights violations continue. NSAGs have destroyed essential utilities and public infrastructure, which has aggravated poverty and worsened the economic conditions of people in the BAY states.The crisis coupled with the COVID pandemic, has compounded the vulnerabilities of the displaced men, women, girls, and boys resulting in massive unmet needs. A combination of the above factors has triggered new displacements. According to UNHCR, 338,000 people have sought refuge in neighboring Cameroon (131,000), Chad (20,000), and Niger (187,000). In the BAY states, a total of 8.4 million people are in need of protection and humanitarian assistance according to the Northeast 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview. This includes 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million IDPs, 3.9 million host community members, and 700,000 individuals in areas not accessible to humanitarian actors. Of the total persons in need, 80% are women and children. The global percentage of persons with disabilities is 15% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. Despite COVID-19 and related restrictions, movements across the borders continue to be recorded. From January 2022 to June 2022, over 13,000 spontaneous Nigerian refugee returnees were registered with 63% returning from Niger, 36% from Cameroon and 1% from Chad. The closure of official camps in Maiduguri municipality by the Borno State Government has resulted in secondary and tertiary displacement which an upwards of 150,000 IDPs in Maiduguri will be affected. To respond to the needs of vulnerable populations, UNHCR aims to ensure that protection monitoring services that identify and refer the most vulnerable populations, are in place. The activities will be concentrated in 15 LGAs / 4 locations in Borno state (Bama (and Banki), MMC, Monguno); 9 LGAs in Adamawa state (Madagali, Fufore, Maiha, Michika, Mubi-North, Mubi-South, Yola North, Yola South, Girei); and 5 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu, Bade, Machina, Geidam, Gujba).

2 - Activities

Child ProtectionActivity 1: Sensitization on child rights and protectionCARITAS will conduct awareness and sensitization sessions on child protection, children’s rights, and related issues with children and communities. IEC materials will be published and disseminated on childprotection. Activity 2: Community-based child protection strengthenedIn line with UNHCR/Caritas strategy, and in line with the prevailing context, Caritas Nigeria will identify and provide training/coaching/mentoring community-based child protection committees and alternative care structures in Banki, Bama and Monguno. Activity 3: Management of Child Friendly SpacesCARITAS will manage the Child Friendly Spaces in Banki, Bama and Monguno in Borno state, through which children and caregivers will feel physically and emotionally safe and able to express themselves and discuss sensitive issues and opportunities for social networking, solidarity-building, confidence-nurturing among fellow community members. Activity 4: Provision of material assistance Material support will be provided to children at heightened risk of GBV or other protection concern, including unaccompanied and separated minors, children with disabilities. Parents fostering unaccompanied and separated minors will be provided with NFIs core relief items as a means of strengthening the sustainability of the arrangement. They will also be prioritized for livelihood interventions provided through livelihood partners. Activity 5: Safe School InitiativesCARITAS will promote inter-school/inter-class competitions in selected schools to identify talent, engage children and youth in positive behaviour and promote empowerment. Activities will include drawing, poetry, melody, English/maths competition, drama, debate and more. Protection Monitoring Activity 1: Referral of Protection CasesCARITAS will leverage on exiting referral pathways to referral protection cases in a timely and appropriate manner. CARITAS will work in collaboration with health actors, the GBV Working Group and the Protection Cluster to ensure that persons of concern have seamless access to services. Referencing and counter referencing forms will be put in place to ensure referral activities are documented and reported on. Activity 2: Awareness CreationAwareness creation activities will be organized targeting IDPs, returnees and host community, to discuss specific protection issues and humanitarian principles, human rights monitoring and reporting. Activity 3: Strategic ReportingRegular reports will be written and shared with UNHCR and other relevant partner Organizations. These reports will include an analysis of the protection situation and of protection incidents. Distribution of Core Relief ItemsActivity 1: Disbursement of Cash to Recipients of Core Relief ItemsCARITAS will disburse cash to beneficiaries of UNHCR core relief items, where immediate market access is possible, otherwise in-kind assistance may be resorted to. The CBI assistance will be done through UNHCR Standard Operation Procedures (SOPs). Activity 2: Distribution of Core Relief Items CARITAS will carry out distribution of core relief items to beneficiary households, where the circumstances permit, otherwise in-kind assistance may be resorted to.
