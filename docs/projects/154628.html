Project Summary

To address the widespread risk of rape, sexual assault, sexual exploitation, honor killings, early marriage, and intimate partner violence (IPV) faced by women and girls in Iraq, exacerbated by the recent conflict, the IRC will provide Gender Based Violence (GBV) services through static Women and Girls’ Community Centers (WGCCs) in Anbar, Ninewa, Kirkuk, and Salah al-Din and by working with local partners in Dohuk and Sulaymaniyah. The focus of this project is to empower women and girls by placing them at the center of our services. Within the WGCCs, GBV case management and individual and group focused psychosocial support (PSS) activities will be provided to women and girls at risk of GBV and GBV survivors. IRC’s WGCCs will also provide non-focused PSS activities for women and girls to establish connections between one another as well as the WPE team allowing for the women and girls to build trust with each other as well as within their communities. Outside of the WGCCs, through community engagement awareness raising sessions for women, girls, men, and boys will be implemented on women’s rights and various GBV related topics as well as training GBV services providers, government officials, and community members on the causes and consequences of GBV. Women and girls will make up the majority of our clients who will consist of IDPs, refugees, or members from the host community.

Needs

Across all governorates, in and out of camps, due to stigma, shame, and fear, GBV incidents are under reported and with few service providers that focus specifically on GBV women and girls do not get the support they need to heal. Where there are GBV focused service providers, they are unable to meet the needs of all women and girls due to the overwhelming need across all locations in Iraq. Women and girls have reported to the IRC’s WGCCs that women and girls have been taken captive, repeatedly raped and sexually abused, and struggling to survive. Many survivors face depression, anxiety, isolation, self-harm, and some attempt or commit suicide as they have no network to support them through these issues. During IRC assessments in 2018, women and girls consistently requested safe female-only spaces where they can express themselves freely without fear, speak to someone about their pain and anxieties, and be able to spend time with other women and girls . When asking women what activities they would like to do at the WGCCs, the overwhelming response is that they need income generated and life-skills activities as they have little to no money to support their family. Psychosocial support activities were also requested as these activities provide a space where women and girls can make new friends, help them realize they are not alone in their experiences, gain new knowledge, and be themselves without judgement. Adolescent girls would like to return to school; however, their families lack the financial means, do not see the benefit in girls’ education, or fear for their daughters’ safety while traveling to/from and at school. The WPE team will make referrals to Education (where possible) to see what support can be provided to get the adolescent girls back in formal schooling or remedial/informal education, however in the meantime adolescent girls can participate in BLN classes via the WGCCs.

Protection Risk Analysis

- Interruption or delay of services due to security situation threats - Secondary trauma and burnout of frontline responders while working with conflict affected populations - WPE program staff turnover - WPE activities are not accepted by the community - Fluidity of movement among IDPs effecting targets - Women and girls are identified as GBV survivors

Mitigation Measures

- The IRC will work closely with its security team in each location to ensure that areas of intervention are safe to access for both beneficiaries and staff and activities can be implemented without major interruptions. - The IRC will ensure that a self-care plan is set for staff including methods to provide psychosocial support and space to the social workers who are in direct contact with beneficiaries and survivors. - The IRC will provide staff with a friendly and supportive work environment, focused on capacity support and career progression. - The IRC will implement community engagement with support of key stakeholders and community leaders as this acceptance is vital to allow women and girls to attend the WGCCs. - Complete proper assessments regarding IDP movement and follow up with the other focal points who monitor movements and camp closure. - WPE staff adhere to the guiding principles with a focus on confidentiality and safety of survivors

How is your project accountable to affected population

The IRC WPE program is founded on survivor-centered, women-led and rights-based approaches, thereby ensuring women and girls are consulted and engaged from the design to the implementation of activities and their feedback informs decisions. Within established programs, feedback is solicited from current clients informing the planning and design of services in new projects. The IRC also conducts focus group discussions (FGDs) with women, girls, men, and boys from the community and key informant interviews with community stakeholders. The findings inform overall strategy and the location of the safe spaces, while activities implemented in safe spaces are based on women and girls preferences. Focused psychosocial support curriculums are contextualized and adapted based on women and girls’ prioritization of skills, coping strategies and knowledge they deem necessary to ensure their safety and wellbeing. Interventions for adolescent girls are designed in consultation with both girls as well as their parents. The timing, availability, and nature of activities are based on women and girls’ feedback which are facilitated and led by community mobilizers from the community. Client centered case management services ensure that survivors decide on their healing and caseworkers enable and support their decisions. To ensure activities are meeting the expectations, client satisfaction surveys are regularly conducted by IRC’s MEAL teams to ensure objective responses from women and girls regarding the benefits and satisfaction of the WPE services and activities. These surveys are not conducted with case management clients to uphold their confidentiality. However, for those who receive case management, a client satisfaction survey is completed on their overall experience of their case management session. To ensure neutrality and confidentiality the social worker of the survivor will not administer the survey, this will be completed by the social worker’s supervisor. There are also comment boxes set in all the facilities managed by IRC including the WGCCs. Based on a previous assessment this was the preferred way for women and girls to provide anonymous feedback as the IRC is committed in client’s having a voice and choice in program activities. To ensure persons with disabilities, elderly, and marginalized groups can participate in activities, the IRC will utilize community mobilizers. Community mobilizers are individuals from the community where the programming takes place who know the community and context as well as help the WGCCs to be able to reach those who may not have access to the WGCCs due to disabilities, age, or movement restrictions. Women and girls see community mobilizers as the liaison between the community and the IRC. This provides insight and direct information from the women and girls on where these populations are located and how to reach them. How to reach these vulnerable populations are sensitively touched upon during FGDs.
