Project Summary

The proposed Emergency Food Security and Livelihood Response Program seeks to to help save lives, alleviate suffering through protection against hunger, malnutrition & destitution and maintain the dignity of those affected by conflict, floods and draught by providing an appropriate, timely and effective food security response. The project will improve access and availability of food, protect and restore livelihoods assets for a total of 15 000 food insecure households in areas hosting IDPs, returnees. Mercy Corps targets areas that were highly affected by the ongoing drought in Galmudug, Gedo, the Shabelles, Lower Juba and Baidoa that have experienced acute drought conditions as well as areas of displacements (IDP camps) in Baidoa, Kismayu, Mogadishu (Afgooye Corridor) and Galkacyo/Cadado/ Abudwak. The project will also target new regions with emerging needs including Wooqoyi Galbeed & Toghdeer in Somaliland. The objectives of the project will be achieved through provision of unconditional cash transfers to vulnerable households and those at particular risk of exclusion from accessing livelihoods. This will increase their access to food, alleviate hunger and strengthen purchasing power for essential household items and services. This activity will target at least 60% female headed household beneficiaries, women are economically vulnerable in Somalia. The project will also support Cash for Work activities to generate sufficient income for targeted vulnerable households. This activity targets the rehabilitation of communal assets/ basic services linkage facilities such as roads, irrigation canals and will target between 50 60% female participants taking into consideration gender roles. Moreover, the project will provide support to crop production through enabling access to inputs and training of farmers on good agricultural practices. In collaboration with FAO, the project will support livestock through training and strengthening the capacity of CAHWs and also ensuring availability of fodder.

Needs

Food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and Cyclone Sagar; riverine populations affected by flooding in April and May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. According to the findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, and humanitarian assistance is required to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation, thus humanitarian assistance must be sustained to protect fragile gains made and to prevent a worsening situation. Mercy Corps proposes to leverage and continue to intervene in the 5 regions where it has had programming presence for the past 10 years, namely; Banadir, Lower Shebelle, Lower Juba, Southwest and Galmudug regions in South and Central Somalia, but also extending to new regions with emerging needs including Wooqoyi Galbeed & Toghdeer in Somaliland. Mercy Corps implemented SHF funded and other donor’s emergency oriented projects in parts of the regions, providing much needed humanitarian relief. Assessments by MC indicate erratic rains and dry spells during the 2018 Gu (April-June) season have adversely affected crop cultivation and performance in agro pastoral livelihoods as crops have withered due to moisture stress and pest infestation. In addition, pasture levels are low in most areas with pasture depleted due to high temperatures. Water points have also dried earlier than usual leading to increased livestock migration, poor body conditions and reduced milk production. Consequently, herd sizes decreased and these are fetching low prices in the market.

Activities

• Community Mobilization will be conducted in target villages to create awareness on the project details including selection criteria, entitlements and complaints & feedback mechanism.• Unconditional cash transfers at 80% of MEB in target areas to 1200 vulnerable HHs and those at particular risk of exclusion from accessing livelihoods to increase their access to food, and strengthen purchasing power for essential household items and services. • Cash for Work-Selected households will participate in cash for work mainly in the rehabilitation of community infrastructure necessary to resuscitate livelihoods such as irrigation canals, roads leading to markets. • Refresher training for CAHWs and provide treatment kits prior to start of the vaccination and treatment campaigns. Training to be done in collaboration with Area livestock professional bodies and other actors and using AU-IBAR and LEGS manuals.• Livestock Treatment-Provide livestock treatment to 400 vulnerable households using voucher system. Livestock treatment will be provided in the rainy season when livestock are most susceptible and disease incidences high to HH who cannot afford costs for livestock drugs. • Provide fodder for livestock to support milking and breeding stocks belonging to 400 vulnerable households during the stress periods. Supplementary feeding of livestock focuses on maintaining a core breeding herd, rather than feeding the whole herd.• Provision of agriculture inputs: Availability of agricultural inputs such as farm tools and locally certified drought & pest resistant seeds will increase crop production and availability of food. • GAP trainings- selected farmers will be trained by Mercy Corps and Ministry of Agriculture to improve knowledge and skills in both crop production and livestock.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed activities such as cash transfers will address the food security and livelihoods needs of displaced people in a way that goes beyond care and maintenance to one that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks, preserves safety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities. Mercy Corp’s long standing presence in the target regions endows the project with in-depth knowledge of the vulnerabilities and strengths of the target HHs. The project aims to protect & restore livelihoods whilst strengthening the coping capacities of population affected by shocks through a wide array of activities that contribute to resilience building. Mercy Corps recognizes the need for close collaboration and synergy with ongoing resilience programs and will strive to leverage through existing programming platforms. Moreover, the project design has flexibility on emergency preparedness and response to ensure timely response to new shocks

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will directly target areas receiving or hosting displaced households in order to mitigate the risk of exclusion based on displacement. The project seeks to address the needs of the households displaced and the selection criteria will target those households experiencing effects of displacement such as limited access to alternative income sources and limited livestock holdings. During the targeting process local authority might influence on the selection of locations due to clan nepotism or particular interest and this way risk of exclusion of selection of the most disaster/displacement affected locations may be experienced. To mitigate this risk, Mercy Corps will adopt vulnerability-based targeting with community input approach. In addition, at the onset of the project a comprehensive community mobilization and sensitization activities will be undertaken focusing on the three pillars of; Information sharing, participation and complaint & response mechanism. This process will enable all community members including displaced communities to be fully aware of the project which will be implemented in their community. During targeting priority will be given to female, orphan headed households, minority and marginalized groups, those with many children, elderly and disabled amongst displaced communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mercy Corps in accordance with the Gender-age marker, will systematically collect disaggregate data by sex and conduct gender analysis during program design stages — as a quick, easy, and cost-effective way to mitigate potential exclusion of these group and most important to increase the knowledge base around interventions targeting and benefiting diverse age and gender in conflict-affected communities. Moreover, separate focus group discussion for different age and gender groups will be conducted in which specific voices and challenges experienced will be taken into account in provision of appropriate interventions. In community awareness, women, girls, boys and men will all be provided with adequate information and representation of these groups strengthened for inclusive decision making

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

This project will put deliberate efforts to increase most vulnerable groups participation and influence in contexts of peace, conflicts and other humanitarian crisis. To mitigate impact of armed conflict and violence particularly on the most vulnerable groups (women, minority clans etc.) the project will seek to collaborate with communities to foster peaceful co-existence and advocate for behavior change during community gatherings. The project will map out safe and protected distribution centers for project entitlements to mitigate harm and ensure protection and dignity of beneficiaries is observed. Mercy Corps will also designate female representatives at field level to receive beneficiary feedback and complains from women and girls as this is more culturally acceptable. Random calls will establish and address issues of discrimination, abuse and exploitation against women and girls.
