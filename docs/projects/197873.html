Project Summary

The project will contribute to providing access to quality inclusive education in Chiure and Montepuez districts, Cabo Delgado province, for displaced girls and boys, targeting 15,000 IDPs, through activities involving parents and guardians, school coincils, teachers and the community.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed Action intends to increase access to quality and inclusive education for crisis-affected children aged 6-18 in Chiure and Mueda districts, in Cabo Delgado province, and Erati, Memba and Monapo dustricts in Nampula province by strengthening relevant primary education services and community support for children’s education continuation and enhanced wellbeing.The project will contribute to the provision of access to inclusive quality education: provision of teaching and learning materials to IDPs students and other vulnerable students among the hosting communities; conduct back to school campaign and community mobilization in the selected communities; provide children and youth with alternative opportunities to learning recovery, and out-of-school children with Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP) to support back to learning; equip teachers with knowledge and skills on cross-sectoral protection issues (GBV, teacher code of conduct, MHPSS, VAC Referrals) and inclusive education (multigrade, inclusive pedagogy, classroom management, peace and life skills); and conduct community sensitization, mobilization and awareness raising’ activities with focus on child right to education, particularly girls and other vulnerable children, including those with disabilities. The project will target 12,600 beneficiaries, including girls and boys ages 6-18, including girls and boys with disabilities, and women and men ages 20-60. The tragets are divided by districts: Chiure 2,500, Mueda, 2,500, Erati 2,500, Memba 2,300, and Monapo 2,800.Children aged 6-18: 6,405 girls and 5,915 boys, from which 256 are girls with disabilities and 237 boys with disabilities. Adults aged 20-64: 146 woman and 134 men.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Community Participation• Mapping and identification of IDP students• Establish response and referral mechanisms in the target areas with support of parent-teacher committees Access and Learning Environment:• Provision of scholastic materials to IDPs students and other vulnerable students among the hosting communities • Provision of school uniform to IDP students when deemed necessary • Work with teachers and schools councils to facilitate the integration of IDP students in the host community schools if the intake capacity of the resettlement site school is unable to meet the demand • Organise community dialogues targeting parents and caregivers• Conduct back-to-school and retention programming and community awareness campaigns through community radio programmes and dialogues • Organise catch-up classes with groups of 20 children during the school holidays• Support the development of psychosocial support skills among teachers, parents and communities and ensure cascade of coping skills among children.(PSS to be conducted through radio but 50 families have been selected as a sample for monitoring and follow up through phone calls) • Teachers training on health education and promotion of handwashing and hygiene awarenessTeaching and Learning:• Conduct sensitisation sessions to students on referral mechanism • Train Teachers to promote CP sessions in classrooms as a crosscutting issue.• Conduct awareness radio sessions on VAC• Organise discussion meetings on VAC with School Council Teachers and other personel• Train teachers to teach literacy and numeracy skills.• Train teachers for distance education pedagogy.• Train teachers on necessary skills to help students catch up.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

• The project will identify and understand the IDPs capacities and coping mechanisms through a community-based approach, which will allow us to be more aware of the broader political context, to recognize and understand changes in roles and power relations between women and men, young and old, and majority and minority groups, and to work with leaders and all members of the community to ensure respect for rights and equality. This will help us to ensure that our interventions do not undermine community structures which can provide protection and support.• By working closely with the community, the project will be better informed about the protection risks faced by different groups, depending on their age, gender and background.Collaboration with government, national and international non-governmental partners is also essential to provide protection and assist persons of concern in claiming their rights to humanitarian assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

A gender and age analysis will be carry out to assess different roles and control over resources, inequality/discrimination, including on the level of access to assistance, capacities for coping with and recovering from the crisis they are facing. This exercise will allow the identification of specific needs for the assistance, ensuring it is gender and age sensitive. It will also help the project to better understand what specific needs and capacities women, girls, boys, men and older people affected by the crisis have and what specific threats they face. Collection and use of sex and age disaggregated data will be implemented, allowing a comparative analysis of how the situation distinctly impacts girls, boys, women and men.We will support equal and active participation of girls and women as well awarensess raising on gender equality and PSEA will be carried out with the school community.• A consultation process will be carried out through meetings with stakeholders and with the relevant government authorities, aiming at clear identification of IDPs in host families, as well an assessment of education needs among the students mechanism that Plan International uses include suggestion boxes, community engagement meetings, participation in meetings, surveys and evaluations, help desk, and the national emergency green line “1458”. In all these methods, we ensure the mechanisms are child friendly. These child friendly mechanisms promote gender and age-appropriate, inclusive, safe and confidential ways for children and young people to receive information, provide feedback and meaningfully participate in influencing humanitarian programming. A reference group will be created, formed by existing School Councils Committees, members from host communities and members from IDP families, which will be critical to establish mechanisms for conflict mitigation and to set up complaint and feedback mechanism.We will also put in place Child-friendly and community feedback mechanisms in the context of IDPs.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will follow the guiding principles of working IDPs with disabilities. These include Rights-based approach, with actions targeting persons with disabilities informed by the human rights principles and standards codified in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD). These include principles of nondiscrimination, respect for the inherent dignity, autonomy and independence of the individual, gender equality, respect for difference and diversity, and respect for the rights of women and children. The identification of people with special needs will be done through data regularly collected and shared by INGC. The project will validate the existing data through direct engagement with IDPs management committees.To remove barriers to their access to assistance, the project will include people with disabilities, their families and caregivers in all community-based participatory assessments, provision of training and awareness-raising programs on their rights, to empower them to participate in community affairs and decision-making processes.Particular attention will be given to promotion of the inclusion of people with all types of disabilities in community leadership positions (e.g., through quotas/reserved seats/awareness raising). The referral group formed will be consulted to ensure the children with disabilities will be integrated in the project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

We will work closely with the schools and community structures to assess and mitigate any environmental risks that can also pose a risk, such as the water provision and sanitation, waste management. We will also identify potential environmental impacts and necessary mitigations particularly related to the WASH rehabilitation in the schools. We will also monitor any natural hazards, such as floods and in coordination with relevant satelholders prepare a contingency plan to mitigate and manage potential risks and reduce people’s vulnerability.
