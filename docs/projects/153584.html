Project Summary

Through this project, IOM will bolster the WASH cluster’s capacity to respond to emerging humanitarian response in locations with the greatest needs through the management of a portion of the WASH core pipeline. In 2019, IOM will continue its commitment to support the WASH Cluster by aiming to procure and manage core pipeline supplies to benefit a total of 625,000 people in need. The project activities are in line with the four stated WASH Cluster objectives, and will serve to support timely and equitable access to WASH services to mitigate WASH-GBV risks, and support the prevention of famine and disease outbreak for conflict affected and other vulnerable populations in South Sudan. Supplies procured will be prioritized to benefit vulnerable people affected by prolonged food insecurity, malnutrition, GBV risks, disease outbreaks and renewed conflict.

Needs

The protracted conflict in South Sudan which began in December 2013 has resulted in multiple displacements across the country, compounding the WASH needs of an already vulnerable population. The coping abilities of these vulnerable people have been further eroded by prolonged food insecurity, malnutrition, GBV risks, disease outbreaks and renewed conflict, and led to significant displacement across the country. The widespread lack of safe drinking water, developed sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable WASH related diseases. As conflict continues and violent attacks targeting civilians persist, people will remain in concentrated displacement sites and host communities. Overcrowded living conditions with repeated bouts of flooding, poor sanitation and hygiene practices have left people precariously exposed to waterborne diseases such as cholera and hepatitis E, along with acute watery diarrhoea, severe malnutrition and pneumonia. WASH needs are widespread and remain immense, particularly as many communities who have had their infrastructure destroyed are unable to rebuild without assistance. The remaining infrastructure in many remote locations is unable to provide adequate services due to population increases putting a strain on existing services. The WASH Cluster has identified 5.7 million people in need, based on population and IDP figures in areas that face conflict-induced displacement, food insecurity/malnutrition, and a high-risk of disease outbreak. Accessing many remote locations in South Sudan with lifesaving humanitarian cargo is becoming increasingly challenging, as conflict and increased road attacks, combined with a highly undeveloped road network, drastically slows movements. This difficult task is made more problematic during the rainy season, when road transport is not an option and all supplies must be moved by air. The WASH Cluster has conveyed the need to maintain a robust and high performing core pipeline to ensure adequate prepositioning of WASH supplies in key locations throughout the country. This effort responds to the need to strengthen the humanitarian planning and response among the large number of aid agencies providing assistance in the country. The WASH Cluster has indicated the necessity of providing integrated WASH responses in critical areas with limited humanitarian presence, and the core pipeline will provide crucial support to ensure swift access to WASH emergency relief items in a coordinated fashion. IOM has managed the core pipeline in South Sudan since 2014 and successfully supported the WASH Cluster to meet objectives. Many partners rely on IOM as pipeline manager for the provision of materials from the Cluster’s common pipeline. Maintaining a steady pipeline is essential to meet needs in a timely manner as they are identified. IOM’s pipeline manager works closely with the WASH Cluster and State Focal Points to ensure prioritized and coordinated coverage across the country, minimizing gaps and the overlap of services. IOM also works closely with other Clusters, such as the Shelter/NFI Cluster, Health Cluster and the Logistics Cluster to ensure more vulnerable and underserved populations have access to WASH core pipeline supplies to maintain basic hygiene standards. In 2019, IOM will continue its commitment to support the WASH Cluster by aiming to procure and manage core pipeline supplies to benefit 625,000 people in need.

Activities

The most vulnerable and underserved populations will be targeted, specifically those displaced (including protracted and new displacements), at risk of GBV, disease outbreak, and/or those facing nutritional crisis. The WASH Cluster core pipeline will provide emergency WASH supplies to implementing partners, so they have essential emergency WASH relief items to respond adequately to needs in a coordinated manner. Women and adolescent girls of menstrual age in school and/or community will receive crucial support, such as MHM kits, to practice menstrual hygiene management with dignity. Vulnerable people affected by Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) or Moderate Acute Malnutrition will be prioritized to receive the hygiene kits designed for household usage. Proposed activities are as follows: 1. Procurement of adequate WASH emergency supplies targeting 625,000 displaced people, returnees, refugees and the most affected host community members;2. Preposition WASH emergency supplies to Malakal, Bentiu, Wau, Bor and Rumbek;3. Ensure appropriate storage and management of the WASH emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu;4. Undertake regular monitoring of WASH core pipeline supply delivery, storage and utilization;5. Support the WASH Cluster in strengthening the accountability of the pipeline program in South Sudan, through regular reporting of pipeline status, collecting utilization reports from partners, and undertaking post distribution monitoring of supplies distributed by partners. IOM will ensure WASH supplies procured will not only benefit beneficiaries targeted by the WASH Cluster, but also vulnerable population targeted by other sector clusters, such as Health, Protection and Shelter/NFI. Prioritization will be given to distribution for mitigation of WASH GBV riskd, people affected by acute malnutrition, as well as disease outbreak preparedness through integrated responses.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IOM will continue its commitments to engage the affected communities at all phases of the program cycle, namely assessment, registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring exercises. IOM will actively coordinate with the WASH Cluster and WASH partners to ensure that community engagement is strengthened through assessments, such as interagency rapid needs assessments (IRNA), the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and other cluster specific assessments. Reports of assessments will be submitted and consulted with the WASH Cluster, which will verify and acknowledge the needs of interventions and emergency responses in determined areas. Vulnerable populations in need of assistance will be identified through formal sector coordination meetings. Communication to WASH Cluster and partners will be conducted in writing and verbally. Information gathered through all these means of coordination will be used to ensure that the items procured through the WASH pipeline take into consideration the needs of the beneficiary population. IOM also conducts periodic post-distribution monitoring and other types of response monitoring to ensure that the assistance is in line with the needs of the beneficiaries. Accountability to partners and beneficiaries benefiting from the Core Pipeline will be monitored through the Core Pipeline Reports that partners submit to the Core Pipeline Manager. These reports provide WASH partners with an opportunity to not only justify how the items were used but also if there were any problems with the items, such as the quality. Beneficiary feedback is also monitored through Post Distribution Monitoring Surveys that the core pipeline undertakes on a case-by-case basis, through which beneficiaries provide feedback on the distribution, the items, and the relevance of the intervention.
