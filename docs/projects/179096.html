Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to provide non formal education, MHPSS and LSCE activities for school-age children (5-17 years) and youth (18-24 years, girls/boys) in Aleppo (Jabel Saman and Haritan sub-districts), Hama (As San sub-district) and Rural Damascus (Nashabiyeh, Qatana, Haran Al'awameed, Maliha Sub-districts). All areas targeted are ranked 4 on HNO 2021 severity scale, except for Maliha sub-district ranked 2. Locations and target have been identified based on the ongoing areas of intervention in the same Governorates and upon assessments and consultation with local authorities and stakeholders, focusing on urban overcrowded neighbourhood (Jebel Saman), remote (As San) and underserved areas (Haritan, Nashabiyeh, Qatana, Haran Al'awameed, Maliha) where the is access of education is hampered by a severe lack education services and infrastructures. The total number of beneficiaries to be served is estimated at 33,665 children and youth, of which 10% leaving with disabilities, and 4,190 adults including education actors and parents. Of the total beneficiaries targeted. The access to the areas of intervention is guaranteed through coordination with SARC and access to schools will be depending on the signature of MoU with Ministry of Education, planned to be finalized in the near future. The activities are designed in synergy with other projects submitted to HRP 2021 and based on the ongoing experience in the Districts targeted, where COOPI is presently implementing multisectoral integrated protection program, complemented in Aleppo with non-formal education components. In the 3 Governorates, the following proposal intends to provide education assistance to group of concerns already targeted with food security, livelihood and protection activities, responding to the risks detected in terms of child labor, early/forced marriage and due to the high presence of women-headed HHs that face difficulties in meeting the education needs due to the harsh economic conditions or the absence of operational schools. COOPI will focus on the following outputs: (1.4) non-formal education, including ALP, SLP, remedial, catch-up, literacy and numeracy classes while also providing (1.5) technical vocational education and training, (2.3) MHPSS and (2.6) awareness raising. The intervention will include material support for education (1.7) facilities and (1.8) supplies, (2.5) teaching and learning materials, while (1.9) social protection will be enhanced through the support to school transportation, food vouchers and vouchers for education. To ensure sustainability of the assistance, COOPI will stress on capacity building of both trainers/teachers and staff on leading and monitoring education services and basic principles related to CPMS, EiE and INEE MS. COOPI foresees a unit cost per child depending on the modality of implementation (in presence/distance/blended modality) and estimates to involve students in learning and recreational activities with a range cost between 95USD (in presence) and 180 USD to serve children leaving in hard-to-reach areas and children with disabilities.The proposed intervention will be adapted to the variety of education needs in the different areas of intervention detected during ongoing and previous intervention, with different approach regarding urban and rural or remote areas, as well as the use of the static facilities run by COOPI in Aleppo and Nashabiyah linked to mobile staff to serve hard-to-reach areas. COOPI proposal will therefore focus on existing resources and capacity to provide NFE in an efficient way, through in-presence, remote and blended learning modalities that will be identified in each location and involving the local community to find suitable but less economically demanding solution, as already ongoing in other sectors of intervention.

4 - Needs

The estimated people in need (PiN) of education assistance in the areas targeted by COOPI are 645,536 of which 488,295 in Jebel Saman, Haritan sub-district, 151,403 in Nashabiyeh,Qatana,Haran Al'awameed and Maliha sub-districts and 5,839 in As San sub-district. As highlighted in the key findings from the need analysis conducted for the 2021 HNO and based on HNO 2020, 2.45 million children (3 to 17 years old) are estimated to be out of school and 1.6 million were at risk of dropping out. Due to protracted conflict jointly by the unprecedent economic crisis and the impact of COVID-19 on the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours, as well as the shift to remote/blended learning modalities, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, are likely to be increased in 2021.Teenagers between 12-17 years old are the most affected, and in need of different learning, skills development and vocational training. The protracted exposure to violence decreases attendance to school, leads to drop out, and affects learning outcomes by limited concentration caused by psychological stress. In addition, harassment, bullying, violence, and poverty push children to drop out from school exposing them to increased protection risks, including child marriage and child labour. A particular effort is needed to assist children with a disability who are routinely the most marginalized and excluded group, especially in emergencies context as Syria. Therefore, the project will respond to the need of overcoming the barriers, in term of physical access and lack of inclusive education systems or special education curricula) keeping learners with disabilities out of the education system in Syria

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposal is designed to facilitate the enrolment, retention and reinsertion of dropout children at school, the provision of remedial classes, vocational training for youth, awareness and capacity building for education actors. The action proposed intends to complement the ongoing activities in Food Security, Livelihood and Protection sectors in Aleppo, Rural Damascus and Hama, by leveraging on existing material and human resources. In all targeted areas, COOPI will deliver school-based PSS for children, teachers and trainers. employing experienced protection staff. BTL Campaigns will be carried out to mobilize the community and sensitize parents. To respond to parents' concerns related to the actual adoption of C-19 preventive measures, as well as distance, lack/high price of transportation, absence of schools and diversion of resources to meet other basic needs, COOPI will provide food vouchers, support to transportation and vouchers for education.COOPI will have a preferential and direct access to children with disabilities thanks to the ongoing provision of hearing/speech and physical therapy services, thus ensuring they will be able to enjoy the right to equal access to learning and recreational activities, enhancing their social reintegration. COOPI will enhance community engagement through the establishment of community education committees. Learning achievements and targets will be verified through tools such as CMS, summative learning assessment, student attendance logs, lesson plans, will support COOPI monitoring of the quality of education activities. COOPI will participate in Sector 4Ws and collect disaggregated data. Complaints/feedback mechanisms will be to monitor accessibility, adequacy of services.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Students are exposed to violence, physical punishment or bullying. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: a) Ensure Child Safeguarding polices are in place; b) Personnel employed in learning facilities signs a code of conduct that enables inclusive safe, violence--free learning spaces, promote well-being and a protective environment; c) All education personnel and COOPI staff are regularly trained on positive discipline, child safeguarding and Code of Conduct;Protection Risk 2: Lack of consideration of information about context and social dynamics, hostilities in new accessible areas. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: a) Ensure the new communities are informed about project activities, services provided, beneficiaries targeted and scope of the intervention; b) Recruit the staff with adequate knowledge of the context and respecting the base and gender balance. Protection Risk 3: Insufficient support from, or undue influence by, members of the community or local authorities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: a) Develop feedback mechanism on the quality and usefulness of the services provided; b) Recruit staff who is familiar to the context; c) Tailor activities to be context and conflict sensitive and refer to sector guidelines; d) Inform community members about inclusion/exclusion criteria.Protection Risk 4: Beneficiary and other information is not sufficiently protected. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: a) Inform and train relevant staff/trainers about COOPI data protection policies and procedures; b) COOPI will define a clear contingency plan with closure procedures that includes the protection/destruction of data.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by education actors or service providers when delivering assistance. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Establish formal and informal complaint and feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA; Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; Established mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with the PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal points have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter and all staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Delay in the implementation of the activities. ASSUMPTION: Delay in the signature of the MoU with Ministry of Education. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will continue to follow-up the negotiations for the signature of the MoU in collaboration with SARC. COOPI will also approach Education sector to advocate with the Ministry of Education on the signature process. RISK: Direct access to the target areas and HHs is not permitted or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. Access to new areas is granted and later withdrawn by the authorities. ASSUMPTION: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will maintain constant communication with agencies, government and in country security monitoring networks. All staff will receive "security on the field" trainings in order to gain knowledge how to act if facing security issues. RISK: Education services provided are not fully coordinated with other humanitarian actors/sector of intervention. ASSUMPTION: Other humanitarian actors might not have the capacity to assist additional beneficiaries or the referral pathway or specialized services needed is not fully designed/implemented MITIGATION MEASURES: In this case COOPI will advocate with the sector to fill the gap and finding exit strategies. RISK: Beneficiaries and staff cannot access the most remote area of intervention. ASSUMPTION: The presence of UXOs may hamper access to the villages in new accessible areas. MITIGATION MEASURES: in this case COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for staff, volunteers and communities in UXOs recognition, involving community FPs and will coordinate with SARC for information on progress of UXOs clearance operations.
