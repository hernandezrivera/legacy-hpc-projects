Project Summary

The aim of the multisectoral proposed intervention is to enhance the access to quality non-formal education and ensure the provision of Psycho Social Support services for vulnerable boys and girls.
The project is based on Cesvi multi-years presence and experience in Libya, contributing to the humanitarian response for the affected population, both Libyans and non- Libyans, in Protection and Education Sectors. The proposed intervention has been designed on the basis of the results, lessons learned and gaps identified during previous interventions.
The action foresees the establishment of 3 Social Centers, one in Misrata and two in Tripoli, which are meant to provide protection and education related services to vulnerable boys, girls, men and women to be identified through a vulnerability assessment. School aged boys and girls out of school or at risk of drop out will be targeted and provided with non-formal education activities and structured Psycho Social Support (PSS) services. Their caregivers will be engaged in activities promoting positive parenting and Child Protection and child well-being.
The location of the centers will be decided upon the population distribution in the urban areas of Tripoli and Misrata and in coordination with the other education and protection partners operating in the two cities, as per related Working Groups 4Ws and service mapping.
Education is crucial to offer a stable and safe environment for the most vulnerable boys and girls and to address psychosocial needs. Through its current collaboration with the Resilience Research Unit (RiRes) within the Psychology Department of Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore of Milano, in Libya Cesvi has been developing a specific innovative methodology in implementing PSS and educational programs and continuously equip operators with tailored tools and competences on how to support children and their caregivers and reinforce resilience process in people affected by crisis events.
Among the children involved in the center based activities, minors exposed to or at risk of violence and abuse will be supported through case management services. Finally, through a mix of both center-based and outreach approaches, the intervention envisages the organization of awareness raising sessions, community events and other community based activities aimed at enhancing social cohesion and raising awareness among communities on Child Protection.
This project falls under HRP Protection SO1, SO2 and SO3 and HRP Education SO1 and SO2.
Concerning the Protection sector, the intervention envisages the following outputs:
SO1:
• Needs and protection risk of affected population are identified (OUTPUT1)
SO2:
• Psychosocial well being of girls, boys, women and men improved (OUTPUT 2)
• Specialized services provided to girls, boys, men and women (OUTPUT 3)
• Affected population better informed on how to prevent and respond to protection concerns (OUTPUT 4)
SO3:
• Capacity to provide community-based protection services and address protection concerns at community level strengthened (OUTPUT 5)
• Capacity to provide quality protection services enhanced (OUTPUT 6)
For the Education component, the project envisages the following outputs:
S01:
• Access to conducive learning environment education for vulnerable children and youth enhanced (OUTPUT 7)
SO2:
• Capacity to provide quality educational services enhanced (OUTPUT 8)
The project will target a total of 2400 individuals in Tripoli and Misrata:
Out of the total target, 50% is represented by school aged boys and girls involved in PSS and educational activities, while another 50% is represented by caregivers and other adults involved in parenting skills program and other community based activities (social events, awareness sessions).
The educational and PSS activities will target out of school children, representing the 60% of the total children population, and children at risk of drop out (40%).
90% of them will be mixed migrants, including refugees and asylum seekers; 10% will be reached among Libyans, including IDPs and returnees.
Mixed migrants population will be reached out through a series of centers-based and outreach activities that will aim at identifying, involving and ensuring direct participation of most affected and vulnerable population. Community mobilizers will support the outreach teams in conducting regular vulnerability assessments in urban areas. Awareness sessions and social events will enhance the acknowledgement of the targeted population about the services to be provided by Cesvi. It worth to highlight that Cesvi, thanks to its rooted presence in both targeted area and its community-based approach envisaged in all interventions, has already well established connections with mixed migrants communities.
In order to enhance the capacity of Cesvi operators to respond to the identified needs and guarantee the quality of the services, 6 educators, 17 CP operators and 9 Community Mobilizers will be trained on CP approach and teaching methodologies.
The proposed intervention is complementary to the partnerships, and related targets, that Cesvi is planning with Unicef and UNHCR for 2019.

Needs

Children have been severely affected by the protracted crisis in Libya, deprived of access to basic social services and in need of protection and psycho social support to overcome the trauma of violence. Thousands remained out of school, as schools were damaged by armed conflict or used as shelters for IDPs. According to Cesvi assessment in Tripoli and data from EWG, mixed migrants children face particular difficulties in accessing education. As of July 2018, it was reported that refugee and migrant children have little or no access to education to education in 65 per cent of the muhallas in Libya (HNO 2018).The lack of official civil documentation and school certificates hinder their possibility to be enrolled in Libyan schools. Children from sub-Saharan African countries face particular difficulties to access schools due to discrimination and language barriers. In Tripoli, according to the Education PIN figures, 5.893 refugees and 6.193 migrants school aged children are in need of educational support, while for Misrata these figures are respectively 1.506 and 3.669.Although refugee and migrant children have specific challenges in accessing public education, the crisis still ongoing in the country is negatively impacting the whole public education service, leading to a deterioration of the quality of education for all the children, including Libyans. Violence is reported to be widespread in the Public Schools, from teacher and among peers, pushing children to drop out from school and leaving them exposed to a myriad of protection concerns. Children who are out of school or at risk of drop out are more exposed to a number of protection risks.Refugees and migrants, including children, face many of the same protection challenges as Libyans primarily due to direct exposure to the general violence widespread in the country. However, they also face additional protection issues as a result of racism, discrimination and their irregular status. The risks of abduction, kidnapping, extortion, robbery, sexual exploitation and abuse, forced labour, slavery, arbitrary detention and human trafficking are very high. As the country’s security situation has deteriorated, xenophobic sentiments and abuse against migrants have increased, especially targeting those from Sub-Saharan Africa.An estimated 35% of migrant children in Libya are unaccompanied (DTM Round #21 ) , thus increasingly in need of specialized and targeted support and assistance. Those who are not in detention but not registered as refugees or accessing services are difficult to identify and assess by humanitarian actors. Therefore, community-based activities, which include the involvement of community members in the response, are therefore needed to identify the needs of ‘invisible’ children and young people.

Activities

The action foresees the establishment of 3 Social Centers, one in Misrata and two in Tripoli, which are meant to provide protection and education related services to vulnerable boys, girls, men and women.A community-based approach, aiming at reaching out and identifying the most affected population and involving them in each phase of the project, will be mainstreamed in both center-base and outreach activities presented here below . ProtectionOUT 1:Act 1.1: Conduction of Vulnerability Assessment to identify needs and vulnerabilities of the target population (target 2400 individuals);OUT 2:Act 2.1: Provision of structured and sustained psychosocial support group activities for girls and boys (target 1200 individuals);Act 2.2: Provision of programs on child well-being, development and parenting skills for caregivers (target 360 individuals);OUT 3:Act 3.1: provision of integrated case management services for boys and girls at-risk or affected by abuse or violence (target 135 individuals);OUT 4:Act 4.1: Awareness activities on prevention and response to violence against children targeting boys, girls, women and men (target 720 individuals)OUT 5:Act 5.1: Capacity building of individual community members on CP approaches (target 9 individuals);Act 5.2: Organization of community-based activities promoting social cohesion (Child rights day, National days, recreational activities) (target 2400 individuals);OUT 6:Act 6.1: Capacity building of women and men from service providers on CP approaches (target 17 individuals);EducationOUT 7:Act 7.1: provision of essential learning materials/school supplies to school aged children (target 1200 individuals)Act 7.2: Provision of non-formal education services to school aged children (target 1200 individuals).Activities will be tailored in accordance with children age, language and cultural background. For older children, aged 14-18, the project will pilot a component of S.T.E.M Education (Science, Tecnology, Engineering and Mathematics).OUT 8:Act 8.1: Capacity building of education personnel on child protection and improved teaching methods (target 6 individuals)

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Access to the provided services will be ensured through the identification of the most vulnerable children (needs/vulnerability assessment) and the prioritization of those affected by protection risks, including but not limited to child labor and survivors of abuse and discrimination. A dedicated transportation allowance will be guaranteed to grant regular access to the Social Centers where the activities will be implemented.It has to be noted that the provision of the transportation allowance has a considerable impact on the cost of the intervention. However, due to the security situation in the Country and in accordance with Cesvi experience in similar ongoing intervention, the allowance is fundamental in order to ensure the safety and avoid the early drop-out of the children from the activities. The safety of both children and caregivers will be ensured, as per Cesvi Children Safeguarding Policy, PSEA Policy and CP Minimum Standards.The dignity of the beneficiaries will be safeguarded through their active involvement in all the phases of the project implementation, promoting participation and empowerment. Particular attention will be given in ensuring the confidentiality of all the data collected to avoid any further harm.
