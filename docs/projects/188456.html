Project Summary

In support of the Education Sector, UNICEF through this project in 2022& 2023, UNICEF will further support its Generation Unlimited Initiative and No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), female and male including those with disabilities, to have access to age and gender sensitive, learning and skills building opportunities for improved learning, empowerment, and employability. UNICEF will build on strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most vulnerable adolescents to strengthen skills development, and employability (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches) through adolescent-friendly spaces and mobile teams. UNICEF will support access for young girls and boys to multi-sectoral services and opportunities, including technical vocational and education training (TVET), employability skills and life skills and citizenship education. UNICEF will support integrated approach to gender and age-appropriate service delivery maintained strong focus on promoting development and participation of adolescents and youth in north-east Syria, Aleppo, Dara’a, Rural Damascus and Hama. The project will support the access of young people to skills-development programming that is comprehensive and reaches the most vulnerable adolescents, especially those not in Education, Employment and Training, IDPs and returnees. The project will promote alternative pathways and learning beyond schools and implement 21st century skills-development programming. The project will support development of foundational, transferrable, technical and vocational, and entrepreneurship programming to prepare adolescents better for their future, and to unleash their potentials and increase their learning, leadership, and employability opportunities. UNICEF will promote the efficiency and effectiveness in TVET education services nationwide through (i) expanded access and service delivery; (ii) enhanced quality and relevance of TVET provision; and (iii) improved TVET systems including strengthen the capacity of service providers and evidence generation. The project will target the most marginalized adolescents and youth groups in particular those who are out of school, the poor, those from child- or female-headed households, returnees, IDPs, youth in the camps, adolescents with disabilities, and young people affected by the crisis. The project plans to reach a total of 400,000 young people in Rural Damascus, north Homs, south Hama, Idleb, Dier-ez-Zor, east and north-east Aleppo, and other locations. This includes the newly accessible locations such as Dara’a Al-Balad and towns in Dara’a where the security situation has been improved, a new generation of young people, who have been lost for more than 10 years, has appeared. Through this project, UNICEF will expand its support to young people in NES, mainly for the focusing on returnees and displaced people in Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh, including those in camps, collective shelters and urban settings. During 2021, IDPs have begun returning from the camps to their places of origin in Ar-Raqqa. Projects engaging young people projects will be essential needs for their effective integration in their communities. In addition, since 2014, many young people have been staying in camps and collective shelters without freedom of movement and facing other risks, thus offering them activities that will enhance their skills and empower them to be agents of positive change in their community. UNICEF will focus on the most deprived areas in rural and urban areas include camps (Hol, Roj, Newroz, Areesha, Mahmoudli) in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa, the collective shelters in Al-Hasakeh city, and the rural areas in Al-Hasakeh Qamishli, Malikia, Yaroubia, and Ar-Raqqa. The project will implement the activities for the young people affected by the crisis to acquire the right skills, become empowered and resilient community members.

4 - Needs

Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, record-level price increases and cuts in subsidized goods have prompted an increase in the number of people in need. Moreover, COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on basic services while also depriving young Syrians of their jobs and livelihoods. The combined effects of these concurrent crises contributed to a significant contraction of the overall economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access essential services, and undermine social cohesion. Young people in Syria have missed out opportunities to develop and learn. They have limited access to skill-development opportunities and basic services including safe transition from school to work. Syrian girls are vulnerable to child and forced marriages and have limited of no access to services and learning opportunities (Humanitarian Action for Children/UNICEF2022). Syrian youth are struggling with high unemployment rates (20.9 per cent of total labour force, Arab Youth Survey2021),lack of matching between youth skills and labour market needs due to dramatic changes in the economy, poor skill-development programme, lack of access to formal education and poor quality of education, difficult transition from school to work, lack of alternative and flexible learning & educational pathways

5 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will support 200,000 vulnerable adolescents and youth per year, female and male, especially those not in Education, Employment and Training (NEET), to access to structured capacity development sessions, including non-formal TVET, life skills and citizenship, entrepreneurship and leadership skills training. The project will support the employability of young people and provide them with opportunities for economic self-empowerment. include creating fora for adolescents and youth to generate economic or social value for them as individuals, or for their peer groups and communities. UNICEF will support the TVET system specially for the most vulnerable youth to be equipped with TVET and entrepreneurship skills and support their access to labour market. UNICEF will focus on the following pillars and related interventions: 1)support the TVET system including strengthening the institutional capacity and enhance the quality and access to services.2)support skills development and learning pathways to prepare adolescents and youth for smooth transition to adulthood and to labour market (leadership, life-skills and entrepreneurship).3)enhance evidence generation and youth engagement. UNICEF will continue to secure appropriate and vary means for affected populations to provide feedback, complaints on the issues affect them and quality of services. UNICEF has a strong Monitoring system in place with required capacities to monitor programme interventions towards planned results.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio pf protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance networking for the benefit of people at risk and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in dignified way is integrated in project activities. Consequently, as pre-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plan are guaranteed.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Targeting the most marginalized people may encounter many risks from safety and security to social acceptance and maintaining a quality and timely delivery of services. The spread of COVID-19 with its high likelihood may affect the participation of people in adolescents’ friendly spaces. UNICEF will promote the digital engagement of people as well as continue to respect measures to prevent the transmission of COVID-19, including wearing protective equipment while engaging with people, regular self-monitoring of health, consistent temperature checks and regular hand sanitation.Potential conflicts between IDPs and host communities as well as the instability of security situation in some areas have a medium likelihood. UNICEF will continue to promote social cohesion service and youth-led initiative and awareness-raising to mitigate such risks. Community resistance to change in many locations due to social norms leads to preventing young girls or people with disabilities from participating in the activities. UNICEF will continue to promote behaviour change and engagement of community through different pathways. UNICEF will continue to prioritize targeting the most disadvantaged people and will improve trust-building with local communities.
