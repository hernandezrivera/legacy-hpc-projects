Project Summary

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria due to conflict resulted in widespread displacement of more than 2 million people. Due to increased insecurity , people do not expect to return to their homes and have to remain within their temporary settlements. On the average, IDPs in the target area are displaced for almost 3 years . In the target area of Jere , the Multisectoral needs assessment conducted in September 2019 , showed that expensive food is a barrier for 79% of the respondents. Jere continues to be classified as “ Severe in living standards and well being measures and in over-all severity in the Humanitarian Needs Overview.
The target of this intervention are the crises affected Internally displaced populations (IDPs) and host communities in Jere, specifically in Gongulong and 14 adjacent villages and settlements Biriri, Bololo, Bololo Shuwa, Bulabulin, Bulamari, Gabdori, Gongulong, Goniri, Grema Chelluri, Gremari, Gumsuri,Modugabdori, Ngonari, Umarti, Yerimari
From 2016 until 2019, ZOA’s food security interventions using cash based transfers in the target area resulted in targeted households being food secure during the project period including the lean season as evidenced by 98% of population having improved CSI scores. Targeted households also improved diet diversification from 2.4 to 6.3 food groups averagely consumed per day
The purpose of this intervention is to build on the immediate relief and short term gains of ZOAs emergency and relief efforts and move forward to build livelihoods that will enable the targeted households to build resilience . The intervention is a bridge from relief to future development interventions . This aims to move Households beyond relying mostly on aid and build capacity and resilience. through agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods to support household food security. The resilience will further be achieved through the start-up and development of Village Savings and Loans (VSLA) model adapted to the local context and capacities,
As such, the over-all goal is Reduced hunger, improved nutrition, and increased food security for hunger-affected individuals and households for 1,300 most vulnerable households through both increased agricultural food production and non-agricultural income generation.
ZOA intends to achieve this to complement existing cash programming in the same area with distribution of agricultural livelihood kits with seeds and tools supported by demo plots allowing beneficiaries to plant food crops within the safe spaces of the area. Non agricultural livelihood kits will be distributed according to the livelihoods that women participate in strengthened by the organization of community business volunteers., ZOA intends to keep moving forward as the situation improves and eventually organize VSLAs to consolidate the gains and move full cycle from relief to development.

1 - Needs

Describe the Assessed Needs the project will Address through Its activities. Please also list needs assessments ServicesZOA conducted a livelihoods assessment in March 2019 of both IDPs and host communities in the target areas. In general the assessments showed that the target populations practice both agriculture and non-agriculture activities. Despite this and the proximity of the areas to the major markets in Maiduguri, incomes remain a extremely low levels leaving populations vulnerable to food insecurity . The average monthly expenditure of an average household is 23,734 NGN which is insufficient to cover needs in food , health, education and others. The assessment showed that 59% of the are population is still involved in agricultural production, both for own consumption and sales purposes on either owned or rented land . Rental of land from host communities by IDPs is common. Casual labour by men and boys is practiced by 52% of the population. All farmers produce during the rain season (May-October), but only 14% farms during the long dry season. Both men and women are involved in those activities. For those in agricultural activities , the challenges to gaining sufficient income include secure access (66%). Those that do access the land cite lack of tools & equipment (88%). Hardly any improved inputs (seeds, fertilizer) are used to increase yields. 71% of the population depends on seed storage from the previous season. 81% of IDPs and host communities are involved in non-agricultural activities like firewood collection, petty trade/small scale business and casual labour. 68% of the households are involved in firewood collection, 55% is involved in casual labour, 24% is involved in petty trade and 2% sells wild fruits. Women and girls are particularly involved in petty trade activities and firewood collection, whereas men are mainly involved in firewood collection and casual labour. Main limitations to earn sufficient income from non-agricultural activities are: lack of capital (91%), security concerns (29%) and absence of opportunities (38%). ZOA aims to build on the opportunity that 81% are involved in nonagricultural activities but see lack of capital as barrier (91%) and increase this through the infusion of appropriate livelihood kits , training and community support including the formation or savings groups that will evolve into VSLAs. In terms of protection concerns , the livelihood kits . For those engaged in agriculture , appropriate agriculture kits will be provided together with demo plots and training support.The assessments include ZOA Livelihood assessment in March 2019, MNSA conducted September 2019, PDMs and FGDs conducted by ZOA together with community ocular inspections for livelihood opportunities as ZOA conducted livelihood

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Beneficiary targeting, selection and registration exercises - ZOA utilizes a criteria that allows of targeting and selection of the most in need or vulnerable, The vulnerability criteria includes variables such as female and child headed households, vulnerable members such as pregnant and lactating women, elderly, PLW and chronically ill, large household members and dependency and in cases such as food security, access to land and productive assets. A socio-economic score is determined and a cut-off to determine the number. In the case of livelihood, the ability to engage in livelihood will be part of tehe selection criteria. This is followed by biometric registration using fingerprint scanners and tablet phones. ZOA is part of the Nigeria joint response funded by the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs through the Dutch Relief Alliance. As such, NJR members coordinate targeting through regular planning and operational meetings, joint assessments and the establishment of communities of practice according to sectors for harmonization of targeting and selection. ZOA is also an active participant of the Food Security Sector Cluster and Cash working groups and regularly submits 5Ws and refers to the dashboard of partner presence. Activity 2: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the community. Activity 3: Agricuture and Non agriculture kits distribution- distribution is conducted with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. ZOA distribution system through the use of color coded cards and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of ZOAs commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women. Activity 4: Sensitization on Protection issues and gender based GBV . These are sessions where women and girls join focused groups discussions that identify risks , threats, vulnerabilities and align these with community capacities to mitigate or external sources for reporting. ZOA has already conducted gender analysis training for staff and has a gender analysis framework ready for implementation for its year 2020 project. This is also how ZOA intends to integrate PSEA issues and prevention in its project. The gender analysis, coupled with sessions on identifying community mechanisms of prevention PSEA. ZOA has also established presence in the PSEA network task force to establish connections with the referral mechanisms where present.Activity 5: IYCF/HDD sessions- women beneficiaries will be selected and grouped. Bi-monthly sessions will be conducted in groups to enhance the nutritious preparation and feeding of food bought from multi-[purpose grants. Community nutrition mobilizers and lead mothers together with ZOA staff come together for regular sessions designed to increase the knowledge of mother and child pairs in proper feeding practices and preparation of various foods that diversify the household diet. ZOA does not do nutrition and considers this activity as a form of labelling to strengthen the use of cash for food. Activity 6: Post distribution monitoring , community feedback sessions and operationalizing CRM- The ZOA CRM system is operationalized on field through help desks during distribution, face to face conversations, suggestion boxes and hot lines Activity 7 Endline and projct evaluation
