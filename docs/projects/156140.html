Project Summary

Adequate supplies of clean water are essential to maintain health and reduce the risk of epidemics, especially in overcrowded settings such as IDP camps. Water is essential for cooking, drinking and cleaning. If people do not have adequate supplies of clean water, they will obtain it from sources that are likely to be contaminated. In 2018, WHO assessed the quality of water in Aleppo, Homs, north-west and north-east Syria and Rural Damascus. The water of ground wells, reservoirs and over jerry cans need to be regularly tested in order to prevent diseases from unclean water and ensure that water is of high quality when consumed. WHO worked with national authorities and partners, WASH sector partners and local communities to disinfect polluted water and make it safe to use.

Needs

Based on WASH assessments there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance, 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need (displaced people in camps, informal settlements and host communities). IDPs living in IDP sites are amongst the most vulnerable groups in Syria. In the northwest the scale of displacements to IDP sites witnessed since the beginning of 2018, has contributed to a deterioration of already overused WASH facilities and increased the need to scale-up operations and enhance the focus around more sustainable solutions (connection to existing water networks and/or establishment of simplified water and sewer networks). Access to WASH services has been identified as the number one priority by displaced people in 30 per cent of camps and collective centers in northwest Syria, the number two priority in 40 per cent and the number three priority in 30 per cent. Water quality monitoring and response system programme has 2 components. First is of humanitarian nature and the second is for resilience building. Regarding the humanitarian aspect, there is a need for securing safe drinking water in IDP camps in Aleppo, Deir Ezzor, Raqqa and Al Hassakeh Governorates. Specifically, there is a need for ensuring that water in reservoirs is safe for drinking and that water tankers do not cause the contamination of drinking water. Regarding the resilience building component, and as regular/network water supply is being restored in areas of new returnees, water reservoirs are no longer being used by the IDPs and returnees. As a result one of the following happens:• Stagnant water remains in reservoirs which are not filled regularly and algae starts growing inside the reservoirs. As a result, the reservoirs themselves become a public health hazard. Such reservoirs either need to be flushed with heavy doses of chlorine or removed. The water quality programme would provide information on this issue.• Agencies keep on providing drinking water to reservoirs where network water is being provided; hence wasting scarce financial resources. The water quality monitoring programme would provide such information to agencies to modify their trucking programmes and/or remove un-needed reservoirs.• Returnees may start using uncontrolled drinking sources (i.e. existing shallow groundwater wells in their neighborhoods due to lack of regular network water supply). Such wells are usually contaminated with sewage water due to destruction of this network and leaking sewage recently. The water quality monitoring programme would help to locate and characterize water quality in existing groundwater wells as treatable or untreatable (depending on type of contamination and cost of treatment). This information would allow UN agencies and INGOs to decide whether they should disinfect existing groundwater wells (for example with chlorine pumps) and provide local drinking stations for returnees, or designate the groundwater wells as health hazard and hence install reservoirs in areas of returnees, which of course would serve to create more efficient water trucking programmes for the entire area, and also would allow UN agencies to better coordinate with the water utility to prioritize the restoration of drinking water networks in areas of returnees.* The bacterial contaminated water is associated with increase morbidity of some diseases especially water borne diseases. To reduce morbidities related water contamination and response to diseases alerts in any area in Syria; monitoring of water quality is one of the essential investigation measures to identify the sources of contamination and conduct appropriate response measures to reduce the risk of diseases associated with poor water quality. In 2018 more than 1865 ground wells, 521 reservoirs and over 190 jerry cans were tested. The proposal of WHO in 2019 is to expand the activities of water quality monitoring in the areas of severity scale above 3.

Implementation of activities

1. Water quality for reservoirs in IDP camps in Aleppo, Al Hssakeh, Deir Ezzor and Al Raqqa is determined so that actions can be taken on the spot to secure their safety.2. Water quality for uncontrolled sources in areas of returnees is determined so that actions can be taken on the spot to secure their safety.3. Water quality for water sources in areas which report alerts of water borne diseases is determined, so that to identify source of water contamination (wells, water tankers, jerry cans, ect), and identify needed interventions for the control water borne disease outbreaks. The project in Aleppo and Rural Damascus will be in coordination with ministry of Local Administration and Environment and water institution, SARC, ICRC, and unicef. interventions to improve the water quality undertaken based on the results of tests. Reports and findings will be share with the WASH cluster as well reported in WHO's monthly reports. Field testing using the bioluminescence method, and Laboratory testing (Bacteriological and physical and chemical analysis) to determine:• Physical properties of the sample such as color, smell, conductivity, turbidity….etc.• Chemical pollutants in the sample such as nitrates, nitrite, ammonium, phosphate, sodium, ….etc.• Total Heterotrophic plate count, total Coliform bacteria, and Escherichia coli.

Protection Risk Analysis

As per WASH sector risk analysis, relevant risks include: Risk 1: Limited access to safe water forcing IDPs and vulnerable groups to use untreated ground water/wells. * Mitigation: Along with early warning and alerts system reporting, water from target areas is tested and communicated with WASH sector for required response (treatment/water trucking).Risk 2: Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards*Mitigation: Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Agencies conducting water trucking may not react to results of water quality monitoring if water is unsafe for drinking; hence IDPs are exposed to water-borne diseases. * Mitigation: Full coordination with all agencies to ensure speedy response with required water quality measures in place. 2. Returnees continue drinking polluted water from uncontrolled sources in their areas; hence returnees are exposed to water-borne diseases. * Mitigation: Full coordination with governmental authorities and other agencies in order to ensure that uncontrolled sources of drinking water are either depolluted or put out of service.
