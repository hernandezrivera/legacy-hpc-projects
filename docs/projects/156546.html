Project Summary

The proposed project aims to catalyze positive change by increasing access to services through: 1) GBV service provision and referrals; and 2) increasing awareness and encouraging discourse at the family, community and governmental level around issues pertaining to the health and well-being of women and girls, including survivors of GBV and other vulnerable groups.
In order to reach this objective, IMC will support a comprehensive GBV program that prioritizes support for GBV survivors, including expanding safe entry points and referral pathways, as well as working to engage different age, gender, and diversity groups and stakeholders in the community to raise awareness on GBV, strengthen GBV referral pathways, and minimize risks.
Psychosocial support services for persons at risk and survivors of GBV will be provided in a newly established Women and Girls safe space to ensure respect, privacy and confidentiality for the survivor, and to prevent stigma.
At the same time, IMC will also convene community outreach activities to inform the population about GBV and its negative consequences, safe motherhood and family planning, available services and referral pathways, and the benefits of reporting GBV cases to health facilities immediately.
IMC is proposing an integrated package women and Adolescent Girls , GBV prevention and response services, addressing IDP, host and returnee communities needs.
This will create comprehensive and efficient service delivery, and facilitate referrals between sectors and case management for beneficiaries with different needs. It will also increase community participation to foster community acceptance and promote uptake of services by targeted population. The program is in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan and Protection Overview for Syria
Standards of implementation will be based on SPHERE Standards and international best practices such as UNFPA minimum standards, IASC GBV guidelines, IASC guidelines for Sexual and Reproductive Health, Adolescent girls and Youth and GBV prevention and Response programming in emergencies, and will be designed in close consultation with local officials and communities.

Needs

The 2018 assessments (WoS Assessment Findings of the Humanitarian Needs Overview) confirmed that gender-based violence continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The fear of sexual violence, often associated with abduction, is a concern raised by women and girls in the majority of assessed communities in Deir Ez-Zor governorate, psychosocial support as well as medical treatment for survivors of sexual violence and/or domestic violence were reported as not existing and needed. The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence contributes to survivors not talking about violence when it happens. Women and girls also fear honor killing as a result of sexual violence. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Quantitative data findings report high levels of child marriage in Deir Ez-Zor governorate. While adolescent girls were mostly affected, girls below the age of 12 were also reported as vulnerable to child marriage in around 46% of communities Movement restrictions for women and girls also featured as a major negative coping mechanism also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately their rights. And it has been reported as ‘very common’ and ‘common’ in more than 80% of communities and existing in all communities. With men absent, injured, killed, or unable to find employment the burden of responsibility often falls heavier on the shoulders of women and girls to maintain households. However, these additional responsibilities do not necessarily lead to greater empowerment or freedom for women. Invariably, it leads to an increase in workload and sometimes to additional abuse as men resist a perceived threat to their dominance. From aid distribution to gaining documentation, to attending school, guarding against exploitation and abuse is a constant challenge. IMC is proposing establishing a women & girls safe space to provide comprehensive GBV program that prioritizes support for GBV survivors, including expanding safe entry points and referral pathways, as well as working to engage different age, gender, and diversity groups and stakeholders in the community to raise awareness on GBV, strengthen GBV referral pathways, and minimize risks.

Implementation of activities

IMC will deliver the proposed project through establishing one new women and girls safe space and designing an outreach program in Deir Ez-Zor governorate. The center aim to provide the women and girls with a physically and emotionally safe space to meet, feel comfortable and enjoy the freedom to express themselves without the fear of judgment or harm. The project will create an opportunity for positive coping mechanism for women and girls including GBV survivors, through -structured and non structured psychosocial support activities,--early childhood development trainings for young mothers and pregnant women to make them feel more prepared for motherhood (around 200 mothers, pregnant women, and adolescent girls) and empower them in taking an active role in the family and in their children upbriging-life skills program for adolescent girls which will enable the youth to become active members and agents of positive change in their community- recreational, educational activities coupled with basic PSS services These activities will also contribute to empowering families to protect children and to enhancing the resilience of the entire community. The project will also increase the integration of girls into the active community participation and give them the opportunity to be visible agents of change, which can contribute to decreasing gender inequalities. The WGSS will also deliver specialized services for GBV survivors through case management and referrals. Women, girls and the community will be consulted in order to understand the security risks in the community, and the types of community support systems that existed for women and girls before the crisis. This part of the assessment will help to determine the need for such a center and/or the type of resources it may take to engage the community in the initiative. IMC will be implementing the following activities within the proposed interventionBeneficiaries will have access to a feedback mechanism that will enable them to provide their comments and evaluation regarding the services provided in a confidential way and also contribute with providing suggestions for new services.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Unstable security situation, which might limited the access for the supervisor to reach the locations and do direct monitoring and supervision of the implementations of the activities. Mitigation strategy; prepare a remote supervision plan to supervisor the activities accordingly.Shortage of skilled staff: based on the health department experience in the implementation area, MHPSS department has some concern regarding finding capacities in that area. Mitigation strategy; provide specialized training to recruited staff and building capacity of the community in the targeted area. Difficulties of finding suitable places that comply with protection needs for the beneficiaries, also, getting the needed approvals on time.Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Mitigation strategy: IMC will establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities, communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended. Moreover, IMC will avoid the exclusion from assistance of persons with no identity documents within the community center.
