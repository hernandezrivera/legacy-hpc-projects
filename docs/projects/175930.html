Project Summary

Health Link south Sudan proposed to provide a 12 months project on equitable access to integrated safe WASH services, to IDPs, host communities, and returnees. The strategy is to integrate WASH into Health, Nutrition, Educations, and mitigate WASH-GBV related issues hence improving the overall health of the vulnerable communities under the thematic humanitarian response pillars. A total of 33,240 project population targeted (13,628 Males: 19, 611 Females) in Torit, Ikotos, Lopa/Lafon, Magwi, and Kapoeta North counties. According to the cluster strategic objectives the following activities will be done:
1.1. Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package.
1.1.1. Health Link intends to establish -5 OTP/SFP sites in each county.
1.1.2. Treat 2,485 SAM/MAM cases, targeting every month 51 cases.
1.1.3. Select and train -5 Community Nutrition Volunteers per sites (25).
1.1.4. Provides a discharge package- Chlorine tablets (3-months), soaps, water container (Jerrycans and bucket without taps), Reusable masks, and ORS for home based hygiene management
1.2. Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level. Under this objective 33,240 individuals; Women, girls, boys, and men will have enabled and improved access to clean safe drinking water (05 boreholes) from a protected water points in the settlement site of the host community, IDPs, and or returnees and within supported health facilities (5) HFs. This will be through;
1.2.1. Drilling of 2 hand pumps; 01- in Parajok returnees site and 1- in Paringa of Magwi and Kapoeta North.
1.2.2. Selection, training and deployment of 45-community based-volunteers (BHWs/HHPs).
1.2.3. Procure and distribute 20 Ltrs buckets and 20 Ltrs Jerrycans to 5,540 Households.
1.3. Mitigate WASH related- GBV issues at community levels/IDPs, returnees, and host communities. A total of 5,210 women, girls, boys and men, and disable persons have access to a safe clean safe sanitary facilities.
1.3.1. Distribute i-WASH Kit-Menstrual and personal hygiene (COVID-19) packs and Spot light to 5,210 (M=2,136, F=3074, women, girls, and boys.

1.3.5. Procure and installs 200- Ltrs tanks to supports hand washing at 5-health facilities, 5-schools, 5- markets.
1.3.6. Establishment of functional integrated 5-ASRH and Women Girls Friendly Spaces attached to each target health facilities, 1 per county.

1 - Needs

South Sudan humanitarian needs have remained high even after 2 years of Peace agreement; several basic services and livelihoods remains closed or destroyed. Further the outbreaks of COVID-19 pandemic made impossible the progress towards SGDs in the country.The HNO-2021, estimated 5.6 million people need WASH services, and further analysis showed 310,000 refugees lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. Overall 17% uses latrines, 4% have shared latrines, and 36% have access to safe water, while 34% uses surface water with distance over 30 minutes’ walk resulting in 60% increase in cases of diarrhoeal diseases in 2020 compared to 2019 (HNO-2020). This is further made more complex with the lack of knowledge about hygiene promotion among communities in crowded settings. 16% cases of violence against women and girls during water collections have been reported in 2020. Among communities in South Sudan, only 39% have access to hand washing facilities. In locations hosting returnees, IDPs, and where land conflicts and communal issues are high, WASH remains a target point of conflict. In most cases children’s, women, and disable persons are the most affected. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washings practices increases the risk of cholera, diarrhoeal, and vector-borne diseases. While host communities and non-displaced people are in need of WASH items and facilities, increasing needs are expected among the returned refugees and IDP groups. According to IPC-4 report, 2020, 3 counties in EES are at severity level 4 of food insecurity compounded by low WASH services, an increased case of malnutrition amongst children will be high if nothing without response. Accordingly the GAM rate in under-fives is 11.2% an increase from 6.8% in 2018, the stunting rate is 1:3 in children especially those residing in IDP settlements. However, with effective and sufficient WASH services delivered these situation can be reversed. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure and a high chances of further disease outbreaks. A WASH assessment reports by Health Link across 112 health facilities in Juba, Ikotos, Torit, Magwi, and Lopa/Lafon indicates a huge gap in the availability and capacity of WASH related services and facilities to provide quality of care. This increases the risk of gender based violence and abuse in situations where services are not proportionate to the population size and exacerbating protection issues. Further with anticipated 8,000 of returnees resettling back from neighbouring Uganda, DRC, Sudan, Ethiopia, and Kenya, the need for better WASH is very crucial (IOM DTM- November 2020). This factors has prompted Health Link SS to devise strategic approaches to integrate WASH into Health, Nutrition’s, and mitigate WASH-GBV issues in collaborating with Nutrition’s, Health, and WASH clusters to deliver this services to the 77,741 target population of IDPs, host communities, and returnees in Kapoeta North, Magwi, Ikotos, Lafon, and Torit Counties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HLSS has in place the PSEA policy, safeguarding policies and other Code of Conduct integrated in the recruitment and contractual documents. However, equally the providing of information about SEA to the beneficiaries is very critical to enhance community reporting of any form of SEA committed with the beneficiaries. As a bottom line, HLSS has PSEA policies and practices aim to prevent sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by its employees and it provides that any allegations of SEA are responded to in a timely and appropriate manner. All new staff in emergency context are oriented on this policy before deployment to deliver WASH services to the beneficiaries. It is the principle of operations that HLSS ensures beneficiaries are oriented on their responsibilities of reporting SEA through a confidential protection desk that is always made available and linked to CMR services and legal support. This is always done during various community engagement process in the course of the project implementation. HLSS will also ensure beneficiaries education on SEA and reporting channel throughout the project period. The community-based feedback mechanisms to prevent and respond to SEA will be established. New staff at all service delivery points will be trained in how to handle such cases with the level of confidentiality they deserve. HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use. Additionally Health Link will ensure the BHWs/BHT based in the community are linked to project monitoring and coordination with the WASH protection committees, the 3Ws and MOB groups. This will ensure a coordinated effort at community base to ensure accountability to the affected population on issues regarding sexual abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Providing an environment where beneficiaries have a feedback mechanism in place usually creates the confidence to monitor, evaluate and reports on WASH services and projects being delivered to them. The most vulnerable especially women and PWD and elderly will particularly be given an opportunity to express and share their views in the simplest way or in local language specific to the population group. This can be in form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions and community meetings. The information collected will be analysed, and provided responses accordingly at different levels, and will also guide for inclusion of missing components or change of project approach to implementations whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries and the donors. • Suggestion boxes placed at 3Ws group Office and open every day will be use to captures ideas and views of beneficiaries during the project period. This suggestion boxes will be control by the beneficiaries who opens through the Women Water Watch (3Ws) groups and Maintenance and Operations of Borehole (MOB) groups, and the BHWs groups to ensure transparency in collating the feedback responses for further actions and a special consideration will be put in place to support the PWDs and those illiterate populations so that their feedbacks shall be implemented throughout the project period. • Sandwich response and feedback will be encourage especially in situation where evidence suggests an improvement in a certain benchmarks between two given outcomes. Disable unions, women groups, and the illiterates will be oriented with evidence based reporting’s to ensure adherence to what is workable and effective than the mere generalized recommendations. • Evidence, effects, and change approaches whereby Health Link technical management team will ensure deviation from the sets of objectives is analyze through groups of the vulnerable beneficiaries, staffs, and other partners to ensure the compliance and appropriateness of outcomes to the most affected beneficiaries. Each vulnerable groups will be involve in the visualizations, and narration of the possible cause-effects analysis to ensure their ideas are fed into the redesign and provides a benchmark for evaluations. • Group feedback is the final approach which Health Link intends to utilize. Taking FGDs as the major approach here, the technical project team will organized together with vulnerable groups a focus group discussion based on the observed deviations or gaps in achieving a set of objectives. During this group discussion various members are ask to deliberate on factors affecting their utilization and uptake of a given services and then the views are incorporated into the project management approach. This also involves the establishment of a WGFS to enable beneficiary’s routine internal engagement for analysis of project and channeling this to the project management for improvements and action.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HLSS provides an environment where beneficiaries have the confidence to provide feed-backs on health care projects being delivered to them. This can be in form of short messages, phone call-backs, voice messages, and focus group discussions and community meetings. The information collected will be analyzed, and provided responses accordingly at different levels, and will also guide for inclusion of missing components or change of course whenever it is appropriate and in the interest of both beneficiaries and the donors. Health Link is aware of the need to redesign and improve project outcomes based on practical analysis from the ground. Special attention will be given to mitigation of WASH-GBV related complaints, issues affecting water usage and access, issues regarding sanitation and safety audit conducted by the women groups selected to form the 3Ws in the project site. Weekly bulletins, monthly meetings, and quarterly reporting’s to the various line ministries, MoH, MGSCW, MOPI, INGO, clusters, and other partner’s including beneficiaries will be ensured. This will enable timeliness and promptness in addressing project externalities such as communal conflicts, insecurity, and inappropriateness of resource distribution amongst beneficiaries. The feedback from beneficiaries will be implemented at 3-different levels. Feedback requiring actions of beneficiaries themselves, feedback requiring action of donors and project teams and feedback requiring the action of line ministries. At all these levels focal points will be established for beneficiaries to know where they can channel issues, complaints, request, and suggestions for project change, improvement or ad-hoc meetings to address a critical issues. Health Link is also quite optimistic of the constant changing environmental factors in South Sudan, and based on this, problems arising during the project period will be solve on spot basis or case by case bases as waiting for a fixed meetings schedule can affects outcome of a given case. Therefore in doing this beneficiaries suggestions, comments, complaints will be incorporated as soon as possible in project implementation phase without delays. Its normally a common analysis that project teams and beneficiaries only wait for project team to initiate meetings to provide feedbacks, however in this project approach beneficiaries will be empowered to organize meetings that calls the project teams to attend, and there will be encouragement of beneficiaries to communicate both negatives and positive feedbacks as both can feeds into a given effects in project executions. For example positive feedbacks encourages upward progress with the given approach, while negative feedback implies the team to get back to the drawing board and realign processes to improve the overall approach if a given outcome is expected. This method will ensure active participations from beneficiaries into issues affecting projects from both the positive and negative perspectives. Lastly but not least Health Link will promotes the active presentation and recording of success stories especially from the beneficiaries side after consent and sharing with donors to ensure their voices and actions are translated into national and international evidence of project actions. This will extract the positive energies in each beneficiaries as they regards their actions as something that will transform the general community. This action not only promotes evidence and best practices, context based; but also it’s an incentives which motivates the rest of the members to participate in personal efforts, team efforts, and overall community effort to yielding better outcomes for their benefits. This is the core of the community led approach which is the backbone of the current MoH approach adopted to ensure sustainability of service at all levels.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project targets vulnerable populations in IDPs, host communities and returnees. Health Link South Sudan recognizes that beneficiaries have rights to access the basic essential WASH, health care, shelter, rehabilitation especially the disabled, SGBV victims through MHPSS services to mention but a few. Under this project, HLSS will ensure equal access for men, women, boys and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality. Beneficiaries especially survivors of SGBV will be supported to seek legal assistance through the county led legal system in place. Health link will establish a WASH protection committees (the 3Ws-Women Water Watch groups) and trained them to empower them and together with HLSS WASH officers shall conduct safety audits to ascertain the safety of beneficiary’s and whenever required provides the appropriate care, management, and treatment to the affected beneficiary at household levels. This will involves the use of the feedback mechanism, toll free lines for reporting, the audit report will be shared with humanitarian partners and recommendation implemented by the HLSS WASH department. HLSS shall form a referral pathways and train the field officers/WASH Officers and documents all complaints and provide feedback to the water users. HLSS will work with the project team and beneficiaries to ensure siting of latrines, water points to be rehabilitated, and access to other services are in safe environment and free from harm to both women, girls, boys, elderly, and PWDs. This will ensure ensuring water committee presence at water points to monitor use, slashing of water points to provide open environment free from harms, and ensuring latrines rehabilitated are having lockable doors and sited in safe area for all beneficiaries.Finally where appropriate the project teams will together with the affected population leadership reach the offices of humanitarian coordination to present their recommendations and suggestion for actions to addressing GBV issues. This may take the form of allowing women, girls, and PLWD to be represented during international days of commemoration of the global efforts to reduce inequality and inequity to services.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

South Sudan is still facing the worst form of food insecurity across many states and communities. WASH is considered the backbone to better health according to UN- SDGs. Effective WASH services ensures better educations, nutrition’s, health and overall development of an individual and community as well the nation.Through this project livelihood protection will be strengthen to ensure appropriate coping mechanism due to the emergencies and other natural disasters such as displacement. This will empower community to adopt alternative livelihood sources for survival hence promoting the cost recovery at household and reducing the ultimate cause of poverty and hunger at household levels. Example the planting of vegetable and selling to the local markets to ensure financial gains and support to the family financial needs.Through the available boreholes and water points rehabilitated, community will be trained on small scale agricultural activities which complement their household subsistence farming practices to ensure supplementary food rations are reaped from the available water sources. This will includes the use of simple manual irrigation incorporation and working in collaboration with food security and livelihood partners to helps reach affected communities with short period seeds for short term farming. Vegetables and other short term cereal will be advocated for during the project period.Training of the community on food balance sheets would be another options to help address improper food consumption. The affected communities, returnees, and the IDPs will be selected by their own leadership and in collaboration with FSL partner’s short orientation trainings will be organized to ensure family can adopt simple sustainable farming and agriculture practices to enable better nutritious feeding at household levels.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project was designed to significantly address access constraints to basic services including protection concerns of the most vulnerable section of the population especially women, children, older persons and persons with disability. Although the political environment remains fragile, the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement is beginning to take shape and there is currently relative calmness. This is anticipated to further improve access to remote areas, reduce risk of new displacement and subsequently stable system that will ensure protection of humanitarian workers and beneficiaries. The implementation of this projects will ensure equal access to men, women, boys and girls to basic and essential services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality. Beneficiaries will be given access to basic i-WASH Kit’s (MHM packs, Face mask, and underwear’s) package to promote their hygiene practices and general management of menstrual conditions especially amongst the girls and women in the project site.Health Link will ensure to work with the host community to provide for safety and security of IDPs, returnees and other affected persons to enable their access to other basic services in the area. This involves the linking of the IDPs/returnees leadership to the leadership of the host community and promoting social coexistence amongst communities. Finally the project will be linked with all routine services in the community and through this approaches affected population would be assured of adequate access to any other services as a citizen of the country. Effective referral pathways will be established to ensure person requiring a certain services is referred immediately for the particular services in the project locations. Strengthening the capacity of responsible leadership especially the community and beneficiaries leadership to take up responsibilities of care, support, and empowerment of affected individuals. This is in particular to the PLWD and the children who in most cases feels disempowered and marginalized due to lack of presentation in decision making.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Citizens and the overall population are continuing to face difficulties to provide adequate nutrition as required due to factors ranging from economic, social, environmental, and inadequate food stocks at household levels. This project has an integrated approach of ensuring the drivers of basic health are incorporated in all its process of implementations. This includes the effective coordination with other partners such as health, WASH, FSL, GBV/Protections and education clusters supporting nutrition outcomes in the community. In this approach the project will establish an effective inter-cluster sharing of vital and decision making information regarding nutrition’s in the affected populations. Secondly the project aims to improve the knowledge of mothers in ensuring better nutrition diet for the growing children. This includes the orientation and during health education at household levels the delivery of messages of dietary types, hand hygiene in meals preparations, safe waste management and disposal, and early seeking of care whenever a child is sick. This will ensure protection of livelihood from depletion as community in dire needs of survival can resort to selling of household foods to earn a living, and the consequence is endangering the feeding and survival of a child.Thirdly the project will strengthen the capacity of responding staffs and volunteers to ensure effective nutrition prevention communications especially in the early management of SAM. When the staffs are well equipped with knowledge they eventually delivers behaviour changing messages to the mothers and caretakers, and this has a bearing in decreasing the prevalence of malnutrition amongst children in the whole nations.Another options that this project will work on is the investment in data and information for decision making to responding partners. Sharing of information and transmission of valid, reliable, and complete data sets regarding nutrition status, WASH, health, and overall protection in the affected community will be prioritized to ensure such information and deciosn feeds into the planning and singing of program to address nutritional issues in the community. Finally the project will work with local leaderships at grassroots to break down barriers due to social norms and cultures affecting women, men, and children in their development and survivals. Such traditional cultures which has negative impact on the overall progress towards achieving the SDGs will be address through a community wide approaches to ensure best practice and adopting context specific behaviour aiming to promotes child developments.
