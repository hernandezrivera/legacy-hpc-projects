Project Summary

In 2022 and 2023, UNRWA will continue to deliver basic, quality and inclusive education to almost 50,000 Palestine refugee students through 102 schools managed by the Agency across Syria, of which 39 are running in second-shift in buildings on loan from the Ministry of Education to compensate for UNRWA schools that have been destroyed or severely damaged by the conflict.
While schools in Syria re-opened on 2 September 2021 for in-person education, the growing number of COVID-19 cases amongst education staff and students across the country over the last quarter of 2021 highlights the challenges of preventing transmission in schools, particularly given overcrowded classrooms. Therefore, throughout the year, UNRWA will remain ready to shift to a remote or blended learning modality. Self-learning materials will continue to be updated and be available to all students.
UNRWA, through its Education in Emergencies (EiE) programme, helps to ensure that Palestine refugee children can continue to access education, even in times of emergencies. Below is a description of the key elements of the response in Syria for 2022:
1) Support to Student Access to Education: Back-to-school kits (including school bags) will be provided at the beginning of the next academic year (2022/2023); stationery will be procured and provided to students at the beginning of the 2021/2022 second semester in January 2022, to alleviate some of the economic burden faced by parents. UNRWA, will also support students returning to damaged camps such as Yarmouk and Ein el Tal by providing transport (contracted buses) to take them to schools in nearby areas.
2) Psychosocial Support: UNRWA will continue to provide PSS to address the additional stress created by the COVID-19 pandemic. Psychosocial counsellors will be deployed in all Agency schools to provide individual and group counselling and support to students and teachers.
3) Safe and Secure Learning Environment: A number of training sessions, incorporating COVID-19 prevention measures, will be conducted for teachers and principals on topics such as GBV, child protection and addressing violence against children (AVAC). Similar training sessions will be delivered to parents through parent-teacher associations (PTAs), which will continue to play a pivotal role in developing a holistic approach to education for Palestine refugees.
Reusable masks will be procured and distributed to students and teachers. In addition, disinfectants and sanitizers will be provided to schools to ensure proper hygiene practices are in place. UNRWA will also conduct minor repairs to some UNRWA school facilities that require urgent maintenance.
4) Sustaining Quality Teaching and Learning: Through this appeal, summer learning activities will be organized, contingent on the COVID-19 situation in Syria during summer 2022. In addition to academic support, the programme would include extracurricular and PSS activities to students. Support classes will be organized for Grade 9 students in preparation for official end of cycle exams.
Printed copies of self-learning materials (SLM) will continue to be updated and made available to all students in the event that access to the Agency's Digital Learning Platform (DLP) is not available. Where needed (i.e., during school closures), UNRWA will support/subsidize internet and SMS costs for students and teachers (up to 50,000 students and 2,000 education staff), and will provide tablets/mobile devices for Grade 9 students and teachers (up to 4,550 students and 210 teachers).
Through this project, UNRWA will cover the cost of additional temporary staff hired (up to an estimated 271 daily paid teachers and school attendants) to address the increasing needs that have arisen due to the ongoing crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic.

4 - Needs

After eleven years of conflict, it is critical that formal and non-formal education services are approached as long-term investment by not only children and families but also by humanitarian actors. COVID-19 and worsening economic conditions have exacerbated the consequences of underinvestment in education service delivery. The consequences of this will continue in 2022-2023.Formal education will continue to have a limited absorption and retention capacity and be carried out in largely inadequate and at times unsafe, learning environments. Education capacity and effectiveness will continue to be constrained by insufficient numbers of paid teachers, lack of teaching and learning supplies and a lack of continuous professional development for teachers and other education personnel.UNRWA has lost 40% of its classrooms due to the conflict. Almost 60% of Palestine refugees, including school children have been displaced at least once since the start of the conflict. Many of UNRWA schools are run in second shift in schools building on loan from Ministry of Education. Schools are in dire need of maintenance and new furniture/equipment. The Agency is striving to deploy and maintain educational staff that can deliver quality education, following the displacement or departure of many qualified staff outside of the country. COVID-19 has added another layer of needs for increased space and provision of sanitization materials. Palestine refugee students are dealing with trauma and stress

5 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will impliment the following activities: •Direct provision of educational services to up to 50,000 Palestine refugee students, through 102 schools; •Provision of psychosocial support for students and staff by counsellors in all UNRWA schools; •Regular maintenance of UNRWA schools and availing basic furniture where necessary •Provision of support and catch up classes to students, in addition to summer learning activities; •Offering students the supplies and materials they need for learning; •Delivery of training to school principals, teacher and students on safety and security, including ERW awareness) and protection; •Procurement and distributing internet packages to staff and vulnerable students where remote learning becomes necessary due to COVID-19 measures; •Procurement and distribution of disinfectants, sanitizers, and hygiene materials to schools; • Procurement and distribution of masks to students and staff; • Offering transport to students returning to damaged camps. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has different structures in place to monitor its interventions, including Education. There is a dedicated Area Support Team that follows up on Protection interventions and a Programme Support Office that handles monitoring and evaluation of activities. UNRWA has an extensive Result Based Monitoring (RBM) systems with indicators for the Education programme and holds quarterly management reviews. The Education Programme conducts regular monitoring field visits.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium • Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is delivering training and ToT sessions to staff to address violence against children (AVAC) and to decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer to peer violence. 2. Self-care sessions are conducted with the UNRWA education staff and counselling and psychosocial support is provided to the students through UNRWA school counsellors 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against children from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. 4. UNRWA provides training on positive behaviour management techniques in the classroom to education staff. 5. UNRWA creates child-friendly learning environments. Protection risk: Students/families referring to negative coping mechanisms due to the declining economic situation (drop out, child labour, early marriage, drug consumption) • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is aware of the lack of professional support for children and their families in need of PSS. UNRWA is doing the outmost possible to contribute to fill this gap. Psychosocial support activities in schools are enhanced, in close cooperation with the UNRWA protection team. 2. UNRWA PSS counsellors provide personalized assistance to students who are identified as having very specific and/or urgent need.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, and SEA including in UNRWA schools• Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1 . UNRWA provides training on protection and gender mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 2. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 3. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 4. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 5. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. 6. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of Palestine Refugees in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk: UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to Palestine Refugees needs because of lack of funding or due to overwhelming increase in humanitarian needs. Mitigation: 1. Ensure efficient and sustainable delivery of operations and services prioritizing those most in need 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to mobilize funding 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems to make best use of available resources 4. Continue to monitor interventions through results-based monitoring framework. Risk: UNRWA staff and Palestine Refugees are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. Adapt programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. Provide PPEs to frontline staff and avail sanitization materials at offices and distribution centres.
