Project Summary

The proposed response plan seeks to contribute to food security and livelihoods intervention on deteriorated communities (Host communities, IDPs Returnees and refugees) in need (IPC3+) as a result of economic crisis, repeated climate shocks, primary widespread of flooding, conflict and insecurity in Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South counties of Northern Bahrel Ghazal in South Sudan through the implementation of CO2#: Emergency Agriculture by Increased food production through in-kind support of farming inputs, fishing kits, livestock restock treatment and vaccination, CO3#: Capacity Building on Good Agronomic Practices for both trainers and farmers, providing context-specific value chain inputs and training to increase food production, market interventions, fishery and farming value chains through an investment of post-harvest handling equipment, climate resistance agriculture, disaster risk reduction, amongst others.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a dire humanitarian crisis, where acute food insecurity and malnutrition levels have risen to alarming rates, particularly in the states of Northern Bahr El Ghazal. The highly food-insecure populations of Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South counties are in locations with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe floods), the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, and low agricultural production due to heavy floods and influx of refugees from Sudan as result of ongoing civil war.The situation in South Sudan especially in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Unity State, Upper Nile, Warrap and Jonglei 67% malnutrition burden continue to worsen with an estimated 6.3 million people in the area experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, Micro-Economics crisis, climate shocks, conflict and insecurity which paint a bleak picture- (IPC Phase 3). According to the (IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition snapshot September 2024 Report), 47% of the analyzed population of 13.4 million people are experiencing high levels of acute food security (IPC phase 3 or above). Picturing the situation more worse compared to same period last year said the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties classified in IPC AMN Phase 3. In defiance of entering a favorable post-harvest period, projections from of October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to deteriorate in 13 counties, while improvement in acute malnutrition is expected in only four counties. The AMN classification in 76 counties is projected to remain at the same in the post-harvest projection period which pose the humanitarian response alert. Continuous heavy rains since May, combined with controlled water releases from Lake Victoria, have significantly contributed to raised Nile River levels, affecting up to 472,000 people across 26 of South Sudan's 78 counties and Abyei Administrative Area. 82% of the most affected communities base on (UN OCHA-Flood Snapshot updates 30/8/2024) are in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, Jonglei and Unity States, 26 counties affected by floods which lead to poor harvest and accessibility as well as inter-communal conflict, cattle raiding, and general insecurity, which have formed the perfect storm for recurrent displacement, further made worse by a combination of heavy rains and massive floods.The influx of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is another need a critical exacerbating putting additional pressure with on an already fragile country. 1.71 million people facing critical levels of acute food insecurity. In (Upper Nile State) an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or aboveIn the harvest/post-harvest undisputed projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, it is estimated that 6.1 million people which is equivalent to (45% of the population) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or worse situation with the high need for humanitarian support.The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap which need the crucial intervention.

2. Project activities

Activities are planned under CO2 and CO3. CO2: Enhance food production will be attained through. i) In-kind distribution of crop, vegetable, and fishing kits to 13,248, IPC3+ households (Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South Counties in Northern Bahrel Ghazal), a) Mobilization of communities, b) PIC formation and training, c) Identification and registration of beneficiaries d) Distribution of inputs, f) Post distribution monitoring, reporting. Medicair will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood input and fishing kits. Fishing kits will be provided to locations with accessibility to fishing grounds to enable them to participate in fishing to meet their food needs and income supplement during crisis times. Rapid awareness on the proper use of fishing kits will be conducted to ensure that fishing is conducted in a way that does not deplete fishing capacity, reduce exposure to water pollution by responsible fishing and disposal and improve fish handling methods and hygiene to improve the nutrition of households; 2) provide crop and vegetable kits and tools. Vegetables, crop seeds and assorted tools will be provided as standard kits. 5 types of preferred vegetable seeds will be provided to the beneficiaries in a total of 8 Kg per household. A beneficiary preference of seeds will be conducted, and ecological and culturally adapted types of seeds will be prioritized. The distribution of seeds will be conducted both in Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism and static operations in locations with elevated IPC phase 3+ and in which Medicair has established presence. Simple awareness creations on farming practices and methods as part of capacity building will be conducted on the spot during the distribution to ensure the recipient households know the production practices and methods and PSEA measure considerably as well as Accountability of Affected Population; 3) Distribution of fishing kits and assorted vegetable seeds as standard kits. Medicair will distribute fishing and vegetable kits to the most vulnerable households to meet their livelihood in 2025 HRP. This will reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shock by 1) capacity building to beneficiaries receiving livelihood support such as GAPs and improved fishing and preservation. The project provision of seeds and tools such as fishing, vegetable and crop seeds will be complemented with capacity building to improve skills and knowledge towards improving production and productivity. CO3: Resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids. i) Strengthening livestock, fishery, and farming value chain through a) Infrastructural support on fish preservation infrastructures (fish drying racks, salting facilities, cold storages, and smoking facilities to 1500 fisher folks a) Farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, exchange visits and farmers’ shows b) Establishment of the demonstration plots, c) Formation of fishing & Farming groups, d) Vocational training across the 12 targeted project locations Community mobilization and sensitization e) beneficiary identification and registration, training iii) Market development Training on SILC community groups. Climate action, including assessment of climate risk, project consultation, Mapping of climate-related risks and dissemination of anticipated risks/disasters, construction or renovation of flood control dykes and establishing complain mechanism and feedback mechanism as well as consideration of gender inclusivity.
