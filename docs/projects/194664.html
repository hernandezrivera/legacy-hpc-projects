Project Summary

The Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) situation in South Sudan remains dire. (All counties including Administrative area of Ruweng) Central Equatoria (Juba, Terekaka, Keji Keji, Yei, Upper Nile State (Longechuk, Maiwut, Ulang,Nasir,Malakal,Baliet and Manyo), Jonglei ( Fangak, Pibor, Pocholla, Twic East, Ayod,Akobo, Uror, Bor), Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil Centre and Aweil North), Warrap ( Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South) and Lakes State (Yirol, Rumbek North, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Wulu).. The specific states and counties targeted for this project remain worst hit by lack of WASH services as a result of floods and a number of other factors including general insecurity, poor socio-economic conditions where there are no markets or if available, functionalities are at minimum. Women and children are among the most affected population and hence increased deaths among U5. According to OCHA Humanitarian Needs Overview Report, it is estimated that 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will continue being impacted in 2023 due to inadequate access to safe water and improved sanitation facilities. This number is higher than the 5.6 million people in need reported in 2021 and this is attributable to protracted conflicts, COVID-19 and perennial floods which have constantly devastated the entire Country for the past 3 years.
The situation is worse in Fangak and Canal Pigi (severity level 5), Abyei, Budi, Kapoeta East, Ayod, Pibor, Twic East, Uror, Awerial, Cueibet, Aweil (Center, East, North), Guit, Leer, Mayom, Rubkona, Fashoda, Longochuk, Luapkini/Nasir, Maiwut, Malakal, Manyo, Melut, Panyikang, Renk, Twic, Jur River and Tambura (Severity level 4). Sanitation facilities across the entire Country are uncommon with 80% of households lacking access. Using of latrines has been considered a taboo in many parts of the country especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population. Constructed latrines are hardly used in certain areas due to societal pressure with some people preferring walking long distances to practice OD even at night.
Floods have damaged already constructed latrines and the situation coupled with conflicts that displaced households from time to time have resulted into households’ fatigue and discouragement to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities.
This project is therefore in line with WASH cluster specific objectives in the HRP (2022)-2023 which are to;
1. Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition
2. Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality
3. Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster
4. Mitigate WASH-related GBV and disability inclusion
5. Provide sustainable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster
Coalition for Humanity will address these gaps by
1. Rehabilitation of water points, distribution of WASH core pipeline items like aqua tabs, water storage facilities and hand washing facilities.
2. Construction and rehabilitation of flood resilient latrines within nutrition facilities and public institutions in the community where IDPs seek temporary shelter during floods.
3. Identification of vulnerable girls and women of child bearing age and provide them with MHM kits.
4. Conducting Protection monitoring and GBV risks analysis at water points to ensure safety of women and girls
5. Training hygiene promoters to create awareness and sensitize communities on the importance of good nutrition, importance of latrine construction and water treatment
6. Establishment and training of water resource management committees that will take over the maintenance of the rehabilitated water points beyond the project funding.
This project will target 20,000 HH (120,000 Individuals) in Central Equatoria (Juba, Terekaka, Keji Keji, Yei) Unity (All counties including Administrative area of Ruweng), Upper Nile (Longechuk, Maiwut, Ulang, Nasir, Malakal, Baliet and Manyo), Jonglei (Fangak, Pibor, Pocholla, Twic East, Ayod, Akobo, Uror, Bor), Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil Centre and Aweil North), Warrap ( Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South) and Lakes State (Yirol, Rumbek North, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Wulu). The beneficiaries will be segregated by gender as follows;

1 - Needs

It is estimated that 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2023 due to inadequate access to safe water and improved sanitation facilities. Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal were found to have more severe WASH conditions with Fangak and Canal-Pigi having been classified as Counties of greatest need. Households travel for more than 30 minutes to access water something that is problematic to households with members living with disabilities. Low coverage of infrastructure has pushed 49% of households to rely on unimproved or surface water sources for their daily needs. Surface water is often untreated before use and this coupled with widespread open defecation has become a public health risk. Lack of jerry cans has also contributed to poor water storage at household level making the situation even worse.Insecurity is another factor that has greatly affected access to water. Protection concerns are common at water points something that has inflicted fear amongst women and girls due to unguaranteed safety. Some 12% of women and girls have reported avoiding water points due to possible GBV cases. Sanitation facilities across the entire Country are uncommon with 80% of households lacking access. The situation being worse in Unity State, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei with only less than 10% of households reporting access to sanitation facilities. South Sudan has historically demonstrated one of the lowest latrine coverages globally. Using a latrine has also been considered a taboo in many parts of the country especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population. Constructed latrines are hardly used in certain areas due to societal pressure with some people preferring walking long distances to practice OD even at night. Floods have also become a disaster by damaging already constructed latrines. This situation coupled with conflicts that displace households from time to time have created a form of fatigue and discouragement of households to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap.

2 - Project activities

1. Construction and Rehabilitation of water points: Coalition for Humanity will construct/ rehabilitate and disinfect existing dysfunctional water sources in order to improve access to clean sufficient water in the community. 2. Household water treatment: Identification, registration and verification and distribution of Aqua tabs, jerry cans and other water storage containers to vulnerable households. 3. Construction and rehabilitation of flood resistant sanitation facilities: Construction/ rehabilitation of community latrine blocks segregated by gender. The latrines will be constructed in institutions like nutrition facilities, churches, schools, health facilities where IDPs are currently residing due to floods and conflicts. The latrines will be able to withstand collapsing soils due to perennial flooding in the project locations. The latrines will also have specific doors fixed with ramps and commodes to address needs of people living with disabilities 4. MHM kit distribution at community and Institutional level: Coalition for humanity will identify vulnerable girls and women of child bearing age and distribute menstrual hygiene and dignity kits. 5. Community and institutional hygiene promotion: Coalition for Humanity will organize targeted outreach on importance of proper sanitation and hygiene, proper nutrition at household level, water treatment and proper menstrual hygiene management. Awareness will take place door to door, at water points and in community meeting. Community members will be educated on proper water storage, water treatment, good nutrition and importance of owning and using latrines. This activity will be achieved through the use of community dialogue tools, fliers, and posters. 6. Training of Hygiene promoters: Coalition for Humanity will Identify hygiene promoters per affected Payam and train them on water quality monitoring, importance of hygiene and sanitation, water treatment and menstrual hygiene. The hygiene promoters will be assigned to reach households with hygiene information and monitor water treatment behavior, monitor access to latrines, hand washing facilities and nutrition status of household members especially children under the age of five and lactating mothers. 7. AAP/HLP/Protection/GBV safety audits: Coalition for Humanity will conduct AAP/HLP/Protection/GBV safety audits at water points and distribution sites to monitor the risks that are likely to affect women and girls on their way to collect water.

3 - Project indicators

The project indicators include: 1. Number of water points constructed/ rehabilitated 2. Number of people (men, women, girls and boys) accessing safe water from improved water sources. 3. Number of flood resistant sanitation facilities constructed and segregated by gender 4. Number of men, women, girls and boys accessing improved sanitation 5. Number of women and girls accessing MHM kits 6. Number of people reached with awareness on importance good sanitation and hygiene and nutrition. 7. Number of hygiene promoters trained on WASH awareness creation 8. Number of latrines constructed addressing needs of people living with disabilities

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Local staff are leading the process of collecting the feedback from the beneficiaries especially individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are illiterate, women and girls. Most of the meetings and consultations are delivered in local languages hence helping members to effectively contribute. We also encourage beneficiaries who have skills in different languages(Nuer, Dinka, Arabic) to provide it as they know it and if need be get our translators to interpret for us the content they shared. This helps in ensuring nobody is left behind in our project intervention. Coalition for Humanity will also ensure that feed back from people living with disabilities is used to improve sanitation facilities to address their needs. This will include construction of ramps, commodes and ensuring enough space and lighting in the latrines. There will be specific doors for persons living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the beginning before project role out, a number of inception meetings will be carried out. These consultative and engagement meetings with beneficiaries will help further explain the nature of support to the beneficiaries and obtain their feedback on how such support will be delivered to them. The inceptions and other consultative meetings will build on earlier engagements (pre project design engagements) done with members of the community while this proposal was being prepared. Setting up complaint mechanisms and referral channels during and after the project are some of the important tasks to be carried out. Effective community mobilization meetings and Focus group discussions, safety audits with different groups mostly women and girls will be done to seek their views on safety of distribution points.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for Humanity has Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place. All staff members including consultants and volunteers sign it before engagement. The organization protection focal points in the field locations organize standalone sessions on PSEA. PSEA discussions are also incorporated in meetings and trainings that are conducted from time to time. PSEA is therefore an ongoing process and is one of the important aspects of our project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback gathered from the beneficiaries during inceptions meetings, monthly and complaints feedback boxes placed in strategic locations are collected, analyzed and the feedback used to improve and adapt the project implementation strategy. An action on feedback from the beneficiaries is incorporated into the project implementation matrix
