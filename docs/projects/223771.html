Project Summary

According to UNICEF's global commitments outlined in the IASC Framework and the Core Commitments for Children in Humanitarian Action (updated in 2022), the Venezuela Country Office, in collaboration with OCHA, ensures effective humanitarian coordination through the following actions:
Leading or co-leading three clusters (education, nutrition, and WASH) and one area of responsibility (child protection).
Contributing to inter-cluster coordination.
Incorporating inter-agency coordination functions within its programs at subnational levels, while promoting and facilitating coordination among local humanitarian actors.
Enhancing the strategic integration of the aforementioned sectors within the Local Coordination Forums for humanitarian planning and monitoring.
This project aims to coordinate these clusters and the area of responsibility for child protection. The main objective is to ensure continuity for coordinators and information management staff at the central level, while also covering operational costs, including travel, capacity-building initiatives, and increased participation in local coordination forums.
Within the Cluster Approach, UNICEF is dedicated to facilitating coordination functions, establishing assessment and information management systems, strengthening capacity-building efforts, prepositioning supplies, and offering technical support and guidance to cluster members. UNICEF advocates for compliance with quality humanitarian standards, highlighting child rights, gender, and the integration of protection, while also promoting principled humanitarian actions that align with established humanitarian principles. Furthermore, UNICEF seeks to enhance the involvement of local and national NGOs within the cluster framework.
As the Cluster Lead Agency (CLA) for Nutrition, WASH, Education and Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AoR) in the Protection Cluster, UNICEF will implement the six core functions alongside Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and PSEA as outlined by the IASC. These functions encompass supporting service delivery that is consistent with the Humanitarian Response Plan, guiding strategic decision-making for the HC/HCT through thorough needs assessments, developing and executing cluster strategies, monitoring and assessing performance, strengthening preparedness capacities, and advocating vigorously on behalf of cluster members and those affected.
By sustaining and improving the effectiveness of these clusters, the initiative aims to enhance the overall response to the needs of children and communities facing crises.
Contributing to localization through the enhancement of local CSO.
