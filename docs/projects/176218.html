Project Summary

This project aimed to achieve objective which is to reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including gender-based violence, through the provision of specialized protection services in Mundri East (Lui, Kedi’ba, Lakama’di and Lozoh), Mundri West (Mundri) and Mvolo (Mvolo and Yeri) counties, to 16,000 beneficiaries who includes (6623g/5333b/1769m/2275w). With total envelope of $479,999 of which AYA have already secured $45,000 for CP programming in Mundri- 2021.
Active Youth Agency (AYA) intends to continue running and establishing child friendly spaces and work in community based psychosocial support programming, conduct case management to survivors of child abuse, create awareness raising using different methods and most creative ways which include door-to-door awareness campaigns, community outreaches, radio talk shows, mass campaign through the use of public address systems and street theatre, boda-boda talk –talk, talking radio program with pre-recorded messages, use of social media platforms such as face books, whatsApp, histograms, tweeter, printing and distribution of IEC materials. Training of Care givers, parents and community leaders on Child Protection, parenting skills to help preserve family unity and effective child protection means and methods, develop and disseminate referral pathways using the GBV best practices, conducting quarterly case based simulations with the Case Workers and group coaching on the complicated cases to support the staffs improve on the weak points, refresher training to Case Workers and CFS Animators, form and train community based protection networks in Mundri East and Mvolo, conduct monthly meeting with community based protection network members, work with parental groups, establish and strengthen Child Protection School Clubs, support the formation and strengthening of child protection desks especially through working with schools and other non-child protection actors, and work to trace and reunify the separated and unaccompanied children with their parents in Mundri East specifically.
Active Youth Agency (AYA) is running projects with International Rescue Committee in Caring for Child Survivor’s Initiative in Juba, supporting children during the COVID-19 pandemic in Mundri with support from War Child and UNICEF. These two projects are running and currently building capacity of Active Youth Agency in various aspects of Child Protection. AYA has functional Child Safeguarding policy that guide the conduct with children in its areas of operation. All the staffs have been taken through the policy. All the staffs of AYA have signed Code of Conduct that helps to work in guided way to avoid abuse to the beneficiaries. The organization specifically has PSEA policy and all staffs are taken through the policy and have full knowledge and implication of breaching the rule. AYA participated in training conducted by Cash Working group on Core skills for Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) and a member of Cash Working Group in South Sudan. AYA is GBV Prevention Technical Working Group (TRG) Co-coordinator, supports coordinating prevention activities across South Sudan. AYA is GBV Working Group Co-coordinator for Mundri. The organization will use the coordination skills to mainstream Child Protection across sectors which include GBV, WASH, Livelihood, Nutrition, and Education among other selected sectors. AYA is also among the few GBV Data Gathering Organizations (DGOs) in South Sudan piloting Gender Based Violence Information Management System Plus (GBVIMS+). Therefore, there is already existing capacity in terms of case management and quality CP programming. This presents good match for the project and its mainstreaming across the sectors.

1 - Needs

Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate protection services or will have protection concerns in South Sudan in 2021. An estimate conducted by Protection Cluster in 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). However, entire people in need (PIN) estimated to be 8.3 million2. These attributed to rising food insecurity (IPC classification- December 2020), insecurity and sub-national conflicts, economic crises or rise in commodities, floods, disease outbreaks that is supported by weak health systems and institutions, persistent gender inequality, early and forced marriage, and child abuse in different forms including neglect, child separation from families, inadequate services specifically targeted to the children. The early pregnancies as result of COVID-19 restrictions coupled with the weak legal institutions to hold the perpetrators accountable gave opportunity for the out-of-school adolescent girls and boys to suffer the violence quietly. The main drivers of Child Protection needs include widespread insecurity, sub-national violence and recurring flood, increased displacement places an extra burden on limited available services. The recent conflict that displaced 6200 people from Tindilo and Tali Payams of Terekeka to Kedi’ba mainly children and women is an example (IRNA- Mundri East- 26th-27th January 2021). The returning population in Mundri East towns and, Mundri West and, Mvolo County which has left children and their care givers with limited to no services (IRNA- 16th-27th March 2020). 2020 floods also posed threat to the population of Greater Mundri Counties. Food destroyed, homes destroyed, people sleep in open with limited or no privacy which makes children to develop bad characters and negative copying mechanisms (Mundri East IRNA- October 2020).Mundri East has experienced the violent conflict since 2015. The County has been in persistent war and the communities especially children remained with poor or no services. After the signing of Revitalized Peace Agreement in 2018, the normalcy returned to the other parts of the County. Kedi’ba and Lozoh remained under stress due to presence of National Salvation Front. Schools remained closed, level of distress increased with the children and separation from parents in form of forceful recruitment or enlistment in the rungs. The increased number of Internally Displaced Persons from Terekeka County in Mundri East particularly Kedi’ba Payam leaves the IDP children in need of psychosocial support services, life skills and skill building services. The same displacement resulted to children getting separated and unaccompanied. The IRNA assessment conducted in Kedi’ba in 26th-27th January 2021 suggested number of separated and Unaccompanied Children who needed multiple supports. Mvolo County remained without any service despite the huge humanitarian situation those stems from Inter-communal fighting which regularly push people from their areas to Mvolo town. The persistent gender inequality in the county is one of key driving factors for child abuse. This heightened the rate of early and forced marriage in the County. Children suffer psychologically due to lack of basic services, psychosocial support services and parental guidance on how to lead decent lives (IRNA- March 2020). The illiteracy rate among the children remained high in Mvolo. While no organization offer child protection services that is targeted towards the protection of children from abuse and exploitation. In Mundri West, COVID-19 and floods affected the population. The reports indicated 87 girls getting pregnant within one month in three health facilities. This poses threat on the lives of girls and boys whose development is affected in one way or another. The children associated with armed forces and armed groups were not properly reintegrated with their families.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AYA has PSEA Focal Point and the organizational staffs are sensitized on the policy. There is also Code of Conduct AYA has developed and signed by every staff. There will be continuous awareness to the community about the involvement of AYA’s staff in exploiting the vulnerability of the community and also availing the contacts of the Focal Point at any stage of activity implementation. If found, there is already body set to investigate or refer.AYA will help the communities to access equal services, during meetings share about Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and work with partners to report cases related to SEA to enable the community to receive aid without fearing any harassment or abuses. There is already IOM poster on PSEA that will be used. AYA’s new staffs will be given code of conducts to sign alongside the contracts. Education and enforcement of code of conduct is vital which AYA will conduct by ensuring that all new staff and volunteers are oriented and also that there is an annual refresher session for existing staff and volunteers. AYA has the Focal Point on SEA and the organization is capable of doing its own investigation. There has been no report involving our staff. However, community does get awareness and there has been sharing that AYA uses its channel of referral.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

AYA will have feedback sessions with the community leaders, beneficiaries including persons with disability and old age to communicate the results of complaints received from the beneficiaries. This will help to instill the trust and above all the centrality of protection where recipients of aid are at the center of the aid delivery.Different modalities will be used in disseminating the feedback using the appropriate channels and methods of delivery. This includes using sign language, communicating and meetings to enable every category of community to feel that their complaint is heard and been addressed. AYA will also work to address the complaints through going to other partners for meeting and discussing the feedback if it concerns the services of other partners.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

AYA will set complaint desks in all the project activity areas. The focal person will be visible and community leaders, members, persons with special needs will be sensitized on how to launch complaint during distribution or any activity. The community grievances will be documented by the focal point person and addressed appropriately.There is already complaint mechanism in place that AYA use for collecting complaints, analyzing and disseminating the complaints for decision making purposes. The complaint desk is established in each women and girls friendly space, it can be inform of suggestion boxes, client satisfaction survey for the consumers of AYA's services or focus group discussion. The table is also placed and marked complaint desk. This helps the beneficiaries feel comfortable to launch their complaints. There are also already established phone contacts given to community for launching complaints against the activities of Active Youth Agency. This therefore, will be communicated and addressed as it arises.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The complaints will be used in redesigning the activities of Active Youth Agency (AYA). The views of beneficiaries will be incorporated to reflect the best practices and therefore, making the aid friendly to all the beneficiaries. The regular project management meetings, weekly, monthly and quarterly plans will take in consideration the complaints or feedback from the beneficiaries regarding to the services offered by AYA.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Proper planning, consultation of every group of beneficiaries, disseminating PSEA messages, making every staff sign Code of Conduct before starting any engagement with the community, strengthening community complaint mechanism, dissemination of humanitarian principles to the key stakeholders, training staffs on key GBV guiding principles and conducting assessments prior to the distribution of items. AYA will orient all of its volunteers on six principles of PSEA. This will include all the staff either part time or full time volunteers. Community awareness shall be done to communities on issues pertaining sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. The contacts for the focal points shall be given to the community members to enable reports of any act constitute SEA. Therefore, the proper feedback mechanism to the community shall be established to generate and disseminate some feedback to the communities. All these will help to mitigate the risks that are anticipated. AYA have signed data management protocol. This will mitigate the challenge of mismanagement of data. AYA will integrate awareness on UXO and disseminate it to the children and beneficiaries of the project.
