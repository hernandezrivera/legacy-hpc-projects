Project Summary

Please provide a short summary of the intervention, describing its purpose and target. – Limit 4000 characters.
JAM’s proposed interventions in 2019 will target 20,000 households in partnership with WFP,FAO and the community leadership in Jonglei ( 3420HH), Warrap ( 3332HH), Northern Bahr el Ghazal ( 4344HH), Central Equatoria ( 1409HH), Western Equatoria ( 5088HH) and Unity( 2407HH) States .Most of the targeted counties are in the IPC phase 3,and 4 with a possibility of phase 5 without humanitarian assistance. The project aims at increasing immediate access to nutritious food, safeguarding livelihoods and assets while supporting the sustainable recovery of agriculture and pastoral livelihood systems in the vulnerable households in the target communities. The Interventions are designed to help address the immediate food needs while also catalyzing recovery of productive assets, food production, and livelihood systems. It will simultaneously deliver short-term emergency relief and support the safeguarding and recovery of assets (infrastructure, inputs, skills, labor) that underpin long-term resilience of agriculture production and rural livelihood systems. General food distribution and cash-for –assets will address the immediate food needs as well as livelihoods assets restoration. Households unable to participate in work projects due to age, health, or other considerations will receive unconditional cash. Agricultural Livelihood support will provide inputs for families to meet their immediate food needs while restoring their own crop production and access to fish. Use of seed and tools alongside capacity building will ensure that there is improved production through adoption of better farming methods. Vulnerable households in riverine communities will be provided with fishing kits to increase fish consumption therefore enrichening diets with vital proteins, vitamins and micronutrients diversifying diets which otherwise tend to focus primarily on carbohydrates. To safeguard livestock assets and pastoral livelihoods, the project will support the scale up of emergency livestock interventions. Animals in target areas will be vaccinated/treated against a variety of debilitating conditions that threaten livestock productivity, value, health and survival. Training will be provided to increase the number of veterinary teams able to recognize the signs of malnourishment and disease in livestock and provide appropriate treatments and vaccinations.The project will not only address the immediate causes of food insecurity but also build on structures to address the underlying causes of food insecurity through capacity building of the locals and also integration with public health targeting, WASH, and nutrition .The project will work with community leaders to identify specific needs at the Boma level, resulting in a list of individual beneficiaries. The Boma-level targeting will ensure minimum coverage inclusion of women and women-headed households, as well as other vulnerable groups, including but not limited to elderly and handicapped individuals.

Needs

The performance of the economy in South Sudan has remained poor since the onset of the protracted crisis in 2013.The currency depreciation had been rapid and accompanied by high inflation. Consumer price inflation in the year to March was measured at 161% (UNMISS February-June 2018).Public sector workers continue to face prolonged delays in receiving salaries, in some cases reportedly extending to five months, and levels of spending on public services and infrastructure remain very low. The conflict has limited the population’s safe access to arable land, disrupted livelihoods and left significant segments of the population highly dependent on humanitarian assistance, all contributing to high food levels of insecurity. The latest South Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification report (September, 2018) shows that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Food security has improved slightly with the green harvest in September relative to July and August, and further improvements are expected in the post-harvest period between October and December 2018 when the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse is most likely to reduce to 4.4 million (43% of the total population). However, an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). These estimates are in the presence of planned humanitarian food assistance.. The project will not only address the immediate causes of food insecurity but also build on structures to address the underlying causes of food insecurity through capacity building of the locals and also integration with other sectors such as WASH and nutrition.The project aims at addressing the short-term needs in emergencies, the medium term needs through rehabilitation programmes. The emergency interventions stabilise populations who do not, in the short term, have the capacity to meet their basic material needs. This means, this has meant targeted feeding programmes, distribution of seeds & tools, interventions in water and public health targeting those suffering the effects of conflict

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The BCC messages will be developed by the project with several variations adapted to local social, and gender contexts. The food and nutrition status enhancement component of the project will target women/mother groups through BCC messages, promotion of dietary diversity made possible by the production of different types of food. Members of women/mothers groups receiving these nutrition-sensitive interventions will also likely be members of the crop and livestock FFSs that will be inclusive of all genders and age groups. Given the importance of gender and social exclusion in South Sudan, BCC messages will be adapted to the cultural context and gender dynamics in the household and community. This involves specifically targeting husbands, mothers-in-law, or other influential figures in the community when appropriate.

JAM has helpdesks at the food distribution sites that offers two options that can help answer questions on AAP and PSEA.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A risk assessment undertaken during project preparation indicates two types of vulnerabilities for the proposed food security project: (i) macro risks, and (ii) project risks common to unconditional cash transfer programs. Macro or country risks identified are (i) political commitment will be maintained;, (ii) a deteriorating revenue position of the government will enable participation of staff, and (iii) weaknesses in the supply side of health and nutrition provision. Project risks include (i) targeting and selection risks, given the large scope of close to 120000 beneficiaries; (ii) payment and transfer risks and (iii) implementation capacity risks typical for projects with numerous project locations in remote and inaccessible areas. Targeting risks include manipulation of the household master list, political interference in beneficiary selection, and errors in selection of beneficiaries. Several measures and mechanisms are in place to counter and mitigate these risks by inclusion of several strong anti-corruption mitigation measures in the project design, including: (i) an interagency governance structure ranging from country to beneficiary ( PMCs) levels to coordinate supply-side and compliance monitoring; (ii) beneficiary targeting based on objective, poverty-based criteria that mitigate against intervention and manipulation; (iii) channeling payments directly to beneficiaries; (iv) robust monitoring and audit systems, including rigorous spot checks and community groups engagement in oversight and monitoring; and (v) transparency and accountability through a strong focus on public information campaigns and a grievance redress system.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Expanding cultivation in the project areas will likely require addressing several crosscutting factors that potentially inhibit the use of land for agricultural production. These include social and political consideration such as on-going conflicts over land ownership, grazing rights. For unconditional cash grants, markets are not well integrated because of trade barriers that include trade route barriers such as conflict, poor road access, storage constraints. Food prices and the price of food between markets have both sharply increased. These increases vary depending on the market and the food product and are attributable to the worsening economic and political conditions hence affecting the value of the unconditional cash grants. Though there are several technical deficiencies that likely limit agricultural production in the country; the evidence base in other weak and fragile states suggests that many production barriers are likely to be political instead of technical. Therefore, conflict resilience must be promoted by learning from and adapting coping strategies.Poor market access for producers and weak storage infrastructure have potentially reduced agricultural productivity in these surplus areas in the project. The possibility of wasting much needed agricultural produce d insecurity, lost food surpluses may act as a long-term disincentive toproduce future agricultural surpluses. These will be mitigated through capacity building in post-harvest preservation and cost-effective and innovative way for smallholder farmers to store their grains is through community level low-capacity metal silos. Through cash –for-assets, the road will be rehabilitated/rebuild to enhance access to functioning markets for the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?
