Project Summary

The proposed intervention is in line with the 2019 HRP priority areas defined by the Food Security Cluster. Indeed the proposed project will target Tel Afar, Tilkaif, Hamdanyia. The project complies with the 2019 HRP Strategic Objective 1: post-conflict transition towards durable solutions.
The main objective of the project is to support return and recovery processes of IDPs in Federal Iraq.
Based on TGH past and ongoing FSL actions, the present project has been designed taking into account the various local contexts and needs that strongly differ from area to another. Considering the diversity of needs calling for tailored need based responses, TGH proposes a range of solutions.
Past actions implemented both in Federal Iraq and KRI (fodder and animal distributions in Rabia area, mobile veterinary clinics in Tilkaif and Rabia area, partnership with Dohuk Veterinary College) and recent assessments have allowed TGH to develop a strong understanding of agriculture related needs.
Based on TGH observations in IDP camps (in which TGH implements CP activities) and field assessments, barriers to return for IDPs toward their areas of origin needs to be overcome with an adequate support to allow a durable and dignified return. Most vulnerable population groups will be targeted by the project (farmers with a limited number of animals, matching a range of vulnerability criteria such as the level of incomes, number of children, etc.).
Proposing a mix immediate and longer term solutions is mandatory to allow immediate and durable returns of IDPs: they need to be able to quickly restart a productive activity, while the extensive damages on productive private and public assets / infrastructure call for a longer term recovery and rehabilitation approach. To that extent, TGH aims at implementing quick impact activities (e.g.: mobile veterinary clinics, fodder and animals distribution) and working in parallel on sustainable activities such as rehabilitation of water efficient irrigation assets, training of veterinary staffs, implementation of solar powered irrigation, mapping of available irrigation resources for a more sustainable water management, rehabilitation of market chain infrastructures, etc.
Considering the damaged social fabric due to the recent events and the diversity of ethnic groups, TGH will pay a strong attention to the do no harm approach within this project. Equity and transparency in the delivered support will be crucial to avoid fueling or creating tensions.
Since part of the present project is already funded by the French Development Agency (36 months project- starting early 2019), significant operational and means synergies are expected between the foreseen activities and funds.

Needs

In addition to the consequences of the conflict in Syria that started in 2011, the conflict that broke out in Iraq in 2014 led to massive population displacements which went on following the retaking of ISIS-controlled areas by Iraqi forces and the Battle of Mosul until its liberation in July 2018. The 2018 Iraq Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) recognize that 8.7 million people require assistance in the country; among them 1.5 million IDPs, 2.2 million returnees, 3.8 million of vulnerable host community residents and 226,000 refugees from Syria.The agricultural value chains have been severely affected by this conflict. The World Bank has assessed the total losses of the agricultural sector at 2.1 billion USD. Nineveh Governorate suffered the highest losses (655 MUSD). The agricultural sector in Nineveh has been largely disorganized by the conflict: structuring public infrastructures such as irrigated perimeters, slaughterhouses, markets etc. have been damaged, as well as private equipment and assets (orchards, poultry houses, feed production units…).As the conflict resulted in the destruction of rural areas and the widespread looting of agricultural assets, those in the farming industry were forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms to meet their families’ needs, including the selling of productive assets (livestock, equipment, supplies). Both host communities and internally displaced persons (IDPs) residing in rural areas are hugely affected by poverty and food insecurity. Roughly 55% of Nineveh’s livestock was lost during the conflict, and losses keep increasing as farmers sell animals to get much needed cash, and because of the scarcity of pastures. The recurrence of animal diseases (with lack of governmental resources to implement appropriate vaccination campaigns), the competition over the use of communal pastures and the cost and availability of animal feed are the main constraints to the sector’s recovery. It is estimated by the Ministry of Agriculture that around 120,000-150,000 cattle; 750,000-1,000,000 sheep and goats are in need of immediate support.Field assessment carried out by TGH in 2018 in Hamdaniya district in Nineveh Governorate confirms the above conclusions and highlights access to irrigation, agricultural infrastructure and high-value production are the main priorities for farmers. In Telkaif and Tel Afar, access to veterinary supplies and services is still a strong need as identified by TGH through previous and ongoing intervention.The return movements of IDP toward their areas of origin have been ongoing with the progressive liberation of the areas from ISIS. However, the level of destruction and the lack of livelihood opportunities resulting in years of ISIS occupation and the military operations are important barrriers to durable returns of IDPs. Restarting agriculture and creating new opportunities are key factors for the future reconstruction and stability of Nineveh. For Telafar district, the areas of Telafar district centre and Zummar sub-district will be targeted. for Tilkaif district, the sub-districts of Tilkaif district centre, Wana, Al Qosh and Faida will be targeted

Activities

The main objectives of the present intervention if to address the diversity of needs in terms of Food Security and Livelihoods based on different humanitarian dynamics. In Federal Iraq, areas liberated from ISIS face extensive damages including in agriculture sectors (loss of productive assets and value chain infrastructures, destocking of animals, etc.) while in KRI Syrian refugees remain in a protracted situation with no short term perspectives of return in Syria. Therefore the proposed project proposes to support the agriculture recovery process through a re-dynamization of the agricultural sector in liberated areas to support return movement of IDPs towards their areas of origin. The proposed intervention combines activities that are already funded and activities for which TGH will seek financial resources in order to fully deploy its global strategy. Within the frame of a French Development Agency fund for Hamdanyia District, TGH has been granted a three year project in consortium with Mercy Corps – lead agency- and PAO, activities falling under this grant are notified as “funded”.To answer identified needs, TGH is planning a threefold intervention;*Support to high value agricultural production, such as vegetable farmingAccording to the different situations prevailing in the foreseen areas of intervention, the activities planned under this section aims at relaunching, diversifying or expanding the agricultural production in order to improve household livelihoods, diet diversification, develop and disseminate new agriculture techniques and ultimately decrease the dependence to humanitarian aid. Area of Hamdaniya (funded)- Market linkage with local areas to ensure sustainability of the vegetable production- Introduction of solar-powered irrigation schemes- Sustainable management of natural resources (mostly water)*Support to animal healthOne of the main factors of destocking dynamics in the foreseen areas of intervention in Federal Iraq is the lack of access to appropriate veterinary care and treatments. As a consequence, morbidity and mortality rates have seen significant increases, contributing to farmers’ vulnerability. TGH has developed in the past year, a mobile veterinary clinic approach which has shown its relevance to local context; vulnerable famers (selected based on a range of vulnerability criteria including the number of animals, number of children, woman headed household, etc.) are supported with treatments provided by TGH veterinary doctors, farmers also receive advice on animal care and disease identification. TGH has developed a partnership with the faculty of veterinary medicine of Dohuk that allows students to participate to the activities and develop their skills. Other activities aiming at encouraging restocking of animals and improving the global health status are planned, as described below:Area of Hamdaniya (funded); Area of Tel Afar and Telkaif (not funded)- Focus on vulnerable households (less than 50 livestock heads, income stream only from husbandry, female headed households, etc)- Distribution of livestock to the most vulnerable small herders households- Distribution of fodder in winter season- Deployment of mobile veterinary clinics- Rehabilitation of dipping areas - Capacity building of veterinary services (livestock mapping, medicine stock management, material dotation, veterinary student on-field training, para-vets)*Support to rehabilitation and construction of agricultural infrastructureAs a part of TGH integrated approach aiming at increasing the impact of its project, TGH will also intervene on public value chain infrastructures, highly impacted by the conflict, that have a significant role in agriculture product dissemination and an influence on the potential incomes for farmers. Area of Hamdaniya (funded)- Based on market and value chain studies, identification of relevant collective market infrastructure to be rehabilitated; either linked to sector specific production or post production.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks are critical in the ongoing context of humanitarian work in Iraq, and most even specifically so for child protection actors. It is TGH’s inherent role to implement activities aiming directly at preventing and responding to protection risks, especially child protection risks.Some key protection risks identified for the hereby-proposed intervention are the following:- Risks related to perception of ISIS affiliation among beneficiaries, especially in Sunni communities in Federal Iraq, with specific risks encountered by adolescents and women. - Unequal access to services between age, gender, social/ethnic/religious groups, etc, that can be caused by tradition, reluctance to gather with peers, distance, security constraints, lack of information, etc- Safety risks for beneficiaries and staff due to working in areas still contaminated by ERW and IEDs, which is especially true for breeders.- Safety risks for staff linked with regular check-point crossing and volatile context prevailing in Federal Iraq

Mitigation Measures

Risk mitigation strategies include a large set of measures:- TGH is mainstreaming protection in all its intervention, including the Do No Harm principles and the Age Gender Diversity (AGD) approach to take into account age, gender and special needs in all its activities, from the designing of the intervention to the implementation, monitoring, project’s closure and evaluation. TGH’s approach is inclusive and promotes social cohesion, especially in locations where different religious and ethnic communities live together, or locations with a mix of Host Communities, Returnees, IDPs or refugees. TGH conduct regular needs assessments to ensure intervention are based on needs, vulnerabilities and risks, and are designed to provide an adapted response.- TGH works in close collaboration with authorities and involve communities at all stage of its intervention, to ensure the acceptance, relevance and adaptation of its activities. TGH operates a regular monitoring of security context and developed Contingency plans. Regarding the risks of ERW/IED, TGH is in contact with both local actors and with UNMAS. TGH teams are deployed only in areas considered as safe in terms of ERW/ IED, TGH teams have received regular briefings from specialized actors regarding explosive hazards and the behavior to adopt when facing such risks.- In terms of staff security, TGH has strict internal security procedures and regularly trains and updates its staff members. Permanent security context follow up allows TGH to deploy its teams based on a sharp analysis of the risk prevalence.- Protection Risk Analysis are done on a regular basis and Risk mitigation measures are set for all interventions.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability to affected population (AAP) is ensured through various mechanisms, such as - Community consultation at the assessment, design, implementation and project’s closure’s stages, through interviews (key informants, community leaders, etc) and Focus Group Discussions (organized per age and gender); TGH will adjust the planned activities based on consultation with communities that will be conducted all along the project. This permanent dialogue will allow maintaining the project relevance and ensure it is fully in line with beneficiaries’ needs.- Regular communication on available services (Communication with Communities – CwC)- Involvement and empowerment by supporting community based groups (farmers’ group, women’s cultivation groups) to design, organize and monitor activities - Set-up of ideas box and complaint feedback mechanisms (hotline, regular meetings with communities,etc.) in order to increase participation and adapt the response.- Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) : a sample of beneficiaries of the proposed activities will be interviewed in order to gather quantitative and qualitative feedbacks on the support they have received. These modalities aim at increasing population’s participation, appropriation and influence on the intervention. TGH provide them with feedbacks following consultation and complaints.
