Project Summary

The ongoing Israeli blockade has devastating consequences on all aspects of life. Gaza Strip has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world (more than 50%), and 53% of Gaza's population live in poverty. On top of the chronic crisis, the emerging of the COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded the already fragile situation leaving families and caregivers unable to provide for their children’s basic needs, increasing the number of children displaying high levels of stress-related symptoms, requiring additional protection interventions aiming at reducing vulnerability and increasing protection. A Child Protection Rapid Assessments was conducted by Tdh in 2020 has shown that violence against children and resorting to negative coping mechanisms are common in the Gaza Strip. The Assessment has revealed that 63% of the study participants reported the use of physical and verbal violence against their children and children expressed stress manifestations such as aggressive behavior, anxiety, and unwillingness to go to school. The Protection Cluster has identiﬁed providing MHPSS services, case management as key interventions needed in the Gaza Strip. With such context, it is undoubtedly that the need for the provision of protection responses for affected children and their families, such as case management and MHPSS services are highly demanded. This Action is a response to such need and it is consistent with HRP's first Strategic Objective and it the second Objective of Protection Cluster which is Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conﬂict-related violence. The proposed activities include the provision of child protection services, including case management, MHPSS services, and awareness-raising activities focusing on reducing the prevalence of GBV and on how to deal with a traumatized child. Overall, 7,625 persons will benefit from the project (1,300 girls, 1,300 boys, 2,513 women, and 2,512 men). Of the targeted children, 300 children will benefit from case management and 100 will benefit from MHPSS provided to children survivors of GBV. Of the total targeted, the provided protection services will be timely and of good quality protection responses that are tailored according to children's needs and the best interest of boys and girls, equally. Under this Action, a total of 7000 persons will beneﬁt from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV (4000 persons) and how to deal with traumatized children victims of the current situation and general child protection (3000 persons).
The total allocated budget for this project is USD 350,000. The targeted beneﬁciaries, including children and adults from different age groups, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project by identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneﬁciaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according to the speciﬁc needs of children, taking into account their gender. To closely monitor and follow up on the results and achievements of the project, a Project Monitoring and Evaluation Plan will be developed at the start of the Action with the participation of the four partner organizations. The plan will comprise the indicators, assessments, and means of veriﬁcation tools deﬁned in the logical framework. The Project team will coordinate with the relevant authorities, child protection actors, the Protection Cluster, GBV-sub-cluster, and CP-AoR.

1 - Needs

The ever-deepening crises resulting from the ongoing blockade, occupation, and political lift have led to a progressive deterioration in the political, social, and security conditions of the Gaza Strip. Currently, the unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip is 52%, more than half of Gaza's population live in poverty, and 68% of households are experiencing severe or moderate levels of food insecurity. The emerge of the COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded the already fragile situation leaving families and caregivers unable to provide for their children’s basic needs. The pandemic is expected to have wide-reaching economic and social impacts; earlier this year, the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) estimates that at least 53,000 families have fallen into poverty, due to the loss of a source of income. In Gaza, the share of poor households is expected to reach 64%. According to reports from the World Bank (2020), the pandemic has pushed a greater number of households into poverty and food insecurity, with an increase of at least 10% of the current poverty rate. Recently, Tdh has conducted a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) to assess the impact of COVID-19 on vulnerable children and their families. The study involved collecting primary data from 603 households benefiting from Tdh projects (352 households) and the Cash Transfer Program of the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) (251 households). The Assessment covered the impact of COVID-19 on different areas, mainly child protection, coping mechanisms, education, and food security. The study findings have revealed that most vulnerable families face difficulties in securing enough food and water for their families, in addition to the limited affordability to purchase hygiene material (Tdh, 2020). The Assessment has also revealed that poor families resort to negative coping mechanisms such as sending their children to work and reducing the quantity and quality of consumed food to alleviate poverty and to reduce vulnerability (Tdh, 2020). Finally, about 63% of the study participants reported the use of physical and verbal violence against their children (Tdh, 2020). Finally, according to Protection Cluster, a total of 198,000 children in Gaza require structures protection and psychosocial support, 274,000 adults require, MHPSS services, and 36,000 children are in need of individual case management (Protection Cluster, 2020). Additionally, Tdh and partners work has revealed an increase in the number of children who require case management and MHPSS services mainly due to an increase in the prevalence of distress among children, an increase in the violence against children, along with an increase in the number of children engaged in child labor and in the number of children being out-of-school or at risk of. The most affected groups at children aged between 10 to 16 years old. In general, children with disabilities and girls are more subjected to GBV however, boys are at higher risk to be engaged in child labor. Access of vulnerable children to comprehensive child protection services that involve the provision of individual case management needs to be enhanced. Additionally, activities that could empower their families like cash-assistance, and food vouchers. All these factors illustrate the importance of providing children and youth with integrated protection services, including structured MHPSS and child protection case management. This project is a response to needs. The targeted beneficiaries, including children and adults from different age groups, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project by identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according to the specific needs of children, taking into account their gender and their age groups.

2 - Activities

This Action aims to increase access to protection services including case management, MHPSS services, individual counseling to vulnerable children who have child protection concerns, including children display symptoms of psychological distress, children survivors of GBV, and children victims of different forms of child abuse and violence against children. The proposed activities will include the provision of case management and MHPSS services to 600 children, including 100 children survivors of GBV, and 500 children who have different child protection concerns, including all child kinds of child abuse. The provision of services will utilize a mixed modality of remote and in-person service provision to reach a large number of the affected population giving the current COVID-19 situation. In total, the provided protection services will target boys and girls equally, 300 boys and 300 girls. According to child's age, different PSS/MHPSS manuals will be used, for instance, children below the age of 12, Psychosocial Rehabilitation of Children Affected by Armed Conﬂicts and/or Violence Guide will be used and Healing and Education through Art Guide will be used for children aged from 13 to 18 years old. The project staff will also use child protection psychosocial manuals developed by Tdh. Additionally, a total of 4000 persons (1000 men, 1000 women, 1000 boys, and 1000 girls) will beneﬁt from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV, as a remedial activity to tackle the issue of GBV. Additionally, under this Action, awareness sessions on how to deal with traumatized children- victims of the current situation and general child protection will be conducted targeting 3000 members, including (1500 men and 1500 women). Protection services will be provided to all children in need, regardless of their age groups; services will be also provided to their families regardless of the gender and age of the head of the household.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh and partners will fully respect, protect, and fulﬁl beneﬁciaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh and partners will achieve this by protecting beneﬁciaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and Id numbers. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data that could reveal any identiﬁcation data, all publications/reports will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneﬁciaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access to humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment that maintains and respect beneﬁciaries' rights, including respecting and maintaining their privacy and not demeaning them. Tdh and partners have zero-tolerance for exposing beneﬁciaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measures will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Code of Conduct, and conﬁdential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP, PSEA, and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identiﬁed cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and ability status will receive protection services according to their assessed and identiﬁed needs. All Project beneﬁciaries will have equal access to services, including beneﬁciaries with disabilities and beneﬁciaries living in the Access Restricted Areas. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. The proposed activities are based on the different needs assessment done by the Tdh and partner team in which beneﬁciaries engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneﬁciaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews. Beneﬁciaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as with any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products. To overcome access problems related to COVID-19 mitigation measures, Tdh and partner will ensure access to children living with disabilities through obtaining lists of children with disabilities from organizations that provide services to children with disabilities. Also, Tdh and partners have already a list of children with disabilities in need or previously benefited from MHPSS services. Those children will be contacted using different blended methodologies that involve in-person modalities and remote modalities. If needed, Tdh and Partner might conduct MHPSS services in places that are accessible to children with disabilities that could involve home visits and provision of services in grassroots organizations. Also, Tdh and partners will develop the MHPSS manual that could be used by children with disabilities themselves. Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children has already developed such tools for children with hearing disabilities.
