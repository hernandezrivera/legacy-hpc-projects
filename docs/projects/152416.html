Project Summary

SPEDP proposes a 12 months project aiming at providing an equitable access to safe WASH services for internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities and returnees. It will integrate WASH in nutrition, health and GBV mitigation initiatives. The humanitarian situation in South Sudan still remains dire despite significant efforts made on the provision of humanitarian assistance since the conflict broke out on 15th December 2013. Several surveys and needs assessments conducted by partners and UN Agencies highlighted on the significant needs in WASH, nutrition, health and protection. This project targets 84,750 vulnerable people (18028men, 21167 women, 21318 boys and 26057girls) among IDPs, returnees and host communities in Central Equatoria, Jongolei and Unity with minimum WASH packages. The activities will focus on drilling, protection and rehabilitation of water points; construction and rehabilitation of institutional sanitation facilities; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing; promotion of hygiene education through door to door visits, participatory hygiene sessions in public places and school children for effective behavioural change; distribution of hygiene kits to mothers and caretakers of children admitted for severe and moderate acute malnutrition (SAM/MAM) treatment at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC. Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of child bearing age accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions so that girls will not miss attending classes during their periods. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through anaccountability to affected populations (AAP) community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses. This project will focus on the various needs of men, women, boys and girls identified through focus group discussions (FGDs) with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities and pregnant lactating mothers incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. Throughout the project life cycle, SPEDP team will coordinate with the authorities, community and the key partners on ground who are implementing WASH, nutrition, health, and protection for integration of activities in order to avoid project overlap and duplication.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan still remains dire despite significant efforts made on the provision of humanitarian assistance since the war broke out in December 2013. The December 2013 and July 2016 conflicts forced about 1.5 million people to flee their homes and took refuge in the neighbouring countries including Uganda, Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), Kenya, Sudan and Ethiopia while 1.9 million people were displaced within the Country with many more hiding in the bushes and swamps in remote areas. The situation in the targeted locations remains dire and strongly influenced by continuous population movements, food insecurity, malnutrition, economic crisis and disease outbreaks like Cholera that affected 11,214 people including 190 deaths in 24 Counties between June 18, 2016 and June 27, 2017 (UNOCHA June 28, 2017) bulletin. Some areas in Central Equatoria, Jongolei and Unity are still marked as hotspots category 1 and 2 according to UNICEF Cholera Epidemiological Report, April 2018. All these challenges dictate the living conditions of IDPs, host communities and returnees. The (October 17th, 2018 IRNA report) in Kajo Keji and SPEDP (6th July, 2018 assessment) in Old Fangak and Pigi Canal highlighted significant needs of WASH, nutrition, health and protection services. WASH continues to be a prevailing problem that increases the spread of water-borne diseases with compounding effects on malnutrition. The situation is predicted to worsen due to high influx of returnees from the neighbouring countries as a result of the revitalized peace progress signed in Khartoum on 12th September 2018. This background prompted SPEDP to scale up its operations in the Counties of Central Equatoria, Jongolei and Unity with a total of 377,496 IDPs and 822,435 people in need of WASH services (2019 HNO WASH Cluster updates).

Activities

The key interventions SPEDP will be addressing in the targeted Counties of Central Equatoria, Jongolei and Unity are as follows:– 1. Water Supply 1.1. Drilling of 10 boreholes in OTP/SC and Communities in Yei, Kajo Keji, Morobo and Lainya Counties to provide the expected huge number of returnees, IDPs and host community with access to safe water. 1.2. Engaging in rehabilitation or repair of 50 hand pumps in OTP/SC and Communities that will be distributed according to number accessed in the targeted project locations. Women and girls will be consulted through female staff for the allocation of the sites for rehabilitation1.3. Engaging in rehabilitation of 10 springs to the community in Morobo County to provide the community with access to safe water supply. SPEDP will identify the safe sites for women and girls access to avoid GBV incidences. 1.4. Supporting rehabilitation of 15 hand dug wells in Lainya, Morobo and Kajo Keji Counties to provide the community with access to safe water for women and girls. 1.5. Training and distributing water purification products (PUR/Aqua tabs) to 5,000 HHs in Pigi Canal and Fangak for accessing surface water for drinking. 1.6. Training to 170 community based pump mechanics that include both men and women to ease operation and maintenance of hand pumps in the targeted project locations. 1.7. Supporting formation and training of water source sanitation committees, comprising of 7 members in each rehabilitated water source (3 female: 4 male) since water collection is considered as responsibility for women. 1.8. Provision of 20lts buckets and collapsible jerricans to 5,000HHs from vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities without water containers for fetching and storage of water. 1.9. Conducting water quality testing and monitoring of water points and at household level. 2. Sanitation: 2.1. Constructing 30 blocks of emergency institutional latrines (4 stances each) that are gender appropriate, secure and user friendly giving special attention to the safety of girls. 2.2. Rehabilitating 30 blocks (4 stances) institutional latrines in targeted counties of Central Equatoria and Unity State with secure and safe access for women and girls. 2.3. Mobilizing Community on the use of local available materials for construction of household latrines as well as promotion of cat sanitation in densely populated IDP sites without latrines to reduce open defecation. 2.4. Promoting safe domestic waste management at household level through open public clean up campaigns. 3. Hygiene: 3.1. Training to 176 Community Hygiene Promoters and Volunteers (88 females and 88 males) to disseminate key messages to the affected populations.3.2. Forming and training of 30 school hygiene clubs with 40 members in each club to promote hygiene in schools so that the same messages can be carried at home by the pupils for behaviour change in the Community. 3.3. Engaging the community hygiene promoters in key hygiene messages i.e. personal hygiene (hand washing with soap and ash at critical times) domestic hygiene (food hygiene) protection of water sources (water hygiene/safe water chain) and environmental hygiene (safe excreta disposal and solid waste management). The project will conduct door to door visits and organize participatory hygiene sessions in public places aiming to reach 84,750 people. 3.4. Distributing MHM kits to 2,500 adolescent girls and 1,500 women of child bearing age accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions so that girls will not miss attending classes during their periods. 3.5. Distribution of hygiene kits to 2500 mothers and caretakers of children admitted for SAM/MAM treatment at OTP/SC.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project team will inform the beneficiaries about the assistance through project inception meeting (during project launching) and community mobilization meetings by sharing the project objectives, project expected outputs and the deliverables. Furthermore, information will be disseminated during review meetings, workshops and training programmes.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP adopts bottom-up, inclusive and participatory processes to identify needs and constraints of beneficiaries. The messages are context specific and tailored for gender and age groups through the involvement of men, women, boys and girls and including the community leaders. This helps SPEDP to figure out the priority needs of affected populations and give due consideration to their perceived needs.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The WASH facilities shall be established in consultation with women and girls by female staff to minimize cases of sexual exploitation and abuse when accessing water or sanitary facilities in locations that may be far and not secure. Lights or torches will be provided to access sanitary facilities at night.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultations will be conducted with the local authorities, community leaders, Church leaders and community representatives. Special attention will be given to women, youth, people with disabilities, elderly people and other vulnerable groups to voice their different opinions on the design of WASH facilities and other items provided

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

SPEDP will establish complaint mechanisms that include formal and informal mechanism to respond the different literacy levels of the community. The informal mechanism will include community members have easy access to Project Managers’ contact numbers for sharing any complaints related to the project. The formal complains shall be gathered through setting up information centres, complaint boxes and mobile teams seeking feedback. Additionally, the targeted population will be consulted to give their views on how they prefer to channelize their complaints to SPEDP project team without any obstacles ensuring safety, security and confidentiality.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community will be engaged to participate in project monitoring and evaluation through established committees comprising of RRC/RoSS, county WASH department representatives, community leaders and community representatives from all the vulnerable groups to enable them know whether the project is progressing well and meeting the set targets including lessons learnt.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP will ensure protection concerns are mainstreamed in all activities of the project. SPEDP will work in close collaboration with protection actors including GBV and child protection Sub-clusters so as to strengthen the capacity of the project team. SPEDP team will review the social, economic and political context of the project locations and use conflict sensitivity approach to minimize the associated risks. The conflict sensitivity approach helps to strengthen staff understanding of the conflict dynamics and therefore taking measures to decrease harm in the community. It adopts referral pathways in order for receiving relief support for survivors of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV). Safety audits will be conducted to understand risks of every gender. SPEDP will also closely monitor the protection cases in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as the youth, female headed households and the most vulnerable.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP team will discuss with all representative samples of society (e.g. men, women, girls, boys, elderly, ethnic groups, persons with disabilities) accessing the services to ensure their safety and dignity and avoid causing harm; services and assistance will be provided in a way that preserve the physical integrity of all population groups and culturally appropriate. Since women and children are being responsible for fetching water, they will be consulted for the safety of the locations for water facilities and types of containers they use for fetching water. People with disabilities and children will be consulted on the design of WASH facilities for their easy access. SPEDP will coordinate its operations with local Government authorities, leadership of the opposition parties, community leaders and development partners to enable easy access for service delivery to the neediest people.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The community members will be consulted on power dynamics as perceived by them and how equity can be achieved in their perspectives by mainstreaming protection. SPEDP will closely monitor protection cases in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youth, female headed households, and the most vulnerable

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Sanitary facilities will be disaggregated by gender and lockable from inside. During distribution, people with disabilities, elderly people and pregnant and lactating mother will be given priority to receive their items. Distribution centres will be located at central point for easy access by all the beneficiaries and distributions organized early enough to mitigate GBV and other protection risks.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will measure access of beneficiaries through baseline survey at the beginning and endline survey at end of the project to measure how beneficiaries access WASH services based on distance and time. Focused group discussions (FGDs) with all groups will help to adopt measures that ensure access to the project deliverables by beneficiaries. Post distribution monitoring and feedback stories will measure access of beneficiaries to services provided.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure the safety of beneficiaries through routine monitoring, FGDs with affected population, collection of feedback stories or complains registration and processing.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The dignity of beneficiaries will be measured through baseline surveys and FGDs with women and girls by female staff. Project staff will ensure full respects of the dignity of all the beneficiaries. The project will promote the dignity of beneficiaries by enabling them to influence decision-making and play a more active role in their welfare and development.
