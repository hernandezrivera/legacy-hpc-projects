Project Summary


The 2023 Food Security Cluster Response Plan for South Sudan provides the context for this project. The project will contribute to FSL objectives (CO1) “provides emergency food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations” and CO 2“enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihoods and fishing and livestock supports”, directly linked to Strategic Response Plan (SRP) objective “reducing mortality and morbidity, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents of the most vulnerable populations.
The project aims to address the immediate food and livelihood needs of people facing severe food insecurity in 8 Counties across four States of South Sudan. In 2023, the project will target 362,824 people expected to face severe food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) in the five States specifically 54,000 people in Central Equatoria, 133,774 in Jonglei State, 41,180 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, 34,248 in Unity, and 99,622 people in Warrap State. Project participants will be selected through a rigorous community-based targeting process with due emphasis on vulnerable groups such as female and child-headed households, the disabled, the elderly, and IDPs.

The project will be premised on two major interventions to achieve its overall objective. These interventions will augment and reinforce each other. The primary intervention will be the direct delivery of food assistance to affected people which will be complemented by interventions targeted at restoring local food production through the provision of key emergency livelihood inputs and resilience-building measures. By Transferring food assistance to vulnerable people, the project fills immediate consumption gaps and addresses ongoing hunger. This action will stabilize households in the short run by safeguarding livelihoods and remaining assets. This will set a more resilient foundation on which households can build on and engage in more productive livelihood activities.
Building on the consumption stability achieved through food assistance, the project will extend appropriate emergency livelihood inputs and skills as well as support community participation in asset creation actions which will lead to the recovery of local food production and the development of durable and productive assets. This will in turn enhance food security, restore livelihoods, and build resilience to future shocks and stressors.
Specific interventions include:
a) General food distribution and cash-for Assets: These two modalities are meant to ensure immediate access to adequate and nutritious food to address ongoing hunger. As much as possible and feasible, this assistance will be used to encourage targeted community members to engage in complementary asset creation activities such as the restoration of croplands which will in turn improve farmers’ productivity and resilience to shocks;
b) Providing emergency livelihood assistance including the distribution of crop and vegetable seeds, agricultural tools, and fishing kits coupled with skill enhancement measures to stimulate local food production. Vegetable demo plots and kitchen gardens will be promoted to showcase best practices for improving production. To strengthen linkage with nutrition measures, kitchen gardens will be promoted as simple interventions that can promote improved nutrition at the household level, especially for families with malnourished children. The adoption of better farming and fishing methods will improve food self-sufficiency and reduce dependency on external food assistance in the long run.
The project will set up feedback mechanisms through which targeted community members can voice their needs, and preferences and demand inclusive processes. COVID-19 and Ebola risk communication will be an integral part of the project’s engagement with the target community.

1 - Needs

The food security outlook for South Sudan remains bleak for a significant proportion of the population. During the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51% of the population) will likely face a Crisis level (IPC Phase 3) of acute food insecurity. This is projected to increase to 7.8 million people during the projection period of April-July 2023 (IPC Snapshot, Oct 2022-July 2023). As a result of this, an estimated 8.2 million people will be in need of humanitarian lifesaving assistance in 2023 (FSL Cluster Draft HRP 2023). • Of the most food insecure States between Oct-Nov 2022, where over 50% of the population continued to experience high, levels of acute food insecurity are Jonglei (68%), Unity (66%), NBeG (62%), Upper Nile (58%), Warrap (57%), and Lakes (57%). Factors including endemic conflict, deep-rooted intercommunal violence, extreme climatic conditions resulting in drought and localized flooding as well chronic economic constraints all converge to form a complex humanitarian context. Moreover, the outlook for malnutrition is expected to be worse Between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition (SMART & FSNMS 2022). Acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, and Warrap. With 95 percent of livelihoods dependent on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism, and animal husbandry, climate shocks are likely to lead to greater food insecurity and vulnerability. Drought and flooding often lead to livestock mortality and a decline in the amount and viability of available land for cultivation. This will also lead to the disruption of traditional seasonal migration routes of pastoralists and limited grazing land for cattle which may increase the risk of inter-community tensions in affected areas. Climate change-driven heavy rains and floods are now impacting about one million people in South Sudan every year, aggravating an already precarious food insecurity situation. Above-normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years, resulting in widespread asset and livelihood (crops and livestock) loss and displacement. According to a recent report by FEWS-NET (Sep 2022), average to above-average rainfall is forecast to continue through October 2022, likely leading to atypically extensive flooding for the fourth consecutive year and an increase in flood-related humanitarian needs in 2022 The project will target 8 counties all facing acute food insecurity namely Juba county from Central Equatoria State; Pibor, Bor South, and Twic East Counties from Jonglei State; Aweil Center and Aweil South Counties from NBEG; Guit from Unity state; and Gogrial West and Twic from Warrap state. Due to the multiple impacts of shocks, a significant number of the farming families in the proposed counties missed the previous year’s agricultural season, remaining without access to food and livelihood opportunities to meet immediate household needs. Crop productivity remains low due to the limited size of cultivated land associated with labor shortage, unreliable rainfall, and limited availability and access to agricultural inputs including seeds and tools. While fishing is a key livelihood opportunity, its potential is underutilized due to limited access to appropriate fishing tools and production and preservation methods. The project will ensure timely efforts targeted at stimulating local food production by capitalizing on the existing livelihood opportunities through improving access to inputs and skills which will go a long way in improving household food security and enhancing the resilience of affected communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will strictly ensure all vulnerable groups including people with disabilities, women, and girls are fairly represented in all community mobilization events and local targeting committees related to the project. This Is done to help the most vulnerable people have influence over the local decision-making processes and ensure their perspective and needs are properly considered in the project’s implementation. The project will ensure all information communicated to the target community is appropriate and tailored to local specific circumstances. The information will be delivered in local languages and will be culturally and gender-sensitive. Printed information will take into account the high level of illiteracy rate and will be presented in pictorial forms. Telephone helpline numbers will be provided for the greater community to report any cases of concern and this will be supported by the helpdesk instituted at every activity forum. Other complaint and response mechanisms will be available alongside the above inclusive channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Because of information gaps, fear, and limited understanding of the procedures for complaints, a significant number of the beneficiaries may not use complaint feedback mechanisms. The project will take the following actions: a) conduct proper sensitization to all beneficiaries to ensure they are aware of the availability of complaint and feedback mechanisms, where it will be located, and what kinds of complaints can be reported; b) strengthen and establish a new safe and secure feedback mechanism that encourages beneficiaries to feel free to express their views for example toll-free lines, c) put in place clear and simple complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to make it easier for beneficiaries to give feedback; d) ensure beneficiaries get responses to and provide feedback for their complaints on a timely basis. This will build trust in the CRM system and encourage continued use of the platform in the long run.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will use various channels to inform the affected population. The Project will organize wider community sensitization events before the start of the implementation to inform the target communities about the objective of the project, the specific activities involved, and other support services available. For every activity, the pre-address checklist will be developed and used to ensure it provides information on PSEA and channels of reporting. The project will assign sufficient field monitors and closely work with local institutions such as Project Management Committees (PMCs) to reach the community on regular intervals with key messages and information including on targeting criteria, entitlements, distribution place and times, protection against sexual exploitation, the importance of using the complaint mechanisms including and the channels available for reporting any abuses related to this matter. The Project will take advantage of all community mobilization platforms to sensitize the community on PSEA including during targeting, training sessions, participation for asset creation, and during distribution periods. Printed materials such as posters and banners will be used to disseminate information on entitlements and other key messages on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse in a manner that is appropriate and understandable to a specific community. Where opportunities exist, the project will use local media channels, in particular, radio broadcasts about the project and its strategy regarding PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will carefully review feedback or complaints collected from beneficiaries for course correction. Depending on their nature, the feedback will be categorized into non-sensitive and sensitive feedback or complaints. The Non-sensitive feedback commonly concerned with the implementation of activities/dissatisfaction with activities are commonly collected through the feedback helpdesks, project review meetings, stakeholder’s meetings and are analyzed by a team of M and E, Gender, Protection and Accountability team, technical officers, and project manager and immediate actions are taken in the consecutive activities for course correction and future programmatic consideration. Beneficiary feedback or complaints on fraud and corruption, abusive behavior, and sexual exploitation and abuse are considered sensitive cases and handled at the country office level and are reviewed through the higher management organ. Where required an investigation team is commissioned by the CD to investigate and recommend actions to management

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To Mitigate risks from violence, the project will: a) conduct specific risk analysis regarding security situation of the targeted areas before deciding on the specific location and period of key project’s activities; b) Set up registration/ distribution sites closer to settlement areas so as to reduce distance travel and exposure to the risk;c) schedule registration/distribution early enough in the day so as to avoid traveling at nightfall and; d) coordinate with local protection partners and UN system to obtain regularly updated information on security situation and forage protection support when necessary. To mitigate the risk of Sexual exploitation and abuse, the project will: a) provide regular training to JAM field staff on PSEA, GBV and gender equlaity to enhance their awareness on prevention and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and other forms of sexual violence so as to reduce and protect from the risk of being a perpetrator; and awareness on human rights protection. b) conduct specific risk analysis to identify factors that expose women and girls to sexual violence and take the necessary protection measures. For instance, distribution sites will be set up closer to settlement areas to reduce distance travel and exposure to therisk. Distribution will be scheduled early enough in the day and priority will be given to vulnerable groups such as women and girls so as to avoid traveling at nightfall which may expose them to SGBV and SEA; c) put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms- suggestion boxes, helpdesk, at distribution sites to provide safe and secure avenues for women and girls such as use of telephone lines to encourage them to report on any act of abuse and seek for assistance. Confidentiality will be maintained at all times to ensure reporters anonymity.To mitigate risk of non-participation of marginalized groups in the consultation and decision making process,the project will strictly ensure all vulnerable groups including people with disability and women are represented in key local community processes and decision making structures. Particular attention will be given to the local community based participatory planning (CBPP) exercise, Project Management Committees(PMCs) and Boma targeting committees. Ensuring gender balance and equal opportunities for marginalized groups such as key local community institutions will empower them to influence local decision making processes related to the project’s resource allocation. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms will be addressed by provision of user friendly channels depending on the forms of vulnerabilities. Because of information gaps, fear and partial understanding of the procedures for complaints, a significant number of the beneficiaries may not use complaint feedback mechanisms. To mitigate such risk, the project will: a) conduct proper sensitization to all beneficiaries to ensure they are aware of the availability of complaint and feedback mechanisms and what kinds of complaints can be reported;b) strengthen and establish new safe and secure feedback mechanism that encourages beneficiaries to feel free to express their views; c) put in place clear and simple complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to make it easier to beneficiaries to give feedback; d) ensure beneficiaries get response to and feedback for their complaints on a timely basis so that they have trust in the system and continue to use the platform in the long run.
