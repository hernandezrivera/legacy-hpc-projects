Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian WASH assistance to 450,000 beneficiaries (148,500 women, 112,500 men, 94,500 girls, 94,500 boys - 12% of whom are estimated to be either elderly or disabled) to mitigate the suffering of populations affected by natural or manmade disasters and reflect all 3 objectives of the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan.
Under Objective 1, IOM will aim to assist crisis-affected population (approximately 200,000 individuals) by ensuring dignified and equitable access to basic WASH services. Emergency assistance will be provided to people newly displaced by conflict or natural disasters through the implementation of early recovery approaches where possible, connecting the need for short-term emergency interventions with long-term solutions by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. The objective is to not only focus on WASH emergency interventions but gradually contribute to socioeconomic inclusion of forcibly displaced populations and returnees by directly increasing access to basic services for the displaced and host communities and indirectly increasing employment opportunities.
Under Objective 2, IOM seeks to improve and sustain access to safe water in target communities (approximately 150,000 individuals), including returnees, especially in Blue Nile, east Sudan, Abyei, North Darfur and South Darfur as these communities primarily rely on agriculture and livestock, therefore water is critical to their livelihoods.
Under Objective 3, improving and sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and hygiene messages will address malnutrition related risks and increase community resilience to disease outbreaks for approximately 50,000 individuals. IOM will consider emergency water provision, access to proper sanitation facilities and hygiene campaigns; coupled with the establishment and rehabilitation of water points. This will include environmental impact mitigation such as the provision of solar technology to power water points and monitoring ground water to moderate depletion levels. Exit strategies will be developed to alleviate aid dependency in protracted emergencies by strengthening self-reliance and facilitating community solutions in a more holistic approach.

1 - Needs

The needs assessed for the proposed interventions are based on the gaps observed in 2020, as well as the gaps and further insecurities which resulted from renewed conflict and tensions, leading to new displacements, the unprecedented flooding during the 2020 rainy season, the COVID-19 outbreak and the further deterioration of the economic situation in the country. Women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities remain the most vulnerable groups in need of assistance and protection. In locations where easy access to safe water is lacking vulnerable communities resolve to collect water from unsafe water sources, increasing the risks of water borne disease outbreaks and exacerbating malnutrition rates among children. IOM currently provides WASH assistance across all five Darfur States, South and West Kordofan, Blue Nile, Abyei, Gedaref, Kassala and Red Sea States, and is planning to continue its interventions where over 250,000 individuals are still in need of safe water, sanitation facilities, and targeted hygiene awareness and waste management campaigns. Voluntary returns from IDP camps to places of origin have also increased in 2019 and 2020, a trend that is expected to continue throughout 2021 and WASH support will be required to support residents and returnees for their sustainable reintegration and the promotion of durable solutions. In North Darfur, IDP camps, such as Tawilla, Rwanda and Debenaira Camps, still have vast gaps in sanitation and hygiene, whilst water quality levels and availability need to be monitored regularly for the prevention of water borne diseases. Every year, IDP camps and hot spot areas continue to be heavily affected by the floods, therefore, needs for critical WASH services will increase during the rainy season. IOM also proposes to provide life-saving assistance to vulnerable resident IDPs living among host communities in localities and villages around Tawilla in order to address the vast gaps in water, sanitation, and hygiene. Low level of awareness on proper hygiene and sanitation, the lack of food and potable water, and the lack of access to basic services severely affects many IDPs and residents living in North Darfur. In East, West and Central Darfur, there is a need to continue addressing WASH gaps for IDPs, returnees and host communities who have limited to no access to WASH services. In South and West Kordofan there are at least five locations that require increased access to potable water, sanitation facilities and hygiene campaigns for returnees, IDPs and host communities. In the two Kordofan States, high number of IDPs and refugees still reside with hosting communities, putting further pressure on the already limited resources. As the population increases additional water sources are needed, open defecation is widely practiced and there is a dire need for hygiene awareness campaigns and garbage collection systems. The chronic underdevelopment of the eastern Sudan region has contributed to the scarcity of water resources and lack of infrastructure severely affecting communities’ livelihoods in the area. In Blue Nile, vulnerable returnees from Ethiopia along the main border areas continue to face challenges when returning to their areas of origin as they have very limited access to basic services, especially WASH services. Access to safe water and other basic services remain among the lowest in the country; Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above the 15% international emergency threshold levels. Furthermore, the provision of safe water, sanitation, and promotion of safer/preventative hygiene practices are an essential part of prevention efforts during infectious disease outbreaks such as COVID-19. Combined with improved access to WASH services, IPC measures and RCCE efforts are an effective way to prevent or limit transmission of diseases. IOM will continue to enhance national capacity through the provision of essential WASH services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provide access to safe water through the construction or rehabilitation of water points including hand pumps and water yards that use sustainable and environmentally friendly (or renewable) power sources such as solar technology; in critical locations such as health-care facilities and schools. 2. Support the coverage of operation and maintenance (O&M) costs of water points for the most vulnerable communities who cannot afford water fees and support the establishment of a water fee systems once the sites are handed over to the community. 3. Monitor water depletion levels/ ground water availability to improve resource management and evaluate the impact of constructing new water points in the local environment. 4. Provide households with sanitation facilities and improve community’ approaches to sanitation and addressing protection concerns by constructing, in consultation with community members, more gender, age and disability sensitive latrines with durable materials including special consideration for the design and construction of latrines to prevent groundwater contamination, and including locks and lighting to address protection risks. 5. Assess hygiene practices at household level and conduct hygiene campaigns that include topics on methods to prevent water borne diseases and the fecal oral transmission that may exacerbate malnutrition, especially targeting women and children. IOM’s hygiene teams will be mobilizing women and local community members in validating the key messages and as key promoters of hygiene. 6. Use Community-led Approaches to Total Sanitation (CLTS) to engage the community (including equal representation of all groups), in designing and evaluating (community feedback) the responses and supporting construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of the sanitation facilities and promote safe and improved hygienic practices in an effort to reduce open defecation. 7. Increase community awareness of safe improved hygiene practices (including providing hand washing facilities and supplies as well as Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities for COVID-19 prevention) and engage the community in systematic waste collection campaigns. 8. Prepositioning Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits inclusive of context-appropriate items to assist at least 9,000 vulnerable households (HHs) for possible renewed displacement and prevention of water borne diseases and healthy MHM. 9. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) tools are implemented to measure impact of emergency interventions. 10. Community members will be trained to establish waste and water management committees, consisting of both men and women, to collect tariffs from community members to support maintenance of facilities and develop contingency and durable solution plans. 11. Train female and male hand pump mechanics / chlorination technicians (the training will include safe chlorination techniques and practices and water quality monitoring, testing and analysis). 12. Draft Operation and Maintenance guidelines for the operation of water supply systems. 13. Promote capacity building activities for local authorities at the state level to improve the overall management and sustainability of the water sources. 14. Contribute to the establishment of a WASH pipeline for WASH partners through the prepositioning of WASH emergency items. All distribution and hygiene promotion activities will follow national guidelines on COVID-19 preventive measures, such as physical distancing, use of PPE and no large gatherings will be organized.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and waste management campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper / responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’seconomic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on finding alternatives fromfuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to fluctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits and identification of risks associated to accessing WASH services. This inclusive participatory approach assures the inclusion and promotion of ideas from vulnerable women’s and girls’, and persons with disabilities’ voices, who are often excluded from community decisions or underrepresented during decision making processes and face more barriers in accessing services, especially with regard to selecting the location for the construction/ re-construction of the latrines and rehabilitation of water points with the use of the spare parts. Consulting all community members will therefore ensure that their needs are not only addressed but protection risks are addressed and mitigated.
