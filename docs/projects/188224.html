Project Summary

SME:
The majority of vendors that NGOX works within its voucher program survive on slim margins and are highly susceptible to market shocks and stresses. The main objective of the SME activity is to support the sustainability of the vendors and stabilise their revenue through small business development technical assistance and small cash grants so they can adapt to market disturbances and currency instability.
NGO X intends to provide support to 1158 vendors between January 2022 and December 2023 through a comprehensive approach involving small business technical assistance and small cash grants.
The cash grants and technical assistance will support vendors in managing and adapting their businesses in the face of continuous and unpredictable challenges; thereby maintaining or expanding the availability, quality, diversity of food products in markets in our food security areas of operation.
Access Road Rehab
NGO X will rehabilitate access roads from Darkosh community to IDP sites to ensure continued access to 7 IDP sites and a COVID-19 quarantine facility. The road will provide access to and from the camps that accommodate 3,026 individuals, and to the quarantine centre by Darkosh community (28,626 individuals).
NGO X will implement a Cash for Work scheme involving the most vulnerable households among the IDP and host community population assisting in carrying out the site analysis activity under for this project; The site analysis will use the CFW modality to hire workers to assist NGO X Engineers during the land surveying and measurement taking. The inclusion of a CFW scheme will complement the need for additional sources of income and allow participants to engage in the improvement of the sites that they inhabit (increasing sense of ownership), contribute to mental health benefits linked to staying active, whilst responding to needs in the camps and neighboring community.
The access road rehab contained within this project covers the period of 2022 only.

4 - Needs

SME: Markets in NWS are functioning, but they are highly vulnerable to shocks and stresses. The majority of vendors survive on slim margins and are highly susceptible to market disturbance and sometimes request for their contract with NGOX (as a participant in our voucher programme) to be terminated because of this. Inability to restock on time due to lack of capital is the most reported reason for voluntary contract termination, demonstrating that vendors in NWS need support to best supply their communities. These market challenges place constraints on NGOX’s voucher program, as there are limited vendors available in each market as replacements, and also impact negatively on the community, resulting in less choice, and reduced availability of affordable, diverse, and nutritious foods. Access Road Rehab. During winter, uncontrolled runoff from intense rainfall erodes unsurfaced and gravelled roads leading to camps and communities, leaving them impassable especially to people living with disability using mobility aid. Standing water can cause waterlogging and stop traffic. An effective winter preparedness strategy is imperative to ensure continuity of access for IDPs and host communities to key services such as WASH and health facilities, markets, livelihood opportunities, and kids access to schools.

5 - Implementation of activities

SME: Distribution of small cash grants to vendors. The grants are cash transfers distributed to the selected vendors using financial service providers and are provided in two instalments. This is complemented with technical assistance provided through group and one-to-one coaching sessions. When possible, female vendors receive individualized technical assistance support (in the form of coaching) from female staff. Technical assistance needs are determined based on an SME assessment and following an application process. Technical assistance topics offered to vendors are (but not limited to): Marketing, Record-Keeping, developing a Business Idea, and Creating a Business Plan. Access road rehab: Prior starting the implementation, Beneficiaries’ consultation sessions will take place with affected population representatives including Women, persons with disabilities and older persons, through focus group discussions and interviews to get feedback on the proposed intervention and ensure meeting the needs and preferences while facilitating the access between the camps and Darkoush community. Contractor-led modality will be used to rehabilitate the road, contractors will be encouraged to engage young adults in the work, and CFW will be utilized for site analysis related work, the work will be monitored by NGOX team at all stages to ensure achieving the required quality. Completed work will be handed over to the Local authorities which will sign off the completion certificate.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of exacerbating real or perceived inequalities through unequal distribution of assistance, which further erodes social cohesion and generates intercommunity grievances. Risk of excluding vulnerable communities from access to early recovery assistance by failing to factor in dynamics inhibiting their access (e.g., checkpoints, lack of access to civil documentation etc) Risk of exacerbating tensions between communities and local authorities or of losing credibility/ impartiality in the face of communities by failing to create mechanisms for greater community participation and responsiveness of authorities in the delivery of essential services. To mitigate the above risks, the ERL sector is investing in increased local context sensitivity capacities of partner staff. It is also making available context analyses of prioritized areas and guidance tools to support the conduct of context-sensitive early recovery. An updated guidance note on context-sensitive early recovery was shared with partners in 2021. Several sector partners participated in local context analysis exercises. In the course of 2022 and 2023, ERL sector partners will increase capacity building for staff on context sensitive essential service rehabilitation and livelihoods programming. NGO X has been programming in the target locations for a number of years and has developed extensive capacity for conflict and context sensitive programming, which will be applied alongside the cluster measure above.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Female vendors are targeted by criminals (because they are carrying large sums of money) on way back from the financial service provider where they redeemed their cash grants.Prevention/ Mitigation: • As per SoPs, the distribution point must be easily accessible to beneficiaries’ homes. The security assessment also considers the safety of roads to/from distribution sites. • Monitor the safety of beneficiaries through all stages of the distribution (way to distribution, distribution point, and way back from distribution)• If this is widespread, engage in discussions with Local Councils to enhance security measures for beneficiaries, otherwise NGOX would consider suspending assistance. • Continuous follow -up by the Access team of the security situation in the NGOX AoO. Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by NGOX staff/vendors/FSPs/contractors on SME programme participants, or camp residents Prevention/ Mitigation: • Consult women, men, girls and boys and persons with disabilities and older people about their access to delivery mechanisms and identify any additional safeguarding measures that need to be put in place •Conduct PSEA trainings with staff and inform of sanctions if PSEA policies are violated •Ensure Code of Conduct and PSEA protocols are shared and signed by and financial service providers and contractors •Conduct awareness raising activities with beneficiaries on entitlements and rights, and NGOX’s feedback mechanism


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: • Aid delivery and information sharing in Northwest Syria remains permissive and enabled. Security conditions remains sufficiently stable to allow NGOx to carry out construction and rehabilitation activities. • Items required for the activity remains available sufficiently and timely in the local market. • Access to Syria from Turkey remains possible, and commercial commodities are supplied to the Syrian market for the items that are not produced or manufactured inside Syria. Risk 1: Disruption of cashflow into Syria via Iraq/Turkey means NGOX is unable to pay our contracted FSP for Vendor cash grants, contractors for road rehab, or CFW participants. Mitigation: •NGOX has in place an agreement with the PTT for the transfer of funds from Turkey to N Aleppo. •NGOX has multiple bank accounts with different banks in Iraq for the transfer of funds from Iraq. • NGOX is seeking alternate banks to move money into Iraq. • In-kind procurement of assets can be easily done so NGOX can distribute the grant to vendors in-kind instead of cash R: Road construction and infrastructure activities are better implemented during non-rainy days. Delays in implementation could result in carrying out works during winter which could impact the quality of work and the required time to deliver the activityM: NGO X has developed a thorough workplan that has considered contingency planning for delays, so that all infrastructure work should be completed prior to winter weather
