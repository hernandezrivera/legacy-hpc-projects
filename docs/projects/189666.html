Project Summary

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, the need for protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance in all challenging catastrophies of drought, flood, conflicts and violence initiated by the communities, land disputes, Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighbouring countries.
According to assessment did by joint humanitarian unity state, the resulted war which is currently turned into conflicts have devastated all living style and disappeared peace, severe poverty, instability, local economic meltdowns, displacements, formation of too many groups that targets civilians in form of raping women, girls and children, recruiting child boys into military, child abduction, demolition of local educational facilities in the villages in the regions, especially the greater Upper Nile region(e.g Unity State). As a result of this harsh crisis toward humanity and the innocent people across the country, many became homeless, refugees in the neighboring countries, IDPs fenced in POCs, victims due to loses of family members, fully depending on humanitarian Aids and fundings. In other circumstances the grassroots which have a less access to Aids specially the farer remote areas, people remained conditionally under threats of different diseases due to lack of primary health facilitations in the areas compare to urbanely in the protection sides which is clearly a war/conflict results that end road constructions for better accessibilities.
