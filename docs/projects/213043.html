Project Summary

In response to WASH infrastructure requirements in northwest Syria, where a large portion of the population relies on alternative water sources, the Peace Space Program aims to strengthen water and sanitation infrastructure in the long term and ensure the sustainability of the Ariha community's water stations to residents affected by conflict and earthquakes in northwestern Syria. The planned intervention will respond to the needs of 57,000 individuals through strategies such as rehabilitation of the water system, ensuring operation, and maintenance of two water plants (Southwest Station and North Station) and the public electricity grid for sustainability. In addition, a low-cost energy source will be secured through the installation of solar energy systems at these stations.
A cost recovery system will be established by subsidizing the operation and maintenance costs for a period of 12 months at the mentioned stations and three water stations in the Jericho community to ensure a smooth exit strategy. The Peace Survey will then conduct a final survey of the water station using the tool by the WASH cluster. The organization will be responsible for maintaining the solar energy system in the southwestern station and the northern station.
In addition, SOP will extend sewage lines to facilities and houses in the city's rural areas, integrating them into the network instead of disposing to farms or open places or using septic tanks. Furthermore, it will perform general maintenance on the city's sewers and damaged manholes and pipes from the previous shelling.
Due to the outbreak's risks of cholera, the prevalence of Leishmaniasis, and the increased risk of waterborne diseases such as diarrhea, typhoid fever, and skin infections in northwestern Syria, the Peace Space Organization is actively prioritizing the provision of awareness and hygiene sessions for the community. A dedicated team of hygiene promoters will conduct educational sessions targeting all community members, including vulnerable groups, women, adolescent girls, and individuals with disabilities. The comprehensive approach aims to address multiple health challenges, ensuring that the community is well-informed and equipped to prevent the spread of various diseases and safeguard their well-being.
The proposed intervention will also include the establishment of WASH user committees at the community level. Peace Space will encourage women and adolescent girls to be part of these committees, representing vulnerable and marginalized groups in decision-making and empowering them to raise their voices. The WASH User Committees will serve as the core of risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) modalities, controlling the spread of cholera and addressing hygiene promotion.
In addition, Space of Peace will implement a safety audit questionnaire on a quarterly basis to guide recommendations to improve the security of women and girls in the target community. Recommended actions based on safety audit data should be interpreted as initial engagement points to strengthen GBV response and prevention. Space of Peace runs a community gender-based violence center in Jericho and provides protection counseling in this area. The proposed intervention will mainstream protection principles throughout the project cycle, while consulting men, women, boys, and girls to ensure equitable service delivery taking into account the specific needs of these groups. Female staff will be appointed as community mobilization and hygiene promotion officers, and a protection specialist in the Gaziantep office will be consulted to manage hygiene promotion, cholera prevention, and implement safety audit for gender-based violence during the implementation of the proposed intervention. Feminine hygiene promoters will take the lead in promoting hygiene awareness among communities in the targeted area.
