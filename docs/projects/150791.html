Project Summary

PUI has an extensive experience in documenting and responding to settler violence (SV) and related military operations, which has been increasing in the last two years. The purpose of the project is to support the resilience of the most exposed communities and individuals and strengthen the methods aiming to mitigate or prevent the risk of settler related incidents. Needs have been identified to strengthen, improve and increase awareness among exposed communities on reporting and documenting settler violence incidents and on methods to hold duty bearers accountable on IHL and IHRL violations. The project will target 354 direct beneficiaries, but we estimate that the entire population of the 57 targeted communities will be indirect beneficiaries.
The action will contribute to the protection cluster strategic objective SO1: “Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations” and will be both remedial and environment-building in nature.
The settler violence community based awareness project contains three layers of action.
Recently, an inter-clusters mechanism led by OCHA was created with the support of PUI and OHCHR, in order to trigger and coordinate protection as well as material responses to SV incidents. PUI will continue its efforts to contribute to the coordination of protection responses (including emergency and remedial ones) in all communities affected by settler violence in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, including mapping of key actors, identification of gaps and development of effective referral mechanisms for responses, in agreement with OCHA and pending availability of resources for that purpose. In this framework and beyond the response side, OHCHR and PUI agreed that there is still a need for a deeper analysis on the settlements related threats (including but not only settler violence) and their impact on population. The first part of the action will focus on strengthening policy and advocacy efforts in this sense through the collective work launched recently under the Settler Violence / Settlements Core Group co-chaired by PUI and OHCHR, under the lead of the Protection Cluster.
The second part will supplement the first part by strengthening alerting skills at community level to support the inter-cluster mechanism the ‘OCHA settler violence tracker’. The targeted communities do not currently report incidents on settler violence in a comprehensive way, either because of normalization of the violence or because of lack of awareness and knowledge on how to document settler violence and related military operations. The communities targeted are either located close to new outposts that are thus expected to face severe threats of settler violence and military related operations, or they are communities identified by key stakeholders as highly affected by settler violence, but with a very limited documentation of incidents and under-reporting in the inter-cluster tracking tool. Identification of needs for capacity building and activity implementation will be planned and coordinated together with community members, taking great care for age and gender diversity representation. Identification of relevant and diverse community members for trainings on documenting, reporting and how to pass on information to relevant stakeholders, will also be done in tight coordination with the targeted population.
The third part will target clusters of communities identified as highly affected by settler violence and exposed to the same sources of threats arising from specific settlement and outposts. The project will facilitate joint discussions with communities facing identical threats of SV (in types and origin) - to foster joint reflection on possible methods of mitigating threats and holding duty bearers accountable, possibly including e.g. ways of holding IF and settlements security guards’ accountable, a need which already has been indicated by several communities. The cluster approach will be focusing on increasing the cooperation between communities. It will specifically emphasize women participation in identifying protection threats, strengthening the links between communities and participating in decision making processes on designing collective strategies, including ways to tackle specific risks faced by most vulnerable groups such as children. The outputs of the clusters of communities will additionally feed advocacy efforts of the SVCG.

Needs

The project scope and structure are based on needs assessments conducted by PUI during the last year and will complement the extensive work done in the last 5 years to build the network of Community Eyes Watch currently covering 71 communities in the West Bank and under handover to the Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission (CWRC). PUI updated its settler violence survey in the summer of 2018 and reviewed the rankings of 281 communities to list the most at-risk communities as identified by key stakeholders. The list of most exposed communities, when triangulated with incidents documentation from sources such as PUI Early Warning System (implemented under the WB Protection Consortium) or the inter-cluster settler violence tracker, it indicates gaps in incidents reporting from communities highly affected by settler violence (ranging from low reporting to absence of reporting). List of targeted communities and classification of most at-risk communities will be updated based on future trends, as well as on OCHA’s planed similar ranking exercise. The targeting will also include communities identified as in proximity to new outposts mapped by the Applied Research Institute–Jerusalem. PUI past experience and current has also shown crucial need to improve the representation and engagement of specific groups, especially women and youth, in the process of mapping protection needs related to settler violence and possible ways of mitigating and preventing these and their consequences. This lack of engagement has been further confirmed and reflected during focus group discussions (FGDs) conducted by PUI between August and October 2018 in 6 communities (Urif, Asira Al Qibliya, Aqraba, Qaryut, H2 in Hebron and Susiya) as part of a community participatory process to identify protective measures needed at community level. These FGDs have also confirmed the need for activities specifically targeting the most vulnerable groups and the mitigation of their exposure to settler violence threats and/or attacks, especially in neighbourhoods located close to settlements /outposts and at time of SV escalation. Currently, most responses to settler violence focus on households or single communities, but the clustering methodology has been identified as an approach that will improve the cooperation between communities exposed to the same sources of protection threats. This will allow communities to share experience and develop effective strategies to strengthen their resilience and collectively engage in ways of holding stakeholders accountable for IHL/IHRL violations and for them to contribute to advocacy strategies. As stated in the ToRs, endorsed by members and the ICCG WB, the Settler Violence / Settlement Core Group has identified the need for joint efforts to analyse on settlement related threats, in order to provide strategic direction for HCT advocacy, feed the HNO and HRP, as well as for inter-sectorial responses through the ICCG.

Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach to 21 pre-identified communitiesMeetings will be held with communities identified as most vulnerable to settler violence and related military operations, but with little or no reporting on incidents in the current tracking tools. Content will include an introduction to protection principles, presentation of settler violence trends and objectives of the project. Community representatives will discuss protection threats for their community and needs for support in terms of awareness raising for community members. Activity 2: Awareness raising sessionsAwareness raising activities will be conducted for community members, paying close attention to the representation and participation of all specific groups, covering the subjects of IHL and IHRL, reporting and documentation of settler violence incidents, settler violence trends & settlement expansion trends, analysis and observations, tailored to accommodate the needs highlighted in the initial meetings. Sessions will be facilitated by PUI team and will involve 10 members per community (total target: 210 community members) Activity 3: Joint resilience building activities for clusters of communities (8 clusters pre-identified including 36 communities)Workshops will tackle the need for joint and collective efforts from communities facing similar threats (in their types and origin) to share strategies to prevent and mitigate the risks and consequences of settler violence and related military operations and hold duty bearers accountable. Initial discussions with community representatives will focus on identifying key members across communities to participate in workshops. Participants (4 per community, total target 144) will jointly identify collective needs and ways to strengthen resilience across the cluster of communities, and be supported by the project team to implement selected strategies (link with relevant stakeholders, communication, etc.). Activity 4 Beyond coordinating regular and ad hoc meetings, along with OHCHR, PUI as co-chair will contribute and facilitate other members to provide the deliverables of the subgroup identified (i.e quarterly updates, factsheets, policy brief and other materials).
