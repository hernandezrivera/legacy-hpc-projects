Project Summary

This project aims to provide the educational services to children with educational
problems, such as school dropout or students who were unable to go to school earlier,
students who are unable to read and write of both certificational ages i.e. ninth and
twelfth grade. Students who find it difficult to understand their essential school subjects
as well.
This project will provide educational services to 800 students and 100 teachers during the
year of 2022, and 800 students and 150 teachers during the year of 2023.
Targeted areas are: Kafar Batna, Haza, Hamuria, Gisreen, Alaftarees, Alashaary, Saqba,
Beit Sawa, Ein Tarma, Al Mohammadia, Darya, and Qudsaya.
CRS, the Child Right Society, has had a good experience in the fields of education since
2006. It also has had many partnerships with the UNICEF since 2015. And through its
distribution in Damascus countryside (Eastern Ghouta centers - Qudsaya centers), CRS
could afford its educational services to approximately 5000 children.
The centers of CRS will be used to serve this project and the following standards will be
taken into consideration: low academic achievement, low standards of protection, low
standard of living for the family, children mental health, residence state, displacement and
returnees, people with disabilities and those without family care and juvenile.
Educational programs will be approved in line with the education sector's response plan,
and the state curriculum.

3 - Needs

During the provision of educational provided by CRS, there noticed that most of thechildren in Eastern Ghouta have educational problems. In addition, children withoutparental care and juveniles, and high rates of school dropout which rates about 96% ofchildren without family care and juveniles, and about 22% of children in Eastern Ghouta. Adecrease in reading and writing skills was also observed for 91% of children enrolled informal education in public schools in Eastern Ghouta, the poor teaching environment fromteaching aids and the educational stimuli more than 90%, the spread of school violence,and the low ability of mothers to help their child in educational advancement. Lowteaching performance and teachers' communication skills as well.

4 - Implementation of activities

The centers of the CRS will be equipped with 3 classrooms in Eastern Ghouta, 2classrooms in Darayya, and 4 classrooms in the centers for those without family care andjuveniles in Qudsaya. The classrooms will be equipped with tablets through which storiesfor early childhood children will be presented. They start hearing the story then read it.After that, there will be related questions to ensure their understanding andcomprehension. Moreover, the intelligence games that allow the senses to move on anddevelop thought in a way that serves the educational process and the development of thechild's personality helps facilitate the delivery of the idea and attract the child. All of thesewill be used with all children included in this project. Days of the week will be allocated asfollowing: a day for science, music, drawing, theater, mathematics and mental arithmetic,sports, and time will be devoted each day to reading story and writing in both languagesArabic and English. Mothers will also be empowered with mechanisms to help their childadvance in academic achievement. Educational services for the certificates (ninth andbaccalaureate) will be provided in the form of intensive sessions in mathematics, physics,chemistry and both languages (Arabic and English). An evaluation of the quality ofservices provided will be conducted by specialists, in addition to evaluating thebeneficiaries' development through pre- and post-tests, and periodic tests.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiariesseeking services at CRS facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. Atorganizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited andconsidered a gross violation of CRS Code of Conduct, which all CRS employees mustsign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by CRS Safeguarding andWhistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete amandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and onreporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. Awareness andinformation sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism arealso provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident.
