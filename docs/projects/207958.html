Project Summary

UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency GBV responses in the next humanitarian project cycle, in keeping with its mandate under the IASC. UNFPA therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV AoR, will continue to provide and further expand quality life-saving GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services in Northern Mozambique in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNFPA is dedicated to maintaining its leadership role in emergency GBV responses during the upcoming humanitarian project cycle, aligning with its mandate. UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency gender-based violence (GBV) responses in the next 2024 humanitarian project cycle, in keeping with its mandate under the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). UNFPA therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR), will continue to provide and further expand quality lifesaving GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services in Northern Mozambique in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in three provinces. Collaborating closely with implementing partners, and stakeholders, and in coordination with the GBV AoR, UNFPA aims to sustain and expand high-quality, lifesaving GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services in Northern Mozambique. This effort involves working alongside provincial governments and institutions across three provinces, Cabo Delgado, Niassa, and Nampula, to provide and enhance access to survivor-centered GBV services in conflict-affected areas and locations hosting internally displaced persons. The focus is on improving the availability and reach of critical GBV prevention and response services for women and girls facing limited access to multi-sectoral support. In response to rising gender-based violence (GBV) in conflict-affected areas, UNFPA together with the cluster, prioritizes emergency services, including critical GBV services and enhanced referral pathways. GBV case management and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) initiatives address survivors' immediate needs and psychological well-being. The strategy underscores community engagement through capacity building, structured awareness initiatives, and information dissemination in safe spaces for women and girls. Community engagement leads to improved outcomes and by preventing a dependency relationship on outsiders from forming, capacity building encourages local people to take action on local issues themselves. Capacity building fosters a sense of ownership and empowerment so that community partners gain greater control over their own future development. Mobile brigades offer integrated GBV-related services, focusing on clinical management of rape and post-rape kits. Safety audits identify and mitigate risks across humanitarian sectors. Capacity building extends to various stakeholders, including GBV service providers, local authorities, Linha Verde staff, activists, and humanitarian actors. Livelihood and life-skills training target socio-economic vulnerability, supporting GBV survivors' recovery and reducing broader risks in displacement situations. Leveraging partnerships with local authorities, the strategy strengthens the national multisectoral mechanism (Centro de Atendimento Integrado - CAI) for integrated assistance to women survivors of violence. UNFPA, as a lead of the GBV AoR, will continue providing crucial guidance for GBV prevention, mitigation, and response in Mozambique in general, and in Northern Mozambique. Additionally, it will spearhead GBV data collection and information management through GBVIMS. This will involve reinforcing leadership by enhancing human resources and the GBV capacity of AoR partners.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project aims to increase the availability of and access to quality lifesaving GBV response and prevention services across the northern provinces of Mozambique. UNFPA will increase its support and services to female-headed households, adolescent girls, and widows and focus on empowering them through women and girls-friendly spaces (WGFS) and integrated SRH/GBV outreach services. UNFPA will expand its interventions ensuring a survivors-centered approach within integrated SRH-GBV services and continue providing lifesaving specialized quality GBV services including comprehensive case management, mental health services, psychosocial support, and establish and reinforce referral pathways throughout implementing areas. Geographically, the response prioritizes districts with high concentrations of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and spontaneous returns. This targeted approach aims to address specific challenges in locations witnessing protection issues, low civil documentation, and impediments to freedom of movement due to conflict-related factors. The concise and focused response strategy aims not only to meet immediate needs but also to establish a sustainable framework for preventing and addressing gender-based violence in challenging conflict-affected areas, seeking a lasting impact on the lives of those affected by GBV. While targeting the three provinces, Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula, the intervention will mainly focus on: 1. Individual GBV Case Management including support to existing Centros de Atendimentos Integrados (CAIs) or One-Stop Centers. 2. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits with GBV and PSEA awareness-raising initiatives.3. Training caseworkers on the safe and ethical management of GBV-related data. 4. Individual justice, legal aid, Clinical Management of Rape, MHPSS to GBV survivors including specialized care for conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) including GBV CM and MHPSS capacity-building. 5. Construction, maintenance, and repair of women and girls’ friendly spaces/community centers and psychosocial support services through WGFS 6. Develop and roll out economic empowerment initiatives to support the resilience of women and adolescent girls. 7. Safety and risk mitigation activities through GBV Safety Audits, Referral pathway mapping and strengthening activities, GBV risk mitigation training and assessments. 8. GBV awareness-raising including public communication campaign sessions organized during mass awareness-raising events such as International Women's Day (IWD), 16 days of activism against GBV, and others.9. Provide training and awareness to humanitarian actors on victim assistance services and how to make referrals for SEA survivors. Furthermore, UNFPA will continue in leading the GBV AoR - GBV coordination mechanisms and improving the quality of GBV response by enhancing the capacity of member organizations and ensuring coordination among partners. This will involve updating referral pathways, training packages, advocacy messages, and SOPs.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project promotes meaningful access to assistance by employing inclusive approaches that consider the diverse needs of different groups. Providing GBV services is critical to ensuring survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. As a result, UNFPA carries out a thorough risk analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce the risks of GBV. UNFPA prioritizes community engagement and facilitates decision-making processes that are inclusive and transparent. By actively involving community members in project planning, implementation, and monitoring, we aim to foster a sense of ownership and avoid any potential harm. UNFPA, as GBV AoR lead, will organize and facilitate training for a variety of humanitarian actors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. Besides, UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are confidential by building the capacity of its partners on the guiding principles of working on GBV so that survivors of GBV will feel comfortable accessing them but also to make sure that there are no negative consequences in accessing them.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project is principally about promoting gender equality, including across age groups. UNFPA integrates tailored approaches that acknowledge diverse needs in different contexts. The intervention will therefore include ensuring equitable access to services and opportunities for women and girls affected by the crisis.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

To remove barriers to access to assistance, UNFPA implements different targeted measures. This includes making facilities (WGSS) and services (for example, Mobile Brigades) physically accessible and ensuring staff training on disability awareness and inclusive practices. Additionally, UNFPA tailors assistance to address specific needs arising from different disabilities, ensuring that support services are accommodating and responsive. Continuous feedback mechanisms and consultations with individuals with disabilities are integrated to adapt and improve the intervention, ensuring meaningful access and support. In addition to involving people with disabilities and disability advocacy groups in various consultation processes, UNFPA broadens its collaboration with national non-governmental organizations specializing in disability support. This expansion of partnerships aims to strengthen our efforts in addressing the needs of individuals with disabilities more comprehensively.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project maintains a commitment to minimizing its environmental footprint and implementing measures to mitigate any potential impact on the environment. UNFPA Mozambique and its implementing partners will be guided by the UNFPA Social and Environmental Standards (SES) as outlined in its Environmental Efficiency Strategy (EES) 2021-2025. The thematic areas that most apply to the Mozambique Context are Biodiversity, Ecosystems and Sustainable Natural Resource Management, where UNFPA and its partners utilize natural resources responsibly to construct Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS), and recycle Capulanas, old school uniforms for the socioeconomic activities within the WGFSs. To mitigate environmental impact, UNFPA will further implement various measures such as waste management protocols, utilizing eco-friendly materials where feasible, and promoting energy efficiency in operations. In 2023, almost 1 million people were internally displaced, fleeing from the ongoing conflict in Cabo Delgado and the northern region, where attacks perpetrated by armed groups have intensified in the last years. In addition, Mozambique is one of the most natural disaster-prone countries in the world. Due to its geography, a high share of the country’s districts are at risk of recurring natural disasters, including tropical cyclones, droughts, and floods, especially those in the coastal and riverside areas. From 2013, more than 25 weather hazards have been reported in Mozambique, among them the devastating cyclones Idai and Kenneth that hit the country in 2019, creating severe disruption to critical services and posing serious threats to health outcomes. Thousands have been displaced due to natural disasters, the most recent being Tropical Cyclone Freddy March 2023, which hit the country twice, bringing heavy rains and floods. The disruptions caused by Freddy, intensified by a cholera outbreak, led to around 1.3 million people needing humanitarian assistance in 2023. Tropical storms and cyclones in Mozambique bring along heavy rains and severe flooding, creating many challenges to the humanitarian response. In terms of preparedness for the cyclone and flooding season, UNFPA Mozambique Country Office (CO) developed a strategy that includes: Coordination (Internal level and Inter-agency level), continue to lead the coordination of the GBV Sub Cluster (GBV AoR) and the SRH Working group (SRH WG) at national and sub-national levels and ensure UNFPA’s active participation in the Health Cluster at the national level to address the essential needs of women and girls impacted by the cyclones and flooding in a coordinated and effective manner; Participation in preparedness activities carried out by humanitarian partners including contingency planning workshops/meetings led by the National Disaster Management Institute (INGD) and OCHA, development of Mozambique’s HNO and HRP.
