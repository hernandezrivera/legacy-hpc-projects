Project Summary

Project Proposal Summary: Food Security and Livelihood Enhancement Project
Project Title: Strengthening Food Security and Livelihoods in crisis affected Communities in Payinjiar, Mayiandit, and Leer Counties of Unity State.
Project Duration: 12 months
Project Location: Payinjiar, Mayiandit and Leer Counties
Project Goal:
• To improve food security and enhance sustainable livelihoods for vulnerable households through integrated agricultural practices, capacity building, and economic empowerment initiatives.
Objectives:
1. Enhance Agricultural Productivity:
• Promote climate-resilient farming techniques and diversified crop production to ensure year-round food availability.
2. Strengthen Livelihood Opportunities:
• Support income generation through skill development, microenterprise support, and market linkages.
3. Improve Nutrition and Food Utilization:
• Promote awareness of balanced diets, food storage, and preparation practices to combat malnutrition.
4. Build Community Resilience:
• Establish local resource management systems and support sustainable natural resource use.
Key Activities:
• Capacity Building:
• Conduct training sessions on modern, climate-smart agricultural practices, livestock management, and aquaculture techniques.
Input Provision:
• Distribute high-quality crop and vegetable seeds and fishing kits to farmers to enhance productivity.
Economic Empowerment:
• Facilitate access to microloans and grants for small-scale enterprises and cooperative farming initiatives.
Infrastructure Development:
• Develop small-scale irrigation systems, storage facilities, and access roads to improve production and marketability.
Health and Nutrition Campaigns:
• Organize workshops on dietary diversity and conduct nutrition-sensitive agricultural programs.
Expected Outcomes:
1. Increased agricultural yields and reduced dependency on external food sources.
2. Enhanced household income through diversified livelihood options.
3. Improved nutritional status, especially among children and women.
4. Strengthened local capacity for disaster resilience and food system sustainability.
Target Beneficiaries:
• 24,000 individuals (6,000 HHs), including smallholder farmers, women-headed households, and unemployed youth in Payinjiar, Mayiandit and Leer Counties of Unity State.
Budget:
Estimated total cost: $200,000.
Monitoring and Evaluation:
• Conduct baseline, midline, and end-line surveys to assess progress.
• Engage stakeholders in regular review meetings and adapt strategies as needed.
Project Sustainability:
• Integrate project activities with local government plans and collaborate with community organizations to ensure long-term impact.
This project will provide immediate and long-term solutions to food insecurity and poverty, fostering resilient communities capable of withstanding future challenges in Payinjiar County.
The project will be implemented by RAA based in Unity State. The integrated response will reduce the vulnerability of affected populations and facilitate sustainable restoration and resilience strategies for crisis affected population by creating a foundation for community resilience. The integrated response will include, risk mitigation and food security and livelihood intervention. The project will run for 12-months targeting 24,000 individuals (6,000 HHs), of people in need in Southern Unity State i.e Mayiandit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties for 2025. The project will cost $200,000 only.

1. Needs

The assessed needs of the population affected by crisis in Mayiandit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties in Unity State are explained hereunder.1. Food unavailability and Access. • Limited Agricultural Productivity and low crop yields due to flooding and poor soil fertility, outdated farming techniques, lack of inputs, or inadequate irrigation systems has resulted in food insecurity in the areas of Mayiandit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties. • Dependence on humanitarian’s assistant where almost everyone in the community is not able to cultivate or work for his/her own to secure food to the family 2. Economic Livelihoods.• Lack of Diversified Income Sources: Heavy reliance on subsistence farming or a single source of income, making households vulnerable to economic shocks.• Unemployment and Underemployment: Limited job opportunities, especially for women and youth has made it difficult for the community to cope up with the situation.• Market Access Challenges: Poor infrastructure, lack of transport, and limited knowledge of market trends hinder selling produce at competitive prices. 3. Nutrition and Health • Malnutrition and Food Insecurity: Prevalence of undernourishment, especially among children, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly have been affected the most in Mayiandit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties. • Low Dietary Diversity: Insufficient access to nutritious foods like vegetables, fruits, and protein-rich sources has contributed to malnutrition.• Lack of Nutrition Awareness: Limited understanding of balanced diets and appropriate food preparation practices. 4. Climate and Environmental Challenges • Climate Change Impacts: Unpredictable weather patterns, floods, and other environmental stresses reducing agricultural productivity. • Depleted Natural Resources: Overuse of land, deforestation, and lack of sustainable farming practices. • Vulnerability to Disasters: Communities are ill-equipped to recover from natural disasters or environmental shocks. • Social and Gender Inequalities • Limited Access to Resources for Women and Marginalized Groups: Women and disadvantaged groups like IDPs, Returnees and Refugees who fled their Country place of origin have sometimes been borne by the host to access the resource and often lack access to land, shelter, training, and decision-making opportunities. • Unequal Workloads: Women disproportionately bear the burden of unpaid care work alongside farming responsibilities5. Capacity and Knowledge Gaps • Lack of Skills and Knowledge: Farmers lack training in modern, climate-resilient agricultural practices, business management, and financial literacy.• Weak Cooperatives and Producer Organizations: Limited capacity of local organizations to coordinate and advocate for community needs is always a challenge in Payinjiar County.

2. Project activities

The key response priorities and activities are. 1. Emergency Food Assistance Response Priorities: Immediate access to food for vulnerable populations facing acute food insecurity in Mayiandit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties. • Prevent malnutrition and save lives during the flooding. • Activities to be carried out. • In-kind food distribution: Providing staple foods based on culturally appropriate dietary requirements to some selected extreme vulnerable household and people living with disabilities. • Cash or voucher assistance: Allowing households to purchase food directly from local markets, promoting market functionality in Payinjiar County. • Supplementary feeding programs: Focused on children, pregnant, and lactating women.2. Livelihoods Protection Response Priorities: • Prevent the loss of critical livelihood assets during emergencies. • Support households in maintaining their income-generating activities. • Distribution of agricultural inputs: Seeds, tools, and fertilizers to farmers. • Provision of livestock support: Veterinary services, feed, and restocking of herds. • Cash-for-work programs: Engaging communities in asset creation, such as irrigation systems, dyke construction or small roads to access the vulnerable community in the remote areas. • Fishing gear distribution: Support for fishing communities with nets, boats, and training.3. Livelihoods Recovery and Resilience Building Response Priorities:• Restore and diversify livelihoods for longer-term stability. • Enhance resilience to future shocks (climate, conflict, economic). Activities.• Skills training and capacity building: For income diversification (e.g., vocational training, entrepreneurship programs). • Small business grants or loans: To support microenterprise recovery or creation and VSLA. Community savings and loans groups (CSLGs): Promote financial inclusion and access to credit. • Climate-smart agriculture: Training and provision of flood-resistant seeds, agroforestry, or water-efficient farming methods. • Irrigation and water harvesting: Rehabilitation or establishment of water resources for farming. 4. Food Production Support Response Priorities:• Strengthen local food systems to ensure year-round food availability in Payinjiar County. • Build sustainable agricultural practices to boost productivity in Payinjiar County. Activities. • Farmer field schools: Hands-on agricultural training to improve productivity.• Promotion of kitchen gardens: To ensure household-level food security. • Post-harvest management training: Reduce food loss through better storage and processing techniques. 5. Nutrition-sensitive Interventions Response Priorities: • Address malnutrition and improve access to nutritious foods. • Link food security with health and nutrition outcomes.• Activities Nutrition education: Promotion of dietary diversity and appropriate infant feeding practices. Biofortification: Distributing nutrient-rich seeds (e.g., iron-fortified beans). • School feeding programs: Targeting vulnerable children to ensure food access and education retention in some primary schools in Mayiendit, Leer and Payinjiar Counties. 6. Market Support and Value Chain Development Response Priorities: • Strengthen local markets to improve access to food and economic opportunities. • Link producers to markets and enhance income-generating opportunities. Activities .• Market assessments: • To identify gaps in food supply and demand. • Strengthening value chains: Support for processing, packaging, and marketing of agricultural products. • Support to traders and vendors: Providing capital, infrastructure, or training. 7. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Preparedness Priorities: • Mitigate the impacts of future shocks and stressors on food security. • Increase community capacity to adapt to climate change and other risks. Activities. • Early warning systems: Establishing community-based warning mechanisms. • Contingency planning and capacity
