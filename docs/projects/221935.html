Project Summary

The continued displacement of vulnerable communities due to protracted conflict and floods, compounded by climate change-related disasters throughout 2024, has significantly escalated the scale, scope, and complexity of civilian needs in the northern regions of Aweil East and Twic counties. This situation has led to the displacement of tens of thousands of individuals, a widespread loss of livestock, disruptions in agricultural and pastoral livelihoods, and the destruction of civilian property along with vital humanitarian facilities and assets.
From July to September 2025, these two counties experienced a second wave of unprecedented and catastrophic flooding, exacerbating the cumulative impacts of ongoing conflict and environmental calamities. As a result, Aweil East and Twic counties are now facing a dire crisis characterized displacement and loss of shelter by individuals household leading to exposuring of vulnerable pregnant women, people with disabilities, elderly and childrens to harsh conditions as result of flooding and conflicts.
During the dry season, an increase in cattle raids by armed groups from Unity and Lakes has raised insecurity levels in Twic counties, supplemented by Misseriya attacks in Aweil East, further destabilizing the region. In response to this multifaceted crisis, this humanitarian intervention aims to deliver life-saving services to those affected by disasters and conflict. Specifically, this will involve providing essential shelter and non-food items through a community-clustered Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) response to address the ongoing humanitarian crisis in Aweil East and Twic counties.
This targeted response seeks to assist approximately 15, 000 individuals in Aweil East and Twic counties (2850 males, 3150 females, 3600 boys, and 5550 girls) Provision of life-saving shelter and NFI materials to flood affected households, training of disaster risk reduction and climate mitigation Additionally, these intervention will seek to support the elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, people with disabilities, vulnerable host communities, child headed households to overcome the dire humanitarian suffering.
The Water for South Sudan Foundation will implement this project with state and county authorities, local communities, and various actors involved in procuring shelter and non-food items across both states. The foundation is committed to ensuring that the program yields meaningful and relevant outcomes tailored to all affected individuals' specific needs, priorities, and preferences, including girls, boys, women, and men.
Moreover, the Water for South Sudan Foundation is dedicated to fostering an environment where community members can exercise their rights, such as equitable access to quality services, the ability to voice their opinions regarding the program's effectiveness, and the opportunity to participate in decision-making processes that impact their lives. Building relationships founded on trust, mutual respect, transparency, and open communication is paramount, as the foundation engages with vulnerable individuals and communities.
Notably, the program will also prioritise the inclusion of people with disabilities, ensuring that their specific needs are considered during all phases of planning, implementation, and evaluation. This commitment aims to guarantee that their perspectives are integrated into the design of project components, fostering a holistic and inclusive approach to humanitarian assistance.

1. Needs

A Deterioration in shelter Needs with a total number of individuals in need (PIN) across the country is projected to reach a staggering 9.3 million by 2025, representing a dramatic increase from the estimated 2.2 million in 2025. Among this growing population, approximately 4.2 million individuals will require assistance with shelter and non-food items (SNFI). This significant surge in demand for humanitarian support can be attributed to multiple interrelated factors. Notably, the timing of the Inter-Agency Needs Assessment (ISNA) aligned with the peak of the flooding season, which led to extensive displacement among communities. Furthermore, the ongoing Sudan crisis have affected the purchasing power in the market of vulnerable household, exacerbating the situation. The increase in PIN is predominantly driven by compounded shocks stemming from extended conflict, widespread flooding, escalating food insecurity, rampant inflation, soaring food prices, and a critical lack of access to basic services. Within Aweil East and Twic, humanitarian assessments estimate that a total of 97,454 individuals is projected in Aweil East and Twic , which equates to approximately 6.7% of the national population, will be left without adequate shelter by 2025. The challenges faced in 2024, including methodological changes in using the Global Shelter Severity calculation, decreased investment in the housing sector in South Sudan, and insufficient funding for the SNFI Cluster, have all contributed to this alarming increase in humanitarian needs. The emerging market challenges in Warrap state and northern bahr el ghazal project a difficult year 2025 in Aweil East and Twic an estimated 137444 individuals are expected to be affected, with significant impact, particularly in Aweil East and Twic Counties. The demographic breakdown reveals that approximately 27% of those affected will be boys, 28% girls, 21% men, and 24% women, all aged between 5 and 59 years.The ongoing conflicts, border tensions in Twic, frequent flood disasters, and deteriorating economic conditions are anticipated to further disrupt livelihoods and trade activities. These factors will likely exacerbate humanitarian needs in 2025, placing additional strain on individuals' ability to carry out daily activities, especially as flooding continues to undermine livelihood opportunities in both counties. Throughout 2024, the simultaneous inflow of returnees and the recurrence of flooding have led to a large-scale displacement crisis, preventing vulnerable populations particularly in the Aweil East and Twic Counties, from undertaking essential journeys to secure shelter and non-food items. Vulnerable groups face substantial obstacles when attempting to relocate westward, as such journeys necessitate traversing regions plagued by insecurity, where internally displaced persons (IDPs) have reportedly experienced robbery or violence.In response to these urgent needs, the proposed intervention aims to provide emergency shelters, essential non-food items (NFIs), and transitional housing, while actively involving local communities in the design and implementation processes. This community-centered approach is intended to enhance living conditions, foster stability, and diminish vulnerability, particularly among women, children, and the elderly.

2. Project activities

In response, Water for South Sudan (WFSS) is dedicated to saving lives and upholding the dignity of approximately 15,000 individuals in precarious situations. The project comprises several key activities designed to address the immediate needs of these populations. 1. Needs Assessment: A foundational step in our project is the thorough needs assessment. Utilizing cluster-endorsed tools, we will conduct comprehensive evaluations in coordination with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and other local partners. 2. Market and Cash Feasibility Assessments for potential of cash-based interventions. 3. Community Engagement. 4. Feedback and Response Mechanisms. 5. Community-Based Targeting and Registration 6. Distribution of Emergency Shelter Kits. 7. Distribution of Lifesaving NFI Kits: .8. Provision of HHs with NFI Replacement Kits: . 9. Alongside the distribution of materials, we will engage in sensitization efforts, educating communities on the appropriate use of NFI and shelter materials. 10. Training sessions focusing on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) will be organized for community members and local committees. 11. Post-Distribution Monitoring: WFSS strongly emphasizes a cash-based, voucher-based, or hybrid modality for delivering NFIs or shelter materials whenever market assessments indicate their potential. In conjunction with these efforts, WFSS aims to enhance community knowledge and skills regarding Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) approaches. To ensure that our interventions are conflict-sensitive and do not exacerbate existing tensions, we apply a conflict sensitivity framework throughout our project activities. Our commitment to the "Do No Harm" principle underpins all our activities. WFSS is acutely aware of the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of different demographic groups, including men, women, boys, and girls. To promote the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries, we will implement a comprehensive array of measures:1. Consultations: Throughout the various phases of project design and implementation, WFSS will engage in consultations with diverse groups including men, women, and children from various backgrounds. This engagement will ensure that gender and conflict sensitivity inform our risk assessments2. Regular Monitoring: To actively gather insights on participants’ experiences and feedback from other community members, regular monitoring will be integrated into our programming. 3. WFSS will place a priority on addressing the needs of women based on vulnerability criteria. 4. All WFSS staff, in collaboration with local authorities and community leaders, will receive comprehensive training on the principles of protection and conflict sensitivity. 5. Child Protection: We will implement specific measures to safeguard children engaged in our programs. All activities will have child protection protocols in place to ensure their safety and well-being. 6. Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Prevention: Implementing comprehensive measures to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) is integral to our programming.7. Enhancing community awareness regarding protection issues is vital for fostering a safe environment. Educational sessions will be arranged to raise awareness about rights, available support, and the mechanism for reporting abuse. 8. To maximize our efforts, WFSS will work closely with local organizations, including women’s groups and child protection agencies. WFSS is dedicated to addressing the needs of vulnerable individuals affected by displacement, flooding, or conflict through a holistic approach that combines immediate assistance with long-term strategies for resilience and recovery. Our emphasis on community involvement, targeted assistance, and protection measures underscores our commitment to serving those in need while promoting dignity, safety, and sustainability.
