Project Summary

This project aims to respond to the education needs and well-being of the vulnerable school-aged children and adolescents in Area C, Hebron, East Jerusalem, and Gaza Strip to enable them to access education.
The main drivers of education-related vulnerabilities include attacks on schools, movement restrictions, poor and/or inadequate classrooms, and WASH facilities, and poverty. These factors undermine the quality of education, generate protection concerns, and contribute to the early school dropout.
This project will contribute to the 2022 HRP Education Objective 2: “Increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children” and Education Objective 3: “Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies.
Several assessments show that the realization of the right to education remains severely compromised in these locations. The Education Cluster recorded 119 education-related incidents in 2020, affecting 7,757 students and 615 teachers and school staff. The lack of safe access has significant negative consequences for students’ schooling. It results in a decrease of school hours, increased dropout rate of children, discouraging teaching staff, and displacement or separation of families as they seek solutions in getting their children in schools elsewhere.
The need for MHPSS has been exacerbated by the COVID 19, the recent escalation in Gaza Strip, and the chronic protection challenges the children experience going to and from school as they cross Israeli military checkpoints and closed military zones or pass by settlements where they may experience harassment and violence as reported by the education cluster.
Furthermore, the outbreak of COVID-19 significantly compounded children’s vulnerabilities due to intermittent closures of schools as part of the measures adopted to contain the pandemic. As of the end of August 2020, 1.3 million children had lost at least three months of schooling. The move to online education also negatively impacted children from remote, disadvantaged areas of the West Bank and Gaza. Many students lack internet access, devices, and adequate digital literacy to effectively utilize the online learning opportunities.
The project will ensure safe access to school for 31,000 students aged 6-16 and their teachers in H2, South Hebron Hills, Bethlehem, South Nablus, East Jerusalem, and other selected areas in the West Bank. They will be supported with a holistic package of interventions, including training school counselors, volunteers, and school teachers on child safeguarding, safe identification and referral, and Psychosocial Support interventions.
In partnership with local NGOs, UNICEF will support 30,000 vulnerable children and adolescents (40% females, 1% CWD) with Mental Health and Psychosocial (MHPSS) school-based activities. The MHPSS interventions will be implemented after school and during the summer vacation with support from the Ministry of Education and local implementing partners.
5,000 children (50 % girls) will be provided with remedial education interventions to mitigate the learning loss and improve their numeracy and literacy skills.
The project will support over 60,000 poorest students in West Bank and Gaza aged 6-17 (50% girls), who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue in their education by providing basic, urgent school supplies, including stationery kits. These children, if not supported, are at risk of dropping out of school.
UNICEF, in partnership with local NGOs will support 300 schools to operationalize safe schools protocols to prevent infections, including COVID 19 and ensure safe learning environments for children. 1,500 schools will be provided with school hygiene kits to support them operationalize the COVID 19 protocols.

1 - Needs

The HNO estimates 579,173 (56 % females), including 13,398 Children with Disabilities in Palestine need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. Children with disabilities, girls, and children in Area C, Hebron H2, East Jerusalem, and Gaza are most vulnerable. Attacks on schools, unsafe access to schools due to the education-related incidents and escalations mainly for the children in the Gaza Strip, the Israel-controlled areas of the West Bank, and the Seam areas have left more than 170,680 children and adolescents (73,716 males) in need of MHPSS. Children from Area C, Hebron H2, and East Jerusalem continue to witness a non-permissive environment, incidents of settler violence, increased threats of demolition, and displacement that challenge their access to schools. Military harassment and settler violence against children on the way to school is the primary concern of parents in these areas. Girls face a higher risk of school dropouts due to parents’ fear for their safety during the school commute. Around 15,768 children and their teachers (9,160 males) are estimated to be in need of different protection services. In the Gaza Strip, the shortage of adequate school infrastructure is driven by a range of factors, including the longstanding Israeli closure, the internal Palestinian divide, and the recurrent escalations of hostilities. At least 331 education facilities in Gaza Strip, including over 136 schools, were damaged during the May escalation, affecting 121,092 children (59,008 girls; 62,084 boys). This has worsened the existing shortage of classrooms, where 65 % of schools are operating on double or triple shifts to mitigate the overcrowding of classes. The multiple school shift results in shortened school days of only 4 hours every day. The lack of adequate, safe, and accessible school infrastructure is also a major concern in vulnerable areas of the West Bank, especially in H2, Area C, and East Jerusalem, mainly due to restrictive planning regime set up by the Israeli authorities, which prevents the construction and upgrading of schools. There is a shortage of more than 3,000 classrooms in East Jerusalem for the public system. Many schools operate in private residential properties, which do not meet minimum standards. Nearly 60 per cent of schools lack playgrounds, libraries, or science labs, and at least half of the schools suffer from substandard physical conditions. The outbreak of COVID-19 significantly compounded the children’s vulnerabilities due to prolonged closures of schools and kindergartens and burdened an already strained educational system. As of the end of August 2020, 1.3 million children had lost at least three months of schooling. Many students from remote, disadvantaged areas of the West Bank and Gaza strip lack internet access, devices, and adequate digital literacy to effectively benefit from the online learning opportunities and manage learning loss. Only 44% of households with children (10-17 years old) who are currently enrolled in education have the required devices (desktop, laptop, or tablet) to enable their children to access online learning materials (PCBS 2019 household survey). Girls and children from poor families were particularly affected. Around 24,789 children (12,568 males and 12,221 females) need remedial education to enhance their literacy and numeracy skills and mitigate their risk of dropping out of school. More families have been pushed into poverty due to the COVID 19 pandemic and the recent escalation in Gaza Strip. Parents are unable to afford basic school supplies and schools for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out. Girls are more vulnerable in this context as parents focus their expenses on basic family needs and prefer to offer education opportunities to boys. Many households have expressed a lack of financial capacity to support their children’s education (MNSA 2021 report). The need for maintaining a minimum level of hygiene standards when schools are open such as hand washing facilities, adequate functioning toilets, cleaning, and disinfecting materials to keep schools safe from COVID 19 infections and other diseases has become more urgent. However, many schools located in poor, vulnerable locations will not be able to afford the required resources to operationalize the safe school COVID protocols. at least 300 schools in vulnerable communities need to establish school health committees to implement hygiene protocols and prevent disease infections, including COVID-19. 1,500 schools in the West Bank and Gaza will need school disinfection supplies to be able to maintain the required hygiene standards.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will follow a clear multisectoral approach, build on the humanitarian-development nexus approach, and coordinate with WASH, Health, and Child Protection clusters to deliver its education interventions. Based on the needs and in line with the Education Cluster HRP strategy, this project will respond to the identified needs through the following activities1. Provide children and adolescents with recreational after school interventions and/or summer activities to support their well being and PSS. 2. Support a holistic package of interventions to ensure that children have safe access to schools3. Support rehabilitation of schools to provide safe environment. 4. Provide remedial education to mitigate learning loss to low performing children;5. Support most vulnerable schools to operationalize COVID 19 safe schools protocols 6. Provide most vulnerable children with individualized teaching and learning materials to mitigate the risk of them dropping out of school.UNICEF will support 30,000 vulnerable children and adolescents (40% females) (10,000 age 6-12 years old) and (20,000 age 12-18 years old) with MHPSS school-based activities. The activities will be implemented after schools and during the summer vacation. The program will utilize UNICEF’s Adolescent Kit for Innovation and Expression to enhance adolescents’ resilience, well-being, and learning readiness. The Kit provides a package of guidance, tools, activities, and supplies that support learning life skills and community engagement while prioritizing psychosocial well-being. Tools like “inspiration cards” and “emotion cubes” help engage adolescents in a range of activities that build on their strengths and provide them with opportunities to learn new skills, develop positive relationships, build their self-esteem and coping skills, and give back to their communities. A team of psychologists will be deployed in each of the centers to coordinate mental health promoting activities and provide guidance to activators and volunteers. All facilitators involved will be oriented on AAP and PSEA. UNICEF will support 4,500 children (51% girls) from Hebron H2, Area C, and East Jerusalem through a holistic approach to a protective learning environment. The intervention package will include training school counselors, volunteers, and school teachers on child safeguarding, safe identification and referral, and Psychosocial Support interventions. Team Up sessions will be conducted for the children in the communities to create safe environments for the children. In partnership with local NGO’s, UNICEF will work with community members to assess l protection issues affecting children’s safe access to school, use the findings to develop local solutions to protect children from chronic protection challenges while going to and from school. UNICEF will support the rehabilitation of 26 damaged schools in Gaza to improve children’s access to a safe learning environment using the building-back better approach (BBB). The BBB Key elements will cover the application of Child-Friendly Schools (CFS) Standards, promote community engagement, expand classroom facilities where space permits and enhance WASH facilities, including for menstrual hygiene management (MHM), and access to clean water in schools, increase solarization, support disability-friendly access to facilities and support quality learning through access to functioning school libraries, science labs, and remedial learning to address learning losses. 5,000 children (40% females) will be supported with remedial education to mitigate the learning loss. Teachers in the targeted schools will be trained on how to manage lost learning. This will include training teachers on how to identify regressed knowledge and skills amongst their learners and underperforming students and how to support the identified gaps with remedial education. A pre-test and post-test will be administered at the beginning and end of the project respectively to monitor learning progress. The need for maintaining a minimum level of WASH services at schools has become more urgent. UNICEF will support vulnerable schools to operationalize the safe school operations COVID protocols. 300 schools in vulnerable communities will be supported to establish school health committees to maintain hygiene protocols and COVID-19 prevetion measures. UNICEF will build on the success and lessons learne from the school health committees that were developed and supported in 190 schools. 1,500 schools in the West Bank and Gaza will also be supported with school disinfection supplies to be able to maintain the required hygiene standards in their schools.The project will support 60,000 poorest students in Gaza aged 6-17 (50% girls), who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue their education by providing basic, urgent supplies including stationery kits. Many parents are increasingly unable to afford basic school supplies and school fees for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out. Girls are more vulnerable in this context as parents focus their expenses on family basic needs and prefer to offer education opportunities to boys. The contents of the stationery kits were developed based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries and will be procured from the local market to boost the local economy. all UNICEF implementing partners will ensure that Accountability to Affected Populations’ system is developed. UNICEF will work with Protection cluster to address issues related to protection issues, GBV and referral of cases to relevant services and to timely address potential SEA concerns”. UNICEF will also collaborate with WASH cluster to ensure adequate WASH services in schools. UNICEF and partners will ensure that the proposed action does not further harm or negatively affect the children (girls’ and boys’ learners) and women and men (teachers, local community members, and staff working with cluster partners).

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Based on the nature of interventions in the project, cash transfers are not relevant. The needs of individual beneficiaries will be met through response interventions that are not cash based. Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback from affected populations will inform the corrective measures and re-design interventions based on beneficiaries’ feedback and recommendations. Beneficiaries will be regularly informed, at the start of the project, of what they can expect from the project and staff, as well as being provided with clear information on details about how they can give feedback and complain if they experience or witness misconduct by representatives. Community-based and child-friendly participant satisfaction assessments using developed tools and through quarterly focus group discussions with women, men, girls, and boys will regularly inform the project of what is working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions will also allow children, caregivers, and community volunteers to evaluate the various activities. Partners will be required to embed an accountability framework into the monitoring plan which will contribute to reporting on the AAP indicators in the log frame.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

During the implementation of all activities, the implementing partners will be asked to include in their plans a complaint mechanism and channels sensitive to PSEA. For instant, at all centers implementing after-school activities and summer activities, complaint boxes will be placed in private places and beneficiaries will be oriented on how to place their complaints in the box and that they will be anonymous.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. All UNICEF partners have been assessed on their risk to SEA and given access to a capacity-building program. Which continuous this year. UNICEF is implementing a project to strengthen the capacity building of 12 national NGOs on AAP & PSEA. The work includes the development of AAP & PSEA Context Analysis, capacity building, and AAP/ PSEA system strengthening. UNICEF SoP has also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint. During all planned training, a session on AAP/PSEA will be included as part of the training agenda in order to raise awareness of all participants on AAP/PSEA. The session will include information on complaint mechanisms and channels for providing complaints.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end users, including SEA. UNICEF also conducts end user monitoring field visits to ensure safety and quality of services and supplies provided. UNICEF and partners will ensure that the proposed action does not in any way further harm or negatively affect the children (girls’ and boys’ learners) and women and men (teachers, local community members and staff working in Implementing partners). UNICEF will put measures in place to ensure that IPs adhere to Child Safeguarding Policy, along with a reporting mechanism. Staff will be trained on the policy upon signing their contract and will be supported to prevent harm to children they work with. IPs will also commit to support local organizations, and partners to be aware of the importance of a child safeguarding policy. The beneficiaries will be informed, at the start of the project, of what they can expect from the IP organization and staff, as well as being provided with clear information on details about how they can give feedback and complaints if they experience or witness misconduct by representatives. COVID 19 measures will be adhered to during all implementation to mitigate the risks of COVID infections due to their participation in face-to-face activities such as training.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will target the most vulnerable humanitarian-affected children in Area C, H2 Hebron, East Jerusalem, and Gaza Strip. Beneficiaries will be identified using the following selection criteria:Children (boys and girls) in schools in the most vulnerable communities will be targeted by the project. The selection criteria for the schools will include: (a) schools with poor performance based on the MoE data, (b) located in marginalized areas where children are hard to reach by teachers, (c) schools that are hard to be connected with the distance learning platforms due to poor internet connectivity or frequent electricity cuts and (d) located in the most vulnerable poor communities. Children from these schools will benefit from a package of interventions outlined above. The action will support schools from these communities to establish school health committees including children and the committee members will be trained on how to develop and implement actions to maintain COVID hygiene protocols and prevent disease infections including COVID. Schools damaged during the recent Gaza escalation will be prioritized for building back better and will ensure accessibility of classrooms and WASH facilities to children with disabilities. Recognizing that participation of girls in some communities is more sensitive due to social-cultural concerns, and based on lessons learned from previous years, Implementing Partners (IPs) will adapt interventions to ensure access and acceptance by girls and their caregivers. The methodologies employed by IPs will ensure suitability for both males and females. When necessary, some methods will be re-adjusted to respond better to the different needs of male and female students, School committees and teachers, and appropriate to the local and cultural context.
