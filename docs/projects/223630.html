Project Summary

Mozambique, one of the most climate-vulnerable countries in the world, frequently experiences severe climate shocks, including cyclones, floods, and droughts. Currently nearly 140,000 people are displaced due to the impact of extreme weather events in central and southern Mozambique. Working closely with authorities, this project aims to protect and assist vulnerable, climate-affected communities through anticipatory action (capacity building and early warning messaging) and responsive protection interventions (direct service provision).
UNHCR is targeting 70,651 vulnerable adult men and women and elderly men and women in Nampula, Sofala and Zambezia provinces.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique, one of the most climate-vulnerable countries in the world, frequently experiences severe climate shocks, including cyclones, floods, and droughts. Currently nearly 140,000 people are displaced due to the impact of extreme weather events in central and southern Mozambique. Working closely with authorities, this project aims to protect and assist vulnerable, climate-affected communities through anticipatory action (capacity building and early warning messaging) and responsive protection interventions (direct service provision). Key target groups: Vulnerable adult men and women and elderly men and women in Nampula, Sofala and Zambezia provinces.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Activity 1: Development, translation, and dissemination of Early Warning Messages in Nampula, Sofala and Zambezia to prepare communities in the event of a climate emergency. Activity 2: Dissemination of Practical Guide for Protection Mainstreaming in Climate Disasters Nampula, Sofala and Zambezia to local authorities, community committees, national and international organizations involved in the climate response.Activity 3: Protection capacity-building of local authorities and community groups (e.g., protection principles, PSEA, identification and referrals of people with specific needs, protection mainstreaming) in Nampula and Zambezia.Activity 5: Provision of civil documentation in Nampula and Zambezia to restore affected peoples access to basic rights and services.Activity 6: Provision of MHPSS services in Nampula and Zambezia to address trauma experienced before and during displacement.**Cash-based interventions (CBI) for protection services will be a component of this project.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR takes into consideration the protection of all persons affected and at-risk across all sectors and throughout the programmemanagement cycle. Ensuring a do no harm approach is part and parcel of what we do and we take the issue of protection and inclusion very seriously. In fact, all UNHCR’s programming are designed according to the principles of Age Gender Diversity (AGD) mainstreaming. The guiding principles of AGD approach, community based, and right based approaches will be used to gather specific protection risks from different categories of affected populations through systematic discussions with men, women, boys, and girls.Protection is at the core of every UNHCR intervention in Mozambique and globally. Its community-based and AAP approaches involve regular community engagement (CE), two-way communication with communities, and the implementation of complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM). UNHCR ensures that people are at the centre of its interventions, providing feedback before, during, and after, and mitigating the risk of acting in a way that causes harm or is not in the best interest of the population.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNHCR systematically involves affected populations in the design of its interventions, as part of its accountability to persons of concern and in line with its Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) Policy. UNHCR consistently seeks to ensure that all affected populations can equally enjoy their rights, have non-discriminatory access to protection, services, and assistance on an equal footing, and can participate fully in decisions that affect them and their family members and communities. UNHCR supports government and civil society partners in adopting an age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach; protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) through its robust PSEA policies, with zero tolerance at their base; and ensuring accountability to the affected people (AAP), including by improving two-way communication with communities to promote safety, inclusion and meaningful participation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR engages and consults people with specific needs, including people with disabilities, through an age, gender, diversity (AGD) approach in order to identify the specific barriers they face and ensure disability inclusion in the provision of life-saving assistance, including in making referrals to specialized services as needed. By applying an AGD approach in all Protection activities UNHCR aims to ensure that all affected populations, including those with specific needs such as persons with disabilities, enjoy their rights on an equal footing and participate meaningfully in decisions that affect their lives, families and communities.
