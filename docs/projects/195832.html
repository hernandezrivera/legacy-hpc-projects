Project Summary

Overall, the Food Security Sector (FSS) estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity, which is at least 54.4% of Syria population (HNO 2023). While the food insecure figures have slightly declined by at least 4% compared to last year’s record increase, the number of people facing acute food insecurity remains extremely high, pre-Lebanese financial crisis period. Lack of access to livelihood opportunities is a major challenge that most rural households are facing and one of contributing factors that is leading most families to leave their place of residence in search of livelihood opportunities. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 68,571 crisis-affected households (equivalent to 411,429 people) in Syria, through support to diversify their livelihoods and income sources and improve their food and nutrition security. This will be achieved through supporting vegetable and poultry production in Syria.
The overall objective of the proposed action is to improve the food security and nutrition of vulnerable crisis-affected households and the project seeks to deliver the following outputs:
Output 1: Increased household food production (family farming) for 40,000 households.
The output aims to increase vegetable/poultry production of 40,000 vulnerable households (of which 30 % are women-headed) through the provision of full vegetable production packages, which include vegetable seeds/seedlings, and organic fertilizers, or through provision of egg laying hens with feed and vitamins.
Output 2: Increased income generating opportunities through agriculture related livelihood support. The market-driven income generating opportunities include examples such as mushroom production or beekeeping, food processing, etc. for 28,571 households.
This output aims to support 28,571 vulnerable households (of which 30 % are women-headed) to generate income from agriculture related livelihood opportunities
It is expected that the project support through provision of vegetable and poultry inputs, income generating opportunities and associated training such as business skills and marketing, will diversify the diets of the targeted households and also increase household income and employment creation through the sale of products, for the most vulnerable members of the households, especially for women and youth. It is expected this will lead to improvement of livelihoods, increased local availability of food, increased self-reliance and improved food security and nutrition of crisis-affected households, especially the most vulnerable.

3 - Needs

FSS estimates the PIN at 15 million (HNO 2023). The complex drivers of the protracted food security Syrian crisis, and contributing factors led to record agricultural production losses for the second consecutive season and consequently a sharp decline in livelihoods and food insecurity for most farming H.H. It now requires to go beyond short-term responses, and focus on scaling up ER, restoration, protection and promotion of livelihoods, over the short- to-medium-term. Including work around the entire food system and its related value chains. This project will enhance H.H production of vegetables and poultry through income generating opportunities to increase dietary diversity and micronutrient consumption. And will provide inputs to improve H.H food availability through backyard poultry, vegetable production, food processing. H.H-level farming is an activity often undertaken by women and this target group will be prioritized by this intervention. This support will be combined with skills training including marketing, and capacity building to strengthen the technical skills of beneficiaries and improve their food production and income generation capacities for food and nutrition security. H.H food production activities, namely vegetables, poultry, food processing, will also target vulnerable groups with limited access to land (IDPs, the landless rural population, urban/peri-urban populations).

4 - Implementation of activities

Output 1: Increased 40,000 H.H food production (farming H.H). Activities: 1)Refine composition of vegetable production inputs based on local conditions. 2)Identify beneficiaries as vulnerability criteria. 3)Contracting IP for distributing vegetable production inputs. 4)Procurement and distribution vegetable production inputs (assorted seeds/seedlings, micro irrigation inputs, fertilizers) or poultry production inputs (15 egg laying hens, feed and vitamins). 5)Conduct training for trainers for technicians to facilitate community-level training. 6)training the targeted H.H using the farmer field school approach and poultry production with focus. 7)Create awareness on basic nutrition and health, especially for women. 8)P-D assessment and impact evaluation. Output 2: Increased 28,571 H.H income generating through agriculture related livelihood support. Activities: 1)Identify viable agriculture-based livelihood in target location, based on community consultations, market access, location and situation of natural resources, 2)Contracting IP to distribute the required production inputs. 3) beneficiaries Selecting according to vulnerability criteria. 4)Procurement and distribution of production inputs (mushroom kits, beehives, food processing tools) for the establishment of small-scale production. 5)Train the targeted H.H using farmer field school approach, focusing on food safety, quality and marketing. 6)assessment and impact evaluation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted due being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (IDPs and host communities; returnees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance. 3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and monitoring. The project taken into account protection elements, in line with do no harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted due being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included also, to limit possible frictions, and special attention will be for returnees helping them in their re-settlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating assets. An accountability system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place.
