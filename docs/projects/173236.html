Project Summary

In North Darfur, GOAL works closely with the Ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources in Khartoum and in North Darfur as well as FAO on identifying needs, gaps incoverage, and on programme implementation. In addition, GOAL remains an active participant in the FSL Cluster at both national and state levels where FSL programming is implemented, including North Darfur. GOAL’s FSL programming is closely integrated with other sectors, especially nutrition, health, and protection through targeting vulnerable households (including affected by malnutrition, people living with disabilities, and women headed households) in catchment areas of supported clinics. GOAL will also incorporate protection and women’s empowerment approaches. With OFDA funding, GOAL proposes to focus on the IDP populations in Kutum Town as well as Kassab and Fata Borno Camps near Kutum, and complementary funding (SHF) will be used to reach vulnerable host communities in Kutum and Al Waha localities to ensure all relevant communities have their needs addressed. Activities are closely coordinated with other FSL actors in North Darfur to eliminate any duplication of effort. Through the FSL Cluster and Cash Workin Groups, GOAL incorporates and shares lessons learned and harmonized approaches to livelihoods activities in order to maintain high technical standards in its work. In South Kordofan, GOAL will work with Village Development Committee (VDC) in areas where they are already established and functional. GOAL has identified that displaced households in North Darfur and South Kordofan are facing multiple shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic that has severely limited livelihoods, which has come on top of economic erosion, failed harvests that are stretching the lean season, tribal violence in South Kordofan, and displacement. In response, GOAL plans to target 400 households with Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) transfers in order to meet immediate food security needs during the COVID-19 pandemic and prevent further deterioration of assets and use of more harmful coping strategies, and to put households in a better position to engage in productive livelihoods in the future.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC Projection for October - December 2020 an estimated 6,380, 766 million people or higher in the coming months will be IPC Phase 3 & 4 , the highest figure ever recorded in the Sudan (IPC 2020) and worse food security outcomes through December 2020. In South Kordofan it is projected that 398,268 (22%) and in North Darfur 526,294 (21%) will be in IPC Phase 3 and higher needing increased humanitarian assistance needs due to extremely high staple food prices and persistent macroeconomics challenges, which have reduced household purchasing power and has been exacerbated by the loss of assets and livelihoods from flooding and persistent conflicts. The average price of the local food basket increased by nearly 200 per cent compared to 2019, according to the World Food Programme (WFP). The inflation is pushing up prices of basic food, like sorghum, which is now 240 per cent higher than one year ago and more than 680 per cent higher than the five-year average. The stable food prices are expected to remain high with spiraling inflation. In addition, prices of agricultural inputs for the Food Security and Livelihoods Sector increased dramatically, which has reduced procurement levels considerably, with fewer people getting assistance. The price of sorghum in 2019 was the equivalent of $645 (at the official rate), now it costs $1,425. The price of donkey ploughs in 2019 was $39, and now it is $74. Rashad locality hosts over 3,500 returnees and over 22,000 IDPs, vulnerable households rely heavily on their livestock as a food source. A GOAL’s assessment found that only 23% of households were able to consume milk and 46% of households had poor dietary diversity. In nearby Talodi locality, much of the available farmland is inaccessible, due to continued presence of SPLM-N or, in many cases, landmines and UXO. Households in Talodi reported much more diverse sources of income, but livestock remains an important source of income as well as nutrition, particularly for IDPs who lack access to land. Rashad and Talodi both have significant grazing area, hosting over 434,974 and 443,653 animals respectively, but veterinary and extension services are not functioning well due to lack of equipment, vaccines, vehicles, and fuel for community animal health workers to travel. GOAL have already provided 200 households in Rashad and Talodi localities with improved dairy goat breeds (for improved milk production) and training and intends to pass on 200 more goats in the next cycle. The assessment also noted that South Kordofan is well suited for beekeeping and was well-known for this prior to the conflict, especially Talodi locality. GOAL’s identified and earmarked for further support four community groups that previously practiced simple beekeeping but are unable to get bank loans or other financing to fully rebuild this as an effective livelihood strategy. However, according to the April – June 2020 DTM Sudan Registration Report noted that with the recent evacuation of displacement camps in South Sudan (namely, Ida, Agung and Faamir) have resulted in the largescale return of affected populations to northern Sudan (their country of origin). Consequently, the scarcity of available services, transportation, and infrastructure in areas of return have resulted in secondary displacement to Kadugli, South Kordofan. According to WFP (Food Security Monitoring Survey for Q1,2020), 44 percent of refugee households in Sudan are food insecure. The level of food insecurity is particularly high among refugee households in Darfur, where 58 per cent are food insecure compared to 37 per cent in Southern and Eastern Sudan. Youth unemployment is very high in North Darfur and South Kordofan indicating a high level of need and risk of joining militias. The MCA is aimed at mitigating this risk and providing for essential items to households with vulnerable children, elderly, women headed households and youth.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming andpromoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while makingsure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks toassist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externallyto ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people withdisabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentionaland unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partners are aware ofprotection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used inprogramming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups.Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs andnorms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV.Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those atrisk are vital considerations for GOAL.
