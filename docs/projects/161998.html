Project Summary

WHO’s response strategy is aligned with the overall Health Sector Strategy as articulated in the Joint Response Plan 2020. The activities prioritized under this project have been selected based on the Health Sector needs highlighted by the mid-term review of the Joint Response Plan 2019, Joint Multisectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA)- September 2019, and Population In Need figures (ISCG, November 2019). WHO remains the only actor in support of the MoHFW with the mandate and capacity to set priorities, minimum standards, and support quality health service delivery.
In 2020 WHO, as the lead of the Health Sector, will continue to work towards equity and quality of services, advocate on behalf of the Health Sector on inadequately addressed issues, strongly engage government in localization efforts and stimulate documentation and publication of lessons learned in the crisis.
WHO will focus on quality service delivery as part of efforts to sustain what is achieved over the last two years of the response while maintaining capacity to continue support response to needs that result from emergencies due to natural disasters and disease outbreaks in this protracted humanitarian crisis as well as to ensure continuity of emergency services during the monsoon and cyclone seasons.
Going forward, WHO intends to not only address the immediate needs, but also providing regular and timely information on public health risks and requirements and filling gaps through effective coordination of partners and effective response ensuring access to life saving interventions mediated by rationalized health service delivery system.
The underlying principles will be to liaise with national government on the governance and leadership through Civil Surgeon’s office and MOHFW Coordination Cell, support in developing standards, guidelines and protocols, extending of national programs to the FDMN settings and; avail of health workforce, vaccines and essential technology, health information, service delivery based on minimum services package, for boys and girls, women and men in both the Rohingyas and host community.
The planned interventions will be guided by periodical service availability assessments and external independent reviews and assessments will be used as an opportunity for the Rohingya and host community populations to review the work of WHO

Objectives

The overall goal of the WHO emergency response strategy is to reduce morbidity and mortality among the Rohingya population and host community in Cox’s Bazar. The emergency response will build on existing health infrastructure, partner network, community resources and structures. The response will integrate resilience building components to foster sustainable solutions to the Rohingya crisis and for the affected host community in a system strengthening manner. In support of this aim, WHO will support the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Bangladesh, the Civil Surgeon’s Office, Cox’s Bazar, Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner’s Office (RRRC), and work in conjunction with sector partners, in particular those in the health, WASH, protection and nutrition sectors. 1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases. 2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to communicable disease risks including diseases with outbreak potential, and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone 3. Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behaviour and utilization of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender and age considerations and vulnerable groups. 4. Strengthen health sector coordination, information management and monitoring towards achieving rational, standardized and accountable health service delivery

Needs

The need for coordinated health response with the ministry of health for the over 130 health partners providing health services to the affected population in Rohingya camps and host community remains priority. This will ensure minimal duplication of efforts, fill critical gaps on availability, safe access and quality of health services for men, women, boys, girls and at-risk groups.• The refugee population remain vulnerable to weather-related emergencies which also impact disease trends disproportiately for men, women, boys and girls. There is need for continued coordination of emergency response and preparedness in relation to monsoon and cyclone to minimize health impact on the affected Rohingya population and host community• Need to ensure adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities that address the needs• Address critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetric care, newborn care, family planning provision (particularly long acting methods).• Need for continued strengthening of health facilities to deliver GBV care based on identified gaps on service availability, access and quality from 2019 GBV quality assessment of PHCs and other information sources • Need for continued advocacy effort of extending national health programs (such as EPI, TB/HIV/AIDS) to Rohingya men, women, boys and girls • Address shortages in Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity• Address mental health and psychosocial support training gaps and limited information on substance abuse to inform response interventions• Need for health care waste management solution including safe disposal of expired medicines and enhanced water quality surveillance in collaboration with DPHE, UNICEF and WASH actors.• Rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistance.• Adolescent health needs, including sexual and reproductive health as well as • Need to address risks for communicable diseases• Immunization coverage for Pentavalent 3 among children under 1 year old improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 78% by end of September 2019 but requires substantive improvements to achieve >95%. • Need to strengthen and sustain system for prevention, early detection and response to outbreak prone diseases.
