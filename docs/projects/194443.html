Project Summary

South Sudan is a complex multifaceted protection crisis with over 57% of the 5.6 million people in need of protection services in 2023 being in severe need. The humanitarian situation is driven by severe climatic shocks, economic meltdown, and frequent conflicts resulting in increased displacement.
In Twic East, Duk, Bor South and in some areas of Yirol East and Awerial, floodwaters have not retroceded since 2020, with populations remaining displaced - unable to access food, safe drinking water, livelihood activities, protection information and assistance and are too far from full recovery and rebuilding of their lives. This has resulted to increased reliance on humanitarian assistance.
Similarly, recurrent subnational violence and localized violence among community-based militias or civil defense groups in Tambura and Jonglei have displaced civilians, exposing communities, particularly children, women, persons with specific needs, people with disabilities, elderly and other vulnerable community groups to multiple protection risks and vulnerabilities, including grave violations such as killing, injury, family separation, GBV/CRSV, sexual exploitation and abuse, targeting of civilians, revenge attacks and killings, cattle rustlings, burning of house and property, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abductions, constraints on safe and equitable access to services, housing, land and property, and other protection risks associated with negative coping mechanisms. COVID-19 has exacerbated the scale and urgency of these needs. Persons with disabilities are particularly affected in such emergencies and they face physical and psychosocial threats, including, risks of drowning, behind left behind during flights, SEA and other extreme shocks. They tend to lack food, shelter/NFIs, medicines, and assistive devices.
CINA will therefore reach 16,500 persons of concern (5,800 men, 7,500 women, 2,560 girls, 2,240 boys) including 2,400 persons with disabilities with timely lifesaving protection services including, Protection assessments and monitoring; Psychosocial support services and access to documentation; Protection awareness and prevention messaging; Peace building and peaceful coexistence; Strengthening protection monitoring systems within the community; and Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.

CINA will employ a number of approaches including static programming with field-based roving response in the proposed locations and use of cash assistance for comprehensive case management in line with protection principles. To ensure centrality of protection, Protection Monitoring System will be done in collaboration with Protection Cluster, to inform the Do No Harm and protection, conflict sensitive and child-centered approaches to other sectors’ programming and response and inclusion of protection aspects during data collection. Community engagements will be ensured through regular consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and streamlining of complain and feedback mechanisms, incorporated in the project design and implementation and coordinated with Communication and Community Engagement Working Group. AAP and protection mainstreaming will be upheld, including SEA prevention, survivor assistance, accountability and investigations, with technical support sought from PSEA Task Force.
CINA has a strong static presence and access to the proposed locations, currently implementing general protection, child protection, GBV, EORE, HLP besides education, FSL and health programs and operates field offices, with qualified personnel in Tambura, Bor, Twic East, Duk, Awerial and Yirol. CINA owns two new motor speedboats, currently stationed in Twic East and providing access to Twic East, Duk, Awerial and Bor, including their surrounding islands.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is unprecedented. Over 835,000 people have been displaced by floods. There is political instability, with growing uncertainty about political process to achieve peace, which is likely to cause more localized and subnational violence, resulting in more civilian displacement (HNO 2022). This is exacerbated by lack of local government capacity to ensure safety, security and access to basic services, exposing vulnerable populations to increased protection risks, mainly affecting women, girls, adolescents, elderly, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups, who continue to suffer violence, loss of lives, psychosocial distress, GBV, SEA and other protection threats related to negative coping mechanisms. Protection Assessments in hard-to-reach areas by REACH in August 2021 found out that Yirol East, Yirol West, Awerial and Duk are among top five counties in South Sudan where women and girls mentioned that their main protection concern is conflict which does not allow them access to their preferred market and housing, land and property rights. Revenge attacks and killings, road ambushes and cattle related violence continue to prevail in Yirol East and Yirol West, although there is relative but fragile stability witnessed in 2022 due to peace efforts by the new state government in Rumbek. IRNA reports from Duk and Twic East in December 2021 and June 2022 respectively indicated immense protection needs in the islands. About 58,069 people have been displaced to over 52 islands in Twic East. The whole county is submerged under water, with people’s resilience to rehabilitate the local dyke for three years in a row waning. In Duk islands of Koyom, Amech and Kawer, members of the 27,006 IDPs and host communities there reported that they sought safety in the islands due to violence, cattle raids and abductions which claimed lives of 43 people, 37 wounded and others who went missing during violent attacks in July 2020. Undoubtedly in 2023, subnational violence may continue. In Tambura 5 IDP sites, there are 18,533 IDPs and 541 persons with specific needs as of July 2022. 60% of IDPs are settled in Tambura, 20% in South Yubu and 20% in Mupoi. A deadly attack to Baidit Payam in Bor South in February 2022 claimed many lives, with populations currently displaced to islands and Bor town. On the 7th March 2022, Duk was attacked resulting in multiple deaths, casualties and loss of livelihoods. These violent atrocities are likely to overturn the recent peace gains between the communities in Jonglei and GPAA in absence of political intervention, peace dialogues and support to the affected people’s recovery. Continued displacement has weakened the protective environment and undermined people’s coping capacities, with over 60% of IDPs intending not to go back to their areas of origin (HNO 2022). When such conflicts and crises ensue, the most vulnerable people including children, women, adolescents, youth, the persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, etc. suffer greater risks and vulnerabilities. The persons with specific needs usually lack access to protection information, livelihood opportunities, food, medical care, legal aid and counselling, shelter and NFIs and psychosocial support. Tensions over HLP issues and other community resources compound existing forms of intercommunal violence, especially against women whose HLP rights are often violated. GBV against women and girls and PWDs become rampant. The child survivors of violence (including unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children) usually lack appropriate responsive services to survive and preventative and resilience services to cope. Needs of PWDs are often neglected while they suffer most. Urgent action is therefore required to deliver the desperately needed protection assistance in line with protection cluster objectives for HRP 2023 in the proposed priority locations.

2 - Project activities

1. Protection assessment and monitoring through Protection Monitoring System (PMS) will be conducted to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. This will be done through static programming in the state and field-based roving response in hard-to-reach areas of the same county. Protection monitoring systems will be strengthened within the community. Findings and recommendations will be shared with protection cluster for dissemination.2. Psychosocial support and access to documentation will be provided to individuals, groups, specialized PSS and support to caregivers of persons with specific needs.3. Protection awareness and prevention messaging. This will be conducted with aim to prevent and mitigate protection risks through an integrated approach of harmonized key messaging informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis.4. Peace building and peaceful coexistence. This will be achieved through conducting peace building awareness raising activities and training of community based protection networks (CBPN) and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) committees on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. Particularly, youth will be involved as guardians for peace building in the community. Youth volunteers will be trained to provide community awareness and reporting protection incidents, including those affecting persons with disabilities and work as caretakers and champions for vulnerable groups of households with disability to voice and safeguard their needs. 5. Prevention and mitigation of protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Based on existing CINA's cash programming experience (as a member of Cash Working Group), the use of standard vulnerability criteria and protection risk analysis, the persons with specific needs (PSNs) will receive protective cash-based assistance in connection with safe, dignified, and voluntary return. This will be provided as part of individual protection assistance (IPA), case management and safe referrals for PSNs.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash based programming for this project will be informed by a well coordinated improved market analysis, which will examine market functionality predictors such as an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased quality products in the market, resilience of households, gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and gender perception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction, for it to feasibly implement cash based assistance.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

From CINA's past experience in cash programming in protection, cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent protection needs offering dignity, flexibility and choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. Assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basic needs or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach and greatly contributes to centrality of protection.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

About 15% of people targeted by this project will be persons with disabilities. Other direct beneficiaries will be women, girls, including those who are not literate. They will be involved in project designs, implementation and monitoring and their feedback will be used to provide assistance. For example, the vulnerable PSNs will decide which type of assistance eg. assistive devices should be provided for them?, protective cash or unconditional cash?, how to receive information and assistance?

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CINA and project team will ensure transparency and information sharing with different groups throughout the project cycle. During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will beinformed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. Community-based protection networks will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. Activities such as awareness raising, caregivers sessions, and PSS activities will be used to pass important information about the activities of the project. CINA will strengthen its existing community beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms through display and correct use of complaints box at CFS, schools, Helpdesks, and during awareness sessions. CINA will also use feedback forms during M&E activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA has an existing PSEA policy and a PSEA focal person. CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer.A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible. CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve PSEA objective. CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify andreport it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at Communitycenters and beneficiaries well be sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head Office. A hotline number and email address will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CINA will ensure that women, men, children, adolescents, youth, persons with disabilities are involved and meaningfully participate in and influence decisions from the needs assessment, project designs, assistance delivery, through to project evaluation. To this end, their feedback during any stage of the project which will be used to guide planning and response.
