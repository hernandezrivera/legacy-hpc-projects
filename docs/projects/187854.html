Project Summary

The project focuses on improving access to GBV services in the WGSSs will be established in Idleb (Darkoush) ,in Aleppo (Afrin) , Aleppo ( Sheikh El-Hadid) and in Idleb (Sarmin). The community centers aim to provide a space where men, women, boys, and girls can socialize and strengthen previously fractured social networks to ensure that a greater number of people have access to the information related to GBV, CP and GP services in their communities. The planned number of centers is (4) where women and girls can socialize and gain access to lifesaving GBV services, including case management, PSS for survivors focusing on vulnerable groups such as divorced, widows, females with disabilities, as well as targeting male survivors of sexual violence. Also, they will gain access to other GBV activities such Life Skills, Free spaces for women and girls, vocational Training, Awareness raising by using (GBV-SC toolkit), training of Humanitarian actors (non-specialist) on GBV risk mitigation (Sadad will take advantage of the inter-agency capacity development initiatives) In addition to providing direct support service and referrals to other services according to the referral paths that are shared by the sub-clusters and creating a service mapping through coordination with actors in the area, continuously updated.
Sadad will also provide Community-based protection services to ensure that IDPs and host community are effectively involved in the project life cycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns (Sadad is member in PSEA Network). ased on the HNO 2022, 6,044,884 children need CP services so will enhance equitable access to quality Child Protection services including parental skills, MHPSS services, PSS, capacity building, awareness raising, Case management & Referrals for children survivors of violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, child labor, early marriage, children exposed to severe/extreme psychosocial distress ((10455beneciaries including (5105Males (650 Elderly, 3700 Adults, 955 Children, 600Young Children, and 550 PwD). Legal services to increase legal awareness among affected population on how to access CSD and HLP documentation including provision of individual legal assistance. Protection monitoring service through conducting service mapping and establishing referral pathways and reporting on protection concerns and issues.The proposed intervention will be implemented with particular emphasis on quality services to People with Disabilities (PwDs), PwDs will be targeted through center and community AR program, specialized services, and supported by transportation from and to the WGSSs which will be rehabilitated to be inclusive for PwDs.inaddtion to elderly women and men.
Sadad will consult with the community members at the beginning of the project to introduce the project activities and have them involved in the selection of the community center's locations, as well as Sadad will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics such as; referral and communication skills to enable them identify and refer people in need to the case management team.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social species of targeted populations.
the project will consider the environmental situation from the perspective of children/youth with disabilities and the individual needs for all of them ,The communities centers will be provided with disabilities facilitation;“e.g. ramps, toilets.
Sadad will use the M&E tool kit to monitor the GBV activities; Sadad will monitor the quality of the project also, through KAP surveys and semi-annual FGDs.

4 - Needs

Protection risks and vulnerabilities identified in 2021 are anticipated to continue throughout 2022. These include, but are not limited to, psychosocial trauma and distress, particularly among children and other vulnerable groups; increased rates of GBV; increased reliance on negative coping mechanisms; child protection concerns, specifically violence against children; and exclusion and discrimination, particularly with regard to persons with disabilities and older persons. According to MSNA Findings- HNO-2022, 62% of HHs mentioned that women show signs of distress in the last 3 months, compared to 31% of HHs mentioned the same for girls, 71% of communities/locations (compared to 62% last year) identify child marriage as an issue for girls aged 12 to 17. financial hardships were a key reason behind cases of early marriage in 72% of communities who took part in data collection. 17% of communities/locations mentioned that PSEA is an issue for women in the last three months and 14% of them mentioned the same for girls.As for MSNA Key Informant Protection Needs Assessment level for HNO 2021, in Aleppo 45.1% of the KIS reported Lack/Loss of civil documentation while 73.4% in Idleb reported Lack/Loss of civil documentation.According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Violate PSEA or CSG, CoC principles. Mitigation: - Signing PSEA and child safeguarding Sadad’s policy by all staff. - getting training on PSEA for all staff. - PSEA awareness through brochures, videos. - Assigning complaint mechanism for SEA issues. policies of Code of conduct, PSEA, child safeguarding is mandatory for all staff and vendors working with Sadad. Risk: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral Risk: Persons with disability have di cult accessing protection services Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Ensure all CCs staff are properly trained on how to identify persons with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services, Provide transportation support for persons with disability. Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of CCs and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to services.Sadad will seek to ensure addresse all risks.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sadad has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Sadad is active member in PSEA Network and will collaborate with the PSEA network to support quality implementation in project. Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers. Mitigation: Sadad ensures to have in place accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Sadad safeguarding policy. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk: Lack of understanding of employees about PSEA issues. Sadad understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, con dent and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about Sadad position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children.

It includes meetings with religious and community leaders. Risk: Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment. Likelihood: Medium Impact. Assumption: Affected populations including PwD will contribute to the successful implementation of the project activities throughout the project lifecycle.
