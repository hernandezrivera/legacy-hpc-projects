Project Summary

Poor WASH situation in Lakes state has predisposed vulnerable and affected populations in Rumbek centre and Rumbek East Counties of Lakes state to WASH emergencies, this has led to increased needs for WASH services since the few available WASH facilities have been over-stretched by the IDPs, host communities and the reported dozens of returnees. The project targets a total of 11,111 beneficiaries desegregated into 4,559 males and 6,552 females. The planned response aims at improved access to WASH services by rehabilitation of boreholes in communities, schools, health facilities and at the women and girls friendly spaces. WASH messaging and campaigns, establishment of gender segregated sanitation facilities and hand washing facilities and distribution of WASH NFIs and joint monitoring & evaluation. This will improve the quality of life among the beneficiaries in the two targeted Counties by reducing the risk of WASH related disease outbreak and subsequently GBV risks associated with long distances covered in search of WASH services.
The project will increase WASH services at locations where community members remain vulnerable to WASH related diseases outbreak as identified during joint assessment conducted through an interagency process. This will be done through rehabilitation of 40 communal water points, rehabilitation and upgrading some of the community and institutional boreholes into solar powered water yard, rehabilitation and construction of communal and institutional latrines and bathing facilities, installation of 12 handwashing facilities in public places/markets,6 handwashing facilities at the women and girls friendly spaces ,6 primary schools and 5 health units. Training of 20 community Hygiene Promoters in each county, form and train one school hygiene club in the 6 targetted schools, train 4 health workers in each of the target health facilities on infection control and cholera risk reduction, training of Water User Committees with appropriate gender representation in all the 40 rehabilitated boreholes to support efficient use and maintenance of WASH facilities and to ensure sustainability. A total of 4244 HHs have been affected in both Counties of Rumbek East and Rumbek Central.
According to August, 2023 IRNA, Some people have been displaced to high grounds in Gok Akon and Amongpiny Central in Rumbek centre and Pacong in Rumbek East . The consistent need of the population includes NFIs/ES, food, water and hygiene, and Health services.
In Rumbek Centre and Rumbek East counties, WAV will improve access to safe water through rehabilitation of 20 and 15 broken hand pumps respectively and re-construction of one (1) community protected wells. During the project cycle, WAV field team will coordinate with key WASH, health and protection partners including local government authorities on the ground to help carry out joint regular monitoring sessions. WAV will also prioritize weekly and monthly reporting and information sharing.
The WASH services provision will also integrate mitigation of the GBV risks to women and girls who are frequently deprived of WASH support due to access constraints and insecurity. Lack of water compounds risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when walking to remote water sources an points. WAV will ensure water points and sanitation facilities are accessible within acceptable distance to avoid long walks to reach water points. MHM and dignity kits will be provided to women and girls of reproductive age to help uphold dignity and curb barriers to social interaction an access to WASH service provision..

1. Needs

Few years ago, after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people including refugees which has also been escalated by the conflict in Sudan resulting into high numbers of influx of returnees and refugees in South Sudan . Hence, South Sudan remained a protection crisis since 2020 and lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. A second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of conflict in Sudan all are compounding factors, hence worsening the needs of vulnerable persons. The Inter-Cluster Working Group (ICWG) in Rumbek conducted an Initial Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) in Rumbek East and of Rumbek Central County-from 14th -17th May 2023 to assess the humanitarian situation of the flood affected populations. The team consisted of UN Agencies, INGO and NNGO, RRC, Government officials, with participants from various humanitarian organization, representing all clusters (Education, FSL, Health, NFI&ES, Nutrition, Protection, and WASH). The Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP, edited in 2022) reported the following WASH coverage for South Sudan: 50% basic service for water, 30% with limited service, and 13% unimproved service and 7% surface water. For sanitation, 61% of the population are practicing open defection, 20% use unimproved latrine, 9% have limited service and 10% use basic service. Rumbek Central and 153,085 South Sudanese women, men and children are estimated to not having access to adequate WASH services, HNO 2020. Only 29% of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns with a significant number of people in need increased to about 64% from 2023. The September UNOCHA led IRNA to Rumbek Centre county which WAV participated; shows about 80% of community boreholes are non-functional. Many primary health facilities are massively destroyed while the few existing facilities are overstretched by populations seeking health services. The increase in GBV cases in the 2 locations was likely due to declining food security and access to WASH services as majority of women were reported to be collecting firewood and water in bushes far away from their homes, lack of water compounds risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when walking to remote water sources (Humanitarian Needs Overview Report, 2023). In February 2023, 38% of assessed settlements in Rumbek centre and Rumbek East counties reported incidences of women harassment had a large impact on their access to WASH services. WAV WASH needs assessment conducted in the month of October in Rumbek Centre and Rumbek East counties; 80% of assessed settlements reported no latrines usage, boreholes was reported to be the primary source of drinking water and are therefore severely affected by the increased numbers of broken and non-functional boreholes in the County. Accesses to WASH NFIs are gravely affected due to collapse of markets. The, 2023 IRNA had the following key findings in Rumbek East; Most people in these affected areas are defecating in water as a result of their pit latrines collapsed, the water source especially the boreholes are submerged and contaminated therefore, the people have resorted to drinking the surface water without treatment with PUR, some households do not have containers for collecting or storing water.

2. Project activities

WAV will carry out the following activities in the four Counties; Act 2.1: Rehabilitation of 40 non-functional boreholes; 20 non-functional boreholes in Rumbek East, and 2 Mini Water Yards), 20 non-functional boreholes in Rumbek Central targeting health facilities, schools, public places/markets and community hand pumps. Act 2.2: rehabilitation of the damaged community protected wells in age in Rumbek Centre county, the works will include; reconstruction of apron, repair of the drainage, fixing the outlets, cleaning and backfilling the surrounding with murram and fencing. Act 2.3: Training of Water User Management Committees; all the 40 rehabilitated boreholes and one (1) community protected well will have trained water user committees charged with the responsibility of operation and maintenance to ensure sustainability. Act. 2.4: Carry out flushing, disinfection and chlorination at identified contaminated water points after a joint assessment with local authorities. Act 2.5: Establishment and installation of handwashing facilities in 8 primary schools, 5 health facilities and 12 in public places including markets and will then be handed over to WASH committees for O & M, refilling and soap usage monitoring. Act 3.1: Provide 20 blocks genders segregated community latrines in the two Counties; support community efforts in using local materials to construct simple and cost effective communal latrines, equip locals with basic skills on construction of household latrines. Provide with latrine digging kits, tarpaulin and latrine slabs. Act 3.2: Rehabilitation of 4 blocks of institutional latrines segregated by gender including design improvements to ensure safety, security, privacy and dignity of users especially women and girls and provision of a separate room for MHM in 2 primary schools and 2 health facilities. Act 3.3: Provide 2 blocks of four stances of latrines in target markets/public places in Rumbek East County. The exiting one block which is dilapidated will also be targeted for repair and desludging. Act 4.1: Conducting hygiene promotion training for 20 CHPs (10 female and 10 male) in each of the four Counties. The CHPs will be trained on participatory hygiene and sanitation promotion sessions, water quality monitoring and bucket chlorination. Act 4.2: Training of 4 infection control teams in each of the 15 targeted primary health Care Units in Rumbek East/centre to help in contact tracing, case identification and referrals of suspected cholera and AWD cases. Act 4.3: Formation and training of 11 pupils and two teachers in all the 26 target primary schools in the four Counties, the teachers will serve as the patrons of the school health clubs. Act 4.4: Conduct hygiene promotion sessions on prevention of cholera, COVID 19 and other WASH related diseases among the target populations in the target Counties targeting health facilities, primary schools, markets and other public places and at household levels. 4.5: Establishment of referral pathways in Rumbek East host communities, schools and health facilities; referral for suspected cholera cases and AWD to treatment/health Centrals by school and community based cholera response teams including CHPs. Act 4.6: distribution of WASH NFIs to vulnerable populations in the target locations; specific WASH NFIs including soap, buckets with taps, water treatment tablets, tarpaulin, filter clothes and hygiene kits will be distributed. Act 4.7: Focused Group Discussion with different beneficiary groups at each project site to incorporate view on changing needs and on design improvement of WASH facilities earmarked for construction/rehabilitation. Act 4.8: Joint Monitoring of activities with local authorities and partners on the ground; and reporting (daily, weekly and monthly). Act 4.9: carrying out Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM); to assess the satisfaction levels and receive feedback from households receiving WASH NFIs. The PDM report will then be shared with the cluster.
