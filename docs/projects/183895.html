Project Summary

The integrated WASH and FSL project will targeted refugees in Khartoum,White Nile and Gadaref states, through provision of adequate access to WASH services including rehabilitation on exist hand pumps, construction of shared household latrines, Construction of shower blocks, distribution of Soap and jerrycans for a number of 6000 house holds, in addition to organizing and conducting of vector control activities. A total of 2500 women and girls also will be targeted through Provision of Personal Hygiene Material. The project also will Operate and maintain 5 water sources in Basondha, Jabel Awlia and Aljableen localities as well as construction of shower blocks. On the other hand the project will also provide livelihood opportunities

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa. South Sudanese make up the majority. Many others fled violence and persecution in neighboring countries, including Eritrea, the Central African Republic, Ethiopia, and Chad, but also the wars in Syria and Yemen pushed people to seek safety in Sudan. Most refugees live in out-of-camp settlements, host communities and urban areas, while others stay in 24 camps (10 at the East Sudan, 1 at Blue Nile State 10 at White Nile State, 2 at East Darfur State and 1 at the Central Darfur State). Sudan continues to generously host and receive additional asylum-seekers.As of 30th September 2021 a total refugees and asylum-seekers in Sudan, 1,111,329 individuals, from which (73%) are women and children. This integrated project will target a number of 3174 household (15,870 individuals in Khartoum,White Nile and Gadaref state. . Refugees and asylum seekers often lack adequate support to satisfy their basic needs such as education,WASH, health and livelihoods opportunities .Normally people who move from home countries to another country of asylum are characterized by a number of traumatic experiences, such as abandon of places of origin and correlated networks, risk of becoming a victim of violence, abuse and lack of adequate services. The response to their needs, including medical, psychological, social, economic and legal has been identified as a top priority intervention. Refugee youth suffer from lack of educational opportunities or vocational skills training, making it difficult for them to find a livelihood when they become adults so project will enhance the capacity of Youth through vocational training and capital materials

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In recruitment process HOPE will continue to recruited project staff from targeted communities, the staff involved and fully dedicated to implement and follow up on .Project staff will be oriented on Code of conduct to avoid potential risks of violence, exploitation and abused of the beneficiaries of the project. The integrated project will adopt community based approach that promotes the active engagement and participation of all community segments in design and implementation phases to ensure the community by-in and sense of ownership. HOPE team confirmed that the Community management committee and water committees compositions considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve the decision making and allow the voices for these groups. Following the participatory and community based approaches, targeted communities will be involved in discussion, consultations and decision regarding planned activities. Through this, the project intends to ensure full participation and strengthen the ownership of communities. This will contribute to provide the target communities a concrete plate forum to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and needed actions to improve the protective environment. APP is core activity, Additionally applying Do No Harm principle to clearly done in this project through involvement and inclusion of the communities and hosting communities with equal access to direct assistance based on vulnerability criteria.HOPE will consider the dignity and confidentiality of beneficiaries by using and follow Safety, Access Desegregated Date (SADD), in monitoring and data collection and will strongly prioritize the best interest of the beneficiary by following protocols and standards. As part of the Do No Harm approach, HOPE organization will provide training to all project team members on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and code of conduct. This will help to mitigate the risk of harassment, abuse and other violation to be committed by staff members against the beneficiaries. In addition, the project team will conduct regular consultation with community leaders/ members and local authorities and receive their feedback about the project delivered service through complaint mechanism. This will help to identify any negative consequences that might be caused by the intervention as well as the necessary corrective/mitigation action

The project considered AAP through the community participation in designing, developing and implementing of all project activities. This was partially done through consultation meetings and focus group discussion. The project applied participatory approach by involving beneficiaries in the development of the implementation plan. Community management committees and WASH committee’s members were provided by a full briefing of project commitments, targets and accountability frame work, also they will be fully equipped with complaint handling, reporting and information sharing tools. HOPE will organize and provide training to all project team members, stakeholders and key community actors on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and code of conduct.
