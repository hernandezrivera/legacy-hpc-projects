Project Summary

With the overall objective of reducing the vulnerability of displaced populations in north-east Nigeria against Covid-19 and contribute to the containment of a possible outbreak, IOM CCCM aims to support the overall COVID-19 preparedness and response efforts through facilitating the immediate, adequate and equitable delivery of critical services to the displaced population in camp and camp-like settings.
CCCM activities will continue to provide site facilitation support and site level coordination in formal and informal camps in the following Borno locations: MMC, Bama (including Banki), Dikwa, Gwoza (including Pulka,) Ngala, Monguno, Mafa, Jere, Kala Balge (Rann), and Damboa; and in the following Adamawa locations: Yola North, Yola South, Girie and Song.
In collaboration with the World Health Organization (WHO) and the State Ministry of Health for Borno, DTM will be collecting point of entry (POE) data for all person moving through the border locations in Ngala, Monguno, Damasak, Kala Balge, Bama and Pulka. DTM will produce monthly dashboards to show all the movements recorded and all the data will also be shared with the health partners. DTM will conduct mapping of partners including emergency contacts to be shared with all persons using the POEs. Furthermore, DTM with its wide network in the North East, North West and North Central will produce periodic maps to show restrictions imposed on all states in the selected regions.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation is expected to worsen due to COVID 19, particularly due ongoing stressors experienced by the health system including disrupted health system in many inaccessible LGAs, non-functional health facilities, lack of health care staff, overwhelmed response capacity of international humanitarian agencies and lack of resources. A tightening of pre-existing movement restrictions, economic deterioration coupled with strained public services will further increase humanitarian needs and compound existing operational challenges. As per the HNO, 7.9 million people are expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 of whom the humanitarian partners will target 5.9 million of the most vulnerable people. The unforeseen shock, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, is likely to impact current projections. The capacity of the health system to address the possible impact of the pandemic, remains limited. Over 2.03 million people remain internally displaced (DTM Round 30, Dec. 2019). An estimated 40% of the displaced population reside in the nearly 298 displacement sites. The spontaneous nature of sites coupled with existing gaps in humanitarian assistance make individuals in displacement sites extremely vulnerable to a multitude of risks including, but not limited to, flooding, wind, disease outbreak etc. Due to seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities as a result of continuous displacement due to military operations and low access to essential healthcare, north-east Nigeria remains highly endemic for to diarrheal and other diseases (malaria, measles, Lasa fever, Meningitis, Hep E). New influxes of IDPs, refugees-returnees into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements exacerbate the risk of disease outbreaks. The impact of the displacement for communities and persons displaced is huge and has remained key to increase in disease situation with consequences. Borno is currently experiencing an outbreak of measles with over 21,052 cases as at the end of December 2019. This is due to non-immunization amongst displaced children from locations that are security-compromised, which further puts at high risk children under 5 years already facing dangerously high levels of malnutrition, endemic malaria and other epidemic prone diseases. Cholera outbreak in Adamawa and Lassa fever in Borno has just stopped as no more cases reported during the last four weeks. The deterioration of the security situation in the past months has exacerbated the situation especially for displaced women, children and the elderly being the most vulnerable.

2 - Activities

CCCM teams will continue to provide and enhance its site facilitation capacities in formal and informal camp and camp-like settings to ensure that IDPs have access to critical services such as food, water, firewood and health. This will be achieved through -: Site Facilitation and Coordination through monitoring of critical needs and gaps and referral to service providers; CFM to strengthen community-based engagement (through text messaging hotlines, feedback boxes and audio recorders) and inform the response planning; Mapping of the most vulnerable persons; Site improvements to prepare for the rainy seaso n and mitigate risks on potential breeding of new diseases including COVID-19 in camps; Decongestion of camps, where possible, to help stop the spread of the virus; Camp sanitation activities through daily disinfecting and cleaning led by community volunteers and community groups; Promoting the use of hand washing stations ensuring they are well distributed and functional in all camp entry points and key area s . Specific activities to support the risk mitigation activities and referral systems of the Health and WASH partners will be implemented in all camps and camp-like settings. CCCM partners will work closely with the Health actors on site to ensure that camp residents are aware of protocols on reporting of cases, have access to the nearest health facilities and/or isolation centers in the most congested camps. As guided by the Health sector, the COVID-19 related information will be widely disseminated by CCCM partners in support to the Health partners on its response/case mgmt.. and adhere to its protocols. The following activities will specifically support the Health and WASH partners: RCCE through information dissemination of IEC materials, sharing of key Health and WASH messages among the communities, awareness raising on preventive measures of COVID-19; Sharing off site-specific referral system with the communities to inform on available health services specific to COVID-19 such as isolation centers and primary health care facilities; and management of the Self-Quarantine Shelters in high-risk and congested areas where persons with travel and contact history from the camps and nearby host communities will have to be quarantined for 14 days as per NCDC and Health Sector guidelines. IOM will also support populations displaced out-of-camps through the establishment of information centers to provide a central information sharing unit that will facilitate the awareness of IDPs in a particular location on the available services specific to COVID-19 such as isolation centers and medical assistance. In collaboration with the WHO and the State Ministry of Health for Borno, DTM will be collecting POE - data and sites with population of interest on persons moving through the border locations in Ngala, Monguno, Damasak, Kala Balge, Bama and Pulka. The data collected will be on affected population, status of POEs, medical measures in place, settlement type and transportation mode as well as basic health questions and contacts that will allow health partners to trace individuals , should they be deemed at high risk or have come into contact with persons who have tested positive for COVID 19. Emergency contacts will also be distributed to all persons screened at the POEs. COVID-19 related awareness raising materials will be distributed. DTM will produce monthly dashboards to show all the movements recorded and all the data will also be shared with the health partners . DTM will conduct mapping of partners to highlight response gaps, including emergency contacts to be shared with all persons at the POEs. DTM will also produce periodic maps to show restrictions imposed on all states in the selected regions. DTM will also continue COVID-19 related data collection activities on IDPs, including: analysis of existing data, collecting additional indicators on Covid in camps and tracking of specific events.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. IOM has already established CRM in many sites across Borno which has been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors. IOM has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. IOM also actively uses practical recommendations from beneficiary feedback surveys as advocacy tools at the sector level to influence change.
