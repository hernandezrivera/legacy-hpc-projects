Project Summary

Taking into consideration existing need of IDPs and host communities population affected by conflicts, the project aims at benefitting displaced and host community girls and women living in the districts of Pemba, Ibo, Quissanga, Metuge, Montepuez Balama and Namuno, thought provision of quality life-saving, prevention actions and protection mainstreaming, actions in General Protection, Child Protection and GBV

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project is aimed to implement and improve the case management and referral system to identify and manage cases of vulnerability among children and women in Pemba, Ibo, Quissanga, Metuge, Montepuez Balama and Namuno districts. The main objective is to strengthen the case identi􀂦cation system and the coordination between the response services, involving competent local authorities and creating a close link with the case workers. They will be also able to provide protection and psychosocial support services for children, adolescent, women, caregivers and communities. AVSI will rely with its long experience on urbanplanning on support the IDPs on the HLP assistance based on indication of the local authority. AVSI is already working closely with DPGCAS and SDMAS, using the same identi􀂦cation and reference forms, involving staff in training and holding monthly meetings to coordinate the referral system. This approach is aimed at sharing the work with the local authorities, in order to support and allow them to carry on after the end of the project. At the end of the project the trained activists shall continue supporting their communities and might be supported by the district departments of Acçao Social and Education. The Action we look to target 45.000 beneficiaries: 20.000 of GP, 18.000 of CP,7.000 of GBV.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• Setting up Animators Groups • Community Awareness and caregiver’s engagement: Parents and community will be made aware about the importance of their commitment and engagement in their children’s wellbeing and how they can contribute, facilitate and improve it • Identification and training of a network of community based volunteers (CBV). In order to encourage the sustainability of the initiative and to foster the appropriation of the essential mechanisms of protection by the target communities, people for each neighbourhood will be selected during the awareness-raising sessions held in the communities by AVSI staff in order to become community based volunteers, playing a role of “antenna” in the community. • PSS training for communities volunteers, Animators and Case Workers: In order to encourage the sustainability of the initiative and to foster the appropriation of the essential mechanisms of protection by the target communities, communities volunteers and caregivers will participate in a specific training aimed at developing their capacity of identification of children and community members in need of psychosocial support and to give them facilitation skills to handle awareness sessions on stress management, positive parenting, positive discipline, child rights and protection and social inclusion in their communities • Recruitment of case workers and training of case workers: Case workers will follow up on cases referred by the community based volunteers and the animators and will work hand in hand with them to increase theircapacity of identification of CP cases, while supervising the activities in the multipurpose spaces • Provide Technical and Strategic Support to CP Committees and Government Structures to Develop Child Protection Policies, or Conduct Assessment, or Build internal Capacity to Implement Community Based Programs • Women Friendly Spaces: AVSI will set up WFS in order to create recreational and PSS mechanism among the women that suffered by displacement • Complaints mechanism and GBV report will be set up to avoidand referer any possible abuse • HLP mechanism will be create to support the IDPs on its new settlement in order to the law • Legal assistance on certification will be deliver for those households that lost their legal documentation

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

AVSI will be engaged in creating a protected and safe environment, in particular in an emergency context, focusing on the improvement of child well-being and family support through case management and provide access to basic services. Animators/facilitators groups are aimed at creating a protected family environment: providing psychosocial support in emergency and case management are two key actions to be planned in all the interventions. Moreover, this project will be implemented jointly with the AVSI-UNICEF child protection programme. Case Management activities allow the identification and management of the most vulnerable cases. Through a mapping of the districts, AVSI is able to have a clear picture of households and their residences.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AVSI will provide information to women, girls, boys and men in affected communities about the organisation, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver so that communities and people affected by crisis are fully aware of the expected behaviour of humanitarian staff, including organisational commitments made on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. (CHS 4.1 and 5.6). AVSI’s staff will collect feedback in appropriate ways (e.g. groups are segregated by sex and age, confidential where necessary) and make sure the analysis of the feedback is disaggregated by at least age and sex at minimum so that there can be comparisons of the issues according to roles and norms. Complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritises the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages and refer complaints that do not fall within the scope of the organisation to a relevant party in a manner consistent with good practice (CHS 5.7). AVSI will, at all time, assess the implementation of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and other collective standards including the IASC AAP commitments, Gender Equality Programming, Minimum Operating Standards on PSEA; and the Best Practice Guide to establish Inter- Agency Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms (CBCM) and its accompanyingStandard Operating Procedures through self-assessments, peer review, third-party verification or certification

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Inclusive activities are mainstreamed in all AVSI projects. Animators and Case workers are trained on safe identification and referral and according to the needs, specific topics are tackled concerning physical or mental disability. In some cases, special educators are hired to provide continuous support and mentoring to teachers on the inclusion of children, from needs assessment to progress monitoring, as well ascoordinate the work of specialized service providers. Activities can be implemented in different context (non-formal, formal, or informal). In different projects we engaged also specialized services – speech therapy, psychology, psychomotor therapy, mobility assistant for the visually impaired children, etc both for the assessment of child’s needs and for the provision of support services. Referral pathways are always mapped and shared. The engagement of parents for these activities, in the parental meetings to discuss the child’s progress and problems is fundamental.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed activities will adopt an environment-friendly approach by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. With a strong community engagement and participation; on rehabilitation activities, specific attention regarding the site selection and sourcing of materials, favouring the use of local resources, like the locally sourced building materials, sun-cured soil bricks produced in affected communities and the limiting of the use of timber wherever feasible, for all rehabilitation and construction. Finally, health and hygiene education will have a component of key messages regarding the environment.
