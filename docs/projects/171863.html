Project Summary

Overall objective: to provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for the most vulnerable people affected by the humanitarian crisis in Luhansk region of Ukraine (GCA). SOS CV UA works in the Luhansk region, focusing on child protection, with complementary provision of non-food items to the vulnerable families. The main aim is to mitigate the impact of conﬂict on such families with children and ensure that children are protected in family environment.
We plan to apply for financing with the project that will have three components: 1) Comprehensive case management in the frame of SOS stationary center in Stanytsia Luhanska (own contribution) 2) establishment of the shelter for temporary placement of women and girls victims of domestic violence and single mothers with children in difficult life circumstances 3) capacity building of local social workers and state authorities on interdepartmental response and interaction on child protection issues and provision of social services (contribution of donor will be asked)

1 - Needs

The project is going to deal with acute needs of the most vulnerable groups of population. Single-headed households with children are among such groups prioritized by humanitarian community in Ukraine. UNICEF need assessment of poverty shows that COVID-crisis aggravated an already bad situation for families in difficult life circumstances, which cannot diversify their income, and traditionally have the highest levels of poverty . According to IOM Ukraine Survey , 87% respondents noted an increase in food prices, some 40% reported using savings to purchase food or reducing other essential expenses to afford food and over half reported switching to cheaper foods. A negative impact was reported by 81% of SME, with 47% reporting a full and 34% partial shutdown, 43% had to dismiss some staff or offer them leave without pay. Most people reported significant stress, fear and anxiety in connection to quarantine measures and the anticipated economic downturn. Loss of jobs and livelihoods, reduced purchasing power, amid rising prices and increased utilities tariff, together with increased reports of GBV cases by at least 50 per cent, were inevitable consequences of quarantine restrictions. Access to regular healthcare was deprioritized or disrupted by COVID-19. In spite reviving business activity since July, the economy is expected to contract by 6% in 2020, inflation will have accelerated to 4.7% by the end of this year. Absolute poverty is expected to increase from 27 per cent to 44 per cent, with the impact on children even more profound – expected growth of absolute poverty is from 33 to 51 percent. This estimation is expected to be worse for the conflict-affected areas of the Eastern Ukraine, as the ‘contact line’ is the most affected space, where the hostilities are active and mobility of population and aid restricted. The consequence of this could be an increase in number of families, which abandon their children or send them to boarding schools. Provisional repeated closure of the checkpoints for crossing the contact line due to COVID-19 will mean that many households will stay without no access to some services in NGCA. Furthermore, ongoing hostilities and economic hardships led to increased family - and gender-based violence and neglect of children, which also was reflected in sharp increase in appeals from children and adolescents to hotlines, with each third appeal associated with child violence and abuse . For now there is only one shelter for victims of violence in Lugansk region, while necessity of its establishing is emphasized in latest Emergency Response Plan (page 13). During our emergency response and activities, we repeatedly receive requests for temporary accommodation from the beneficiary women with children, who suffer from domestic abuse and unable to pay for rent. While protection of children's rights, responding to gender-based violence and family violence are parts of the national policies and strategies, currently limited services exist for child witnesses or survivors of violence, including in the conflict-affected areas. Besides, existing community services are often understaffed and cannot effectively respond to the problems of children and their families.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

For the next year, SOS CV UA plans: Activity 1. Case management-based support on the basis of community centre in Stanytsia Luhanska for at least 170 children (85 girls and 85 boys), 110 adults (80 women and 30 men). The main categories of families are disadvantaged families: headed by single mothers, foster and kinship families, families with members with disability, extended families, low income, conflict affected families etc. - they have high risk of getting a child into a boarding school. To prevent it, SOS UA has been providing long term social support: services of a social educator, speech therapist, psychologist, protection information (including on prevention of GBV), NFIs in accordance with the assistance plan developed by our social worker individually for each family based on the needs assessment. Activity 2. Emergency relief for 26 beneficiaries - single women, young women, incl. pregnant women in difficult life circumstances and victims of domestic violence and their children - by establishment of the shelter for temporary placement for no more than 6 month. This component is planned to be implemented in partnership with Luhansk State Civil–Military Administration. It will be a contribution to large complex project “Center for providing a range of family-oriented services”, established by 3 village councils to support children and families in need at the local level. Shelter is planned in building, which is in the state property, and after one year of our support the activities will be continued by the state. The shelter will serve as urgent dwelling for at least 26 women, young women and their children (50% boys and 50% girls), including those with disabilities, during a year. They will be supported by a social worker, who will provide comprehensive support, managing family as a single case, attracting all available resources of community for medical, legislative, educational and other forms of assistance. They will also benefit from psychological support of our professionals, small humanitarian assistance upon need, support for achievement of self-reliance. Activity 3. Capacity building of about 30 local social workers on interdepartmental response and interaction in child protection issues and provision of social services The Accountability to Affected Population in the project will be ensured by: 1) feedback and complaints system serving to adjust and change operational plans during the programme cycle so affected populations can comment on the quality, appropriateness and/or adequacy of programme interventions, and report violations of the law, SOS policies and Code of conduct 2) Signing the acquaintance and strict following by all the project staff such key documents as the SOS Code of Conduct and SOS Care Promise, where accountability obligation is included 3) proper informing the beneﬁciaries on project criteria and priorities, informing whenever changes in the project are made.
