Project Summary

The project intends to improve the nutritional status for 9,389 under five children (8,435 girls and 972 boys) SAM children in White Nile (773 children in Guli, 1,071 children in Tandaliti and 4,406 children in Eddiwuim) and Kassala state (2,043 in River Atbara and 1,096 Haemshkorieb localities). The project will also target 132 pregnant & lactating women (PLW) in Atbra River, Kassala, particularly those with children aged 0-6 months. It will support the provision of nutrition-specific services through existing structures, in conjunction with maternal and child health services. The project team will also ensure integration of this intervention with other similar interventions. The project will deliver these activities through community services adopting the sector objectives. The project takes a people-centred approach with the most vulnerable affected people at its’ core. The vulnerable groups will be specifically targeted through this humanitarian response focused on access to nutrition services with due regard for protection, disability, equitable access, gender equality and environmental sensitivity. The project will be implemented over a period of one year.

1 - Needs

Poor nutrition is a major threat to the survival and development of children under-five in Sudan. Nowadays Sudan have more malnourished girls and boys U5 than 30 years ago, due to an increase in prevalence in the past 2 decades from 20 to 32 per cent underweight and from 32 to 35 per cent underweight combined with population growth. In Sudan, approximately 2.7 million children under five suffer from malnutrition annually, out of whom 522,000 suffer from severe acute malnutrition. Over one in three children U5 (2.3 million) are too short for their age (stunted) with rates above 30 per cent in 128 out of 188 localities, making Sudan one of the 14 countries where 80 per cent of the world’s stunted children live. One in six U5 girls and boys are too thin for their height (wasted), while nearly 700,000 children per year suffer from severe acute malnutrition in Sudan - about twice the number of severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children in need in Sudan compared to Yemen. Kassala State in Eastern Sudan is one of the regions where a large share of the population are at risk. According to an assesment by the World Food Programme 22 percent of households in Kassala suffer from chronic food insecurity, while 26 percent are chronically moderately food insecure (WFP, 2012). On average four per cent of under‐five years old is severely acute malnourished. Chronic malnutrition rates are amongst the highest in the country too. Around 60 per cent of children are affected by all forms of chronic malnutrition with over 30 per cent of the children severely malnourished. Maternal mortality is amongst the highest levels as well with 245 maternal deaths per 100,000 births. Further, child marriage rates in Kassala are very high as well. It is estimated that 17 per cent of the children under 15 are married. According to the most recent S3M survey report the GAM among pregnant women in Kassala state was 14.65% with 5.12% SAM and 9.47% MAM. The severity ranking for malnutrition among PLW is high in Nahr Atbara (Atbara River) with PLW GAM at 25.01 which is severity ranking of very high at 4. While GAM among children under 5 years old is 10.21% with 8.6% MAM and 1.95% SAM. However, the GAM among pregnant women is above emergency threshold (>15%) in the localities of Refi River Atbara (20.67%), Rural Western Kassala (17.1%), Rural Aroma (15.37%). The highest prevalence of SAM among pregnant women was reported from Refi Atbara River locality (8.54%), followed by rural Kassala (4.72%). The GAM among under 5 years old in Hameshkorieb locality, Rural Telkok (18.42%) is beyond the emergency threshold and it is the highest. It is 14.46% in Refi Aroma, 11.52% in Refi Kassala, 10.4% in Wadelhilew and 10.13% River Atbara. The GAM is 7.59% in North Delta among under 5 years old. While in White Nile the nutrition status of children is similarly worse. According to inter-agency assessments been carryout during heavy rains and floods at August, 2020 for the White Nile localities it has been identified that there is insufficient capacity of health care workers on IYCF and health education sessions. The nutrition service is almost abescent in most of health facilities noting that MUAC screening is not conducted recently. Based on S3M results, White Nile State is characterised by high level of malnutrition. The stunting prevalence is high rate among children under five years of age at 36.6%, wasting is at 14.4% and underweight at 29.8%.There are three localities resulted the high level of underweight (Edwiem, 34.7%, Alsalam 34.4% and Gli 34.5%).Among the localities, Kosti is the highest sever wasting by MUAC at 11.70%. Edwiem locality is the highest global wasting prevalence at 16.65% which is higher than WHO emergency (15%), while Guli locality is the second-high prevalence at 11%. Umrimta locality was high prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding, at 84.62%, while the Edwiem, Guli and Tandality localities were highest at (70 – 79)%.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will work in White Nile (Tendalti, Guli, and Eddiwuim localities) and Kassala States (River Atbara and Haemshkorieb localities) to provide nutrition-specific interventions through community & facility-based services. Activity focus will be screening 92,291 children aged 6-59 months through MUAC, and 45,947 Pregnant and Lactating Women through MUAC. It will also provide outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without complications for children 6-59 months through supporting 35 existing OTP sites to provide nutrition services for 6,866. Inpatient care will be provided in stabilisation centres for 1,030 girls and boys. 29,923 children will also be targeted for MAM treatment through provision of supplementary feeding including vitamin A/ micro-nutrient supplements (31,586 children). This will include supporting mother support group education targeting 23,511 mothers to ensure adequate IYCF practices. For PLW at risk of malnutrition, the project will provide targeted supplementary feeding.
