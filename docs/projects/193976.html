Project Summary

Considering the current context in Sudan in particular Central Darfur the project will be implemented in West Jabal Marra (Health and Nutrition) and Zalingei town (Health, Nutrition and WASH). The proposed project will be complimented by other IRS ongoing projects in the target areas. Due to the humanitarian context, (protracted armed conflict and new conflicts that have led to large numbers of IDPs, deaths and destruction of properties) coupled with hyperinflation and civil and political unrest that have caused adverse trickling effects in the target areas, high cost of living, poor harvest due to failed rains and poor agricultural practices, poor health seeking behavior, the area is estimated to be a high priority in the HRP/HNO 2023. The proposed 6-month project will lead to reduced suffering, increased, and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for 154,550 conflict affected people (men 50,847; women 66,920; boys 17,310; girls 19,473) out of which 77,274 will be IDPs, 38,638 Returnees and the remaining 38,638 host community members. The overall project goal is to address the immediate needs of conflict-affected people through integrated health, nutrition and WASH, as identified during IR Sudan’s Needs Assessment and analysis leading up to HRP/HNO 2023. The project will employ a 3 level implementation strategy whereby IRSIRS will do direct implementation of interventions, work with local partner and line ministries and departments and last one being through working with the affected people to achieve project goal. The interventions will be lifesaving, preventive services, improving the quality of services and improving the living conditions, wellbeing and safety of the affected people. Insecurity and threat to life of both affected people and staff and loss of property and displacement still remains a great risk for the project and IRS will work with relevant authorities to reduce the risks involved by strictly adhering to security advisories, full implementing the do no harm policy, conducting security and conflict sensitivity assessments and updating the Conflict Register on a regular basis. IRS has three sub offices in Central Darfur at the State Level in Zalingei, West (Nertiti) and Central (Golo) Jebel Marra where the project will be implemented. The main office is located in Khartoum with over 200 members of staff, 85 (45% Female) of whom are based in Central Darfur. The project staff are based in the West and Central Jebel Marra and Zalingei localities and are able to interact daily with relevant stakeholders including other humanitarian actors, government departments and ministries to facilitate project activities and ensure continued access. Although IRS implements all activities, the team prioritizes collaboration with local actors throughout the project cycle. IRS’ ability to implement quality SRHR and protection programming in such a sensitive context, access to hard-to-reach and conflict-affected areas, and the large amount of institutional donor funding illustrates IRS is highly qualified and accepted as a leading agency in Sudan. In addition, IRS has well-established community relations through well-functioning community structures that will support the project implementation. Members of IRSIRS staff are from the community and speak local languages which supports IR’s continued access and community-based approach, for example the ability to access the IDP camps and IDP informal settlements in Jebel Marra. IRS is uniquely posited as a faith-based organization, enabling IRS to address deeply held beliefs around inclusivity, gender relations, culture, and religion; thus, opening opportunities to contribute to structural and sustainable change.

1 - Needs

Intense clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for civilians in Sudan. As of May 21st, at least 1,042,114 individuals (209,136 households) have reportedly been displaced across the country since 15 April, according to estimates by IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). The current clashes are creating an unprecedented humanitarian crisis in Sudan, and especially in Khartoum, West, North, Central and South Darfur and North Kordofan. Prior to the conflict, there were approximately 3.8 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), the majority of whom (approximately 79%) were located in Darfur and these IDPs were facing severe humanitarian needs as per HNO 2023. According to UNOCHA’s March 2023 reports, Central Darfur hosts the third largest protracted displaced population in the country, with about 550,000 IDPs living in the state, of whom about 467,000 need assistance in 2023. It’s projected the ongoing displacement resulting from the conflict may increase an already substantial IDP caseload, exacerbating significant pre-existing humanitarian needs in the country and as well as in central Darfur. The situation in Central Darfur is very challenging especially Zalingei town that hosts about 207,335 IDPs (HNO2023). The recent clashes between SAF and RSF took place in Zalingi Town between 18 and 20 May 2023 may exacerbate humanitarian situation. There are communication challenges from Zalingei, however local sources suggest potential displacements of IDPs and host community if fighting continues to intensify. Which calls for urgent humanitarian response. Prior to conflict Sudan was facing multisectoral humanitarian and development challenges with significant increases in food and other commodity prices, hyperinflation, reduced harvest, conflict, and acute food insecurity rapidly increasing. Climate change and flooding has caused havoc, and this has affected farming activities with crop failure being a common challenge faced by several smallholder farmers. Due to the high cost of living and chronic food insecurity in the area, chronic and acute malnutrition has been on the rise: Despite efforts by IRS and partners and the high recovery rates reported by IRS (98%-75% as per Sphere standards), readmission rates remain high. According to a SMART Nutrition and Mortality Survey conducted by IRS in West Jabal Marra in September 2022, the GAM rate was reported as 13.4% which is at the critical threshold as per SPHERE Standards while children who were underweight was 39.2% and stunting was at 51.2% which indicates the poor malnutrition situation mainly due to inadequate diet, poor nutrition practices and disease and food insecurity at the HH level. The survey results coupled with IRS nutrition data clearly indicate the current challenging situation and the high risk of deterioration amidst the high cost of living. The health sector is reeling with numerous challenges due to the increasing number of patients putting pressure on the available resources, thus compromising quality service delivery. According to a needs assessment in Central Jabal Marra in June 2022, 71% of the population including women and children do not have access to clean and safe water, with very limited access from protected sources within 500m and amounts falling below the Sphere standards of 15 liters pp per day. Open defecation is at 51%, with 88% having no access to adequate hygiene and sanitation facilities. In line with revised 2023 HRP, IRS project has been revised to prioritize lifesaving program¬ming under SO 1 and 2, thus our project will focus on locations where people are now estimated to live in ‘extreme’ or ‘catastrophic’ humanitarian condi¬tions in Central Darfur.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IRS is committed to implement environmentally friendly activities and is involved in local forums such as the Water Users Association, to reduce environmental harm, for example by ensuring that all transport is highly coordinated to minimize the use of fuel and reduce emissions. IRS will also incorporate environmental monitoring into internal reporting systems so that field staff are systematically checking that supplies are disposed of in a safe way. At the health facility level, IRS will work with health facilities in monitoring Hospital Incinerators function ability, waste segregation and proper disposal of wastes. This includes working with the SmoH to ensure that communities and health service providers receive communication on both solid and medical waste management. Before embarking on construction of hand pumps and Mini Water Yards, WES will conduct geo-physical surveys over extraction and depletion of water from underground reservoirs. The project will also use solar power in the MWY (diesel generator is however needed as reserve). The construction of new pit latrines and rehabilitation of existing ones will ultimately lead to reduction of open defecation and reduce further contamination of the targeted areas, which will not only have positive effects on environment, but also to health aspects of the communities. IRS will use UNICEF and WES approved construction designs that are safe and have been shown to be environmentally friendly. The Sudan Agricultural Research Centre in the SmoA carries out soil tests to find out the impact project activities have on soil and provide detailed reports to partners. To ensure protection of forest cover, IRS will avoid supporting and or engaging in activities that involves deforestation and will promote agro-forestry to increase forest cover in the target areas.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IRS prioritizes protection (including child protection and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA) and safeguarding against any form of abuse (direct and indirect) against target population and staff. Preventing the risk of abuse or neglect and stop it from occurring will be practiced and adhered to at all times of project implementation. The distribution of assistance such as farm tools, seeds, dignity kits will be done in an environment that will not expose the target population to physical hazards, violence, or other forms of abuse. IRS will integrate safeguarding mechanisms and measures into all areas of the organization, to minimize risk and address concerns and incidents appropriately when they arise. IRS holds its staff accountable and any form of sexual exploitation and abuse, violations against protection, inclusion, and safeguarding policies. Any reports are investigated in line with IRSHR policies and the full respect of the principles of confidentiality and the right to be heard during the process. The project will mainstream protection by prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm, providing meaningful access, participation and empowerment while also being accountable to the affected communities. This will be accomplished through counselling, and information dissemination and access to services and rights, including how to prevent, anticipate, and address risks of violence, discrimination, and marginalization towards vulnerable groups by enhancing awareness and community resilience. Girls and boys will be protected from violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect through capacity building initiatives, by establishing protection forums, which will include enhancing the role of faith leaders to mitigate risks associated to protection. Moreover, the social mobilization and advocacy team will create awareness in the target communities on GBV prevention and protect GBV survivors and at-risk groups. Likewise, community committees will be oriented on IRS’s safeguarding/PSEA policies to ensure the safety and dignity of the communities, throughout the project cycle. Health and Nutrition Staff will be trained on GBV referrals and disseminate GBV key messages through health and nutrition outreaches and services. The project will establish an effective referral mechanism for protection related concerns in case the target population need to utilize those services, by conducting referral mapping inviting all relevant stakeholders. This will also be achieved by ensuring that all IRS staff always adhere to the CHS, and that target population are aware of gender equality and GBV through awareness sessions. IRS will apply a context sensitivity lens through the project life cycle by conflict and context analysis. The key drivers of conflict and violence include disputes over grazing lands, water and pastures, tribal and communal fights over socio-economic gains, revenge killings and attacks. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

A comprehensive conflict sensitivity approach will be ensured by using IRS’s designed tool of ’6A Inclusion Marker’, incorporating 6 core principles: Analysis, Adapted Assistance, Attention to Negative Effects, Adequate Participation, Accountability and Adequate Capacity. PSEAH sawareness raising sessions for both men and women will be conducted taking into consideration the cultural difference of the roles of men, boys and women and girls. IRS has a child safe guarding policy that is adhered to by all staff and all that are contracted by IRS to implement activities on its behalf. IRS has an established Beneficiary Complains and Response Mechanism (CRM) in place in the target project locations that promotes openness and community participatory initiatives and also includes engaging Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) to channel their complaints to IRS and enables beneficiaries to provide feedback both positive and negative and also to raise complains against IRS staff and or against the nature of project activities being implemented by IRS. The CRM channels through which the community will channel their complaints and concerns at all times without victimization. The Community Feedback Boxes are strategically placed outside IRS premises as well as at community level where community members can drop complains and feedback as the boxes are checked on a weekly basis by the MEAL Officer. In addition, complains phone number and email is provided in posters placed in all project sites to enable community members report complains and provide feedback without victimization. IRS focal persons in the project sites will document the feedback, complaints, and actions taken to address them and or to mitigate reoccurrence. This are documented in the CRM Registers that are shared with the CO for further actions on a monthly basis to enable the senior management to follow on the feedbacks and complains and provide support in addressing and or mitigating the same. IRS has a very strict culture of non-discrimination and or punishment against those providing or raising complains against specific staff and or project activity implementation. IRS will be accountable to the community members themselves through quarterly locality-level discussions with communities and one-on-one interactions with individual stakeholders. The meetings are two levelled the communities plan for their own meetings where complains and feedback are raised with the second level involving IRS and community members where IRS organizes the meet and listens to any concerns, complains and feedback or suggestions they have, address and mitigate problems and incorporate suggestions and feedback into the project design as much as is feasible. IRS will ensure that communities are aware of the project activities, goals and objectives and their roles and entitlements so that they are able to demand for the same if not provided and be able to carry out community audits on the project activities.
