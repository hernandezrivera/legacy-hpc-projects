Project Summary

Project Objective: Civilians living in high risk conflict-affected communities in the Gaza Strip are better prepared to protect themselves in case of armed conflict and are empowered with the knowledge they need to mitigate risks associated with explosive weapons.
The proposed interventions supplement the NPA Palestine Country Strategy 2016-2019 in Gaza and are aligned with Protection Cluster Objective 2: “Prevent and mitigate the effects of the Occupation and conflict related violence.” Project activities are also specifically aligned with Protection Cluster Objective 2 of the HRP, “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence,” and will contribute to this objective through the provision of Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) and Explosive Remnant of War (ERW) risk education for civilian residents (adults and children) of the Gaza Strip.
The proposed interventions are planned to take place in 2019, representing the second year of NPA's CPP implementation for 2018-2020. Over three-year programme period, the programme will be implemented using the same methodologies; training adults and children, but for new beneficiaries and new communities which will be prioritized based on Ministry of Education planning and in consultation with Protection Cluster and the Mine Action Working Group.

Needs

Since the 2014 war on the Gaza Strip, civilians have witnessed dramatic changes in all aspects of their lives, including their safety and security. Gaza remains in a state of protracted low intensity conflict in which Israeli military incursions and airstrikes are sporadic yet common place. People living in Gaza are exposed to perpetual uncertainty and political instability that could surface and become volatile with very short notice, or completely unexpectedly. Surrounded by the possibility of military escalation in the Gaza Strip, its residents and communities must be prepared for this eventuality and preemptively implement safety measures to mitigate the risks associated with armed conflict. By far the greatest conflict related risk threatening civilian life in Gaza is the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Explosive weapons such as aerial bombs, missiles, projectiles, etc., cause three simultaneous effects: blast, heat, and fragmentation. Blast is the pressure wave that causes debris to be thrown and has enough force to knock down walls, destroy vehicles, and collapse buildings. The explosion also generates heat which is radiated in all directions causing anything in its path to be burnt and can ignite flammable materials. The design of most explosive weapons also includes the addition of metal that ‘fragments’ into ‘shrapnel’ that can impact anything within a radius of the blast. Anything the explosion makes contact with may also itself fragment, such as glass, stones, household materials, etc. which can be equally as life threatening for anyone in the explosion’s path. Experiences from previous wars in Gaza clearly tell a story that local populations unable to seek refuge during use of explosive weapons have a better chance for survival through better preparedness. Safety and preparedness measures exist which can help civilians mitigate the risks associated with the three effects of an explosion and help to improve civilian survivability during and/or after an attack occurs. Knowledge of safety measures, risk mitigation techniques, proper evacuation procedures, basic fire safety, and basic First Aid can help civilians prevent loss of life either during or in the aftermath of an attack. Public ERW awareness also plays a critical role in protecting civilians against ERW which can litter populated areas during a conflict and pose a threat long after an attack occurs. So far in 2018, for instance, there have been 4 accidents involving ERW resulting in the injury of 6 people in Gaza. Since 2014, a total of 152 people in Gaza have fallen victim to ERW (7 dead, 135 injured) and the prospect of renewed high-intensity conflict underscores the need to educate civilians about these threats. Despite the clear and present need to raise awareness about emergency preparedness and protection in Gaza, concerted efforts focused on improving individual and household risk mitigation measures have been limited. A field survey conducted in 2015 by NPA on 1,755 households in vulnerable areas of Gaza showed that the vast majority of civilians (99.98%) in these areas had never received emergency preparedness and risk education training, many civilians had little to no first aid (84%) or fire safety (91%) experience, and the presence of ERW was still a perceived threat (93%). Civilians surveyed generally held many misconceptions about what constituted safe behaviour during a conflict and lacked basic information that could help improve the safety of themselves and their loved ones in the event of an attack. NPA surveying of 822 adult residents of Gaza in Sept-Oct 2017 confirmed this finding and showed that the implementation of Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) risk education training greatly improves beneficiaries’ knowledge of conflict risks and safety measures and enhances communities’ ability to respond adequately in a crisis situation.

Activities

As a leading global advocate against the use of explosive weapons in populated areas (EWIPA), as well as one of the world’s largest Mine Action operators, Norwegian People’s Aid (NPA) aims to remedy the existing gap regarding emergency preparedness risk education in Gaza and other conflict affected countries throughout the world. To this end, NPA began developing a new initiative in 2013 called Conflict Preparedness & Protection (CPP) to empower civilians to mitigate risks associated with armed conflict and explosive weapons. This project will utilize NPA’s existing CPP framework, methodologies, and educational materials which have been effectively employed in Gaza since 2015 to expand the provision of emergency preparedness risk education training to adult heads of household and school aged children. To date, NPA has provided emergency preparedness risk education training to over 61,625 beneficiaries across the Gaza Strip in cooperation with the Ministry of Education and other local partners. Participants of NPA's CPP risk education trainings receive instruction on the following topics: -Home preparation and blast protection safety measures-Conflict risk mitigation measures-Safe evacuation procedures-Basic fire safety-Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness-Improvised First Aid solutions in conflict situations NPA’s CPP framework utilizes separate curriculums for adults and children, designed to convey safety messaging in a manner which is appropriate and effective for each age group. All training sessions incorporate high quality training aids, educational materials, and media to enhance the learning experience. This project aims to achieve its objective via the following activities; conducted in partnership with the Ministry of Education (MoE), the UN Mine Action Service (UNMAS), the Deir El Baleh Rehabilitation Society (DBRS), and the Ma’an Center for Development: Activity 1: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education training for children (girls and boys) attending government schools in vulnerable conflict affected communities in the Gaza Strip; 1.1. Conduct educational CPP theatre shows for students from Gaza’s five districts, promoting awareness of emergency preparedness and risk education principles through a child friendly dramatic performance and interactive workshop period;1.2. Train 50 school teachers to deliver emergency preparedness and risk education training in the classroom and equip each with high quality training aids and informational print materials;1.3. Conduct CPP emergency preparedness and risk education training for children attending government schools through interactive in classroom training sessions; Activity 2: Provision of CPP and ERW risk education and support for adult heads of household (women and men) 2.1. Conduct CPP risk education training for adult heads of household across Gaza’s five districts through community based training sessions;2.2. Provide emergency equipment and supplies (grab bags, First Aid kits, fire extinguishers, etc.) to households in Gaza’s most vulnerable conflict affected communities using voucher mechanism; Gender balance: Selection of schools targeted under this project will be done in such a way as to ensure a proper balance of female and male student beneficiaries. NPA will also coordinate with the Ministry of Education to ensure that an equal number of female and male teachers are selected to implement CPP training in their respective schools. For adult focused emergency preparedness and risk education activities, NPA will work with the MoE and with local partners to ensure a high female participation rate. All educational materials and media used in this project has been designed to be gender inclusive and to promote women’s empowerment.

Indicators

Activity 1: Indicator/Target 1: 10,000 children (5000 girls and 5000 boys) have received training on conflict preparedness and protection risk education; Indicator/Target 2: 10,000 children (5000 girls and 5000 boys) have received training on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) risk education; Activity 2: Indicator/Target 1: 10,000 adult heads of household (5000 women and 5000 men) have received training on conflict preparedness and protection risk education; Indicator/Target 2: 10,000 adult heads of household (5000 women and 5000 men) have received training on Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) risk education; Indicator/Target 3: 750 households in Gaza’s most vulnerable conflict affected communities have received emergency preparedness equipment/materials; Schools based activities will target government schools via NPA's close existing partnership with the Ministry of Education. NPA and UNMAS will liaise with UNRWA over the course of this project to explore if CPP activities can be expanded to include UNRWA schools. Coordination: NPA is an active member of the Mine Action Working Group (MAWG) and will coordinate all project activities through it, providing monthly progress reporting on the above indicators to the MAWG and UNMAS. Vulnerable Populations & People with Disabilities: NPA’s CPP framework has been designed to meet the needs of those most vulnerable to the direct and indirect effects of armed conflict. NPA will prioritize targeting accessible at risk areas and communities most severely affected by past conflict, as well as vulnerable populations such as women, children, people with disabilities (PwD) and caregivers, and refugees where possible. NPA had worked with its partner, the Deir El Baleh Rehabilitation Society (DBRS), to adapt its CPP its messaging and methodologies to better meet the needs of PwD. NPA and DBRS finalized this process in early 2018 and will implement these changes under this project. Protection & ‘Do No Harm’: Preventing harm to civilians is the core of NPA’s CPP programme and, as such, ensuring the safety and dignity of targeted communities is critical to our project plan. NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of ‘do no harm’ and has designed this project’s activities to empower beneficiaries and mitigate threats against them without jeopardizing their wellbeing. Monitoring & Evaluation: NPA has developed a robust monitoring and evaluation setup for its CPP programme activities in Gaza that will be implemented over the course of this project. Daily risk education reporting from the field is submitted and monitored via NPA’s electronic tablet based information management system. Routine field visits will be conducted by NPA, the MoE, and DBRS for monitoring and quality assurance. A baseline and endline survey will be conducted via pre and post training questionnaires and comparative data analysis to quantitatively measure the effectiveness of the trainings. Interviews and focus groups with project beneficiaries and MoE trainers will be conducted to collect qualitative data and feedback regarding the project and its impact.
