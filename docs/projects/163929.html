Project Summary

Bonyan designed its Food Security interventions to meets the actual food needs in Syria and comply with the strategic objectives of both the HRP and FSA Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance with assessment findings and key indicators. Bonyan 2020 Food Security program will focus on time-critical interventions which lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and a speedy return to longer-term development are also imperative integrating cluster 3 objectives in order to provide inclusive respond to the specific needs in each targeted locations by improving farming livelihoods, crops production and productivity, food security and food availability targeting at both geographical and household level and then providing needs-based assistance to meet the needs that vulnerable households (prioritizes vulnerable children including adolescents/youth, girls and boys living with disabilities) who cannot meet for themselves ~ 8100 INDV (4050 F/ 4050 M), of which 4000 are IDPs, 2800 are Host Community, and 1300 are Returnees.
Obj.1: Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance targeting 1,000 HHs ~ 6,000 INDV (3000 F/ 3000 M, 4000 children <18)
Bonyan will provide 700 Food basket response for IDP influxes for 700 HHs ~ 4200 INDV (2100 F/ 2100 M) in order to improve food security score. On the other hand, Bread in the middle east’s culture, Syria particularly, is an essential part of food the daily meals and because of high ratio of carbohydrates which provides body with as energy and give them the feeling of satiety, Bonyan will participate in Bread supply chain through supporting local wheat farmers, wheat millers, and bakeries to distribute bread with less prices enabling access to 300 HHs to bread rations ~ 1800 INDV (900 F/ 900 M) The distribution will last for 60 days and will be subject to vulnerability criteria.
Obj.2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives targeting 350 HHs ~ 2100 INDV (1650 F/ 1650 M)
The project will provide critically needed life-saving farming inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, farming tools and introduce farmers to modern agriculture methods to protect their crops from various natural hazards. Targeting 250 HHs of small farmers ~ 1500 INDV (750 F/ 750 M)
Another 100 of vulnerable HHs ~ 600 INDV (300 F/ 300 M) will receive livestock along with feed for 4 months.
Bonyan will provide training, supervision, and consultations for the beneficiary’s households by expert agronomists and veterinarians aiming to prevent crop, animal losses and increase the production.
Registration of beneficiaries in all activities will be through outreach campaign by announcing at accessible points for all groups. The selection and prioritization criteria will also be published clearly. Transparency of shortlisting of beneficiaries will be achieved by establishing a committee in each community consist of one member from LC, one community leader and the team of Bonyan. This mechanism ensures accountability and promotes trust between Bonyan and beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurityOverall cereal production has improved this year in comparison to 2018. However, wheat production estimated to be at 2.2 million tons remains lower than the pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tons (2002-2011) and that of the domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tons. However, at 2 million tons, up from 0.4 million tons in 2018, barley production exceeds pre-crisis levelsThe number of people estimated to be food insecure is 7.9 million or 39 percent of the total population of Syria. Of this at least 569,477 (6%) are estimated to be severely food insecure. The majority of those are located in Aleppo, Idleb, Rural Damascus and Ar-Raqqa governates. Additionally, a further 1.9 million people are projected to be at risk, with the governorates of Hama, Homs, Deir Ez Zor and Idleb having the highest proportion of the population being at risk of food insecurity. Some 47 percent of all sub-districts in the country are thought to be either severely or moderately food insecure. The governorates estimated to have the highest levels of severity are Aleppo, Idleb, Hasakeh, Deir-ez-zor, and Dara. At least 37 percent of the food insecure population are in North West Syria. Of the total PiN, the ratio of total male to female is 51 to 49, with a substantial number of children (at least 42%) while adults and elders represent 54 and 4 percent of the PiN respectively

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about FSA needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators. Provide 700 food baskets in the emergency cases to 700 HHs ~ 4200 INDV (2100 F/ 2100 M) of the most vulnerable peopleProvision of flour or bread directly to 300 HHs ~ 1800 INDV (900 F/ 900 M) for 60 days (1 pack of bread for every 3 persons), through purchasing the wheat directly from the farmers, providing the millers with the wheat in order to produce flour, and producing a supported bread after supplying bakeries with needed materials Provide 250 small farmer HHS ~ 1500 INDV (750 F/ 750 M) with farming inputs, including legumes, vegetable seeds, liquid fertilizers, and basic farming tools following standard agricultural kits. BO will provide training, supervision, and consultations for the beneficiary’s by agronomists. The technical consultancy aims to prevent crop losses and increase the production.Distribute 4 animals of small livestock, and fodder for 4 months to 100 HHs ~ 600 INDV (300 F/ 300 M) accompanied with basic veterinary services.
