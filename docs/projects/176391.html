Project Summary

The overall aim of the proposed Lulu Care project is to contribute to strengthening prevention, mitigation and response to Child Protection issues as well as strengthening quality of care for vulnerable children while engaging the caregivers and local leaders. This project will be targeting 14,534 vulnerable children from IDP and host communities from 3 counties (Juba, Tonj South, and Kapoeta South) of Central Equatoria, Warrap and Eastern Equatoria States respectively, with significant impact aiming at helping children and young people affected by armed conflict. Lulu Care will work closely with all relevant stakeholders and communities to provide life-saving child protection prevention and response services through establishment and updating the child CP referral pathways with the child protection case workers and GBV. Focused and non-focused age appropriate MHPSS services (level 2 and 3) will also be provided to 10,000 vulnerable children, in addition 2000 (1500 female and 500 male) caregivers will be trained in positive parenting skills and psychosocial support in addition to establishing of peer-to-peer support groups for the caregivers. Lulu Care will also support 420 girls and 350 boys in vulnerable situations through comprehensive case management and referrals to specialized services including Early Childhood Mental Health, Social Skills Groups for Children, and School Advocacy. The project will also target 350 adolescent girls and boys in providing life skills and resilience training and comprehensive sexuality education (UNESCO guidelines). Lulu care will continue engaging in identification, documentation and placement of children with protection concerns (UASC). To strengthen the community structures to prevent and respond to child protection issues Lulu Care will train 60(30 females and 30 Males)government social workers / caseworkers on child rights and the recent updated Child protection Minimum standards. To prevent early/forced marriages, prevention of child abuse and exploitation and advocating for children rights Lulu Care will Conduct 6 awareness raising campaign sessions in the communities. The project will target IDPs, returnees as well as host communities. Lulu Care will build upon existing efforts and target vulnerable children, while coordinating closely with the Child Protection and GBV Sub-Clusters and other partners in implementing this project. Home- to -home visits shall be conducted to enlighten the communities on the Covid-19 prevention measures and response. Lulu Care is currently operating a GBV toll free helpline (662)a remote service delivery with this there will be expansion of life saving CP and GBV services to support child survivors, through coordination with other service providers and other sectoral clusters, this will be harmonized through CP and GBV referrals. Furthermore social workers will be trained in CP and GBV case management for better service delivery through the support for a centralized coordination system and the strengthening of existing referral systems and direct child survivor assistance . Awareness raising will be conducted to inform the children,caregivers and communities on the helpline through community based approaches on the same note follow-up processes will be conducted to exploit the attention generated tend to have limited long-term impact. Following the directives to open schools Lulu will ensure that school going children are supported 6 teacher workshops shall be conducted that will enhance knowledge and skills of teachers on how to support and encourage Children through the period of Covid-19. Lulu will conduct 6 Safety Audits/ service mapping to inform on child related GBV risks with community participation. Additionally ,Lulu will train 500 households especially in IDP centers on kitchen Vegetable Gardening to supplement on the household diet to reduce malnutrition cases among children and caregivers (lactating mothers and pregnant women).

1 - Needs

During the recent displacements in South Sudan, families especially children boys and girls are greatly affected psychologically as they seek for safety, their daily routine are greatly disrupted, environment changed , human rights violated , generally exposing them to many child protection issues such as child abuse, violence, neglect child labour and violence due to their low ability to protect themselves. 2.3 million Children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection in 2021. Children continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 most affected counties.(HNO2021) The situation has been heightened with the current outbreak of Covid-19 in early 2020, Left without access to the protective environment of school by the pandemic, the risk of children being neglected, abused or exploited increases significantly. The COVID-19 pandemic poses enormous risks to children, women, girls, families and communities including mental health and psychosocial distress, intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labour among others. In Kapoeta South and Tonj South like any other part of the country it has amplified the struggles that children are already facing to receive a quality education since majority children and students have been forced out of schools in addition to other child protection services. Even before the outbreak of the virus, there were thousands out-of-school children across the two counties, principally due to poverty, poor governance, or living in or having fled an emergency or conflict. Lulu Care in its recent survey conducted in Tonj and Kapoeta South towns depicted the struggles that children are going through, many are currently in poor working conditions in quest for food that has prompted the current occurrences of child sexual exploitation and abuse leading to early pregnancies and apparently to dangers of abortions among the young girls. According to the recent IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition October 2020 – July 2021 ,about 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021. This will prompt the high prevalence of diseases (affecting up to 36% of children under five). Tonj South at severity of 4, Juba and Kapoeta South counties at 3 means that there is a high need to save the lives of the children and caregivers (pregnant and lactating mothers). The recent IRNA( multi-sectoral needs assessment 12-17 May 2020) conducted in Tonj South by partners indicate the continuous use of children in inter-communal conflicts ,sexual abuse of women and young girls has also been reported. According to RRC the inter-communal conflict have resulted to movements instigating the number of children missing .Children, women, girls and elderly can be seen depressed, traumatized and psychologically unwell. There were children associated with armed groups particularly new division 10 (newly created army division with its based in Yinh-Kuel Boma, Thiet payam Tonj South County) Tonj South county. There is no service providers’ coordination mechanisms in place in Tonj South, at such GBV and child protection services are dire since no service mapping had been conducted before. According to Child and Adolescent Survivors Initiative (CASI),Coordination between CP and GBV service providers is essential in the evolving COVID-19 context to address gaps, maximize coverage, and minimize overlaps in support services for child and adolescent survivors of sexual violence.Child protection concerns in Kapoeta South County and Tonj South are currently driven by the limited livelihood options, food consumption gaps, and lack of well-functioning child protection structure.

2 - Project activities

Activities: Establish referral pathway in Tonj South and update the CP referral pathways in Kapoeta South and Juba among the child protection case workers and GBV Actors. Provide focused and non-focused age appropriate MHPSS services (level 2 and 3) to 750 vulnerable children. Train caregivers (800 female and 400 male) on positive parenting skills and psychosocial support; establish peer-to-peer support groups for the caregivers. Support 420 girls and 350 boys in vulnerable situations through comprehensive case management and referrals to specialized services. 254 children formerly associated with armed forces and group and other vulnerable children are provided with life skills and resilience training and comprehensive sexuality education (UNESCO guidelines). Identification, documentation and placement of children with protection concerns (UASC). Build the capacity of 40 child protection staff, 120 government officers, and 40 community-based child protection structures in CPiE: Targeting 200 participants. Train government social workers / caseworkers on child rights and the recent updated Child protection Minimum standards: Targeting 60(40 females and 20 Males). Conduct 6 awareness raising campaign sessions (To prevent early/forced marriages, prevention of child abuse and exploitation and advocating for children rights).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Lulu Care has developed a PSEA policy and inducted the staff who adhere to the values and principles outlined in the Code of Conduct. In accordance with these values, the organization operates and enforces policies in Beneficiary Protection from Exploitation and Abuse. The organization will conduct trainings with relevant leaders, develop information education and communication materials with key messages on PSEA in addition to awareness campaigns to the communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Lulu Care will use numerous tools for beneficiaries to provide feedback, they will be made available from the project planning, implementation and evaluation phases, we shall ensure that they are in local languages and community mobilizers will be provided with skills to support the vulnerable persons including people with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Lulu Care will institutionalize feedback mechanism to allow beneficiaries , girls and boys, women and men including persons with disabilities to voice their successes, as well as complaints and concerns to stakeholders on quality andimpact of the project as a means to measure accountability to affected population. Our feedback models on activities in includes beneficiary scorecards about the services, complaint collection boxes; displaying activities and budget of this action in visible places, periodic joint review sessions of beneficiaries and the local authorities further more Lulu Care utilizes the Joint stakeholder , monitoring visits, surveys, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), focus group discussions, one-to-one interviews, community meetings, notice boards, community monitoring of service delivery andparticipatory budgeting.Lulu Care operates a toll free helpline that is used to collect information and feedback from the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Lulu Care listens and is responsive to beneficiaries feedback during the project implementation. To ensure that accountability mechanisms are accessible,meaningful and effective ,the organization will be context-specific, and based onconsultation with users, ensure inclusivity and accessibility to information,empower beneficiaries, consistently closing the loop with a commitment to transparency and accountability, enable collective responsibility for giving voice to beneficiaries ensure do no harm and be appropriately resourced. All in all the feedback will be used to shape the mishaps and challenges that may affect the implementation of the project. Importantly is that Lulu care is operating a helpline that will be used to collect feedback during the project implementation, monitoring and evaluation.
