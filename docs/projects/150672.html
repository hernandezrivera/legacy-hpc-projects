Project Summary

The project responds to Cluster Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive quality educational services for vulnerable children. It will enable 100 (out of 409) vulnerable schools in Gaza to continue functioning through the provision of necessary teaching and learning materials. The schools will be selected based on the vulnerability mapping conducted by the Islamic Relief, UK, 2017
The project will also support over 33,000 (50% girls) poorest students ages 6-17 who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue in their education by providing basic, urgent supplies including school bags, school uniforms, warm clothes, and shoes. The students will be provided with these materials via the e-voucher system designed with the World Food Programme (WFP) to avoid stigmatizing children and families and allowing the option to select their own supplies.

Needs

According to the poverty survey conducted by the 2017 Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics about 53% of Gaza population is living in poverty. The already dire living conditions for children across the Gaza Strip further deteriorated since 30th of March 2018. The electricity crisis, cuts in civil servants’ salaries including teachers, UNRWA crisis and overall economic decline, has led to an increased level of poverty and increased household vulnerability. Parents are increasingly not able to afford basic school supplies and schools for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out. Girls are more vulnerable in this context as parents focus their expenses on family basic needs and preferred to offer education opportunities for boys. Similarly, due to lack of operational budget, schools are not able to procure basic teaching supplies and the new school year has been ushered with many schools, in the most vulnerable areas, struggling to afford basic stationary, paper or chalk. On 14 October 2018, UNICEF/Education Cluster has received official request from the MoEHE Gaza to support provision of stationary supplies to schools most in need with the amount of 1,745,707 NIS. Through this project, UNICEF is seeking to support the vulnerable schools with stationary and teaching and children with school bags, uniforms, warm clothes and shoes. UNICEF wishes to ensure that vulnerable children boys and girls not only access schools but learn in an enabling environment. Under the project, UNICEF will ensure that schools located in marginalized or underserved areas have the resources and teaching materials that enable teachers to teach and the learning/teaching process to continue.

Activities

The following activities will be conducted after the vulnerable children and schools have been selected. The criteria for the selection of schools will include the number of vulnerable children in the poorest localities indicated in the poverty report issued by the PCBS and the location of school in high vulnerability localities as per Islami Relief survey. The names of beneficiaries will be verified through the OCHA list of hard ship cases. Activities: 1. Provision of school supplies to 100 public school in Gaza. Schools will be selected based on the vulnerability mapping conducted by Islamic Relief in 2017, that included specific criteria such as, located in high vulnerable localities, schools located in Access Restricted Areas (ARA), and schools serving poor children and communities. 2. Provision of school bags and basic stationary to 30,000 children ages 6-17 from the most vulnerable households. The list of vulnerable households will be obtained from Ministry of Social Affairs in Gaza and priorities will be given to families of female households. School children in these families will be considered the top priority. Disaggregated data about male and female students will be verified by ministry of education through the School Management Information System (SMIS) 3. Provision of school uniforms, jackets and shoes/ rain boots to approximately 3,300 children in need using e-voucher programme (with WFP). Families affected by environmental threats/ natural disasters e.g. seasonal floods will be prioritized. Priorities will also be given to female households. Disaggregated data about male and female students will be verified by the School Management Information System (SMIS).
