Project Summary

The current outbreak of COVID-19 represents an unprecedented and growing public health challenge, also threatening and impacting Bangladesh including vulnerable Rohingya refugees and their host communities.
Médecins du Monde (MdM), a non-governmental medical organization for international cooperation and humanitarian action, has been working for Rohingya refugees and their host communities on health education in an approach of capacity building of people. Therefore, MdM is in a unique position to contribute to the combat against the spread of COVID-19.
The main objective of the intervention is to improve the knowledge and prevention/protection capacities of the target communities in relation to COVID-19 including imminent threats such as increase in gender based violence (GBV). It falls under the second objective of Health Sector - COVID-19 Response Plan: Prevent transmission and amplification of COVID-19, by enhancing infection prevention and control in community and health care settings and effectively communicate critical risks (counter misinformation) to men, women, boys and girls. This is in dire need as there has been rapid spread of COVID-19 in Bangladesh with some cases in host communities as well. Frontline volunteers’/workers’ capacity must be reinforced for preparedness and response. Besides, not only the strengthened vertical health service model, but also a strengthened community coping mechanism in the crowded, resource scarce setting especially in the refugee camps through coordinated and cooperation approaches by actors is indispensable. These dual components should not only be effective for fighting COVID-19 but also contribute to addressing people’s overall health and protection issues. While implementing emergency capacity building intervention, we will also take time for helping people continue and improve all necessary healthcare and protection especially for vulnerable ones and build enduring social resilience. This approach should also contribute to the fourth objective of Health Sector - COVID-19 Response Plan: Ensure continuity of equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with a special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases while adapting services to the current context.
To ensure undisrupted cohesion with the overall response in Cox's Bazar, the project will regularly follow up and exchange trends and recommendations through coordination with the Health Sector, Community Health WG, Communication with Communities WG, GIHA WH and GBV Sub Sector.

Activities or outputs

In collaboration with and under guidance of Community Health Working Group and other technical groups, we will implement the following: 【Component 1: Community health activities targeting refugees】1.1 Trainings on CHWs' ToR; COVID-19; non-communicable diseases; emergency medical service; GBV basics, disclosure of GBV and referral according to existing protocols1.2 CHWs' home visits to provide risk communication on COVID-19, health education and WASH supplies and IEC materials; and coduct basic monitoring and health services. They may take part in COVID-19 surveillance, case detection and referrals1.3 CHWs organize monthly coordination meetings in each camp, where issues and challenges are identified and addressed, and lessons are shared among CHWs. They also use this meeting to conduct a refresher session on a selected topic, based on CHWs’ performance monitoring and/or in response to their demands. Issues and challenges that are not resolved at this meeting are referred for further negotiation and coordination with other stakeholders1.4 Forming Reproductive Health Awareness Groups (RHAGs) consisting of key community leaders, both male and female, including Maji and other influential people in each camp area. RHAGs' role is to support community health work undertaken by CHWs, encourage positive attitudinal and behavior changes and access to health services at individual and community levels, and take leadership in identifying and addressing health issues in their communities in coordination with CHWs1.5 Quarterly RHAG meetings are organized at the camp level by CHWs. The meetings review the progress, identify issues, challenges and lessons, and develop actions to be taken 【Component 2: Community health activities targeting the host community】2.1 COVID-19 training for CHWs and key health personnel under the oversight by the Community Health Supervisor (CHS)2.2 Provision of protective equipment for use in community clinics and outreach activities2.3 Thoroughly publicized referral pathways between community clinics and CHWs supported by CHS2.4 Creation and reinforcement of contact network between community clinics and community leaders for immediate COVID-19 response2.5 Home visits by CHWs under supervision2.6 Outreach activities and awareness raising sessions on COVID-19 at clinics by health personnel in collaboration with CHWs and CHS 【Component 3: Improved community awareness and capacity in GBV prevention in COVID-19 context】3.1 Staff at 3 supported PHCs trained on links between COVID-19 and GBV, health consequences of GBV, identification of GBV risks and the essential support: first line support, communication techniques, principles for appropriate survivor-centred support and updated referral pathways 3.2 Local partner’s GBV programme officers supported and available for technical support to PHC teams3.3 Existing and new community health teams mobilized and trained on COVID-19 messaging including awareness of related GBV risk and available support; identification of GBV risks and referrals, dealing with GBV disclosure3.4 Community outreach teams and staff at 3 PHCs supported with personal protective equipment3.5 Referral pathways up-to-date through coordination with GBV actors including local women organization3.6 Community awareness activities in the catchment area of 3 select PHCs through household visits, gender-separated neighbourhood sessions with women and men, meetings with local leaders on the topics: COVID-19, related heightened risk of GBV / domestic violence, positive masculinity, role of neighbourhood support and services available in the area)3.7 Provision of appropriate support to identified GBV survivors by select PHCs and outreach teams including coordination with available protection actors and referrals according to CHWG protocols
