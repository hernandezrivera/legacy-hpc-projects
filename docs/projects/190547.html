Project Summary

As of February 2022, a total of 4.51 million IDPs have been identified thus far across the country. Of the total displaced population, the main causes of displacement were conflict which displaced 3.63 million IDPs (81%), drought which displaced 420,887 IDPs (9%), social tension which displaced 171,727 IDPs (4%), seasonal floods which displaced 122,901 IDPs (3%) and flash floods which displaced 119,807 IDPs (3%). Through Site Assessment round 28 (Dec 2021-Jan 2022), 2.06 million IDPs were identified in 1,440 IDP sites across 9 regions in Ethiopia. Notably, there was a +37% increase in the number of droughts IDPs this round due to the intensifying drought, especially in Afder, Dawa, Dollo and Liban zones in Somali region and West Guji zone in Oromia region.
In terms of people affected by the drought, the Somali region is the most affected with more than 3.5 million people, followed by Oromia (more than 3.4 million), SNNP (more than 1,1 million), Southwest (more than 200,000 people) regions. Overall, at least 286,000 people have been displaced by drought between October 2021 and March 2022 across the drought-affected areas (183,000+ in Somali, 103.000 in Oromia).
According to the ESNFI cluster, peoples in need in the proposed locations such as Arero Woreda (1,469), Guchi Woreda (4,261), Kumbi Woreda (2,531), Moyale (OR) Woreda (3,539), Siraro Woreda (1,660) and Moyale (SM) Woreda (10,785) are residing in extremely poor conditions who need urgent emergency shelter and NFI assistance. IDPs are living across all woredas except Arero woreda whereas returnees are found in Arero and Moyale (SM) woredas.
GE assumes the project activities addresses the strategic objectives with significant positive impact on the identified needs of the respective clusters. The project activities are all a priority to the respective sectors. GE aims an in-kind or cash for non-food items assistance for IDPs and shelter repair kit assistance for returnees. To maximize the impact on the intervention, GOAL proposed this ESNFI standalone applications whereby the other GOAL Ethiopia projects are implemented with the same sectors with BHA, ECHO, UNICEF, and IA funding sources and/or proposed for standalone allocation with other sector applications in this EHF/HPC. During implementation phase GOAL easily will do rapid assessments to determine the actual needs in the proposed locations.
The ESNFI proposed interventions for this HPC will be.
 Emergency shelter repair kits distribution for 1,100 HHs returnees (1,000 Arero woreda and 100 Moyale (SM) woreda).
 Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 Lowlands- Drought Response) distribution for 10,510 HHs IDPs (3,622 Guchi, 3,008 Moyale (OR) & 3,880 Moyale (SM)).
 Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 Disable Lowlands- Drought Response) distribution for 1,753 HHs IDPs (639 Guchi, 531 Moyale (OR) & 583 Moyale (SM)).
 Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 Highland - Drought Response) distribution for 3,562 HHs IDPs (2,151 Guchi & 1,411 Siraro).
 Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 Disable Highlands- Drought Response) distribution for 629 HHs IDPs (380 Kumbi & 249 Siraro).
 Conduct independent beneficiary identification and verification process based on revised SOPs considering national/regional cluster standards.
 Information sessions, establishment and roll-out of community response & feedback mechanisms.
 Conduct HLP assessment.
 Conduct regular joint and independent monitoring.
 Conduct post distribution monitoring.

1. - Projects activities

- Beneficiary Targeting and Identification: Jointly with the local authorities at Zonal or Woreda level GOAL will conduct beneficiary identification and registration for the emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits activities as relevant in accordance with the Guidelines for Needs Based Targeting of Humanitarian Response in Displacement Areas and Shelter Cluster guidance. Then, GE will conduct independent beneficiary verification process based on revised SOPs considering national/regional cluster standards. Finally, the registration process will be conducted together with representatives of the Woreda DRMO, kebele officials, female representatives and a committee representing the affected community.- Procurement, transportation, and distribution of emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits package for returnee and IDPs respectively. In-kind distribution is identified as the appropriate modality due to the market functionality, availability and quality of the items; therefore GOAL will procure emergency shelter repair kits for 1,100 HH and Non-Food Items Kits package for 16,454 HHs (of these, 2,382 Disable Lowland and Highland HH) through a competitive bidding procedure, transport them to the returnees location and IDP camps, and then distribute them to targeted households with the support of the local authorities, IDP camp representatives, respected local leader, and daily laborers who will act as distribution agents. GOAL will support targeted households to meet their shelter repair needs as per the cluster standard, Shelter damage prioritization draft guideline for Oromia/Somali BOQ and supported by community sensitization, carpenter training and HLP training/support.- Community involvement: GOAL will ensure that households actively participate in all stages of NFI distribution and shelter rehabilitation from inception to evaluation, including where external carpenters are used. This will ensure transfer of knowledge and skills to the community.- Community sensitization and technical support: GOAL will provide sensitization on rehabilitation of shelter and appropriate framing materials if required. GOAL will ensure that beneficiaries receive instructions on creating adequate living spaces using materials provided and those gathered locally. Capacity building will be carried out with the targeted community on construction and maintenance to ensure safety and full compliance to design. GOAL will provide technical support drawn from the Shelter/NFI Cluster Technical Working Group guidelines to ensure a build back better approach while conducting shelter repairs. - Demonstration sessions will be conducted where necessary. The demonstration shelters will be handed over to the most vulnerable people from the targeted households who cannot construct their own shelter. - Information sharing with community before receipt of emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits package. In conjunction with the relevant local authorities and IDP camp representatives, GOAL will conduct information sharing sessions in advance of the distributions, to inform the stakeholders about the project, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, targeting criteria, entitlements, information about the distributions (time, date, location), modality and feedback mechanisms. - Protection mainstreaming: GOAL will make sure that people with specific needs or vulnerabilities are prioritized and supported. GOAL will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout all activities.- Feedback mechanism will be established- HLP assessment will be conducted before the shelter repair kits distribution to avoid the potential risk of eviction of beneficiaries from their shelter after construction and the documents will be one of the vital documents to guarantee their right to use the land.- Regular joint and independent monitoring of the project activity with local government- Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project is designed in consultation with the local government and regional cluster need and priorities. Government agreement will be reviewed by relevant stakeholders and ensure that the proposed interventions are in line with the actual needs of the affected communities. GOAL Ethiopia (GE) has institutionalized accountability matrix across all levels, ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources. GE believes and uses community networks, linkages, partnerships, and coordination which enables effective activities, service delivery and advocacy, maximizing resources and impacts, and coordinated, collaborative working. GE will ensure the proposed interventions are accessible to all who need them, evidence‐informed and based on community assessment of resources and needs. Therefore, GE ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. GE considers it of paramount importance that beneficiaries have access to information about their entitlements, have access to GOAL’s Code of Conduct and can provide feedback on programs. During any community activities discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the Donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organisation with feedback, requests for information, and grievances. Moreover, joint supervision and monitoring activities are conducting on regular base which involves the local government and the community representatives from all categories like gender, age, ethnic and health status. A Community Response Mechanism will be established for all locations for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during activities, entitlement posters will be printed and displayed at relevant locations. The CRM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and national government, partners, and GOAL staff. Core principles of GOAL’s CRM are confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. GOAL has dedicated third party (independent from operations) in-country resources in place to manage the CRM. All staff are trained on accountability measures, including the CRM, and comprehensive guidelines and SOPs are in place. Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries of the selection criteria, their entitlements, and GOAL’s Code of Conduct. GOAL is committed to a having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

In conjunction with the relevant local authorities and IDP camp representatives, GOAL will conduct information sharing sessions in advance of the distributions, to inform the stakeholders about the project, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, targeting criteria, entitlements, and feedback mechanisms. GOAL will prepare the targeted community members to receive shelter repair kits and NFI, by providing information about the distributions (time, date, location), and modality. GE will ensure that households targeted to receive support fully understand how and where they will be able to collect their in-kind materials. Protection messages will be provided during the information sessions to ensure that targeted communities are not at risk when carrying cash or in-kind items, and do not share financial or identification details with others, and that all risks to the beneficiaries are mitigated. Targeted beneficiaries will be provided with information on what gap/need the cash (where this modality is employed) is expected to cover (shelter repair, essential NFIs). For shelter repair, GOAL will use a phased approach based on incremental payments to ensure that cash has been used for the intended shelter repair activities. GOAL will establish a complaints and feedback response mechanism (CRM) for community feedback on Shelter repair kits and NFI distributions. All targeted households will be provided with information on the project, GOAL, their entitlements, and modalities for collection of Shelter repair kits and NFI, as well as ways to contact GOAL and provide feedback through the CRM or by other means. Male and female representatives from the targeted community will be selected to take part on a community feedback committee during the distributions. More details are provided in the Accountability to Affected Populations Section. GE is using integrated complaints mechanism which is more participatory not just consultation. Before establishing GE ensures the mechanism is age and gender appropriate, safe, and confidential channels and conduct effective community awareness raising campaigns and create multiple complaints channels which more confidential and user-friendly. Project staffs are well training how to empower the community to use the channels, and how to employee appropriate complaint handling mechanism.
