Project Summary

Wamo Relief Rehabilitation Services (WRRS) through an integrated approach will be able to provide life saving health care services including, immunization, Ante-Natal Care (ANC), Post Natal Care (PNC) Basic Emergency,Obstretic and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) with referrals of the complicated cases and mental health cases for treatment to Referral hospital for further management of the complicated cases, treatment of childhood illnesses, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) services integrated together to the flood affected Households (HH) and other public health related concerned , These services will also not exclude Internarlly Displaced Persons (IDPs) and populations in Baidoa, Garowe and Lower Juba. We will also provide Training's to our team of nurses, mid-wives and community health workers on BEmONC, Intergrated management of childhood illness (IMCI), training of community health workers and volunteers on the promotion of hygiene, management and prevention of AWD/cholera cases.
The services will be targeting beneficiaries: men, women, boys and girls in mobile/ community and static health care centres the mobile services will entirely target far to reached Households (HH) while the static ones will be offering health care services for the nearby HH through treatment of illnesses and control of communicable diseases. Immunization will be offered to both pregnant and under 1 years to ease the burden of vaccine preventable diseases. Coordination with all stakeholders including the marginalized and the less minority groups will be adhered to for accountability.

Needs

Health situation in Somalia has been of alarming rate with several of the indicators and the prevalence of diseases being on the rise. Lower Juba is one of the areas in Somalia with chronic high levels of acute malnutrition beyond the emergency threshold. It is also a district where the IDPs report high prevalence acute malnutrition according to FSNAU surveys. Almost 1 in every 2 children suffering from a communicable disease, limited access to health facilities, very few women with access to Maternal and child care services including delivery services (Health cluster reports) , very high morbidity rates with child illness being highest , poor access to vaccination services and 1 in every 5 pregnant or lactating women being acutely malnourished (FSNAU,2018). The latest FSNAU assessment conducted among Dhobley IDP indicate the nutrition situation has sustained critical in the last six seasons and is unlikely to improve in the next season (FSNAU 2018).13.3% of children 6-59 months are acutely malnourished with 1.8% being severely malnourished and at even greater risk of morbidity and death,. Of concern is the under-five death rate which is slowly appreciating and is now at 0.89 (FSNAU 2018). The areas are struggling to recover following the drought experienced in 2017 and 2018 floods due to concerted efforts of partners, donors and other stakeholders. The calamities worked negatively to worsen morbidity rates, infant and young child feeding practices, and food and water prices All this amidst trade disruptions, poor access causing limited humanitarian assistance further worsening the nutrition situation.Currently, These population struggles to meet food requirements and are still experiencing shocks and effects of the same that make made the situation likely to deteriorate further. The population is also still highly vulnerable to more shocks that could push them back to crisis situation or beyond if support is not sustained. Morbidity rate is also high.

Activities

Act 1.1.1 Provide Consultation services to an average of 40 consultations per day to offer treatment of common communicable diseases and appropriate follow ups to the affected population with an aim of reducing the disease burden. Act 1.1.2 Provision of ANC and BEmONC at MCH site. (In adherence to WHO standards.) BEmONC services that WRRS intent to deliver based on the presentation of the mother. Cases requiring Advanced/comprehensive emergency obstetric care will be referred referral hospital.Act 1.1.3 The project will provide Immunization services (EPI) as part of primary health services integrated with nutrition services (through referrals of the malnourished children and mothers to the out-patient therapeutic program) will be delivered by the vaccinators in the MCH. Children and pregnant women with malnutrition will be referred to nutrition centres in close proximity for nutrition care and support.Act 2.1.1 Provision of curative services. including management of childhood illnessesAct 2.1.2 Through the Health workers the project will provide services to mental health cases but refer emergency cases from the MCH into the major referral Hospital. sensitization services will include the need to have children immunized, early health seeking at the health facility for sick patients.Act 2.1.3 capacity building of staff Act 2.1.4 The project will equip the supported MCHs sites with essential drugs, vaccines, to deliver lifesaving health services from UNICEF and (WHO in cases of outbreaks like Cholera, measles and other haemorrhagic fevers) the procured medical supplies.Act 3.1.1Provision of surveillance services. On a weekly basis, the project will share Surveillance for epidemic diseases received at the MCHs including, AWD/Cholera, measles through case registration using DEWS tools with Health clusters, WASH cluster, sub-clusters and MoH for prompt response in case of an emergency outbreak. For public health concerned responsible chain of command to respond.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The level of conflict and catastrophic that have been experienced in Somalia have resulted in need to set measure to mitigate this including other risks. The ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and cities. Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. In IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. IDPs with their perceived links with non-state armed actors face increased exposure to security threats and discrimination in assistance provision from the host community. specific concern is the large number of drought and flood-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks. WRRS will work closely with the local authorities to address some of the concerns encountered that may pose risk to the beneficiaries being targeted in the intervention.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Displacement affects individuals of all gender, ages and sexes, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and needs may evolve over time. Many IDPs, whether recently displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, are compelled by a lack of access to adequate assistance and livelihoods, to resort to negative coping mechanisms with serious protection repercussions. For instance, children are at a heightened risk for recruitment into fighting forces, child labor, early marriage and dropping out of school. This project targets beneficiaries with specific activities tailored to meet the needs of affected population with specially designed activities for each age group (e.g. children under 5, pregnant and lactating mothers, women of childbearing ages).

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. As a result of general insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between the government, KDF other security forces within the region and AS, and supporting actors. This is further aggravated regarding violations for marginalized groups where societal inequalities result in reduced protection. WRRS will work closely in collaboration in providing education on Disaster Risk Reduction, and how to go about in areas with explosive remnants of wars.
