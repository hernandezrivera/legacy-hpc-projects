Project Summary

The considerable efforts made by the Government, together with WASH Partners, have resulted in 86% of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs reporting having enough water to meet their household needs. 97% of sanitation facilities are also reported as functional. Challenges remain, especially in terms of the operation of water networks and Solid Waste Management, as only two-thirds of the waste is currently properly processed, and a new landfill site will need to be built in 2024. Also, there is limited and proper management of solid waste, menstrual hygiene management for adolescent girls and households, and fecal sludge management in Ukhiya FDMN camps. Considering these needs, IRB has designed the project for 12 months to address the WASH vulnerability in Camp 2 West and Camp 2 East along with the surrounding host communities living in Ukhiya Upazila under Cox’s Bazar district. Major WASH interventions will be Pit latrine construction/repair, Sludge desludging, bathing cubicle, hand washing station construction/renovation, Deep tube well installation, construction of WASH block, Women’s Hygiene center construction, Communal and household level solid waste management and hygiene promotion in the FDMN’s camps and host community areas.
Islamic Relief Bangladesh has a plan for the construction/decommission of 65 cubicles of latrines, 25 Bathing cubicles, and 20 hand-washing device installations at Camp 2 West and 2 East. IRB will continue to repair and maintain 415 latrine cubicles and 150 bating facilities at the above-mentioned camps. Furthermore, IRB will continue the operation and maintenance of existing water supply pipe networks and 500 water points to ensure a safe water supply for the targeted FDMNs in Camp 2W and Camp 2E. Consequently, considering the fecal sludge management, IRB has a plan for latrine desludging to reduce environmental detrimental as well as controlling AWD in responsible areas and provision of 2000 latrine sludge collection and management in camps 2W and 2E. This proposed project will also implement a large portion of integrated WASH facilities along with a waste management system in the FDMN community. Considering the inclusion, IRB has the plan to construct 3 Women's hygiene centers and continue the operation and maintenance of existing Women's hygiene centers in our responsible 02 camps. In view of the affected host community, 7 WASH blocks will be constructed in the educational institution and market area with an easy access system for persons with disabilities and aged persons. There is also a provision for the installation of 18 deep tube wells in the affected host community, especially the vulnerable host community areas of Ukhiya upazila. Provisions have been made for drainage cleaning and solid waste management. IRB has the provision of distribution of water storage kits to the FDMNs at camps 2W and 2E as well as the host community of Ukhiya Upazila. IRB always enhance improved hygiene promotion and hygiene practices and provide necessary session, orientation, and training to WASH volunteers, staff, community people, user groups, and management committee as effective stakeholder of the project. IRB will conduct hygiene awareness sessions at the block level as well as provide need-based gender and age-appropriate hygiene kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable families in camps 2W and 2E. IRB will provide a full WASH package in blocks B & C of camps 2E and A & B blocks of camps 2W including HP promotion, Solid Waste Management, Water, and sanitation. According to the WASH sector Strategy, IRB will focus on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH infrastructure in the mentioned Camps and blocks.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

IRB followed an integrated approach to select their responsible Camps in consultation with the WASH sector, UNHCR, and camp-level actors to ensure inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and delivery services to the FDMNs and the host community. IRB will work as WASH camp focal for Camp 2 West and as a WASH actor of Camp 2 East, responsible for providing WASH-related support. Currently, there are 95 piped water networks across all 33 camps, six in camp-2W and thirteen in Camp 2E. About 14% of households reported not having enough water to meet all their HH needs; consequently, they do not meet WASH sector standards (WASH Sector J MSNA-2023). IRB’s assessment in camps 2W and 2E highlighted that the current rate of 36% of desludging latrines is not sufficient. As a result, significant numbers of latrines remain unusable. According to WASH sector data, 38 % and 17% of female family members feel unsafe using the communal latrine during the night, respectively, for Camp 2W & and 2E. Considering the threat and concern of using the communal latrines, IRB plans to construct Women's hygiene centers and continue the operation and maintenance of existing Women's hygiene centers in their responsible camps. 64% of FSTPs do not meet acceptable COD standards in these camps. Repair and maintenance of sanitation facilities are the most urgent needs in camps 2W and 2E. In Camp 2E, 19 people use every functional latrine, whereas 18 people in Camp 2W. There is a need for functional latrines in these two camps. According to the WASH sectors monitoring report (May 2023), 44 % of HHs are using tap stands as a primary source of drinking water in Camp 2E and 45 % in Camp 2W. IRB will continue the operation and maintenance of existing water supply pipe networks and water points to ensure a safe water supply for the targeted FDMNs in Camp 2W and Camp 2E. According to MSNA, 12% and 39% of households have visible waste in the vicinity of their accommodation, respectively, at Camp 2W and 2E. IRB’s response will consider environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion, and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. Thus, IRB has provision for drainage cleaning and waste management in these camps, along with latrine desludging activity kept in the project. Besides these, there is a need for safe drinking water sources like DTW in Ukhia Upazila, sanitation facilities, and WASH blocks in host community schools. IRB will spend 25% of its budget on the WASH gap interventions in the host community under Ukhiya Upazila. 70% of HH are using tap stands as the primary source for drinking water. According to J MSNA findings, 86% of households reported having enough water to meet all their HH needs. An average of 20 liters of domestic water is collected per person daily in camps. 62% of female family members reported feeling safe using the communal latrine at night. 7% of the FDMN community reported that they have their own private latrine (among those, 65% of latrines built by the community, and 35% have privatized the communal latrine). Wash Sector will take the initiative to discuss the way forward regarding this issue within the small technical group. Based on the observation data, 71% of HH segregates waste at the HH level in Camps. According to the survey, the percentage of soap received is 90% (According to the last six months of data). However, all partners raised the issue that they would ensure 100% distribution. Sector focal &amp; AFA will focus more on it. Regarding the MHM kit distribution, only 62% have access to the MHM kit, but all wash partners have completed their distribution twice a year.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the JRP and Joint Need Assessment, Islamic Relief Bangladesh will assess the needs of the target population for JRP 2024 and deliver assistance in collaboration with the WASH sector, site management, and government. IRB team has made strong coordination with the WASH sector from the both technical and management levels, consulted with the sector lead for camp allocation, and attended online meetings at DPHE. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) will continue to work toward drinking water solutions to ensure health hygiene issues. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing hygiene kit packages to FDMN households in Cox’s Bazar. WASH support will include improved latrine designs with a focus on existing latrine repair and maintenance. The modalities chosen in this proposed project are developed and approved at the WASH Sector level and ISCG. The camp (Camp-2w & camp 2E) has been selected in consultation with the WASH sector, UNHCR, and camp-level actors. An integrated approach will be followed to ensure inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and delivery services to the FDMNs and the host community. This project is designed for 12 months but IRB has funding commitments from Islamic Relief Worldwide up to December 2024. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) has also secured funding from its partner countries IR USA and IR Canada for 2025 operation in FDMN camps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

During the project implementation, each action will be considered throughout implementation, some key actions are significant during emergencies and the project cycle's assessment and design stage. Ensure safe and appropriate locations and routes to WASH facilities in the selected camps to avoid potential threats of violence, abuse, or exploitation against girls, women, and children. Ensure availability of water, menstrual kits for girls and women with/without disabilities, and other hygiene kits based on the needs. Make infrastructure adaptations such as ramps, wide doors, handrails, sufficient space within the facilities, door locks for latrines, and other WASH facilities so that all individuals can access and use facilities in safety and dignity. Latrine design must preserve users' safety, privacy, and dignity, including women, girls, people with special needs, and people of gender-diversified groups. WASH facilities design must preserve the safety, privacy, and dignity of users, especially women, adolescent girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. During setting up WASH facilities, ensure community consultation to reduce community tension. Ensure no tension or inequality could lead to violence and harassment of one group. Promote women's hygiene center to ensure a conducive protection environment in WASH systems approach will integrate age, gender, and diversity principles throughout this WASH project period. We can establish the principles of "DO NO HARM" and "LEAVE NO ONE BEHIND" to all sorts of WASH sectoral activities. Ensure community-based protection initiatives and capacity development, Conduct awareness-raising pertinent to a wide range of existing protection issues that are closely related to camp-based WASH volunteers and most vulnerable excluded right holder’s categories of the community to get access to life-saving protection information and strengthening community structures though essential WASH assistance.

3.3 - Age & Disability

This project will ensure that the location of the water points and latrines are accessible for the FDMN community of the selected camps, considering the different gender identities, ages, disabilities, and backgrounds. Ensure that persons with disabilities (e.g. persons – adults and children - with physical disabilities, older people, individuals with severe functional difficulties persons with visual, speech, and hearing impairment, and persons with mental health conditions), and minority groups can access services. Ensure that beneficiaries know their right and necessity to safe drinking water, and where/how to obtain it. Ensure that you understand the barriers to access and design your program accordingly. Monitor and review access, discrimination, and whether water or other services are being diverted. Identify what are the power dynamics within the intervention area to assess the access to water resources, accessibility on decision making and the ability to influence decisions on water resources.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This WASH assistance project of IRB will ensure community consultation to consult for the inclusion of persons with disabilities and older persons. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) mainstreaming will be followed during this project implementation. To ensure transparency and accountability of relevant stakeholders, particularly beneficiaries, the project will apply the Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM). The camp field office will set up the complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) at the Field level. They will collect feedback from CFM boxes. The complaint box and festoon with Cell Phone numbers will be set in the targeted communities, sites, and public safer places in their own language. This mechanism will create opportunities for the beneficiaries/ stakeholders to come up with their concerns/complaints which will be considered by the project during/ before finalizing the selection of appropriate beneficiaries by maintaining an organizational complaint-resolving process. For effective CFM, a CFM box will be established at the Union Parishad complex, CIC office, Distribution point, camp-based market/main road for the refugee camp areas, and other important places for the host community. Besides, at the project office, it will use the local CFM register book. One cell number will also be given to each beneficiary and stakeholder and it will also be hung in different public places. Moreover, an orientation session will be provided to the selected beneficiaries on effective CFM.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This proposed project will contribute to emergency preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction support as well as climate change issues. WASH services are critical to communities and save lives during emergency situations. The project will provide emergency water supply sources if any disaster occurs. The project will ensure natural hazards that will not compromise WASH infrastructure and service delivery and this interruption of WASH services will decrease the number of affected people and damages beyond the area of direct impact. This project will provide attention through necessary DRR interventions. The project will protect WASH investments and integrate into ‘regular’ work to ensure new vulnerabilities are not introduced or reinforced through new or existing WASH services. The project has a provision of emergency response activities as rapid response. IRB has an emergency response team for conducting rapid damage assessment, physical verification, identifying the most vulnerable stakeholders, and providing appropriate and most affected FDMNs in our responsible catchments area. IRB has a dedicated volunteer team, staff, and skilled management to coordinate with sectors as well as the government.
