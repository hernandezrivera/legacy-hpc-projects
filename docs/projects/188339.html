Project Summary

The economic collapse and the significant reduction in Government’s social spending have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. Assessment data from August 2021 (MSNA) indicate that the income gap has continued to widen, with average household expenditure now exceeding income by 50%, compared to 20% in August 2020. Only 10% of households have an income above the cost of Syria’s Minimum Expenditure Basket.
Needs are particularly acute in urban and peri-urban slums where families, especially families headed by women, families with orphan children and IDP families are disproportionately affected, leaving them unable to provide for the basic needs of children. Needs are even more acute for these families at specific times during the year when they face growing expenses (e.g., during wintertime or prior to the school year). Post-Distribution Monitoring survey conducted in 2021 among beneficiaries of UNICEF multi-purpose cash interventions indicates much higher socio-economic vulnerability among female-headed households (FHH) than among male headed-households (MHH): over 40% of FHH were relying on charity, remittances or borrowing, compared to only 4% among MHH. The average monthly expenditure among FHH was 40% lower than among MHH.
To respond to this situation, UNICEF is implementing a Basic Needs Support Programme, which main objective is to support highly vulnerable families in urban and peri-urban areas respond to the essential needs of their children and help them cope with the growing socio-economic hardship at critical times during the year to reduce their negative coping mechanisms.
The programme has two components: (i) three-rounds of cash transfer to vulnerable families with children (0- 17 years old); and (ii) referral mechanism where the most vulnerable children and their families are accompanied and referred to social services in the communities. This referral component is critical to help families better respond to the needs of their children, beyond the cash transfer intervention, thus contributing to strengthen their resilience.
In 2021, UNICEF Post-distribution monitoring survey indicated that the top-ranked categories of expenditures with the cash transfer were as follows: 42% reported that the top category of expenditure was food, followed by 29% for clothing, 7% for medical expense, 6% for rent, 5% for debt repayment, and 5% for heating fuel.
In 2022 and 2023, the project aims to reach 30,000 families (over 150,000 people, including over 80,000 children) each year with cash assistance and referral services in urban and peri-urban slums in 5 Governorates.
Building on the partnerships established in 2020 with a large network of national NGOs, UNICEF will identify vulnerable families through three targeting methods: (i) geographical targeting, focusing on areas with high severity of needs; (ii) categorical targeting (families with children); (iii) vulnerability targeting (families with greater socio-economic vulnerability such as FHH and families with orphan children)
The programme strategy is in line with the Multi-Purpose Cash interventions falling under the Food Security and Agriculture; Shelter/ NFIs and WASH sectors result framework 2022. UNICEF closely coordinates with the CWG at national & sub-national levels
Accountability to beneficiaries’ is ensured through three main channels: (i) redress and grievance mechanisms; (ii) Post Distribution Monitoring Surveys (PDMS); (iii) on-site monitoring activities.
The project targets 30,000 families each year disaggregated as follows: (i) 31% IDPs, 30% host communities, 39% returnees; (ii) 52% females, 48% males; (iii) 5% persons with disabilities. In 2022, the average severity scale in the sub-districts where the programme will be implemented is 3.5.

4 - Needs

MSNA data (August 2021) shows that the average household expenditure (SYP 449,642) exceeded the average household income (301,377) by 49 %, compared to 20 % in 2020. The income deficit of households where the head is a person living with a disability including FHH (71 %) is significantly higher than that of MHH (46 %). Furthermore, income insufficiency is overall highest amongst IDP households residing outside of camps (58 %). Needs are particularly acute in urban and peri-urban slums where families, especially FHH, families with orphan children and IDP families are disproportionately affected, leaving them unable to provide for the basic needs of children. Needs are even more acute at specific times during the year when they face growing expenses (e.g., during wintertime or prior to the school year). PDM surveys conducted in 2021 among beneficiaries of UNICEF MPC interventions echo MSNA/HNO 2022 findings and indicate greater socio-economic vulnerability among FHH: over 40% were relying on charity, remittances or borrowing, compared to only 4% among MHH. The average monthly expenditure among FHH was 40% lower than among MHH. To respond to those needs, the project aims to reach 30,000 families (over 150,000 people, including over 80,000 children) each year with cash assistance and referral services in urban and peri-urban slums in 5 Governorates. Considering the continued deterioration of the socio-economic situation, needs in 2023 are expected to remain acute.

5 - Implementation of activities

The programme is implemented by national NGOs responsible for the identification, registration, verification of beneficiaries, evaluation of their eligibility, and provision of referral services. To ensure the integrity of the programme, there is a division of roles whereby UNICEF uses the services of a third-party financial service provider (FSP) to transfer the cash grants, while a national Implementing Partners implement the referral component. The main activities are:1- Training of field monitors and orientation of IPs on the programme’s objectives and criteria2- Communication campaigns in the selected areas with clear messages on eligibility and duration3- Identification and registration of beneficiaries through IPs existing registries, complemented by door-to-door outreach and home visits4- Verification of the family composition, children specific characteristics and needs 5- UNICEF field offices conduct regular assessments on the targeted locations to ensure access to markets6- Provision of three rounds of cash transfer (one every two months) through Financial Service Providers 7- Referral services to families through the duration of the cash assistance. It is the provision of social services to the most vulnerable families and strengthens their linkage with the official humanitarian actors and governmental entities. 8- Monitoring activities, including PDM, randomized verification of beneficiaries, and centralized grievance and redress mechanisms

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk # 1: Social tensions generated by lack of clarity on eligibility criteria, or harassment towards beneficiaries receiving the cash transfer. Likelihood (high) Mitigation measures: (i) clear communication strategy on eligibility criteria and selection modalities; (ii) strict verification mechanisms; (ii) continuous on-site monitoring; (iv) complaint and response mechanism (iv) rigorous monitoring schemes; (v) procedure for data protection developed by the IP; (vi) Field monitors ensure that eligible children are not excluded. Risk # 2: Abuse or exploitation by the IPs. Likelihood (Medium) Mitigation measures: (i) pre-screening of IPs and close monitoring of the implementation; (ii) IPs are required to develop an internal procedures to identify, track and respond to possible cases of abuse, exploitation or gender-based violence.; and (iii) UNICEF systematically train its IPs on prevention and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, as well as abuse of authority and conflict of interests. Risk # 3: Protection risks related to walking in a long and unsecured distance between the targeted locations and the FSP outlets. Likelihood (Medium) Mitigation measures: (i) coordination with the FSPs to expand their outlets to nearby locations to the targeted beneficiaries, (ii) coordination with the FSPs and IPs for a clear distribution plan and prior notification to beneficiaries, (iii) increase the duration of cash delivery from 24 hours to a week.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF has its PSEA policy in place and ensures establishing mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition,UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance the networking for the benefit of people at risk, and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness-raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in a dignified way is integrated in project activities. Consequently, as per-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plans are guaranteed. This includes providing technical support in the fields of Child Safeguarding, GBV, Code of Conduct and PSEA.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The overall socio-economic situation in the country continues to deteriorate, increasing the needs of targeted populations. Risk #1: Delays in obtaining official approvals can delay implementation likelihood (Medium) Mitigation measures #1: (i) development of standardized agreements with the IPs across targeted governorates to ensure speedy review; (ii) establish good communication channels with MoSAL officials to ensure all challenges are shared and addressed in a timely manner. Risk #2: The COVID-19 pandemic can slow down the implementation and affect the delivery of referral services likelihood (Medium) Mitigation measures #3: (i) closely monitor the situation of COVID-19; (ii) implement alternative ways of delivery of cash (i.e. ATM rather than cash over the counter); and (iii) providing PPE for all field monitors. Risk #3: High level of inflation limit the effectiveness of the cash component. likelihood (High) Mitigation measures #3: (i) negotiate with the Central Bank a preferential exchange rate; (ii) adjust transfer values on inflation Risk #4: Delay in the process of cash distribution to beneficiaries due to the limited absorption capacity of the FSPs likelihood (High) Mitigation measures #4: Expansion of the FSPs as feasible Risk #5: Difficulty to obtain data of eligible families as per the targeting criteria Likelihood (high) Mitigation measures# 5: Conduct door-to-door visits by the field monitors of IPs in the targeted locations.
