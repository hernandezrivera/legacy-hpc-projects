Project Summary

In the Far North region, the Boko Haram insurgency in Nigeria has resulted in 227,581 internally displaced persons (IDPs) (UNHCR, October 2018), who are struggling to survive in strenuous conditions in villages or small towns in the area. The region is predominantly arid and regularly hit by drought and seasonal floods. The arrival of IDPs in food-insecure areas has severely affected host communities as well. As a consequence, the existing chronic vulnerability and structural food insecurity have been aggravated. In addition, access to basic services is also more limited than in other regions of the country including the education.
WFP will implement a school feeding programme in the Far North region to complement the Government’s education inputs and its work in addressing emergency needs, and to support the education of children affected by crises, particularly girls. It will provide incentives for children to enroll in schools to minimize the risk of forced recruitment into armed groups. The school feeding programme will assist 80,000 school children – of whom 50 percent will be girls – for 165 school days in 12 months with flexibility to ensure that schools in crisis areas are targeted. Home-grown school feeding has been adjusted to 5000 to be piloted in line with recommendations of the Systemic Approach for Better Education Results (SABER). School voucher procurement will incentivize purchases from men and women smallholders to create a value chain.

Needs

The overall insecurity, the influx of Nigerian refugees, and the internal displacement have negatively affected the local communities and population since the outbreak of the Boko Haram insurgencies. In addition, local population have suffered from chronic poverty and natural disasters. The results of the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis (CFSVA), jointly conducted by the Government (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, or MINADER) and WFP in May 2017, revealed that 3.9 million Cameroonians are food insecure, and among them 211,000 are severely food insecure. The Far North region shows the highest figures, with 33.7% food insecure households (around 1,460,000 people), of which 3% are severely food insecure. Mayo-Tsanaga division has an alarming food insecurity rate (65.6 %), followed by Mayo-Sava (37.8%) and Logone-et-Chari with 13%. These high prevalence rates are the consequence of poverty (65%), high food expenditure (54.2%), and inadequate food consumption (36.2%).The results of the Food Security and Monitoring Survey (FSMS) indicate that, in the Far North region, the prevalence of food insecurity decreased from 20% in October 2017 to 11% in February 2018. This relative improvement is partly because the period of the survey coincided with the second cycle harvests, coupled with the provision of humanitarian food assistance. However, levels of severe food insecurity remain high and stable constant levels between the last two rounds of FSMS. For 24% of households in the Far North, at least 65% of expenditure is spent on food. In addition, among those 24%, 13% of households are extremely vulnerable economically so at least 75% of expenditure is spent on food). In addition, 7.6% of households sold animals in the Far North to address their food needs and 8% of households reduced their health and education expenses. In the Far North, the access to basic services including the education service is still limited and many children are out of schools due to the vulnerabilities of their households. WFP aims to support 80,000 schoolchildren in local primary schools with the school feeding as it will not only address the food and nutrition needs of the children but also will increase the attendance rate.
