Project Summary

PUI is proposing an emergency preparedness project to support 500 households (2,750 individuals, 1,357 females, 1,393 males, 118 persons with disabilities (PwDs)), affected by manmade and/or natural disasters, from Gaza Strip with restricted and tailored non-food item (NFI) e-vouchers assistance of an average value of 400 USD per household. The action is designed to mitigate the negative impact of shocks such as escalations of violence with Israel, internal conflicts, flooding, storms, earthquakes, etc. upon temporarily or permanently displaced households. According to the Cluster Standardized Shelter NFI packages and combined Kits, PUI will provide the targeted HHs with a Household Kits + clothes (HH kit: 315 USD+ clothes: 85 USD) The action will be fully coordinated with the shelter cluster, the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) and active members of the shelter cluster. The list of affected households will be obtained from the MoSD and/ or MoPWH and validated by the Shelter cluster. It is worth mentioning that this conditional NFI assistance is meant to be complementary to an MPCA assistance aimed at covering basic needs, which would be delivered by another humanitarian actor or by PUI trough another project.
The use of e-voucher modality aims at reaching the beneficiaries even in case of restrictions on movement due to security conditions in the Gaza Strip. The assistance will be delivered to the affected households through the PALPAY platform. It will also allow beneficiaries to purchase NFIs according to their preferences and priorities, taking into account the needs of individual with specific vulnerabilities within the HH (PwDs, babies/infants, pregnant and lactating women, older persons, etc.). Through PALPAY's platform, PUI will be able to identify the shops to be used for voucher redemption and control the items that are allowed to be purchased during the specified redemption period (one month) taking into consideration the specific needs of women, girls, and PwDs. It is worth noting that PALPAY has a network of 350 accredited agents and over 1,700 points of sale (shops) in the different governorates of the Gaza Strip. PUI, in collaboration with Palestinian Environmental Friends (PEF) and local CBOs, will conduct regular rapid market assessment and monitoring before, during, and after the intervention to check the availability of goods included in the vouchers, ensure the market is functioning and conduct price monitoring. PUI will ensure all items included in the Household kits (PK2: Bedding set, PK3: kitchen set, PK4: Washing set &PK5: cooking set+ lighting) are available in the selected shops. In addition, PUI will ensure that specific items needed for diverse groups (women, girls, infants, PWDs, etc.) are available in the selected shops.
The intervention comes in line with the HRP2023’s 3rd strategic objective: “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued” and contributes to the achievement of the shelter cluster’s 3rd objective: “Increase capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner”. The intervention will respond to the most urgent Shelter/NFI needs of the target group, thus improving their capacity to respond to other basic needs, and will contribute to better living conditions for vulnerable households affected by manmade and/or natural disasters.
Through this intervention, PUI will support its implementing partner PEF as well as local partner CBOs in developing their capacities and enabling them to intervene in emergency settings through tailored and timely CVA assistance. This will enhance their capacities to assess and respond to such emergencies in the future, thus strengthening the exit strategy of the proposed intervention.

1 - Needs

• Manmade disastersWhile the occupation remains a major driver of shelter needs in Gaza, the housing crisis is impacted by a combination of different factors; escalations, the blockade and the high costs of construction, and limited economic opportunities as a result of over 15 years of Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment, the deteriorating socio-economic situation, and recurring waves of hostilities.Due to the most recent escalation, in August 2022, around 1,500 housing units were damaged or destroyed, including 97 units severely damaged, and 26 units destroyed, leaving around 600 individuals still internally displaced. This is compounded by the remaining caseload of May 2021 escalation, where 62,000 housing units have been damaged or destroyed, out of which 916 housing units were subjected to severe damages and 1,688 housing units were destroyed and resulted in the displacement of 12,000 people which 7,000 remain displaced in addition to 3,000 people still displaced from previous escalations. Families, affected by escalations, usually flee with little more than the clothes they are wearing and consequently find themselves displaced, sometimes for several weeks or months, with little or no personal belongings. In addition to food, they urgently need certain non-food items to survive, including items for shelter (tents, plastic sheeting, blankets, sleeping mats), cooking (stoves, pots, fuel), and hygiene (buckets, soap, jerry cans, etc.). Moreover, household members with specific needs, such as babies/infants, women (including pregnant and lactating), older persons or PwDs are also left deprived of basic items needed to cater for their needs (diapers for infants, sanitary pads, assisting devices, etc.). • Natural disastersDuring the past decade, several heavy storms have pummeled the Gaza strip, causing localized flooding in multiple areas across the Gaza Strip. These include the Khan Younis, Rafah El-Sheikh Radwan, Nafaq, Manara and Saftawi areas in Gaza City; the Al-Amal area and a section of Salah ad-Din road in Khan Younis; the Abu Rashid area in Jabaliya; and the Mashrou’ area in Beit Lahiya.Additionally, the MSNA2022 WASH analysis revealed that 12% of Gaza households reported being affected by flooding in the 3 years prior to data collection (of these households 41.2% reported their shelter being impacted by flooding and 87.1% reported floods disrupting their daily activities).People in Gaza are unfortunately likely to continue to need emergency shelter and non-food items (NFIs) assistance as a result of repetitive displacement episodes following conflict escalations, as well as exposure to harsh weather conditions and protection concerns related to manmade and natural hazards. The needs are exacerbated by the imbalance in growing housing needs compared to the limited available resources, the blockade, and material restrictions imposed on Gaza. Such shocks affect diverse groups of population in different ways, and preexisting vulnerabilities are generally exacerbated. As such, HHs with PwDs as well as Female-headed households are disproportionately impacted by such shocks. It is also observed that poor housing conditions and damages caused by conflict disproportionately impact most socioeconomically vulnerable and marginalized households (UN Women Gender Needs assessment 2022).Maintaining an adequate stock level of NFIs is considered an absolute necessity in light of the manmade or natural disasters imposed on the Gaza Strip. According to the Shelter cluster strategic analysis, future possible scenarii in light of the continued instability include new escalations affecting the Gaza Strip, with potential displacement of a large numbers of people, forecasted to up to 600,000 individuals.

2 - Activities

AActivity 1: Provision of restricted, tailored and time-critical NFIs e-voucher to vulnerable and shock-affected households with a focus on IDPs. PUI will support 500 vulnerable IDP households (2,750 individuals, 1,357 females, 1,393 males), affected by natural or manmade shocks in the Gaza Strip, through providing restricted and tailored NFIs e-vouchers assistance through the PALPAY platform. In the context of this project, according to shelter cluster contingency plan, PUI will intervene in small and medium-scale emergencies. According to the shelter cluster, small-scale emergencies are defined as emergencies with a number of newly affected people ranging between 3,000 and 18,000 individuals, and where it is assumed that normal communication means (phone networks, Internet, etc.) are not largely interrupted. While medium-scale emergencies are defined as emergencies with a number of affected people between 18,000 and 60,000 individuals.The list of affected HHs will be obtained from the MoSD/MoPWH and validated by the Shelter cluster and PUI’s local partner through a random verification exercise. The action will also be fully coordinated with the shelter cluster, MoSD, MoPWH, and active members of shelter to avoid any overlapping or duplication in the provision of assistance. A priority will be given to households with specific vulnerabilities, such as female headed-households, households headed by PwDs or including PwDs, child-headed households, elderly-headed households, households with people with chronic disease, households with pregnant/lactating women, large households, etc. This conditional NFI assistance is meant to be complementary to an MPCA assistance, in order to ensure that the beneficiary households are able to cover their basic needs and can dedicate this conditional assistance to needed shelter/NFI items. This MPCA assistance could be delivered by another humanitarian actor or by PUI through another project, and would ideally be received before the NFI voucher. To this end, PUI will closely coordinate its action with other actors, relevant clusters/working groups and Ministries. If the situation allows, before the distribution, PUI and its partner PEF will conduct orientation sessions with the selected beneficiaries HHs explaining the project’s details, selection process and the provided assistance, introducing the protection mainstreaming principles and explaining the feedback and response mechanism (FRM) including complaints about SEAH. Arrangements will be made for head of households with specific needs such as FHH, PwD, older or chronically-ill HoH, to ensure they have access to the information (through door-to-door visits, phones calls, etc.).Each beneficiary household will then receive an e-voucher with an average value of 400 USD to buy the needed non-food items (NFIs) according to their preferences and priorities. According to the Cluster Standardized Shelter NFI packages and combined Kits, this amount will correspond to a Household NFI Kit + clothes (HH kit: 315 USD + clothes: 85 USD). According to PUI and PEF’s previous experience, 85 USD (300 ILS) is a sufficient amount to cover basic clothes needs for a family of 5 in emergency context (focusing on basic underwear + children clothes). The PALPAY's platform will enable PUI to identify the shops to be used for voucher redemption and control the items that are allowed to be purchased based on the nature of the emergency and the actual needs. In addition, the PALPAY’s wide network will ensure a timely response and easier access to affected households even in case of lockdowns, curfews, and/or movement restrictions which could be imposed in Gaza Strip during the different emergencies. PALPAY has a network of 350 accredited agents and over 1,700 points of sale (shops) in the different governorates of the Gaza Strip. PUI will ensure that PALPAY services are accessible to persons with disabilities (whether physical, sensorial or mental), as well as to illiterate persons. PUI will also ensure that protection and notably SEAH risks in accessing services for diverse age groups are properly assessed and mitigated, in particular in regards to FHH, PwD headed-HH, etc.PUI, in collaboration with its partner Palestinian Environmental Friends (PEF) and local CBOs, will conduct a rapid market assessment before the intervention to check the availability of goods included in the vouchers, and ensure the market is functioning. Notably, PUI will ensure all items included in the Household kits (PK2: Bedding set, PK3: kitchen set, PK4: Washing set &PK5: cooking set+ lighting) are available in the selected shops. In addition, a regular price monitoring exercise will be carried out on monthly basis throughout the whole intervention to ensure that there are no significant changes in the prices. PUI will make sure all needed NFI items for women, girls, children, PWDs and other groups with specific needs remain available in selected shops. Through AGD-sensitive consultations with beneficiaries held before the release of the assistance, PUI/PEF will collect detailed information on specific preferences and needs for the different types of shelter/NFI items, and coordinate with selected shops to try and ensure that those are available as much as possible.PUI will conduct orientation meetings with the selected beneficiaries to provide detailed information on the project’s activities, timeline, selection criteria and feedback and complaints mechanism (FRM). Additionally, PUI protection officer will explain PUI’s PSEAH policies complaint mechanism. During these sessions, PUI will share the details of the contact persons for PSEAH, distribute the hand out FRM leaflets and cards which include the contact information in case of facing a misconduct/misbehavior from the humanitarian worker or the community. Moreover, protection staff will place PSEAH awareness printouts and the FRM mechanism posters on local partner’s advertisement boards.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Evidences showed that beneficiaries of humanitarian aid projects generally prefer voucher assistance over in-kind distribution of NFIs. The modality gives the target BNFs greater flexibility and freedom of choice in the purchase of the needed NFIs according to their priorities and preferences. Also, it contributes to enhancing the sense of dignity. Moreover, the use of voucher modality in GS context, where goods are available, and markets are functioning, is important and plays a vital role in revitalizing and boosting the local economy especially in light of the chronic cash liquidity issues. The voucher also ensures the attainment of the intended objectives of humanitarian assistance through restricting to some degree what recipients can buy or where they can make their purchases.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s complaints and feedback mechanisms is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. It provides unique and invaluable sources of information to be used for better project management and outcomes. It can help identify and address mistakes, shortcomings, improving the quality of the intervention or project. For example, the idea of increasing the number of installments provided to the vulnerable households was a result of the collected feedback and recommendations raised by former beneficiaries in similar PUI interventions. In the same way, the choice of distributing cash rather than in-kind derives from beneficiary feedback, as it provides them the flexibility in the management of their expenditures. The MEAL team will strive to ensure that feedback, whether positive or negative, will collected and followed up on by PUI staff independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be shared with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Officer and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises. Project modalities or objectives will be discussed with the program and technical team, in order to better respond to the needs and concerns of the served population.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

PUI uses various two-way communication channels: oral, written, easy to read, face to face, via email and through meetings; to ensure that feedback and complaints mechanism is easily accessible by all beneficiaries including people with specific needs such as PwDs and children. These include (but are not limited to) feedback boxes, hotline number, email, and the pages of the organization on social media. PUI designed a kids-friendly interactive hand-out card including the hotline number, drawings, and painting exercises to increase awareness regarding their rights, and to encourage them to complain if they faced a misconduct/misbehavior from humanitarian workers or the community. Additional publications were designed to the same purpose i.e. posters and leaflets.During the beneficiary selection processes, orientation meetings with the beneficiaries, and the field visits, PUI’s team systematically provides detailed information on the FRM, sharing the details of the contact person FRM leaflets are handed out and the process is also explained orally, to account for those who cannot read. These meetings/visits will follow an AGD-sensitive approach to ensure that all population groups, and notably women, have access to the needed information. Also, PUI tries to disseminate the information of the FRM through local partner CBOs and key community leaders, handing out cards, posters, and other means of information dissemination regarding PSEAH and the FRM mechanism, ensuring cultural appropriateness and inclusivity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may also be provided in the field, through text messages, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number). Moreover, diversity among PUI staff in terms of gender will allow an inclusive and gender-sensitive approach, where beneficiaries complaints will be handled by same-gender staff in order to encourage the most vulnerable to share their concerns. The MEAL team will be providing an initial feedback in less than 24 hrs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEAH is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. Moreover, PUI will provide targeted PSEAH sessions to project personnel, partner staff, service providers ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project.• Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed (i.e. the Protection Cluster Service Directory) and complemented by other resources identified by PUI if needed. Furthermore, all PUI field staff have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partner, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people (for instance by including the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities in its questionnaires to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities); conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes the Disability Mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, conducting inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
