Project Summary

In 2022, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) continue to face multi-faceted crises which are worsened by conflict, floods, impacts of COVID – 19 pandemic, and protracted displacement from locations of their habitual residence. Displaced families often have inadequate shelter and household goods which expose them to heightened protection risks. Increased mobility, continued insecurity and protracted displacement has over the years eroded household coping mechanisms and livelihood options especially considering the poor nature of local markets without stocks for household goods and shelter materials.
UNHCR’s project will focus on responding to the humanitarian needs of IDPs and IDP and refugee returnees with targeted and protection focused core relief items (emergency shelter and non-food items – S/NFIs) prioritizing the most vulnerable households and persons with specific needs especially women and girls.
The project will contribute to the following sectoral objectives:
SO 1 – percentage of the targeted population that is able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition.
The project will identify the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees through protection monitoring and assessments including through IRNAs and RRMs. The assessments and inter cluster discussions will inform the decisions regarding the specific response modalities between in kind and cash assistance. Unique contextual factors including market conditions will be considered.
SO 2 - Enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI services.
The project will deliver timely lifesaving activities while streamlining protection-oriented initiatives including COVID – 19 preventive measures. The project will strengthen community-based protection through the involvement of community based structures for self-management in response delivery, ensuring that their capacities to cope, recover, contribute to and achieve solutions are enhanced.
SO 3 - Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures.
In 2022, UNHCR will increase its active participation in humanitarian coordination forums including the ES/NFI cluster both at national level and in the field, strongly advocating for protection mainstreaming and joint vulnerability assessments, the results of which will be very useful in the optimization of the limited resources. UNHCR will endeavor to provide timely response to the most critical identified needs of vulnerable IDPs and returnees, among other affected populations. Protection risks remain high among women, girls, boys and persons with specific protection needs which may include transitional shelters and other material assistance.

1 - Needs

Continued displacements as a result of subnational violence hampers sustainable strengthening of households coping mechanisms to various man made and natural shocks. In 2021, South Sudan experienced severe floods comparable to the unprecedent flooding of 1962. The floods resulted in damages to houses and livelihoods which has affected around 780,000individuals in many locations across the country. individuals and households in Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei states were the worst affected. The COVID 19pandemic worsened the situation of vulnerable households especially IDPs and returnees. Much like the rest of the world, the direct and indirect impacts of COVID – 19 aretaking numerous manifestations which include shortages of commodities in the local markets and weakened capacity to spend. These shocks continue to drive the need for various forms of humanitarian assistance including emergency shelter and NFIs. Localized and intermittent sub-national violence continues in many counties resulting in affected households losingtheir shelters and household property as well as new displacements. Continuedviolence from one year to the next is creating increased dependence on humanitarian assistance to provide emergency shelter and temporary solutions. While there are pockets of hope for durable solutions for IDPs and returnees in locations affected by intermittent violence, there remains huge needs for transitionalshelters and rehabilitation of damaged vital infrastructures which areimportant in building community resilience. In 2022, country wide return movements will revolve around progress in the implementation of the peace agreement and presence of humanitarian and development actors in areas of return. However, sporadic and area specific spontaneous returns and relocation movements will continue. Support for solutions and building community resilience to shocks will remain challenging and urgent in many of the return locations. Up to 88%of IDPs live in host communities and spontaneous settlements, some of which are in hard-to-reach locations with limited infrastructure and services.

2 - Project activities

Improve access of persons with special needs (PSNs) to emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs through in-kind/cash assistance. UNHCR will target vulnerable new IDPs and returnees. - Conduct protection, needs and market assessment, verification and NFIs distributions for vulnerable households in targeted areas. - Engage in joint needs assessments and gaps analysis - Support persons of concern with in kind standard emergency and transitional shelters and lifesaving NFIs.- UNHCR will use the Humanitarian hubs for the distribution of NFIs.- Conduct local market analysis on availability of materials - Support persons of concern with flood kits- Support persons of concern with cash in hand for emergency shelters depending on market conditions.- Mainstream AAP through IDPs, returnees consultation; in interagency participatory assessment (PA). - UNHCR will continue to assess the HLP and conflict sensitive issues with focus on protection response to support the most vulnerable IDP and returnee families to reduce the protection risks and associated vulnerabilities. - Strengthen coordination with ES-NFI cluster and other partners on ground to ensure protection mainstreaming in overall interventions process. - UNHCR will continue evidence-based programming to assist the vulnerable households, including responses from feedback mechanisms - Reporting on activities including post-monitoring missions - UNHCR will continue to advocate for Protection mainstreaming awareness raising in the community through their partner organizations. - UNHRC will continue to strengthen the area-based coordination to assess the protection risks and needs of returnees through co-leading solutions working groups at state and national level. - UNHCR and partners will further strengthen and expand its outreach activities in line with the IDP protection strategy and the needs. - UNHCR through an expanded operational coverage will ensure protection monitoring and participation in RPAs, IRNA and RRM. - Ensure regular inter-cluster dialogue for context appropriate response

3 - Project indicators

UNHCR will make available the required disaggregated population data and profile, sector data and conduct routine collection of data for project indicators to inform protection analysis and successful evidence-based decision making in project implementation. Protection monitoring and participatory assessments will inform a robust situation analysis and ensure targeted population, including those with specific needs and the most vulnerable, are consulted during the design, implementation and follow-up of the activity. Rights-based and the Age, Gender, and Diversity Approach principles are applied throughout the programme cycle to ensure that the voice, needs and feedback of persons of concern, particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities, women and children are considered, and those gaps, challenges and solutions are identified. Various community outreach channels and communication modalities will be used to ensure accountability to affected communities in the response. This includes feedback and complaint desks, hotlines, focus discussion groups, community-based networks, and community coordination meetings.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance provides flexibility in accessing and prioritizing materials and services in circumstances where persons of concern may have no alternatives particularly when displaced. Cash assistance is a more dignified form of assistance that gives displaced persons the ability to immediately prioritize and choose most urgently goods and services. IDPs and returnees face heightened protection risks with weakened coping mechanisms and livelihood options which can be complemented by cash. Additionally, cash can be used in many settings where markets are functional. As such, UNHCR will review JMMI reports in addition to carrying out localized market assessments to determine functionality of markets before using the cash response modality. Therefore the use of cash assistance response will be dependent on the operational context and feasibility assessments.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market functionality is considered a key component in project design particularly to inform on socio- economic conditions in a given area, determine potentiality for sustainable returns and enable designing of solutions-oriented projects. This is captured during the multi sector needs assessments, or through the community-based networks that provides information on viability upon which the context and design is adopted. The project design and CVA considerations will be adapted accordingly to the current context and feasibility.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

An annual participatory assessments exercise is undertaken using the Age, Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all project locations or implementation areas. The PA outcome sets out the agenda for program planning and design. It is a feedback session engaging the communities which involves representatives from all target populations including youth groups, community leaders, women groups and persons with disabilities for feedback on implemented projects and provided UNHCR resource for advocacy and review if any of its project implementation based on the needs of the persons of concern.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNHCR's age, gender and diversity (AGD) policy is at the forefront of each intervention as it ensures that all persons of concern fully participate in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. Coupled with a community-based approach, through consultation and participation, communities engage meaningfully and substantively in all programmes that affect them and play a leading role in change. Through these networks, two-way communication will be engaged to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNHCR has a zero tolerance policy with regards sexual exploitation and abuse and has clear communication and messaging strategy to inform persons of concern that will be absorbed in to the project. UNHCR has systematically integrated the issue of PSEA in its information campaigns, trainings and meetings with PoC’s and applied through the AGD lens. A PSEA focal person is usually appointed and discreet information sharing lines made available.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

UNHCR actively promotes the consultation and participation of communities in all programmes that affect them and play a leading role in change. As such, communities are meaningfully and substantively engaged to ensure enhanced impact of interventions in the lives of persons of concern. UNHCR incorporates the knowledge and resources of communities to achieve a holistic intervention which ultimately strengthens their capacities. If communities affected by crises are empowered, they are in a stronger position to protect and support their families, promote social cohesion and peaceful coexistence with host communities, respond to the aspirations of young people, and rebuild their lives. Feedback and complaint mechanisms, community-based networks, coordination meetings, and regular meetings with IDP leadership and communities have been rendered crucial. Setting up via protection or complaints desks provides the needed feedback and complaint mechanisms in order to ensure a two-way channel which maintains the integrity of persons of concern likewise providing feedback for appropriate action. There will be hotlines in addition to complaint boxes depending on location, which feedback on the modality and items disbursed can be given.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNHCR undertakes protection and risk assessments ahead of project implementation directly or through partners and in some instances through the multi sector approach. Many of the risks in the targeted locations are identified and mitigation measures taken. UNHCR and partners works to mitigate risks on PSEA and SGBV at project design stage incorporating the zero tolerance element. Annual participatory assessments are undertaken across all project locations using the Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming methodology. Feedback sessions engaging communities including youth groups, community leaders, women groups and persons with disabilities for discussions on implemented projects and needs. These sessions provide UNHCR and partners resource for advocacy and review of projects implemented to adjust to changing needs.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will ensure that persons with specific needs and vulnerable IDPs and returnees access cash cash assistance, where market functionality allows, and non food items in a dignified and timely way. The project will work closely with community self management structures which are often part of the GBV referral pathway not only acting as conduits of GBV information about services but also important sounding boards within communities regarding availability and accessibility of GBV services.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate induced shocks which are increasing in both severity and frequency, especially floods, affect and effect displacements across many parts of the country. Increasing communities and household resilience to such shocks through support to construction of more durable households in addition to tailor made flood response kits will contribute towards the reduction of flood related displacements in 2025.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project will ensure that IDPs and returnees benefit from improved access to basic household services which are important in strengthening coping capacities. Support to households especially affected by unprecedent floods will contribute towards community resilience to natural shocks which are increasingly induced by the changing climate. Reduction in the number of people with significant food consumption gaps will be driven by an enhanced focus on cash assistance to increase household spending capacity which provides households with flexibility to meet the most pressing needs while gradually addressing long standing issues.
