Project Summary

The intervention will be aligned with the framework of the strategic objective 1,2,3 of the HRP 2021 which is designed to complement the efforts of the Nutrition sector to target the most vulnerable and sever areas . The project seeks to implement and support life-saving services for the treatment of acute malnutrition in children under five and vulnerable pregnant or lactating women (PLWs) in Areesha camp, Qamishli city, Dir ez zour city , Izraa, Rural Damascus (Az-Zabdani and Madaya cities) Homs and Hama, but also some of the key determinants of malnutrition through promotion of improve infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and healthy behaviors linked to unnecessary deaths amongst vulnerable populations in the mentioned areas. By integrating CMAM program into the already established PHC clinic of the implementing partner in the target areas supported by outreach team to screen and identify cases of malnutrition among boys, girls and women amongst IDPs and host community. Also by establishing a CMAM fixed centers in the target area supported by outreach team of trained volunteers of the implementing partner, so unmet needs of the acutely malnourished boys, girls and women amongst the vulnerable returnees, refugees and IDPs in the target areas can be served. The project plans to conduct focused trainings for the local partners volunteers and MoH to build their capacity, to provide structured CMAM and IYCF services and activities to those in need, case management and referral, capacity building activities to local partners through the training of the volunteers and community members, and support facility and community based 1-1 counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices. divided into urban and rural areas, coordination with the existing partners (NGOs) and local partners will be to identify and divide the sectors of outreach activities. Regarding the treatment , Local partner’s centres are located in the target areas and they are accessible for beneficiaries according to Sphere standards. As well as distribution of breastfeeding kits ,new born baby kits for PLWs, in addition to the distribution of the PPEs ( personal protection equipment) on volunteers, staff and beneficiaries as protective measurement of working in COVID outbreak.
All activities will be coordinated with the Nutrition sector and working groups to avoid any overlapping or duplication in activities, since TdH is an active member of Nutrition sector.

4 - Needs

Chronic malnutrition remains one of the major public health and development concerns in Syria. Poor nutrition situation is largely attributed to the continued exposure of children and mothers to unsafe living conditions, diseases (including COVID-19),suboptimal IYCF practices, high maternal malnutrition, economic downturn, low purchasing power, poverty, food insecurity, family separations and poor care practices, early marriages, and destruction of infrastructure causing limited access to health services and depletion of skilled human resources, and inadequate sanitation and water supply.Based on HNO2021,almost 90,000 children under 5 years in Syria are acutely malnourished. Malnourished children with complications have increased two-fold in some sub-districts and more stabilization centers are needed. Almost one in three pregnant women are anemic while maternal malnutrition remains a major public health and nutrition concern in Syria leading to poor intrauterine growth high risks during pregnancy and childbirth complications. It is estimated that more than 0.2million mothers are malnourished and an additional0.9million women at child-bearing age are suffering from anemia of various reasons.Malnutrition in all its forms, requires immediate, comprehensive, and multi-sectoral services to fully address the nutritional needs of pregnant and lactating women and children in Syria.The depreciation of the Syrian pound and deteriorating household economy aggravate the nutrition situation of mothers and children. Based on the severity scale (3-4), nutrition humanitarian services are needed across the following areas, Qamishly, Areesheh, Deir-Ez-Zor, RD (Madaya, Zabadani, Tal, Abrin), Homs, Hama, and the surrounding areas of Izr

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-PR1: The beneficiaries may have no access to the centers . M1: TdH will consider this point upon the selection of the center location and specifications in addition to the involvement of women and girls in the activities timing and center shifts to ensure their accessibility during the day.PR2: The volunteers may be not understand the cultural context .M2: TdH will ensure female-to female communication, and male to male communication between staff and beneficiaries to respect the cultural background of the area of intervention and improving access to services, majority of the volunteers in IYCF & CMAM are females for the sensitivity of the topics , in addition TdH will ensure that majority of staff and volunteers will be selected from the same area of intervention to have community acceptance, and culture background.PR3: COVID outbreak during the kits distribution M3: TdH will provide PPEs to staff and beneficiaries during the distribution, and will ensure dividing the beneficiaries into different shifts and groups to avoid any potential gathering PR4: abuse from staff to beneficiary during the project implementation.M4: all TdH staff and local partner volunteers will undergo a CP training, and PSEA training, in addition, all of them should sign the code of conduct, in addition to the assignment of the CP committee on field level and office level for reporting and response.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities.M1: SARC and SCFPA displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations, However, if SARC or SCFPA lost the access to project locations, TDH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with local actors, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue.- R2: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities> M2: TDH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TDH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU.- R3: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria.M3: TDH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources.- R4: new quarantine by the local authorities due to COVID outbreak .M4: TdH has on-line policy for approvals and working from home, also, the PHCs will follow COVID measurement protocols , in addition, TdH health Advisor will conduct training to TdH staff and local partner volunteers for COVID measurements during humanitarian response and will distribute PPEs to both beneficiaries and staff.
