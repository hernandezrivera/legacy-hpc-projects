Project Summary

The combined internal political divide, the blockade and restrictions imposed by Israel, periods of conflicts and the systematic denial of Gazans’ rights have put the Gaza Strip territory and its population into a protracted crisis for more than a decade. Added to the protracted crisis, an acute crisis occurred with the Great March of Return, since March 30th 2018. As of 22 September, 191 people have been killed and 20,833 injured by the Israeli forces . According to the HNO 2019 of the Protection Cluster, 295 913 children are acutely vulnerable, 3540 children have been affected by the GRM-related violence while 1845 were injured. Among the children, children with disabilities are barely visible while disability remains source of vulnerability, with a poor access to services including health, education mental health and psychosocial. Girls are also at high risk of gender-based violence due to restrictive gender norms of the broader community. The security, political and economic situation have a great immediate and long term impact on children and their families whose resilience capacities affected negatively.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on HI’s rapid needs assessments that showed a lack of quality and accessible protection and MHPSS services; while the children affected by conflict-related violence – and their caregivers, are facing high psychological distress; among children, girls and children with disabilities are the most vulnerable ones.
The proposed project falls under the multiyear HRP strategic objectives 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”; and 3: ‘The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. It further falls under the strategic objective 2 of the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan of the Protection Cluster for the occupied Palestinian Territories, which is “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.”
The project aims to ensure access to quality protection and MHPSS services to children affected by conflict related violence with a focus on children with disabilities and girls. To do so, the project will be based on a two pronged approach. First, personal support will be provided to children affected by the conflict in particular children with disabilities, with a focus on protection and MHPSS quality services (individual and collective psychological care, assistive devices, case management and referral). On the other hand, the project will mainstream MHPSS and mental health services to develop inclusive environment to respond to the individualized and specialized needs of children affected by conflict, particularly the specific needs of girls and children with disabilities. The implementation method relies on strong partnership with local organizations which will be financially and technical supported by HI and on coordination with relevant actors.

Needs

The combined internal political divide, the blockade and restrictions imposed by Israel, periods of conflicts and the systematic denial of Gazans’rights have put the Gaza Strip territory and its population into a protracted crisis for more than a decade. This crisis is characterized by a collapsing economy with a poverty rate of 53 % an unemployment rate of 53.7 % and an erosion of infrastructures, basic services and people’s coping mechanisms. The situation in Gaza is complicated by the multiplicity of issues affecting people hence resulting into the suffering of people on the psychological level; all were considered as external factors affected the PSS well-being of people. In 2017, UNRWA’s study revealed that 48.9% of the adult persons’sample was identified as having poor PSS well-being, and 62.8% of the same sample would warrant depression screening. Likewise, among students, younger children in particular appear to be experiencing serious difficulties (30%). According to the HNO 2019 of the Protection Cluster, 52,098 persons – including 26,049 children – have MHPSS problems linked to the GMR, on top of 210,000 adults and 295,913 children already acutely vulnerable by end of 2017. The violence at the fence and rounds of hostilities continue to affect children with cumulative 30 fatalities, 3,540 injured, including 1,523 children hospitalized. Thus, we observe increasing MHPSS consequences from violence on adults and children; up to 26,049 children in need of MHPSS expected to grow as symptoms usually appear sometime after the events. This in addition to over 295,000 children already in need of MHPSS support In December 2017 a rapid assessment on child protection in Gaza was implemented by TdH-Swiss and indicated that 96% of key informants across Gaza strip reported having observed manifestations of PSS distress in children. Out of those key informants who perceive inequality in children’s access to services, in particular 50.6% stated that children with disabilities have a reduced access to services . The data collected in the frame of an ongoing emergency project implemented by HI for persons with injuries indicate that 80% of persons with injuries have difficulties to emotionally manage their feeling of experience, 214 children at school age having difficulties to move, see, hear, speak and speak and/or to emotionally manage their feeling of experience. Under its project ongoing addressing needs of rehabilitation to persons with injuries due to the GRM, Through 5 referral networks supported by HI technical and financial support and managed by 4 local partners in the 5 governorates of the Gaza Strip, children and persons with disabilities or injuries are having access to different services including health, education, PSS and legal aid – while several existing mechanisms require stronger coordination among actors and technical support. HI has conducted a rapid assessment in May 2018. It showed that: MHPSS services are largely inaccessible; MHPSS professionals have limited capacities to deal with persons or children with disabilities, and negative family and community attitudes towards disability in particular cognitive and intellectual disabilities. Among the children, children with disabilities are barely visible while disability remains source of vulnerability. The Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics’ census of 2017 indicated that 6.8% of Gaza Strip populations have some kind of disability. Women, girls and boys with disabilities, in particular those who have intellectual and mental disabilities, are facing socio-economic barriers to access MHPSS services due to double social stigma of access such services and the stigma of disability. There is gap in knowledge and skills of MHPSS service providers to respond to the urgent needs of persons with disabilities.The restrictive gender norms of the broader community means girls with disabilities are often denied their right to access services and the extreme stigma.

Activities

The overall objective of the project is to support protection mechanism in order to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence for children. To do so, the action will be using a two pronged approach which includes responsive, remedial and resilience activities to reduce the vulnerability and risks behind exclusion of children with disabilities from accessing services. The first component will provide conflict affected children with structured individual and group psychosocial services and their caregivers with peer to peer psychosocial support. Children with disability will receive assistive devices to have better access to PSS services, and those who cannot access center based MHPSS services will be provided with outreach services. The second component will provide mainstream PSS and mental health service providers with technical support to be able to respond to the needs of affected children through development of adapted ways and tools of intervention benefiting children with and without disabilities. Service providers will get necessary tools and materials. All children will also be supported to access other services, based on holistic case management and strengthened referral pathways and creation of welcoming and accessible environments for all. The project will be implemented in partnership with three local community-based organizations and in close coordination with CP MHPSS WG. The M&E system will monitor the project’s progress through a project quarterly comprehensive review; narrative and financial reports of the local partners, field visits. A final project evaluation will be conducted. Community including PSS staff, parents and children with and without disabilities will be engaged in the project monitoring and decision making through satisfaction/impact surveys and focus groups, representation in the projects’ steering committees, trained parents will be engaged by facilitating and leading peer to peer sessions for others. Expected Result 1: Protection and MHPSS needs of children including children with disabilities due to conflict in Gaza are adequately addressed by CBOs and service providers. 1.1 Provision of structured group and individual PSS sessions targeting children with and without disabilities and their care givers. 1.2 Provision of outreach PSS services for children with disabilities in particular girls and their caregivers living in ARA and facing barriers to access center based services. 1.3 Provision of mobility assistive devices for children with disabilities/injuries due to GMR or excluded from accessing services to enable them to access PSS and mental health services. 1.4 Conduct outdoor inclusive recreational activities including children with and without disabilities and their caregivers to support the changing of community negative attitudes towards disability. 1.5 Provision of technical support for child protection workers and their organizations on how to adapt the response ways and tools to be responsive to the needs of Children with and without disabilities. Expected Result 2: Needs of conflict affected children in particular children with disabilities mainstreamed in the humanitarian responses of PSS and mental health stakeholders. 2.1 Adapt the physical environment of 3 local partners to allow for children with different types of disabilities to have improved access to inclusive psychosocial services. 2.2 Raise awareness of children’ caregivers and community members on issues related to disability with special focus on protection and psychosocial needs of women and girls with disabilities. 2.3 Strengthen the links between the existing referral networks and case management pathways to provide holistic services for children with and without disabilities. 2.4 Technically support the mainstreaming of children with disabilities needs to the inter-agency coordination of psychosocial support via the Child Protection and MHPSS working groups and clusters.
