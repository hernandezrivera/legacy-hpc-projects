Project Summary

The proposed project aims to respond to the urgent health care needs of internally displaced persons (IDP/ Personne Déplacée Interne) and to reduce morbidity and mortality among 6-59 months children with SAM in 16 health areas in the Niono district in the Segou Region of Mali.
As for the IDPs, mobile clinics will provide free and quality primary health care to the displaced persons, mobile teams will remain flexible and adapt their interventions in the event of IDP settlements in new sites or the outbreak of a situation requiring an emergency response. ALIMA/AMCP-SP will set up three mobile clinic teams to provide better access to IDP sites, each team consisting of 3 staff (1 nurse, 1 midwife and 1 caregiver). Additionally, 1 community health worker (CHW) per IDP site will be trained by ALIMA/AMCP-SP. These teams will be supervised by a medical referent (responsible for the project's medical activities), based in Niono. Health care will be tailored to the needs identified for each site, including those of pregnant women and children under 5 years of age, who are most vulnerable to the living conditions on the IDPs sites. ALIMA/AMCP-SP curative activities will be combined with preventive activities to reduce vulnerability, such as prenatal consultations for pregnant women, malnutrition screening for children under 5 years of age, routine Extended Program on Immunization (EPI) for children aged 0-11 months and training of women in malnutrition detection techniques using the MUAC (Middle-Upper Arm Circumference) by mother approach, in order to detect malnutrition earlier and more effectively and prevent complications for their children, as well as epidemiological surveillance. Assisted deliveries and complicated deliveries will be referred to the relevant health facilities (Centre de Santé Communautaire and Centre de Santé de Référence) so as not to destabilize the district's health care cost recovery system.
Regarding nutrition activities, ALIMA/AMCP will support 16 CSCom and 1 CSREF (16 ATFC and 1 ITFC) in the Niono District with the management of outpatient children with SAM, according to the actual PCIMA protocol, and the community sector will be strengthened, with a large-scale MUAC by Mothers training campaign for childbearing aged women, re-dynamization and training ReCos (Community Relays) on MUAC by mothers approach, and on defaults and drop-out rates monitoring.
In case of emergency, ALIMA/AMCP-SP will be able to quickly assess needs and react accordingly.

1 - Contexte

According to the December 31st 2019 report of the Commission on Population Movements (CMP), a total of 207 751 internally displaced persons were recorded in Mali as of December 2019, compared to 120,298 on the same period in December 2018 (i.e. an increase of 72%). In the Ségou region, 24 291 displaced persons are registered in February 2020, 56% of whom are settled in the Niono circle (11,360 IDPs). However, ALIMA/AMCP-SP team and the Niono Social Development Services (SDS) carried out an evaluation mission of IDP sites as part of the implementation of the OFDA-funded Niono IDP Assistance Project, which identified 80 settlement sites with 17,173 IDPs in 12 municipalities of the Niono health district (HD/ District Sanitaire). No new assessments could be carried out, but internal displacement continued to increase in Niono Circle. These displaced populations are fleeing inter-community violence in the Macina circle (Segou region) and in the Mopti region (Tenenkou and Youwarou circle), seeking refuge in the Niono circle. IDPs who arrived in Niono settled in small sites near the villages, grouped according to their community affiliation. Their living conditions are very precarious: valuables have been abandoned and displaced people are living in vulnerable and precarious shelters. Access to food is difficult for this population, whose resources are limited. The situation of internally displaced persons is particularly dire, notably regarding health care. The ALIMA/AMCP-SP assessment indicates that their living conditions expose them to respiratory and skin diseases as well as waterborne diseases. Children under 5 years of age are particularly vulnerable to these diseases. According to the September 2019 SMART survey, the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in Segou region is estimated at 1.3% for 2019 (but the upper band indicates 2,6%). It should be noted that in the current ongoing intervention this is a passive screening carried out only using the MUAC measure with a Shakir band (MUAC), unlike the SMART survey, which is based on MUAC and weight/height ratio (Z-scores) screening. Thus, the result of the SMART survey of September 2019 gives a prevalence of SAM by MUAC of 1% and 1,73% respectively at the weight/height ratio in the Ségou region. In view of these results, it is likely that if A/A used both measurement techniques (MUAC and P/T Z-scores ratio) among displaced persons, the prevalence of SAM in these populations would be higher than the SMART average prevalence in the Segou region, or even similar to the figures found by Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) in April 2017 (by 3.5%). As mentioned above, the nutritional situation in the Ségou region is very serious. The Niono HD, has a population of 526,662 inhabitants, including 98,486 children under 5 years of age (18.7% of the population). The number of SAM cases expected throughout 2020 is 3,183 children, including 2,744 in outpatient care (ATFC) and 439 inpatient care (ITFC). During the first quarter of 2019, the Niono Health District recorded 319 admissions out of the 1,447 expected in the 25 ATFC (21.5% coverage) and 26 admissions out of the 221 expected in the CSRef ITFC (11.7% coverage), thus reflecting very low coverage in the management of SAM cases in the Niono Health District. This low coverage is particularly linked to the problem of access to care for children under 5 years of age, a lack of screening, the inactivity of community relays, the lack of support for care structures (even if MSF-B, ICRC and Solidarités International support some health area, not all are covered), repeated nutritional intakes disruptions, and the population impoverishment due to the intercommunity conflict (overloading local people, who must help the displaced). Moreover, the ITFCis no longer supported by TdH and is suffering from a lack of HR and materials.As the inter-community conflict worsens, this critical nutritional situation is likely to deteriorate, as is the humanitarian situation.Given the nutritional crisis and the low coverage in the management of SAM, an intervention is needed.
