Project Summary

Severe WASH needs have been identified in the locations of Northern Bahr El Ghazel, Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria as a result of multiple displacements, insecurity, economic hardships, food insecurity, malnutrition and disease outbreaks. Despite several efforts to alleviate suffering caused by the aforementioned triggers, challenges in meeting up to acceptable standards have been encountered which span across several thematic areas of WASH, Health, Nutrition, Protection and Food Security. Provision of adequate WASH services is key to achieving sustainable solutions to communities in emergency.
HUMANITARIAN AID FOR CHANGE AND TRANSFORMATION (HACT) works in the locations of Aweil North, Aweil West, Pibor, Budi, Lafon and Yambio providing an integrated multi – sectoral response to mainly IDPs and vulnerable host communities. HACT has presence and field offices in Yambio implementing an Access to Justice project, Lafon implementing SSHF WASH project, Aweil implementing a UNICEF Education project and Pibor through support from RRF.
HACT will provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees, through temporary and sustainable solutions to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through community approaches. The response aims at mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH including provision of dignity, hygiene and non-food item kits.
As a result of high GAM rates in the areas targeted, WASH integrated interventions starting from SAM/MAM admission at OTP/SC are planned to provide WASH in nutrition for famine prevention minimum package of interventions.
Since the locations targeted are disease prone, a WASH in health response is planned for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction. The project seeks to provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an adhoc basis. This will include setup of WASH services, training of health workers and other stakeholders as conveyors of appropriate hygiene practices.
A total of 40,000 people are targeted through this 12 month project, more than 53% of those targeted female in the counties of Aweil North and West, Pibor, Budi, Lafon and Yambio. 38% of the beneficiairies are IDPs, 60% vulnerable host communities, 2% of these returnees.

Needs

Chronic WASH needs continue to surface in parts of Jonglei, NBeG, EES and WES states, mainly affecting women and children majority of these multiply displaced. OCHA estimates close to 534,000 persons in the counties targeted are in need of humanitarian support due to adverse effects of conflict, displacement, food insecurity, economic hardships and lack of infrastructure. The areas targeted host close to 23,000 IDPs, which despite integration into host communities, tend to display higher chances of vulnerability. WASH cluster projections for 2019 and vulnerability assessments estimate a total of over 342,000 persons will be in need of WASH services in parts of Jonglei, NBeG, EES and WES. Remoteness and access issues in/to locations of Pibor, Budi and Lafon have further worsened the already dire situation with majority of the locations assessed lacking essential WASH infrastructure, communities reliant on unsafe water due to inadequate safe water sources. A similar trend is observed in NBEG and WES state. Less than 30% of the communities assessed reported their main source of drinking water is an improved source (borehole, tap stand & water-yard) and available in under 30 minutes, with over 70% of the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the main source of drinking water is an unimproved source (river, swamp, unprotected well), (REACH, 2018). Water points are shared by both animals and humans leading to breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitations and repairs. Cultural practices and attitudes towards excreta disposal and personal hygiene have further undermined efforts to achieve WASH prospects. Villages assessed displayed crude methods of excreta disposal thereby raising risks of water related diseases. Less than half of the populations use latrines, due to unavailability, safety concerns, knowledge and attitudes amongst other challenges. Protection risks have been increased to girls and women due to insensitivity in gender during WASH project implementation. Girls and women stray into bushes for convenience due to a lack of adequate, safe and dignified sanitation infrastructure, with cases of harassment reported especially amongst IDP populations. Safety at water points too makes women and girls resort to nearby contaminated water sources. A lack of dignity, hygiene and NFI kits further hampers access of females to common basic services such as education. There’s equally a direct correlation between poor WASH and malnutrition especially amongst under 5s and pregnant and lactating women. (ACF, 2018) Insufficient WASH facilities have played a big role in the rise of water related and diarrheal diseases, which have undermined efforts to reduce levels of malnutrition High prices have made it difficult for population to access WASH consumables such as soap, purification tablets, buckets etc. Provision of emergency WASH services will reduce likelihood of deaths due to water related diseases.

Activities

A total of 40,000 people are targeted through this 12-month project, more than 53% of those targeted female in the counties of Aweil North and West, Pibor, Budi, Lafon and Western Equatoria. 38% of the beneficiairies are IDPs, 60% vulnerable host communities, 2% of these returnees. WASH activities for IDPs/host communities and returnees • Provision of timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations. • Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH- related diseases.• Enabling of vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. Sanitation services WASH activities to Mitigate WASH- related GBV • Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers • Provision of safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis • Provision of safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions. • Provision of safe drinking water and nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM. • Provision of WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Ensuring smallstock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent smallstock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction. • Provision of a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad- hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community • Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level
