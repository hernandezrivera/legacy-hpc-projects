Project Summary

Despite its obligations under international law, Israel continues to violate with impunity the relevant provisions of applicable IHL and IHR conventions (CRC, ICCPR and UNCAT) relating to the rights and protection of boys and girls in armed conflict. 2017 continued the trend seen in 2016 of deterioration of the security situation in the Palestinian Territories, including East Jerusalem leading to a sharp increase in human rights (HR) violations of Palestinian children, specifically in mental and physical violence against children during arrest and detention. Furthermore, incarceration and sentencing guidelines have worsened dramatically, with jail sentences of ten years for participation in demonstrations.
The project will address, in a gender sensitive and age appropriate way, violations of HR amounting to torture and ill-treatment. The Public Committee Against Torture in Israel (PCATI) will bring to bear our specific experience and expertise to document violations of human rights and present to the detainees the avenues available to them should they suffer a violation of these rights. Victims will then be given pro bono legal counsel and pro bono legal accompaniment in the process of demanding accountability.
This project responds directly to the overarching need to prevent and mitigate the impact of violations of children’s rights including the need for:
• Identification, monitoring and documentation of torture and ill-treatment allegations
• Advocacy immediate and time sensitive interventions with Israeli authorities and relevant actors
• Provision of legal aid and services to support access to justice
• Child protection services including detection and case management and initial response to child arrests and Detention.

Needs

The staggering numbers of boys and girls in distress, suffering from severe violations of their rights amounting to ill treatment and torture have been documented extensively, including by OCHA (HNO 2018, sub-cluster reports including legal task force) and PCATI publications. Our most recent assessment of the situation and needs of girls and boys undergoing ill treatment likely amounting to torture shows that of the 600 Palestinian girls and boys in detention, an estimated third of them report being subjected to physical and non-physical violence, time critical situations (PCATI’s Info-Sheet: State of Ill-treatment, “Policy Paper: Palestinian Minors in East Jerusalem”, UNCAT assessment report), suffering immediate and lasting psychological and sometimes physical damage. Their access to the needed health care is limited by the availability, capacity and logistics of detention and prison service providers, problematic transfer conditions, and prison regulations. This concurs with the need identified at cluster level of improved quality and reach of multi sectoral services (legal, psychosocial, health) to support prevention-, detection-, response- and referral- pathways to improve access to existing services for victims of HR violations in and after detention. Detained Palestinians often encounter physical, psychological and sexual violence during their arrest and interrogation. According to PCATI's database, women and boys are particularly prone to be subjected to sexual based harassment, threats and violence. All detainees are likely to encounter inhuman detention conditions, including but not limited to sleep deprivation, lack of regular and sufficient access to food, water and toilets, and lack of hygiene in the cells; these conditions have particular consequence for females. Complaints arising from these allegations encounter a culture of impunity from the Israeli authorities, only extremely rarely leading to criminal prosecution or disciplinary actions. Despite its obligations under international law, Israel continues to violate with impunity the relevant provisions of applicable IHL and IHR conventions (especially those of the CRC, ICCPR and UNCAT). Specifically, since the beginning of the Great Return March in March 2018 there has been a new deterioration of the situation in oPt leading to a sharp increase in human rights violations of Palestinians including girls and boys, involving the excessive use of force by Israeli security forces. These arrests have been added to the regular volume of arrest, detention and interrogation in the West Bank, which continues apace. Girls are much more rarely arrested, and therefore are a minority target of this project.

Activities

This project prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries according to the Do No Harm principle. The planned intervention enables equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of acutely-vulnerable groups of girls and boys in and after detention, protecting their specific needs.Indicator a.1: Identification of grave violations against children, specifically allegations of torture and ill-treatment, with a special focus on abuses of girls and boys affected by conflict-related violence by Israeli Security Forces and analysis of ensuing trends. Indicator H1.1. Providing pro bono urgent specialised legal services and representation to children (girls and boys) affected by conflict-related violence regarding allegations of torture and ill-treatment as well as support to their family members. Referrals to other services and individual follow up as needed.Indicators C1 and C2. Advocacy to international actors raising protection concerns specifically regarding the use of torture and ill treatment and the particular vulnerabilities of children in and out of detention. The project has been developed by PCATI, but in the implementation phase, and as means to ensuring and victim-centered holistic approach, PCATI will work closely with Palestinian organizations based on thematic areas in.a. identifying detainees, conducting visitations and collecting testimonies, we will work with Nadi-Al-Aseer .b. referrals to psychosocial support to victims and their families, we will refer cases to Sawa.c. cooperation with the TRC- The Ramallah-based Treatment and Rehabilitation Centre for Victims of Torture, in cases where need arises for treatment and rehabilitation.d. Cooperation with the Israeli Medical Association in dealing with medical ethics through the promotion and recognition of the use of the Istanbul Protocol in Israel as a tool to identify and document the physical and psychological signs of torture and ill treatment.e. Cooperation with Physicians for Human Rights in Israel, in carrying out joint legal actions when specialized medical advice is required by the victim. The key to PCATI’s resilience facing unprecedented pressure on human rights defenders resides in a double axis of strength: belief in our vision that freedom from torture is a quintessential and primary human right, combined with our ongoing MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning) system helping us calibrate our efforts to the vital needs of survivors of torture. The MEAL system is built around pre evaluation, intermediate evaluation and final evaluation procedures. A Pre evaluation will be undertaken at the beginning of each activity to ensure the coherence of the response plan and activities foreseen with the profiles of the beneficiaries and the most recent developments on the ground since the needs assessment. This is the basis for our early warning early action mechanism. Intermediate evaluation involves:• Debriefing and evaluation with a) the girl or boy victim of torture b) his/her family c) with the director of PCATI’s legal department. Progress will be measured against the specific indicators as agreed upon in the planning.• Monthly departmental MEAL with the entire project team around issues related to implementation challenges, risks, mitigation and analysis of learning.• Periodic reporting to cluster membersFinal evaluation will be undertaken in-house against the established indicators and expected results. The analysis will be reported in the final project report.

Indicators

The following indicators will be dis-aggregated according to gender and age: 40 incidents of grave violations against children and violations of human rights and IHL are identified and screened (including an anticipated 35 boys and 5 girls), 20 cases monitored and documented (3 of which are girls), 15 testimonies are taken, 11 children (2 girls and 9 boys) are interviewed in depth and provided with comprehensive legal advice and legal assistance, in prison and detention and outside of it. 20 cases referred externally for rehabilitation or other treatments and services as relevant. An anticipated 10 reports on allegations of torture and ill treatment would be presented to Israeli authorities and pursued adequately. 2 fact sheets are disseminated. Public outreach: 4 press releases, 80 journalists receiving press releases, 10,000 people reached through social media posts, 3 op eds, news articles and interviews, 2 reports or fact-sheets on IHL and IHRL violations, conflict-related violence and relevant trends generated, 4 local briefings and/or field visits. National, regional and international advocacy: 5 bi- and multi-lateral briefings and advocacy meetings with members of the international community for raising urgent protection concerns, 30 diplomats reached in meetings and briefings to raise protection concerns, 4 briefings and meetings with national authorities and policy-makers. This project responds directly to the overarching need to prevent and mitigate the impact of violations of children’s rights including the need for:• Monitoring and documentation, immediate and time sensitive interventions with Israeli authorities• Child protection interventions including identification and urgent response to child arrests and detention, where there are indicators of abuse and ill-treatment potentially amounting to grave violations of Children’s rights and/or torture.• Provision of legal aid and services to support the access to justice for children during and after detention• Advocacy initiatives including direct legal advocacy with local duty bearers, briefings and field visits for national and international policy shapers, and creating media discourse as relevant and useful for each case individually.
