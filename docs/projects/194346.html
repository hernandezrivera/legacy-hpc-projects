Project Summary

In South Sudan,an estimated 8.2 million people, including refugees and Abyei, are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July) . This is comparable with the situation described in the FSL sectoral analysis (December 2021) and IPC analysis (March 2022) where food security needs remained large and severe. The main drivers are covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to conflict and insecurity). Livelihood needs in Tonj South, Yei, Jur river, Kapoeta North and Magwi counties, are dire as most families return either from refugees camp or IDP camps or affected by climatic shock.
The proposed intervention aims to saves lives, enhance early recoveries and build community resilience by addressing the main drivers of vulnerabilities. The intervention will seek to support 30000 vulnerable people with emphasis on enhancing and sustaining emergency food production and livelihood support deprived during conflict and floods. To further strengthen resilient livelihoods income diversification the project will provide pathways for communities to recover and adapt to climate variability. FS-SS' activities; Community mobilization and sensitization, project orientation, identification, selection, verification and registration of beneficiaries, distribution of agriculture inputs(tools-hoes, moloda, rakes, and seeds-groundnut, maize, sorghum, cowpeas, tomatoes, onion, eggplant, Okra, Kale, kudra, Amaranth), distribution of tip-tapes and fishing kits, formation and training of VSLA groups, Technical guidance/training on; agronomics practices, IPM, post-harvest management, entrepreneurship skill, Soy meal preparation, kitchen gardening, and fish preservation and marketing. Guidance on sustainable agricultural land-use practices, PDM and feedback to targeted communities, and Creating market linkages between traders and producer groups.
The primary project focus will be hands-on farming, sanitation, and nutrition training and equipping poor families and vulnerable people with inputs and skills to build resilience.
FS-SS plans to target 1600 households (HHs) for agriculture inputs distribution and training on vegetable and staple crops to increase food availability. 800 HH will benefit from VSLA support that will enable them to save and invest in building income-earning capacity to develop resilience and coping mechanisms against shocks by earning alternative incomes. 500HH will receive fishing kits to boost fishing activities to complement their diets and sell the surplus fish to earn some revenue. FS-SS will also promote COVID- 19 awareness and sanitation training to reduce disease prevalence in the community to 1000 HH. 500HH shall be provided with a tip-tap.
The project will conduct preventive nutrition training to 500 HH through the preparation of nutrient-dense meals and support to kitchen gardening. This will be implemented through conducting cooking classes comprising of preparation of soya milk, soya eggs, soya meat, soya snacks, rainbow dish.
The intervention is expected to reinforce and strengthen the protection of the various groups affected by the conflict. Livelihood roles are expected to change to include women as decision-makers in household income generation hence reducing the risks of SEA accosted with poverty. FS-SS will conduct FSL based protection risk analysis to mitigate all risks to various groups of women, girls, and children, and the marginalized of the affected group. Awareness on GBV, sexual exploitation, and child abuse and exploitation shall be conducted.
FS-SS' proposed project aims to support IDPs, returnees, host and vulnerable communities in Tonj South-Warap state , Yei-Central Equatoria state ,Jur river-Western Bharel ghazal, Kapoeta North and Magwi County –Eastern Equatoria state to increase their livelihood and self-sufficiency.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, An estimated 8.2 million people, including refugees and Abyei, are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July) . This is comparable with the situation described in the FSL sectoral analysis (December 2021) and IPC analysis (March 2022) where food security needs remained large and severe. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 79% of the population will likely face. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.4 million children under the age of 5, and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2023. 59 counties are projected to be in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) and in the worst-case scenario, several counties in Jonglei (Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Mayendit, Leer and Koch) and Upper Nile (Renk) might fall into IPC AMN Phase 5.People in 76 counties face extreme needs by the lean-season 2023. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition, and school dropout. Insecurity disrupted agricultural activities, leading to the loss of livestock and other assets, restricted physical movement, and access to markets, and disrupted market functionality. Tonj South, Jur river, Yei,Kapoeta north and Magwi Counties have remained in serious humanitarian crisis due to cumulative effects of years of armed conflicts, floods, ethnic confrontations, dry spell, livestock diseases, and plant pests and disease outbreaks which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and displaced thousands of people and livestock in these communities. In 2023, 84,000 people in Tonj South, 136,000 in yei, 80,000 in Magwi, 94,000 in Kapoeta North and 142,000 in Jur river are respectively in need of humanitarian assistance and livelihood support.The major need include; (1) Food assistance (in kind/ cash & voucher/ hybrid) as part of a holistic multi-sector response to IPC Phase 4 & 5 HHs and as many IPC Phase 3 HHs as resources can allow meeting immediate food needs; (2) to maintain food availability and access across farming and agro/ pastoral communities in the medium to longer-term requires emergency cropping (cereals, pulses & vegetables), livelihood (fishing kits) and livestock (vaccine, treatment & feedstocks) support; and (3) addressing deeply rooted causes of food insecurity needs strengthening of coping mechanisms and building resilience while linking with peacebuilding and development responses. Some are harmful to children such as early/forced marriage and child labour. Both men and women report relying on small-scale livelihood activities to mitigate the difficulties they face, including fishing, hunting, farming, harvesting honey, brewing alcohol and selling charcoal. Some of the vulnerable women and girls opt to exploitative survival sex in exchange for basic needs.An estimated 152000 people in the four counties (Tonj South, Jur river, Yei,kapoeta North and Magwi) will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), of which 30,000 in yei, 27,000 in Magwi ,36,000 in Tonj South,31,000 in Kapoeta North and 28,000 in Jur river counties by lean season 2023 (IPC Nov 2022). It is critical to support these households to restore lost crops and livelihoods, thus increasing food availability through personal production and a small income for increased food access

2 - Project activities

FARM STEW South Sudan proposed the following activities to enable the affected households to recover from the shock and build resilience (i). Through the provision of access to emergency agriculture inputs and extension services, FS-SS will contribute to improved agricultural production, harvesting and storage of food, and hence its security through building beneﬁciaries capacities and farmers. Proposed activities include distribution of main crop and vegetable kits together with tools, and establishment of demonstration plots using the Farmer Field School Model and Capacity building for the County Agriculture Departments and Villages based Advisors(VBAs); 1600HH will benefit from agricultural inputs. 500 farmers will benefit from training on basic agronomic practice including post-harvest management and handling, IPM etc through VBAs to enable improvement in food production in areas of IPC 3. 20 VBAs shall be trained and deployed to conduct extension services among the farmers and 20 demonstration plots shall be established to build the capacity of farmers as part of resilience enhancement. (ii) Support to value chain addition through conduct training on value addition through the preparation of nutrient-dense meals like rainbow meals, soya milk, soya meat, soya mandazi, etc., milk hygiene, handling, and preservation, together with ﬁsh processing and preservation, linkage of farmers to market through village-based advisors. 500HH will benefit from fishing kits. 200 women shall benefit from training on milk handling, procession, and marketing. (iii) Support restoration and livelihood diversiﬁcation by developing and strengthening ﬁnancial services through Village Savings and Loans Association (VSLAs). 800HH will benefit from the VSLA program to diverse livelihoods and build resilience.(iv) Training and capacity building; on-farm training through setting demonstration plots to train farmers during field days and income general activities (IGA) training through entrepreneurship/business skill training. 20 farmers' field days shall be conducted on the 20 established demonstration plots to create awareness and build the farmers' capacity of 500 farmers on good agronomics practices, IPM. 500 HH shall be training on nutrition-sensitive agriculture to have a nutritious diet at their household level to reduce cases of malnutrition. 100 VBAs shall be trained on entrepreneurship skills to enable them to improve on the seed value chain system through providing linkage between farmers and seed companies (v). Integration of cross-cutting issues of gender, disaster risk reduction, nutrition, sanitation, and natural resource management in all FSL project activities. 200 individuals shall benefit from training on CMDRR and CMNRM. 20 GBV awareness session shall be conducted to reinforce the projection aspect during activities delivery. 1000 individuals shall benefit from COVID19 preventive and sanitation awareness sessions. 500 HH shall be provided with training and tools for tip-tape to boost sanitation at the household level, hence reduction in diseases that translate into more active labor in the field agriculture production.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Typically, FS-SS passes information through the caretakers and immediate family members (brother, son, uncle, etc.) of the disabled to get information about the project. Secondly, FS-SS will use community meetings conducted in local languages. Also, the project community volunteers will be involved in passing information through door-to-door visits and also collect feedback from the venerable members of the communities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be assured through two-way communication mechanisms. FS-SS will conduct community mobilization of all beneficiaries to clarify what the project is providing and what support they can expect from the project. Weekly or monthly meetings will be organized and announced for community reports along with follow-up actions taken. The project will link effectively with MoAF, SSRRC, local chiefs, and communities to follow an inclusive programming approach. Furthermore, FSSS will request leaders and members to raise any issues. FS-SS will also establish and appoint people in the community so that beneficiaries can report any concerns linked to the project. All complaints will be documented in the weekly build-up of this project based on farmers' existing knowledge and training them on improved agriculture practices. FSSS involves the community to assess, identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. It will be a kind of participatory method. The method will prioritize vulnerable people, including IDPs, host communities, and female-headed HHS.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All FS-SS staff are trained on PSEA and are in a position to create awareness and inform beneficiaries on risks related to PSEA. FS-SS always distribute flyers, make posters and T-shirts carrying messages about PSEA. Beneficiaries and community leaders are availed phone numbers and people to approach in case an incident of PSEA occurs. FSSS will set up an anonymous complaint mechanism whereby any beneficiary aggrieved will be able to complain. A complaint will be channeled to a project person charged with that responsibility and trained on protection matters. The identity of the complainant will not be revealed to non-authorized persons in the project. Where practical, FS-SS will use mobile phone technology whereby an aggrieved person will instantly send a complaint through a short text message from where s/he will be. There will also be complaint handling mechanism committees established at the village level. FS-SS has successfully used these mechanisms in Eastern Equatoria where it is implementing its integrated food security project. It has a confidential procedure of whistleblowing and reporting where reported information can be investigated and referred to relevant authorities if found to be true. FS-SS will conduct protection sensitization sessions for female-headed households to train them about potential protection issues, available complaint mechanisms, and coping mechanisms to deal with problems should they occur. These sessions will help women understand what steps they should take in case of any protection issue and the types of support that FS-SS can provide. The FSSS field-level Senior Management Team will be involved in the distribution and post-distribution monitoring to ensure that protection-related problems, mostly related to women-headed households, are addressed. FSSS will ensure that training and other project activities fit into female beneficiaries' day-to-day working schedules, including distributing tools linked to sites accessible to women and children within the community. FSSS will employ participatory assessments to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. These criteria will prioritize vulnerable people, including IDPs, host communities, and female-headed HHS. FSSS will also perform periodic feedback sessions with community project beneficiaries to understand their protection concerns and design tailor-made interventions in collaboration with the UN and other agencies with protection programming

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

FSSS will use a participatory implementation strategy where communities will be consulted on the project that will best serve them. The project will do its best to adapt to the needs and suggestions of the beneficiaries. . FS-SS extension officers will work with the community leaders to inform the project's purpose, duration, activities, and implementation. The communities will also be active participants in reviewing beneficiary selection criteria. The implementation modalities suggest the best ways of optimally using the project resources. The views of the beneficiaries will be incorporated into the project implementation plan in a progressive manner.
