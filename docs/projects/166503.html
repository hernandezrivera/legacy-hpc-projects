Project Summary

A) Project Rationale:
Northeast Nigeria faces one of the world’s largest protection crises in the world, violence
perpetuated by Boko Haram (BH) and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP)
has displaced nearly 2.5 million people, hindered livelihoods, and restricted affected
populations from accessing basic services and humanitarian assistance. Adamawa,
Borno, and Yobe are among the most affected states, with an estimated 1.7 million
internally displaced people (IDPs), many living in camp settings.
Moreover, the protracted nature of the conflict reinforces pre-existing gender inequalities
across the Northeastern states. An estimated 700,000 women and adolescent girls are at
risk of sexual violence and exploitation, with 54% of IDPs being female and half of them
of reproductive age; about 260,000 of these women are currently pregnant. Over 50,000
births will face complications with a high-risk of maternal mortality due to lack of
appropriate SRHR services and insufficient referrals for secondary care and SRHR-trained
medical staff.
Poor security conditions in local government areas (LGAs) in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe
prevent IDPs from returning to their homes, while IDP camps frequently face attacks from
BH and ISWAP. The main protection threats in camps include physical assaults and GBV,
notably women forced into transactional sex. According to HRP for 2019-2021,
individuals who are most at risk of GBV and insurgency-related violence include female
and child-headed households, unaccompanied children, and adolescents. While armed
groups often forcibly recruit, abduct, arbitrarily arrest, and detain men and adolescent
boys, many female IDPs in camps have been forced or coerced into marriage and sexual
exploitation. These women and girls suffer from severe stigmatization by fellow IDPs and
the society, as well as insufficient support to promote their health and reintegration.
Emergency response must address the mental health and psychosocial support
(MHPSS) needs of these vulnerable affected populations.
B) Proposed Response
HAI proposes “Access to specialized MHPSS case management and reintegration of
IDPs, survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA in Northeast Nigeria,” in alignment with the
Nigeria HRP priorities for the country. Over 12 months, Access will provide quality MHPSS
and SRHR services and will enhance the reintegration of IDPs into their communities,
including people affected by armed groups and survivors abductions, GBV and SEA.
HAI will improve the mental and physical health of IDPs (SO1) and support the urgent
reintegration of people impacted by armed groups into their communities (SO2). Utilizing
a community-based approach, HAI will provide quality emergency MHPSS services and
specialized case management to IDPs through trained community outreach workers
(CWs) and psychologists. Access’ CWs will provide psychoeducation awareness
sessions to IDPs and will teach coping skills to community members through a gendersensitive,
peer-support group model. For individuals presenting complex mental health
needs, Access’ roving psychologists will provide individuals with specialized and gendersensitive
mental health care.
Access will also support IDPs to access SRHR services through mapping existing SRHR
and HIV/AIDS services that are suitable to the needs of IDPs women and girls affected by
GBV and armed groups’ violence. Roving nurses will provide SRH services to IDPs
including accurate SRHR information and individual counseling, prevention and treatment
of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV/AIDS, and accompaniment to
relevant services for emergency cases. Under SO2, HAI will support people impacted by
armed groups to have access to income generating activities and supplies through
vocational training targeting female heads of households and provide microloans to
individuals to leverage the received training. Livelihood activities will be culturally and
geographically appropriate.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria results in complexand destructive consequences for women, men, girls and boys who are abducted andexploited. The ongoing conflict is characterized by deliberate and widespread attacks oncivilian populations including sexual violence, abduction (especially of girls and women),forced disappearances, child and forced recruitment, forced displacement, and the use ofsuicide bombers. Female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separatedchildren, and adolescent boys and girls are particularly at risk of gender-based violence(GBV), forced recruitment, and human trafficking, while adolescent and adult males facethreats of arbitrary arrests, prolonged detention, and often torture and other abuse duringdetention. According to the GBV Sub Sector annual report for 2018, of all the GBVincidents occurring in the context of sexual slavery and sexual violence reported, at least36% were perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) and occurred to individualsheld against her/his will, abducted or kidnapped. Of the women and girls who were forcedand abducted from their homes by NSAGs, many have been raped, forced into marriageand labour, abused physically, sexually and/or emotionally, exposed to sexuallytransmitted diseases, and often impregnated by their captors. Violence against womenand girls, including sexual violence, and exposure to trafficking and abduction iswidespread but grossly underreported. The confict affected population is facingadditional stressors created by the displacement itself, such as family separation,difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities tolivelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potentialstigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. These factors can contribute toincreasing risks of exposure to GBV. Exposure to GBV risks also includes exposure tosexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments byarmed actors and at risk of arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms ofviolence, yet the life-saving services are not readily available in some areas. Increasedaccess to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survival sex. Thus,need for provision of continuous psychosocial support, engagement of women and girlsin livelihood activities, will encourage women participation in decision making, andensure we mitigate all risks that might predispose women and girls to GBV. HAI recognizes that crises like COVID-19 impact men and women differently, often exacerbating gender inequality and gender-based violence. The stay-at-home policy of respective governments increase gender-based violence and abuse of children. Furthermore, women and girls are also at increased risk of infection due to their disproportionate role in disease response as health professionals, community volunteers, and caregivers. In response, HAI employs a gender-conscious approach to the pandemic that adapts to meet the unique needs of these affected populations and provide justice programming, critical for the success of programs. HAI has worked globally in the field of trauma-informed MHPSS for over thirty years. HAI employs a multi-tiered MHPSS approach ideal for complex settings including public health crises likeCOVID-19. HAI identifies individuals who may benefit from immediate counseling with peers or health care workers,while also recognizing those with more severe symptoms who must be connected to specialized care. Further, HAI is built to provide direct care to survivors and people who may have experienced trauma related to COVID-19, including, health care workers, community leaders, caregivers and those suffering from stigma, severe isolation, or generalized fear.

2 - Activities

SO 1: IDPs have improved mental and physical healthIR 1.1: IDPs have increased access to quality emergency MHPSS services to enhance wellbeing among survivors/vulnerable individuals. Activity 1.1.1:Train Community Outreach Workers (COWs) (at least 50% females) to provide basicemotional and psychosocial support (PSS), to survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA, and Covid-19 positive and survivors beneficiaries.Activity 1.1.2: Trained COWs provide psychoeducation awareness sessions to IDPs,survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA on conflict, displacement-related and COVID-19 pandemic related stressors.Activity 1.1.3: Trained COWs teach coping skills to community members through aCommunity-Based, gender sensitive, Peer Support group model, particularly focusedon the needs of IDPs, including needs and stressors during Covid-19 pandemic. Activity 1.1.4: Roving psychologists provide individual, specialized mental health care when appropriate (for referred critical cases, including cases related to Covid-19). Activity 1.1.5: COWs and psychologists receive consistent clinical supervision. Activity 1.1.6: HAI team provides staff care sessions to prevent burnout and secondary trauma, including related to Covid-19.IR 1.2: IDPs have improved access to sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRHR) with aspecific focus on women, including those imposed to higher risks caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.Activity 1.2.1: Map existing SRH/HIV services and identifies the most sustainable sources for SRHR services and products, appropriate to the cultural context. Activity 1.2.2: Train COWs on SRHR outreach andon SRHR referrals. Training will include culturally appropriate information considerationsaround participants’ age, gender and sexual orientation and gender identity/expression(SOGIE). Activity 1.2.3: COWs conduct outreach to sensitize IDPs and host communities on SRHR services and risks of Covid-19. Develop key messages and IEC materials on SRHR and Covid-19. Activity 1.2.4: In partnership with the Ministry of Health (MoH), COWs androving nurses provide SRHR services to IDPs, such as: - Accurate sexual and reproductivehealth information (by COWs); - Specialized and culturally-appropriate individual SRHRcounseling (by roving project nurses); - Prevention and management of STIs, includingHIV and Covid-19 testing and counseling (by roving project nurses); - Accompaniment to relevantservices for emergency cases (by COWs). SO2: People impacted by armed groups are better integrated into their communities. IR 2.1: People impacted by armed groups have improved access to income generating activities and supplies, including during Covid-19.Activity 2.1.1: Train staff on gender norms/how to leverage opportunities to tackle powerimbalance and stereotypes throughout the project activitiesActivity 2.1.2: Provide vocational training to female heads of household.(Based on geographic location and culture, different LGAs specialize indifferent crafts, labor; production of locally available personal protective materials such as face masks, sanitizers and soaps as prevention to COVID-19 ) Activity 2.1.3: Administer microloans to individuals including for initiatives for production of locally available PPE itemIR 2.2: People impacted by armed groups and host communities are able toresolve disputes and understand each other. Activity 2.2.1: Awareness raising with stakeholders, including religious and tribal leaders, around issues that people impacted by armed groups might experience. Activity 2.2.2: Lead community mediation sessionswith affected populations and community members discussing issues that peopleimpacted by armed groups face. Community mediation sessions might take differentforms not limited to meetings but also including dance activities, storytelling, communitytheater and performance and others. Activity 2.2.4: COWs support individuals in obtaining civil documents, including IDs, marriage,birth, death certificates.
