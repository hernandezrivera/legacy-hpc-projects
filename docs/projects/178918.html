Project Summary

The project will enhance the resilience of the crisis-affected population and contribute to the local economy recovery in North Syria through vocational and skills training (VST), livelihoods support, support local TVET centers, and rehabilitation of infrastructure.
The vocational training and livelihoods support comprises a broad-based market assessment of the trades and skills which are in sever need in the north of Syria after the long years of conflict. The project will deliver vocational training for 4,500 young women and men on different modules which are identified through the market assessments. The project will distribute vocational kits for 1,200 trainees/entrepreneurs and who are graduated from the VST program and 300 productive assets to rural entrepreneurs to start their own small businesses and linking young entrepreneurs with potential markets. QC will thoroughly monitor the business and job efficiency status of the participants in order to ensure project effectiveness.
In aim of supporting local and public TVET services providers, the project plans to support 12 centers with necessary equipment and vocational tools to enhance their services delivery and then increase the access of the targeted communities to free TVET services.
Moreover, the project contributes to the restoration of basic services through rehabilitating 10 KM of access roads with drainage systems. As part of this activity and with possible referral to other rehabilitation efforts in the targeted areas, 500 families will benefit from cash for work opportunities.
The project targets men and women in the working age (17-65 years old) with particular focus on youth (17-24 years old) and people living with disabilities. In addition, the project ensures to target more vulnerable group, for example, female-headed, child- headed, elderly-headed families, families with multiple displacement, and families with large household members.

4 - Needs

Over 10 years of conflict in Syria has resulted in significant destruction of the country’s infrastructure, socio-economic dynamics and opportunities, basic services and social cohesion.Based on the HRP 2021, The estimation of people in need of early recovery and livelihood support is 11.6 million; 51 per cent are estimated to be women, 45 per cent children, and 26 per cent persons living with disabilities (PLWD). The overall poverty is reached new levels with an estimation close to 90 per cent, with an increase of 3-4 per cent points compared to 2019 and the extreme poverty is estimated between 55 and 65 per cent. At the end of 2020, unemployment is estimated at 50 per cent of the working-age population, and over 60 per cent of youth. 1 in 3 Syrian households in north-west Syria report daily wage labour as their main livelihood activity. 60% of households in north-west Syria have reported that the livelihoods as one of their top three priorities, which came the second top priority after food.74 per cent of available basic socio-economic infrastructures are damaged (53 per cent severely damaged). Further deterioration of the socio-economic conditions affecting IDPs, residents, and potential returnees is expected, adding to the risk of social fabric rupture.This economic hardship has forced people to adopt reduced coping strategies due to the loss or lack of sustained livelihoods, for example, reduced food consumption, deferment or delay in seeking necessary medical care; reduced hygiene practices, increasing public health risk; the spending of savings and the accumulation.References:HRP 2021 – ERL Sector Needs and Response Summary, Syrian Arab Republic, OCHA, 2021.Socio-Economic Overview, HNAP Syria, 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

The main activities planned to be carried out within this project are:- Deliver vocational and skills training to 4,500 beneficiaries on different training modules identified through the market assessments conducted the targeted locations. Skills and business development training will be provided to 500 rural entrepreneurs. - Provide capacity and equipment support to 12 local TVET centers which are providing free services to their communities. The estimated number of beneficiaries of this activity is about 6000 individuals.- Provide 1,500 beneficiary (1,200 to craftsmen/entrepreneurs and 300 to rural entrepreneurs) with productive assets to support trainees/entrepreneurs in establishing their small businesses and enterprises.- Support 500 in accessing short-term work opportunities using cash for work modalities. The cash-for-work opportunities linked to rehabilitation works planned by the project and in the form of labour-force support to other local rehabilitation efforts.- Rehabilitation of access roads (10 KM) to increase access of the targeted communities to basic and economical services. The estimation of beneficiaries of this activity is 40,000 individuals.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Creating or exacerbating community tensions arising from the beneficiary selection and targeting. Mitigation measures: - Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent. - Obtain validation of the prioritization and the vulnerability criteria and weighting from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms.- Ensure communication and accountability by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation. - Set up a mechanism for receiving complaints and response mechanism to elicit beneficiary & community feedback.Risk: women participating in the vocational training are exposed to GBV. Mitigation measures: - Ensure safe transportation of women to/ from training centers. - Set up of training centers close to the residence of beneficiaries. - Organize separate session for women, whenever applicable. Risk: Cash for Work beneficiaries working on rehabilitation of roads are exposed to risks of land mines explosive remnants of war whilst undertaking rehabilitation activities. Mitigation measures: - Provision of Risk Education sessions to CFW beneficiaries, mainly Mine Risk Education when undertaking activities deemed to be risky.- Coordinate with partners concerned with Mine Action about the safety of the specific area in which the rehabilitation activities take place.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Escalation of the security situation. Assumption: The security situations remain calm in the target locations and areas of operation remain safe for staff and beneficiaries to access. Mitigation: - Prioritize vocational training in relatively stable areas. - Project implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and may be able to retain access for longer period, particularly for CFW activities. - Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment. Risk: Increase in the price due to devaluation of the Syrian Lira. Assumption: It is assumed that a significant devaluation of the Syrian Lira does not occur. Mitigation:- monitor the prices, fix the rates of the compensation of CFW through US dollars to avoid any losses due to the exchange rates. Risk: Issues of HLP in terms of roads. Assumption: The rehabilitated roads are not new ones. Mitigation: All the roads will be repaired and rehabilitated, not constructed. Risk: Cash and/or assets assistance may be misused, stolen, or diverted.Assumption: All cash and asset assistance provided to the beneficiaries are used to cover their basic needs and for income-generating activities. Mitigation: - Conduct reference checks for BNFs in coordination with local authorities. - Adapt safer cash transfer methods. In this project, PTT centers will be used.- Limiting knowledge of cash movements and smaller/more frequent transfers.- Varying distribution days and locations.- Screening all vendors.- Agreements with BNFs.- Post-distribution monitoring and follow-up.- Ensure proper complaint and feedback mechanisms.
