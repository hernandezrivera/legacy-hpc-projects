Project Summary

An estimated 2.6 million people are displaced in Somalia. Coping mechanisms have been reduced over successive years of drought and conflict, leaving IDPs vulnerable to shocks and inhibiting a sustainable return to places of origin. Of the total displaced population, 2.4 million people are living in IDP sites which lack dignified living conditions, enough access to basic services, land tenure and adequate resources to sustain their populations. An estimated 360,000 of people in IDP sites are persons with various disabilities and 48 per cent are women. Marginalized clans and vulnerable groups such as women and people with disabilities are reported to be particularly affected and at risk of discrimination
Poor IDP living conditions in informal sites: The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Sites are overcrowded which increases the risk of floods, fire, disease outbreaks and GBV and child rights violations.
Belethawa host over 18 old IDP camps,with over 38,000 IDPs from Damas,Khadiijo hajji,Tuulobarwaaqo,Garbaharey and Ceelcade from 2012-2017,due to prolonged drought and conflict between SNA,KDF and AS and some of the families fled from KDF airstrikes in many different locations in Gedo Region,mostly rural areas, while Dollow is currently hosting around 50,700 IDPs in two makeshift sites located in public land: Kabasa and Qansaxley. The high population density in these camps and the subsequent concentration of service provision makes service monitoring and coordination key aspects of CCCM related needs.
On march 5 hostilities escalated in Gedo region, Jubaland State, resulting in large scale displacement of civilians mainly from Belet Xaawo town and the nearby Belet Amin,Camp Ajuran,Alla-Aamiin and many IDP sites, Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) settlements. As of 5 March, an estimated 56,000 people (9,000 households) have been displaced from their homes according to the Regional Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (R-ICCG) in Gedo and report that some of the IDPs have moved to villages outside Belet Xaawo town, Doolow and Luuq.Belet Xaawo is one of biggest towns in Gedo region. It is strategically located on the Somali- Kenya border. Movement of goods and persons doing business between the two countries has spurred the growth of the town in recent years, which have been affected by closure of the border. Authorities estimate that the current population of Belet Xaawo is around 120,000 people (20,000 households) together with an estimated 38,000 old IDPs (56,000 current displaced households).
The new IDPs are an addition to 207,000 displaced people living in Gedo region. On 3 March, the regional Education Cluster reported that three schools had closed, disrupting classes for over 1,540 students. Another 2,000 students who go to school in Mandera, Kenya, can no longer attend class because of the border closure.
Preliminary assessment conducted by OCHA and partners indicates that the IDPs need CCCM,shelter, NFIs, WASH supplies, food and health assistance, vulnerability of the general population in Belet Xaawo since tensions continue,In addition, community mobilization and the enactment of representative governance structures are needed to facilitate dialogue between service providers and affected populations, and to enhance accountability towards them. in an effort to respond the immediate needs of IDPs and host communities SOVA will provide camp coordination and camp management and beneficiaries will be capacitated and this project will benefit 10800 individuals from 1800HHs and The proposed project will enhance community communication and feedback mechanisms through construction of community information centers, employing community outreach

Project Activities

SOVA will continue CCCM activities in Baardheere, Dollow,Belethawa and Garbaharey Gedo Region of Jubaland state. SOVA will coordinate the delivery of multispectral services in sites by representing CCCM in relevant coordination forums; conducting site verications on a quarterly basis; mapping the services available in sites all the four targeted districts; monitoring service delivery in sites. SOVA will improve living conditions of displaced people in Gedo IDPs by supporting community led site maintenance activities to ensure maintenance of sites; conducting safety audits to identify areas of high protection risk ; implementing emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites and developing new land for the relocation of displaced people. SOVA will strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations by supporting diverse and inclusive governance structures; providing capacity building to existing governance structures on CCCM and related principles; providing community spaces for management committees; and operating timely and responsive feedback and complaint mechanisms and 12700 households with individual beneficiaries of 76200.

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization SOVA will ensure diverse and inclusive governance structures are present in sites that represent all subsets of populations. SOVA will ensure 2 way feedback mechanisms for populations to voice their concerns on services and site governance. SOVA will provide information to service providers on service availability and access in IDP sites to ensure non biased targeting of IDP sites by partners. SOVA will encourage the participation of women through activities speciﬁcally geared towards reducing barriers to women’s participation. Where no governance structure is presence SOVA will support communities to come together and agree on the type of representation they would require that allows the participation of all groups in decision making Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services SOVA will conduct safety audits of sites in collaboration with the appropriate partners to identify safety risks and mitigate GBV . SOVA will ensure the training all community workers in GBV referrals. SOVA will coordinate with GBV partners to ensure that GBV referral pathways are operational and known to community members in IDP sites. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas SOVA will work with HLP partners to ensure that all SOVA managed sites have their eviction status mapped. SOVA will work with local authorities to mitigate evictions of displaced populations SOVA will ensure the land tenure of all public land designated for relocation of displaced populations is documented legally.
