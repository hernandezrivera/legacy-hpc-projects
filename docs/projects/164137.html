Project Summary

IMC proposes to continue its current programming implemented within its community center (RAC – Recreational Activity Center) in Jaramana, through 2 mobile teams deployed in 3 areas of Eastern Ghouta and 2 shelters in Damascus. In addition, IMC aims at scaling up its activities by deploying 2 additional mobile teams (locations to be determined based on needs assessments) to reach those most vulnerable categories facing a range of different barriers to access these services.
While case management services will be provided within the mobile teams, Jaramana community will be able to access these services at the IMC PHC clinic located in the same neighborhood.
PSS activities will be conducted both at the RAC and through the mobile teams and include non-formal education and skill development (such as literacy, English and Arabic courses), social and recreational activities (such as handcraft, music, theatre and art classes) targeting children, youth and adults.
Specifically for children with intellectual disabilities and developmental delays, IMC will continue implementing its rehabilitation and inclusion program while also targeting their parents/caregivers with training programs aiming at strengthening their capacities in assisting their children's mental, physical and educational development.
Other positive parenting skills programs such as Early Child Development (ECD) programs, Caregivers Skills Training (CST) will be offered at the RAC.
Both at RAC and mobile teams, IMC will also organize and conduct awareness raising activities on different topics related to mental health, child protection and GBV.
Overall this intervention will create opportunities for the target communities to establish new positive coping mechanisms and strengthen the existing ones, ultimately contributing in improving their wellbeing, reduce protection related risks and enhancing their resilience.

4 - Needs

Preliminary findings of the MSNA informing the HNO 2020, confirm that complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail across Syria, with an estimated 13.6 million people in need within the protection sector, out of which 42% are below 18 years of age. Damascus and Rural Damascus governorates alone have over 3.6 million people in need, out of which 3.2 in locations where the severity of needs ranks from 4 to 6. Violence, protracted displacement, and extensive destruction of infrastructure and basic services continue to erode coping mechanisms of individuals and communities, causing disruption of previously existing community protection mechanisms. Child labour and child marriage are reported in all 14 governorates, in 84% and 69% of assessed communities respectively, depriving many of their childhood and basic rights, including education. Access to basic rights and services are further jeopardized for children with disabilities and children with no official documentation. Information gathered by IMC within the communities in Eastern Ghouta served by Protection mobile team, reveals that children access to education is extremely low due to the destruction of schools and the shortage of teachers and that there is a total lack of recreational spaces, children and youth clubs or safe playgrounds. The conflict has also resulted in increased physical, verbal and psychological violence within the families, among children and even in the schools. In Jaramana, beneficiaries of different activities conducted within IMC recreational center, report lack of livelihood opportunities and financial constraints as their main challenges, which combined with displacement and disintegration of families and reduced availability of basic services, further add on their vulnerability and exposure to harmful coping mechanisms.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities 1) Case management services within mobile teams: services will include psycho-social support, internal referrals for services provided by IMC clinics; external referrals to other external service providers (legal, health, livelihood, vocational training, etc.) according to service mapping and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). IMC case managers will ensure provision of services in the respect of the principles of safety, confidentiality and self-determination.2) Psycho-social support activities will be conducted both at the RAC and through the mobile teams: - Non-formal education and skill development (such as literacy, English and Arabic courses), social and recreational activities (such as handcraft, music, theater and art classes). - Youth Empowerment Program (YEP).3) Rehabilitation and inclusion program for children with intellectual disabilities and developmental delays and their caregivers, whereby they both will participate in individual and classroom-based sessions led by IMC Psycho-Educational Specialists and speech therapists. Children will then join social and recreational activities alongside their peers with no disabilities (inclusion program). 4) Parenting skills programs: - Early Childhood Development; positive parenting skills program for parents/caregivers of children with emotional difficulties; Caregiver skills training; Peer-to-peer support sessions 5) Awareness-raising activities for staff and community members: these will be designed based on the most prevalent protection risks reported within each community and will utilize context adapted IEC materials.6) Training to IMC staff as well as community members in different topics including PFA, GBV prevention and Child Protection, as per needs assessed in each community of intervention.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1. Lack of quality services and referral expose beneficiaries to further harmLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only. 2) Supportive technical supervision provided by qualified staff. 3) Service mapping including only providers implementing ensuring survivor centered approach. Risk 2: Low participation and stigmatization of beneficiaries, resistance from the target community.Likelihood: Medium/high. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) Community and local leaders’ engagement in all phases of project cycle, including joint discussions on activities that are considered priority for the target beneficiaries; 2) Adaptation of messaging to the context and culture, and based on feedback and inputs from targeted communities and beneficiaries; 3) Community based feedback and complaint mechanism in place. Risk 3: PSEA and safeguarding related risksLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) IMC policies and procedures in place, including effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms; 2) All staff provided with training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding issues.
