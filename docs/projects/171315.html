Project Summary

The proposed project aims to reduce key mortality/morbidity rates among targeted women, men and young children in the Jordan Valley by providing mobile health clinic services. Through this one-year project, MAP will partner with PMRS to contribute to improving the health status of 26 targeted communities in the Jordan Valley, by providing essential PHC services; including preventive, diagnostic and treatment services, growth/developmental checks for young children, health awareness raising sessions and first aid training. There are two main objectives:
• To improve access to quality PHC services within the targeted vulnerable women, men and children
• To increase health resilience (ability to cope with disease, illness and trauma) within the targeted women, men and children
The provision of a dedicated clinical team will ensure continuity of care. the team will include a general physician, women’s health care doctor, two community health workers, lab technician and driver, and it will be equipped with a vehicle, clinical equipment, essential medicines and consumables, and have access to key diagnostic tests.

1 - Needs

The Palestinian National Institute of Public Health in 2018 confirmed a significant rise in levels of stunting in Bedouin children under the age of 5. Mobile clinic staff can detect early signs of malnutrition and prevent stunting, which is irreversible and leaves children unable to achieve their full social, academic, economic and health potential. Moreover, in early 2020, WHO highlighted the lack of mobile clinics for vulnerable communities in the West Bank, stating that “At the end of 2019, mobile clinic coverage of communities in Area C fell from 85 to 60 per cent of people in need, according to WHO’s tracking system”. The 2017 HNO confirmed the difficulties of communities in Area C to access PHC due to geographical distance, settler violence, checkpoints and/or barriers. The 2017 HNO also stated that the health sector is often the first and sometimes only point of access for GBV victims, therefore there is a need to improve the detection, treatment and referral of GBV victims and survivors to ensure that they can access specialized protection services. Given PMRS’s strong presence in the targeted communities, they have greatly encouraged beneficiary involvement in the project planning and design, particularly women and youth. Their feedback, together with close liaison with other stakeholders to avoid duplication of services, has informed the selection of 26 communities in Jordan Valley; supporting life-saving interventions through mobile clinic services and filling critical gaps.- https://www.ochaopt.org/content/mobile-clinics-funding-delivering-basic-health-care-vulnerable-communities-west-bank-drying- https://www.ochaopt.org/sites/default/files/humanitarian_needs_overview

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The feedback will be used during the different stages of the project: Design: the feedback will contribute to deciding the types of services provided and methods of delivery, as well as mobile clinic schedule and location in each community, in addition to identification of cases in need for home visits, to ensure services are accessible equally for all target groups. Implementation: feedback on the clinic services and facilities will be reported to MAP through phone calls, group meeting and interviews to make sure they match people’s needs. MAP will take appropriate action by communicating with PMRS, community representatives or other relevant stakeholders to address any complaints in timely manner. Monitoring and Evaluation: beneficiaries will provide feedback to MAP by the end of the project through satisfaction surveys that will reflect their overall opinion on the clinic services. Potential improvements will be considered during the design of next project phases, including adding services if necessary or changing the way certain activities are delivered like awareness raising.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MAP staff will share our relevant safeguarding policy with the local partner. Training on safeguarding and GBV will be conducted. MAP staff will conduct regular site visits to ensure proper beneficiary feedback. Direct interviews will be conducted as well as focus group discussions. The target communities are under continuous attacks by the occupation forces, and are under the risk of home demolition or property confiscation. For this reason, the clinic’s services were provided in already existing structures in the community while the staff and equipment/supplies were transported via vehicle..

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The mobile clinic is accessible for all members of the target communities. The community leaders communicate the mobile clinic schedule with all community members. Those who are unable to go to the clinic are provided with home visits by the mobile clinic team. Further, health services and education will be provided according to need and access for all will be ensured.MAP will ensure people with disability are included in project activities through providing regular home visits to cases identified in the target communities. PwD will receive required checkups and test and will be referred to other health services providers as needed. An indicator will be set to measure number of PwD reached. Regarding awareness raising, the clinic team will include PwD in the door-to-door awareness visits with their families and will provide them with produced awareness material. Moreover, feedback from PwD will be particularly encouraged and the clinic staff and MAP will ensure to collect complaints during home visits.
