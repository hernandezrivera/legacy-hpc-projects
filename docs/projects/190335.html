Project Summary

Millions are displaced. Over 90% of the Tigray population urgently needs emergency assistance. There is limited access to basic services, in particularly to food, health care and other basic facilities. The level of destruction of health facilities, water structures, schools, and the lack of supplies is critical. Humanitarian access to Tigray is severely hampered. Critical shortages of cash, fuel and relief commodities seriously hinder the humanitarian response (ECHO factsheet 14, Feb. 22). Out of the 1.8M IDPs in the region, 23% (420K) are living in collective sites that need high priority. Women and children IDPs as well as host communities require comprehensive protection services to relief from trauma and to be protected from further violence. It was reported by protection partners that severe food shortage has led to mental and emotional health concerns, especially among children.
WASH cluster coordinator has brainstormed the result of damage assessment done by RWB in August 2021, 54.35% of 17,080 water points in 5 zones were non-functional as per functionality assessment. This directly affects 3,570,682 people not having access to safe drinking water, which can cost 73 mil USD for repair and rehabilitate the systems. In almost most IDP sites (schools), the number of latrines are very few, the existing latrine doors are missing raises privacy concerns for users , Cleanliness and maintenance is lacking, No handwashing or bathing facilities and open defection is a common practices.
According to the UNOCH report, large number of schools are damaged that required rehabilitation and make ready for the possible school opening. There is a likelihood of school opening within some months but families are not in a position to purchase scholastic materials and schools also need minor rehabilitation of class rooms, latrines and intensive cleaning.
The main purpose of this project is to support 52,451 (32,618 F & 19,833 M) conflict's induced IDPs and local communities by responding to immediate needs through an integrated multi-sectoral approach. Based on an independent assessment report and information from secondary sources, the project has prioritized three sectors: Education, WASH and Protection to address the crises. The education sectoral intervention will support students with provision of scholastic materials, rehabilitation and cleaning of schools and conducting schools feeding program. The WASH intervention will enable access to potable water and improve hygiene and sanitation practices in areas currently suffering from critical water shortage. The protection component will provide essential support to the communities; because of the conflict, women and children are highly exposed to SGBV and trauma, therefore the project will provide essential services to protect these groups. The project will be implemented in four woredas (Gulo Mekeda, Kilete Awlalo, Frewini and Adigrat Town) of Eastern Zone of Tigray, for 6 months with a total budget of 1.64 million dollar.
CISP has a long presence in Tigray and has active office in Mekelle, Shire and Adigrat towns and adequate supportive and technical staff in the field of education, protection, WASH, Livelihood both in emergency and development. The Adigrat office will be responsible to coordinate and implement this project

1. - Projects activities

1. WASH 1.1. Water trucking: it will be provided for up to 3 months to address immediate water needs of the conflict affected 18681 people (14076 IDPs & 4605 Host communities). The water will be transported with tanker trucks with a capacity of 20m3 to deliver water twice a day from motorized boreholes existing in the close ranges of the respective distribution sites. The project intends to deliver water to beneficiaries with WASH cluster minimum standard of 7.5 liters per day per person. 1.2. Provision of water treatment chemicals In order to ensure the safety of the beneficiaries & protect them from water borne diseases , WTC will be provided to 6510 HHs (32550 individuals) using unprotected water sources for human consumption. 1.3. Provision of 13 water storage tankers of 10m3 capacity to IDP sites & health institutions to serve as water drop off points for water trucking. 1.4. Expansion of water system to communities, schools & health institutions from the main water sources to health posts & schools where IDPs are settled & there is no access to water. 1.4.1. Pipeline extension from Awlalo water source to Kisanet School located in Kilete Awlalo woreda: currently the school is being a shelter of over1020 IDPs displaced from Astbi & other parts of the region. 1.4.2. Pipeline maintenance & extension from Freweini town water system to Fereweini Primary Hospital, which was damaged during the conflict. 1.4. Promote hygiene & sanitation practices to prevent the risk of water borne diseases & COVID-19. According to the assessment conducted to prepare the proposal, hygiene & sanitation practices in the IDPs sites are so poor, that public awareness creation to promote hygiene & sanitation practices is very necessary. 3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

CISP has a strong alliance in implementation of emergency and development projects in the region for more than three decades and have a strong and well structured working relationship with other humanitarian agencies. This provides a good foundation for managing this project in an organized and harmonized manner. Coordination is a key strategy to create a resilient people in disaster prone areas like east and south zones of Tigray through creating synergy and complementarity and avoiding duplication of resource and efforts among various stakeholders. Despite, implementations of project hampered by lack of access, following the current conflict in Tigray a number of humanitarian agencies are newly arriving in the region to support the people in need, and different platforms have been created / reactivated to coordinate all humanitarian and development activities in the region and regular meeting are being undertaking by these cluster leaders. Due to the constraints for access to some areas and lack of humanitarian data, the CISP is in regular contact with humanitarian actors in the region to strengthen coordination of ongoing and planned humanitarian interventions. CISP is an active member of these platforms, which helps to coordinate and create synergy with other similar operation including the government emergency response plan. CISP is a member and active participants of in most coordination platforms in the operational regions and federal levels. For instance, CISP is a member and active participant of WASH, Education, Protection, Sh/NFI and ATF clusters working at federal and regional stages as well as playing a major role in Tigray cluster. Therefore, coordination of all actions including this one is a mandatory mechanism that will be realized throughout the project period. The coordination, which is aimed to create synergy and complementarity will be done sharing information, engaging in meetings, undertaking a joint planning, assessment and conducting monitoring and evaluation collectively. In addition, the CISP will use humanitarian coordination platforms/review meetings to share the good practices and project experience for advocacy, replication, and scaling up good practices. CISP has also influenced key actors to mainstream protection and ensure inclusion of women and children's in humanitarian actions and help to establish a co-ordination mechanism in the regional different clusters. The project will also adopt the Ethiopia Humanitarian Country Team (EHCT), guiding principles to coordinate the humanitarian operations in Tigray, and border areas of Afar and Amhara regions.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will consider the Feedback and Compliance Mechanism (FCM) as accountability for the affected populations (AAP) and the donor to deliver the project objectives to the best satisfaction of end beneficiaries/clients through client response programming approach (CRP). The CRP will systematically listen to the perspectives of our clients and use their feedback to make decisions and communicate and explain to our clients how their feedback has informed our actions. The mechanism involves people at every stage and has been developed in a participatory way with representatives from all stakeholder groups including the most vulnerable and excluded. This was done through involving the community in the process of assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation and then set up complaints and response mechanisms including listening exercises; perceptions studies; community score cards; story-telling, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), SOP and others. In case that a project receives complaints or feedback that fall outside our remit, we will have a system in place to refer the feedback onwards to someone who is in a position to deal with the issue (known as referral pathways). The mechanism will handle sensitive and non-sensitive complaints with the proper procedures to handle both types. The mechanism for handling complaints will also take into account specific contexts and safety to recognize potential dangers and risks to all stakeholders involved in each process, including staff, complainants, witnesses and others. The mechanisms will include ways to prevent harassment, injury or harm to those wishing to raise an issue or complaint. The system will ensure the confidentiality of program participants when managing their complaints. This will assure that any information given is restricted to a limited number of people on a need-to-know basis and is not disseminated more widely. Feedback and complaints will be handled objectively and with fairness. All users will be made aware of the procedures; so that they understand its purpose, have sufficient information on how to access it and understand how it works. It will also be accessible for all groups within the community, including the most vulnerable and those with disabilities; this will be done through multiple channels to ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, responding and resolving complaints. The system will be guided by policy and procedures that set out the key characteristics of the mechanism, such as who is responsible for receiving and handling complaints, how feedback will be recorded, reviewed and actions communicated, who has access to what information. Response time. The policy or guidelines will also clearly articulate, or signpost the process for managing sensitive complaints. The FCM will also be complemented by post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the clients' feedback and satisfaction on items distributed by the project.
