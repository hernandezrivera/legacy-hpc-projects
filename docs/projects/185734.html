Project Summary

"The project incorporates with both the Rohingya refugees and the extremely vulnerable host communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The camp side activities This Activity will support 750 Rohingya refugees HHs with Community Cooking and Learning Centres (CLC) and Gender Based Violence (GBV) awareness funded by World Vision Hong Kong and New Zealand-MFAT. CLCs complementary safe spaces for women and men to engage with services, learn and hear relevant messaging mainly Kitchen Gardening, Nutrition food preparation, Pastry making, IYCF, GBV, DRR, Child and Mother care to improve safety for women and children. Also empower Lead Mothers to capacities and support community others people. Provide Vegetable gardening training and inputs to involve women in kitchen gardening to support fresh vegetable for family nutrition.
On the other hand, at host communities will address the short-term food security crisis and medium-term livelihood recovery needs of most vulnerable 5229 HHs. Targeted HHs will be drawn from Ukhiya and Teknaf Upozilla with priority given to villages adjacent to refugee settlements while simultaneously addressing other social dynamics such as women’s mobility restrictions and income generating restrictions by engaging males in the same communities through building awareness on GBV prevention through men care group. Vulnerable will be provided home gardening and off farm IGA training and input vouchers to purchase home gardening tools and certified seeds for nutritious food production. Trainings by government extension workers will be facilitated to provide ongoing support for project beneficiaries beyond the project lifetime. HHs will be supported with financial literacy training and business plan development for income generating activities (IGA), along with multi-purpose cash transfers to start/ enhance IGAs.
Another segment of the project will be working to support vulnerable HHs in Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub-Districts in Cox’s Bazar to lead them out of poverty. Especially 2880 ultra-poof HHs will be receive productive assets and strengthen producer group and local market place construction and ultra-poor family link with service provider.
"

1 - Identified Needs

"Forty-eight months after 700,000 women, men and children fled violence in Burma en masse, the Rohingya refugee population in need in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh stands at 896,879 , of which 51.52% are women. As well as the host community people in Cox’s Bazar stands in a very vulnerable situation. The Government of Bangladesh leads an international humanitarian response effort reaching 1.2 million Rohingya refugees and host community members in the Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas. Prior to August 2018, Cox’s Bazar District was classified as Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). The rapid refugee influx disproportionally impacted Ukhiya and Teknaf, where the majority of new arrivals settled. The population of these two upazilas nearly tripled with refugees now outnumbering Bangladeshi’s 3:1. Despite ongoing blanket distribution of staple food, the proportion of Host Community- HC House-holds HHs with a poor or borderline Food Consumption Score spiked from 31% at the start of the influx to 80% one year later with dietary diversity also falling from 3.7 to 2.6. Of the total Ukhiya and Teknaf Bangladeshi population of 541,021, approximately 378,715 are currently in Crisis.While rates of malnutrition have been historically lower in Teknaf and Ukhiya than national averages. According to the 2016 SMART nutrition survey, stunting rates are notably higher—at 43% for Ukhiya and 48% for Teknaf, exceeding the 40% World Health Organizations “very high” threshold—indicating chronic malnutrition driven by poor socioeconomic conditions and possibly early exposure to illnesses. Prevalence of underweight children in 2016 was nearly 33%, exceeding the WHO’s “very high” threshold of 15%. Data from soon after the influx showed minimum dietary diversity for women in the host communities as not too varied from refugee women: Only 30% of host community females achieved minimum dietary diversity (compared to 24% of refugee women) and an average of 3.61 food groups consumed (compared to 3.39 for refugee women) under the acceptable threshold of five.The worsening crisis is primarily due to the twin phenomena of rising food prices and falling wages. Soon after the influx, HH food expenditure increased to 18.73 percent in Ukhiya and 16.75 percent in Teknaf, while the proportion of “poor” HHs (living on <$60/month) increased from 9.6% to 21.7%. The impact is greater for female wage earners, who on average are paid 60% of a male day laborer wage. 88% of local HHs depend on a single source of income, making them highly vulnerable to the economic and environmental shock of the Rohingya influx. Concurrent with their deteriorating food security, the attitudes of Ukhiya and Teknaf residents toward refugees is worsening: 79% blamed Rohingya for their increased cost of living, Tensions have increased since January 2019, particularly among communities adjacent to refugee camps. Local people have also called for equity in aid between them and the refugees. Enhanced humanitarian investment in the host community is urgently needed to diffuse rising tensions. The Pandemic has effected the Ultra-poor families in the Host communities. The Project will face challenges to achieved its goal due to this COVID19 crisis. The vulnerable & ultra-poor families needed more support to ensure food security than the general population of the country. Stable accommodation is the urgent need for these families which is often undermined. This project plans to implement different learning session for awareness, Cash transfer through cash for work for immediate support. The vegetable gardening and develop marketing activities will develop the capacity of beneficiaries on fresh production and access to nutritious food for Rohingya and host community people. Project plan to involve host community people in IGA program to provide assets and capacities both for create opportunity for family and also men care approach to reduce gender vulnerability for life balance. "
