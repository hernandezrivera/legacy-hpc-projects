Project Summary

The project will target the most vulnerable communities in Area C with humanitarian interventions aimed at alleviating the challenging living conditions of the target population. These vulnerable communities suffer from limited access to water services due to the Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area. The proposed interventions will target unserved and underserved communities with essential WASH services that will also protect the most vulnerable families under threat of forcible transfer. The main targeted areas of the project are the farming and herding families who depend on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods. The most urgent need for these communities is the lack of access to safe water, at the required quality, availability and affordability at the household level. Families have developed coping mechanisms to respond to the limited access to water - mainly through rain water harvesting and the collection in cisterns to deal with the water shortages they are suffering from. This has also forced the families to reduce their water consumption to less than 30 l/c/d and to reduce their expenditure on basic services, such as health and education to cover high water costs.
Deteriorating socio-economic conditions, increasing poverty and heightened protection risks are compromising the rights and living conditions of families in the Hebron City and the H2 area as reflected in the 2018 OCHA led H2 household survey. According to the UN Strategy for Hebron, approximately, 40,000 Palestinians (20% of the total population of Hebron City) reside in H2. Four Israeli settlements in H2 inside the city with approximately 500 settlers. This is in addition to the presences of Israeli forces and the segregation of roads and restricting access to education, health services and water supply, all this has led to the closure of businesses and abandonment of houses and therefore the displacement of the inhabitants of H2 area. There are 1409 Palestinian families in the H2 area living within the Israeli security restricted area in severe conditions due to the Israeli restrictions against movement, shelters rehabilitation/maintenance and community services development.
The intervention will target unconnected households in Area C communities to increase their capacity to treat and safely store water at the household level and to increase the quality of water consumed. This will be ensured through the installation of water filters; hygiene practices will also be promoted and improved through awareness sessions especially for those practicing open defecation. Also, the most vulnerable community institutions in Area C and H2 area will be targeted, through rehabilitation/construction of the WASH facilities in 10 institutions (schools, clinics, youth and women centers).
The proposed interventions ensure protection mainstreaming as all activities are guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, as well as participation and empowerment. Gender and protection mainstreaming are Integrated as per the checklists facilitated by the WASH Cluster. The full intervention will also consider safety and dignity of affected population, meaningful access for all WASH facilities, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2018, 156,000 people across the West Bank are either unconnected to a water network or receive water only once a week or less. Area C communities are particularly affected by this water problem. In total, around 270,000 Palestinians in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure. The assessments conducted by the UNICEF led WASH Cluster in 2017, led to the PWA approved Water Master Plan for Area C. This plan details the water needs of 247 communities in Area C, indicating that most of the targeted communities consume below 30 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms. Further assessments conducted by AAH during the summer 2018 in Area C indicated that water scarcity is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. This is considered a context in which urgent humanitarian action is required to remedy, mitigate or avert an immediate and direct humanitarian risk. Seam zone areas suffer from lack of connections to water networks and violence. Settlers regularly target Palestinian livelihoods and water sources, the combined effect of these constraints, coupled with a growing number of infrastructure demolitions, push families into displacement, poverty and food insecurity. A further assessment conducted by WW-GVC in August 2019 shows that 38 communities in the West Bank are dependent on the direct purchase of water, have a daily per capita consumption below 60 liters, and rely exclusively on water trucking for 84% of the summer period. Over 90% of them are in Area C where access constraints pose challenges for the trucks to reach remote localities. This is usually coupled with subsequent increases in the final cost of water, up to 50 NIS/m³. Constrained access to water for drinking and household use purposes particularly affects children, elders and people with disabilities as well as women and girls in three different dimensions: 1) quality of life due to restricted access to drinking water and subsequent obstacles to access to hygiene materials and safe menstrual hygiene management; 2) feeling of protection, due to the long distance to fetch water exposing to settlers and Israeli Forces violence; 3) division of duties, given that no access to safe water affects the roles of women, children and men reducing the positive effect of the division of labour between family members. Moreover, access to adequate sanitation and safe household/personal hygiene is a concern in communities facing water shortages in the West Bank. Water quality surveillance conducte by CESVI (2018&2019) proved that regardless water source and quality at the point of delivery, water for human consumption is contaminated in the area of intervention due to cross contamination during collection and storage. Additional contamination is also present inside the household before consumption. Children under 5 are particularly vulnerable to waterborne diseases, as are pregnant who could expose their fetus to impaired growth; and children being breast-fed run the risk of malnutrition. In late 2017, 175 households were assessed in Area C of Hebron governorate. It confirmed that open defecation was a common practice. Indeed, 14.2% of people had no latrines at all, while 64.1% very basic latrines, often not properly used. 69.3% of the people reported not having the possibility to hand-wash in the toilet, confirming a hygienic risk for public health. It is assumed that the practice of open defecation is common for 10% of the target population, who do not have/will not have connection to piped water network in isolated rural areas. A further assessment has been conducted in 2019 showing similar results. Finally, the situation is confirmed by the Oxfam study “Addressing the needs of Palestinian households in area C of the West Bank”.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will facilitate coordination meetings with stakeholders to coordinate the activities and locations to avoid gaps and overlapping. Municipalities and village councils in the targeted locations have been identified as local partners for collection of data related to the baseline and end line. UNICEF will lead the advocacy efforts with key stakeholders and International Community, linked with the provision of the following WASH interventions: 1. Rehabilitation/construction of water networks for communities:Construction of pipelines and filling points for Bedouin and herding communities: the pipelines will be connected to the PWA/municipality systems. 14 communities in Hebron H2 and Area C will have improved their access to water through transmission pipelines and water networks; it will reduce household dependency on high cost water trucking and poor water quality in cisterns and enhance their ownership. This will be implemented by contractors or/and village council members and beneficiaries and will build upon their knowledge of the area and of the potential risks involved. It will improve the capacity of water service providers, targeting the most vulnerable communities. 2.Construction/rehabilitation of household cisterns for vulnerable families, with special attention to female-headed households and families with disabled family members in Area C and seam zones: 15 household cisterns will be constructed in Hebron governorates. A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted including female-headed households and families with disabled members. The activities will be implemented by households to reduce the Area C construction associated risks. This will include chlorination campaign and distribution of chlorine tablets to the community to improve the quality of new storage capacity. 3. Construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in households and institutions: WASH facilities in 10 institutions will be evaluated and rehabilitated based on the WASH cluster minimum standards. Moreover, the communities at risk of sewer flooding during winter will be supported by the construction/rehabilitation of their sewer systems. This will target communities facing serious environmental and health crises due to the bad sanitation services. It will be associated with hygiene promotion campaign to improve hygiene practices and water management skills for women and children. 4. Improving access to safe drinking water through water trucking and development of national/regional/local governance mechanisms. This will prevent forcible transfer of targeted communities by improving their access to WASH services with an integrated protection approach by working at three levels: i) distributing trucked water; ii) developing capacities of water distribution chain stakeholder at local, regional and national level; and iii) development of the governance system to manage water distribution. The project will increase the conditions of unserved households, targeting 6,687people in 38 communities and 9 schools for 3 months. 5. Improving access to adequate sanitation and safe household/personal hygiene: This activity will be composed of the following four components: 1) conducting a baseline assessment to evaluate the access to WASH practice; 2) installing 400 households with Gravity Driven Membrane technology to minimize the risk of cross-contamination and training in 400 households; 3) collecting and analyzing water samples in households from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use and analyzing samples to assess water quality; and 4) promoting hygiene and sanitation practices with kit distribution, especially for households practicing open defecation. Gender and protection mainstreaming are Integrated as per the checklists facilitated by the WASH Cluster. All proposed activities will ensure protection principles on safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.

3 - Indicators

The project activities will be continuously monitored by UNICEF and its implementing partners to ensure that the project is delivered on time and on budget. The project will also be monitored and evaluated within the UNICEF Integrated Monitoring and Evaluation Plan applied across all UNICEF programs. Project implementation will be reviewed as part of the Annual Programme Review jointly conducted by UNICEF and project implementing partners. Regular field visits to the locations and quarterly progress reports produced by partners and submitted to UNICEF constitute an integral part of the project monitoring mechanism.UNICEF programme officers will conduct regular field visits to the project sites as well as review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to systematically ensure quality of the interventions during programmatic visits. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. The programmatic visits will also assess bottlenecks, barriers, challenges and lessons learned. This is in accordance with UNICEF’s requirement for the Harmonized Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT). Financial spot checks will also be conducted in line with HACT requirements. The findings of the monitoring visits will be the basis for adjusting the level of technical / administrative engagement to ensure optimal achievement of the project. UNICEF will have regular meetings with the main stakeholders (such as PWA and the WBWD), beneficiary communities and implementing partners to discuss the findings of field monitoring and adopt solutions in case of constraints. The implementing partners will establish and maintain a system for monitoring progress of the implementation of their specific Project Document using the defined results, including outcomes, outputs, indicators and targets set out in the logical framework. Updates on indicators will be submitted through (at least) quarterly reports to UNICEF, through UNICEF’s online reporting system. The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted communities through improving their access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy, and improve the coordination platforms.
