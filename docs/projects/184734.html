Project Summary

There are protracted humanitarian crisis in Somalia is multi-layered and complex. Limited development coupled with recurring climatic shocks, such as drought and riverine-/flash-flooding give rise to high levels of need among affected populations, while insecurity and conflict severely hinder access to humanitarian actors. The majority of internally displaced persons (IDPs) reside in overcrowded shelters in densely populated urban areas, further increasing their exposure to the risks and impact of COVID-19. (REACH DSA Hobyo and Cadaado Factsheet March 2021). A recent in Mudug (Galkacyo town), Middle Shabelle (Jowhar town) and Galgaduud ( Dusamareb and Cadaado town) IDP site verification and assessment exercise headed by CCCM cluster conducted in Galkayo and Dusamareb,Cadaado and Jowhar in 2021, identified 155 IDP sites (90 in Galkacyo, 47 Dusamareb, 12 Jowhar, and 6 in Cadaado) hosting more than 40,000 households. Compared to the last site verification conducted in Sept 2021, there was an increase of more 20 IDP sites with more than 10,000 individuals.. In response to this, PDA will strengthen in Galkacyo, Dusamareb, Cadaado and Jowhar, site-level coordination structures to ensure access to quality basic services, improve living conditions in sites and mitigate protection concerns strengthen community-based led activities promoting on community self-management.

1. - Projects activities

Overall Objective: To improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs in sites and settlements and ensure equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need, with a clear focus on moving toward attaining durable solutions with the full participation of the displaced and host communities Activity 1.1.1: Site-level coordination structures to ensure access to quality essential services: PDA will set up CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts and establish CCCM in relevant coordination forums. There is a field-level coordination structure in Galkacyo, Dumareb, Cadaado and Jowhar towns counterparts, line ministers, partners, IDP leaders, and CCCM in relevant coordination forums at the field level. PDA will consult camp leaders/gatekeepers and partners working at the site level for a harmonized approach to maintaining high standards to improve coordination at the site level. PDA will set a procedure for camp closure of the IDP settlements in line with the cluster’s exit strategy for long-term durable solutions as part of the exit strategy. Activity 1.1.2: Site maintenance to improve living conditions in sites and mitigate protection concerns: Relying on data collected from the field, partners, local coordination meetings, clusters, as well as through multi-cluster assessments, PDA will map existing community infrastructures in the IDP settlements to ascertain which need improvement to minimize protection risks and ensure safety within target sites, PDA intends to install 60 solar lights in communal areas (especially in areas leading to latrines and water points), which will aim to increase protection and prevent SGBV at the community level. Activity 1.1.3: Access to information and community participation. In collaboration with Shelter and CCCM cluster and other relevant partners (including IOM cluster lead), PDA will conduct intention surveys to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported. This will involve conducting Detailed Site Assessments of all target sites basis then establishing/updating the master list of sites at an appropriate geographic level. After collection and analysis of this data PDA, disseminate to the relevant stakeholders to inform humanitarian programming. Under the leadership of/collaboration with the Ministry of Health at the federal government and Southwest Level - The National Risk Communication and Community Engagement Task Force, PDA will conduct massive awareness-raising campaigns on COVID-19 in Galkayco and Cadaado IDP camps in Galmudug and Jowhar of Hirshabelle state. This will involve risk communication and awareness campaigns (including debunking of myths, rumours and stigma) among vulnerable populations, including IDPs, persons with disabilities and marginalized communities. In addition, COVID-19 messaging will be done through mass media through radio, television, print posters and billboards. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion and marginalization/Protection mainstreaming: To avoid tensions with the local community, PDA will include host communities in decisions on-site location and planning. Also, PDA will consult host communities in identifying assistance needed and consider providing the host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic services and facilities. In regards to evictions, PDA will liaise with the local authorities to know the existing land and property tenure arrangements, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land when selecting sites. Also, PDA will advocate with local authorities and/or property owners to obtain formal agreement to use the land, as a precaution to reduce the risk of eviction, rental costs or conflict erupting due to lack of clarity on these issues. Furthermore, PDA will identify the power dynamics in the area which will include who has access to camp services, who manages facilities, who can influence decisions on camp services. PDA will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Preventing risks of exclusion and marginalization 1. Reduced risk, PDA will ensure that access routes and distance to the camp services are accessible to all as well as the vulnerable groups. To improve access, PDA will introduce village based service delivery to those who cannot access services from distance and deliver their entitlement to their homes. This will be done through robust community mobilization leading to home based distribution and mobilizing young community members to help the most vulnerable ones (i.e disabled and elderly). PDA will routinely collect beneficiary data disaggregated by sex and age to effectively improve programming, PDA will ensure that men and women have fair and equal access to work opportunities in the camp and that they receive the same benefits for their work. Also, offer livelihood opportunities for persons with disabilities and older persons. Furthermore, PDA will consider the gender balance of camp staff and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. PDA will also establish a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for accountability. PDA will use information boards to display CRM phone numbers during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaints. PDA will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. Protection of civilians/conflict violence: PDA will ensure, that the location and access routes to camp services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. PDA will identify areas in and around the camp that could be potentially unsafe or risk of violence, especially gender-based violence e.g. around showers, latrines, child-friendly spaces, schools and routes to schools, health facilities, water collection points, etc. This will be done PDA will also advocate for installation lights outside and on the access route to the facility with the government of South West State Somalia. Besides, PDA ensures effective safety and security measures are in place including establishing community patrol groups, where possible, to support the prevention of forced recruitment, looting, GBV, and other threats.
