Project Summary

The project intends to provide integrated inclusive nutrition and health services targeting vulnerable population including children, adolescent girls, PLW and chronic ill and elderly people. The ultimate goal of the project is to contribute to the improvement of nutritional status of the camp and host communities and increasing access and coverage of nutrition and health services. CMAM, IYCF, SC treatment and micronutrient supplementation are key strategies the project employs to treat and prevent malnutrition. Facility and community based approach will be used to reach the community though various interventions including growth monitoring and promotion (GMP), mass screening, referral and treatment of malnutrition (CMAM), C-MAMI, IYCF counseling services.Active male forum and mother support group for community awaresss and active partcipation regarding project modification-needs will be exceuted. Integrated health services and micro-nutrient supplementation for children, adolescent girls and PLW. With vast knowledge and experience in emergency and disaster management, the project will have contingency plan to respond to manmade and natural disasters including disease out breaks, monsoon season and cyclones with close collaboration with all relevant stakeholders. Project will also focus on the beneficiary feedback/needs/recommendation every stages through monitoring, complain feedback mechanism and nutrition emergency surveillence sytem etc.Being Active memeber of PSEA network project will ensure the prevention and response mechanisms are in place regarding PSEA.

1 - Identified Needs

"The last updated ACF-SMART nutrition survey round 5 at camp findings in 2020 indicated an overall prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among children 6-59 months of 11.3% (95% C.I 8.9-14.2), and prevalence of chronic malnutrition (stunting) of 30.9%, depicting serious and critical levels of malnutrition respectively as per the WHO/UNICEF standards at makeshift camp. The prevalence of GAM per WHZ among children 6-59 months was 14.8% (11.2-19.2) which falls in the “High” threshold of public health emergency concern as per the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF threshold for acute malnutrition, with an upper confidence interval, 19.2%, exceed the threshold of “Very High” at register camp of Nayapara. According to ACF-SMART survey (Jan-Feb 2021) for host community overall weighted GAM prevalence by WHZ among children 6-59 months was 9.3% (7.5 –11.5 95% C.I) of which 9.9% (7.2 - 13.5 95% C.I.) in Ukhiya and 8.9% (6.8 - 11.6 95% C.I.) in Teknaf Upazila. The overall acute malnutrition situation is in the “Medium” category of public health emergency concern, with an upper confidence interval within 10-14% to the “High” or Serious threshold.The prevalence of anemia among children aged under five years remains a major public health concern within the operation with an overall prevalence of 39.1% (threshold 40.The COVID-19 pandemic outbreak since the beginning of 2020, considering the possible impact on overall malnutrition will be addition burden.Based on the above data ACF identified the following needs to address:1. Inclusive access to malnutrition treatment services (OTP, SC, TSFP)2. Inclusive access to malnutrition prevention services (BSFP, micronutrient supplementation, IYCF, nutrition and health education)3. Access to information Infant and young child feeding and C-MAMI services4. Micronutrient supplementation for children, adolescent girls and PLWs 5. Access to services for disable children6. Strong referral mechanism and treat to SAM with medical complication to Stabilization Centre7. Linking with nutrition beneficiary to health service needs"
