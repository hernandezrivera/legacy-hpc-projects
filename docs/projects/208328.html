Project Summary

Considering the continuing deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the Far North regions, which
affect access to quality education and protection services, IRC in partner with DG ECHO proposes to
continue to implement an integrated protection approach base on the PEACE project lessons learned,
to Improve the well-being, resilience and learning environment of school-age girls and boys, the most
impacted by humanitarian crises in Cameroon's Far North region context.
. IRC offers an integrated approach to education and child protection, to improve the well-being,
development, safety and learning environment of school-age girls and boys while strengthening local
community-based services and child protection. IRC will work to ensure that the children 18,432 targeted
by this Action are safe in their schools and communities and receive support when they experience harm.
Through the programming of innovative activities initiated in the PEACE project, pupils in Koranic schools
will continue to have access to quality education in a safe and secure environment. IRC will integrate a
holistic localization approach into the Action through the formalization of a partnership with a local
organization with the capacity to reach the children and populations most affected by the crisis, including
providing psychosocial support, case management and community engagement interventions. Finally,
particular emphasis will be placed on coordination with state actors, especially in terms of the
implementation of Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP) activities, but also on supporting educational
actors to develop risk and disaster reduction plans in schools and learning institutions. Based on evidence
and aimed at improving access, quality and safety in education, the project aims to achieve the following
results:
- School-age girls and boys enroll in and attend safe, effective, and appropriate formal and non-
formal education programmes, supported by caregivers, communities, and local partners.
- School-age girls and boys receive support and access to protection services to reduce their
exposure to risk and improve their safety and access to education.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

n Cameroon, IRC found that targeted attacks caused 144 primary schools in Mayo Sava and Logone et Chari to close in the 2016-2017 school year, with other schools having been taken over by the military or used to shelter displaced population. The financial costs of schooling have been identified as a salient concern in Cameroon, especially for girls, refugees and IDPs. Many caregivers are reluctant to send their children to school due to safety concerns and the lack of safety and early warning systems to prevent and mitigate recurring risks. The ability of adults living in times of conflict and displacement to provide nurturing care for their children may be hampered by their own experiences of trauma, compounded by economic hardship and social isolation. This can result in an increased incidence of children’s exposure to interfamilial conflict, violence, or emotional neglect. Many teachers lack qualifications, experience, and basic skills in the projet target areas, with low teacher capacity being a significant barrier to achieving quality education and learning outcomes for children. A lack of basic teaching and learning materials is also a challenge to the quality of instruction, and the continued influx of displaced children is a factor to the extremely overstretched classrooms and school conditions. Abuse and exploitation of children is widespread in the West Africa region, with the majority of children experiencing violent discipline and more than 80% of children having experienced at least one deprivation of their basic rights. Protection concerns are compounded in conflict affected areas of the Lake Chad Region due to the presence of non-state armed actors and on-going violence. Displacement exposes girls and boys to protection risks including separation from their families and exposure to violence in their homes and communities, heightening levels of distress. Displaced children are less likely to be enrolled in school and highly implicated in household livelihoods through potentially risky activities, such as collecting firewood. Girls face a particularly high risk of sexual violence and early marriage. Boys face heightened risks of recruitment into armed groups and different forms of banditry, leading them into a cycle of violence that inhibits their access to learning opportunities and community life.
