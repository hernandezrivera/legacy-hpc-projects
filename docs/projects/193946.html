Project Summary

This Food security and livelihoods (FSL) project is designed with the overall objective of saving lives and building resilience of the most vulnerable HHs and reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition through ensuring availability of nutritious food to 160,000 HHs (800,000 individuals 416,000 female & 384,000 male) (in 21 localities in the five states including (Kadugli, Abu jubayhah, Abassiya, Ar rashad and Al buram localities in South Kordfan state), (El Kurmuk, Geissan, Wa almahi and Baw localities of Blue Nile Region), (El Geniena, Jebel moon, Kulbus and Kreinik in West Darfur), (Bendisi, Gharb jabal marrah, Shamal jabal marrah and Wasat jabal marrah of Central Darfur) and (Karrari, Jebel awlia and Um bada localities in Khartoum state). Besides, this project is prepared in alignment with three FSL strategic objectives (SOs). The project also focuses on two major areas, the first focus area is ensuring conflict and other disaster affected people in the five states including IDPs, returnees, host communities and nomads receive timely, adequate and required assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock. The other focus area is improving availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis. To achieve these, WR participated in inter-agency assessments conducted through 2022 in West Darfur and Blue Nile in the tribal conflict and defined urgent needs of displaced people and vulnerable hosting communities. Also, WR conducted Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) and key informant interviews in Khartoum, South Kordfan and Central Darfur states to define needs. To this end, project plan has been prepared with active involvement of the community and activities are prioritized for implementation based on community priorities and triangulated with data from different sources (IPC, HNO and CFSVA). IDPs, returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities are the central focus of the project. Provision of agricultural inputs, improving livestock veterinary services & water schemes, income generating activities, conditional and unconditional food assistance, various capacity buildings, saving and loan schemes, restocking and gender & environment are the main focus areas. Even though women constitutes significant portion of the households, they are often hindered by many traditional norms from making real progress which make it difficult for them to access new agricultural technologies as well as improved livelihood opportunities. This project will mainly target women headed households in order to build their capacities which allows them to sufficiently increase their productivity beyond consumption.
The project will integrate protection beside FSL activities focusing on awareness sessions on PSEA, GBV and child protection referral pathways for project staff and community based structures working in/supporting FSL program implementation. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. World Relief will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries themselves. As described further below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls and children while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability. Furthermore, this project is designed to not cause negative impact on the environment. Appropriate protection measures including seedlings production and plantation, community awareness on environment, safe disposal of wastes, germination tests and focal on local verities.

1 - Needs

The Latest acute food insecurity data indicates that around 9.65 million people across Sudan were highly food insecure and classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3 or worse). The situation is projected to worsen between June and September, with up to 11.7 million people (a further increase of 2 million) likely to be in Crisis. According to Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, An estimated 3,245,496 people face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity in West Darfur, Central Darfur, Blue Nile, Khartoum and South Kordfan states (IPC 2022) which need urgent action to save lives 691,781 people from total estimation are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 4), while around 2,553,715 people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3). The increase number of people under stress and any additional shock could push them to severe levels of hunger. Some target localities in areas affected by floods during the rainy season have shifted to higher levels of food insecurity compared to 2022 projection; all localities targeted by FSL/WR are in IPC phase 3 to phase 4. According to CFSVA economic vulnerability plays a major role in this food insecurity as 95 percent of households spend more than 65 percent of their total expenditure on food. On state level, the highest prevalence of food insecurity was observed in the Darfurs, the Kordofans, and Blue Nile. West Darfur had the highest level of food insecurity at 65 percent, followed by Central Darfur at 59 percent, North Darfur at 56 percent and Blue Nile at 50 percent. West and Central Darfur, Khartoum host the highest caseload of IPC Phase 3 and Phase 4 during the projection people from June to September 2022. Based on various food security assessments, the most vulnerable groups are IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities. The highest severity of acute food insecurity is recorded in all five states. The number of households facing crisis impact is expected to remain high among IDPs and people recently affected by tribal clashes in Darfur, Kordofan flood-affected households, Ethiopian and South Sudanese refugees in Blue Nile and poor households who have exhausted their food stocks, and urban poor households. Intercommunal conflict incidents have significantly increased in Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordfan and some other parts of the country during 2022. The impact of floods and heavy rains damaged and destroyed, homes, infrastructures, cropped land and livelihood assets. Because of stressed food security situation and multiple effects of the lean season, outcome data on nutrition showing a worsened nutritional status especially among children under-five. Massive folks of insects in Darfur states with adverse effect on crops which undermine efforts of vulnerable people (women, children and other people with special needs). Absence of organic fertilizers usage to improve the chemical (nutritional) and physical properties of the soil; Communities poor access to income sources due to lack of skill and market networks; Environmental degradation exacerbated by firewood need, burning charcoal as a means of income, cutting trees for construction and income, and overgrazing the grassland contributed to degradation of the natural vegetation and soil, which also counter affected the agricultural production and depletion of water sources; Poor agro forestry, soil & water conservation practices contribute to soil erosion; limited skill of agricultural extension worker.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project provides maximum care not to cause pollute the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activities implementation. Environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed sensitive to the environment and necessary mitigation measures will be to reduce the risks. The project will not encourage the use of toxic chemicals and commercial fertilizers, rather the communities will be trained and supported to apply biological and cultural pest control and organic farming. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to Do-No-Harm principles. WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create awareness to the community on environment. The irrigation facilities and the hand dug wells for livestock will be accompanied with drainage ditches to avoid water stagnation. The project works together with the Ministry of Agriculture so that the different types of inputs provided to the community pass through quality check based government standards and other quality check measures. In addition, the seeds and related inputs will be inspected for presence of pests and weed seeds not to introduce to the localities. In this regard, the project will not import or introduce planting materials out of the Ecological areas. Through other programs, WR will support with several tree seedlings in the project proposed localities which will be distributed to individual households, planed in public places, and provided to different institutions including health facilities, schools and social centers. Moreover, there will be environmental protection campaign at villages’ level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensure that all activities to contribute to improving the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women access to and control over assets created by the project. Women and girls are overburdened in domestic and agricultural activities and are sole responsible for in the household. Besides, women have almost no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and develop strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. Women and women headed households will get priority to address their needs. More than 60% of the beneficiary households will be women. Improved technologies that reduces the burden of women are included in the project so that women get enough time for domestic and other reproductive activities. WR recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable/minority groups. While implementing this project, WRS will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights including SGBV problems. Mobile veterinary services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. The organization will also be sure to promote conflict sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations will be mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. In addition, the M&E system segregates data by gender and age groups to measure and analyze how the project is affecting different groups. The organization will focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding of the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity. In addition, the organization ensure that all staff, volunteers and contractors working with the organization sign code of conduct. WR will fully utilize Do No Harm approach in the implementation of FSL interventions ensuring that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without any discrimination

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The actions proposed by WR are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through needs assessment, field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. In addition, the intended communities have also confirmed their commitment to fully participate through the entire project life cycle. Overall the community will be involved in the project as depicted below. Through their representatives the community had been involved in needs assessment and formulation of this project; WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level development committees and extension workers. These committees established to oversee the work of WR in their respective areas or locality. In addition, CAHWs and extension workers, who contribute to the strengthening of livestock and agriculture-based livelihoods of the communities, and provide feedback through various reporting and communication mechanisms. The community level engagement materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators conduct monthly meeting at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box to get the feedback from beneficiary communities. The beneficiary and community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR coordinators will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with all concerned including higher management. Also, the actions taken to the community findings will be shared back to the community.
