Project Summary

UNICEF proposes admissions into severe acute malnutrition therapeutic treatment as an entry point to target beneficiaries for emergency safety net cash assistance. This design aims at channeling assistance to the most vulnerable families using severe acute malnutrition (SAM) as a proxy for extreme vulnerability. In addition, to prevent children from relapsing into acute malnutrition by addressing the multifaceted causes of malnutrition (including extreme poverty, lack of access to basic services, disease outbreaks, poor hygiene) and aiming to enhance impact of nutrition therapeutic interventions. Cash assistance could also help reduce related negative coping mechanisms such as sharing nutrition therapeutic commodities at household level. The program will also aim to build on current cash plus interventions including positive messaging in regards to nutrition and other targeted messages to change behaviours and social norms that may be contributing to vulnerability, extend community engagement, as well as accountability and feedback systems. Geographical targeting will start with the areas where UNICEF is already providing cash assistance in Bay and Bakool and aims to be extended to rural areas in those districts, which may encourage return in safe areas and initial contributions to rebuilding livelihoods and resilience to shocks and crises. The selected household upon diagnosis of SAM, the mother or the regular care giver will be enrolled to the UNICEF supported therapeutic feeding centres. Upon admissions mother or the regular care giver will receive monthly cash assistance. The beneficiaries will be register in SCOPE allowing them to track beneficiary across the different type of assistance received. This would also enhance coordination efforts with other partner to avoid duplication of assistance being received (medical treatment vs cash transfers).

Mitigation Measures

2a) Children under 5 years of age who are diagnosed with SAM in UNICEF supported facilities will receive the cash based assistance. As this is a medical diagnosis it should not be influenced if the child is part of a marginalised community or minority group. 4c) The cash will be dispersed to the caregiver of the child who brings them into the facility for treatment. The intention is for the cash to be dispersed using "mobile money" so that the caregiver is not at risk of theft of physical money. The intention is to put in place validation measures so that it can be confirmed that the registered mobile number of the caregiver is the one receiving the cash into their mobile money account. 4d) UNICEF has partnered with Africa Voices Foundation to provide a feedback system via mobile text messages for reporting of issues (including non-payment, missing identification or PSEA). This mechanism will alert the project team to issues so that they can be resolved in a timely manner or referred to appropriate support.
