Project Summary

The goal of the Community Based Demining (CBD) project is to clear explosive remnants
of war (ERW) according to humanitarian need, and to promote development by creating
large scale employment. These two activities combined will help to promote stability and
security through the destruction of ERW: 1) which explosives can be harvested and sold
on the black market, or kill/injure civilians; and 2) through the employment of fighting age
people who may otherwise be vulnerable to recruitment by terrorist networks.
In addition to direct implementation of clearance activities, HALO will partner with local NGO Shabelle and Hiran Mine Action Consortium (SHAHMAC) to conduct Risk Education, and will support SHAMAC in the delivery of explosive ordnance disposal callouts, and non-technical survey of contaminated areas. This will help support local capacity, with HALO staff local to Hiraan building the capacity of the LNGO, in direct mentorship.
In addition to the durable solutions and intervention targets detailed in the response plan, this project seeks to improve the inclusion of those with disabilities, reduce the risk of further disabilities, and provide coordination of victim assistance interventions. It will achieve this through:
- Provision of job opportunities to people with disabilities which do not affect manual labour (as deminers)
- Provision of job opportunities to people with disabilities which hamper manual labour (as watchmen, cooks, and support staff)
-Reduction of further disabilities by the clearance of contaminated land and destruction of explosive ordnance which is the root cause of disabilities
-referral of victims within area of operations to victim assistance services at a state and national level

Project Activities

Manual minefield clearance: The employment of 2 sections of 10 deminers for 12 months of manual clearance. These teams will conduct clearance of high priority minefields in Hirshabelle State. Deminers will clear ground using a combination of Minelab F3s detectors and full excavation, depending on minefield conditions. Training: HALO Somalia will hire and train 24 community members from flood affected communities to conduct manual mine clearance. HALO will recruit employees from villages nearest to contaminated land, with the goal of achieving a 50-50 gender recruitment for new hires. By recruiting from nearby villages, employees will be able to return to their homes at night and benefit from their own mine clearance. Experienced HALO team leaders will oversee all the operational activities. New employees will be trained in full excavation. HALO will partner with the Hirshabelle mine action consortium, a local NGO, in order to provide the following activities: Risk Education: A Community Outreach and Risk Education (CORE) mixed gender team will deliver 12 months of mine risk education to persons and communities vulnerable to landmines and ERW. CORE teams communicate the risks of landmines, IEDs and UXO through presentations and classes to ERW affected communities. Teams will also provide mine risk education in IDP camps throughout Hirshabelle state. Risk education seeks to reduce the risk of injury from mines/ERW by raising awareness and promoting behavioural change. - This activity will be solely carried out by the local NGO, but will be quality assured by HALO senior staff. This will help build local capacity, and provide training and oversight by HALO's experienced mine action staff. All staff are local to Hiraan, and as such, this presents an opportunity for organic Somali led instruction and local capacity building. Non-technical survey (NTS): NTS identifies confirmed and suspected hazardous areas through community engagement and evidence based study. It is essential in developing a clear picture of contamination, support the development of a national work plan and build SEMA’s national database. HALO conducts NTS through community survey forms and local stakeholders lead the engagement. -This activity will be supported closely by HALO staff, who will develop the capacity of the local NGO to carry out this activity. EOD call outs/ Stockpile Destruction: HALO Somalia engages with both security force and community held stockpiles, seeking to destroy dangerous legacy ammunition. Stockpile destruction and call outs address the availability of legacy explosives which may be repurposed into IEDs. Explosive ordnance is destroyed in single item demolitions using thermite torches (T-Jets). Mixed-gender community outreach risk education (CORE) teams negotiate the handover of community stockpiles/items, and respond to community EOD call outs.-The local NGO will identify community call out, and will be supported in their conduct of demolitions by HALO staff. This will ensure oversight and coordination of EOD activities, and ensure control of deflagration materials. Financial management of the LNGO will be administered by HALO, ensuring accountability of project funds' disbursement.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). - Mitigation measures include engagement in a service mapping in its area of operation and reach out to cluster AoRs for specific entry points to referral processes and case management in the event that beneficiaries disclosure instances of abuse and require further forms of protection assistance. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. - HALO has both informal and formal complaints and feedback mechanisms. These are based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe, and easy to use. HALO receives a regular flow of feedback and complaints through its network of Community Liaison Officer’s and through data collection activities. In order to make sure that people are able access the complaint and feedback mechanism we will run through the mechanism as a drill to make sure that all parts are working and functional. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area.- This project is Likelihood HIGH – project is specifically designed to target areas of high explosive hazard contamination and remove this threat. Impact is LOW as HALO Trust is expertly equipped to deal with contamination as part of the intervention and all HALO staff follows strict SOPs at all times. As this project is specifically designed to remove this threat our mitigation methods are imbedded within our activities. Strict SOPs are adhered to at all times which significantly reduces the risk to our staff. CORE teams are trained in the identification and disposal of ERW. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources HALO employs extensive community liasion and stakeholder mapping efforts into its project planning and delivery. HALO has a substantial history in the area of implementation, and is aware of the clan dynamics, which would engender social conflict. All hiring of personnel and vehicles is done locally and transparently, and is locally supported. Sustained engagement ensures that potential conflicts are anticipated and diplomatically resolved. HALO has worked in Hiran for 5 years, and has long-term commitments in the region. Accordingly, it takes a sustainable approach to project delivery.

Please provide further explanation

The protection environment for civilians remains a significant challenge in Hiran. Limited governmental authority, clan conflict, and legal impunity has resulted in protection violations such as forced displacement, gender-based violence, child rights violations, arbitrary detention, forced recruitment, and forced eviction. Through employment, the project will reduce the use of negative coping strategies that perpetuate protection concerns such as family separation and child marriage. Through clearance of mine-impacted land, communities are able to use previously unsafe land again. This project makes land safe again for communities through the clearance of mines and other explosive ordnance so that communities can safely access their land and resources. This is a long-term solution as land will be safely accessible to communities improving their conditions for long-term socioeconomic development. The return of unsafe land to communities also works to reduce clan tension related to land disputes by clearing contaminated land for productive use by the targeted communities. - The project supports efforts made by the national government to provide protection and humanitarian assistance to internally displaced persons, by reducing obstacles to accessing their rights. For IDPs, the subsequent loss of access shelter, lands, livelihoods, and social networks can negatively affect their ability to assert and enjoy access an entire range of fundamental rights. The intervention clears access to vital resources and services such as health care facilities, water and agricultural land by removing physical barriers for IDPs and the wider community in accessing these facilities. Furthermore, the project hires vulnerable community members, which might include IDPs, reducing their dependence on aid to meet their basic needs, facilitating their sustainable reintegration and limiting their protracted dependence on aid, which erodes durable solutions. IDPs are often unfamiliar with the area in which they resettle, and are not aware of the contamination risks. The project reduces the risk that IDPs will be further exposed to further threats during the settlement process through the delivery of explosive ordnance risk education. The benefit of HALO’s community based approach is apparent in the immediate and sustainable results experienced by the community, which serve to accelerate reintegration. HALO will hire gender-balanced teams of deminers, recruiting both IDPs and the host community members. Such integration facilitates mutual understanding, working to reduce the risk of discrimination, which can lead to attacks and gender-based violence. Working together, alongside host communities, IDPs will have access to replacement protective social and community networks that foster positive mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. Furthermore, clearing access to land reduces the pressure on existing resources created by the influx of IDPs, which can lead to tension between IDPs and the host community, influencing the sustainability of their settlement. HALO Somalia is registered in Somalia and is an registered organization in Hiran to conduct humanitarian mine clearance, survey, and EORE. HALO Somalia conducts its interventions with authorization from the Somalia Explosive Management Authority (SEMA).
