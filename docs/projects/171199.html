Project Summary

The projections of recent FSNAU assessments reveal that there is a dire need to gear the humanitarian efforts towards addressing the risk of malnutrition and similarly the projections done earlier in 2020 showed that malnutrition levels were above the emergency threshold level in most of the parts of the country and correspondingly the national median GAM prevalence in children was reported to be around 13.8% and this translates to 0.96 million boys and girls. Out of this 162,000 are affected by life-threatening severe acute malnutrition while around 800,000 are moderately acute malnourished and at the same time. In addition to children under five years of age, a further 571,159 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance. Cumulative vulnerabilities, including those related to gender, disability and age poses a risk that undermines the livelihood and underscores the need for an equitable humanitarian response. Women and girls, especially those with disabilities face additional challenges as well as heightened risks of gender-based violence in Somalia.
These vulnerable population groups are more exposed to the risks of malnutrition due to the prevailing “triple threat” i.e. COVID 19 pandemic, continued floods and desert locust. COVID 19 pandemic had an adverse effect on the service delivery whereby access to the population of health and nutrition facilities was compromised. However, with the adoption of the revised guidelines, the critical lifesaving nutrition services were resumed on time. The impacts of this triple threat not only led to the aggravation of the shocks but also rendered the vulnerable groups to the causes of malnutrition such as food insecurity decreasing purchasing power and morbidity due to water-borne diseases and low immunization services. Similarly, as a result of floods, there were reported displacements that further exposed the population to poor livelihood conditions. The main contributory factors, therefore, include the high number of children affected by acute watery diarrhoea, malaria and the concurrent reported low coverage of measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation (FSNAU).
This project aims to improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases.
The project responds to gender-specific needs such as thought nutrition intervention services to vulnerable drought or flood-prone areas and conflict-affected zones and communities of Banadir, Afgoye, and Marka.
The project will run 1 district hospital stabilization centres (Afgoye SC) covering both 3 fixed and 3 mobile OTPs services in each district to strength the referral pathways in the above-mentioned districts from the community to fixed nutrition centres and district hospitals as part of the child and maternal morbidity and mortality reduction activities and overall health system strengthen especially in Lower Shebelle and Banadir regions where most of the vulnerable maternal and children resides and mostly are IDPs.
The project is targeting 8,888 ( 20% of total SAM in 3 districts ) SAM cases of U5 children, 26,683 ( 20% of MAM cases in 3 districts) of MAM cases of U5 children, 8,634 (30% of PLWs in 3 districts) of PLWs and 1,333 of SAM with complications ( 15% of SAM cases in 3 districts).
This project will cover staff incentives for child nutrition services, 3 ambulances to increase referral from communities to the nutrition centres and district hospital for stabilization of complicated cases and capacity building for frontline staff in nutrition facilities to increase the quality of care of nutrition services in 3 districts of Lower Shebelle and Banadir regions in Somalia.

Project Activities

1. Provide screening for 146,516 children 6-59 months and 73,885 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) through 3 static OTPs and 3 Mobile OTPs in 3 target districts in January to December of 2021. 2. Provide admission and Treatment for 8,888 cases of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complication in line with Somalia IMAM guideline in the 3 target districts during 2021. 3. Provide 8,888 children (4,000 boys and 4,888 girls) 6-59 months with deworming tablet and Multiple Micronutrient Supplementation 4. Provide Referral of 1,333 SAM cases with medical complication and failed appetite to nearest stabilization centre targeting in the 3 target districts during 2021. 5. Provide Nutritional education and Referral of 26,683 MAM cases to nearest TSFP in the 3 target districts during 2021.6. Provide micronutrient supplementation, Vitamin A and Iron folic supplementation to 8,634 pregnant and lactating women in the 3 target districts during 2021.7. Provide Integrated management acute malnutrition (IMAM) Training to147 nutrition staff and 70 community health and nutrition workers during 2021 in the 3 target districts.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement: The project will seriously take a note of the risk facing displaced and vulnerable groups are excluded from the resources including humanitarian aid by the host communities or any other groups. Marginalized groups including minority clans, and other marginalized tribes at risk of being neglected will be given equal opportunity to participate as well as the people with disability. During the selection of nutrition sites and training access these groups will be considered. PAC will recruit some of its project staff from displaced populations especially for positions such as community nutrition workers in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our project services and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project will note that making particular provision for persons with specific vulnerabilities – e.g., women headed-households, pregnant or lactating women, persons with disabilities (physical or mental), persons with HIV/AIDS or other chronic illnesses - to ensure that everyone has equal opportunities, is not discriminated. this project of IDPs seeking designed must not be subject to discrimination for reasons related to their displacement. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age: PAC therefore will first take into account of the different needs, recognize the potential barriers faced and ensure women and men access Nutrition services equally. All data will be dis aggregated by sex and age and apply a gender analysis. PAC will involve from the outset women, girls, boys and men, including those that belong to the vulnerable groups, in health assessments, priority setting, programme design, interventions and evaluation. All nutrition consultations, examination and care will be in privacy. Nutrition personnel (women and men) will be adequately represented in gender theme groups, Communication strategies will highlight specific nutrition risks affecting women and men, as well as targeting adolescent girls and boys in local Somali language and include physical and mental health services and their locations and ensure all hard to reach vulnerable members are fully aware of the existing services Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence: This project will consider women and girls are at increased risk of violence due to the on-going civil unrest in this project area and may be unable to access assistance or make their needs known. On the other side the men's roles as protectors in this otherwise conflict affected area has placed them on greater responsibility for risk taking. PAC will work closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensive Conflict sensitivity in the program; do-no-harm approach Protection of women against Gender Based Violence (GBV); The program provides a provision to mainstream GBV monitoring and prevention mechanisms including; provision of referral notes to victims of GBV, gathering data of GBV cases in the program target areas and providing regular reports to relevant stakeholders including protection cluster as well as specific agencies that thematic focus is to prevent GBV in Somalia.
