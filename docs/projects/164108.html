Project Summary

In support of the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector Objective 7 (Strengthened Communities and Participation), UNICEF through this project in 2020, will further support its No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), girls and boys, to lead and implement civic and social initiatives and with the objective of contributing to social change and community cohesion, Strengthen access to services: skills development, employability and livelihoods.

4 - Needs

The health, education and WASH infrastructure is in critical need of attention to facilitate socio-economic recovery and addressing the 6.5 million internally displaced persons. The prolonged conflict and the mass displacement of populations away from their areas of origin continues to put strain on the social fabric. The presence of IDPs in host communities is increasing the stress on social services and physical spaces in the present places of abode, leading to potential rapture in the social fabric. Adolescents and youth are among the most severely affected by the consequences of crisis and conflict and continue to struggle to cope with the crisis, as a result of the loss of adult-care providers, disruption of social structures and a sense of normalcy, and surrounded by a constant environment of violence and insecurity, limited access to education and safe and decent livelihood opportunities, they face heightened vulnerabilities and an insecure future. Girls in particular remain especially vulnerable to forms of gender-based violence. Recent participatory action research conducted inside Syria by adolescents and youth among their peers highlighted the negative coping mechanisms being adopted by young girls and boys who are increasingly frustrated and disenfranchised. Adolescent boys and girls continue to struggle to cope with the crisis, as a result of the loss of adult-care providers, disruption of social structures and a sense of normalcy, and surrounded by a constant environment of violence and insecurity, limited access to education and positive engagement opportunities, they face heightened vulnerabilities and an insecure future. They lack both informal and formal channels to effectively participate in social, civic and economic life. Social capital has been severely affected and social fabric remains shattered – and with it the social development of adolescents and youth. This social capital needs to be re-established for social cohesion and peacebuilding

5 - Implementation of activities

Vulnerable adolescents and youth, girls and boys, will have equal access to structured capacity development sessions, to be provided by local partners, of at least 40 hours as per the standard training package, following which they will work individually or in teams to implement civic community initiatives. These initiatives will aim at improving the overall well-being of all young people and have the added objective of contributing to community cohesion. Emphasis will be placed on ensuring sustainability by supporting capacity development of partners to explore alternate resource mobilization strategies, expanding partnerships and strengthening linkages with/integrating adolescent engagement across sectors. Examples of adolescent led community initiatives/activities include: Initiatives implemented for the Global Hand washing campaign; social mobilization for polio immunization; actions aimed at promoting gender equality and indeed prevention of gender-based violence at local level, such as child marriage; prevention of child to child violence; participation in local decision-making processes and increasing youth participation in social accountability through participatory research/evidence generation. Civic engagement activities/initiatives focusing on social cohesion includes promotion of peace and harmony through cultural and sports events at community level, youth-led community dialogue, volunteer action, capacity development in mediation and conflict mitigation. Routine monitoring will be used for measuring the reach, the vulnerability profile, the quality of the training, the outputs (wrt initiatives and coverage) and qualitative impact of the civic engagement interventions. UNICEF and partners will adopt Strategic use of C4D approaches in capacity development (behavior changing communication) to strengthen the capacity of youth to support community engagements for behaviour and social change. Adolescent girls will be specifically targeted for empowerment.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Adolescent and youth empowerment may lead to greater exposure for young people causing them unintended harm and also motivate youth to unintentionally put themselves in danger while addressing sensitive issues; Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; Sport/cultural/civic engagement activities in open spaces may lead to increased risk due to unexpected mortars. To mitigate such possible risks to young people, the principle of Do No Harm must be emphasized throughout all stages of programming. Working with parents will also be critical to ensure equitable participation and rights of boys and girls. Risk assessments of all facilities will need to be conducted and safety measures will be supported. As the project will address actual needs of young people and voices of young people will be adhered to, the principle of Do Good aims to support a process whereby young girls and boys are able to share their views freely on interventions relevant to their needs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project risks include; Lack of adequate resources, continued conflict affecting programme progress, restrictions and limitations affecting access and reach, Sanctions on Syria limiting online access opportunities and engagement and Community resistance to change. The key assumption is willingness of young people to participate. Mitigation measures include: Evidence based advocacy, Preparedness plan for different scenarios and LS for resilience building, supporting innovation and Syrian platforms, promoting behaviour change and engagement of community through different pathways.
