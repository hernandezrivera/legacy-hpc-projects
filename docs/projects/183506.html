Project Summary

The current context in the oPt has been highly impacted by the pandemic of COVID-19, resulting in additional burden on an already stretched education system. This is translated into schools closure and, at times, additional requirements to turn into online education in a context in which, a large number of students, is unable to follow this approach. According to gender analysis conducted by HI in Gaza and West Bank, 56.4% of the surveyed children with and without disabilities could not follow online classes due to the lack of internet connection and educational technologies or lack of classes / adaptive didactic support from the schools. The study also shown that girls were more affected (65%) than boys (47%). Whereas the pandemic impacted children and educators' psychosocial aspects and well-being, the escalation during May 2021 on the WB and Gaza further aggravated the situation leaving children and educators in an urgent need for emotional and psychosocial support. Observations from the field showed an urgent need of building the capacities of school counselors in providing Inclusive psychosocial first aid. The Education cluster estimates that children with disabilities are one the most affected by the protracted and acute crisis: they lack basic assistive devices and education technologies that would facilitate their learning following the mixed approach of face to face and online learning, and are in need of psychosocial support (PSS) and remedial education. Since 2014, HI has strived to improve the access of children with disabilities to education both in Gaza and West Bank (mainly in Area C); by building the capacity of 260 educational staff in both areas, implementing schools emergency rehabilitation and adapting 36 schools. HI also has been providing specialized education materials, created 22 resource rooms, and developed technical guidelines on the inclusion of 750 children with disabilities and 341 children with learning difficulties in education, developed inclusive education practical manual to improve the delivery of adapted education services responding to children’s individual needs, and increased the capacities of targeted schools to respond to emergencies. Moreover, HI provided more than 614 psychosocial individual and psychosocial sessions to 705 children, teachers, and parents, including responding to COVID-19 emergency with the provision of 581 PSS kits. Building on this solid experience, the project aims to enhance the access of children with disabilities to inclusive education services in emergencies. The project will respond to the education cluster objectives, namely two and three, by identifying dropped out children with disabilities, enhancing the capacities of self-help groups on the localities level to support the children’s return back to education, strengthening their mobility, providing visual and hearing assistive devices, transportation, school kits, education materials and stationary, school-based structured individual and group psychosocial services. HI will also support the provision of holistic services through referrals to other stakeholders. Furthermore, the project will look at developing an enabling safe educational environment that is adequate to respond to emergencies through the provision of technical support for mainstream and special education service providers. This will include development of inclusive emergency response plans on schools level, accessible emergency schools rehabilitation, and capacity building to educators and children in addition to providing technical support to MoE to intervene on the level of Educational platforms to make it accessible for children with disabilities. The project will operate with two local implementing partners in Gaza and West Bank.

1 - Needs

According to Ministry of Education statistics for 2020/2021 scholastic year, 980 children with disabilities are enrolled in mainstream schools. However, the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) indicated that out of the 35,675 children with disabilities living in oPt, 27.4% have never accessed education. According to UNICEF Out-of-School Children (OoSC) report published in 2018, 1 in 3 children with disabilities aged 6 to 9 -and about 1 in 2 children with multiple disabilities of the same age- do not attend school. A recent Gender Analysis Study in Education conducted by HI in West Bank area C and Gaza (2021), shows that 18% of the surveyed boys with disabilities had never attended school, in contrast to the 11% of surveyed girls. On the level of drop-out, the percentages came similar among the surveyed boys and girls with disabilities (8%); the top two reasons of drop-out are lack of teachers capacities to include children with disabilities in education and inaccessible physical environment of schools. The study also reports that 27% of surveyed boys with disabilities had no access to assistive devices, while the percentage is higher among girls with disabilities (35%). During COVID-19, the access to quality education has been affected to all children, especially on children with disabilities. According to the study, 65% of the children stated that they couldn’t follow online classes with a gender gap of 65% among girls compared to 47% of boys. The main reasons are the lack of access to internet connection and educational technologies, and lack of support on schools level in relation to adaptive didactic support from schools. On the intersectionality of disability, safety and GBV, the study reports that 31.3% of surveyed parents think that both boys and girls with disabilities will be more secure at home; 70.3% considers that both girls and boys with disability are more exposed to sexual abuse when they are away from home, and 50 % would be ready to marry their girl with disability under 16 years old, with a higher percentage in Gaza (65.91%).In the West Bank: the project will target area C communities in the Jordan Valley and Jerusalem suburbs in specific. According to an infographic produced by the Palestinian vision organization for the Jordan Valley communities; the rates of drop-out each more than 5% among children in school age. The percentage of disability among community members is considered very high as for some of these communities the rate reaches 7%- 9.8%. Around 200 children with disabilities enrolled within these communities, in addition to 200 children with learning difficulties, are in need of support to more inclusive emergency practices and provision of specialized materials. 38 schools in these communities are in urgent need to inclusive emergency rehabilitations. In relation to educational technologies in case of lock down or quarantine of students, 74 schools are in need for educational technologies and assistive technologies in specific for children with disabilities to be able to continue their face-to-face or online education. Additional needs based on a rapid assessment conducted in these schools are; provision of assistive devices and referrals, provision of educational materials to resource rooms, capacity building for school staff on inclusive practices, capacity building to school counsellors for inclusive PSS, provision of inclusive PSS support on community and school level, awareness raising of children and parents to available complaints mechanisms, adaptation works, inclusive emergency preparedness plans and provision of emergency materials. In Gaza: HI will be scaling up its intervention in the sector of inclusive education to cover the needs of Khan-Younis and North Gaza, the ongoing education project has identified the following needs; limited equipping of resources rooms, limited capacities of school key staff and teachers on how to interact with boys and girls with disabilities, lack of schools accessibility for these children with physical and sensorial disabilities and inclusive emergencies and preparedness plans.

2 - Activities

The project includes two main components to reduce the vulnerability and risks behind the exclusion of children with disabilities from accessing education. The first looks at strengthening the coordination among community actors; the second aims at improving the learning environment of mainstream and special schools. As such, the project envision two main results targeting both West Bank and Gaza. Result 1: Strengthened coordination between the community actors to identify and support provision of specialized services for dropped out school girls and boys with disabilities to increase their access to education. Main activities: 1.1: Provision of technical and financial support to local partners and Self Help Groups (SHG) to identify boys and girls with disabilities who dropped out from schools through awareness raising and capacity building; this includes conducting awareness raising on community level in relation to HI safeguarding policies and trainings for community members on reporting PSEA cases. Consultation meetings will be conducted with formed groups on best mechanism to be followed for reporting cases. 1.2: Support referral pathways to identified boys and girls with disabilities to specialized rehabilitation services, special education services, referrals to GBV, PSEA services and mainstreaming schools in collaboration with the existing referral networks of HI partners, and collaboration with protection cluster, which will improve their inclusion in the regular schools.; 1.3: Provision of mobility, hearing, visual and educational assistive devices and educational technologies for boys and girls with disabilities; 1.4: Provision of safe accessible transportation for vulnerable boys and girls with disabilities to enable their access to schools and reduce their risk to dropout from schools, this will be ensured through enhancement of coordination on local communities’ level, and working closely on accessibility of transport means; 1.5: Donation of educational materials and stationary, emergency kits, hygiene kits to girls and boys of poor and vulnerable households.Expected result 2: Improved safe and protective learning environment for the targeted mainstream schools and special schools under emergency situations. Main activities: 2.1: Support targeted school to develop inclusion action plans through the use of Schools Inclusiveness Assessment tools. The project will target children with different types of disabilities; physical, visual, hearing, and taking into consideration children with learning difficulties. Thus, based on assessment results, actions will include responding to physical environment accessibility, informational accessibility, development of individual educational plans, disability inclusive PSS support; 2.2: Support the adaptation of Ministry of Education online education Platform for more accessible features for children with disabilities, which will include accessibility features for children with visual impairments, providing sign language interpretation to videos and recorded contents, work on simplification of contents for children with learning difficulties based on consultations conducted with resource room teachers; 2.3: Provision of technical support to male and female teachers of mainstream schools using the Inclusive Education. The technical support is directed towards the specific educational needs of the children; which includes didactics for teachers on how to ensure inclusion within classroom, inclusive teaching methodologies, assessment of educational needs, coordination with resource room teachers to follow children’s individual educational plans, links with referral committees at school level; 2.4: Donation of educational materials and stationary, hygiene kits for female and male teachers in mainstream and special schools; 2.5: Provision of technical support to mainstream and special education schools to develop disability inclusive emergency preparedness plans (includes the provision of emergency kits). Inclusive response plans will be developed that will include the needs of children with disabilities in terms of mobility and evacuation, signage and visual alarming system enhancement of “buddy system”, informing staff and parents to discuss these needs during the IEPs discussions, enhance emergency medical card as an emergency preparedness; 2.6: Provision school-based disability inclusive psycho-social and outreach psychoeducation for boys and girls with disabilities and their parents. This includes training of school counsellors on inclusive PSS strategies; adapting tools, and methodologies; 2.7: Conduction of emergency school accessibility gender responsive rehabilitation including accessible latrines and wash facilities for girls and boys with disabilities and accessible instructions; 2.8: Development PSEA' feedback and complaint mechanism among special education schools and enhance awareness of among children with and without disabilities and their teachers and parents in mainstream school for existing mechanisms (through child friendly posters installed in schools).All project interventions will be implemented respecting a Do No Harm approach. The approach is being ensured on many levels for HI interventions; on the institutional level HI has its developed policies related to safeguarding including child protection policy, PSEA policy, gender policy, and policy of anti-fraud bribery and corruption. On the operational level; HI focuses on protection against abuses, violations and sever neglect of people with disabilities. As a reflection on project interventions, HI ensures capacity building of its staff, partners and all stakeholders in relation to awareness of safeguarding policies. HI ensures that targeted communities including children with disabilities are aware of its feedback and complaint mechanism in addition to enhancing local partners actions towards setting their own safe accessible complaints mechanisms.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

When responding to the needs of children with disabilities, it is all about access to services and level of inclusiveness of provided services. Thus, the intervention is mainly focused on the delivery of inclusive accessible services in education or to support children with disabilities access to education system. In kind donations will be the other modality in the intervention, due to lack of adequate resources in targeted schools in the vulnerable communities, in addition to the socio economic situation of families in targeted areas and specifically families of children with disabilities, who are in need of specialized materials.
