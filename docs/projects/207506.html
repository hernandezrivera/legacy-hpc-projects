Project Summary

Guided by the IASC Minimum Standards, UNFPA will promote, coordinate & provide lifesaving, comprehensive, multi-sectoral & inclusive GBV prevention & response services to Rohingya Refugees & Bangladeshi host communities in Cox’s Bazar, in line with the survivor-centered approach and the principles of value-for-money and accountability of affected populations.
UNFPA will provide integrated & age-responsive GBV/SRH services including case management, MHPSS, clinical management of rape, voluntary family planning, STI testing & treatment, and emergency referrals, in 53 Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) and 15 health facilities. UNFPA will also promote & advance gender equality & women’s empowerment, and foster social behavior change among women, girls, men & boys in 10 Women-Led Community Centers (WLCC) that offer PSS, life skills development, self-reliance activities, GBV prevention & awareness raising and emergency referrals.
Through WFSs & WLCCs, UNFPA will provide life-skills based empowering programs for adolescents & youth (A&Y) aged 10-24 to prevent violence against adolescent girls & child marriage. The Girl Shine program will equip girls with skills & knowledge on GBV, service availability, bodily autonomy, gender equality, MHPSS, SRH, menstrual health and emotional skills. Alongside, the Champions of Change program will engage boys in safe spaces to identify and challenge harmful social norms that perpetuate inequality and redefine masculinity.
The project will mobilize communities to challenge gender-biased social norms & harmful practices and prevent GBV through community engagement, awareness raising and enhancement of women & men’s caregiver roles. Key modalities that will be used are the SASA! Together approach, to address social norms that perpetuate gender inequalities; and structured, community-level sessions for A&Y caregivers.
Across UNFPA-supported facilities, Rohingya women, men & young people will benefit from increased volunteer opportunities; skills development training; and a pilot intervention for the provision of Dignity Kits items through voucher modality; to contribute to their self-reliance and livelihood, thus to community wellbeing and social cohesion. Community engagement and social cohesion will also be promoted through Women Support Groups, Community Watch Groups and Self-Help Groups.
The project will address the needs of vulnerable groups by integrating targeted measures in programming and developing partnership with specialized organizations, including women-led organizations. UNFPA will capacitate partners on intersectional vulnerabilities to enhance GBV programmatic support and case management services for persons with disabilities, gender diverse populations and elderly women.
As both beneficiaries & aid workers are at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, UNFPA will advocate & provide technical guidance alongside capacity development to IPs in coordination with ISCG PSEA Network to ensure safeguarding for beneficiaries and that PSEA referral, reporting mechanisms & policies are in place. UNFPA will strengthen PSEA Champions to prevent SEA & raise awareness on reporting mechanisms among the Rohingyas.
Under UNFPA leadership, the GBVSS comprises 67 members managing the inter-agency GBV response in Cox’s Bazar as per IASC Minimum Standards. GBVSS will engage with & enhance the capacities of members, other sectors & government stakeholders for effective, coordinated & integrated GBV prevention, mitigation & response. Focus will be on ensuring the continuity, quality & sustainability of comprehensive GBV services, case management & multi-sectoral referral systems with the rollout of Digital Referral Pathway App (eRPW). Regular community-based GBV risk monitoring & joint safety/service audits will support such efforts. GBVIMS Task Team of GBVSS will continue its transition to GBVIMS+ with features for online GBV case management & incident recording, embracing more advanced technology.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

After 6 years of displacement, GBV continues to be a major concern for the health and wellbeing of both Rohingya refugees and Bangladeshi host communities in Cox’s Bazar, putting their physical and emotional well-being at risk. Rohingya women & girls, who represent 52% of the total refugee population (UNHCR population factsheet, August 2023), continue to be subjected to GBV, with high rates among women of reproductive age. Among reported GBV incidents, 90.6% took place in survivors’ residences, suggesting compromised safety even in their homes. 79.7% of aggressors were husbands and 16.5% were family, friends or parents (GBVIMS Quarter 2 Factsheet, 2023). Data suggest the need for community-based interventions and continued provision of lifesaving GBV services (Joint Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment (J-MSNA), 2023). Rohingyas continue to seek support from Majhis (54%), CiCs (57.8%) and WFS (10%) in case of sensitive issues (J-MSNA, 2023). However, survivors risk not receiving time-sensitive services such as clinical management of rape, MHPSS and emergency referrals if their first entry is not a trained humanitarian aid worker. Moreover, 72% of perpetrators were unemployed, daily laborers or lacked livelihoods, which could correlate to increased incidents of GBV in the camps (GBVIMS Quarter 2 Factsheet, 2023). Access to lifesaving GBV services of most vulnerable & marginalized groups remains hampered. Among married refugee adolescent girls (15-17 years), 17% reported being abused, while adolescent girls in host areas also have unaddressed protection needs, i.e. high risk of child marriage, lack of community safety, parental and peer violence (Age & GBV risks facing Rohingya & Bangladeshi adolescents in Cox’s Bazar, 2020). GBVSS service audit found no disability-friendly entrance in 48.4% of audited GBV facilities, indicating a worrisome gap in inclusive intersectional programming to address the needs of persons with disabilities. Absence of data on the access of gender-diverse population and elderly women to lifesaving GBV services may be indicative of the gaps. The reasons behind this scenario are connected to other trends, i.e. polygamy; inadequate shelter & WASH arrangements; insufficient lighting; lack of income-generating opportunities (GBVIMS Quarter 2 factsheet, 2023). J-MSNA emphasized the importance of multi-sectoral coordinated preventive measures. Within the protracted crisis, J-MSNA highlighted that key programming priorities shall be lifesaving GBV services & referral mechanisms; and community engagement to transform harmful social norms & behavioral change, in line with GBV Minimum Standards & IASC Guidelines. As per the community consultations conducted by UNFPA to design the project, women & girls defined WFS as havens for authentic self-expression, open discussions on their emotions without fear of being judged, while WLCC are seen by men & women as places to receive support, GBV awareness, referral, and opportunities to get income & livelihood support. Nevertheless, men & women from both refugee & host areas reiterated the need of engaging men & boys in GBV awareness programs and curriculum-based life skill training. The GBVSS Service Audit (2023) found that 25.8% of existing service providers lacked targeted GBV programming engaging men & boys. There is also an immediate need to mobilize resources & capacities to enable PERU & other first responders’ mechanisms to function in times of emergencies. Bangladesh is a disaster-prone area - an acute vulnerability factor for women impacting their livelihood, mobility, access to essential services, and gendered roles & responsibilities (M. Nasreen, Violence against Women During Flood & Post-Flood Situations in Bangladesh, Action Aid, 2008). The findings above were confirmed by 120 respondents of 24 FGDs conducted by UNFPA & partner organizations on 22-26 October 2023 (15 women & girls, 4 FGD women & girls with disabilities, 5 FGD men & boys).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In line with Principle 3, UNFPA will adopt an integrated approach to improve service delivery by avoiding geographic overlaps and handover 2 Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) in Camp19 and Camp 20E during 2024, as per GBVSS rationalization plan. UNFPA will strengthen the integration of GBV & SRH services in 53 WFS and 15 heath facilities; integration of GBV/CP/Protection referral pathways; and programming targeting adolescents and youth through the WFS and the Women-led Community Centers. UNFPA will actively seek opportunities for integrating facilities & reducing overhead costs in coordination with other Partners, drawing from the successful model planned in Camp 11 for integration of GBV, CP and Protection Services. UNFPA and IPs will also continue to participate in camp-level coordination platforms and to liaise with the CiCs. In line with Principle 4, UNFPA will ensure predictability and continuity by continuing to operate in the same areas where it has an already-established presence and with the same well-established IPs. Part of such interventions will be financed through already-mobilized multi-year funding. UNFPA will also adopt a standardized approach to programming to ensure that all UNFPA-supported GBV services maintain the same minimum quality standards as per IASC Minimum Standards, despite being managed by various IPs, thus providing equitable services to refugees across camps. In line with Principle 5, UNFPA will provide support in case of emergency situations through its IPs based on their assigned area of interventions across 29 camps. These IPs have already demonstrated such capacity, as demonstrated by their response to cyclones and fire incidents in 2023, and this includes the prepositioning of Dignity Kits in their warehouses and facilities (WFS). Lastly, UNFPA will continue to collaborate with the GBVSS, relevant working groups, and the ISCG at large to ensure a transparent communication and flow of information on a timely and regular basis.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Through the project, UNFPA will continue to ensure that protection mechanisms and principles are in place by applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits & related IASC protection standards. UNFPA will provide GBV lifesaving services, which incorporate the protection mainstreaming principles & approaches. In particular by applying the “do no harm” principle to affected people within facilities & outreach activities, UNFPA will ensure to provide services to the Rohingya refugees and host communities while minimizing harm and maximizing the positive impact of aid and assistance provided to affected communities. This will be ensured by conducting regular needs assessments and participatory discussions with the beneficiaries to ensure that the services provided are tailored to their changing needs; and regular monitoring & evaluation of the quality of the service provided. Lastly, UNFPA will apply a holistic and participatory approach to promoting the engagement of rights holders at individual, community & institutional levels through community-led social mobilization such as SASA! Together. UNFPA applies a survivor-centered approach in programming, specifically through GBV case management services. All service providers deployed in UNFPA-supported facilities will abide by this principle and provide services based on the consent, needs and wishes of the survivor while maintaining confidentiality. Service providers will share GBV-related information with other providers on a strict need-to-know basis while managing comprehensive referrals to specialized protection-related services for legal assistance, safe shelters and advocacy. The service providers also share aggregated non-identifying GBV-related data to the GBVSS/Protection sector according to the GBVSS Information Sharing Protocol and data protection protocols.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA will target all age categories and follow a twin track approach to ensure disability & age inclusion. For mainstreaming, UNFPA will ensure GBV services are increasingly disability inclusive and accessible by: 1) using Washington Group Questionnaire; 2) conducting disability accessibility audits at facilities; 3) enhancing capacities of IPs, service providers and volunteers on GBV, PSEA, referrals, case management for persons with disabilities & caregivers, and basic sign language; 4) developing disability-inclusive IEC materials, including on PSEA; 5) engaging persons with disabilities throughout project cycle; 6) strengthening partnership with disability-focused organizations. UNFPA will take targeted actions by: 1) organizing GBV awareness outreach sessions & WFS service information sharing sessions for persons with disabilities & caregivers; 2) providing dedicated “tom-tom” referral services & cash; 3) improving data gathering by capturing Sex Age Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) to ensure programmatic improvements are made. UNFPA’s GBV interventions will adopt an adolescent-friendly & age-sensitive approach to information & service provision to adolescents and youth (A&Y). The project will support life skills education (LSE) programs (Girl Shine & Champions of Change) for A&Y and caregivers to equip them with clear understanding of gender equality, safety, child marriage & ASRHR, and empower them to act as change agents in the community. GBV case management, referral & services for adolescent girls will be strengthened with focus on caring for older persons, child survivors, & married and/or pregnant adolescents. WFS will continue the life-skills GEMS curriculum alongside regular sessions on GBV, SRHR, PSEA, DRR & emergency referral tailored to A&Y needs. UNFPA will support A&Y community activism & peer support groups at community level as part of the SASA! Together, as well as Young Mother Support Group in WFSs.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

All interventions & actions must apply an Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach, feedback & response mechanism must be accessible to all AGD groups and inform operations, learning & adaptations of programmes. Inclusive community participation & engagement will be a hallmark of the project, ensuring to represent various community members, i.e. women & girls, men & boys, community leaders, and vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities & gender diverse population. UNFPA will conduct extensive consultations and assessments among target populations & key stakeholders for planning, implementation, monitoring & evaluation of interventions, i.e. assessments & surveys; community consultations & FGDs with relevant stakeholders; bilateral dialogues with government officials & community leaders. Consultations will inform & involve community members in all phases of the project; proactively identify & address risks; and ensure transparency & AAP. Development of relevant technical documents & materials is done in consultations with and through participation of targeted community members to ensure appropriateness, sensitivity, relevancy & accessibility. The following UNFPA tools are used to ensure feedback amongst all communities: Community Feedback Mechanisms, Grievance Response Mechanisms, Client Satisfaction Surveys, and PSEA complaint boxes. UNFPA conducts FGDs regularly with affected populations and minority groups in WFSs & WLCCs to gather feedback on the services provided. This allows UNFPA to take appropriate actions to improve the quality of services, including through capacity building for service providers, case workers & volunteers, thus ensuring that service & programmatic gaps are addressed. All staff including UNFPA, IPs & volunteers are and will be trained on PSEA and awareness raising will continue for community people on PSEA. UNFPA with local NGOs capacitates and empowers community members through community watch groups, women support groups etc.

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) and sexual harassment (SH) and actively advocates for this principle across the organization, IPs, vendors, service providers & beneficiaries. The organization is committed to preventing SEA and providing immediate support to victims. UNFPA works with other organizations to create a workplace free from SEA. Since 2017, UNFPA has been a member of ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and appointed PSEA Focal Points for the organization. Training on PSEA is a top priority for UNFPA staff when they join the organization, including on staff obligations to report any wrongdoing and survivor-centered support. UNFPA's Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) ensures that staff can easily report misconduct through an online platform/hotline. PSEA focal points are available to receive complaints & report them back to the OAIS. All personnel involved with UNFPA, including volunteers, contractors, and vendors, receive training on PSEA. The organization has a retaliation policy in place to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. Under this project, UNFPA aims to continue building the capacity of their staff and related personnel on SEA and related issues. UNFPA will also strengthen PSEA Champions to prevent SEA and raise awareness on reporting mechanisms among the Rohingya population and host communities. UNFPA will conduct community-based awareness modules and use audiovisual & pictorial resources in awareness sessions to ensure communities are aware of SEA & reporting mechanisms. The organization will work to strengthen reporting channels and ensure conducive reporting mechanisms are available in each UNFPA-supported facility. Whenever needed, UNFPA will conduct capacity assessments and support the implementation of capacity strengthening plans to ensure IPs are maintaining the required PSEA and safeguarding mechanism in coordination with ISCG PSEA Network.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure full adherence to DRR/DRM standards, including Bangladesh’s National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. GBVSS contributed to revise the Emergency Preparedness & Response Protection Plan (EPRP), which includes preparedness planning with 67 partners & identifying GBV focal points for PERU. UNFPA IPs are actively engaged & part of PERU and receive training by UNFPA, including on GBViE. UNFPA-supported social mobilizers are also trained to respond to disaster-related emergencies to support community members during acute and post-disaster phase. A pool of PFA providers (68 case managers & case workers) from WFS is ready to be deployed in case of emergencies to provide PFA services & facilitate emergency referrals. 260 community volunteers are trained on PFA & referral pathways to assist in emergencies. 585 members in Women Support Groups and 390 in Community Watch Groups are trained on DRR & GBV referral to support their community during post-disaster emergencies. Case workers & social mobilizers received ToT on Fire Safety, Security & Risk Mitigation and will cascade it to other staff and community members. Through its IPs, UNFPA prepositions, stores & distributes customized Dignity Kits to respond to reduce vulnerabilities of affected women & girls during acute emergencies. UNFPA will also pilot one Women’s Shop for provision of locally-made Dignity Items to better respond to women’s hygiene needs while ensuring that waste is minimized and circular economy is promoted. UNFPA actively promotes eco-friendly practices by discouraging the use of plastic and polythene banners. Solar panels are installed at WFS & WLCC, thus helping reduce reliance on fossil fuels.UNFPA will provide community-based awareness programs on climate change through art-based activities and audio-visual content, and DRR training through its A&Y programming.
