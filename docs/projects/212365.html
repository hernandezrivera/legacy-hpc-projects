Project Summary

In response to the devastating earthquakes in Türkiye and Syria, this NGO has launched two large scale humanitarian responses. We are delivering responses in Türkiye (Türkiye Earthquake Response 2023).
The common thread across our responses will be our focus on the needs of children. By delivering health, water, sanitation and nutrition support, we will ensure that children survive this devastating crisis. By focusing on social and emotional wellbeing and with our safe return to learning approach, we will ensure that children continue to learn, despite disruption to education services. By supporting services for particularly vulnerable children, unaccompanied minors and children with disabilities we will ensure that children are protected from additional harm. Across our responses, we will respond to the deep psychological impacts of the disaster through our quality MHPSS approaches, and ensure tailored age-appropriate and gender sensitive interventions throughout. By distributing cash grants and vouchers, and supporting early livelihood recovery options, we will ensure that children and their families have a safety net to get them through this crisis and build resilience. And by advocating with governments across the globe, we will ensure that the rights of children are upheld.
We will leverage our leadership on and engage in humanitarian coordination structures to ensure the rights of children are promoted through the broader response efforts and to ensure our response complements those of other actors. Our programs will build national capacities, and be delivered with and through local actors and with input, feedback and guidance from the populations directly affected by this crisis.
For this project, MEAL team will track progress on the project indicators on a monthly, quarterly or yearly basis, as needed. All data will be disaggregated by sex and age. Data collection by staff is collected via electronic platforms such as Kobo, while paper might be used in sensitive areas (e.i. due to restrictions in Roj), which has the beneficiary information included. This data is manually inputted to a protected electronic system, which is used to report against the Indicator Performance Tracking Tool (IPTT) and collated monthly. The data will be reviewed and analysed on a monthly basis for necessary programmatic interventions.
