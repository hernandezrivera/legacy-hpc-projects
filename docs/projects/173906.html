Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to safe and quality education for crisis-affected children, with a particular focus on girls. The action will facilitate access to education for out of school children and adolescents to enable them to obtain the basis for integration into the formal education system. It is urgent to re-engage out-of-school children with the education system since, as these children grow up, their chance and ability to resume school decreases. The project will address gendered barriers to education and sensitization on gender-based violence, through teacher training, awareness campaigns and the promotion of equal and safe education for girls and boys, regardless of their status (IDPs, returnees or host communities.) To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, and will ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The project result will be achieved through activities aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response, the project will be developed through the lenses of gender and conflict mitigation, which are two concomitant perspectives to the dissemination of the principles of protection in the project. At the end of the action 20 000 persons including IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community’s children will benefit the above package of education assistance in order to scale up their access to qualities education opportunities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Access to education: In 2020, the Far North region remains the poorest in Cameroon with the lowest education rate. The primary completion rate is estimated at only 46%. The Region has been classified by the Government of Cameroon (GoC) as a priority education zone. The International Organization of Migration’s June 2020 Displacement Tracking Monitoring estimates that nearly 494 144 people have been displaced due to conflict in the Far North Including 321, 886 Internally Displaced Persons, 48 769 Out-of-camp refugees and 123 489 returnees. 64% of these population are children with critical education needs. Needs of school infrastructure and equipment: During the ongoing crisis, classrooms have been destroyed due to the conflict, vandalism and obsolescence, and functioning schools are overcrowded, which is suboptimal for teaching and learning. Following data received from Regional office of MoE in 2020, 62 schools remain closed due to the security context. Across the region lack of infrastructure and teachers are reasons why some children leave school. Needs of teachers: There are currently 310 schools with a single qualified teacher. This translates to a teacher to pupil ratio of 1 teacher to 162 children. This situation has led to a number of children and youth being out-of-school for several years. It is customary for communities to hire local teachers to complement the insufficiency of trained teachers. However, these locally hired teachers lack technical background and skills to deliver quality teaching for primary school pupils and are regularly underpaid by the community. Need for alternative education: There are few flexible, alternative education opportunities that accommodate those who do not fit into the formal system. There is no data reflecting the extent to which protection risks, security issues, and gender-based violence (GBV) affect education; however an education assessment conducted by NRC in the Far North found that cultural factors combined with the stresses of displacement exacerbate the risk of child marriage, especially for girls. NRC and Plan International, in collaboration with the Ministry of Basic Education in Cameroon, have developed an Accelerated Education Program to increase access to education for children who have been out of school for more than two years Additionally, NRC will conduct catch-up classes for children and youth who had been actively attending school prior to an educational disruption, which provides students with the opportunity to learn content missed because of the disruption and supports their re-entry to the formal system.COVID-19 mitigation measures on education: As a result of school closures to restrict the spread of COVID-19, some children, especially in exam grades, are in need of remedial classes to enable them to catch up on the curriculum.Pyscho-social support: Children who have experienced trauma linked to the conflict and/or the COVID-19 pandemic may be in need pf psycho-social support to help them to cope with the trauma, enabling them to engage in learning activities.
