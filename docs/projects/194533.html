Project Summary

According to the IPC released in November 2022, there are over 6.6 million people in South Sudan experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between October and November 2022. Food insecurity in the country is highly driven by ongoing conflicts, and climatic shocks, citing from the Humanitarian Needs Overview published in February 2022 states that climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors. Above-normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2022 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years constituting a rise in food insecurity countrywide.
Unity state is one of the most food insecure states, that contributes to the estimated 7.8 million people (63% of the population) who likely will face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the lean season projection period of April to July 2023. Approximately three-quarters of the state's population is undergoing severe food insecurity, as well as acute malnutrition children attributed to several factors, but primarily because of conflict (including tribal and interclan fighting) and secondarily because of inaccessibility due to poor infrastructure and swampy nature of most of its land that makes it susceptible to floods, resulting to destruction of crops, properties which equates to hunger.
In response to the above problem, Welthungerhilfe (WHH) proposes a project aiming at mitigating the humanitarian crises in Unity State by ensuring the provision of needs-based emergency aid to prevent or decrease morbidities, mortality, and acute malnutrition, especially for vulnerable people in acute humanitarian crises triggered by flight and displacement, droughts, floods, and anticipated periods of hunger.
The project approach will revolve around addressing issues of climate change through training of 20 DRR committees to develop vulnerability mappings for their specific context on boma (village level) or Payam level (sub-county level), depending on context. The committees will present a cross-section of the population since all ages and genders will be represented. The project will therefore target 56,000 beneficiaries in total in the form of IDPs, returnees and host communities. More specifically, the intervention will target, women and children-headed households, including women of reproductive age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), unaccompanied minors, people with disabilities, the elderly and chronically ill with various activities that include the distribution of NFI kits consisting of various household items, cash for work activities to help the most vulnerable households meet the food consumption gap while building household and community assets that reduce the risk of disaster, strengthen livelihoods and build resilience over time. They will also benefit from agriculture crop production training, distribution of ruminants and support to livestock health to help build their resilience to shocks. The project will seek to reduce the deterioration of people’s physical and mental well-being through coping mechanisms. Target groups will additionally be linked to other ongoing interventions implemented by Welthungerhilfe and other agencies so that they can benefit from the services offered.

1 - Needs

The project will be implemented in Panyijiar County, Unity State which for the fourth consecutive year has been affected by widespread floods mainly due to river overflows and the above-average rains that the state and other parts of the country have faced. The FAO Flood Impact Report of December 2021 showed that Unity state suffered the highest livestock loss in the country with 354,725 losses out of a population of 2,589,634 equivalent to 13.7%. In the CFSAM report of June 2022, Unity state was reported to be facing a 13% decrease in cereal harvest thereby indicating that access to cereal would be a challenge and this may result in vulnerable populations facing hunger and starvation. The Humanitarian Needs Overview of February 2022 indicated that 90,000 (75%) people out of a population of 120,000 residing in Unity state are classified as people in need. Of which out of the classified people in need, 66% of the people fall in the severity phase, 20% in the extreme severity phase and 14% in the catastrophic severity phase. Panyijiar is one of the counties forming part of the Unity state, based on the IPC projections released on 3 November 2022, the food insecurity situation in the county is classified as an emergency (IPC 4) with a very high likelihood of households sliding to the worst acute food insecurity phase 5 if humanitarian food assistance is not provided on time. The continued flooding in the area has deemed crop production unviable thereby reducing the ability of the affected communities to cater for themselves. There is a need to identify dry land which must then be used to its optimum levels to produce crops that facilitate vulnerable household access to diversified nutritious food. The flood situation has also placed the population, particularly women and children at risk as the fragile dikes holding back flood water may fail if not repaired on time. There is a need for Cash for Work Activities (CFA) that are targeted at repairing the existing dikes and ensuring the safety of the population. The CFA will also be designed to enhance livelihoods through agriculture production on the available limited dry land within target areas. Torrential rainfall has been witnessed consecutively from 2020 through 2022, flooding water has been rising in flooded locations in lieu of receding. As an outcome, the displaced communities will have to endure displacement for some time before they can return to their original place of dwelling. Having lost most of their household goods during the early period of floods and without the ability to replenish household items given to them earlier on, there is a need to restore and maintain the dignity of the affected population with non-Food items kits to be able to meet their daily needs. Also, a need for communities to be organized and form structures and systems that enable them to cope with disasters by reducing the impact on their daily lives. Currently support to the affected population has focused on providing them with immediate needs but as the flood situation continues without an end in sight, the need for disaster mitigating measures and preparation for an escalation of the disaster is required.

2 - Project activities

Citing from the IPC report of November 2022, considering the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan, there is a need for immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, particularly those with a high share of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. In response, the project will implement activities that are in alignment with the cluster objectives to prevent famine, enhance production and strengthen the ability to absorb shocks. Under CLFSC/CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3), the project will support 24,500 people by undertaking cash for work/cash for asset activities for extended basic needs and early livelihood recovery. The cash will provide households with the opportunity to make their own decisions regarding food needs and other coping mechanisms. In alignment with the CLFSC/CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5), the project will distribute agricultural seeds for food production to 31,500 beneficiaries and will also support them, through agriculture training. Livestock support will be provided through the provision of small ruminants (which are not prone to livestock diseases), fodder production, livestock vaccination and basic medical care for livestock to preserve the existing herd. Also, in alignment with the CLFSC/CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks, the project will provide on-the-spot-training on emergency agricultural practices and development of emergency preparedness plans for early detection of droughts, floods, famines, pandemics, animal diseases, plant diseases, and pests. Under the project Output A: Provision of local and immediate emergency assistance to enable the protection of especially children, women, elderly, and people with disabilities in humanitarian crises.Activities will include:(1) Creating district/county-based vulnerability mappings/profiles (2) Early Warning training, public awareness, and education to the communities on critical aspects of disaster mitigation(3) Developing emergency preparedness plans and establishing community-based mechanisms for early detection of droughts, floods, famines, pandemics (Covid-19, cholera outbreaks), animal diseases (e.g., foot-and-mouth), plant diseases, and pests (e.g., Fall Army Worm and locusts) (4) Distribution of NFI kits Under the project Output B: Enabling the targeted population to cover individual basic food and non-food needs in the short-term include:(1) Protecting essential livelihoods through the distribution of agricultural/garden kits (2) Training and reinforcement on emergency agricultural practices for short-term food production and(3) Cash for Work/Cash for assets (this activity will help to increase theincome of the target group, strengthen their resilience while at the same time creatingcommunity assets and boosting the local economy(4) Distribution of agricultural seeds (restoration of livelihoods to a small degree)(5) Distribution of small ruminants which are not prone to livestock diseases and therefore easier to manage at the household level.(6) Fodder, vaccination, and basic medical care for livestock to preserve the existing herd.(7) Destocking livestock (Emergency slaughtering for immediate food access) (8) Agricultural training

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Welthungerhilfe (WHH) is a member of the South Sudan Cash working group and participates in the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI). In considering market functionality, WHH considered information from the monitoring initiative as well as our own observations of the market functionality in Panyijiar county. The county has a relatively small but upcoming market. In October 2022, the JMMI report indicated that the South Sudan average Multi-Sectoral Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB) stood at 92,461 SSP. With specifics to Panyijiar County, the JMMI conducted assessments in the two main markets and the finding was that the MSSMEB in Nyal stands at 65,527 SSP and in Ganyliel at 123,368 SSP. Market functionality in Ganyliel was observed to be limited and reduced at Nyal, however, with the ending of the rainy season, road access to both markets is expected to improve and this will result in improved market functionality. The status of market functionality is still adequate to support the cash intervention. It is envisaged that the cash-for-work (CFA) component will incentivize the functionality of the market as it will help to inject cash into the local county economy. The availability of cash within communities provides an opportunity for increased circulation thereby creating spin-offs that benefit other households not directly targeted by the project. Being a member of the South Sudan cash working group, WHH receives regular updates on markets from the clusters which have been used in the design of the proposed intervention. WHH participates in market monitoring data collection in all areas of operation. If there is a sudden change of market to dysfunctional status or unsafe access to markets, WHH will change the delivery modality from cash to an in-kind approach where feasible and advisable.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will undertake cash-for-work activities (CFA). The flexibility that cashes offer makes it a more dignified form of assistance as it empowers households to make their own decisions, choices and priorities based on need. Cash is a less visible form of assistance than in-kind aid, reducing the risk of extortion or theft. Cash also acts as a strong incentive for quality work in the construction of dykes within the identified areas. Cash transfers make people in need less likely to resort to harmful coping strategies, such as survival sex, child labor, family separation or forced marriage. They also directly benefit the local economy and can contribute to peaceful coexistence between different communities. Welthungerhilfe (WHH) is opting for cash because it can be used for multiple purposes by communities thus improving livelihood options beyond the food dimension. The use of cash avoids transportation and handling costs of materials and offers the possibility of utilizing the cash earmarked for such costs on other activities. WHH in partnership with the World Food Program (WFP) has standing agreements with cash transfer agents to ensure the security of the cash, particularly during its transportation to sites where beneficiaries receive it.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Internal mid-term reviews and lessons learnt workshops will be held to ensure all feedback is considered accordingly. Individuals from the most vulnerable groups will also be provided with the opportunity to represent their groups’ issues in some of the meetings and workshops. Channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at the community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handle special interest groups’ complaints and ways of getting feedback from them through a random sampling of individuals for one-on-one engagements. WHH is also in the process of engaging a protection advisor who will develop tools, materials, and strategies to facilitate feed-in and feedback with the most vulnerable members of the community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement will include feedback through meetings and interactions which may be conducted through meetings, phone messages and even one-to-one sessions with concerned persons. During project implementation WHH staff will meet with community leaders and community members and obtain inputs and feedback relating to the project. Regular monitoring surveys, including post-distribution monitoring will also provide opportunities for information sharing. During such exercises, WHH staff will engage directly with the community. Welthungerhilfe will deliberately organize feedback meetings at community level. Normally, all Welthungerhilfe projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Welthungerhilfe (WHH) has a policy on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse which every employee is expected to sign at the beginning of their contract and to adhere to its dictates. There is also a requirement that each one of the WHH employees undertakes an annual refresher course on PSEA. It is also a requirement of the policy that beneficiaries of all projects are informed of this policy during the inception meeting and reminded of it during implementation engagements. The policy provides for sharing of information on PSEA during inception meetings, activity mobilization meetings, information posters and through loud hailers. Communities will also be informed of channels through which they can register or report any PSEA cases for WHH investigation. Refresher training for staff will be held at the beginning of the project at inception and staff will be expected to disseminate PSEA information and follow the laid down procedures in reporting cases which they suspect, notice or those brought to their attention by others. Reporting on PSEA will be an integral part of community training on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Welthungerhilfe (WHH) staff working on the project will be expected to compile feedback reports from their findings during project implementation. All feedback will be escalated accordingly for decision-making. The feedback will be handled by different levels including project management committees to influence needed adaptations to project delivery. Where the project is doing well, the prevailing modalities will be maintained, however, where feedback indicates unacceptable behaviors of implementation modality, the project management team will seek to rectify the issues. Issues that can be adapted at the field level will be done immediately through the head of the Project, and those that require approval from other quarters will be escalated accordingly. Any adaptations to the project will be documented and communicated to all relevant stakeholders in the shortest possible time. Adaptations may include making the project activities more receptive to the main target group of the most vulnerable groups such as children, women, people living with disabilities and people living with a chronic illness.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project is targeting 800 beneficiaries to be trained in early action plans and/or vulnerability and capacity assessments and to reach affected population, disaggregated by gender, age and disability as part of emergency response activities implemented in anticipation/response to disasters/crises. WHH projects focusing on Disaster Mitigation strategies will linked to the food security project to ensure flood displacement does not become a permanent feature in the targeted location. The project will also contribute to the efforts of other stakeholders including local authorities in mapping flood displacement alleviating plans.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Welthungerhilfe (WHH) actively participates in various working groups such as Cash Working Group, GBV/ Protection Mainstreaming/ and Communications with Communities (CwC) Working Group on a national level and County and state levels. Through these forums, WHH will contribute towards collective outcome 2 by complementing advocacy efforts for the rollout of the GBV prevention package through all sector activities. WHH is also in the process of enlisting the services of a protection advisor who will coordinate with other sector advisors in WHH to ensure proper mainstreaming of GBV prevention across all sectors of interest to WHH. WHH is in close collaboration with other protection sector actors to whom referral pathways are already in place to facilitate timely access to quality case management.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project contributes to the reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero) through the provision of seeds, tools, and agriculture training for self-made production of cereals and vegetables for targeted households who have access to land. The project will also procure and distribute small ruminants to vulnerable households so that they can benefit from milk, meat, or resale of the animals to enhance their status. The project will therefore report on beneficiaries who are able to address immediate food and non-food needs through the provision of cash and in-kind support provided through the project activities.
