Needs

Since 25 August 2017, when the latest Rohingya refugee influx from Myanmar to Bangladesh began, the number of refugees has steadily grown in Bangladesh. In an environment that is increasingly challenging for refugees, with heightened social tensions and recent calls for refugees to return, a coordinated, refugee-centered approach is needed to ensure protection and solutions are prioritized for refugees, while access to lifesaving support, particularly to the most vulnerable individuals, including women, children, and the elderly, is maintained. At the end of 2019, refugees continue to possess uneven capacities and power to participate in decision making due to age, gender and diversity-related challenges. A comprehensive approach including coordinated programmes to increase the safe promotion of refugees, including women, in decision making roles in needed. To date, after two years of mobilising women, women and adolescent girls continue to face restrictions in accessing services and managing personal, household and community resources. Persons with disabilities and the elderly - especially women, need more services, communication, feedback and complaint mechanisms to be accessible; and inclusion in activities and decision-making, particularly in relation to service provision. With a view to contribute to effective coordination, and enhance the protection of refugees in Bangladesh, UNHCR and the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) embarked on a joint registration exercise targeting the population, in 2018. The exercise helps advance all refugees’ access to protection services and humanitarian assistance, protects refugees against refoulement while ensuring that refugees’ right to voluntarily return safeguarded once conditions are conducive for return. At early November 2019, 774,829 individuals were registered and verified. In light of the above, a coordinated humanitarian response will be essential to (1) secure the legal identity of Rohingya refugees which has been systematically denied in Myanmar; (2) strengthen the protective environment for refugees and ensure their enjoyment of fundamental human rights; (3) improve the living conditions for refugees in settlements, while bolstering peaceful-coexistence with, and support for, host communities; (4) empower refugees and supporting the establishment of systems of governance that support resilience; and (5) establish the foundation for solutions, including voluntary repatriation and alternative solutions, while establishing interim measures for refugees to live a safe and dignified existence. Specifically, strategic planning and appeals, consistent and efficient monitoring and information management, day to day coordination (including coordination mechanisms at the Upazila and Camp level), communications and advocacy are needed.UNHCR will continue to actively contribute to strengthen the coordination of the humanitarian response in Bangladesh, in order to build, systematise, and scale up solid mechanisms, processes, procedures and capacities to address the protection and basic human needs of refugees. Support is currently provided by the ISCG Secretariat to the Strategic Executive Group, the District HoSO along with the Sector Coordinators to ensure inter-sector interaction with close synergies among all these diverse actors, and with the core objective of timely, needs-based, and evidence-driven humanitarian assistance, efficient use of resources and avoiding duplication.

Activities or outputs

Humanitarian actors are supporting the Government of Bangladesh’s efforts to ensure a timely and efficient response, unimpeded humanitarian access, a common analysis of needs, and a voice for Rohingya refugees, women, men, boys and girls, and affected host communities. In 2020, UNHCR will continue to lead and coordinate actors engaged in the humanitarian response. This includes ongoing advocacy for the protection of refugees regardless of their legal status and location through active participation in and staffing support to the ISCG based in Cox’s Bazar. In addition, in line with the recommendations of the coordination review and in consultation with the sectors and working groups, UNHCR’s engagement with the Government and humanitarian actors will further increase, in a way that fosters an integrated approach across all sectors, including protection, field, and technical and operational support. Management and sectors linkages will be reinforced, ensuring a timely, coordinated, and adequate response to the humanitarian situation. Specific activities:- Support SEG and HoSO efforts to take decisions on key policy, security and operational challenges, informed by protection considerations.- Manage the response programme cycle: needs overviews and analysis, strategic planning and appeals (including contingencies), and response and needs monitoring. - Monitor and analyse access constraints, and engage relevant stakeholders to address them.- Manage data and information in support of humanitarian decision-making, advocacy and public information, resulting in regular information products. - Work with Sectors and humanitarian partners to strengthen accountability and understanding of community capacities and preferences. - Progress on a roadmap to localisation, including building the capacity of national partners to ensure sustainability of the response. - Galvanize agency and Sector capacity in gender and protection to ensure mainstreaming across the response during all stages of the programme cycle. - Support the development of a Centrality of Protection Action Plan in collaboration with the Protection Sector/actors and ensure it is regularly monitored.- Ensure adequate cyclone preparedness and contingency planning with Sectors and humanitarian partners, including SIMEX prior to the cyclone seasons.- Liaise with key stakeholders inside and outside Bangladesh, including humanitarian partners, donors, authorities and representatives of neighbouring countries, with a view to mobilize resources, promote humanitarian access, and advocate respect for international law. - Reinforce and operationalise decentralised coordination mechanisms through the continued management of the humanitarian hub in Ukhiya, and support to Upazila level coordination. UNHCR support to and leadership in the coordination of the humanitarian response in Bangladesh ensure focus on strengthening the protection and policies of the response. In 2020, UNHCR will support the cost-sharing of office operational costs for the InterSector Working Group. In addition, and in collaboration with UNDP, UNHCR will co-fund the position of Social Cohesion/Host Communities Adviser, while UNHCR will co-fund the position of Transfers Working Group Coordinator, in collaboration with IOM.
