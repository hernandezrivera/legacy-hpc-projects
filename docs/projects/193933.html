Project Summary

DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partner, Palestinian Center for Human right (PCHR) propose this project to (SO1) support the ongoing efforts of (DCA\NCA and PCHR) to monitor and document the violations of IHR/IHRL in the Gaza Strip, Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron H2 and Area A&B. The proposed project will contribute to timely documentation and collection of evidence of human rights and International Humanitarian Law (IHL) violations committed by Israeli military in the Gaza Strip. The project will also entail monitoring and documentation of human rights violations in the West Bank, with focus on grave violations against children, settler violence and excessive use of force against protected persons in East Jerusalem, H2 and Area C, , with a view of (SO2) intensifying international advocacy and reinforce the engagement with international bodies and mechanisms to support investigations against perpetrators of severe IHL and human rights abuses and information sharing to push for concrete actions. PCHR’s Fieldwork Unit will monitor and document the Israeli violations through its experienced team of fieldworkers spread throughout the Gaza Strip, Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron H2 and Area A&B. Field workers will conduct field visits, interviews, fill field forms and reports.
PCHR will also have (SO3) facilitated access to justice for Palestinian victims of human rights violations including women victims of gender-based crimes through Legal Aid unit. Lawyers will support in documentation of key incidents and collection of affidavits to build legal case files to enhance access justice mechanism for victim’s human right violations in the Gaza strip.
Thousands of violation forms are expected to be completed through interviews with victims and/or witnesses before being verified and inserted into electronic database, with supporting evidence, including official documents, testimonials, and affidavits. The collected data will be then fed into the advocacy component which will be jointly conducted by both partners DCA/NCA and PCHR to a comprehensive and effective response in the targeted areas. PCHR documents the violations, verifies them, and shares them with a list of organizations. The documentations and reporting of these violations will be used to raise awareness through advocacy initiatives that aim to enhance accountability for violations of IHL. The Advocacy intervention will focus on enhancing the awareness among the international community of the human rights situation in the oPt, including Gaza with focus on Scandinavian countries. It will also reinforce the engagement with UN and EU human rights mechanisms like human rights council, special rapporteurs, and Commission of Inquiry (COI). In the project period and based on the collected data in the process of monitoring and documentation of the violations, DCA/NCA and PCHR will jointly prepare and finalize a thematic report on “attacks on civilians during 2022 in the Gaza strip and west bank”, and then a joint advocacy trip to Scandinavian countries to will be conducted to disseminate the information collected in the report to policymakers, opinion-makers, and journalists at the European level and EU members state level . This work also includes constant follow-up, meetings, briefs, regular newsletter, and urgent updates. with the delegation members after their trip with engagement and advocacy relevant to their functions. The proposed intervention is in line with the HRP Protection Cluster Strategic OBJECTIVES #1: Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations and 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence and OBJECTIVES #2) Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.

1 - Needs

Years 2021 and 2022 were eventful with significant implications for the human rights and international humanitarian law situation in the occupied Palestinian territory. They were commenced with the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court’s decision to open a criminal investigation into the Situation in Palestine, a milestone that crowns decades of perseverance by the PCHR and other human rights organizations against a lengthy record of war crimes committed by the Israeli occupation. In these two years, Israeli launched two wide-scale military offensives on the Gaza Strip in May 2021 and August 2022 respectively. killing hundreds of civilians, as the occupying power reaffirms its indifference towards its international obligations and highlighting the urgent need for an international intervention to protect Palestinian civilians and prosecute suspected perpetrators of war crimes before international justice. During these offensives , Israeli forces used the most advanced munitions in its military arsenal, including warplanes, artillery, and gunboats, targeting with tons of explosives and guided missiles civilian houses and properties as well as the infrastructure and different governmental, non-governmental and private sectors. As a result, In May 2021, the death toll of the Israeli offensives has risen to 253, including 66 children and 39 women, while the number of injuries has amounted to 1461, including 286 women and 433 children. in August 2022, the death toll of the Israeli offensives has risen to 19 civilians, including 8 children and 3 women; and properties were massively destroyed, causing a humanitarian crisis to tens of thousands of residents. The Israeli forces strikes targeted civilian neighborhoods, causing destruction to the civilian properties, and inflicting civilian causalities. In response to the Israeli attacks, the United Nations’ Human Rights Council established a permanent an independent international commission of inquiry with the mandate to investigate in the occupied Palestinian territory, including East Jerusalem, as well as the territory occupied in 1948, into all violations of IHL and IHRL, and to identify the underlying causes of the tensions in Jerusalem and the causes of the conflict in the Gaza Strip. According to PCHR’s reporting in 2022, the human rights situation in the oPt was on the decline. In the West Bank, Israeli forces continued to impose a new demographic reality on the ground through the construction of settlements, that are illegal and illegitimate under international law, and to let loose its settlers attacking Palestinian civilians and their properties, to force them to leave to pave the way for confiscation of their lands. Israeli forces continued to kill Palestinian civilians, in a premeditated and systemic manner, on military checkpoints, at peaceful protests the occupation, and during its raids into the oPt Also, Israeli forces arbitrary arrest policy against Palestinians continued, as thousands were arrested and subjected to degrading treatment, torture, medical negligence, solitary confinement, and other forms of punitive measures. Additionally, Israeli forces enhanced the restrictions on civilians’ freedom of movement between Palestinian cities, villages, and refugee camps across the West Bank, separating those communities with the intention to create isolated cantons where Palestinians exist and eliminate communities. In summary, all these major developments on the ground in the areas of accountability, shrinking space and increasing violations in the west bank is guiding PCHR to intensify its efforts in the field of accountability and shrinking space. It is so important to continue and pursue accountability avenues for Israeli violations within the accessible international avenues to break the cycle of Israeli impunity and to focus on advocacy work as well

2 - Activities

Strengthen Monitoring, verification, and documentation of the possible IHR/IHRL violations including attacks on protected and/or vulnerable groups in the Gaza Strip, Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron H2 and Area A&B 2. Reinforce the engagement with international bodies and mechanisms to support investigations against perpetrators of severe IHL and human rights abuses and to advocate for human rights in the opt and 3. Enhance access to justice for Palestinian victims of human rights violations including women victims of gender-based crimes through Legal Aid unit. This will be achieved through a well- designed package of activities as follows: 1.1 Monitoring, documentation, and reporting of violations of IHL and IHRL, including of protected and/or vulnerable groups. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. Protection needs and priorities of the targeted communities are taken into consideration throughout the development phase.Affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation activities, their involvement starts at the early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies and affidavits from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Any subsequent interventions are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle.DCA/NCA will assist PCHR in developing an official Do No Harm policy that field researchers and volunteers will be trained on. The field workers are instructed to never intervene in an unfolding confrontation between Palestinians and Israeli security forces or settlers. They do not attempt to stop demolitions, for example, only to document them.All safety and security measures will be considered when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will keep close monitoring for the security situation and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency(www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population.PSEA mainstreaming: DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dkand/ or using PCHR’s complaints mechanism channels – Feedback & Complaints – Palestinian Centre for Human Rights (pchrgaza.org) / pchr@pchrgaza.orgFrom its side, PCHR has an active complaining and Feedback system to promote quality of PCHR's services and beneficiaries' satisfaction, and to solve technical and administrative problems that the employee may face while on duty. The complaining system is applicable to any kind of complaints containing complaints to report any sexual harassment cases. There is five mechanisms of submitting complaints at PCHR: Complaints/Feedback Box, Phone, E-mail, and Website, PCHR's Facebook Page and conducts periodic reviews and assessments of its interventions and services through focus groups with beneficiaries. • DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities that will include awareness raising messages sent via SMS messages among other tools. •DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. • DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

DCA/NCA and PCHR adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions considering the humanitarian principles. All beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society, and to people with disability. A principled humanitarian response will be implemented. PCHR is guided by the notion of principled impartiality, meaning that it is not taking sides in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. It supports groups from both communities working for peace and justice. Also, within the Palestinian communities, it does not engage in political action or preferential treatment of segments of the population. DCA/NCA will manage this response through its office and staff in Gaza and its Country Office in Jerusalem. The selected local partner and DCA/NCA staff in Gaza have experience working under conditions that lead to restricted humanitarian access and adjust timing and approach as required. DCA/NCA has developed an Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan for assuring access. Networking with CBOs and NGOs in the targeted areas, as well as empowering women leaders within safe spaces, will guarantee access of vulnerable groups to project services. The DCA/NCA will conduct regular visits following up on the indicators and response plan and assessing response implementation via discussions with the response team and rights holders and by visiting the response sites. - Safety of beneficiaries is a top priority and is evident in the application of 'do no harm' principle during monitoring, documentation, and Advocacy work. Each implementing partner’s Code of Conduct prohibits exercise of any form of abuse (verbal or physical), and any engagement in any form of sexual harassment or exploitation of other member of staff, beneficiaries or a third party. The Code of Conduct must be read and signed by every staff member upon hiring, volunteers and interns must also read and sign it. Monitoring of IHL/IHRL violations will encompass all affected communities in the OPT- DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives.- DCA\NCA with its partner monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g., persons with disabilities, elderly). -DCA\NCA and PCHR will facilitate access to services for persons with reduced mobility. Furthermore, to manage security risks and access, DCA/NCA employs a security focal point who monitors the security situation daily, exchanges information with other international organizations and the UN and updates staff. Standard security procedures are in place, which all staff are aware of and follow. DCA/NCA is connected and follows UNDSS security updates, which is shared with its local partners, who also follow their local networks for security updates. Additionally, daily assessment for the security situation on the ground in coordination with relevant organizations, partner CBO’s and local committees in the area will take place to ensure the safety and security of staff. DCA/NCA will involve its community groups/Community engagement committees in the project implementation and monitoring. This aims to build community confidence and networks and ensure proper access to affected people.
