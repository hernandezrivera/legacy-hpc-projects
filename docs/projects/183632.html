Project Summary

Ensuring access to improved, safe, equitable, and sustainable WASH services, as well as enhancing preparedness capacity to mitigate and prevent floods, are considered urgent priorities to guarantee the protection, dignity, and resilience of the most vulnerable communities and institutions of the West Bank. The proposed intervention is in line with HRP 2022 Strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and it is therefore complementing WASH cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3. The proposed activities were built upon the WASH Cluster eligible interventions for HRP 2022, targeting the most vulnerable communities and institutions according to the WASH 2022 vulnerability mapping and to the Protection Response Plan, an output of WeWorld-GVC Community Protection Approach. The intervention has been coordinated and endorsed by the Palestinian Water Authority. It is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan and compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The intervention complements and another initiative submitted by the West Bank Protection Consortium for the purposes of the HRP 2022. The intervention is also in line with SDG 6 “Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”. WW-GVC, in partnership with MA’AN aims to targets the core vulnerabilities of people in need by: (1) increasing access, availability, quantity, and quality of water at HH and community level, providing safe water by trucking to 34 communities in Area C (4,806 beneficiaries) for all the dry season; connecting 9 communities in Area C (5,927 beneficiaries) trough installation/rehabilitation of water networks, transmission pipelines, and HHs connection; increasing water storage capacity for 37 communities in Area C (4,974 beneficiaries) trough cisterns construction/rehabilitation of cistern at HHs level and through the installation of 1 PE water tank for each HHs benefitting from the water distribution by trucking (595 HHs/tanks) ensuring the quality of the delivered water; Providing adequate operation and maintenance tools for 1 community in Area C - (2)guarantying a safe and adequate level of sanitation and hygiene services at the household level, through the installation of mobile latrines and construction of HHs sanitation facilities in 5 communities in Area C. (317 beneficiaries) (3) enhancing access to WASH services in institutions, through the provision of safe water by trucking to 6 schools in Area C, for 90 days. Additionally, the schools will be served with adequate and safe PE storage tanks (two for each school) to ensure the quality of the delivered drinking water. (750 beneficiaries),(4) promoting floods mitigation and prevention, by the construction/rehabilitation of stormwater networks and drainage systems in 5 communities in WB (5,770 beneficiaries) (5) Developing awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, to advocate for the right of the most vulnerable Palestinian to receive adequate WASH services. (10 awareness products). The intervention will benefit in total 17,737 direct beneficiaries (4,465 boys, 4,303 girls, 4,266 men, 4,085 women, 276 elders male, 343 elders women, of which 311 PWDs) in 53 communities in the Governorates of Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Nablus, Qalqilya, Tubas, Jericho, Jenin, mainly in Area C (94%), and Area A/B (6%). Moreover, it is estimated that the awareness products have a capacity of reaching about 2.000.000 viewers/recipients in a year. The intervention will be delivered through different response modalities, primarily cash transfer, when feasible, through WW-GVC Community Implementation methodology, or by services delivery through contracting, and finally in-kind modality. WW-GVC and MA’AN ensure that the proposed interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of the WASH vulnerability, increasing the resilience of the community under threat of forcible transfer in Area C, enhancing the protection, supporting preparedness and response on combating COVID-19 effects.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities continue to be an urgent priority in the West Bank, particularly for the 620,000 Palestinians that are not connected to piped water services or suffer from the poor supply. The COVID 19 pandemic outbreak worsened an already compromised reality, in fact, the most vulnerable communities and institutions with inadequate WASH services, cannot adhere to the WHO recommendations of frequent hand washing and environmental disinfection and end up being at a greater risk of contagion. Especially in Area C, water consumption rates can reach less than 35 l/c/d – less than half of the 100 l/c/d WHO’ recommendation and 100 communities are mainly dependent on unreliable, unprotected, and unimproved water sources (ex. rainwater harvesting cisterns) and/or are enforced to purchase tanker water by trucking as a coping mechanism. Additionally, 4,000 communities in the West Bank are challenged by limited access to adequate household sanitation facilities, which lead to negative practices as sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces for their personal sanitation needs, eventually triggering health and sanitary risks and protection concerns, aggravated by COVID 19. Furthermore, according to the WASH cluster, in the West Bank 129 schools count with inadequate or insufficient WASH facilities, including limited access to safe and adequate drinking water. Climate change exacerbates the impacts and increases the frequency of flooding events in the West Bank, affecting 94,000 residents who live in low-lying areas at high risks, on their livelihood, preventing communities' access and thus leading to isolation as well as triggering water-borne related diseases and health and environmental impact. This intervention is grounded on thorough evidence-based assessments identifying communities and institutions lacking WASH services and floods prevention and mitigation measures, and therefore proper coping mechanisms to withstand the protracted threats and the recent COVID 19 pandemic, with a high risk of displacement. Those assessments are: - Community Based Protection Approach (CPA), initiated in 2015, updated on yearly basis, in the framework of the ECHO WB Protection Consortium, where WW-GVC collected quantitative and qualitative data, including a specific WASH sector-analysis for 169 communities located in Area C, H2 and EJ for the development of Protection Community profiles and Protection response plans; - WASH cluster vulnerability mapping updated on August 2021 - Water master plan for Area C targeting 247 communities, developed by WW-GVC and AAH in 2017 in coordination with PWA. Moreover, WW-GVC and MA'AN conducted an in-depth field assessment in September 2021 in the most vulnerable communities of WB, identifying at institutions, HHs, community-level the WASH gaps and constraints on providing high standard quality WASH services, highlighting the communities extremely in need of urgent interventions. Through the assessment, WW-GVC identified and coordinated with PWA, WASH cluster and other main WASH stakeholders to target 45 highly vulnerable Area C communities in the Governorates of Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Nablus, Qalqilya, Tubas, Jericho, Jenin are mainly dependent on the direct purchase of water (trucking) for most of the year, and/or are lacking on unreliable piped water services and water storage, failing to match Humanitarian Benchmarks of 60 l/c/d and even the critical minimum of 30 l/c/d. In these communities, water expenses can account for up to 50% of HHs monthly expenses with a negative impact especially on women who prioritize the use of water for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, and men who prioritize water for their livestock, generating negative protection impacts increasing the risk of displacement and contagion of COVID. Increased access to WASH facilities, such as water networks and latrines, stands as the humanitarian response to improve communities living conditions. Lack of WASH facilities affects also the hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation, critically jeopardizing their sense of privacy and personal safety. 5 communities (Hebron governorate: Hiwara; Jericho governorate: Al Jiftlik - al Musaffah; Nablus governorate: Khirbet Tana; Ramallah governorate: Al Mu’arrajat Centre, Tubas governorate: Al Qaffaf) were identified as highly unserved, not having functioning latrines for all the inhabitants, which leads to open defecation and poses hygiene and health hazards due to the propagation of water-borne disease. Moreover, adequate and safe access to water and WASH services at the school level is essential to ensure a quality learning environment; all the 6 identified in need Palestinian public schools located in Area C (Ramallah governorate: Ras al tin, Wadi as Seeq; Hebron governorate: Susiya, Mantiqat Shi’b al Butum, Wadi ar Rim; Bethlehem governorate: Ash Shawawra), require the provision of sufficient drinking water and augmented storage capacity. Finally, the increasingly frequent extreme weather events in winter, together with the poor maintenance of stormwater networks and drainage systems, pose the identified 5 communities (Ajja, Al ‘Attara, Al Mughayyr, Fahma al Jadida, Khirbet al Hamaam in Jenin governorate) under high risk of displacement and destruction of their properties, with even worse health and environmental consequences if stormwater mixes with garbage and/or overflow wastewater. All considered WW-GVC and MA'AN developed an integrated WASH response dealing with the source of WASH vulnerability at community and institutions level, such as water availability and support to service providers, adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities at HH, communities and schools’ level, and flood mitigation and prevention.

2 - Activities

Proper coordination was performed with PWA, Local Authorities, and major stakeholders active in the WASH sector on the target communities to avoid overlapping. Governorates, Municipalities, and Village Councils (VCs) would be involved as local partners for the collection of data related to the baseline and the end line. Concerning the installation/rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks, and HH connections and the installation/ rehabilitation of household water cisterns, the intervention will primarily encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WeWorld-GVC Community Implementation methodology (CI), which is mixed in-kind - cash modality entailing the creation of a Community Project Committee (CPC) composed of representatives of the communities/CBOs (gender-balanced), which are the local implementing partners. CPCs are responsible for the implementation and maintenance of the infrastructures. The supply of materials for the implementation would be under the full responsibility of WW-GVC and MA’AN and it is considered an in-kind cost. The beneficiaries will receive direct costs coverage for their work through conditional cash transfer modality, and it will be paid upon receipt of a payment request and according to a progress report (verified by the Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that certify the work accomplishment along with the payments amount eligibility. Regarding the provision of PE water tanks for the communities and schools, the development of awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, and the provision of trucked water for the communities and schools, it shall be considered as a full in-kind modality of implementation. While the construction/ rehabilitation of stormwater network/rainwater drainage systems and the provision of household latrines are considered under the services delivery modality. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by WW-GVC along with the intervention. Before any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community will be conducted. Specific SOPs are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks and WW-GVC CI will allow reducing the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment of Area C, enhancing the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries, to guarantee safety, dignity, and avoid harming (do not harm) the affected population. Therefore, the intervention will be committed to ensuring gender sensitiveness and inclusiveness on the facilities design for all, including girls and boys with special needs and/or disabilities. In accordance, the design of the sanitation facilities will ensure privacy for women and children with the provision of dignified space with good physical and sound insulation and will be adapted to special needs for PWDs. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

CASH Modality: the project will encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WW-GVC Community Implementation methodology (CI), where Community Project Committees (CPC) are responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of the infrastructures and will receive direct costs through conditional cash transfer modality. Adequate technical capacity building and transparency insurance mechanisms will be in place to monitor the success of this modality of intervention. For each technical BOQ for water network construction and rehabilitation and for the cistern construction, there is a division into two parts which are the cost for construction material and labor cost. Usually, the construction materials form about 50%, while the skilled labor costs form 50% of the total cost for each intervention. Therefore, the (CI) has been chosen to realize the construction and rehabilitation of water networks and cisterns, since it is a methodology that falls under the conditional cash transfer modality intended as the “provision of financial support to individuals or households, either as emergency relief intended to meet their basic needs for food and non-food items or services or to buy assets essential for the recovery of their livelihoods" allowing the procurement of the material from the partners. Capacity building and monitoring will be done along with the intervention. Service Delivery Modality: when it will not be feasible to proceed with CI (due to technical complexity or difficulty in establishing CPCs), a procurement process will be initiated to select a contractor that will implement the works and deliver the finished product to the beneficiaries.In-kind: This modality was chosen for every other activity in order to provide inputs and tools to beneficiaries to improve their access to WASH services. The beneficiaries will be asked to be committed contribute to operating and preserve and functioning the provided inputs or performed improvement activities.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. The Partners will jointly scrutiny the content of the complaints and/or feedback and if necessary, will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or if disciplinary actions are required. Accordingly, the Partners commit to addressing the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WW-GVC has a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and their communities/institutions, throughout the duration, and up to 6 months after the end of the project. This is part of complementing WW-GVC’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability, and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness, and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions, and inquiries in a secured way, and receives a response promptly. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVC’s Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers, and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to its sensitivity. If the complaint requires follow-up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call from WW-GVC’s Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the monitoring and coordination manager analyses complaints and categorize each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by the WW-GVC CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Accessibility to all population groups is maintained through the whole project life-cycle. Every member of the field staff is obliged to inform and explain all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) and the referral pathway for their complaints, as well as provide the contact information/hotline for the CRM focal points depending on the type of the complaint (female focal point for GBV and PSEA; a male focal point for other types of complaints). Beneficiaries are also informed that the complaints are to be kept confidential specifically for sensitive issues, and they will be dealt with accordingly. The complaints can be verbal by the dedicated project hotline, by email, or by writing, and all complaints are documented in the CRM template by the focal point, dealing with each complaint based on its level of sensitivity. All complaints are tracked and documented from the first day till closing. and the beneficiary should be informed once it is closed officially.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Build upon WW-GVC Community Protection Approach (CPA), the proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children – since the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. This approach is built from a multisectoral perspective, which supports the communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. Particular attention was given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process; further, the intervention takes also into account the indications provided by WW-GVC “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context. WW-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint response mechanism system (WW-GVC CRM) allowing beneficiaries and the affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will in turn influence management decisions. Whenever possible the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measures to meet “Do No Harm” requirements, especially in light of COVID- 19 ongoing outbreak, by using all the preventive measures and equipment to minimize the risk of contagion. Finally, all staff involved in the projects is aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in WW-GVC Code of Conduct and related policies including Child-Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project, in line with the sustainable humanitarian approach, aims to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability, and impartial access by conflict-affected communities and institutions of WB to decent WASH services. The project aims to increase access to water, sanitation, and hygiene, as well as the efficiency, predictability, and equity of the WASH service and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, men with or without disabilities. The planned interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWDs to safe water and sanitation, through the access to safe and equitable water, increasing the HH storage capacity, access to adequate and adapted sanitation facilities, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools and intervention towards flooding mitigation and prevention. The strategy, activities, and materials used for implementing the project were developed and selected to tackle the assessed needs, together with and in the best interests of the beneficiaries, building upon lessons learned from similar projects implemented in the past. As a consequence, the project is expected to positively impact all community members and institution users, but in particular, women, as there will be a noticeable decrease in time and effort spent in fetching water at the household level, especially for girls and women who are usually in charge of this task; therefore, fewer tasks will be imposed on girls and women who will not have to face long walks to collect the water, reducing exposure to the protection risks related to their trips to the water source (i.e. attacks and harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers). In the case of families with PWDs members, access to water will help to alleviate the work of the caregivers and help to overcome difficulties. Finally, a specific design of WASH facilities (i.e latrines) will guarantee their ease of access during use and maintenance to all members of the community and institutions, including girls, women, boys, and men, with or without disabilities of all ages.
