Project Summary

Under the proposed intervention the ACF in collaboration with the SMoH/SPHCDA/SPHCMB and other humanitarian organizations (e.g. WHO, UNICEF/UNFPA and I/NGO's) plans to continue providing life-saving primary health care services targeting 87 facilities and mobile clinics (54 in Borno and 33 in Yobe) in 16 LGAs (10 in Borno and 8 in Yobe) through an integrated approach. The proposed health care services will include support to improve Health system and clinical support, identification, assessment and management of communicable disease, SHRH services, referral of complicated cases to specialize secondary and tertiary care centers, supporting community health to detect and refer, promotion of appropriate healthcare-seeking behavior, provision of pharmaceutical and medical commodities.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict has affected the capacity of the SMoH/SPHCDA/SPHCMB to respond to the population's health needs; more than 46% of health facilities are either fully destroyed or damaged in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states (WHO Report). In Borno state, an assessment of 2,602 facilities in the BAY states revealed that 69% health facilities are non-functional and 39% (292) have been fully damaged during the conflict (Health sector Bulletin, June 2019). There is an urgent need to scale up health services as existing response capacities are overwhelmed due to the scale and burden on health needs. In the three BAY states, 5.3 million people need healthcare services of whom 800,000 are estimated to be in hard to reach areas. Currently, only 33% of the targeted population has been reached with healthcare services (Health Sector, June 2019). ACF aims to prioritize women of reproductive age and children to health services, who suffer from avoidable and treatable health conditions. Northeast Nigeria has the highest maternal mortality rate, compared to other regions, with 1,549 deaths per 100,000 live births (June 2018) and children face a high probability of dying under the age of 5 (lower than the national average where 120 out of 1000 children born are likely to die (WHO, June 2018). The inadequacy of health services offered to women victims of rape as well as difficulty in delivering humanitarian services in hard to reach areas due to the current dire insecurity situation is also major bottlenecks. According to ACF’s 2017 NHMIS Capacity Assessment in selected LGA’s in Yobe State, health workers identified numerous challenges including a lack of adequate training, inadequate human resources and lack of a streamlined monitoring system for health and nutrition in the state. Limited sexual reproductive health services are offered to the 2 million women are at reproductive age and 1.6 million men are sexually active. In remote areas, the inability to access health services causes populations at risk of being impacted by disease outbreaks. There is a high burden of childhood illness in Yobe state, in 2017 rates of diarrhea, symptoms of Acute Respiratory Infection and fever stood at 22.6%, 10.6% and 40.4% respectively, and immunization rates for preventable childhood illness were only at 7%. According to Early Warning Alert and Response Systems (Epi week 25, 2019), the lead cause of morbidity and mortality has been malaria accounting for 31% of cases and SAM accounting for 27% of reported deaths. Endemic malaria still accounts for more than 50% of mortality and morbidity among children with acute respiratory tract infection, watery diarrhea and severe acute malnutrition as leading causes of illness. Limited access to quality health care services, inadequate and suboptimal capacity of the health providers to deliver health services, shortage of medical supplies and commodities are also the challenges. ACF in collaboration with the SMoH/SHPCDA/SPHCMB and other humanitarian organization (WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and other INGOs) planned to support the provision of primary health care services at 87 facilities and mobile clinics in 16 LGAS through an integrated approach. The proposed health care services will include support to improve the health system and clinical support, identification, assessment and management of communicable disease, SHRH services, and referral for the non-communicable disease to specialized secondary and tertiary care centres.

2 - Activities

Health System strengthening and Clinical Support: Health care providers capacity enhancement via training and mentoring, support to ensure appropriate medical waste management system established, and provision of free of charge primary health care services for children, pregnant and lactating mothers and adults. Supporting the supply chain and information management, detection of complicated cases and referral support, minor rehabilitation of fixed facilities as well as the construction of incinerators will the major focus areas to strengthen the health system. Management of Communicable Diseases: Training of health workers on IMNCI, and managing common childhood illnesses in supported facilities, identification, and referral of advanced medical complications among children and referral support to Hospitals. Also, providing immunization services at health facilities and mobile sites will be one of the major focus areas to contribute to the eradication of poliovirus and reduce morbidity and mortality due to vaccine-preventable diseases through coordination and collaboration with SMoH and WHO. Sexual Reproductive Health: Training of health workers on reproductive health care services (SRH package), technical training of community skilled birth attendants on delivery support and follow up visits, Provision of Antenatal and postnatal care services (formal/informal camps or host communities) including supporting delivery at the supported facilities. Furthermore, it will provide clean delivery kit, dignity kit, rapid test kits and medical supplies for reproductive health care in collaboration with UNICEF, UNFPA and SMoH (formal/informal camps or host communities). Community Health: Training of Community Health Workers on symptomatic identification and referral of key childhood diseases, pregnant women and the promotion of health-seeking behaviors (including hygienic practices and IYCF). It will also support community health workers to organize Vaccination campaigns, vitamin A and deworming campaigns.
