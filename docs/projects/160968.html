Project Summary

The proposed 12 months project is intended to enhance the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialised services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs to targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard to reach and priority geographical areas. The intervention is designed to:
1) Increase access to life saving GBV case management, psycho-social support (PSS) and legal aid services for women and girls.
2) Reduce risks to GBV through prevention and response to violence against women and girls.
The project will cost $450,000 and will be implemented in Bentiu, Rubkona County, Unity State. It will provide key multi sectoral services that complement case management and PSS and legal support for survivors according to their wishes. The multi sectoral services for survivors will be provided in line with GBV sub cluster standard operating procedures with a particular focus on guiding principles, survivor centered approaches and referral pathways. According to the 2008 South Sudan Bureau of Statistics, the population of Rubkona was 100,236 people. Of these, the project will benefit 18,000 internally displaced persons, returnees and the host communities comprising of 8750 women, 3900 men, 3850 girls and 1500 boys and 120 persons with disabilities. STEWARDWOMEN [STW] will collaborate with IRC and DRC to provide legal information to women and girls attending the WGFs in order to provide holistic GBV response to women and girls. The utilization of the WGFs will be in line with South Sudan WGFs which ensures that women and girls are protected, empowered and feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable and able to express themselves. STW will also provide legal aid information to about 350 survivors who are already being handled by IRC and DRC through case management. It is envisioned that about 15 survivors who may wish to pursue legal services, will be supported through initiating legal proceedings according to their wishes. STW will also provide trainings to male and female staff, community leaders, and local authorities on GBV guiding principles, referral pathway, the GBV IASC guidelines and legal aid. There will be on going community awareness on GBV through neighbourhood assemblies to disseminate GBV prevention and response information to women and girls who fear to come forward to access the available GBV services and also reach out to persons with disabilities and the sick in the neighbourhoods. These sessions are meant to encourage survivors to timely seek out for help provided at the health facilities and at the WGFs where specialized group psycho-social support sessions are provided to survivors’ to facilitate healing process from traumatic events. To mitigate and respond to the needs of women, girls, men and boys affected by conflict, STW will participate in conducting frequent joint community-based safety audit assessments, address risks identified, and strengthen community based support mechanisms as a means of creating safe and meaningful access to relevant services. STW will support the commemoration of 2020 International Women’s Day and 16 days of activism against GBV activities. We shall also hold consultations with women and girls throughout the project to ensure that their concerns are included in the project implementation.
We will further ensure that all our activities take a “Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach”. IDPs, returnees and local host communities will be targeted, as well as all those at risk of GBV.

1 - Needs

The effects of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services continue to be felt throughout South Sudan with 7.2 million people in need of assistance [South Sudan Situation Report, 11th Oct 2019]. GBV has remained a persistent feature of the crisis, exacerbated by incidents of conflict, but reflective of deeply entrenched discriminatory cultural norms and gender inequality [HNO Report, 2019]. Insecurity has continued to be the primary need and exacerbating GBV [RRC, July 2019]. Protection of women and girls is further exacerbated by the ensued armed conflict. In Rubkona- Bentiu, key protection concerns for women and girls manifests in crowded dwellings, long distance in search of basic services such as water and sanitation facilities, markets, cooking energy and poor attitudes of service providers, including sexual exploitation tendencies. According to the GBV IMS report in Bentiu, [Sept 2019], legal services are very weak and it accounts for the lowest referrals, with less than 1% of survivors accessing justice. Access to justice and legal aid are fundamental rights for GBV survivors. It reduces impunity and promotes long term GBV prevention. Our engagements with communities in Bentiu in the on-going project since March 2019 demonstrate increase on violence against women and girls mainly due to the absence of static formal courts. Incidences of sexual violence, including rape continue to be handled by informal courts. The informal courts on average charge 3500 SSP to register complaints, which cannot be afforded by most women in the area, thus limiting access to justice. Abused women cannot even seek for divorce in the informal courts. Traditional chiefs lack knowledge on their jurisdiction and the GBV legal frame work. The informal courts are not supervised by the local government. There are no permanent Judges in Bentiu. The only permanent state prosecutor lives in Juba. There are no trained police investigators. The police are also hesitant in handling GBV cases and regard it family matter. There is lack of trust between the community and the police. Some police officers have been implicated in rape and other forms of violence against women and girls. Report by the Legal Aid Attorney of STW [October 2019] to Rubkona Prison demonstrate that some rape suspects have remained in detention for more than 3 years without trial. The UNMISS supported mobile court is focused on Bentiu POC, hence not benefitting those outside the PoC. This intervention will strengthen the work of the government by supporting the judiciary to provide legal services to citizens. This will include legal information, education, counseling and assistance. We shall also enhance the capabilities of the local justice structures namely police, customary courts and community leaders to provide justice services to GBV survivors and the entire community. STW consulted the National GBV sub-cluster, UNFPA, DRC and UNMISS Human Rights Division, Rubkona police and prisons department to inform the intervention of this project. Consultation meetings were also conducted with women and girls during August 2019 project review meeting and the feedback of the beneficiaries informed the development of this project. STW will use their eight years of legal aid assistance to GBV survivors through legal aid clinics, mobile courts, paralegals, special protection units, GBV desk, family courts and legal information dissemination through the neighborhood assemblies to scale up the GBV prevention and response activities in the project location and document lessons learned.

2 - Activities

1. Training for staff, GBV Survivor Support Groups, community leaders including traditional chiefs, local authorities, and police on GBV guiding principles, referral pathway, the GBV IASC guidelines and legal aid; the training will be for 5 days bringing together 75 participants. The training is meant to strengthen community based protection mechanisms as these groups deal with GBV in their respective areas of operation. The training of traditional chiefs who are usually men also acts as a form of men engagement on GBV.2. Provide essential services through case referrals to 1500 women and 500 girls; STW will disseminate and support the already existing referral pathway by working with existing service providers in Bentiu such as IRC, UNFPA, Mercy Corps, DRC etc. Together we shall update the referral pathway periodically and disseminate the information to the communities.3. Provide legal aid information/legal education to 2800 GBV survivors i.e. 2600 at WGFS (1300 women, 300 girls) and 200 at the legal aid clinic (110 women, 30 men, 10 boys and 50 girls); Provision of legal aid information will be done by STW staff visiting the IRC and DRC WGFs. Since IRC and DRC will be offering case management, STW will provide legal aid information to 1800 survivors in IRC and 800 in DRC according to their wishes. STW already has an established office in Bentiu that also acts as a legal aid clinic where survivors can readily access services such as legal education, assistance and counselling. Legal assistance involves supporting victims of GBV with prosecution and related costs while bearing in mind their protection is paramount. Hence STW will continue to provide legal education, assistance and counselling to women, men, boys and girls.4. Litigation of 40 GBV cases through the mobile court; STW is currently working with UNMISS human rights division to host the next mobile court in Bentiu. It is anticipated that the mobile court shall prosecute 40 GBV cases through the mobile court sessions. Costs involved during the mobile court will include hiring Victim’s Advocate to prosecute GBV cases, facilitation for judges, refreshments, lunch and transport for survivors during court hearings.5. Conduct GBV awareness sessions through the neighbourhood assemblies; the community paralegals will conduct weekly protection awareness through neighbourhood assemblies throughout the project period. Neighbourhood assemblies are home to home awareness-raising sessions on pertinent issues on GBV prevention and response identified by the very people in those homes. It brings together homesteads within a radius of 50-100 meters together. The resolutions reached in these sessions are implemented by a committee of 3-5 identified during the sessions. The neighborhood assemblies usually reaches out to persons with disabilities, the elderly, the sick and women with babies among others who are constrained in their homes and unable to attend to public events. This gives them opportunity to discuss and contribute to critical issues that affect them. 6. Men led GBV campaigns and dialogues; there will be tailored campaigns and dialogues on GBV men. This will include campaigns and dialogues on end child marriage, Zero tolerance of rape, girl child education etc to challenge negative masculinities, patriarchal norms and harmful practices that discriminate against women and girls and allow violence.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. Through this project, STEWARDWOMEN will address GBV and impunity by providing direct support to GBV survivors through documentation, legal representation and capacity building that will lead to upholding the rights of individual survivors to live dignified lives. Extensive awareness in target communities will encourage participation of survivors in public spaces and demand for their rights. Engaging the relevant government line ministries in the provision and coordination of multi-sector service on the needs of survivors will empower human rights duty bearers to ably identify and respond to the needs of right holders- survivors. The project aims to improve access to justice for GBV survivors in the context of armed violence in South Sudan, which directly links to their right to a just and fair hearing. The right to access to justice is recognized in international and regional human right instruments many of which have been ratified by South Sudan. STEWARDWOMEN will strengthen the informal and formal protection mechanisms through training on GBV guiding principles among others with a focus on confidentiality and as well provision of safe and temporary shelter for survivors in collaboration with other agencies, thus enabling victim protection and enjoyment of their rights to safety. Non-discrimination and inclusion are central to our approach, both to staffing and beneficiary selection to ensure that all persons enjoy their rights.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. The project will be implemented in Rubkona County in both Bentiu POC and Rubkona town which is both a displacement community and host community. UNHCR also reported some 17,000 spontaneous returnees’ arrival in Unity State from Sudan between April and June 2019, following unrest and insecurity in Sudan. All these communities affected by conflict will benefit from the project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. STEWARDWOMEN has already established two family courts in Bentiu POC and Rubkona consisting of 40 members. The family court is an initiative of STEWARDWOMEN in collaboration with traditional chiefs that started in 2013 where traditional chiefs and women leaders are trained together on the human rights based approaches to conflict resolution. It’s called family court because most incidences of GBV happen between known family members or neighbors and hence the chiefs stand in a good position to resolve the issues. During the training, the participants learn about GBV guiding principles, the South Sudan Bill of Rights as provided for in the Transitional Constitution, 2011; the difference between civil and criminal law, the hierarchy of courts in South Sudan and jurisdiction of each of the courts and alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. We shall establish 2 more family courts and continue to work with the already two existing ones. Our Legal Aid Attorney together with the community paralegals also do conduct police and prison visits to provide legal education and counselling to the inmates. During one of those visits, it has been found that some inmates have been in custody for over 5 years without being charged of any crime. The cases that are also registered at the legal aid clinic are litigated by the government High Court in Bentiu during the quarterly mobile court. STEWARDWOMEN is also working closely with the line directorate of Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare; Ministry of Internal Affairs, especially the police; and Ministry of Justice in Bentiu among others.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. The provision of access to justice to GBV survivors will be in line with the South Sudan national action plan and the national protection cluster strategy. Protection of survivors is a key priority area of the National Gender Policy of the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare (2018-2020), which provides for the promotion of a robust policy and legal framework towards elimination of SGBV and institutionalization of appropriate response and protection mechanisms against SGBV. The project is also aligned to South Sudan National Action Plan 2015-2020, which is based on the Mandate of UNSCR 1325 with a special focus on two pillars namely prevention and protection. The project will also take into account National Social Protection Policy Framework produced by the Ministry of Gender, Child, and Social Welfare; and Ministry of Humanitarian, and Disaster Management which sets out to respond to and address the multiple vulnerabilities faced by South Sudanese citizens, with a particular focus on the poorest and most excluded sectors’ (GoSS 2014).

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. STEWARDWOMEN envisions a sufficiently stable environment for protection of survivors. While this proposal is considerate of the protection needs of various groups, deliberate efforts shall be made to undertake regular protection analysis and needs identification for remedial action in the cause of the project. Additionally, STEWARDWOMEN is a women organisation established to address the problems of GBV, harmful customs on women and girls, child labor and trafficking, community insecurity and conflicts, poor governance and illiteracy in South Sudan. With that in mind, STEWARDWOMEN will make use of the referral pathway already developed in Bentiu; disseminate it to the community to seek various services in instances of protection threats. STEWARDWOMEN currently works with 3 social workers and 5 community paralegals in Bentiu who are trained to offer first aid and psychosocial support to the most vulnerable. STEWARDWOMEN also has a legal aid clinic with a female Legal Aid Attorney ready to provide legal education, counselling and assistance to survivors who demand the service. The safety of survivors will be regularly monitored through safety nets established such as the existing WGFs. We shall also ensure that all our activities take a “Do No Harm and Conflict Sensitive Approach”.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. STEWARDWOMEN will work with trained case workers, community paralegals, Survivor Support Groups, traditional chiefs and the line Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare in the implementation of this project. The project builds lasting partnership and collaboration with key service providers, including government institutions in implementation of specific activities for survivors with their own sources of funding that ensures institutional ownership and sustainability. In addition, STEWARDWOMEN also maintains a legal aid basket fund that will continue to support GBV survivors with legal aid services when HRP funding comes to an end. The project has lasting impact on the target groups and beneficiaries to be self sufficient through establishing and mentoring support groups that promote community ownership and enable survivors’ voices to be heard beyond the project lifetime. Through the feed-back mechanisms, the capacity of target communities and beneficiaries are enhanced to hold service providers and local governments accountable, thus improving the quality of service delivery in the area. Capacity building through training, peer support, documentation and sharing of best practices with partners and collaborators will enable them in the future to seek alternative funding and continue the interventions. Besides, STW will sufficiently disseminate the impact of the project and outcomes [evaluation report] so as to attract funding to further sustain the intervention.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

There will be continual project risk assessment due to the volatile security situation in the area. We shall hold project inception meetings for stake holders to solicit their support. We shall hold project induction workshop for the project staff focusing on the relevant humanitarian accountability standards, safety issues etc. We shall continually disseminate security updates from UNMISS to our staff and beneficiaries during activity implementation. We shall ensure compliance to UN and government security and safety guidelines and measures issued from time to time, including clearance from local security agencies for activity implementation as it stands of now. We shall seek permission from the responsible government authority for activity implementation when and as required. We will also seek permission of beneficiaries during activity implementation. We will ensure that activities for survivors are conducted at the WGFS. We shall hold separate engagements with women, girls, boys and men during project implementation to address the needs of the different groups. We shall hold one-on-one meetings with survivors and witnesses who wish to pursue legal action. All project activities will be implemented in collaboration and consultation with women leaders. The referral pathway will be displayed on all public notices and the information streamlined in all project activities. The consent of respondents will first be sought during field data collection, and assured of their confidentiality. The project data base will be kept in a lockable filling cabinet. STW will apply the organization’s child protection policy in dealing with children, which is guided by the principles of the welfare of a child; the rights, wishes and feelings of children is respected and listened to etc. Accountability groups, including women and girls will be formed and consulted regularly to provide feedback on the project.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

We shall hold project inception workshops to set bench marks for project monitoring by key stake holders. There will be regular joint stake holders meetings as complaint and feedback mechanisms and to ensure the voices of beneficiaries are heard. Project committees, inclusive of survivors and women with disabilities will be formed as accountability groups and consulted quarterly to provide feedback and influence our project implementation. STW will continue to support established survivor support groups to reach out to women survivors with key messages on human rights, access to justice and referral pathway. We shall document all project activities, which is a critical component of our communication strategy in the project. Stories of change will be documented in form of videos and newsletters. The media will be an important aspect of our communication strategy. The team plans to use Excel and SPSS software which can link beneficiary feedback and track responses over time. This will provide valuable lessons and accurate credible data for multiple actors on trends, the quality of current programming, and the potential for scaling up impact. There will be regular project review meetings, in addition to end of project reviews, inclusive of key representatives of project stake holders. All progress reports, including project documents will be shared with the local authority and partners on regular basis. We will also ensure that community leaders, especially women leaders are involved in the identification and selection of project beneficiaries. We will ensure that best practices and research reports are shared with partners and posted on our webpage. We shall continue to fill in monthly information on kobo tool to provide GBV sub cluster with new trends of GBV.
