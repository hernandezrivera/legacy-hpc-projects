Project Summary

Aligned to the WASH cluster strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and in line with the framework of the OPT country 2023 HRP strategy under strategic objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”, and strategic objective 3: “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome the protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”, this project contributes to strengthening the capacity of essential WASH services for the vulnerable population threatened by the Israeli restrictions in Area C, West Bank.
The project-specific objective is to provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for Deir Samit and Khallet Edar (Hebron) and Al Walaja (Bethlehem) vulnerable villages located in area C, West Bank to improve access to WASH services that impacted women, children, girls, and boys. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and the WASH cluster and Oxfam's recent assessments.
The project will be implemented in partnership with Applied Research Institute - Jerusalem (ARIJ), the Economic and Social Development Center of Palestine (ESDC), and An-Najah National University. All partners have a solid background in the relevant interventions and will add value to the project thanks to their expertise and strong relations with the targeted communities.
All project activities will be closely coordinated and implemented in consultation with and approval of relevant stakeholders including PWA, and the WASH cluster.
The project will result in establishing, installing, and or rehabilitating water and sewer networks, in addition to producing 3 public health awareness-raising campaigns, and 2 advocacy products, and will pilot a new technique in the desalination treatment system. By its completion, the project is expected to serve 3756 individuals including (902 girls, 944 boys, 938 women, 972 men, 124 elderly, and 62 disabled).

1 - Needs

In the West Bank, the restrictive planning system enforced by the Israeli authorities in Area C and East Jerusalem prevents the development, and even routine repairs and maintenance of water structures in OPT, leaving the West Bank with inadequate WASH infrastructure (wells, cisterns, pipelines, etc.). According to MSNA 2022, nearly all HHs in the West Bank (96.3%) reported employing some kind of coping mechanism in order to adapt to a lack of sufficient water for drinking or domestic purposes, with 66.7% of these HHs reporting having reduced water consumption and 36.3% reporting increased spending on the water by diverting household expenditure normally intended for other purposes. Moreover, almost two-thirds of HHs (60.4%) were reportedly using a covered cesspit/septic tank to dispose of latrine waste, with only one-third (33.1%) of HHs reporting sewer connections as means of latrine waste disposal. According to the WASH cluster vulnerability map updated in 2022, more than 42,085 HHs (7%) in the West Bank reside in areas that are not connected to piped water services or are poorly supplied; where 62% of the HHs consume less than 50 liters of water per capita per day which is half the 100 liters recommended by WHO. This has undermined the hygiene practices of those families and the living conditions of families depending on herding and agricultural livelihoods, primarily in Area C. Regarding access to sanitation services, nearly 367,109 HHs (60%) in the West Bank are not connected to any sewer system, and almost 290,259 HHs in 615 communities are discharging their sewage in adjacent wadies/open areas and/or agricultural areas, creating health and environmental hazards to downstream communities. Based on the needs highlighted above, Oxfam's WASH strategy 2021-2025, WASH cluster Vulnerability Map updated in August 2022, and coordination efforts with PWA, the WASH cluster, Action Against Hunger (ACF) as Bethlehem and Hebron Governorates areas focal point, and Oxfam's local partner ESDC and ARIJ, it was agreed to prioritize Deir Samit and Khallet Edar (Hebron) and Al Walaja (Bethlehem) vulnerable villages under this plan. Accordingly, a rapid assessment was conducted by Oxfam and its local partners ESDC and ARIJ during the last two weeks of September 2022 to identify and validate the specific needs of the three villages, where three FGDs and three KII were carried out in the targeted villages with the participation of 23 persons (9 women and 14 men). The assessment revealed that 42% (600 HHs) in Deir Samit and 39% (250 HHs) in Al Walaja are not connected to the water network. Besides, the average water consumption is less than 30 L/c/d, and the water price is 18 ILS/m3. In Khallet Edar, 87% (500 HHs) of people depend on cesspits due to the lack of sewage networks as the installation of such networks is faced with great difficulties and restrictions by the occupation and the land topography. The financial burden for cesspits’ vacuuming (150 to 200 NIS/10m3). Moreover, participants confirmed that most of the current absorption pits are opened, detartrated, and constructed randomly without considering any health or safety concerns. The assessment also found that the majority of the population has poor hygiene and safety practices which increase the risk of waterborne diseases. Moreover, the assessment indicated that women and PWDs are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting serious concerns about their children's hygiene, health, and safety, as well as PWDs' mobility concerns. WASH needs were prioritized to improve adequate water and sanitation services, including proper sewage disposal and water network through sustainable, environmental, and low-cost solutions addressing the targeted community's current needs. All project activities will be designed and implemented taking into consideration the needs of targeted vulnerable groups.

2 - Activities

The construction of transmission pipelines and extension of water networks and HH connections planned to be implemented will improve access of targeted beneficiaries to a sufficient quantity of water needed for cooking, and personal and domestic hygiene and will alleviate the burden on women & children. Furthermore, the construction of the cesspits will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of waterborne diseases. The complementary hygiene promotion & public awareness campaigns will enhance gender & age responsiveness by enhancing women's knowledge & awareness. While the development of the policy brief and the animation video will contribute to promoting access to safe and adequate WASH services with dignity in Area C. Moreover, the piloting of a new technique to improve access to water services will contribute to strengthening WASH preparedness and response capacity to cope with shocks. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. Explicit plans to reduce risks and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e., discrimination and exploitation, and child protection), will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, and refreshed if the context changes. Oxfam will be responsible for implementing the activities in partnership with ESDC, ARIJ and collaborating with the relevant stakeholders, including PWA, WASH cluster, An-Najah National University, and municipal villages councils, for ensuring access to systems, procedures, and standards to achieve effective, high-quality delivery. Oxfam's particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, and end-users through it is active participation in the WASH cluster, SAG working group, and engagement with related stakeholders, ensuring delivery of the action in accordance with the agreed-upon project action plan and budget in compliance to the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial and procurement guidelines. Targeting relevant stakeholders at national and international levels; Oxfam will conduct and disseminate 2 case studies about 2 different communities highlighting the severity of the Israeli restrictions on Area C, specifically on the access to natural resources and its implications on accessing basic humanitarian needs and their humanitarian situation. Oxfam will also produce a brief animation video to showcase the current WASH situation in Area C with the lack of access to natural resources and demolition threats of WASH structures. Part of the animation will reflect what potential Area C holds regarding the WASH sector and how accessing natural resources will empower residents of Area C to utilize those resources to better their humanitarian situation.It is a direct impact on access to WASH services in Area C. In addition, Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection-sensitive project activities and gender mainstreaming. Oxfam will also support the ESDC and ARIJ in designing and implementing the gender and hygiene promotion plan. ESDC and ARIJ will lead the implementation of the community mobilization actions, and the technical engineering assessment and announce the tenders in the local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam, and their organization's procurement regulations and guidelines. ESDC and ARIJ work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations, including farmers, women, children & youth. They have extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable & safe water, and sanitation services in the West Bank. ESDC and ARIJ were fully engaged in the project's design, needs assessment, & community consultations and will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities & stakeholder engagement. ESDC and ARIJ will be engaged in the implementation of outputs 1.1 and 2.1. ARIJ will lead the implementation in Al Walaja, while ESDC in Deir Samit. Output 3.1 will be implemented directly by Oxfam considering its extensive experience in policy and advocacy, while, output 4.1 will be implemented in partnership with An-Najah National University. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly important in the West Bank context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, and refreshed if the context changes.Oxfam is committed to following an inclusive approach in the design, implementation, and monitoring of assistance to respond to vulnerability in all its forms and reach all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups, and others who are marginalized due to their social status). This entails observing and working towards: Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and accounted for in all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery, and monitoring and evaluation. Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified. The action responds to Oxfam's and PWA's needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilization sessions on gender, water, and sanitation will be implemented, encouraging the participation of men and women community leaders. These sessions will also serve as an awareness-raising tool to sensitize households and the community to accept women's participation. This will contribute to mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam's gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action's response is grounded on the fact that all women's and men's lives and dignity, including people with disabilities, youth, children, and the elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at the community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful humanitarian situations. Supporting women's self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved well-being of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam's humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieving equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the intervention's vulnerability and need. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities, and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age, or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people's concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles to access, whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention, and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality.
