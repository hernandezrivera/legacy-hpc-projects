Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
MC will work on improving access to basic Water and sanitation services through the delivery of water trucking, SWM, Desludging and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure in IDP locations and areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees, and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ, Dana, Daret Azza and Atareb in Idleb and north Aleppo). In NW, MC will continue providing a full WASH package camps and IDP sites, which includes Cholera related interventions, Water Quality monitoring and treatment and hygiene education. Hygiene education, using carefully tailored messages, and the distribution of hygiene and COVID kits will complement the other WASH interventions. Through ongoing Coordination with WASH cluster, working groups, local councils and other agencies, MC will be coordinating its response in relation to the gaps identified and associated needs. In 2023, a special focus will be given by MC to seek solutions for operation and maintenance (O&M), through support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of water supply rehabilitation. MC will work actively to identify and implement water trucking sustainable solutions and cost efficient sanitation management in the targeted IDP locations. Internal water supply networks, water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return. MC will also distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to conflict-affected households based on regional SMEB values, which include hygiene items.

3 - Needs

NES faces a triple water crisis. Only 20% of Key Informants of a recent REACH survey in 736 communities reported access to the water network seven days a week. Without safe water access, millions of people risk losing access to electricity. Waterborne illnesses such as Cholera are on the rise. This disproportionately impacts low-income women and girls; Hasakeh governorate has severe water scarcity and they are facing added challenges in menstrual hygiene management, which anticipates maternal morbidity and mortality and child malnutrition. The 2023 HNO reported 52% of Syrians rely on unsafe water supply modalities (36% in 2022), with rates particularly high in Hasakeh, Raqqa, and Deir ez Zor. In NWS, especially northern Aleppo, aerial attacks and shelling have damaged water and sanitation infrastructure which had already been damaged by war (MSNA by MC partner/IYD) and a lack of regular maintenance/technical staff. Regional drought has reduced supply to the Euphrates River, the main source of water. Fuel and electricity prices going up and the displaced population further exacerbate access issues. In both formal and informal camps there is a huge need to increase the water supply and manage drinking water safety to ensure the displaced people stay away from potential diseases such as cholera which was declared an outbreak in September 2022. 4 - Implementation of activities

(1) Repair & rehabilitation of water systems: Identify critical service gaps and needs for rehab of water stations and networks in both NW and NES. (2) Support to water quality assurance and operation & maintenance of water supply systems: Provide capacity development training around climate adaptation and O&M support for municipal water service team through provision of monthly maintenance costs and necessary materials and equipment. (3) Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems: Provide garbage removal services and provide communal bins in informal settlements, camps and urban settings. (4) Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion incl. Cholera-related messages: Conduct hygiene promotion activities and distribute hygiene kits. Conduct a KAP survey to identify gaps. (5) Improved access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services: WASH package including water trucking, Sanitation services, and SWM. Water quality analysis. Connecting IDP camps and unconnected host communities to a sustainable water source (pipe lines), and installing cost efficient septic tanks will decrease the money going to water trucking and desludging services. SWM for IDPs in camps to reuse and recycle their SW, linked with markets to sell materials, thus is also decreasing the costs of solid waste collection.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps mainstreams protection, gender and inclusion into all WASH programming, and ensures participants’ safety and dignity, equal access, and meaningful participation, and plans to the following activities: Prior to implementation, a protection, disability and gender mainstreaming and conflict sensitive refresher training for staff and partners; a WASH specific protection risk analysis (PRA) at the start of the project, which will be reviewed during implementation; community awareness sessions on rights and entitlements, information on available services, selection criteria, complaints and feedback mechanisms and Mercy Corps’ Code of Conduct prior to the implementation of field activities; and data collected in WASH programming disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Main risks and mitigation measures are: 1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Safety and security of program participants and staff could be negatively affected due to the ongoing conflict. To mitigate this risk, Mercy Corps will conduct ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of program activities, ensuring such decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. Social and cultural norms of targeted communities will be observed during all phases of the program implementation to ensure safety risks associated with real or perceived violation of norms are mitigated. Project staff will complete a mandatory safety and security training; and participants will be sensitized on issues of safety and security so that they are able to recognize threats to their own safety and security and take action to protect themselves. Implementation could be delayed due to COVID 19, security, cholera, or other issues - occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation.
