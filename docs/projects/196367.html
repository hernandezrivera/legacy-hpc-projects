Project Summary

IYD aims to continue providing GBV services in 2 WGSSs, one in Idlib city and one in Sarmada city, and through 4 outreach teams covering Idelb, Marret Tamasrin, Dana, Salqin, Armanaz, and Harim sub-districts, Idleb governorate.
GBV services include prevention activities: awareness-raising, life skills incl. Structured PSS using "My Safety My Wellbeing" program/IRC, WGSS free activities, empowerment/vocational training, and small grants, AMAL initiatives, capacity building for community actors, and emergency response to the new IDPs by providing PFA, referral, sharing information, etc. GBV Response activities include case management linked with case fund and target women and girls inside WGSSs and males inside IYD’s field offices that are closed to the WGSSs in Idleb and Sarmada cities, where a safe space for case management intervention is ensured considering the privacy and confidentiality.
To support women's participation and engagement, IYD will identify 3 WLOs to be supported with need-based capacity development at the admin & program/GBV aspects. Continuous follow-up and consultation will be offered to those WLOs throughout the implementation period.
GBV team in each WGSS consists of 1/Supervisor, 1/Male CM, 2/Female CM, 2 male outreach workers, 3/Female outreach workers, and 1/GBV M&E assistant. One midwife will be contracted to support the implementation of AMAL initiative in each WGSS.
IYD will follow the GBV SC guidelines and SOPs, including GBV SOPs, GBV CM SOPs, CVA & GBV SOPs, Awareness-raising and GBV M&E toolkit, ethical closure guidelines, and the IGA/small-grants within GBV programming SOPs.
We propose to target a total of 4,732 (M: 417, W: 2145, B: 575, G:1595), including 400 PwDs,
The GBV M&E assistant will be trained on GBV M&E toolkit to support the M&E activities for the proposed interventions under the supervision of the GBV technical team.
Effective coordination at Turkey level (GBV SC, SRH TWG, GBV partners, etc) and Syria level will be followed at all project stages to ensure the safe and accountable implementation and the engagement of relevant stakeholders: partners, local councils, community actors, etc.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, needs in NWs are increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. Approximately 2.8 million IDPs are living in 1,389 camps or informal sites in NWs.Women and children represent 80% of this caseload, widowed or divorced women and girls reported Forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children in some cases, higher levels of sexual harassment and exploitation in relation to obtaining shelter/housing, while the older women said that they were exposed to neglect by family members, perceived as an economic burden.Ongoing insecurity and a deepening economic crisis all converged to further exacerbate the risks of GBV for women and girls, who reported higher levels of intimate partner violence. As per WOS GBV AoR 2022 Advocacy Brief. These forms of violence are eroding the resilience of women and girls and can cause severe physical and mental health consequences, including unwanted pregnancies, permanent disabilities, and increased levels of psychological distress, which can sometimes result in suicide. Social stigmatization continues to affect GBV survivors, including women and girls who survive detention and marginalized women and girls more broadly. Further, women and girls are also exposed to deprivation of financial resources, right to education, work, inheritance, and travel. They are also exposed to early marriage and rape, which mostly happens in camps, given the poor security conditions there.

4 - Implementation of activities

The 2 WGSSs will be established in safe and accessible places, 1/Idlib, 1/Sarmada considering it fits the needs of PwDs and elderly. Field team will be hired in each WGSS as follows: 1/Supervisor, 1/Male CM, 2/Female CM, 2/male outreach workers, 3/Female outreach workers, 1/GBV M&E assistant, 1/contracted midwife to support the implementation of AMAL initiative. Activities include: 1) Case management linked with case fund and target women and girls inside WGSSs and males inside IYD’s field offices that are close to the WGSSs in Idleb and Sarmada cities, where a safe space for case management intervention is ensured considering the privacy and confidentiality. 2) awareness-raising targeting MWBG and using ARToolkit and information-sharing sessions. 3) life skills structured PSS "My Safety My Wellbeing" program/IRC. 4) WGSS free activities, empowerment/vocational training based on BNFs preferences, and small grants for those in need of livelihood opportunities in mitigating GBV risks., and emergency response to the new IDPs by providing PFA, referral, sharing information, etc.5) AMAL initiatives, with the support of midwives, to conduct: YMC, health workers sessions, and community actors dialogue sessions. 6) WLOs: We’ll identify 3 WLOs to be supported with need-based capacity development at admin & program/GBV aspects, continuous follow-up, and consultation7) Non-GBV HWs training on GBV risk mitigation8) GBV actors training on GBV. Target BNFs: 4,732 (F:3740, M:992).

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The PSEA Standards will be adopted by the IYD team by taking an active role in PSEA efforts by monitoring and considering SEA risks that may be present in our field activities and how those can be effectively responded, mitigated, and prevented, in addition to ensuring that PSEA mainstreaming issues will be considered during the program implementing process through the procedures explained below:• IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct Which condemns the SEA ( Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of IYD as well as the associated contractors, and volunteers.• IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool, where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA).• IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct.• Co


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Assumption: Cross-border operations are still in effect.Risk: Borders are closed due to not extending the cross-border resolution.Mitigation: IYD will always plan ahead and rely on internal resources until restrictions get lifted.2. Assumption: The current situation is stabilized and has no adverse impact on the project's activities.Risk: The current Colyra situation will have a significant adverse impact on the project, including increased restrictions on movement.Mitigation: IYD will conduct outreach activities through mobile units to, particularly most vulnerable groups. In addition to adopting the standard WHO Colyra preventive measures.3. Assumption: GBV survivors will have safe and easy access to our GBV specialized services.Risk: Stigma toward the GBV survivors.Mitigation: The case management room is located within WGSSs, easy to reach by Survivors, and enjoys privacy.4. Assumption: Affected populations including PwD, will contribute to the successful implementation of the project activities throughout the project lifecycle.Risk: Affected populations will not be included in one or more levels of the project.Mitigation: IYD will include them through the need assessments, complaint mechanisms, beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys, and focus on following the received complaints and suggestions. Also, PwD will be provided with the needed transportation and equipment to allow them to access our available services.
