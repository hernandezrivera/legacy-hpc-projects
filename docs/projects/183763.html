Project Summary

WFP finds that approximately 10 per cent of surveyed households had inadequate food consumption, meaning they have an unstable diet that regularly lacks key food groups. Households in the east and south of the country faced significantly higher rates, standing at 32 per cent in Tobruk, 23 per cent in Murzuk, and 15 per cent in Alkufra. Over 70 per cent of women aged 15 to 49 years old consumed minimum dietary diversity, while most children aged 6 to 23 months did not consume a minimum acceptable diet.
WFP will provide unconditional life-saving food assistance to crisis-affected vulnerable Libyan IDPs, returnees and non-displaced populations in the East, West, and South of Libya based on the assessed vulnerability. The best transfer modality, either in-kind or electric-voucher, will be selected to deliver the food entitlements to the targeted households through WFP's local partners and/or the contracted service providers.
Targeting is implemented geographically, mainly based on the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment findings and household demographic characteristics such as gender, age group, and the presence of women, men, boys and girls living with disabilities as well as Household Food Consumption Scores. Further prioritization exercises are also applied to the project based on the duration of displacements, the assistance provided by local authorities or other food security sector members, and accessibility. Beneficiaries themselves, as members of local crisis committees, are involved throughout the project planning and the food distribution process.
WFP will put its programmatic quality control and assurance measures in place, which include the following: I) training of its Cooperating Partners and its own staff in cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, PSEA, Protection and Conflict Sensitivity; ii) WFP’s output monitoring through the verification of distribution reports and spot-checks and WFP’s outcome monitoring through annual post-distribution monitoring; iii) WFP’s distributions of leaflets and visibility materials to raise the awareness of participants and non-participants about the Common Feedback Mechanism.
