Project Summary

Building on REACH's multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA) to inform the 2019 HNO and HRPs with funding from various donors including ECHO, OFDA and multiple UN agencies, REACH proposes to conduct a country-wide MSNA for 2019 on core humanitarian sectors, including protection, food, health, wash, shelter and NFI, education and cash and access to markets . In order to capture detailed information that better reflects the realities and needs of the population and will enable better targeting by the humanitarian community, the MSNA will be conducted at a household-level across different population groups (IDPs, returnees, and host communities), with supplementary key informant interviews and focus group discussions to triangulate and contextualise information.

Needs

While REACH and other information-based actors have made important strides to provide concrete evidence during crises to develop efficient and adequate programming to address the needs and vulnerabilities of affected populations, the situation in Libya is extremely fluid. To ensure that programming quickly responds to the rapidly changing needs and environment on the ground, it is thus crucial that timely and up to date information is maintained and reinforced. Data available in Libya beyond the REACH MSNA on multi-sectoral needs of population groups often relies on key informant rather than household-level surveys, can be ad hoc, piecemeal, or fail to achieve the geographic and thematic coverage necessary for comparison and prioritization of scarce resources. An update to the MSNA will equip humanitarian actors with the evidence needed to best respond to emerging needs and changing geographic priorities in 2019, specifically feeding the 2020 HNO and HRP.

Activities

Building on its experience conducting Multi-Sector Needs Assessments (MSNAs) in Libya, REACH, on behalf of the Humanitarian Country Team, the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), and the Information Management and Assessment Working Group (IMAWG) proposes to conduct MSNAs in Libya on a yearly basis to continually inform and update humanitarian actors’ understanding of the needs that exist in the country, ensuring strong linkages and coordination with the HCT and the HNO process, as well as providing trends analysis and updates on key sector priorities. The 2019 MSNA aims to assess three groups of Libyans: IDPs, returnees, and the non-displaced population. For all assessed mantikas and population groups, the 2019 MSNA will employ representative sampling methods to ensure that the results can be generalised to the full mantika or population group in question. REACH will use IOM DTM figures to draw its sampling frame for IDPs and returnees, and Libya Bureau of statistics figures for non-displaced populations.
