Project Summary

Building on GC’s successful OFDA and FFP-funded Syria programs, the Investing in Nutrition in Syria, Promoting Infrastructure and Resources in the Economy (INSPIRE) program will deliver an integrated package of assistance to meet emergency food needs of the most vulnerable, and support HH and community food security in north Syria. INSPIRE will: 1) meet the immediate food needs of vulnerable individuals through provision of RTERs and monthly food baskets; 2) increase food security for vulnerable individuals through provision of kitchen garden kits, livestock, fodder, mobile fodder mill, and food production and storage; 3) enhance community food security for people through improved wheat storage and fire control; and 4) support bread production at bakeries through distribution of wheat, flour, and rehabilitation of bakeries and mills. In total, INSPIRE will support 83,225 vulnerable Syrians in NWS.
INSPIRE will: 1) meet the immediate food needs of 2,500 vulnerable HH/ 12,500 individuals through provision of RTERs and 3,125 HH / 15,625 individuals through provision of monthly food baskets; 2) increase food security for 1,200 HH / 7,200 individuals through provision of kitchen garden kits. and 3) support bread production at 5 bakeries serving an estimated 50,000 individuals through distribution of wheat, flour, yeast, and rehabilitation of bakeries and mills. In total, INSPIRE will support 85,325 vulnerable Syrians.

4 - Needs

With the Syrian conflict nearing its eighth year, 6.5 million people are food insecure, and 9 million are in need of food security assistance. Fighting has damaged agricultural lands and food production infrastructure, causing a significant drop in access to food, an increase in prices of agricultural inputs, and a reduction in livelihoods opportunities. The number of people at risk of food insecurity has remained between 8.7 and 10.5 million since 2017. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), nearly half of Syrians spend an estimated 65% of their income on food, and 65% of food insecure households (HH) have adopted negative coping mechanisms related to food consumption. In northwest Syria (NWS), ongoing clashes continue to displace vulnerable HH, and there is a large need for bakery interventions in Turkish-controlled Euphrates Shield (ES) areas in NWS.The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Cluster estimates that 31% of the total population in Syria is acutely food insecure, and the 2020 HNO reports that a further 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity.As noted by Syrian NGO Shafak in their FSL assessment of Aleppo and Idleb, host communities have been plagued with high unemployment and inflation rates making it difficult to purchase food; many people sell assets, go into debt and resort to risky forms of labor as coping mechanisms. As of February 2019, there is an average coping prevalence rate of 82.64 for Aleppo, Idleb, and Hama with an average of 74.8% using less expensive foods, 56.5% reducing meals, 56.59% restricting consumption of adults, 56.80% limiting portion size, and 57.57% receiving help from family or friends. This is a significant increase from twelve months ago.

5 - Implementation of activities

From our office in Gaziantep, GC will be responsible for overall program strategy and design, reporting, grant administration and compliance, procurement, vendor and sub-award payments, monitoring and evaluation (M&E) and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC), and capacity building of staff. Program staff will oversee implementation which will performed by Shafak. Our Syrian staff will enter Syria as appropriate to monitor program implementation. GC will implement INSPIRE activities directly, with the exception of RTERs, which will be distributed by our partner Shafak. For all the activities, we will conduct community vulnerability and technical assessments using GC’s established systems, GC and our local partner will also actively liaise with communities, vendors, and beneficiaries and will identify beneficiaries through an open and transparent process. Similarly, all procurement is conducted in an open, competitive, and transparent manner. This ongoing communication and transparency ensures that communities understand what the program activities are, what the relevant selection criteria are, how to apply for program contracts and/or assistance, and who is providing the assistance.Under GC supervision, the partner will procure RTER items and manage the supply chain and distribution of RTERs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

onsistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines in implementing activities. The Program is based on the "Do No Harm" principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. In settings where humanitarian needs are extreme and resources are scarce, GC recognizes that providing any material assistance has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns, or to negatively impact local economic conditions. We will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from program resources by maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation; and promoting open community engagement and transparency in beneficiary selection criteria and processes, and materials distribution and assistance. GC incorporates beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanisms into program design. Our existing mechanism includes providing beneficiaries with feedback forms and contact numbers when assistance is distributed. We publicly post our anonymous feedback hotline number at public and program locations (such as child-friendly spaces and women and girls safe spaces) in target communities and in Atmeh, so that community members may send feedback to us by phone, SMS or WhatsApp. We provide the hotline number to individual HHs during beneficiary verification, assistance distribution, and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) visits to each HH, to ensure that the most vulnerable or disadvantaged have access to the information and the ability to submit his/ her own feedback.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

GC actively works to identify and mitigate potential risks at throughout all phases of program design and activity implementation. The Bab al Hawa and Bab al Salama border crossings between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are opened and accessible; • GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations; • Active violence does not directly impact GC's offices in Gaziantep, or Qah for a sustained period of time; • Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for deliveries of humanitarian aid, and allowing GC to maintain acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality; • Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance; and • GC is able to obtain appropriate export/re-export license renewals in time to allow for program implementation according to the approved work plan.
