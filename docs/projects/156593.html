Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at improving the food security of vulnerable Syrian households, in both the short and the medium-long term.
In the short-term, ACTED will reduce the food security of conflict-affected communities through the provision of rapid and flexible food assistance. This will include the following 3 modalities: 1) RTERS for vulnerable beneficiaries who do not have access to cooking facilities; 2) Food rations for households living in areas where markets are inaccessible or food is unavailable; 3) Food vouchers for families with access to functioning markets.
In the medium and long-term, ACTED food security assistance will be underpinned by the rationale of giving vulnerable households the resources and skills to obtain a self-sufficient food production. In order to achieve this, ACTED will provide targeted support to households, depending on their needs and size of land. The type of support ACTED will provide to vulnerable households will include: 1) kitchen garden kits and training; 2) polytunnel kits and training; 3) poultry kits. Overall, this project will benefit approximately 72,900 individuals.
ACTED is coordinating with Food Security cluster members and other NGOs supporting the food security and agricultural sector to ensure that there is no duplication of assistance planned in 2019.
While implementing its food security programming, ACTED always respects the nutrition standards of the Food Security cluster. Additionally, while targeting different locations for its programming under Food Security, ACTED also ensures that its interventions are complementary to projects under other sectors to comprehensively respond to needs in the target area. Indeed, for instance, in emergency context and whenever relevant ACTED will complement emergency food distribution with NFI distribution. In turn, ACTED often implements agricultural early-recovery activities such as the rehabilitation of agricultural assets or irrigation canals used for agricultural purposes to support further the agricultural production and availability of food products on the market.
The activities and targets proposed for this intervention reflect the current activities and targets for which ACTED has secured funding for 2019.

Needs

In 2019, food insecurity remains high in Northeast Syria. The food security severity ranking qualified as major in 2 out of 3 districts in Ar-Raqqa (Ar-Raqqa and Ath-Thawrah), 2 out of 4 districts in Al-Hasakeh (Al-Hasakeh and Qamishli) and severe in 2 out of 3 districts in Deir Ez-Zor. Out of the 671,386 people living in Ar-Raqqa in August 2018, 257,968 were food insecure, and 107,054 were considered at risk. In Al-Hasakeh governorate, 374,744 individuals out of a population of 1,010,783 (as of August 2018) were considered to be food insecure, and another 373,125 were at risk. In Deir Ez-Zor governorate, over half of the population in August 2018 (412,125 out of 689, 610) were considered to be food insecure (HNO PIN Severity 2019). Insecurity, lack of financial and physical access to food and depletion of livelihoods are the main reasons for food insecurity. While all Syrians have been affected to some extent by the crisis, internally displaced people (IDPs) and overburdened host communities have been particularly impacted (Food Security Situation in Syria, Expanded version of the Food Security sector HNO 2018). As of June 2018, the livelihood and agricultural assistance provided by partners had reached only 35% of people in need, while the food security assistance had reached the 75% of people in need (Humanitarian Response Plan Monitoring Report, January to June 2018). A Whole of Syria assessment coordinated by the Food Security Cluster found that 33.4% IDPs and 41.2% of returnees are food insecure. 29% of returnees adopt emergency coping strategies, compared to 18.7% reported across all groups at national level. Moreover, an estimated 40% of Syrians spend more than 65% of their expenditure on food, 57.2% of food insecure households have outstanding debt and 65% of food insecure people adopt negative coping strategies. With an 83 - 89% people living below poverty lines, and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80% of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80% of a public service employee’s monthly salary. Finally, unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies (Food security sector narrative – HNO version). The agricultural sector in Northeast Syria has been particularly affected by the crisis. Households living in rural areas depend on agriculture as their main livelihood. Over 75% of rural households grow food for their own consumption and more than 1/3 of the rural households rely on their own production for over a 1/4 of their food requirements. However, due to higher production and marketing costs, and constrained purchasing power, 90% of households spend half of their income on food, against 25% pre-crisis. Prior to the crisis 60% of households had access to finance from any source, this is now 25% (Agriculture in Syria after 6 years of crisis, FAO, 2017). An assessment conducted by REACH Initiative in April 2018 in Deir Ez-Zor governorate has revealed that across most areas, markets have slowly reopened and are now functioning; most people rely on their own production to access food, and to a minor extent, on market purchases or food distributions.

Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at improving the food security of Syrian vulnerable households through a multi-layered approach: Emergency food security assistance to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable households through the most appropriate modalities.ACTED will address the acute needs of food insecure, low income households, who have not received any or enough food assistance in the previous month. The specific modality (in-kind or voucher) will be decided based on results of market assessments. Emergency food security assistance will include:• Distribution of one-off Ready to Eat Food Ratios (RTERs) to cover the needs of a 6-members’ households for one month (for households who do not have access to cooking facilities).• Distribution of in-kind food rations for three continuous months (for households who do not have access to market).• Distribution of food commodity vouchers for three months, where beneficiaries have access to markets. The content of the RTERs will align with the Food Security Cluster recommendations (minimum 2100 kcal per person per day with cooked meals and/or bread with RTERs designed for a household of 5). When assessing modality relevance, ACTED will focus on the evaluation of the FCS and CSI indicators, while also assessing the capacity of access to the market for beneficiaries, the availability of goods and their quality to ensure the potential relevance of a voucher modality. Following the results of these three components, ACTED will determine the highest needs and best modality to respond to them, utilising a methodology from previous projects with proven good results. Support to small-scale food production for vulnerable households ACTED will provide support to vulnerable households for whom farming is not a full-time profession, but who own a small piece of land suitable for cultivating vegetables. The assistance provided will aim at increasing their food security while encouraging them to start a self-sufficient food production. • Distribution of in-kind home gardens kits (one per each agricultural season) to vulnerable, low income households who have access to agricultural land or a backyard garden of a minimum of 20 square meters with access to water for irrigation. Each kit will contain seeds, seedlings and gardening tools. Beneficiaries will also take part in training on kitchen gardening techniques, climate-smart agriculture, planting, nutrition practices.• Distribution of in-kind Polytunnel kits to small farmers or vulnerable households. Each kit will include vegetable seeds, a drip irrigation kit and a water tank. As the tunnels allow for early and late planting of crops, households will be able to harvest and sell their produce at non-seasonal times when the supply is lower and market price higher. Beneficiaries will participate in practical trainings on Polytunnels techniques. This activity aims at ensuring the autonomy of beneficiaries in meeting their food needs but also encourages income generating activities as beneficiaries can sell the production surplus.• Distribution of Poultry kits (including livestock, animal feed and shelter materials). ACTED will ensure that synergies are drawn with other actors and across sectors (including within its own programming) to ensure a comprehensive response at area level.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Perception of discrimination when an activity targets a specific group of people, resulting in the exclusion of othersLikelihood: Medium – Impact: High Mitigation measures: All selection criteria are explained to the local authorities and local communities during sensitisation sessions, during which ACTED responds to any questions or concerns about the selection criteria and process throughout the project implementation. ACTED clearly communicates its humanitarian values and principle of neutrality and ensures that targeted communities have access to the Complaints and Response Mechanisms inside Syria (through Whatsapp and on-site feedback to ACTED staff). ACTED utilises specific SOPs for different complaints aiming to respond within 5 days maximum to each individual complaint. Positive relationships establishing with local stakeholders allow ACTED to identify any potential negative perception of the project implementation and beneficiary targeting and further reinforce communication on the latter. Risk: Low participation of women in the projectLikelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The proposed intervention has been designed and will be carried out in accordance with ACTED gender strategy. ACTED will ensure the dignity and safety of women is at respected and will strongly encourage the inclusion of women in the activities, facilitated by the fact that some activities can be done within the sphere of the home. Experience from other interventions has showed that while most beneficiaries registered for the distribution of kitchen garden are men, it is the women in their households who are then the ones working in the garden, planting and cultivating the seeds. Risk: Lack of meaning full access for the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The special needs of women and girls, elderly and people living with disabilities will be taken into account throughout the implementation of the activities, and in particular during the phase of beneficiary selection. ACTED will ensure that distribution and training sites are safe, easily accessible. During distribution, separate lines will be arranged for women. In addition, the particular necessities of pregnant and lactating women, female head of household, and elderly or disabled women will be taken into account, and should they be unable to reach the distribution sites, they will be able to obtain the kits by proxy. Risk: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measures: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue. All complaints receive a response within 5 days but the length to completely close the complaint will depend on its gravity and measures required (from explanations to the complainer to the launch of an investigation). ACTED has a strong PSEA policy which stresses the importance of prevention but also has a mechanism in place for reporting and investigation.
