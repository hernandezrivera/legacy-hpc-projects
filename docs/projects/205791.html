Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to protect the livelihoods of rural and urban populations, as well as returnees and improve food security through the provision of livelihood support during the main planting and dry season (crop and vegetable seeds, tools, fishing kits, livestock vaccines and drugs), strengthen absorption capacity and build resilience at the household and community level, and support food security information, analysis and coordination.

1. Needs

After twelve years of independence, South Sudan continues to face ongoing and violent insecurity on a local and subnational level. There has been a large displacement of people (estimated at about 360,000 in early November 2023) from Sudan into South Sudan, the majority of whom are highly vulnerable and likely to exert pressure on resources in their areas of re-settlement. All of this is compounded with climate-related issues such as flooding (2023 marks the fifth year in a row South Sudan has seen flooding) and drought that are contributing to further displacement, destruction of infrastructure, and disruptions to agricultural livelihoods. The insecurity, which has greatly increased since 2020, has driven families from their homes, effectively eliminating their housing, food, and financial security, leaving them highly vulnerable to shocks and stresses. Conflict continues to be a key driver of food insecurity in South Sudan. In the towns bordering Sudan, particularly those that heavily rely on Sudan for their market supplies, food prices and transportation costs have started to skyrocket, impacting the purchasing power of market-dependent households who are likely to experience higher levels of food scarcity and food insecurity. The most recent Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis, released November 2023 reports that there are currently 5.82 million people (46.2% of the population) experiencing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse), out of which 35,000 people are experiencing Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State, Rubkona (15,000) County of Unity State, and among the South Sudanese returnees (14,000) who fled the conflict in Sudan. Jonglei (61%) Unity (58%), Upper Nile (56%), and Lakes (53%) are among the most food insecure states in South Sudan, with over 50% of their population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. As of October 2023, inter-agency assessments, led by OCHA, have assessed 924,259 people that have been affected by flooding, with nearly 182,200 people displaced: 84,400 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, 65,300 in Unity State, 30,000 in Western Equatoria State, and 2,500 in Eastern Equatoria State (ACAPS, 2023). Looking ahead to the harvest/post-harvest season from December 2023 to March 2024, an estimated 5.78 million people (45.8% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 25,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, and 1.71 million likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The situation is expected to worsen during the lean season (April to July 2024), during which an estimated 7.10 million people (56.3% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This projection includes 79 000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, and 2.34 million people likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The people in Catastrophe during the lean season are in Pibor County (11,000) of Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Aweil East County (40,000) of Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, and among the South Sudanese returnees (28,000) who fled the conflict in Sudan. Furthermore, food prices have risen significantly in 2023, with the price of Sorghum increasing by 62% in Juba markets in October 2023 compared to the same time last year (FAO CLiMIS Portal). With the continued protracted crisis in South Sudan, livelihood opportunities have decreased, forcing many families to resort to negative coping mechanisms, including transactional marriages, sometimes with children, and child labour. Support to livelihoods and food security is vital to South Sudan’s future, and FAO will continue to work closely with humanitarian and development partners to assess shifting needs across the country, throughout 2024.

2. Project activities

• Distribute 650,000 crop kits (maize, sorghum, cowpea, ground nut, sesame and hand tools) supporting crop, production in the main and second agricultural seasons of 2024 through direct distribution and seed fairs to enhance the local seed sector.• Distribute 1,000,000 vegetable kits (5-7 types of vegetable seed and tool) supporting vegetable production in the main, second and dry agricultural seasons of 2024 • Distribute 500,000 fishing kits composed of hooks, monofilament and twines to increase intake of nutrient-rich food groups and enhance income.• Training of 65,000 farmers throughout the year where needed including nutritional support focus on topics such as good agricultural (crop, livestock, fishery) and post-harvest practices, as well as emerging threats such as Desert Locust and Fall Armyworm infestations and/or outbreaks of transboundary diseases. Strengthening households’ food production and productivity capacity through intensification, diversification and post-production practices is expected to result in more stable production and enhanced resilience. • Vaccinate 10,000,000 livestock against ten main diseases and parasites (e.g. anthrax, black quarter, CBPP, contagious caprine pleuropneumonia, foot rot, HS, LSD, PPR, newcastle disease, sheep and goat pox, and rabies)• Treat 1,000,000 livestock to reduce livestock mortality and increase animal production • Train 1000 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) to enhance network coverage, strengthen service provision, timely administration of vaccines, and on safe methods of carcass disposal • Distribute post-harvest equipment to benefit 50,000 households (including returnees) to support preservation and marketing capacities.• Distribute irrigation equipment to benefit 50,000 farming households to increase production coverage and enhance crop diversification.• Distribution of vegetables kits to support 1000 kitchen garden at Nutrition facilities/Schools/households.• Collection, coordination and analysis of food security information • Support crop assessment, seed security assessment • Develop crop watch bulletins • Support food security and livelihoods cluster coordination • Creation of market opportunities for input, outputs and services improved • Strengthen sustainable management of natural resources and local peace structures
