Provide targeted supplementary feeding for nutritional rehabilitation and blanket supplementary feeding for prevention of malnutrition among vulnerable children and pregnant and lactating women

Start Date

End Date

31/12/2022

Project Summary

WFP will support the treatment of children 6 to 59 months with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) who are wasted through the targeted supplementary feeding (TSF) programme. This intervention will be implemented across the most vulnerable regions, targeting those woredas with the greatest need for humanitarian support, identified in the severity level 3 and 4, based on the needs analysis conducted for this response plan. In addition to the TSF, WFP will also support the blanket supplementary feeding (BSF) programme, with priority to Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) locations, particularly in Northern Ethiopia Regions of Afar, Amhara and Tigray. The BSF will be implemented to support the prevention of wasting among vulnerable children 6 to 59 months and PLW in IDP locations in the Northern Ethiopia regions.
This humanitarian nutrition response by WFP will be implemented with the support of Government and NGO Cooperating Partners (CPs). Additionally, the nutrition intervention will be implemented as per the National Guidelines for Management of Acute Malnutrition. Children 6 to 59 months and PLW will be provided with Ready to Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and Super Cereal Plus respectively, both for treatment and preventive services, although with different rations for each as per National Guidelines.

1. - Projects activities

The WFP nutrition response involves a number of activities to ensure an intervention that delivers optimal nutritional rehabilitation for the malnourished children and PLW. Below are the key activities that WFP will undertake collaboratively with specific partners for relevant activities as outlined below. Procurement and Distribution of SNF: WFP will utilise existing mechanisms for the timely procurement, distribution and management of SNF all the way to the end user. This systems ensures timely delivery of commodity in a safe and healthy state, to ensure efficacy of the commodity is maintained. Nutrition Screening: The Regional Health Bureaus will lead the screening activities through their health extension workers (HEW). The HEWs assess the nutrition status of children and PLW at the community or service provision facility such as health posts or distribution centers in their jurisdictions. Those children identified with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) are referred for appropriate treatment in the health facility, supported by UNICEF, while those with MAM are admitted into the TSF programme supported by WFP. Treatment of MAM: WFP will provide nutrition treatment with support from Government and NGO Cooperating Partners (CPs) at health post, health center and distribution center level. This treatment will be done in accordance to the National IMAM Guidelines, which recommends treatment of children with MAM using RUSF and Super Cereal Plus for PLW. Each child will be provided with 3kg of RUSF per month for an estimated 3 months while 7.5kg Super Cereal Plus will be provided for PLW per month for an estimated treatment period of 6 months. Prevention of MAM: As part of the nutrition response, WFP will support the prevention of wasting through the BSF programme. This intervention will be implemented in IDP locations of Northern Ethiopia regions, where needs and the risk of deteriorating nutrition situation are high due to the conflict. WFP will target all children 6 to 59 months and PLW in IDP locations targeted under this response. Monitoring: In all the regions supported through this Austria contribution, WFP has established Sub Offices, and staff who will conduct regular monitoring and support to the programme. Additionally, WFP through the Third Party Monitoring (TPM) conducts on-site monitoring of activities, culminating in a monthly Dashboard which summarises key programme performance at the implementation site. Coordination: The nutrition response by WFP is part of a wider nutrition response led by the Nutrition Cluster. The Nutrition Cluster convenes all partners including Government, Donors, UN Agencies and NGOs through bi-weekly meetings, to discuss wide-ranging issues including Planning, Programme Review, Resource Mobilisation among others. WFP also convenes a specific TSF Coordination on a monthly basis with all TSF partners, where specific issues are discussed. Complementary Activities: At all TSF centers, WFP provides key messages on infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-e), health and hygiene as well as messages on COVID-19 mitigation measures.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The Nutrition Cluster is a mechanism where all humanitarian nutrition partners at national and regional levels interact. The cluster provides a platform where all partners jointly define the needs, plan response, implement and monitoring the response. Additionally, the cluster provides overall leadership, in matters related to advocacy and resource mobilisation, and facilitates a coordinated response to ensure efficiency and avoid duplication of duties. WFP is a key player in the Nutrition Cluster at both national and regional levels and will continue to contribute to all processes and ensure the WFP response is accountable to the overall nutrition response. TSF Coordination. In addition of the Nutrition Cluster, WFP and its partners (NGOs and Donors) have a monthly coordination mechanism at Federal level, looking at TSF programme. Specifically, the TSF coordination focuses on topical issues of the day, such as recent development affecting humanitarian needs which may require response by WFP and its partners. Additionally, the TSF coordination also focuses on reviewing the ongoing interventions and related lessons and challenges, discussions on possible solutions as well as being a platform for providing updates to partners, for example, on resource situation. The TSF Coordination is currently being done at Federal level and regional levels, and will continue to be utilised as the main platform where the interventions related to this project will be shared and discussed in more detail. WFP will also establish formal partnerships (MoUs) through Government departments, namely the RHB and Disaster Risk Management Commission (DRMC) to support the implementation of activities under this project. In addition, NGOs will also be engaged through Field Level Agreements (FLAs), especially to support the new woredas where WFP had not been operating prior to the drought. Overall coordination between Agencies and other partners will however be done through the cluster mechanism.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

WFP has a well-established Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) channels which include temporary helpdesks and community outreach agents. The cases will be incorporated in the WFP database (SugarCRM) which ensures escalation in case of priority cases, analysis, tracking, reporting and closure of cases. The CFM standard operating procedure (SOP) has been established to ensure the right level of escalation is applied, depending on the content of the complaints, and actions are taken immediately for those cases with high priority. The temporary helpdesks are established at final distribution points to receive real-time feedback, respond to record and report on matters on site. WFP is a member of the complaints committee at woreda and kebele levels and coordinates and follows up timely registration and delivery of services at the community level. Community outreach agents are selected from among the beneficiaries and are trained to provide support to other beneficiaries as ‘mobile helpdesks’. The purpose of this channel is to assist the most vulnerable and those who may not have access to telephones. The ultimate avenue for complaints and feedback will be based on beneficiary consultation while taking note of unique operation context with a view to observe confidentiality, respect, and dignity of our beneficiaries.The program will aim to empower the community by ensuring community consultation is not limited to complaints but is factored in through the project management cycle. WFP will ensure that the community is consulted, and the most vulnerable and hidden members of the community at heightened risk are not only consulted – but maintain their own influence with a view of supporting them to not only know but also claim their rights.
