Project Summary

PAH will implement multisector programme to meet acute needs of 7584 highly vulnerable conflict-affected older people residing in Luhanska GCA as well as Donetska GCA and NGCA in such sectors, as protection, multipurpose cash assistance, WASH, food security and livelihood, and health.
Donetska and Luhanska GCAs. PAH community-based volunteers will provide group PSS in three rounds to 2200 conflict-affected older people through operation of 8 Community Centres and 4 Community spaces. At the same time, PAH will provide individual PSS to the most vulnerable older people through two modalities: 1) home visits of community workers to conflict-affected home-bound older people; 2) individual psychological consultations provided by PAH psychologists upon beneficiaries’ request and via phone hotline. Moreover, 550 most vulnerable older people will be provided with assistive devices, so that it will contribute to the improvement of their physical and psychosocial (PS) well-being. 3000 persons from among PSS beneficiaries will be provided with MPCA during three months to meet their acute needs according to eligibility criteria developed by PAH. At the same time, 450 of the most vulnerable older people will be additionally provided with cash grants for healthcare expenses during 4 months, which will contribute to their physical and PS well-being and will allow beneficiaries to reduce their expenditures on medicines and treatment. PAH will also provide trainings on agriculture for those having plot and willing to do agricultural activities. As a result, 550 most vulnerable conflict-affected older people will be provided with one-time vouchers, which are to be redeemed during March-April 2020 to use them during agricultural season. WASH intervention within given programme is focused on WASH rehabilitation of 7 social and health institutions, so that their residents will have improved access to adequate sanitation. During the rehabilitation, PAH will address needs of people with disabilities. Moreover, safety requirements accordinr to Sphere Minimum Standards will be fulfilled: most of the lavatories will be separate for men and women, lavatory entrances will be adapted for people with disabilities, where possible. According to beneficiaries’ registration data as of October 2019, 48% of older people are people with disabilities in the light of the Washington Group Short Set of Question on Disability. While implementing the programme, PAH will address needs of persons with disabilities, where it is possible, and will ensure system of data collection on people with disabilities using Washington Group Short Set of Questions.
Donetska NGCA. PAH through its local partner will provide social home-based care, e.g. assistance with shopping, changing a light bulb, cleaning, paying bills, cooking etc. to 2800 highly vulnerable immobile older people or those with mobility restrictions. In addition, 550 most vulnerable home-bound older people will be provided with assistive devices, which will make them less dependent on external support. Social home-based care will be supplemented by provision of food kits and WASH NFI kits based upon recommendations of FSL Cluster and WASH Cluster respectively. Moreover, WASH NFI kits will be gender-sensitive. Food kits will be provided in three rounds, while distribution of WASH NFI kits will be one-time. In addition, in order to meet specific needs of people with disabilities, PAH will provide hygiene diapers to 300 beneficiaries with mobility restrictions in need.
Combination of psychosocial support, multipurpose cash assistance, sanitation in institutions and agriculture and food security assistance shall reduce vulnerabilities of the conflict-affected older population living in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts through improved access to and provision of humanitarian assistance, early recovery and overall well-being.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine left 3.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. Prolonging conflict put significant psychological stress and pressure on the individuals but also families and communities that led to increase in vulnerabilities in various sectors: MHPS well-being, food security and livelihoods, water, sanitation and hygiene as well as increasing in general vulnerabilities, such as financial situation, health issues, mobility restrictions etc.According to REACH, 220 000 people live in the 5 km contact line area in Donetska and Luhanska GCA,where 70 000-older people (45% men, 55% women) in need of protection and continuous humanitarian assistance. There is particular need in PSS, as older people are found to be socially isolated after the loss or separation from their close relatives. Having a fear of shelling and possible deterioration of the security situation, they require PSS that can help to tackle symptoms of isolation and anxiety. The most vulnerable conflict affected older people are residing in NGCAs, being often left without the support from de-facto authorities and humanitarian actors. Additionally, people with disabilities are often excluded from humanitarian assistance. PAH conducted multisector needs assessment of older people residing in within 0-5 km along the “contact line” in 7 rayons of Donetska and Luhanska GCAs in January-February 2019. In total, 743 older people were interviewed, where 77%-women, and 23%-men. 99% of all the respondents were at the age of 60+ years old. According to the assessment, 75,3% of total female respondents and 64,9% of total male respondents have mobility restrictions, where 30,1% of both older men and women reported they are home-bound. One in ten interviewed needs assistive devices to move. 95% of surveyed have chronic diseases, 17,5%- disability. At the same time, 75% of older people are in need of PSS. 71% of the respondents are in need of external support, where 18,3% always need it. More than half of the respondents (63%) live alone, 43% have nobody else to live with. For 96% of the respondent’s pensions are the main source of income, where reported income per household during the last 30 days was equal to 3,094 UAH (115 USD). The rest primarily rely on aid from different agencies and income of family members. The main expenditures of interviewed older people are as follows: medicine – 44,7%; food – 36,6%; utilities – 31,4%; winterization – 16,3%, medical treatment – 14,7%. 87,6% of the surveyed have to save on food. 52,6% of the interviewed believe their income loss should be linked to the armed conflict. In April-March 2019 PAH conducted a WASH Assessment focused on sanitation conditions in health and social institutions in Luhanska and Donetska GCAs. It proved high needs for WASH rehabilitation. Majority of assessed institutions have never received assistance in the WASH sector either from humanitarian organizations or from local authorities. According to the findings, 53% of the assessed institutions have lavatories, which are partially out of order. 93,3% of the institutions assessed are not able to ensure safety in lavatories for persons with disabilities due to narrow doorways, no handrails, high thresholds, no cabins or partitions between lavatories.In order to assess needs of older people residing in Donetska NGCA, PAH conducted Rapid Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in 3 small settlements of Donetska NGCA in September-October 2018, where 243 older people were interviewed at homes. 76,9% of respondents-women, 23,1%-men. Only 26% of respondents do not have mobility restrictions and can move anywhere independently. At the same time, 35% of all the interviewed were home-bounded. 98% of interviewed older people were affected by chronic diseases. For 98% of the respondent pensions was the main source of income. 98% of the interviewed did not receive humanitarian/charitable assistance during 3 last months.

TARGET – 550.While implementing the given programme, PAH will ensure system of data collection on people with disabilities using Washington Group Short Set of Questions.PAH ensures accountability to affected population (AAP) through 3 stages:Needs Assessment: Findings of NAs, including collceted beneficiaries feedback, are incorporated into the project design in order to assure the relevance of PAH’s humanitarian response and the effective participation of affected population on the stage of project planning. Design: The needs of the older people and people with disabilities highly influenced the overall design of the project. The project design is based on PAH’s previous experience in terms of planned activities and feedback from beneficiaries, authorities and coordination system (relevant clusters and WGs). Implementation: To ensure a Do No Harm approach during implementation and adequate information sharing and transparency, PAH is committed to share program information with beneficiaries on every stage of project cycle: all beneficiaries will be informed about selection criteria, project duration, project objectives, feedback and complaints channels (F&C), timeframes and aims of each data collection activity, which will be done by PAH project staff, through project leaflets and during data collection activities.Feedback and Complaints: Acknowledging the crucial importance of beneficiaries feedback and complaints (F&C) for improving the quality of programming and for better accountability, PAH is deploying a 3-level F&C mechanism: LEVEL I: Beneficiaries have possibility to leave the feedback and/or complaints (Channels: telephone number and email managed by PAH MEAL team, boxes for F&C, KoBo form available for project team to submit beneficiaries’ feedback to MEAL team, trough enumerators and MEAL team members during periodic and regular data collection); LEVEL II: Feedback and complaints are documented and registered in a database (The database of all relevant F&C received under all PAH projects, classified by the level of importance, the subject of F&C and sensitivity, managed by MEAL team.); LEVEL III: Follow-up on feedback and complaints is ensured and documented (Program follow-up is ensured on a regular basis (ad hoc in case of urgent F&C), responsible persons are assigned, follow-up is documented in the F&C database.)Being independent from Program team, PAH MEAL team is responsible for managing F&C channels and registering F&C. Overall, AAP is tailored to be fully compliant with the IASC’s Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations and other key inter-agency standards on AAP.
