Project Summary

The proposed response is in line with 2020 HNO for Syria as it aims to improve access to quality essential Primary Health Care (PHC) services through support extended to two health facilities managed by the NGO’s partner (1 PHCC per governorate). This intervention will allow the NGO to scale-up and reinforce its response inside Syria by addressing the identified gaps and needs among the affected population through the implementation of different activities at field level as well as through strategic coordination with partners and humanitarian actors present in the area of intervention. The NGO would extend its intervention by replicating successful models of support developed in Dara’a governorate, to Rural Damascus and Qunaitra governorates.
The NGO’s 2018-2020 strategy for Syria aims to ensure access to quality PHC services for host communities, internally displaced persons and returnees. In order to achieve this objective, the NGO would:
- Ensure the provision of quality PHC services in 2 health facilities in Qunaitra and Rural Damascus governorates, managed by the local partner.
- Support partner facilities and medical staff through capacity-building and minor revitalization works, in order to enable the partner to swiftly and sustainably respond to health needs with quality PHC service provision.
The NGO ensures services are accessible to men, women, boys and girls, with or without disabilities, work towards supporting their resilience, and responds to their specific health needs. Level of access all groups is expected to be high for health services to be provided during this project. For instance, male and female staff are part of both health staff, to ensure culturally appropriate service provision and to respect preference of each individual.

Implementing Partners

Activities will be implemented through the NGO’s local partner. Since several years, the NGO has built a trustworthy relationship with local community leaders in Dara’a governorate. Despite political and security shifts, this Dara’a network partner was able to register as a local association with the NGO support and is therefore able to securely operate in all GoS areas. This official registration as a local organization is ensuring a safer working environment for operations and better access. The NGO has identified this partner based on its access to health facilities that will allow the NGO to provide health services and support the revitalization of the health infrastructures in the targeted governorate. The implementing local NGO is composed of doctors and health workers network, already identified as community leaders allowing the association to maintain strong link with the community as well as ensuring a community mobilization, participation and acceptance.

4 - Needs

Per the 2020 “draft” HNO, 12 million people are estimated to be in need of health services across Syria, with females of reproductive age making up about 26,83% of those in need (3,22 million) and with persons living with disabilities making up about 15% of the population (3.07 million people). Furthermore, of those in need of health services, 1,43 million are children under the age of 5 and 336,369 people over the age of 65, who are especially vulnerable to non-communicable diseases.As presented in the June 2019 Syria Health Sector Bulletin, Qunaitra’s estimated total population as of April 2019 was 104,244 while Rural Damascus was 3,218,016. The estimate count of spontaneous returnees between January and April 2019 reached 7,555 individuals for Quneitra and 4,290 for Rural Damascus. Moreover, while the IDP count for Quneitra was lower at 4,829 individuals, it soared for Rural Damascus at an estimated 1,172,182 IDPs as of April 2019 (36% of the total population). The 2019 HNO reported Rural Damascus to have around 2.5 million people in need, with 40% of these in acute need, while Quneitra was reported to have 70,000 people in need with 45% of these in acute need.The 2019 HNO reported further highlights that for an already vulnerable population, many of whom have been displaced multiple times, health risks remain severe and sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need. Non-communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria, with are estimated to account for 45% of all deaths according to the 2020 “drafted” HNO. Furthermore, WHO confirmed the increased gaps in essential health service provision for patients with non-communicable diseases, the high levels of disability, trauma and burns related injuries from ongoing and increased hostilities but also the increased risk of communicable diseases due to displacement, overcrowding and poor immunization coverage.

5 - Implementation of activities

G.O: Reduced suffering, increased and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for IDP, returnees and vulnerable host communities, both women and men, in the governorate of Dara'aS.O:Increased use by internal displaced people, returnees and vulnerable host communities of integrated and gender-responsive quality PHC services in targeted governorate. Results: (1)Quality Primary Health Care services, Sexual and Reproductive Health Services are provided to conflict-affected population (2)The capacity of Syrian health staff and health facilities are improved in order to better meet health needs of conflict affected individuals Activities: (1.1) The needs assessment will be conducted after the NGO provided a needs assessment training. (1.2) Primary health care consultations (Communicable diseases, NCD, health education sessions) are provided in facilities (1.3) Provision of sexual and reproductive health consultations in the facilities (ANC, PNC, FP, GBV). (1.4) The NGO will cover the costs for drugs, consumables and medical equipment to be used in the supported PHCCs. (2.1) The NGO coordination team will deliver training on rapid needs assessment to identified staff; for the partner to do the needs assessment. (2.2) The NGO will train the partner on management of GBV survivors including identification, specific management of cases and referral system. (2.3) The NGO shall define the need for minor renovations (upgrade of the facility ensuring facilitate access by all people experiencing disability, including elderly people) and basic maintenance needed for each PHCCs. (2.4) Implementation of National/international policies, guidelines and tools at PHCCs level. The NGO will employ the services of a MEAL Consultant whose primary responsibilities will include reporting, supervision, and auditing for the partner: the consultant working for the NGO, based in Syria, will conduct monitoring visits in the facilities to ensure quality implementation of activities by the partner.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: Tensions between returnees and host communities for access to services. Tensions between levels of health authorities may result in political driven decisions taken at central level which do not respect the needs of population, returnees. Difficult coordination among actors involved in the provision of services. Areas of operations are targeted by hostilities. Beneficiaries or the NGO staff are victim or witness of any kind of abuse including mismanagement, sexual abuse/exploitation, corruption or misuse of resources. Mitigation measures: Clear and solid communication and information on the project non-discriminatory approach, reinforcement of health workforce on humanitarian values and principles. Build institutional partnership with the authorities at different levels: central, governorate and local. The NGO's security management is first of all based on active acceptance by health and local authorities, communities and other stakeholders. An evacuation plan and a regular monitoring of security are implemented with the partner at the health facilities level, in case of security deterioration or during the visit of international staff. Security guidelines are reviewed according to the context evolution and likely scenarios, and extra rules might be put in place if risk exposure increases. The NGO implemented a complaint and feedback mechanism to be applied in case of individual or collective misuse of resources by teams at project sites (eg. suggestions boxes, available in each health facility). The NGO has a PSEA policy applying to complaints of sexual exploitation and abuse involving the NGO employees and related personnel. A special e-mail address has been provided, which is treated confidentially by the executive office and initiates investigation and takes appropriate disciplinary action. Regarding abuses involving partners’ personnel, mitigating measures as well as the partner endorsement on the NGO PSEA policy will be formalised in the MoU.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: the NGO’s partners are able to work and continue working in the areas of intervention. Government of Syria continues to allow partners to purchase medical supplies within Syria. Data collection at health facility level is feasible. The population have access to health facilities, despite restrictions of movement. The NGO staff is able to conduct monitoring and training visits. Access and supply are granted. Partnerships are in place. Risks: PHCCs have to interrupt activities because of the volatile security context or a change in the administrative control of the area. Money transfer to Syria from Amman is not possible because of the political situation or a worsening of the conflict. Increased displacement due to fighting impacting needs and extension of support required. Basic utility services disrupted (eg. fuel, drinking water). Fuel price increase or shortage, or difficulties in terms of transportation of supplies; Shortages in pharmaceutical supply Mitigating measures: More flexibility for frequent stock shipments to adjust accordingly with needs and avoid any shortage; To identify alternative sources of energy and fuel suppliers with the partner. To identify multiple money transfers in Syria; Maintaining flexible set-up to allow for the swift relocation of health facilities; Temporary closure of health facilities in case of security threat to staff and patients; Acceptance strategy developed in line with risk reduction measures; Security background checks conducted for all recruited staff members; To develop the capacities of medical staff and before the implementation of activities to ensure the necessary number of personnel in partner health facilities; To identify other local partners that can continue the activities in the targeted areas; To coordinate with other health actors and to prevent duplication by attending Health Cluster other INGO meetings in Amman to share information and continuously assess the context and health needs.
