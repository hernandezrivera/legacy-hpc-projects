Project Summary

The project will address the high levels of food insecurity which persist in locations close to the contact line (0-5km), incl. those that are hard to reach. Specifically, the project will look to micro-target and assist the most vulnerable amongst those affected by the conflict, including the elderly, women heads of households, and people with disabilities/limited mobility.

Needs

As the conflict continues to protract and economic recovery lags, the food security situation remains urgent in Eastern Ukraine. Provision of and access to basic services and markets have been disrupted by the line of contact (LoC), especially for those living rural/small urban locations close to it in Government Controlled Areas (GCA). Ukraine is also one of the most mine-contaminated areas globally (HALO Trust), which hinders agrarian-based farming methods on which communities in Eastern Ukraine may otherwise subsist. Food consumption remains low compared to pre-crisis, and has not improved over the past 2 years. There is also widespread erosion of coping strategies during and following winter.Food security needs are highest and most acute closest to the LoC. Within 5km, GCA side, 14% of HHs are food insecure, vs. 11% within 20km, and 9% beyond 20km. Food insecurity is also highest during winter, when 21% of HHs demonstrate poor Food Consumption Scores (FCS; REACH 2018) – vs. 16% in summer 2018, and 14% in summer 2017.Food insecurity is also driven by negative coping strategies, incl. borrowing food from family or community-members (22% of respondents; REACH 2018), reducing the number of meals eaten per day (13% of respondents), and purchasing less preferred or lower quality food items (26% of respondents). REACH 2019 data indicates that reducing essential healthcare spending is prevalent (>40%). At baseline, 7% of ACTED’s 2018 food security beneficiary caseload reported taking dangerous/degrading work as a coping strategy.Socio-economic vulnerability is high close to the LoC: average income in the 5km area is 2000 UAH (REACH, 2018), 61% of actual subsistence level (the 2018 average was 3263 UAH (Ministry of Social Policy)). Average income for ACTED’s food insecure beneficiaries is significantly lower at 1305 UAH and falls to 684 UAH for HHs with unemployed pre-pensioners (40% and 21% of actual subsistence level, respectively). More than 75% of pensioners across Ukraine receive less than 3000 UAH (Pension Fund of Ukraine, 2019). Incomes are unlikely to rise, given the economic downturn (closure of certain industries, out-migration of skills workers, etc.): from 2013 to 2017, unemployment doubled in Donetsk and nearly tripled in Luhansk oblast (WFP, 2018).At HH and individual level, specific vulnerabilities and intersections between multiple vulnerabilities exacerbate food insecurity. HHs that are single-headed, have a disabled member, are woman-headed, have a member with chronic illness, or are headed by a person age 60 or above are more likely to experience food insecurity (REACH, 2018). Women have fewer economic and livelihood opportunities, and are more likely to be dependent on men breadwinners. Single-headed HHs are 40% more likely to be moderately or severely food insecure than the general population. Compounded vulnerability of single women-headed HHs causes further strain on food consumption and socio-economic opportunities.Notably, the population within the 5km area is disproportionately women (57%), retired (36%) and elderly (33%); 67% of those aged 60+ are women, and 36% have a chronic illness (REACH, 2018). 59% of HHs within 5km are headed by women, including 90% of single-parent HHs. Children make up 18% of the target group (44% girls). Eighteen percent of the 50-59 years old group (ineligible for pensions) report being unemployed.Based on data analysis and ACTED program experience, out of the general population in target areas:- Pensioner and pre-pensioner headed HHs are most likely to be food insecure (18% and 16%);- Women headed HHs are 2.5 times more likely to be food insecure than men-headed HHs (20%);- HHs without children and single-member HHs tend to be more food insecure (17% and 21%).- Single-headed HHs are 2.5 times more likely to be food insecure than dual-headed HHs (20%).- Over 20% of HHs with a member with a disability are food insecure.

Activities & outcomes

OutcomeIncreased access to food for and reduced food insecurity of the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern UkraineModalityACTED will distribute unconditional cash transfers (UCTs) to 4,800 vulnerable people living within 5km of the LoC during the 2019-2020 autumn/winter season.UCTs are appropriate to respond to the needs: markets in GCA are functional; communities along the LoC generally have access to food products. Regular ACTED-led market monitoring (under ACCESS) has shown stable upwards price trends, seasonal patterns in availability and prices, and minor fluctuations in availability of goods. In 2018, 97% of ACTED’s beneficiary HHs spent at least part of the assistance on food (55% on average), which was sufficient to result in 82% of beneficiary HHs improving their FCS.UCTs are in line with beneficiary preferences: 73% of HHs within 5km of the LoC in humanitarian need prefer cash (REACH, 2019), and 98% of ACTED’s 2018 beneficiaries were satisfied with cash as a modality.ACTED will distribute according to FSLC guidelines, i.e. UAH 825/person/month. Beneficiaries will receive 5 months of assistance.ACTED works through Financial Service Providers (FSPs) which cover all locations within 5km of the LoC, incl. through a home delivery modality. Thus, ACTED is able to implement in hard-to-reach, isolated areas, and reach beneficiaries with limited mobility/disability/domestic care burden.TargetingACTED will implement in GCA 5km area. Based on REACH and ACTED program data (vulnerability assessments, PDM, CSI analyses, etc.), and in line with the 2019 HNO, ACTED will target:- HHs of elderly, esp. single isolated elderly;- HHs with members with disabilities/chronically ill;- Women headed HHs;- HHs with unemployed members of pre-pension age (50-59);- HHs with multiple children, esp. headed by single parents;- HHs of/with IDPs.Approx. 15% of beneficiaries will be have a disability and/or immobile, reflecting humanitarian indicator data and assessment findings which show that they tend to be less food secure.SelectionWhile most agencies use HH status (e.g. female headed; elderly; IDP…) as beneficiary selection criteria, ACTED uses HH status to target more efficiently its HH level food security assessment. Potential beneficiaries are assessed using a CARI based selection methodology that was adapted to the Ukrainian context by the FSLC with support from ACTED. This methodology uses FCS as a proxy for FSS and, to check correlation and measure project outcomes, ACTED then implements a CSI questionnaire as part of the full CARI methodology on a representative sample of beneficiaries.Gender/ProtectionFood expenditure patterns are similar among women and men. Among ACTED 2018 food security beneficiaries, women headed HHs spent on average 53% of the assistance on food, vs. 57% for men headed HHs. Traditional gender roles can still create additional obstacles for women and girls: ACTED will measure satisfaction of women in men headed HHs with HH expenditure.Feedback from groups who can face additional barriers in accessing markets indicates that they nevertheless prefer cash over in-kind assistance, as they have support networks/can pre-order from shops to ensure that goods are available; etc.AccountabilityCommunity input will be sought in the beginning of implementation through the organization of community meetings and distribution of materials to describe the program, disseminate information on ACTED’s complaints and response mechanism (CRM), and ask for referrals. Local authority representatives, community leaders and volunteers will also be engaged to verify preliminary beneficiary lists as per the vulnerability profiles targeted under the project. They can flag to ACTED if it appears that vulnerable HHs/beneficiaries from the community were ‘forgotten’.ACTED will aim to close the feedback loop on all requests made through the CRM and inform complainants accordingly.
