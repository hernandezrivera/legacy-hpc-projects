Project Summary

With an estimate of 3.257,958 million which is 54% girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022 to 2023 including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance.
The impact of COVID 19, Ebola EVD, communal violence, flooding, insecurity and displacement across the country has severely disrupted the education system and impacted the school aged children. The adolescent boys and young men being vulnerable and most affected the adolescent girls and exposed to increasing protection risks and are subjected to early forced marriage as well as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse SEA due to escalating of COVID-19 pandemic and economic crisis.
The closure of these schools not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affected and hindered children’s access to essential basic needs and services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services.
Following COVID- 19, the closure of schools and broader socio-economic hardship exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. Prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. The school closures impacted the education of over 2.7 million additional children boys and girls.
The current estimated number of out of school children is 3.2 million. Child protection cases increased during COVID-19 school closures and the number of pregnant learners increased as well in some schools. Schools are the key institutions to identify and refer children requiring protective services and case management to mitigate gender base violence threats. Most of the schools in the project focused counties has no referral mechanisms in place.
Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality. In parts of Western Bahr el Ghazal in the project focused counties Wau, Raja and Aweil South active conflict and recurring communal violence led to school closures.
Floods displaced learner’s boys and girls as well as occupation of schools by communities during floods and WASH facilities in schools destroyed. In adequate learning spaces and school supplies. Most of the schools were destroyed by the floods and recurring conflicts. Some schools do not have full learning cycle in terms of all classes. Schools are located in far distances from the community making learners not to access them.
The HRP 2023 education project targeting 2500 children ages 3 to 17 boys and girls as beneficiaries dis aggregated by gender and age boys 675, girls 650, youth women 600 and youth men 575 where most of schools in the focused counties were destroyed, damaged, closed and children being forced out of school based on different drivers.

1 - Needs

Conflict, economic shocks and flooding has affected schools and damaged school infrastructure which made schools inaccessible especially for the most disadvantaged. Due to the flood and conflict affected states and counties such Wau Raja and Aweil South Counties in Western and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states disrupted education infrastructure especially the WASH facilities at schools affecting reproductive age school girls who are more likely than boys to be excluded from education. Increasing climate change, food insecurity, economic shocks and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education teachers and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. These barriers include lack of regular teacher salaries, damaged or non-existent school infrastructure, learners’ inability to pay costs associated with schools, hunger, significant household responsibilities and marriage and pregnancy. Children girls and boys between 3 and 17 years of age in conflict and crisis affected areas including the project focused counties Wau, Raja and Aweil South in western and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states are estimated to not have access to per-school, primary and secondary education in 2023.Teachers and members of school management dis aggregated by gender committees are in need of humanitarian assistance. Lack of trained PTA and SMC to manage school development plans as well as mobilizing learners to go to schools is among the priority needs affecting learning. Lack of qualified education authorities at state and county levels to enforce learning outcomes in schools, Lack of trained teachers in schools which is a priority need for effective learning.
