Project Summary

The proposed project by NRC aims to ensure the right to equitable quality education in a safe, inclusive, and supportive environment for the displaced Rohingya refugees and surrounding host communities. The focus will be on providing access to non-formal education through the Myanmar Curriculum Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP) and Offline learning programme for the Rohingya community. Support to formal and non-formal education for the vulnerable host community will also be provided through remedial classes and offline library as well as need based rehabilitation support to WASH facilities in schools. NRC will support a total of 15,400 learners (55% female and 13% learners with disabilities) with access to equitable and inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment. Specialized services will be designed, implemented, and monitored to ensure access to inclusive education, prioritizing female students, students with disabilities, and youth at protection risks, including the LGBTI community.
The project also incorporates teacher support and capacity enhancement to strengthen teacher capacity to ensure improved teaching and learning support towards the learners. Project response components will include the provision of education supplies in the form of teaching and learning materials. The project will also focus on improving the learning environment through the rehabilitation of gender-segregated WASH facilities, handwashing facilities, and required classroom setup materials. Menstrual health and hygiene of adolescent girls’ learners will be supported through the provision of dignity kits to promote their access to continuous education and retention, and school-level hygiene awareness campaigns will be conducted. NRC will mainstream disability to promote access to education by children with disabilities. This will be through organizing awareness campaigns equally involving children with disabilities, promoting disability-friendly learning environments, and training teachers on inclusive education and gender-responsive educational practices.
The project will also focus on the holistic development and well-being of the targeted population through psychosocial support activities and the integration of co-curricular activities. The NRC Better Learning Programme (BLP), a non-specialized, classroom-based psychosocial support program aiming at equipping youth with strategies to cope with stress and promote their psychosocial well-being. It also includes targeted capacity building for teaching staff and community volunteers to ensure sustainability and promote the psychosocial well-being of learners. Through the Safe and Inclusive Programming Approach (SIP), NRC will strengthen Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs) protection initiatives and referral pathways, thereby contributing to resilience building and improved child protection mainstreaming. Furthermore, NRC will work jointly with the government and other stakeholders to support host community students in reducing engorged learning gaps that will also expedite/accelerate the involvement of the government.
NRC will work with Rohingya and host communities to promote cohesion and peaceful co-existence. NRC will also use a community engagement approach to promote community participation, ownership, and sustainability. Gender-responsive programming will be promoted through awareness campaigns and community engagement. Thus, community education groups consisting of community members and parents/guardians of learners will be formed and capacitated. Community members will also play a key role in engagement meetings and awareness raising of education activities. Crisis-sensitive planning, management, and coordination for quality provision will be ensured in collaboration with other actors as well as the government.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The proposed project aims to address the education needs of displaced Rohingya children and youth, as well as vulnerable host communities. Rohingya refugees residing in camps confront limitations on access to formal education and are prohibited from engaging in work and income-generating activities. With minimal prospects for integration within the host country or a safe return to Myanmar, Rohingya refugee communities in Bangladesh rely entirely on aid provided by local and international humanitarian organizations and United Nations agencies.As per the Joint Response Plan (JRP) PIN 2024, the education sector targets specific age groups: 5 to 14 years (Female 135,905, Male 141,316) and 15 to 18 years (Female 35,112, Male 39,101). Children constitute more than half of the refugee population and bear a disproportionate impact of the crisis, experiencing physical, psychological, and social vulnerabilities. The vulnerability of youth, especially to risks such as child marriage, early pregnancy, human trafficking, involvement in armed groups or criminal activity, labor exploitation, and substance abuse, is exacerbated by a lack of targeted interventions. Adolescent girls and young women face heightened vulnerability, including tighter movement restrictions and severe cultural, religious, and social constraints, as reported by the Norwegian Refugee Council in 2023.Currently, there are minimal educational interventions for adolescents and youth aged 15 and above, leaving more than 49,500 adolescents (15-18 years old) and over 120,000 youth (19-24 years old) without access to any form of learning opportunities. Emergencies and displacement disrupt the transition of young people into adulthood, impacting their education and employment opportunities, as well as their sense of belonging to their communities.Restrictive government policies limiting access to education and livelihood opportunities for Rohingya refugees contribute to the exclusion of refugee youth, rendering them an "invisible group." This contradicts the United Nations Convention on the Rights and Welfare of Children (UNCRC) and Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4) on inclusive quality education for all children and youth. The lack of access to quality, relevant education leaves Rohingya refugee youth with limited prospects. Within the camp setting, they struggle to meet immediate basic needs and face a high risk of rights violations, impacting their well-being and security. The existing learning environment for youth accessing non-formal education is characterized by a shortage of qualified facilitators/teachers, a language barrier, and slow adaptation to the Myanmar curriculum. Consequently, refugee youth are prematurely burdened with adult roles, experiencing forced maturity, trauma, fear, depression, and a loss of hope for the future. Young women and girls bear a disproportionate burden due to gendered discrimination, confronting sexual and reproductive health challenges such as early pregnancy, gender-based violence, and the feminization of poverty.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC collaborates closely with the education sector, CiC office, site management, and camp-level focal points to ensure that services are tailored to meet the specific needs of the camp. NRC focuses on targeting specific age groups, with an emphasis on minimizing gaps within the community where no intervention is currently available. Coordination with other agencies is a key aspect of NRC's approach, aiming to integrate efforts for synergies and avoid duplication. This coordination enhances the understanding of education needs and gaps in project locations.NRC places a priority on evidence-based programming, utilizing sector-related data collection, analysis, and advocacy to inform its actions. The organization actively engages with other actors and stakeholders within relevant coordination mechanisms, including the Education sector, and participates in sub-working groups such as the Disaster Risk Management Working Group (DRMWG) and the Inter-Agency Advocacy Group as deemed appropriate.Drawing on its expertise in youth programming, NRC emphasizes youth wellbeing mainstreaming and incorporates a positive youth development approach. Through specialized sector-relevant technical assessments, NRC contributes to identifying and responding to the specific needs and gaps in education for youth, thereby addressing barriers faced by both refugee youth and vulnerable host communities. NRC is committed to ongoing fundraising efforts and engagement with various funding partners to ensure the continuity of education and skills development interventions. This proactive approach reflects NRC's dedication to sustaining and enhancing its impact in the realm of education and skill development.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

NRC is committed to continuing the mainstreaming of key protection principles, including safety, dignity, do no harm, meaningful access, accountability, and participation & empowerment throughout the entire project cycle. In line with its dedication to inclusivity, NRC will prioritize persons with disabilities, female learners, and gender-diverse groups, with a special emphasis on encouraging the participation of girls through the inclusion of SAAD criteria. The organization places a strong emphasis on protection, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, and accountability, with a specific focus on the most vulnerable populations.NRC has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) aimed at encouraging beneficiary feedback on interventions and modalities employed. The organization is actively working to make the CFM child-friendly, ensuring that children's feedback is considered, and the referral pathway is inclusive of child protection agencies. To effectively integrate protection principles into its initiatives, NRC is dedicated to fostering community engagement and building the capacity of communities to actively participate in ensuring that adolescents and youth have access to education in safe environments. Moreover, NRC prioritizes the training of its volunteers in the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as part of their induction process, underlining the organization's commitment to maintaining a protective and accountable environment throughout its operations.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The current context poses specific risks for women, girls, persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority, and gender-diverse populations, who encounter heightened barriers in accessing education. Addressing these challenges requires meaningful participation of project participants and NRC is committed to ensure inclusivity. As part of this commitment, all NRC Education activities and reports will incorporate sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. Recognizing the gender impacts, distinct needs, and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys, and girls, NRC refers to the J-MSNA, 2023, where 21% of the population does not prioritize the education of adolescent girls (Age 12-18). NRC acknowledges that women face socio-cultural barriers limiting their access to quality and safe learning opportunities. Additionally, lack of formal leadership among adolescent girls and youth in the Rohingya population restricts opportunities to hear their voices.To mitigate the risks of delivering inequitable assistance or reinforcing existing inequalities, NRC will implement community-led creative interventions, fostering meaningful participation of adolescent girls, youth, and caregivers in decision-making processes related to girls' education. Staff and volunteers will receive training on inclusion in education, Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA. Community awareness sessions will cover the prevention of child labor, child trafficking, and child marriage.Physical inclusion will be prioritized through identification, referral, and necessary changes to learning facilities. ICE materials and content will be developed and shared to reinforce key messages. NRC is committed to respecting gender diversity, with targeted outreach and community sensitization focusing on the active participation and engagement of girls, children, and LGBTQI persons. This comprehensive approach underscores NRC's dedication to addressing barriers and promoting inclusive and equitable education opportunities

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Aligned with the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) principles, the achievement of targeted objectives will be facilitated through the provision of services. This will be executed by implementing information sharing, communication, and feedback mechanisms, alongside a commitment to transparency. Throughout the various phases of the humanitarian project cycle, NRC will ensure participation, engagement, and inclusion, with a specific focus on gender diversity. The process will involve the establishment of robust learning and feedback mechanisms. Insights gained through these mechanisms will be reflected upon, and the necessary adjustments will be made through defined processes and pathways. This approach is designed to consider the specific contexts and needs of the affected population, demonstrating a commitment to accountability towards the beneficiaries/project participants. By incorporating these principles, NRC aims to foster a more responsive and inclusive humanitarian project that effectively addresses the diverse needs of the communities it serves.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC prioritizes the safety and well-being of children and youth by ensuring that staff and volunteers undergo training on child safeguarding and adhere to organizational policies protecting them from sexual abuse and PSEA. The organization has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) designed to encourage beneficiary feedback on interventions and modalities. The CFM also addresses reported cases of sexual abuse and PSEA, supported by a protection referral pathway to ensure appropriate case management.NRC is actively working to enhance the CFM by making it child-friendly, allowing for the inclusion of children's feedback and ensuring that the referral pathway is comprehensive and involves child protection agencies. This commitment to child-centric approaches aligns with NRC's dedication to providing a secure environment for all. To effectively mainstream protection, NRC recognizes the importance of community engagement. Communities will be empowered and capacitated to actively participate in ensuring that adolescents and youth have access to education in safe environments. This holistic approach underscores NRC's commitment to creating protective spaces and fostering active involvement from the communities it serves.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC actively participates in the Disaster Risk Management Working Group (DRMWG) and remains committed to being an engaged contributor to interagency disaster preparedness efforts and emergency response initiatives. NRC's Emergency response team will play a key role in providing capacity-building training to both staff and volunteers, enhancing their preparedness and response capabilities. Environmental considerations are integral to NRC's approach, emphasizing the principles of reduce, reuse, and recycle. By incorporating these principles, NRC seeks to minimize its environmental impact and contribute to sustainability in disaster response efforts. Recognizing the valuable perspective and potential of youth, NRC provides opportunities for youth groups to address community-level challenges through youth-led initiatives. This approach empowers young people to actively contribute to community-led solutions for disaster risk reduction, fostering a sense of ownership and resilience within the community. NRC's multifaceted strategy in disaster risk management underscores its dedication to collaborative efforts, environmental responsibility, and the meaningful involvement of youth in contributing to disaster resilience within communities.
