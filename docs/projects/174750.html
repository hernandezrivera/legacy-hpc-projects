Project Summary

UNICEF intends to provide primary health care support to internal displaced populations and host populations in the three departments most affected (Logone and Chari, Mayo Tsanaga and Mayo) of the Lake Chad crisis. This response will focus on the offer of a service package for vaccination, newborn care, the management of killer diseases for children under 5 at the clinical and community level and the fight against malaria and the Covid-19. The primary target of the project consists of 583 096 populations in need including 313 567 internally displaced populations, 119 358 returned populations and 150 171 host population. Monitoring, supervision and coordination will be established to ensure good project management

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Far North region of Cameroon is facing the humanitarian crisis and public health emergencies (epidemics and floods) in a context where the indicators of maternal, newborn and child health are lowSince 2015 the Boko Haram crisis has led to the displacement of populations. In June 2020, 583 096 people affected including 313 567 internally displaced populations, 119 358 returned and 150 171 host population. The departments of Logone and Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga are the most affected. In 2020, we recorded Covid-19 measles epidemics in these populations. And, every year due to the physicochemical conditions of the soils, floods occur, causing significant health problems.These various problems are contributing to the collapse of the health of the already very poor populations. Indeed, according to DHS 2028, infant and child mortality is 102 for live births and only 23.5% of children have received all the appropriate vaccines for the age group and 52.2% have received the measles vaccine. In addition, 11.4% and 17.2% suffered from diarrhea and fever.UNICEF intends to support the implementation of an integrated package of quality primary health care for internally displaced populations, returnees and host populations in targeted areasPlanned Output, activities and expected Results (including # of beneficiaries) Output 1: By December 2021, children under five have and pregnant women access to basic preventive, curative, and promotional health care and services.Direct beneficiaries: - 21,500 Pregnant women - 20,000 children under 1 year- 87,600 children under five - 27,000 families/households corresponding to about 100,000 peopleActivity 1.1: Support outreach vaccination in every month in targeted health districts - At least 20,000 under under 1 years are vaccinated with routine vaccines (Penta 3 and mealses and others) - At least 21,500 Pregnant women received Tetanus vaccine, ferrous folic, Intermittent preventive treatment, Deworming tabletsActivity 1.2: Treatment of children for childhood illnesses (Acute reparatory infections-ARI, diarrhea and malaria) At least 10,000 children receive treatment for diarrhoea  At least 15,000 children receive anti-malaria drugs against malariaActivity 1.4: Distribution delivery and newborn kits- At least 100 health facilities received at least one resuscitation kit- Training of 90 services providers on ICCM Output 2: By December 2020, communities have access to information on preventive and promotional health care to adopt healthy behaviorsActivity 2.1: Protect families against Malaria - 27,000 household receive Treated Mosquito Nets (LLINs)- Training of 150 Community Health Workers on Integrated Community Case Management (iCCM) Activity 2.2: Mobilize communities, families and individual on Essential family practices- At least 27,000 households receive message on Key family practices for good health and COVID- 19
