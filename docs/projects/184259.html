Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to evidence-based decision making for humanitarian operations in Somalia through IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM).
Under the coordination of the Information Management and Assessments Working Group (IMAWG) and in collaboration with all clusters, DTM will provide reliable and up to date information on the number of displaced populations, their location, profile, displacement trends, reasons for displacement, and needs disaggregated by age and sex to better assist and inform the governmental and humanitarian response.
In particular, DTM’s data will feed into the roadmap of the IDP Working Group in order to provide regular updates on the IDP stocks across Somalia.
DTM tools have the potential to be tailored to take into account changes in the national context as well as information needs of responders working across the HDP nexus.

1. - Projects activities

Regular collection of data on populations affected by displacement through implementation of the DTM’s Baseline Assessment. The Baseline Assessment aims to provide a country-wide mapping and quantitative estimates (stocks) of the presence of IDPs, returnees (from abroad and from internal displacement), absentees (people who left the location and have not returned since) and residents at the lowest possible administrative level (neighborhood/wah in urban areas, villages/tulo in rural areas, and IDP sites). Information on population figures will be disaggregated by age/gender, and for IDPs the tools will also capture reasons and dates of displacement. Conducting the exercise with regularity will allow to observe dynamics in populations mobility. Building from the information collected through the Baseline Assessment, DTM will conduct Multi-Sectorial Location Assessments (MSLA), which will inform the humanitarian community about gaps in service provision and will contribute to the accountability of the humanitarian partners. MSLA is highly customizable, allowing not only to capture sectoral data (water, hygiene and sanitation, education, protection, nutrition, health) but also indicators that inform the HDP nexus, as well as COVID-19 specific data. Data analysis and dissemination - Information will be shared regularly with national authorities and humanitarian partners through reports, dashboards, datasets, and maps. Customization of data analysis will be possible at the request of partners as priorities dictate. While all data collected aims to be publicly available, IOM will follow guidelines of the IMAWG/IDPWG with regards to data sharing, as well as its internal Data Protection policies. DTM is currently the co-chair, together with UNOCHA, of the Information Management and Assessments Working Group (IMAWG) and the IDP Working Group (IDP WG), tasked with updating IDP statistics for the humanitarian response). Support to the IMAWG and the IDPWG to coordinate the work of different agencies involved in collecting humanitarian data, and in particular to lead the implementation of the IDPWG Roadmap to provide quarterly updates to IDP statistics will continue in 2022.
