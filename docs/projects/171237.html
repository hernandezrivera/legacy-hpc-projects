Project Summary

HOD has proposed this project which is linked to the Somalia WASH cluster HRP guidelines- Allocation 2021 aiming at saving lives of floods, drought and crisis affected households in Jubaland, Puntland and Galmudug. The project focuses on the immediate impact of floods, drought and conflict related crisis and will provide urgent lifesaving assistance for 2000 HHs affected by the crisis. Activities lined up for this intervention includes construction of new latrines and rehabilitation latrine affected by floods, hygiene promotion, and distribution of chlorine and soaps to promote hygiene. In addition, HOD will provide emergency access to clean water through water trucking, rehabilitation of boreholes, shallow wells and connection to existing water lines where available thus giving sustainable access to water. Other activities shall include awareness campaigns on water usage, preservation and environmental conservation, distribution of hygiene kits, chlorination of existing water storage facilities and rehabilitation of water canals along the Juba River. All these will be monitored and locally managed with the inclusion of established district WASH committees in coordination with relevant ministries and locally established structures including civil society coordination groups.
HOD’s WASH assessment at community, health facilities and public schools (September,2020) shows that there were an estimated 8,400 families (46,998 individuals) in Kismayu affected by the most recent flash floods. This was also corroborated by SOYDA assessment. The main constraint in WASH service delivery was access issue where free movements of goods and supplies by roads was exceedingly difficult due to restriction imposed by non-legal actors. This has impacted timely service delivery and cost of delivering services in Kismayu and surrounding areas. There is high risk also of increased waterborne diseases including AWD due to open defecation since latrines are not available in the newly located camps where floods have displaced families to. Other needs include the need to deploy manpower and cash for work to close river breakages and mitigate further flooding, need to drain flood waters in schools, social centers and key community spots with such tools as drainage dewatering pumps, drainage systems and manpower. Also needed are rehabilitation of latrines and water points, emergency water supply to the affected communities.
280 assessed community water points in Kismayo, Abudwak, Eyl and Taleex revealed that 12.5% are not functioning. 31% of the water points assessed do not provide enough water to the targeted communities. 29% of the assessed public schools do not have water supply systems in their schools and while 68% of the schools do have piped water, 10% of them their water supply is through water trucking, 10% depend on shallow wells while 9% of them depend on harvested rainwater through berkads. 23% of the health facilities assessed do not have water supply system. Abuduwaq district has a 48% gap in the access to safe and clean drinking water as 3845HHs need access to emergency clean water as the same number of people were displaced by the floods. Also, there are no water sources near the settlements in Kismayu and Kismayu districts where these people were displaced to. In Taleex district has a 76% gap in access to safe clean water as there are no boreholes; 29% of the respondents from Kismayu mentioned that they pay for water.
OCHA also reported that the flood waters are receding in late August but the devastation caused has been massive. Other gaps include lack of hygiene materials including soap (65% lacking) and hand washing facilities especially during these Covid-19 pandemic times and the looming AWD. A paltry 45% of respondents indicated using soap to wash hands especially after visiting latrines (Inter-agency Joint WASH Assessment 29th August 2020).

Project Activities

Objective: Ensure crisis affected households have universally sustainable and equitable access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene as well as the elimination of open defecation in Somalia.Outcome 1: Improved sanitation facilities for 2000 floods affected HHs and promote appropriate hygiene practices to affected IDP and host communities to prevent and reduce AWD morbidity and mortality as well as promote basic health and child survival among the community.Output 1.1: 2000 HHs of flood-affected and AWD vulnerable people including the disabled will have access to sanitation facilities through the distribution of hygiene kits, Chlorination of water at water points and construction of gender-sensitive latrines.Activity 1.1.1: Construct 8 new disability and gender-sensitive VIP latrines including handwashing facilities to serve approximately 240 persons (30 persons per toilet as per the SHERE standards). HOD will ensure that the latrines are up to the WASH and SPHERE standards, accommodates persons with disability, sustainable lifespan of the vault and durable. The pit hole will be 1.5m diameter × 3.2m depth, the foundation shall be of a reinforced concrete slab at-least 300 mm off the ground. The latrine wall and roofing will be done using iron sheets to the right gauge and coated to prevent corrosion from the sea moisture which has acid contents.Activity 1.1.2: Rehabilitate 4 old latrines including handwashing facilities to serve approximately 120 persons at a time as per the SHERE standards. The rehabilitation work shall be supervised by the WASH committee in coordination with the project management team to ensure that the latrines are up to the WASH and SPHERE standards, accommodates persons with disability, sustainable lifespan of the vault and durable. The latrine wall and roofing will be done using iron sheets to the right gauge and coated to prevent corrosion from the sea moisture which has acid contents.Activity 1.1.3: Distribute hygiene promotion kits and standard IEC materials to 2000 HHs from IDP and host communities (women, men, boys and girls) affected by floods to prevent the outbreak of AWD in the IDP camps and among host communities. The contents shall include 2-20-liter Non-collapsible jerry cans, 3 pieces of soap and 3 month’s supply of aqua tabs.Activity 1.1.4: Hygiene promotion campaigns will be conducted to 2000 HHs trained as ToT hygiene promoters on general hygiene practices, prevention of waterborne diseases through proper water treatment and storage practices using chlorination and Aqua tabs and hand-washing practices. The promotion will be conducted in IDP camps and health facilities to increase awareness on safe hygiene practices. Activity 1.1.5: Constitute a WASH committee of 12 (6 males and 6 females) to monitor and evaluate WASH activities throughout the project duration. The committee shall have representation from men, women, children, persons with disabilities, ministry of health and local authorities each represented by 2 persons. HOD will conduct On-site and post-distribution monitoring to ensure the works are done as per the plan.Outcome 2: 2000 IDPs and host community HHs affected by floods are provided with access to emergency clean and safe water to prevent and reduce AWD morbidity and mortality.Output 2.1: 2000 HHs from flood affected and AWD vulnerable populations including persons with disabilities have access to adequate, safe and clean drinking water.Activity 2.1.1: Contract a water company to provide emergency water trucking to 2000 HHs monthly for 3 months through water vouchers to ensure access to emergency clean and safe water to the floods affected communities.Activity 2.1.2: Rehabilitate 3 wells for domestic and animal use since the target inhabitants are largely agro-pastoralists with animals depending on water from wells to quench their thirst. This will include rehabilitation of solar water pump, construction of elevated tank, piping and water kiosk.

Mitigation Measures

For the risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, HOD will work closely with government security agencies to guarantee safety of the humanitarian workers. These will include SNA and AMISOM but most importantly HOD will ensure security updates are timely and coordination on matters security are well planned and executed.Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) shall be mitigated by linking works done with other efforts from the protection AoR with the view of mainstreaming certain protection aspects in the planned activities. Clear referral pathways shall be mapped and a focal point established to ensure timely response to protection needs.Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources shall be mitigated through elaborate coordination mechanisms and good information sharing protocols
