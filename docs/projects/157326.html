Project Summary

UPO will ensure that women, men and children have access to information and awareness raising on the safe household water treatment and use of proper sanitation facilities. Women, girls, boys and men continued to be affected by protection related WASH issues, with gender based violence occurring due to insufficient and unsafe access to water and sanitation facilities. • Sustain access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene improvements and awareness is critical to save thousands of lives of children and women. Excretal and poor disposal coupled with lack of proper good hygiene practices characterized by rampant open defecation leaves a large portion of the population at high risk of preventable water related diseases such as cholera and AWD. UPO will ensure that women, men, girls and boys are consulted in the decision making affected have equal access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services to prevent potential public health risk as a result of using unprotected water sources poor sanitation and hygiene practices. • The consequences of poor water, sanitation and hygiene are stark, with more boys and girls dying from water and sanitation related illnesses such as diarrhea; this exacerbates the health of malnourished boys and girls and opportunistic infections like pneumonia and cholera that have been reported in parts of Darfur and Kordofan. Inadequate access to safe water points also has implications on women and the education of girls in particular as they have to spend considerable periods fetching the scarce commodity when they should be in school. UPO believes that through provision of safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to these vulnerable communities that will cover aspects of social mobilization, rehabilitation of water points, sanitation facilities, emergency hygiene promotion, enhancing capacity of the communities and resilience. UPO will ensure active involvement of women and men in decision-making in the provision of safe and appropriate water, sanitation and hygiene services.

Needs

Currently 2.6 million people are in need of WASH assistance according to the HNO 2019 The immediate influx of IDPs is increasing pressure on few shared WASH infrastructures/facilities among the IDPs and the vulnerable host community especially in Jebel Mara area .Despite the provided humanitarian services in camps, rural areas in Darfur and Kordofan , major gaps in WASH sector remained unsatisfactorily responded, the newly displaced people mostly women and children are in lack of urgent WASH services. Only 2.4 3 L/P/per day in most of the visited locations. UPO assessments were jointly conducted by HAC in South, East, North and Central Darfur, to contribute to a common understanding of WASH needs in Golo. In general, the assessments reported that with the exception of few (IDPs) typically walk anywhere from 8 11 Km to collect water. This poses significant risks, particularly for women and girls, who often bear the brunt of abuse and physical and/or sexual violence given their vulnerability to armed groups and male aggressors when traveling to and from remote water sources. At the same time, the water collected is commonly untreated as it is derived from Hafir, ponds, , while open defecation continues to be widespread – compounding the vulnerability among displaced and conflict affected communities to chronic illnesses and disease outbreaks. The assessments carried out to date show that approx. 90% of surveyed host and IDP households lack access to latrines and practice open defecation along Hafir where water is collected for household use and human consumption. Further, hygiene practices and solid waste management require critical attention by continuing to reinforce the capacity of existing hygiene committees, in addition to forming new ones where necessary. Meanwhile, the training of hygiene promoters/volunteers at the camps and gatherings level needs to be intensified alongside the provision of hygiene kits. UPO therefore proposes activities that will help improve the health, hygiene and sanitation practices through behavior change. . UPO also conducted a rapid WASH assessment in in West Kordofan in 13 16 Feb 2018. Overall, the findings reveal that approx. 80% of the communities surveyed lack basic access to sanitation facilities, adequate protection mechanisms are largely absent for women and girls to safely access boreholes, and the combination of overuse of existing water facilities and unavailability of spare parts, along with typically limited maintenance, precludes the proper functioning of water facilities. This trend, in turn, contributes to numerous water facilities being abandoned, with the majority of displaced and conflict affected community members (resorting to unprotected surface water from rivers, Hafir ponds and swamps).

Impact on environment/natural resources

UPO will conduct environment impact assessment before the project starts, to identify the potential risks, impact as well mitigation measures to be applied both during construction and operation phases of the project. the key issues UPO will look into during the assessment are as follows 1- Soil erosion and contamination.2- Water pollution.3- Solid waste generation.4- Public safety and health5- Conflicting demands for water use.For each of the identified impacts, mitigation measures have been suggested as follows: a) Avoidance of major impacts: major impacts are generally considered unacceptable, ones that would endure in the long-term or extend over a large area; UPO will ensure effective mitigation measures b) Reduction of major and moderate impacts to as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP) by planning, designing and controlling mitigation measures. This implies that mitigation measures will be applied until the limitations of cost-effectiveness and practical application have been reached. c) Implementation of good practices for impacts rated as minor, in order to ensure that impacts are managed within good reason
