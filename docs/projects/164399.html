Project Summary

Sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services at the community, primary and secondary care levels remains critical to addressing the health needs of the affected population, especially with the changes in lines of control which likely to trigger disruptions in health services and may affect humanitarian access.
Areas with limited access, under-served areas, newly accessible areas, and those that recently changed control will continue to require revitalization of the health system especially in an area like Der-Ez-Zor which considered hard to reach or areas with newly displaced people which has an additional burden on its previous week health system.
The project will be focusing on providing primary health services through tow primary health care centers that will provide medical consultation, diagnosis, and treatment by different clinics such as a first-aid clinic, pediatric clinic, dental clinic, and OBGYN department, that will provide 24/7 normal deliveries in addition to the OBGYN consultation and family planning.

4 - Needs

The need for humanitarian health assistance is expected to continue in 2020. In Der-ez-zor according to (Reach,2019) KIs reported that no health services were present in 52% of the assessed communities, The most commonly reported accessible health services were private, especially in the East Line. While KIs in 69% of all assessed communities in the East Line reported that at least one health facility in their community was no longer functional. Overall, the main reported barriers to accessing healthcare were the high cost of services (57%). Moreover, according to HNO,2020 indicates the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs particularly for (NCDs), which are estimated to account for 45 percent of all deaths across middle-aged persons. MHPSS needs are critical: prolonged exposure to conflict is believed to increase the prevalence of mental health conditions. Pregnant women will require ante-natal and post-natal care, as well as access to skilled birth attendants, An estimated across Syria 15 percent of deliveries in women and 15 percent of newborns will require life-saving emergency intervention and it's believed that the number in NES is much higher. The Northeast of Syria has additional barriers above all other areas of Syria, which can be summarized by the in-effectivity of the cross line, and the inability to have a consolidated hub for cross border operation, especially this will be more affected during the current year after the refusal to extend the resolution for the NES border, which will directly affect the availability of medicine and the size of operation for cross border members. In addition, the Turkish military operation made additional security pressure on the international non-governmental organization.

5 - Implementation of activities

IPV will establish tow PHCCs in Der-EZ-Zor in Hajin and Thiban subdistrict , with a mobile clinic for each PHC, which will cover the communities in the selected subdistrict after an accurate mapping with the active health partners in the field. Each PHC will have basic departments that will work 8 hours daily, it will include a dental clinic, pediatric, general medicine clinics, and a pharmacy that will provide NCD medication. In addition to a 24/7 first-aid and gynecology clinics that will provide normal delivery for women in addition to consultation, pregnancy follow-up and family planning.Each PHC will provide MHPSS and nutrition services "detailed in nutrition proposal", through CHW, PSS, and GP trained on MHgap, Each PHC will be linked with one ambulance to refer needed cases and deliveries. The PHCCs will be modified for people with disabilities, easy access at the entrance, clinics, pharmacy and WCs. Ongoing coordination with all stakeholders will be done through attending the NES forum HWG meetings, uploading 4Ws and following up with health authorities to avoid any duplication. Ongoing systems of monitoring and accountability will be in place throughout the duration of the project. These will include weekly field visits of the M&E team, as well as the provision of comments/complaints boxes that will be collected on a weekly basis, in addition to a hotline that will be available in each PHCC. Concerns, observations, and complaints will be tracked and responded to appropriately. In addition, satisfaction surveys will conduct on throughout the project. The findings from the field visits, compliant box, the hotline line and the progression toward achieving the indicators will all be put through a comprehensive M&E report which will be shared accordingly with the health department. In the presence of emergency findings, the information will be shared directly with the health department to find the needed solutions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. The risk from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA which is referred to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation by staff and associated personnel. Mitigation: -Effective community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel, with training on the topic.-Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave.2. Gender-Based Violence: health outreach services are often the first and the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance. In order to provide care, survivors must have safe access to facilities, and it is critical that health teams have the knowledge to handle the case.Mitigation: - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services.- Train the staff on psychosocial support , in a manner guarantees safety and confidentiality of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in outreach teams and fixed facilities increase access of women to health services3. The health facilities and providers could face violence against them, the violence is still the main issue facing healthcare providers.Mitigation:-Set up centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries.-Relocate centers in relatively safe areas if anything happened.-Reporting on attacks against healthcare.4. Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups:Addressing barriers by:-Ensure training for health workers on the rights of persons with disabilities.-Ensure health facilities are accessible for all people by adjusting the PHCs with needed equipment.-Ensure all health information is available in accessible formats.Data collection:-Ensure data collected reflected the disabilities which will help participating them in any new plans for other projects.
