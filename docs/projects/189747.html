Project Summary

The Intervention targets conflict affected and vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and host community for distribution of Non food and shelter items in the next 12 months. The project design is informed by assessed, expressed and prioritized Shelter and NFIs needs obtained from conflict affected persons during the IRNA and Inter Agency assessments conducted in Ayod, Duk and Uror counties in 2021 and 2022. Also, the project seeks to meet SNFIs needs prioritized in the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022). This proposed project will address emergency shelter and non-food items needs of vulnerable populations affected by conflict and flooding and classified as facing severe, extreme and catastrophic needs. The project will target 4857 HHs (approximately 34,000 individuals: Males 15000, females 19000) both newly displaced and protracted IDPs and host communities, for emergency shelter and NFIs activities response in Jonglei State (Ayod, Duk and Uror Counties). Distribution of beneficiaries in each county being as follows: Ayod 11000: Duk 12000 and Uror 11000. The modality of implementation is in-kind since targeted locations do not have markets which have no capacity to support cash based intervention. RAO will coordinate the response with the cluster partners in respective counties for effective response. RAO will also ensure that there is proper coordination especially in locations where there is more than one implementing partner to avoid double-counting, and as well reduce duplication of efforts. RAO is a key member of Shelter and NFIs, Education, Wash, FSL and Protection clusters and participates in coordination meetings at the county level. To effectively deliver the assistance, RAO will coordinate the project through the RRC, Chiefs and Cluster systems. Affected persons will be involved in implementation through Accountability to affected persons committees to ensure openness, accountability and local contribution. To ensure an integrated response, RAO will work with Health and WASH partners on joint Covid19 messaging, training on use and disposal of mosquito nets. Referrals of protection concerns will be made through the protection partners. FSL and Nutrition partners will be engaged at demonstration and kitchen gardens where farmers and households will be guided on proper utilization of mosquito nets. Currently, people use the nets to build tukuls, fence gardens and for fishing. Distribution and demonstration of use of mosquito nets will be done in health facilities, at demonstration farms and at nutrition stabilization and treatment centers frequented by mothers and pregnant women.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview report (2022), humanitarian needs in Ayod, Uror and Duk counties have escalated due to several factors such depletion of food stocks, high food prices, reduced market functionality, and access challenges occasioned by the recent floods and worsening of road conditions. Other factors include COVID-19 pandemic, economic shocks and currency devaluation that will continue to erode households’ purchasing power. IDPs and Host Communities in the four counties have the highest severity of needs. For example: According to HNO report, each of the four counties have an estimated population of 100000 to 200000 individuals affected by flooding which ravaged the country in the last half of 2021. Within the affected population, some of the vulnerable groups that may have specific needs include children, women at risk, the elderly, people with disabilities, single-headed household members, and the extremely poor. Being unable to support themselves through communal connections and without coping mechanisms from other resources, the affected persons have very limited or no access to essential life-saving shelter or NFIs. The humanitarian situation of the affected persons worsened in the last quarter of 2021 due to flooding caused by torrential rains that started from September and continued through October 2021. Flooding devastated livelihoods by way of crop failure and livestock death, destroyed houses in most of the flooded Payams and damaged roads leading to restricted movement of goods and disrupted market functionality. Scarcity of essential household goods has resulted in high prices and inability of displaced and vulnerable host communities from accessing essential items such as mosquito nets, blankets and cooking pots at the local level. In the prevailing acute food security situation, vulnerable internally displaced persons and host community who have lost household assets as a result of conflict and flooding require in-kind NFIs support. Such support will enable them dedicate their remaining recourses to buy food and medicine instead of using the money to replenish lost household items. Due to worsening humanitarian situation, negative coping mechanisms include going to bed earlier due to mosquito infestation, reducing meals per day due to lack of sufficient food and adequate cooking items, staying under trees during day and seeking shelter at night from neighbors due to lack of shelter materials, and using animal hides to cover children and elderly persons at night due to lack of blankets. Ultimately there is an insufficient supply of non-food items such as, mosquito nets, cooking pots, blankets and Kangas for women. Providing the affected population with non-food items will also enable them to reduce protection risks associated with living in close quarters with strangers, cooking outside at late hours due to inadequate cooking pots and lack of adequate lighting at community settlements. During assessment conducted in these counties, elderly women were observed wearing torn or old clothes exposing them to elements of weather and indignity. Providing Kangas will better enable their mobility and ability to access markets and other food sources with dignity. Considering these glaring needs and gaps, the proposed project will seek to aid in form of Kanga, blankets, cooking sets, non-food item bags, reusable masks, solar lamps and mosquito nets to the most affected persons. The assistance will cover only internally displaced persons and most vulnerable host community members who fall under the following categories: elderly, pregnant and lactating women (they are exposed to various risks such as sickness, malnutrition and protection thus making them more vulnerable compared to any other group); children; unintegrated persons; people with no community links; severely sick persons; and individuals living with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

ACTIVITY 1: Conduct Inception meetings: Inception meeting will be conducted with IDPs, women leaders, youth and people with disabilities. The purpose being is to provide information on project objective, duration, anticipated results, kind of assistance to be provided, category of beneficiaries targeted in the project, modality of implementation, and role of community and project staff in the implementation process. During inception meetings, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) wil be formed. The CFM will comprise project staff, chiefs and community representatives. A Safe Guarding officer will receive and timely dispense all complaints raised in a safe, accessible and timely manner. ACTIVITY 2: Selection of 90 (47 Females/ 43 Males) local enumerators: Enumerators will conduct mobilization of beneficiaries, communication and sensitization, registration, setting up of distribution points, distribution of non-food items and post distribution monitoring. They will play the role of initiating community level engagements, mobilization of beneficiaries and stakeholders during planning, meetings, trainings, and project monitoring and evaluation. ACTIVITY 3: Selection of beneficiaries: RAO’s project staff will work closely with AAP committee, chiefs, local authorities, site managers, enumerators and existing partners to select 7000 most vulnerable households among IDPs and host community who will benefit through distribution of S/NFIs. The selection criteria to be applied will include: Individuals classified as IPC 4 and 5, women headed households, and children headed households, elderly persons, severely sick persons, pregnant and lactating women, care givers of orphans, separated and unaccompanied children. ACTIVITY 4: Collection of SNFIs from Core Pipeline and distribution of the items: RAO project staff will supervise setting up of distribution venues two days prior to distribution exercise. Each distribution point will be accessible by elderly persons, those living with disability and considered safe for women and girls. The venues will have gender disaggregated latrines, water points and safe spaces where pregnant and lactating mothers can be able to rest, feed their children. Public announcement will be made using public address systems in all distribution locations. On the day of distribution, enumerators will conduct demonstration on appropriate use of the SNFIs prior to handing over the items to beneficiaries. RAO will work closely with health partners who will conduct COVID-19 messaging during distribution days. All distributions will be conducted during light hours to allow beneficiaries sufficient time to walk to distribution centres and return home during daytime. ACTIVITY 5: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: The PDM will be carried out using S/NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires which contains the question that assesses the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed registration and verification processes. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary HHs with focus on the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The project will employ a sample size of 20% of the HHs who will benefit directly through the intervention. ACTIVITY 6: Conduct HLP analysis and awareness: Due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to distribution of Shelter materials.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RAO is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). RAO is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, RAO will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA, training consultants, field staffs and enumerators on PSEA and their policies pertaining to PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution of shelter and NFIs. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection concerns. RAO gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Uror, Duk, Ayod and Akobo. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports, post distribution monitoring reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, Shelter and NFIs cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in Akobo, Uror, Ayod and Duk Counties. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by Reach Organization’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, Reach Africa Organization will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at registration sites as well as distribution sites. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries and distribution of shelter and non-food items

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RAO will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, RAO will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, RAO will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

On feedback and complaints, RAO is committed to upholding Core Humanitarian Standard-Commitment 5 (Communities and people affected by crisis have access to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints). RAO has an active Feedback and Complaint procedure (attached) which guides staff on how to address various types of complaints and the level at which the complaint should be handled. All complaints or feedback received through any complaints channel (Feedback and Complaint Desk or toll free telephone number) during implementation of the the project will be recorded on a Feedback and Complaint log. This log will be maintained by the Safeguarding Officer. All complaints or feedback received will be processed appropriately, timely and fairly ensuring upmost confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants and those affected. The Feedback and Complaint log will be password protected, securely stored to ensure confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants. Hard copies will be printed and filed on weekly basis within a secure complaints & feedback folder. The register will be reviewed on a weekly basis to ensure any open complaints or feedback addressed, processed and closed appropriately and the log is being kept updated. Key learnings or changes to policy, process or working practices arising from received complaints or feedback will be extracted monthly and shared with the RAO’s senior management for wider organizational learning. Beneficiaries and communities, especially the most marginalized and vulnerable will be periodically consulted to ensure they are satisfied the complaint and feedback mechanism is accessible, effective, confidential and safe .RAO employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community will be gathered through multiple channels, especially hygiene promoters during hygiene promotion activities, monitoring activities e.g. post-distribution monitoring (PDM), safety audits (on protection and GBV). The information gathered will be used to inform future programming on the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance, preferred quality and quantity of S/NFIs and protection concerns associated S/NFI in the targeted locations. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where RAO is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

On risks of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, RAO will conduct a safety audit on all registration and distribution points to map existing and potential risks. Safety Audit Findings will be embedded in project workplan and safety matrix to ensure all project activities are conducted in locations and sites where no risk is posed to staff, project supplies or beneficiaries. RAO will ensure registration and distribution points are near settlement sites to reduce on distance travelled by beneficiaries to collect their shelter and non-food items. On risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, RAO has a robust PSEA policy with all the staff properly trained on PSEA. RAO will conduct PSEA training targeting enumerators, community mobilizers and AAPs committees. Also, RAO’S gender and protection officers will conduct sensitization targeting women and girls, community leaders and local authorities on PSEA mitigation and reporting. On risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, RAO’S Child Protection policy prohibits any form of child Labour and child exploitation. Gender Officer and the Protection Officer will conduct community sensitization and awareness to ensure child protection concerns are addressed and mitigated before they occur. Cases of Child Protection will be referred to appropriate child protection actors at project locations. On risks that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The project targeting criteria is clear and targets these category of beneficiaries. Community communication and sensitization will be conducted targeting beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in order to minimize of tensions arising from sections of the community who do not receive the assistance. To mitigate HLP related disputes and conflict, RAO through local authorities, protection partners and chiefs will conduct due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to distribution of Shelter materials to beneficiaries.
