Project Summary

Eight years into the crisis, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity(HNO 2019).
Olive sector suffers from major problems such as the decreased yearly production of olive fruit (up to 50% decrease in 2016 compared to 2011) caused by farmers inability to secure inputs such as fertilizers, and pesticides in addition to the high costs of other services like land-plowing, harvest, pruning of trees, the absence of governmental support, which led many farmers to abandon their lands and many olive cultivated areas to become at risk of being out of production.
This project has been designed based on Needs Assessment in Aug 2018 in 35 villages and based on data coming from the ongoing olive support project of Sadad identifying the major problems and needs based on beneficiaries needs and desire to support more areas of olive trees.
The project will target 22 villages in Al-Ma’ara, Ariha districts in Idleb and 3 villages in Afrin district in Aleppo. Locations are classified among severe districts according to FS Situation Report, thus action is required to save life, and livelihoods especially as these villages have large areas of olive trees. Sadad experienced and technical team in cooperation with LCs and agricultural units will work to deliver the project's interventions focusing on improving food security and supporting livelihood through providing two main component:
-*Improve food security by increasing the productivity of 500 hectares of Olive trees at rate of 2 dunums support per household (HHs with access at least to 0.5 hectare or less of olive cultivated holdings) through providing quality agricultural services as follow:
1-Providing autumn and spring land-plowing service to the 500-targeted hectares of olive trees (tow dunums per HH).
2. Hiring harvest workers and conducting the harvest service to the targeted 500 hectares of olive trees (two dunums per HH).
3- Providing the targeted 2500 HHs with 2500 olive oil extraction vouchers (one voucher per each HH) to extract olive oil after harvest.
3-Conducting technical pruning service for the targeted 500 hectares of olive trees.
*Improving livelihoods and creating means of income for (375 males and 650 females) workers through providing them with cash for work related to agricultural activities as follow:
#100 male-beneficiaries will be hired, trained and provided with cash for 60 working-days to conduct the project's related services and activities during the whole project duration.
#700 beneficiaries (650 females /50 males) will be hired, divided into groups and provided with cash for 30 working-days to conduct olive fruit harvesting to the targeted 500 hectares of olive trees.
# 225 male-beneficiaries will be hired, provided with cash for 45 working-days to perform technical pruning to 500 hectares of olive cultivated areas.
The project also will focus on providing vocational training related to agriculture through:
Providing 225 male workers with 7-days technical training on how to apply technical olive trees' pruning.
As a result, the project will contribute mainly to enhance food security by supporting agriculture and means of income in addition to supporting the resilience of the vulnerable farmers and enhance their methods of coping.

Needs

Now in its eighth year , the humanitarian crisis in Syria continues and thus the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming and remain high. Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), indicates some 11.7 million people in Syria require humanitarian aid and assistance, of them, 5 million considered in acute need in addition to the increased number of IDPs to reach 6.8 million people. Although number of people in need has slightly decreased compared to the previous year, still there are an estimated 6.5 million Syrians considered as food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity according to (FAS HRP 2019) bringing the total up to 9 million Syrians who need of urgent life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihoods assistance.Vulnerable populations across Syria such as people with disabilities, single headed households, females headed HHs and the elderly often face particular challenges physically accessing food, for reasons ranging from a lack of mobility to threats to their physical safety. In many communities, an influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees contribute to socio-economic pressures that also create vulnerabilities for host communities. Food Needs in Syria are driven by many crisis related factors such as lack of financial access, as high food prices, low purchasing power, and lack of means of income or livelihood opportunities which badly affected the economic and living situation of people and led to food insecurity and this has caused heavy reliance on negative coping strategies, a lack of dietary diversity, and significant gaps in food consumption. Moreover, The Agricultural sector, which is also considered as a main source of income for large proportion of people inside Syria is also threatened by factors such as land contamination, depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt; and limited economic opportunities and lack of availability of and/or financial means to access essential inputs, and damaged infrastructure with lack of agricultural support offices or units. This has contributed to high levels of poverty across Syria, with an estimated 64-to-84 per cent of the population is living below the poverty line (HRP2019).According to “Olive Position Paper 2016” based on Syrian Olive Office – Statics and publications, Olive trees play an important role in the economic life of Syrian people and they were contributing up to 9% of the agricultural income in Syria In 2011 with about 1118,000 estimated hectares of olive cultivated lands in Idleb Governorate alone containing about 15 million olive trees (Olive Position Paper 2016). In Afrin, olive trees are the first to be considered in terms of income in the area, which accounts for about 78% of the total land area.Sadad had previously conducted olive agriculture assessments in 30 villages within Idleb Governorate related to Al Maara and Ariha districts in addition to targeting 25 villages with two-stages olive support project and reaching about 2500 olive farming HHs. In addition Sadad has targeted 5 villages in Afrin in a new rapid olive cultivation assessment.Assessments conducted and surveys from the ongoing sadad projects showed that the major difficulties facing olive farmers are the high costs of inputs like fertilizers and pesticides, the high cost of some services like land-plowing, oil extraction in addition to the high costs of harvest and pruning workers with lack of means of income for the farmers HHs especially females-headed have access to olive trees holdings and face difficulties with no family members able to accompany them to the agricultural services. These difficulties have led many of olive farmers especially females to abandon their olive trees holdings and to follow negative coping strategies including selling the assets and depending on humanitarian aid and accordingly this has affected the olive production.

Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through Sadad office in the city of Kafr Nobol and Afrin by Sadad technical agricultural teams including agronomists and agricultural supervisors who have a previous experience with Sadad in implementing Olive support interventions and programs. Sadad will improve the production of olive trees by provide the beneficiaries with high quality of agriculture services through implementing these activities. - First of all, Sadad will priorities vulnerable olive farmers' HHs to benefit from services of the project on clear selection criteria with priority to females headed HHs considering the children and elderly people and PSNs' situations and difficulties within these HHs. - After selecting and documenting the beneficiaries, project teams will start conducting spring land-ploughing for the targeted hectares, while the second part of land-ploughing for the 500 targeted hectares will be conducted in October 2019. - The harvesting process of olive fruit for the 500 targeted hectares will start in the mid of October after contracting with #700 harvest workers (490 host community, 210 IDPs) and provide them with cash for work for 30 working days to conduct the harvest service for the targeted 500 hectares.- During the process of olive fruit harvest, teams will select at least 7 olive-oil extraction mills and signing MOUs with them to ensure the process of receiving vouchers from the beneficiaries and extracting the oil under the supervision of the project's supervisors and service workers. The 7 selected mills will be responsible for redeeming the beneficiaries’ vouchers and then extract oil as identified and agreed (500 kg of olive fruit for voucher). - Providing females-headed HHs with plastic bags and plastic gallons to keep the olive oil after extraction which used to facilitate the agriculture services and work.- Providing females-headed HHs with (2500) olive-oil extraction vouchers (one voucher per 2 dunum / voucher is valid to extract 500 kg of olive fruit) and then starting the process of olive oil extraction in the mills.- Purchasing pruning tools and conduct the technical pruning service to the 500 targeted hectares of olive cultivated lands by contracting with #225 pruning workers and provide them with trainings on technical pruning and provide them with cash for work for 45 working days- The second objective of the project is to provide beneficiaries with Income generating activities linked to agriculture including vocational training through implementation these activities by:- Providing100 male-beneficiaries (40 IDPs - 60 host community) with one-day workshop on integrated olive-cultivation management (timing of applying olive trees services, what is the importance of applying these services, types of olive diseases, fertilizers and how to discover about pest’s activities, etc. In addition to providing them with one-day training on introduction to minimum standards in humanitarian action and who to deal with beneficiaries according to the humanitarian standards.- Providing 225 male-beneficiaries (30 IDPs - 195 host community) with vocational training on technical olive trees pruning, proper pruning skills and how to avoid the negative ways of trees' pruning including excessive pruning.- Providing cash for 60-working days through contracting with 100 male beneficiaries (40 of them IDPs) as workers to conduct the projects related services and activities during the whole project duration (land plowing, overseeing the harvest and oil extraction, transportation, etc.). - Throughout the whole period of activities' implementation there will be a regularly focus group discussions and awareness rising sessions with beneficiaries and provide beneficiaries with brochures and leaflets on protection related issues including GBV, mine action, child protection etc, in addition to brochures on the integrated management of olive trees.

Protection Risk Analysis

Sadad during implementing the activities of the project may face these risks according to the project's activities.Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. Likelihood: high / Impact: High. Mitigation: Sadad will priorities the most vulnerable females-headed or males headed with PSNs HHs, considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. Sadad has partnered with local leaders (to make them aware of whole project’s activities and interventions) in designing the project to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Protection risk 2: Risk of harassment of women and girls by the humanitarian workers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: establishing a confidential complaint mechanism and enhancing accountability mechanisms besides training the staff to deal with beneficiaries in addition to providing workers with training on humanitarian standards and signing code of conduct.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risks of insecurity situation comes from airstrikes, clashes, the attack of regime forces etc. Likelihood: high / Impact: High Mitigation measures: The security situation within the targeted villages is estimated to be safe to ensure best implementation and provision of services especially as the areas are considered to be safe after the spread of Turkish monitoring points. However, there will be a continuous risk assessments and a safety and security plan and activities may be suspended and delayed if there is shelling in addition to putting in place an emergency response plan and a contingency plan which allows alternative solutions to implement activities in emergency situations.2 - Risk of military offensive in the areas of the project. Likelihood: medium / Impact: high. Mitigation measures: there is a risk of a military offensive in the project's locations and if this happened the project's activities will be relocated to another safe area and there will be a plan be to move the project's activities and services to the second area where the project is planned to be implemented and increase the targeting numbers in Afrin district (this area is rich in olive trees and needs assessments between Sadad and AFAD indicated a need to support olive farmers in this area)
