Project Summary

Adopting an integrated protection approach, the project aims at strengthening the protection environment for returnees and hosting communities focusing on (a) GBV prevention and response (in Ibo and Mocimboa da Praia) and (b) community reconciliation through rights awareness raising and sensitization and community engagement in enhancing protection (in Ibo and Mocimboa da Praia as well as Mueda and Palma). In this way, while providing lie-saving support to GBV victims / survivors, the project simultaneously contributes to raise awareness on human rights, the importance of protection and of addressing GBV, and the role that community reconciliation can play in restarting sustainable livelihoods.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project is designed to build a strengthened protection environment for hosting communities, IDPs and returnees, with a specific focus on GBV prevention and response (in Mocimboa da Praia and Ibo) and community reconciliation (in Mocimboa da Praia and Ibo, as well as in Mueda and Palma, complementing already ongoing GBV prevention and response activities in the two latter Districts) through engaging communities in protection. It builds on, and is enhanced by, Helpcode’s protection monitoring activities across Cabo Delgado (including in the targeted Districts) and in GBV prevention and response and livelihood creation for women in Mueda and Palma, realized across 2021/2022 and continuing in 2023. The integrated intervention proposed responds simultaneously to GBV risk prevention and mitigation, GBV response (through provision of services) and building communities capacity to recognize and address protection risk and engage in reconciliation efforts. GBV prevention and response are life-saving, critical interventions in the current scenario in Cabo Delgado, as structural violence against women and girls has been further exacerbated by the conflict and its consequences: improving the quality of services offered to victims / survivors and preventing cases of violence is fundamental to restore dignity and ensure safety for women and girls, as well as for building solid basis for recovery. To do so, the active participation of communities that are aware of the importance of protection is crucial and therefore the project engages also in broader community reconciliation and awareness activities that build on Helpcode experience in promoting peace education through community dialogue and action in post-conflict settings.The aim is to develop and systematize a structured, integrated approach that can, on the one hand, address prevention and awareness needs and, on the other hand, respond directly in an efficient and respectful to individual cases of GBV – by engaging with a variety of actors and stakeholders including (1) the public response system, by strengthening the technical capacity of relevant services and by increasing the effectiveness of the management and referral systems of GBV cases; (2) women and girls at risk / victims / survivors through the Safe Spaces; (3) communities as a whole, with specific attention on the role that men and boys can play in building a protection environment and in promoting reconciliation (for example by collectively analysing how GBV and conflict are strictly connected and finding common peaceful solutions); (4) the humanitarian partners working in sectors other than protection in order to ensure a coordinated response.The main target group of the action will be vulnerable women and girls (at risk / victims / survivors) and women who are household head; however, prevention and awareness activities will have specific approaches for different target groups (men, boys, women, girls). Communities targeted by key humanitarian interventions will be involved in community reconciliation and community-based awareness for protection.The four project components - prevention, response, training, awareness – allow at once (1) for women and girls to receive direct assistance, including aimed at economic empowerment through livelihood training, (2) for women, girls, men and boys to gain a more in-depth understanding of what is violence (3) for public institutions to enhance their capacity to provide quality services, thus aiming at building sustainability of the intervention and (4) for communities to recognize themselves as key actors in building a protection environment.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will be structured across four main pillars /set of activities: 1. Prevention, directed and tailored to different target groups (men, boys, women, girls), including:a. Awareness raising sessions about GBV and PSEA (both small group sessions of maximum of 20 persons; and large group sessions organized during awareness raising events such as IWD, 16 days of activism and others). The small groups sessions will be conducted in identified Women and Girls Safe Spaces;b. GBV risk mitigation activities through GBV Safety audits and GBV specific risk analysis in every step of the activities implementation of the different components. c. Short courses in saving, mathematics and business management; practical workshops for income-generating activities (identified thanks to an initial quick market study, and an assessment of women’s interest and skills); and delivery of kits to start a small business. 2. Response, through:a. PSS and case management in Safe spaces for women and girls. The psychosocial and clinical support (MH) offered by the technical team will be available to every member of the community who recognizes the need for overcoming a trauma, with particular attention to people surviving gender-based violence incidents. b. The case management system will be coordinated with the services active in the area, and with the specific referral pathways already elaborated in the GBV AoR coordination meetings at provincial level. In this way, the focal points of case management can also be the support in the districts for the updated maintenance of the reference system. GBV case management will include: individual case management, PSS support, material assistance other than cash and voucher and referrals. 3. Training aimed at strengthening public services capacity to deliver in GBV response and ensure sustainability, including:a. GBV trainings for staff and partners (GBV risk mitigation/mainstreaming training, GBV basic concepts and guiding principles training). 4. Awareness, through:a. Rights awareness raising and community sensitization on the importance of creating a protection environmentb. Peace education activities through community dialogues, theatre, sport activities, community radio broadcasting (or "radio na rua") aimed at finding peaceful, inclusive, gender and age responsive solutions to potential local tensions and conflicts, including for example in access to resources or opportunitiesc. Establishing community-based protection systems in IDPs sites, hosting communities, returning areas

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project aims at mainstreaming protection for IDPs through combining community-based approaches (aimed at ensuring awareness and participation), ad hoc training and improved access to protection (referral) through existing, strengthened services. Protection mainstreaming is also ensured through coordination with institutional and humanitarian partners and through the incorporation of protection principles into aid delivery.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Helpcode AAP’s strategy abides to IASC 5 commitments, according to the following: Leadership - Promote a culture among staff of acceptance of failure and negative feedback from affected people. - Ensure that relevant staff and managers collect feedback from affected populations in appropriate age groups (sensitive to gender & age); respond to this feedback; and report back. - Work with local and national partners, so that there is a coordinated plan and capacity which is sensitive to gender, age, disability and ethnicity. Transparency - Provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that may impact communities in order to support informed decisions, and engage communities in a dialogue as part of information provisionParticipation - Work with relevant local and national partners and actors to support and influence effective design, assessment, implementation and monitoring of the response.- Engage all parts of a community, including those who are vulnerable or at risk to draft robust, contextual vulnerability and capacity analyses. Inclusivity is ensured through a thorough analysis of local social dynamics, mobility capacity of beneficiaries, differences in the use of time by women and men, identification of “role models” among community stakeholders that can promote social change.Feedback and complaints - Provide information to all in affected communities about the organisation, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave (including commitments made on PSEA), the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Communicate in languages, formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the community, especially vulnerable and marginalised groups.- Establish and maintain effective feedback systems, using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. The feedback is disaggregated by age and sex at minimum.Design, Monitoring and evaluation - Assess the implementation of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and other collective standards including the IASC AAP commitments through assessments of organizational performance on accountability to measure and improve accountability to all persons of concern.- Include persons of concern as partners throughout the operation's management cycle and report the results of assessments and follow up actions to them.-

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work with the District Services of Health and in coordination with the humanitarian partners who have developed guidelines and tools for ensuring disability-inclusive protection in education, in order to go beyond the minimum standards for disability inclusion in humanitarian action.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Among its internal procedure, Helpcode has developed an environmental policy aiming to avoid, minimize and mitigate the environmental impact of its activities in the field, in line with Sphere Humanitarian Standards and global environmental standard, guidelines and tools. In this respect, each project is assessed since design in terms of environmental impact. For the nature of the activities proposed, the project does not entails specific environmental concerns (as the project will work in spaces that already exist, therefore no construction activities are foreseen, nor distribution of materials). In any case, a quick environmental assessment will be carried out again at project kick off and at the end. It is also critical to highlight that the overall implementation context is affected by severe vulnerability to climate change and extreme climate events. In terms of mitigation / preparation, the project foresees to provide ad hoc information during awareness / training activities.
