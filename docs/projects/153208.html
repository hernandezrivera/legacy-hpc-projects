Project Summary

Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) will conduct life-saving WASH activities aimed at meeting the emergency critical needs of vulnerable people in South Sudan affected by conflict, natural disasters, disease outbreaks, and areas of high malnutrition rates. PAH is delivering relevant, effective and needs-based WASH interventions across South Sudan through mobile Emergency Preparedness and Response (EP&R) teams, static presence in cooperation with static partners and coordination with the WASH Cluster.
This project aims at providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases targeting IDP’s, host and the returnees. Interventions will include safe water provision through water point construction and rehabilitation (hand pumps, SWAT systems, water trucking, or provision of household water filters, depending on the needs); provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management); hygiene promotion (hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain and jerrycan cleaning campaigns); capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school's hygiene clubs), and distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, PUR or others).
Total targeted group is 250,000 beneficiaries. The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, poor sanitation coverage and support nutrition, health and education centers with WASH facilities (i.e. GAM rates of more than 27%).
The intervention will include detailed multisector needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies employed by the affected population. Based on the assessment report multisector interventions will be designed accordingly with PAH capacity (sectors of S/NFI, Protection, FSL), or with the support of the clusters coordination, response with other partners will be planned. The main priority is to provide emergency WASH necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH related diseases and limited access to water integrated with other sectors for holistic response.
PAH will use the Emergency Response Teams (ERT) and the static project to reach crisis affected vulnerable communities in difficult to reach locations as soon as areas become accessible. During this action, the assessment team will be deployed within 8 days of the clearance to respond by the EP&R partners and the WASH Cluster. The team can deploy to at least three locations at the same time with a team of WASH Technicians and Hygiene and Sanitation Officers.
Centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services for the displaced and other vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people and strengthening the application of cash – based programing will be part of the employed approach. PAH will implement capacity building of the community to reinforce coping mechanism to manage current shocks and sustain livelihoods, strengthen linkages with development action to support resilience.
PAH will focus on integrating activities with other sectors as Health, Nutrition, Education, FSL and Protection through support with WASH facilities like soap, hand washing facilities, improvement of waste management facilities, access to latrines and capacity building of community health, nutrition workers on WASH related disease preventive measures among others.

Needs

PAH activities are planned to target communities that are faced with crisis of limited access to safe water, poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, food insecurity resulting in malnutrition. Priority will be made for newly displaced populations due to conflict and natural disasters leaving in remote hard to reach areas, protracted IDPs living in collective centers and those integrated within host community. The ongoing violence throughout the country still causing displacement of community and the economic downturn which has put a further strain on already limited WASH services. Based on PAH data collected in 2018 some 90 percent of the population still do not have access to improved sanitation. Half of the population does not have access to improved water sources, with about 35 percent of the existing boreholes estimated to be non-functional. Open defecation is persistent in 61 percent of settlements in the country. Only 28 percent of the population has access to boreholes within 30 minutes. People living in PoC sites and collective centres depend on a consistent supply of safe water, sanitation and hygiene services, and the displaced communities living outside of PoCs are frequently deprived of WASH support due to access constrains and insecurity. Lack of water points brings risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when walking to remote water sources. In urban areas, people struggle to access safe water due to ever-rising prices. Findings in needs assessments conducted by PAH and other partners shows that, in most parts of the country, people are faced with lack of access to safe adequate water, poor hygiene practices and sanitation conditions, food insecurity making people prone to morbidity and mortality resulting from waterborne diseases and malnutrition, especially children under five. Recent needs assessments in Ikotos in Eastern Equatoria state on 12 October, and inter-agency fact finding mission report of Kuda, Sirimon and Rokon in central Equatoria on 4 October, both locations reported of a high hunger rates in the region, related to ongoing insecurity and erratic rainfalls in 2018 with many children and women reported malnourished. Recent report from UN has documented the large vulnerability of civilians in the Western Equatoria region where 900 people were abducted and 24,000 forced to flee their homes during a surge of violence between April and August (United Nations Human Rights) GENEVA / JUBA (18 October 2018). In addition, significant movement of returnees has been ongoing to areas destroyed by conflict in 2016 as in Pajok and Magwi county (PAH Magwi assessment report February 2018). Current report provided by OCHA estimated 63,000 IDPs returned home from Minkaman IDP settlements. Humanitarian partners are planning to provide support to sustain the returnees as most WASH facilities are destroyed and not in use. This adds pressure on existing facilities in towns with limited urban planning and capacity. As more returnees arrive, individual and household coping mechanism is stretched, particularly in the sector of food security and WASH. Such situation exposes people to a high risk of diseases outbreaks and increase in morbidity and mortality rate due to lack of sanitation, access to safe water and high rates of malnutrition. Thousands have not been reached by humanitarian assistance due to funding gaps, logistical challenges and insecurity. Reoccurring conflicts in Equatoria region, Jonglei, Upper Nile States continue to put additional pressure on the populations and might initiate further influx of IDPs in 2019. Mobile teams will continue to be one of the key actors to reach communities in emergencies and remote areas. Apart from mobile teams, PAH plans to continue static presence activities in Jonglei, Central and Eastern Equatoria state, and work with the partners on the ground.

Activities

1) Conducting WASH assessments (PAH’s, IRNA and/or coordinated with partners) in areas with chronic WASH needs, disaster and conflict prone areas, including gender specific needs. The assessments will be conducted in close coordination with the WASH cluster.2) Provision of timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected population and those at risk of disease outbreak through water points repair and rehabilitation to institutions like Health, Nutrition centres and schools to prevent malnutrition and WASH related diseases. Provision of water purification items (PUR and Aquatabs), construction of new water points through rig or manual drilling taking in to consideration the location and distance to women and girls in regards to protection. Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through (training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committee for resilience) water quality tests, both at water points and community level. PAH will be using sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. PAH has its own drilling equipment and team with geologist to conduct geophysics survey before drilling. Manual drilling tool has been developed by PAH technical team, which can be used in locations where manual drilling is feasible.3) Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions; women and school children will be the two groups PAH will mostly focus on while conducting household visits, focus group discussions, training for Community Hygiene Promoters, health workers, teachers, pupils, women group leaders. Activities will be conducted by PAH ERT and static teams. Schools/Nutrition/Health centres will be targeted for hygiene promotion.4) Enable vulnerable and affected population to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, construction of sanitation facilities to Education/Nutrition/Health centres, schools, with consideration for gender separated latrines. Construction of demonstration latrines for HH level using 100% local materials, training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community as an exit strategy.5) Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities.6) Cash programming will be considered, based on initial market and community assessments with focus on hardware component of WASH response, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Cash programming strategies for various sectors are currently developed with the involvement of PAH staff in Cash Working Group.7) PAH WASH projects will be integrated with other sectors when applicable. PAH mobile EP&R WASH team will work together with S/NFI and Protection mobile team, joining interventions where needed. In addition, integrated approach will be encouraged with FSL, S/NFI, Protection, Nutrition and Education. This will be encouraged both in mobile and static responses.8) Post-distribution (PDM), post-intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done by independent PAH M&E team, as long as the location are still accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs gives clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall quality of PAH’s projects.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Meeting with authorities and community members at early stage of project design and implementation to discuss project scope. Focus group discussions with various groups to collect feedback on the needs which is related to providing information on activities carried by PAH. Complaint/feedback system allows beneficiaries to approach PAH team member in case clarification or follow up is needed. This includes establishing help desk, supported by both men and women responsible for providing information to the community members. Interaction sessions during distributions, demonstrations and training. Using IEC materials for the promotion of key messages.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some messages are general for all population, depends on items provided. Specific sessions for women, girls, elders, people with disabilities, will be taking place to ensure safety and security during distributions and on the way to and from distribution site. Needs analysis will focus on ensuring dignity of the beneficiaries through provision of appropriate items and training relevant to different age groups and gender. Women and girls are directed with key messages on menstrual hygiene, though men are sensitized to break the stigma related to menstruation.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PAH has internal regulations on PSEA, with all employees signed commitment. PAH is also a member of the protection cluster and understands the modalities to ensure protection is mainstreamed within our activities. Key staff employed on field level (enumerators, crowd controllers, other dealing directly with beneficiaries) needs to sign the safety code of conduct. Training is provided with clarification on beneficiary abuse, reporting abuses, confidentiality and risk mitigation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Focus group discussions with women groups, HH visits and consultations on specific needs related to gender, age group, disability, also within various sectors. Individual interviews with community members, institutional users (Health facilities, Nutrition Centers, Schools, etc.) on needs, protection concerns, other. Community needs to be inclusive of a wide representation of people in an intervention. Equitable Representation must include: Men, Women, Elderly, Youth, Disabled, Ethnic minorities, the most Economically and/or Politically Marginalized people, Boys and Girls. In practice this is challenging with, for example, women only discussions monitored by community leaders. Ensuring that community gatekeepers are managed and allow fair representation is carefully considered by PAH implementing team. Protection issues that may arise are reviewed with protection team supporting project implementation at all levels.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

All PAH’s stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within PAH mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Men and women are systematically asked to share information about needs and cultural preferences. PAH tries to adjust the intervention accordingly. M&E team is monitoring the community engagement through questionnaire designed to monitor safety of beneficiaries during assistance delivery and AAP. Community members are supporting repairs and rehabilitation of boreholes, construction of latrines, hygiene promotion campaigns and other.

Yes

Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. In addition, PAH will prioritize needs and design all activities with the project interventions in line with the identified and verified needs. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analysed, prioritized with clear recommendations captured from the beneficiaries and later utilized to influence the intervention modalities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measure access of beneficiaries to assistance provided through complaint mechanism, tailored focus group discussions with women groups, youth groups, people with disability, elderly and other community members. Protection specific data collection tools are used by the monitoring team to gather valid information for lessons learnt and future recommendations.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. Monitoring team has protection related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is measured through focus group discussions with the beneficiaries in which the beneficiaries will express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction of the process. Complain desks will be established during activities of distribution, registration and verification to address complains in a dignified way. Meanwhile throughout the project period beneficiary feedback, complaints and means of addressing will be documented in a well-designed form and analysed by the independent monitoring and evaluation team.
