Project Summary

This WASH program targets a population of 18,000 people (3,185 women, 2,225 Men, 5,790 boys and 6,800 girls) who are mainly IDPs and vulnerable host community families in 3 districts of Merka, Qoryoley and Kurtunwaarey of Lower shabelle region. AYUUB proposes immediate WASH interventions to reduce the number of AWD/Cholera cases reported in these areas. The activities to be conducted include, construction of pit latrines with lockable doors and lighted at night, distribution of hygiene promotion kits, provision of safe water through construction and rehabilitation of shallow wells as well as chlorination and hygiene promotion training in villages, schools and OTP centers i.e hand washing with soap at five most critical times. All these activities will enhance access to adequate sanitation facilities especially to women and girls who are at risk of being violated due to lack of WASH facilities. AYUUB expects the Project response will meet with the beneficiaries needs and save lives of the targeted group as planned, the organization, by applying all experience gained from the previous projects, the project will achieve all set goals and objectives respectively.

Needs

Lack of access to adequate WASH services remains a challenge amongst the Somali population resulting to an increase of AWD/Cholera outbreaks. According to the situation report issued on October 2018 by WHO and MOH, Merka and Kurtunwarey districts in Lower Shabelle region have been reported places with new cases of AWD/cholera outbreaks. The ongoing clan conflict has led to massive displacement of people to IDP camps where WASH facilities are limited or even worse unavailable. Lack of these facilities has affected lives of children, women, elderly and disabled who are the most vulnerable in the community hence leading to an outbreak of diseases. This situation means children and women have to search for limited quantities of water; raising protection issues around safe sanitation access for women and girls who are at risk of being victimized when accessing sanitation facilities. The shallow wells in the camps have dried up forcing people to opt for unprotected water. The recent floods also contributed to an outbreak of AWD/Cholera and a number of latrines washed away by floods especially in Qoryooley district whereby the AS militants diverted river water to people’s homes which led to loss of livestock and displacements. There is an urgent need for WASH intervention in this region in order to save lives. This will target a population of 18,000 people (8,015 male & 9,985 female). Construction of additional pit latrines which are lockable and lighted at night with hand washing facilities (To prevent open defecation and protect girls and women from encountering sexual and GBV), provision of safe drinking water and door to door distribution of hygiene promotion kits will reduce the number of AWD/Cholera cases reported in the region. Hygiene promotion including hand washing with soap at the five critical times will be conducted in schools to ensure retention of children in schools and also in OTP/SC and health centers to reduce WASH related mortality/morbidity rates.

Activities

1. Construct 1,000 IDPs pit latrines lockable with Hand washing facilities and lighted at night for 34 IDP camps and communities in Lower Shabelle region. These latrines will be built separately for males and females and marked clearly according to sex for illiterate users.2. Door-door distribution of hygiene kits to 12,400 persons (7,250 female and 5,150 male) in the IDPs camps of Merka, Qoryooley and Kurtunwarey districts3. Train 250 community hygiene promoters (125 female and 125 male) on hygiene promotion including hand washing, water storage and collection, safe solid waste disposal to improve hygiene and sanitation in the IDP camps and the surrounding villages.4. Community hygiene promotion based on cultural and religious practices through hygiene promoters to mobilize 18,000 people (3,185 women, 2,225 Men, 5,790 boys and 6,800 girls) of vulnerable people in the IDP camps. 5. Chlorination of 100 water points to provide safe water to 18,000 people (3,185 women, 2,225 Men, 5,790 boys and 6,800 girls)6. Provision of safe water to 18,000 people (3,185 women, 2,225 Men, 5,790 boys and 6,800 girls) through Rehabilitation and construction of 40 shallow wells and 40 water tanks, in Lower Shabelle region.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and girls are discriminated or prioritized on issues of social economic development, this has made more discouraged the exclusion of certain activities, more worse girls are abused and violated in different occasions by different actors. In Somali, women are rarely represented in political movement. In some occasions many are forced to move from their communities and some also move pre-emptively for fear of violence being deliberately imposed on them. Schools, Health and WASH facilities serves as a safe space for children and is critical to ensuring that children displaced by or affected by drought and con ict do not only have access to limited services but are better able to cope, survive and recover from the crisis impacts and contribute to longer term peaceful development of Somalia. AYUUB organization has operational manual protecting gender right, all staff will be signed code of conduct, at the sametime AYUUB will ensure all people for all ages and gender are involved in all project process, girls and women will be prioritized considering the circumstance they posses.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

AYUUB has a risk and Mitigation Policy in place that will be applied during Project Implementation. The area where the project is planned to deliver the services is full control of SFG, there is no other actors can create armed clash, there can be small intra- clashes between SNA forces, but this will not be target to the beneficiaries of the project and AYUUB in applying its Risk assessment and mitigation policy will do mobilization to both beneficiaries and project staff in the respective area when need calls.Basically, the areas where the project will be carried out is fully controlled by SNA forces, as part of AYUUB policy which was revised after the AS banned NGOs in their held territories, the organization doesn’t deliver visible services like health and Nutrition in the AS controlled areas to avoid looting and staff detention, therefore, impact of armed conflicts will not be experienced in the services areas, except for scenarios like AS hit and run launch against SNA bases.To mitigate such insecurity risks, AYUUB has a good plan in the site selection of the facilities, by having WASH services and facilities where beneficiaries can access with ease and away from SNA bases or government facilities to avoid clashes as a result of conflict.
