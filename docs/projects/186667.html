Project Summary

DRC’s proposed protection activities for 2022, will build on the existing response in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states established since 2015. DRC aims to respond to and mitigate critical protection needs of vulnerable populations through community based protection approach, by building on its gains of its established community protection structures to response to needs of These include people living in formal/informal camps, host and returnee communities. DRC’s strong presence across 31 LGAs in NE Nigeria strengthened by its diverse programming and ability to aptly respond to emergencies over the years will provide an adequate backing for this proposed protection response. Protection programming will target extremely vulnerable persons and persons with protection concerns through a combination of protection related programming responses including; provision of immediate psychosocial support (PSS)services, psychological first aid (PFA) and community based counselling, individual and household level case management to address protection concerns, individualized protection assistance (IPA) to alleviate vulnerabilities, address environmental and remedial risks which expose individuals/ communities to protection risks through protection assessments, advocacy and coordination. DRC will continue to provide ,life-saving information to communities on services offered within their communities, give communities access to services through establishment or strengthening of referral pathways, provide protection related services through IPA, increase duty bearers capacity for a protection capacity for all. DRC coordination and collaboration efforts with key stakeholders will aim to strengthen service provision to beneficiaries whilst ensuring persons of concerns have access to their entitlements and rights while engaging institutions and actors to enhance their duty bearer commitments. Protection centres/ mobile desks will ensure communities feel safe by embracing protection by presence which will ensure protection services are available to communities throughout the year. The centres will provide access to crucial protection related services for communities situated within accessible distances. Which include referral services for gender-based violence (GBV), child protection (CP), housing, land and property (HLP), and general protection services (documentation, other human rights violations). DRC will carryout protection monitoring assessments which will identify protection risks and feed this information back into programming throughout the implementation period to strengthen the response. However crucial advocacy initiatives will be derived from this information to influence duty bearers so that they improve the protective environment of IDPs, returnees and host communities as DRC addresses protection concerns and mitigation of risks.. DRC intends to reach an estimated 61,934 HH (371,785 individuals) (60% female and 40%male beneficiaries will be targeted) in 17 LGAs across the BAY states with general conflict affected communities to improve their psychological and social wellbeing. DRC’s global age and gender diversity policy to programming enhances gender effective programming informed by analysing, application and accommodation of the different needs of diverse categories age, gender, vulnerability, ethnicity, status of health, economic status.protection programming in the next year. Main protection activities will include; IPA andcase management for protection cases, awareness on specific protection related themesguided by the findings of protection monitoring activities, creation or strengthening ofreferral pathways, providing information on services provided and important human rightsawareness, collecting beneficiary feedback through protection desks and clinics,promotion of human rights through advocacy, strengthening the protection environment of persons of concern through coordination and stakeholder engagement,capacity building

1 - Needs

The conflict in Northeast Nigeria has left 10.6 million persons in need of life saving humanitarian assistance. Across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) States protection concerns are blatantly dominating as the primary need of populations of concern. The Northeast conflict has perpetuated a protracted protection crisis with the COVID-19 pandemic further compounding these concerns. The response requires a multifaceted tailored response. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable due to strict gender roles and societal expectations that equally contribute to vulnerabilities and subsequent abuses. Culturally, girls are not encouraged to pursue education, and those who do rarely continue beyond primary school. The Protection Sector recognizes that the majority of GBV cases go unreported due to stigma, shame, and concerns of safety by the survivor. DRC recorded over 4,077 protection cases varying from early/forced marriage, abduction, killings, maiming or mutilation and exploitation in 2021 (Jan - November). This demonstrates a clear need to provide an all-inclusive protection programming encapsuling protection, CP, GBV and PSS services to ensure broader provision of life saving assistance. Borno is the epicenter of the conflict in the Northeast where the largest proportion of the affected population reside. Reports of violence are recorded across all LGAs in Borno. However, in recent months attacks have been concentrated in and around Monguno, Bama, Banki community, Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza including Pulka camps, Askira Uba, Chibok, Konduga, Gajiram, Gajigana, Damboa. Displacements to the urban centers in recent years has resulted in over congestion in camp locations and a draining of resources in host communities. The action of the BSG to return IDPs to their areas of origin which farm centers, Bakassi camps are officially closed with plans for all official camps closed by 31st December,2021.Households are being returned without suitable return conditions being met. Increasing the need for direct protection activities to be made available in existing displacement sites and the new return locations. In Yobe displaced populations arrive from inaccessible areas, some in Yobe, Northern Borno as well and neighboring Niger to Gujba, Damaturu, Gashua, and Geidam. Across Yobe, 2.2 million people, out of a 3.5 million, are in need of humanitarian assistance. Adamawa and Yobe States face similar challenges with the concerned population characterized as integrated IDPs as they live within the host and returnee communities. In the most in need LGAs - Gujba, Gulani, Bursari, Geidam LGAs and Madagali, Michika, Mubi and Yola LGAs. These locations host IDPS living in formal/informal settlements and camps and face recurrent natural disasters resulting in the destruction of road networks, property, and barriers in access to essential resources. Although service providers offer some form of protection programming within designated LGAs, there are noticeable gaps. DRC’s regular service mapping identified that although there is presence of some PSS and GBV providers, as well as information dissemination on protection principles and reporting mechanisms these services do not reach all communities even those with observable need for assistance. There is a significant number of communities without access to shelter, income generation opportunities, adequate identifying documentation, experiencing other protection risks associated with violence, extortion, lack of access to justice and continual loss of property, loss of financial capacity and in constant threat of safety as access to services remains a wish list for the the displaced population.

2 - Activities

DRC will continually update its stakeholder mapping aimed at ensuring direct engagement, coordination, system strengthening of the protection response. Through capacity building targeting protection and other sector actors to reinforce the protective environment of beneficiaries. Targeted stakeholders may include; ministry of women and social affairs, community leaders, traditional/ religious leaders and community volunteers (known community role models who continually represent community concerns and act as conflict mediators) and local authorities. An estimated 1200 stakeholders will be targeted. They will be trained on protection principles, mainstreaming and survivor/ victim centered approaches, human rights approach, community-based counselling, Psychosocial first aid, PSEA and Core humanitarian standards and referral pathways and protection concerns identification and response. DRC will continue to build on capacity building of community structures, 500 community-based protection group members will be trained on protection topics including basic case management and counselling including PFA, PSEA, mediation and conflict resolution strategies, problem solving, COVID-19 guidelines, complaint and feedback mechanisms and, small business management. The trainings will strengthen their ability to provide community-based protection services to community members using a community-based model. Members of the community will be nominated as community representatives and act as important protection services decentralization points as they are accessible to community members throughout the year every day of the week as they live within the community. They will champion awareness activities as they are well versed in understanding the context and know the trends of protection risks. DRC will familiarize the groups on referrals pathways and basic case identification in order to provide support to persons with severe/ urgent protection concerns in the community. The groups/ committees will develop community advocacy initiatives aimed at initiating behavioral change. Through the 30 protection desks/ centres, DRC will identify vulnerable persons with protection concerns and provide IPA, PSS, mediation and referrals to services. DRC IPA may include an element of in kind or in cash support guided by cash voucher assistance DRC guidelines. The protection desks/ centres will act as information dissemination points and PSS safe spaces for counselling activities. DRC’s PSS interventions will fall within the bottom three categories of the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (2007). DRC will target, adolescent girls, women, youth and vulnerable persons for PSS services and mentorship programmes including direct counselling services and group PSS such as sports, art therapies, games, community level skills building on life skills and literacy for adults and adolescents girls and boys who cannot access formal education. DRC’s Protection monitoring strategy will focus on gathering crucial information on the protection risks faced by individuals through the cases reported to DRC on the protection desks and through various community engagement activities including focus group discussions, key informant interview, household surveys and community meetings. The data gathered from this daily and monthly activities will be compiled into reports which will then inform awareness activities, advocacy strategies and response strategies. Coupled with DRC awareness activities 130,614 individuals (78,368 female, 52,245,514 male) will be targeted.
