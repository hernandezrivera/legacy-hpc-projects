Project Summary

UNICEF partners with government agencies, the private sector, and both international and local NGOs to provide critical Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services to vulnerable displaced populations in camps, host communities, and returnee areas. The project prioritizes immediate WASH access for newly displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups, including children, women, girls, and individuals with disabilities. UNICEF will deliver rapid emergency assistance to address the needs of those affected by sudden population movements, ensuring that appropriate WASH facilities are available in healthcare settings and educational environments.
The project emphasizes the participation of affected and at-risk populations in designing interventions and adopts gender-sensitive and inclusive approaches to reduce the risks of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), while ensuring accessibility for people with disabilities. Even in an acute emergency response, it includes efforts to build local capacity for the operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure and strengthens supply chains through private sector engagement.
In the event of disease outbreaks, such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Cholera, UNICEF will respond quickly by providing WASH services in close coordination with the health sector. The organization will also bolster multisectoral efforts to reduce WASH-related risks by collaborating with the Child Protection, Health and Nutrition, and Education sectors.
As the lead in the WASH Sector, UNICEF will oversee service delivery by facilitating platform agreements on approaches, identifying service gaps, advocating for vulnerable groups, responding to urgent needs, establishing monitoring and evaluation systems, and advancing knowledge management.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will provide emergency WASH services to 239,963 people displaced by conflict in Cabo Delgado. UNICEF will work in partnership with government, LNGOs, local NGOs and private sector to ensure that children and their communities have equitable access to lifesaving safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services, while promoting the adoption of safe hygiene practices. The provision of emergency WASH services will target vulnerable displaced populations in returnee areas, camps, and host communities, with a particular emphasis on life-saving interventions and disease prevention. The response will employ a variety of intervention strategies tailored to the specific needs of the IDP population, with a focus on immediate humanitarian relief while keeping in mind longer term solutions. A rapid response mechanism will also be activated to address the immediate needs of newly displaced populations, ensuring that assistance is provided quickly and efficiently. To support this response, UNICEF will procure and preposition WASH supplies, ensuring that partnerships are in place for the timely distribution of these materials as needs arise. These efforts will be closely coordinated with other sectors such as protection, health, nutrition, and education, with the goal of ensuring a coordinated, holistic humanitarian response and enhancing the resilience of affected communities to future crises. UNICEF will prioritize IDPs, including newly displaced individuals and returnees, in the northern districts of Cabo Delgado, as well as in other areas impacted by attacks and displacement. Priority districts for intervention will include Mocimboa da Praia, Quissanga, and Macomia, where ongoing conflict has left communities in urgent need of assistance. Support will also be extended to districts with high numbers of IDPs from previous displacements, such as Ancuabe and Metuge. Special attention will be given to at- high risk groups, including women, girls and people with disabilities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Emergency access to safe water will be ensured through a range of activities, including the rapid rehabilitation of existing water points, the emergency extension of small water networks, the drilling of boreholes equipped with handpumps, and the emergency construction of new water supply systems. Water treatment will involve purification methods such as chlorination and filtration to ensure the water is safe for consumption. Regular monitoring of water quality will be carried out to maintain safety standards. To ensure the longer-term sustainability of water systems following the initial lifesaving response water committees will be established to oversee their operation, maintenance, and payment mechanisms. Access to dignified, inclusive emergency sanitation will be addressed by constructing gender-sensitive toilets, with separate facilities for men, women, and children, as well as emergency communal latrines in resettlement areas. Latrine maintenance will include desludging, safe sludge disposal, and the decommissioning of latrines when necessary. Community engagement will encourage the construction of household latrines (emergency adapted SANTOLIC), with a particular focus on providing solutions for individuals with disabilities. Handwashing stations with soap and water will be installed near toilets and communal areas to promote hygiene. Additionally, proper waste disposal practices will be implemented to prevent contamination of water sources by human waste. UNICEF will focus on critical hygiene promotion through community engagement, aiming for behavior change. Gender-specific and disability-inclusive hygiene awareness campaigns will ensure that all age groups are reached. Hygiene kits, including soap and menstrual hygiene products, will be distributed to those in need. Community participation will be actively encouraged in the planning and implementation of WASH interventions to ensure sustainability. The project will also mainstream gender and inclusion to ensure the active participation of vulnerable groups, such as girls, women, and persons with disabilities

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Safety audits will be conducted during the initial phases to identify potential protection risks across different gender and age groups. These audits will provide a platform for discussing strategies to mitigate these risks with affected populations. Preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, while adhering to the principle of "do no harm," is central to UNICEF's mandate. Sexual exploitation and abuse are considered serious misconduct for both UNICEF staff and non-staff personnel, with disciplinary actions, including summary dismissal, for violations. Any entities working with UNICEF that fail to address sexual exploitation and abuse—through adequate prevention measures, investigations, and corrective actions—will face termination of their cooperative agreements. To ensure strong safeguards and effective responses, UNICEF’s procedures include rigorous screening, awareness-raising, monitoring, and training of partners. UNICEF will systematically engage with affected communities, with particular attention to women and girls. Rapid needs assessments will focus on identifying water point locations, designing and adapting bathing and laundry facilities, prioritizing hygiene items, and tailoring key hygiene messages to the local context. Feedback mechanisms will be established to ensure communities have control over the response and its impact on them. Special efforts will be made to ensure that high-risk groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities, feel safe and are protected from discrimination and gender-based violence (GBV) when collecting water or using toilets and bathing facilities

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project demonstrates a strong commitment to gender mainstreaming by integrating gender and age considerations throughout all phases. It seeks to address the specific needs of women, girls, and elderly individuals, particularly in relation to WASH facilities, ensuring that they are safe, accessible, and sensitive to privacy and security concerns. The participation of the most vulnerable groups, such as women and the elderly, will be prioritized at all stages of the project, especially during the needs assessment and design phase. This will ensure the appropriate identification of water point locations, the design and adaptation of bathing and laundry facilities, the selection of priority hygiene items for hygiene kits, and the creation of key hygiene messages that are tailored to the local context. Feedback mechanisms will be stablished to give most vulnerable people more control over the response and impact on them. Incorporating gender-balanced, trained water committees for decision-making and facility management will ensure equal participation of both men and women, empowering them to assume leadership roles. Furthermore, the collection of gender and age-disaggregated data will allow for tracking the project’s impact, ensuring it meets the needs of all community members effectively.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance through a multi-faceted approach that ensures inclusivity and accessibility at all stages of implementation. Even in the emergency rapid response, community engagement will involve consultations with local leaders, disability organizations, and the affected population to identify individuals with disabilities and understand their specific needs. By gathering this information, the project can ensure that persons with disabilities are included in the planning and distribution processes from the outset. To remove barriers to access, the project will design inclusive infrastructure, such as WASH facilities that are physically accessible to people with mobility challenges. This could include ramps, handrails, and wide doors to ensure ease of access. Additionally, sensory needs will be addressed by providing visual or auditory aids where necessary, such as clear signage with tactile or pictorial representations. Training for staff and partners will focus on raising awareness of the rights and needs of people with disabilities, ensuring that all personnel are sensitive to issues of accessibility and inclusion. Lastly, feedback mechanisms will be put in place, allowing people with disabilities to share their concerns about access to services. These measures will help ensure that all individuals, regardless of their physical abilities, have equal access to the assistance provided by the project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project will have both direct and indirect environmental impacts, particularly related to the design and implementation of WASH facilities, water resource management, and construction activities. These activities may result in changes to local ecosystems, water usage patterns, and waste management systems, which need to be carefully monitored and managed to minimize negative environmental effects.To address these potential impacts, when possible environmental assessments (EAs) will be conducted prior to the implementation phase. These assessments will evaluate the potential environmental risks and identify specific measures to mitigate any adverse effects. For example, the construction of WASH facilities will consider sustainable materials and low-impact building techniques to reduce environmental degradation. Additionally, the project will ensure that water resources are used sustainably, promoting water conservation practices and preventing the overexploitation of local water sources. Environmental Safeguard Standards (ESS) will be integrated into the project’s design and operational guidelines. This includes ensuring that all infrastructure is built with a focus on sustainability and resilience to environmental changes. Waste management systems will be put in place to prevent contamination, especially in areas with high vulnerability to waterborne diseases. The project will also assess and promote the use of renewable energy sources, such as solar power for water pumping and sanitation facilities, to minimize its carbon footprint. Mitigation actions will focus on reducing waste generation, protecting water quality, and enhancing local biodiversity. Additionally, ongoing monitoring will be conducted to ensure that environmental safeguards are adhered to, and the impact of project activities on the environment is continuously assessed and addressed. By incorporating these measures, the project aims to align with ESS requirements and minimize its environmental footprint while ensuring that WASH services are provided in a sustainable and environmentally responsible manner.
