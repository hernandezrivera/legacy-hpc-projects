Project Summary

Mine Action has been identified as one of the key areas of concern within the Protection Cluster as civilian casualty rates continue to increase. This has already clearly been reflected in casualty data collected and analysed by the Danish Refugee Council-Danish Demining Group (DRC-DDG) since the start of the conflict. As reflected in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, three major interventions are crucially required: (1) humanitarian demining, to allow the contaminated land and infrastructure being put back into productive use, sustaining jobs and livelihoods whilst directly supporting existing DRC-DDG humanitarian initiatives in the Livelihoods and Legal sectors; (2) Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), preventing injuries and saving lives for individuals still living in conflict-affected communities; (3) capacity building of state Mine Action (MA) institutions through the provision of training and equipment as well as national-level advocacy on MA legislation, standards, and frameworks. With this three-fold approach, the project will significantly contribute to all three HRP 2021 objectives.

1 - Needs

As to the (1) humanitarian demining, Ukraine ranks amongst the five most severely affected places in the world for casualties as a result of mines (Landmine Monitor Report 2019). On a monthly basis, the number of civilians injured or killed by these hazards regularly surpasses those resulting directly from active conflict (UNHCHR). A 2019 report placed Ukraine as the number two in the world (only behind Mali) for anti-vehicle mine casualties during 2015–2018. Ukraine accounted for 15% of all global casualties during that period (GICHD: Global Mapping and Analysis of Anti-Vehicle Mine Incidents in 2018). Ukraine does not have a centralised mine accident reporting mechanism and database for logging mine casualties, therefore, DRC-DDG has taken the initiative to collect this information from a variety of credible sources for the past six years. DRC-DDG has recorded a total of 1,753 mine casualties, resulting in 1,192 injured and 561 killed from June 2014 to September 2020 in both GCA and NGCA. This makes contamination with mines/ERW/IEDs a serious protection concern. According to DDG’s primary survey data, 96% of contaminated land was formerly income generating, pointing to the fact that the presence of contamination is directly suppressing economic activity via limited movements, denied hunting, fishing, foraging, and firewood gathering as well as psychologically impacting the communities with such fear, depression, tensions. Preliminary estimates by the Government of Ukraine show that about 8% of the lands—totalling at 7,000 km2—recently brought back under its control in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts contain or are suspected to contain mines. A 2019 REACH Assessment, covering approximately 69,000 people living in 53 hard-to-reach settlements (43 rural and ten urban settlements), found that 33% of residents reported a mine incident in their settlement and 27% knew a mine victim. It is, therefore, no surprise that 41% of households reported mines affecting their everyday lives and 24% cited mines as their most common perceived security risk. In 2019, the Protection Cluster concluded that EORE remained inadequate for the scale of the Ukrainian needs. The UN OCHA Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2020 likewise concluded that “explosive ordnance risk education remains insufficient to the scale of needs.” This points to the importance of (2) EORE. A detailed analysis based on the data captured in the DRC-DDG casualties database shows that: males between 35 and 59 years of age are the most at-risk groups for mine/ERW accidents; discounting military activities, traveling, picking up, tampering, handling, or playing with mine/ERWs most often result in incidents, followed by household work, construction, and foraging; landmines represent almost half of all incidents, followed by booby traps, Abandoned Explosive Ordnance (AXOs), and Unexploded Ordnance (UXOs). These findings clearly demonstrate the need for continued live-saving EORE provision to frontline communities, aimed at increasing knowledge and motivate behaviour change. Unfortunately, Ukraine has not received the corresponding level of donor attention, albeit requiring (3) demining capacity building and equipment to tackle a challenge of such proportion. The very essence of the problem in Ukraine is not the lack of human capacity, nor the lack of will to get the job done; it is the lack of a framework and methodology around which to do so. It is, clearly, the role of the international community to support this process intelligently and effectively, ensuring that eventually the government of Ukraine will take the ownership of the issue. Targeted investments are, therefore, key to avoid a more protracted and costly intervention later.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Activity 1: Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, Direct Sessions. Output: Increased safety and security of school-aged children, conflict-affected communities, and IDPs. Outcome: Higher awareness about the risks associated with mines/ERW. Attitudes are effectively changed to encourage less risky behavior and civilian casualties decrease. Activity 2: Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, Training of Trainers. Output: Personnel from schools and communities within the target area are trained to provide EORE, creating a more sustainable capacity. Outcome: Higher awareness about the risks associated with mines/ERW. Attitudes are effectively changed to encourage less risky behaviour and civilian casualties decrease. Activity 3: Land Released. Output: Land and infrastructure is cleared of landmines and ERW. Outcome: Fewer civilian casualties, income generation opportunities are increased, resilience of conflict-affected communities is restored. IDPs are able to safely return. Activity 4: Non-Technical Survey. Output: Eastern Ukraine continues to be systematically surveyed in order to accurately quantify the scale and scope of the threat. Hazardous areas are mapped, analysed and prioritised according to humanitarian impact. Outcome: Existing capacity can be deployed more effectively according to the humanitarian needs. Ukraine is cleared of landmines and ERW in a more efficient and well-coordinated manner. Activity 5: Training of national institutions. Output: State institutions are trained in key skills such as information management, manual demining, EOD, task mapping, MRE. Outcome:The government of Ukraine is better positioned to tackle the issue of landmines and ERW in a unified, standardised and transparent manner. Activity 6: Advocacy interventions at the national level. Output: Government of Ukraine is encouraged to bring about much-needed legislative reform in the demining sector. Outcome: A national mine action centre is created leading to improved and more effective coordination of mine action.
