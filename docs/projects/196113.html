Project Summary

Food Security Sector (FSS) helps to strengthen the Nutrition and Health of the Rohingya refugees and the host communities of the Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila of Cox’s Bazar district. Under the ongoing global climate crisis and rapidly changing local climate, an effective FS programme reduces climate vulnerabilities and protection risks and enhances peace and stability.
BRAC's Food Security Sector in Cox's Bazar will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach, incorporating development nexuses as the crisis enters a protracted mode. As per the sectoral objective 02, BRAC FS sector will provide support to develop the food security resiliency of 20,800 Rohingya refugees through climate-sensitive food production in the camps. Moreover, to promote sectoral objective number 03, BRAC will work on the adjacent host community to strengthen food security of the 216,860 individuals of host communities' households through climate-smart agricultural production, agro-processing, creating market linkages, resilient income opportunities and disaster response preparedness with early actions. BRAC has piloted and developed a “Resilience Framework” considering its impact on host communities in Ukhiya. While developing this matrix, BRAC ensured the participation of all age and gender groups and persons with disabilities from the targeted communities. Furthermore, to achieve sectoral objective 04, BRAC will work to improve the socio-economic status of the host and local community by enhancing the capacity to manage natural resources sustainably and address climate change impacts in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila. The activities will enhance the livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host communities (HC), promote the self-reliance of Rohingya refugees, and strengthen natural resource management of Cox's Bazar's affected areas. BRAC's response will include technical capacity building of the affected communities/persons of concern. These could be homestead gardening, livestock services, fisheries, and inputs/service support.
In addition, agricultural and environmental activities will consider mitigation of the ongoing climate crisis in an integrated fashion. For example, crops that can withstand the local climate and salinity will be encouraged to grow here, considering local food habits and culture.
From the learning of responding to this crisis since 2017, BRAC will focus on sectoral integration and prioritize localization by developing the capacity of local NGOs, CBOs, youth clubs, and government stakeholders through training and supporting them in implementation activities. BRAC will also work for land stabilization, environmental restoration, and local awareness raising to protect the climate, natural resources and local ecology. BRAC aims to emphasize building resilience against environmental risks and hazards. The FS programme will have a contingency plan to protect and replenish agricultural products so that the affected households and individuals most adversely affected by the influx can recover and rebuild their food security and livelihoods.
Thirteen percent of the project resources will be allocated for cash transfer interventions, including unconditional cash transfer and cash-for-work (e.g., plantation, nursery preparation) in the host community. BRAC will aim to cover 90% of beneficiaries to be adult females from both communities, prioritizing widows, female-headed families, transgender, LGB communities, people with long-term illnesses, GBV/SGBV victims and persons with disabilities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Food and Nutrition security of Rohingya and the people of the host community is a growing concern, especially in the current global context. With limited or no access to self-reliance or income-generating opportunities, the refugees remain highly reliant on humanitarian assistance. Almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to food security and livelihoods (JMSNA, Oct 2021). The Resilience Index Measurement Analysis (RIMA) study conducted by FAO/WFP found that about half of the Rohingya households were found with acceptable food consumption patterns. Among the remaining half, 44% have borderline consumption. The male-headed households are experiencing a better consumption pattern than the female-headed households in both the host and the refugee community (RIMA, 2022). Moreover, given the global food crisis, funding shortage, and donors'’ fatigue over this protracted crisis, their lives are at risk due to limited service offers, especially in FSS and Nutrition sectors. Since the Rohingya influx in 2017, there has been increasing economic pressure and intense competition in the host community for local resources, like in the food labour market. The host communities already living below the poverty line have struggled to meet their basic needs, as most have lost their livelihoods due to the influx. These are local farmers, native foragers, and fishing communities. The study of Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) on chronic food insecurity (CFI) analysis of Bangladesh observed that in Cox’s Bazar, a significant portion (70%) of the people is in food insecurity either mild, moderate, or severe. Among them, 25% are moderately and at least 10% people are severely food insecure (CFI, 2022). In addition, the impact of Covid-19 and local commodity price hikes caused by the global crisis pushed them further into destitution. Therefore, it is vitally important for the Host Community to have access to alternative income to reduce vulnerability. Refugees and host communities have strong patriarchal cultures, limiting women's participation in income generation, leadership and decision-making. In addition, food allocation for refugees is based on family size instead of the differential needs in a family, especially of children, pregnant, lactating women, growing children and Persons with Disabilities. As a result, these vulnerable groups have more nutritional deficiency and related diseases than their male counterparts. In both communities, the major dietary share goes to the male members of the family, while inadequate amounts of food consumption, poor dietary diversity and hierarchy in families keeping male members at the top of the consumption pyramid are causing malnutrition, especially among children, pregnant and lactating mothers and girls. Therefore, despite targeting women and girls, they still lag in food security and the nutritional deficiency (Gender & intersectionality report October 2020, UNHCR). In synchronization with sector findings (JMSNA 2020), BRAC's need assessments have also found that COVID-19 has exacerbated these inequalities for women, children and other vulnerable groups - elderly and persons with disabilities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This proposed programme is aligned with the rationalization principles of the FSS. The programme will ensure that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across all the camps. This will lead towards a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugees and also ensure a minimum standard of service delivery by different partners. BRAC will follow an integrated approach to ensure effective and impactful outcomes in project design and implementation. BRAC has been responding to the crisis since 2017. The learning and experience of the last five years show that an integrated approach incorporating relevant sectors like Nutrition, Protection, Education and WASH will have a greater impact on the response of Rohingya and host communities. Moreover, BRAC has been ensuring stability in the refugee operation and improving relations with refugees, the Government and other partners in the camps. As a result, BRAC is considering multi-year funding, especially for critical activities that may require sustained longer-term engagement in Food Security. In addition, BRAC is committed to serving in the FSS in the same areas for several years, which is mentioned in its 05 years humanitarian action plan that will support strengthening relations with government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners in the area. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and coordination meetings. BRAC regularly shares the 5W reports with the FSS, which helps them to understand the types of activities provided by BRAC, as well as to help understand the service gaps. BRAC complies with the FSS guideline, and strictly maintains avoiding any duplication, overlapping in the intervention areas. Notably, BRAC has a strong government Liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals from the government promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended negative effects of any programme. BRAC ensures meaningful access, participation and empowerment of its beneficiaries while its programme design, planning and implementation embed accountability at all levels. Additionally, BRAC's protection and harassment policies are stringently followed, and staff and volunteers are trained on safeguarding, prevention and reporting on GBV/SGBV before deployment. BRAC beneficiaries and volunteers provide feedback through different channels, which allow us to improve the programme quality and prevent or mitigate situations.

3.2 - Gender

Ensuring Gender equality is the core focus of BRAC's strategy. Considering how gender dynamics can facilitate or obstruct an FSS theory of change, BRAC will conduct periodical community-based inclusive approaches (e.g. PRA) and gender analysis, using sex, age and disability disaggregated data to analyse and tailor to food security needs, understand capacities, barriers and the differential food and nutrition requirements for different groups, especially vulnerable groups of women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and female-headed households. BRAC will strive to address the barriers faced by vulnerable genders, mainly women and girls, older people, LGBTQ, and persons with disabilities by sensitising the male members of the communities through community male leaders and volunteers. Through the programmes, they will be linked to critical services in protection and health, ensuring equal representation and prioritisation of the most vulnerable members of the communities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

While the concept of safety itself has broader implications, safeguarding will be conceived as protecting against abuse, sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, intimidation, and violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect and exploitation. BRAC has a strong safeguarding policy including child safeguarding. BRAC has safeguarded its participants and employees in the first place by making their safety a primary consideration at the level of the design of programmes and enterprises. The prevention of sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, intimidation, violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect and exploitation is, as far as possible, built into the work practices. Where the design and its implementation cannot reduce risks sufficiently, BRAC has established secondary safeguarding mechanisms, like selecting and training personnel, supervising, monitoring, establishing multiple reporting channels, investigation, and punishment.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC always believes in diversity in the project or workplace. A wide variety of activities will be conducted with the support of a diversified group of people. All age, gender, ethnicity, race, and religion-diversified children will be eligible to enrol in the learning centres though the most vulnerable will be prioritised, including disabilities. Regarding staff selection, everyone has the opportunity to be a staff regardless of gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, or disabilities irrespectively. A broader spectrum of diversity will be promoted through activities and policy.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is an active commitment to use power responsibly by taking into account giving account to and being held to account by the people humanitarian organisations seek to assist. BRAC will provide beneficiaries with a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory arrangements for diverse communities. Besides this, BRAC will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently. BRAC will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities and special needs through the programme activities, which include the participation of the persons with disabilities, and taking the opinion during the designing & planning of any activities in the programme and ensuring the transparency of the programme.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC emphasizes building resilience to disasters and emergencies and will have a contingency plan to replenish agriculture input for households and individuals most adversely affected. Considering the disaster risk of the area, this programme will provide DRR training to volunteers, and community members thus they can act as early responders to any disaster. Bangladesh is at the forefront of the climate crisis, and the influx has caused an immense impact on its local reserved forests, ecosystem, and animals which are already on the endangered list due to human activities. The water tables have depleted to an alarmingly low level, and the forests have been shaved off. Such impact has caused flash flooding and crop failure. Therefore, BRAC’s FSS programme will give high priority to integrated programming incorporating environmental restoration and recovery by planting vetiver for land stabilization and plantation for environmental restoration.
