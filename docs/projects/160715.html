Project Summary

Nile Hope currently operates in Akobo, Canal/Pigi, New Fangak, Nasir and Leer Counties.
In 2020, through this project, Nile Hope will aim to scale up nutrition service delivery by
increasing coverage through setting up additional outreach and static sites in these
counties. By doing so, we will work towards screening 144,459 children under the age of
five and 128,422 Pregnant and Lactating women. Through SAM and MAM treatment, the
goal will be to reach 10,614 and 17,691 children U5 respectively. 13,860 pregnant and
lactating women will be targeted for treatment and 185 U5 children targeted for inpatient
management of acute malnutrition. Furthermore, to embrace the shift from treatment to
preventive nutrition, 79,950 caregivers will be targeted with MIYCN counseling which will
also incorporate the aspect of growth monitoring and promotion and early childhood
development. In 2020, Nile Hope will seek to try a mix of cascade model and mother
support groups in the different locations to assess the effectiveness of both in its
operational areas. Since GMP has not been a strong component, strengthening will be
required by having it incorporated in routine nutrition activities such as MIYCN counseling.
This will be a key strategy in helping in the early identification and aversion of nutrition
cases that would have ended up in SAM and MAM Programs. There will be kitchen
gardens in all OTP’s, an initiative that will be led by the lead mothers of MTMSG’s with the
guidance of the nutrition staff. Cooking demonstrations will also be held on a routine
basis, with most of the the cooking ingredients (vegetables) coming from the kitchen
gardens that will be set up. This will serve as motivation to the mothers tending to the
gardens to ensure success. Community mobilization and defaulter tracking by the CNV’s
and Community mobilizers shall be further strengthened. Fresh mapping of all operational
areas will be done and additional recruitment of the CNV’s will be undertaken if need be.
This will ensure an improvement in initial and continued utilization of nutrition services
within Nile Hope catchment areas. Ensuring program success will hinge majorly upon
strengthening the monitoring and evaluation aspect. Mentoring and coaching by project
managers and site supervisors and frequent field visits by program coordinators will be
done frequently. Strengthening integration of the nutrition program with with other sectors
such as FSL, Health, GBV and WASH shall be done by improving linkages and
establishing strong referral pathways. Partnering with the FSL team shall improve
success rates with the kitchen garden exercise. Since Nile Hope already has existing
agreements with WFP and UNICEF, continuous and adequate access to the core pipeline
of nutrition commodities is sorted. However, the Organization will still rely on the Logs
Cluster for delivery support to the ground, as has been the practice in previous years. Nile
Hope will strongly ensure a continuum of care and adhere to the principles of CMAM in
totality while observing sphere standards and humanitarian principles. There will equally
be a strong component of micronutritient supplementation through NIDs and where that
cannot be done like has been the issue with Pigi Canal in the past, permission shall be
sort to implement facility based vitamin A supplementation, deworming, Zinc, Iron
supplementation and if applicable, MNP's.

1 - Needs

According to the August IPC analysis, Akobo and Nasir are projected to be in Phase 4come April 2020. Flood related crop destruction, poor food accessibility due to adegradation in the road infrastructure brought about by heavy rains and inflation in theprice of basic commodities in the market are part of the factors that are bound tocontribute to the worsening of the situation come 2020. Food prices in the market are highas most products come across the border from Ethiopia through Gambella. As it iscurrently, the infrastructural weaknesses in Akobo contribute to higher transportationcosts of nutrition commodities, with porters periodically hiking prices. With the extensivegeographical coverage we have, this leads to an ever-burgeoning cost of transport thatproves to be unsustainable in the long run. Clan fights, revenge killings and fear of attacksfrom the neighboring Murle community are a constant threat to normal programmingactivities in Akobo. In 2019, we have had to close some of our facilities in areas wherethese occurrences erupt severally as staff and community members tend to flee the areas.With the high caseloads, this tends to have a negative impact as beneficiaries miss out onthe much-needed lifesaving nutrition services. As the peace in the country flourishes, theinflux of returnees from the neighboring Ethiopia and from other counties within SouthSudan is expected to continue. This is expected to place much more strain to the weakhealth and nutrition system that is currently in place. In April 2019, IMC carried out aSMART survey that indicated an alarming GAM rate of 24.3%, with SAM at 6.7%. That,coupled with August IPC data, puts Pigi in phase 4 come April 2020, going on to show thatthe situation is only going to persist or get worse. The food security situation is alsoforecasted to be on a downward trajectory due to flood related crop destruction whichmeans poor harvests are to be expected. This, in addition to limited access to markets, willmean that households will run out of food at a faster rate. Pigi/Canal also grapples withan increased risk of outbreaks of cholera and diarrheal diseases due to a fragile WASHinfrastructure where open defecation and reliance on the swamp for all their water needs(bathing, drinking water for humans and animals) is the norm. A wide geographicallandscape and challenges in accessibility in most parts is also a problem. Pigi featured ina report released by REACH (June 2019), as one of the top 5 counties where protectionconcerns were reported for both men and women. Exploitation of girls and women andburglary are often reported as the common threat. Hunger continues to persist in NewFangak due to a host of factors. A weak market structure exists that is overly reliant ontrade routes from Malakal and Tunja. These same trade routes are often manned byarmed actors that impede the free movement of goods into New Fangak. Shortages ofsalt, sugar and other basic commodities is the seen as a result and majorly impacts onregions that are not along the river side such as Kuernyang. Blockage of water ways bywater hyacinth is also a problem as it cuts off access to some regions. From the AprilSMART Survey conducted by MEDAIR which indicated a GAM rate of 14.3% with SAMprevalence at 3.2%, Leer county appears to be a county that is on the brink of surpassingthe WHO emergency threshold. Leer is also projected to tip into Crisis by April 2020according to the IPC analysis released in August. Future projections on the food securitysituation look bleak as poor crop yields are to be expected due to flooding that has causeddestruction to crops. The community will therefore be quite reliant on humanitarian foodaid. The WASH situation is still grim as it is still grappling with open defecation and lowsinking of pit latrines in some of the island locations such as Kok, contributing to anincrease in cases of water-borne illnesses.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To ensure inclusion of both men and women in program implementation, inceptionworkshops will be held before the start of each and every program. This will form aplatform where all affected populations whom are targeted for assistance can learnand offer their input on how to better improve the project from the onset. Additionally,bi-annual stakeholder meetings will be held with key community leaders in attendanceto ensure that the project is meeting the needs of the affected population. Views andopinions of each and every shall be up held and no form of discrimination shall beexercised. This will ensure inclusion Training at least 2 nutrition facility staff on GBVshall be done and a GBV help desk setup in each facility to strengthen the referrallinkage. Outreach activities shall be conducted in areas that are vast in coverage. Thiswill shorten the time it takes for women and children to access service thus reducingthe risk of exposure to any form of sexual and gender based violence that they mayface when they trek long distances to reach facilities. Bi-annual safety audits will alsobe carried out in every nutrition facility to ensure the safety of the beneficiaries is takeninto consideration.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

There are suggestion boxes in most of the nutrition facilities already. There are alsothe monitoring teams and there will be similar teams including the CommunityNutrition Volunteers who interact closely with affected persons in their private homesetting and directly ask for feedback on the nature of services provided. The CNVs arelocal people in their settings and are likely to be trusted more with discrete information,same as the local staff. There are also quarterly monitoring plans where a teamselects sample households from the various target groups and engages them throughfocused group discussions to identify their concerns. The concerns are tabulated inthe organization's feedback matrix and addressed urgently where possible or referredto relevant organs within the humanitarian platform in South Sudan
