Needs

The humanitarian context of the West Bank including East Jerusalem remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli military occupation, which marked its 51st year in June 2018. The narrowing of any political horizon to end the military occupation has negatively affected the political and economic situation in the area. Protracted socio-economic, and protection challenges continue. Representing one-third of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem, many of the nearly 840,000 Palestine refugees registered with UNRWA experience challenges marked by continued: (i) violence and force displacement, administrative and punitive demolitions; (ii) restrictions on access to land, services and markets, particularly for those living and/or owing land in Area C and in the Seam zone; (ii) stagnant economy and lack of employment opportunities that erodes the purchasing power of Palestinian refugees thus classifying them as one of the poorest segments of Palestinian society; (iii) food insecurity that particularly acute amongst refugees residing in camps. In 2018 the situation for Palestine refugees remained volatile. Violence linked to the ongoing military occupation has been the outstanding protection threat thus far in 2018. From January until the end of August 2018, there were 464 search and arrest operations in refugee camps by ISF, resulting in casualties (2 refugee fatalities – including 1 minor, 107 injuries – including 24 minors) detentions and damages to private property. Many of these military operations are either initiated as, or develop into, violent incursions characterised by deployment of live ammunition, the excessive tear gas use, damages to refugee homes and property. This precipitates severe psychological stress, physical injuries and at times also fatalities. Forced displacement is a key protection concern. Between January and September 2018, 123 refugees were displaced in the West Bank – refugees account for more than 40% of those displaced thus far in 2018 – which indicates that they have been disproportionately affected; as they are only 25% of the total population. Administrative demolitions are a major trigger of forced displacement and dispossession. A total of 84 refugee structures were demolished by Israeli authorities in the first 9 months of 2018. Despite the fact that overall demolition numbers are lower across the West Bank as compared to 2017, the number of demolished refugee structures is consistent with the same period in 2017. 109 of those displaced were female Palestine refugees, including 53 girls under the age of 18, and 1 female headed households. Children accounted for 59% of all the refugees displaced during this period. With respect to both conflict related violence and displacement, children are usually particularly affected by these traumatizing practices and the psychological impact can be quite severe on development. Young men are exposed to conflict-related violence including physical injuries or traumatizing psychological experiences, in addition to detention. There is evidence that sexual and gender-based violence is increasingly used within Palestine, as a negative coping strategy to address feelings of powerlessness and hopelessness, prevalent as result of the occupation and humanitarian crisis. Research published in 2010 and 2017 established that men who reported experiencing occupation related violence were more likely to perpetrate intimate partner violence and sexual harassment. Displacement and property damage has a particularly detrimental impact on women and girls. Following house demolitions, families often move in with extended family and relatives, often giving women less control over their time and the household and care-giving responsibilities, in addition to over-crowdedness, dependence and an increased risk of family discord and violence. Elderly household members are also particularly vulnerable to these practices, as they may have endured repeated incidents

Activities

Principle ObjectiveProtection threats affecting Palestine refugees in the West Bank are mitigated through advocacy, assistance and access to services. Specific ObjectiveEnhanced response to and prevention of protection threats faced by Palestine refugees in the West Bank - through advocacy, assistance and access to services. Outcome 1: Enhanced accountability and improved practices of duty-bearers vis-à-vis their obligations under International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law to Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Activity 1.1: Incidents of IHL and IHRL violations in refugee camps, Bedouin and Herding communities, East Jerusalem, Barrier affected communities and communities affected by settler violence are systematically and thoroughly documented Activity 1.2: Where consent to do so is secured and when necessary, documented IHL and IHRL violations are addressed on a systematic basis with relevant authorities during meetings, calls or through protest letters/letters of concern. Direct interaction with relevant Duty Bearers to address violations of international law. Different platforms of engagement are used in this process; from real-time interventions through telephone contact, to scheduled meetings and official letters. Activity 1.3: Donors and other international stakeholders are sensitized to the impact of IHL & IHRL violations on Palestine refugees when relevant and possible (through briefings, field trips to affected areas, presentations at relevant forums abroad, etc.)Mobilization activities focus on addressing briefings to diplomats and lobby organizations, reporting to Human Rights stakeholders, field visits for politicians and diplomats to emblematic communities, background briefings for journalists, and briefings for relevant forums abroad..Outcome 2: Refugee households impacted by protection threats are better able to copeActivity 2.1: Refugee households affected by demolitions/evictions & damages following military operations/settler violence are identified and assessed by UNRWA. Activity 2.2 Following assessment, services (case management, emergency cash assistance and individual service referrals) are provided to affected Palestine refugees in line with identified needs. Activity 2.3 Monitoring of cash assistance and case management services. In terms of the role of the beneficiaries in the design, implementation and evaluation of this project this can be summarized as follows:At the design phase for Outcome 1, beneficiaries have been directly involved the design of the activities, including through participation in committees, focus group discussions and surveys to develop dedicated advocacy strategies at the community level, UNRWA ensures that the inputs of vulnerable individuals are appropriately represented in this process. These strategies are the basis of action taken under this project. During the implementation routine engagement with community stakeholders is carried out to ensure the appropriateness, impact and relevance of activities. In addition at the closure of the activities, the community is directly involved in providing to the evaluation that UNRW undertakes and uses for its future programming cycles. At the design phase for Outcome 2, beneficiaries were involved at the community level in terms of identifying the drivers of vulnerability and risk for those affected by protection incidents that are responded to, this included direct involvement in the piloting phase of the eligibility tool. In the implementation phase beneficiaries are directly involved as they are the drivers of their individual case plans with are developed in coordination with the social workers, furthermore they have opportunities to raise concerns and complaints through a dedicated free-to-call hotline. In addition beneficiary inputs are provided in terms of their assessment of whether the assistance provided met their needs, and about additional needs that were outstanding.

Indicators

Protection threats affecting Palestine refugees in the West Bank are mitigated through advocacy, assistance and access to services. Outcomes 1: Enhanced accountability and improved practices of duty-bearers vis-à-vis their obligations under International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law to Palestine refugees in the West Bank.  20 % of UNRWA's advocacy interventions [number] with duty bearers are followed by concrete positive measures by the duty bearer.  65 % of documented incidents for which UNRWA obtains informed consent are presented to the relevant authorities  85 advocacy products produced and disseminated and/or number of meetings/events held  20% of stakeholders-awareness raising initiatives are followed by concrete measures taken by the stakeholders (following a briefing, field trip etc.) Outcome 2: Refugee households impacted by protection threats are better able to cope  80 % of the Palestine refugees affected by demolitions/evictions in the West Bank still reside in the same community three months after having received cash assistance  90 % of the Palestine refugees affected by demolitions/evictions in East Jerusalem still reside within Jerusalem municipality borders three months after having received cash assistance  50 % of vulnerable refugee households impacted by protection threats have re-established the physical safety and security of their residence after receiving emergency cash assistance.  2800 persons receive an appropriate response.  550 persons receive support to alternative housing Monitoring and evaluation: With regards to Outcome 1, throughout the project the impact of UNRWA’s advocacy will be monitored both through tools which have been internally developed, that track interventions made and their outcome – and also through direct engagement with the affected population through established committees, focus group discussions and other representative forums, in order to provide feedback in terms of whether UNRWA is achieving its stated objectives. Formal internal reviews will be conducted on a quarterly basis, allowing UNRWA to adapt its approach to enhance its impact. As part of this process UNRWA actively seeks input from all of the key stakeholders, including affected populations, diplomats and donors, and duty bearers to assess the effectiveness of its work. With respect to Outcome 2, UNRWA has an established monitoring system in place. Following the opening of a case, UNRWA conducts routine monitoring visits to households. In the case of households affected by demolitions will be carried out up to one month after the incident, while households affected by damages to their private property due to military and military operations will be carried out up to three months after the incident. At the closure of a case UNRWA conducts a closure survey with each household to assess the effectiveness and impact of the action. UNRWA has also established a dedicated free complaints hotline to enable households to voice their concerns and seek redress, this is accompanied by a formal process to review contested cases. All these elements feed into routine quarterly assessments that are drawn up to track the effectiveness of impact, and provide opportunities for adaptation of approach. A final evaluation will be done at the end of the project cycle, as a basis for future programming.
