Project Summary

Throughout 2023, Faith in Action (FiA) will be implementing Tear fund Australia and kindernothilfe (KNH) funded projects, through an integrated approach. The project has identified four priorities in 2023 for our programming in Cox’s Bazar, as follows: 1. Reduce violence (household level/intra-community level) 2.Socio-economic empowerment of women and youth 3.DRR, climate change adaptation (CCA), and natural resource management and 4. Community led programming. The Program design of FiA highly prioritizes technical expertise in food security, DRR, CCA and gender-sensitive programming as the central component. This project, however, provides integrated multi sector assistance by enhancing vegetable production and income generation. Thus, FiA commitment to promoting gender, disability inclusion, and GBV is reinforced, and gender and protection will be mainstreamed everywhere. FiA's Protection, Gender, and Inclusion (PGI) approach address the causes, risks, and consequences of violence, discrimination, and exclusion in an integrated way; creates gender diversity in a workplace where both men and women are hired at a similar and consistent rate, are paid equally and are given the same working/promotional opportunities. This project ensures ethical standards and protects staff, and every woman, girl, boy, and man abused by individuals or groups operate from the Do No Harm Principles, has an anti-corruption policy, a “zero tolerance” PSEA, and a code of conduct for all staff and contractors. Integrating resilience-building activities both communities are fully in line with the focus on the triple nexus (development-humanitarian-peace building) approach. Community resilience initiatives will be integrated into food security programming, income-generating opportunities, and DRR activities programming mainstreaming activities that promote peace between the both communities. With this holistic approach, the two target communities will grow with resilience and strengthen local economic activities while using local markets and products that boost the local economy. Throughout the project period, FiA will provide on-farm skill development training on homestead gardening and food processing to support women and youth with considering disability inclusion, vulnerable farmers, women, and LGBT; ensuring their self-reliance household level climate smart homestead gardening and climate-sensitive self-reliance interventions will be implemented for Rohingya and hosts that will have a multidimensional positive impact on the environment. FiA creates more room for more engagement through meetings, workshops, training, field visits, and timely making updates of 5Ws for better coordination with the community and other FSS partners operating in the same geographical area to avoid duplication. Further, the Self Help Groups (SHG) will be set up under various activity interventions to cater to the needs of people with disabilities/ HC; skills training, and market linkages will take place. By focusing on rights holders and communities and engaging a wide range of stakeholders throughout project design, we ensure that projects’ meaningful, increased feasibility and localized in the targeted area even after the project period. The project dreams that the women are empowered and play an active role in family and community for their and their children's rights and establish a just and DRR resilient community. The community accepted their leadership and recognized their participation and contribution; they can be an integral part of leadership and the business community and are contributing to the decision-making process at home, community, and local government level. They have food security and income thus ensuring nutritional needs for their family members and continuing education for their children.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Bangladeshi population living in Ukhiya and Teknaf has fragile food security/ livelihoods. For the host communities, access to income, including casual labour, is relatively common. Unfortunately, most families earn a very low weekly or monthly income due to erratic casual labour opportunities. Only a small fraction of Bangladeshi – and even less so for the Rohingya - rely on stable wages. Poor diet, security concerns, lack of education and livelihood opportunities, health, and WASH conditions continue to remain a challenge to Rohingya refugees’ livelihoods. According to the REVA assessment, in the year 2021, compared to 2019, food consumption among Rohingya and host communities has deteriorated. Despite the current level of humanitarian assistance, 49 percent of Rohingya households cannot afford the minimum expenditure basket (MEB). Discounting the value of assistance (a simulated scenario), the economic vulnerability would significantly increase to 96 percent of Rohingya households consuming below the MEB, reflecting the fragility of the camp economy and its fully dependent on aid to cover the essential needs of almost all households. Two-thirds of Rohingya and one third of Host Community households are engaged in crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies to cover food and/or their basic needs compromising their resilience and future productivity (REVA-5). According to the Multi-Hazard Risk Analysis conducted by HCTT Nexus Strategy for Climate-Related Disasters (2021-25), Cox's Bazar has been identified as the highest risk of being affected by cyclones and landslides. As per the DCA Social Cohesion gap assessment report in October – December 2020; while the respondents were asked about their perception regarding harmony between them, host communities' view of harmony has become more negative over time. According to Dhaka Tribune, the compassion of most of the locals in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila appears to have reached its limit, as many say; their lives were negatively affected due to the prolonged presence of Rohingya refugees (2019). According to respondents from both communities, the competition for employment and livelihood is the main source of tension that breaks the harmonious relationship. 63% of the surveyed Rohingyas said that they are unwilling to build any social ties with the host communities because they are conscious of the power imbalance between them and the Host Community and this is why they simply avoid engagement with those in the host community, due to their vulnerable status. However, 80% of the host population rejected the idea of assimilation of Rohingya refugees into the host population, stating that it is preferable to continue hosting them locally as long as they are not permitted to exit the camps. The situation of women in both camp and Host Community varies by age and position in the family. Generally, young women age bracket (15-29) face resistance from parents, husbands, and in- laws. These guardians are considered responsible for their livelihoods and decide their terms of mobility. The adult female is more than 29 years old. Depending on the condition of their household they are considered responsible for their livelihood. Adult men (over the age of 29) in the Host Community are considered to be responsible for their family and livelihood. They work as agricultural wage labor during the cultivation and harvesting of crops. Unemployment and frustration related to unemployment are common worries within them. Adult males from the FDMN community are usually engaged in laborious work available for 15 to 20 days during winter months (December to February) as there is the surge in the reconstruction effort. They are paid daily. During the rainy season, they do not have work available. Some adult males have non-agricultural skills e.g. tailoring. Some of them have started their shops inside the camps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Providing emergency help to those forced to flee is often the first step toward long-term protection and rehabilitation. In need of that, FiA is ensuring protection throughout the response. Its livelihood or self-reliance activities contribute to the prevention of negative coping strategies, such as child marriage, taking loans, or selling assets and resources. FiA also seeks to protect staff and every volunteer, beneficiary, woman, girl, boy, person with a disability (PwDs), and man engaged in the programs from abuse by individuals or groups. FiA will provide mental health and referral support to cope with any difficult circumstances; feedback mechanisms ensure women won't face GBV. Regular awareness sessions will be to advocate for women and girls and their rights. FiA applies do-no-harm principles and ensures work in a conflict-sensitive manner. One aspect of this will be to include and target members of the host community in the project.

3.2 - Gender

Gender barriers put women & women-headed households in the communities at higher risk of economic vulnerability, often leading to social vulnerability and protection issues. By providing alternative livelihood opportunities, FiA aims to reduce negative coping strategies such as sex &human trafficking ,child marriage and protection issues due to negative coping, such as turning to criminal activities, selling assets, and taking on loans for men. In response, the project will ensure gender sensitization and protection mainstreaming training for all. Then tailor their sector-specific interventions in response to the needs, capacities, and priorities of women and men as identified through assessment, analysis, and planning efforts finally, include targeted actions to address specific gaps or discriminatory practices. FiA maintains gender mainstreaming by ensuring more efficient allocation of resources, especially increased well-being for both genders.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

According to the FiA-approved Child Protection Policy (CPP) all relevant and involved actors have to abide by the policy and code of conduct. Each of them has to sign the CoC prior to coming into touch with the children. FiA will follow the process of staff recruitment; will provide orientation and training to the staff regarding the implication of CPP, and reporting procedures. The children, themselves, will be a great source of information and protection under the umbrella of clubs, networks, and peers. There will be feedback, complaint, response mechanisms, and a national helpline in place. FiA will do all to promote child rights and protection; maintain a strong and close network with the government, UN, Civil Society, Policymakers, and other stakeholders thus create exposure opportunities for the children for their mental health. If there is any case that will be dealt with confidentiality but will provide the best support and create a safe and secure environment for the victim.

3.4 - Diversity

Utilizing lessons and experience from our previous projects in other parts of Bangladesh on ensuring food security for the rural poor households, we will integrate informed and dignified inclusion of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and LGBT in all the stages of the project’s activities with considering; sensitizing project staff on disability and LGBT issues and disability inclusive training methodology and facilitation skills. Providing their need based support and. involving family members in the selection process if needed. FiA will ensure that at least 5 to 12% of the project’s beneficiaries are PWDs and LGBT. However, FiA has a comprehensive mental health strategy; focuses on prevention and care. We provide training for mental health counseling sessions that help staff and volunteers deal with the stress of mental health issues by either offering direct help or coordinating access to professional support.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Community participation is one of FiA key operating principles and the organization regards this as intrinsic to good programming. FiA will ensure proper participation of rights holders through the establishment of inception and close-out meeting; ensuring different stakeholders are aware of the project. The proposed project has developed based on field experience in responding to the Rohingya crisis, a compilation of the needs assessments. The implementation process will follow systematic community engagements & participation. All the services will be informed systematically. FiA has a responsive complaint and feedback mechanism, where community feedback and complaints are integrated into the monitoring system. We take prompt initiative based on the feedback of the community members. Field workers collect feedback and complaints in the field, and report to the mechanism. Anyone also can lodge a complaint and feedback through the FiA hotline.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. This project will be a resilient approach/ disaster risk-informed approach. FiA will support climate-proofing of economic interventions like climate-smart homestead agriculture to the target group, by ensuring household level gardening and tree plantation; will positively impact on the environment. FiA’s DRR training assists in developing and delivering services with knowledge of seasonal vulnerability and risk calendar with guidelines that can protect lives, livelihoods, and resources from natural hazards in a systematic and sustainable manner. It provides life skills training to women and adolescents to create disaster risk resilience environments through off-farm learning, and income-generating activities; following a unique strategy avoiding environmental issues and climate change mitigation measures by adopting renewable energy sources also promoting more sustainable uses of land.
