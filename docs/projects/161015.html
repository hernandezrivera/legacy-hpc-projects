Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable populations in Kapoeta north and payams of Bungu and Ganji of Juba County- CES where most affected populations; Returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and host communities are experiencing sexual and gender based violence mostly rape, sexual assaults, sexual abuse, child marriages and various IPV forms placed upon them due to armed conflict, displacement. The one year GBV interventions will focus on increasing women and girls access to lifesaving GBV case management, psychosocial support (PSS) and enhancing protection services with focus on GBV risk reduction activities. MaCDA would also be conducting safety audits and protection monitoring in order to understand the GBV trends and ensure risk mitigation measures are put in place through advocacy by relevant clusters. Establish, train and supervise community based protection networks CBPNs to increase women and girls friendly spaces access to information about GBV risks, services and risk mitigation. Provide a platform would be conducting community dialogues on protection and GBV related topics, development of community based mitigation plans, GBV prevention messaging (early and forced marriages). Establishment of four women and girls friendly spaces for vulnerable women and adolescent girls to access information on both prevention response services with focus on restoring hope, acceptance, normalcy and enhancing resilience in women and their children. Strengthen the provision of case management services in a timely, effective and efficient manner through establishment of efficient and defined referrals pathways for survivors. Participate in Coordination meetings, GBVWG and services mapping.
Build the capacity of community protection focal points and service providers mostly social workers, community volunteers, police and health workers on GBV prevention and response trainings, GBV case management using IASC guidelines.
MaCDA past experience. MaCDA implemented a GBV prevention and response activities to both IDPs and host communities with support from IOMRRF on a GBV project, Dec 2018 to March 2019 at Rokon and Dolo counties; phase out. MaCDA implementing GBV project with a component of Menstrual hygiene management ( MHM) at Kapoeta South payams. we have been conducting trainings for community protection focal points on GBV prevention and response, training case workers, case management Officers health workers and community volunteers on GBV case management using IASC guidelines. Targeted 1,500 women and girls from kapoeta town, Longeleya and Nachilagur to be trained on making sanitary towels. So far, 638 women have been trained on how to make sanitary towels, some of them have already passed to next levels of making Nickers. It is a pilot project with support from WFP July to December 2019; status ongoing. therefore MaCDA would be using its vast knowledge and practical experience in the field of protection and GBV, Livelihood skills in replicating the same experience to the GBV project MaCDA is about to bring at Bungu and Ganji payams of Juba county- CES.
The total number of Direct beneficiaries targeted is 6,838 individuals.

1 - Needs

June IRNA 2019 assessment report and protection cluster situational update continue to highlight serious humanitarian needs, there is reported influx of IDP returnees into Bungu and Ganji in central Equatoria. The situation keeps worsening due to continues IDPs returnees into the area and this has impacted the lives of the IDPs, returnees and host communities their resources are strain. It is reported that more IDPs returnees are expected to continue arriving into the areas as the situation in South Sudan becomes more calm and safe. The IDPs returnees are coming from mostly Uganda, Juba and yei. The number of IDPs returnees is estimated at over 11,313 at Bungu and Ganji and kurki which is a boma under Bungu. Sexual and gender based violence have continue to be wide spread in the areas due to movement of the affected populations in and out of the communities in search of services like food, water. Wide spread sexual assaults and rape due to the presence of unknown gun men, armed opposition groups, weak law and enforcement forces and cattle keepers in the area. There is no existing women and girls groups and support systems, this have increased the rates of school drop outs due to poor learning facilities, lack of teachers in Bungu and Ganji. As a result most of the adolescent girls have experienced sexual abuse, child and forced marriages. No safe spaces for the women and girls to access information, there are community leadership structures but lack basic GBV trainings. Lack of life resilient items such as Dignity kits has put the lives of women and girls at health risk since they opt to other unhygienic coping mechanisms.The above backdrop cuts across Kapoeta North (Mosigo and Chumakori Payams).Women and girls at these locations face multiple GBV issues. According to Kapoeta region multi sector survey-courtesy of AVSI and PLAN international (Nov-2017)- rape is the leading threat faced by women and girls with 50%.Women and girls also cited forced marriage (17%) and sexual assault (17%) as other forms of GBV experienced in Kapoeta North. Children in these locations also suffer the same fate with sexual abuse and forced into sex rating 17%.In its response, MaCDA will provide lifesaving services to the survivors, psychosocial support and referral. MaCDA would also be conducting life skill trainings and PSS activities for the vulnerable women and girls as part of life saving with focus on building self-reliance among the adolescent girls and women including the girl-child headed families. MaCDA will also distribute dignity kits to the most vulnerable women and adolescent girls in order to restore basic hygiene and health.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Conduct community consultations with local authorities, community leaders and chiefs at the inception of the project to update them on project objectives and seek their support.Activity 1.1.2: Establish, train and support 4 Community Based Protection Networks (each having 16 Members) as well as 16 Social workers on Protection and GBV Mechanisms.Activity 1.1.3: Conduct community outreach activities targeting 4,276 community members with focus on raising awareness on available GBV services, risk and risk mitigation including facilitating confidential referrals to the GBV response team.Activity 1.1.4: Conduct Safety audits and community discussion in order to identify risk faced by community members and vulnerable groups and facilitate developing action points in addressing the community needs in collaboration with the community and relevant partners.Activity 2.1.1: Train Case workers, case management Officers and Psycho social Facilitators on Survivor centered Case Management, Psychological Fist Aid and safe and ethical referrals.Activity 2.1.2: Train community leaders on GBV prevention and response across 6 villages in Bungu and Ganji so as to adhere to the principles of DO NO HARM and ethical GBV reporting and referrals.Activity 2.1.3: Strengthen local level coordination on GBV and improve the functioning of GBV referral pathways to ensure women and girls have timely and confidential access to life saving servicesActivity 2.1.4: Establish four women and girls safe spaces to provide lifesaving, age appropriate GBV case management and PSS services to women and girls to 1,500 women and girls

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

MaCDA has a presence in both Bungu and Ganji payams of Juba County, and this will provides a strong base to launch responses. In both locations, MaCDA have strong working relationships with the local authorities, community leaders and other key actors in the areas. Participating in coordination meetings, and cooperating with other humanitarian partners on ground. MaCDA will work hand in hand with the GBVSC and partners to see to it that there is no duplication of efforts in the areas. MaCDA will holds consultations with key stakeholders in the community to introduce the nature of interventions and ensure community participation and ownership of interventions. Trainings are provided for community leaders on protection and GBV and child protection and focal points are identified to support community based referrals to MaCDA services. In terms of physical access, many parts of Bungu and Ganji are accessible throughout the year except some few villages that may have access problems especially during raining seasons. As part of MaCDA’s contingency plans, key conflict triggers will be identified for a variety of likely scenarios in all locations. Safety and Security Plans will be in place in all locations and are regularly reviewed and updated based on emerging threats, violence and changing security. Bungu and Ganji remain relatively calm by mid of the year 2019, with less violence reported in the areas,

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

This response will be clearly communicated to the beneficiaries in Bungu and Ganji interms of the time frames and sustainability. The communities, both IDPs, returnees and their host families, will be engaged to participate during the response in order to encourage ownership, self reliance and promote gender empowerment and equality.The capacity of the target communities will be developed through: i) Trainings for Community based protection networks, Women Groups and girls group identified to build and enforce community based GBV networks during and after project response phase out. MaCDA will identify the women from the community and train them on GBV basic and prepare them as volunteers after the response phase out. MaCDA will hand over the WGFs centers to the women and girls ii) to increase Community levels awareness on available GBV services ii) iii) Training and promotion on sexual and reproductive rights and health, iv) Marketing /Livelihood skills training and v) Training for fuel efficient cooking stoves (using clay). The Project community volunteers / community facilitators (females and males) will be trained. The selection of community facilitators will be a competitive process, as facilitators will need to commit to the duration of the project, be able to work with different groups of communities by place of origin (IDPs returnees and host communities), age, gender and diversity in their own communities and have minimum reading and writing standards. Having local community facilitators, (a total of 4) will increase community ownership and responsibility for building and developing community prevention of and responses to gender-based violence in Bungu and Ganji. The project is designed for 12 months – to encourage all categories of communities to mitigate and prevent GBV risk factors within communities due to the limited access to cross-sector activities, and services.
