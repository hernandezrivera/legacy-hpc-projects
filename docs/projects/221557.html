Project Summary

The project will ensure that 3,104 (1,904 girls and 1,115 boys) children, adolescents and youth including children with disability have access to education programming and attend schools in Abyei Region while enhancing the capacity of 85 adults (53 males, 32 females) PTA/SMCs. Five (5) climate-resilient semi-permanent structures will be established in line with Inter-INEE's & government of South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and two (2) temporary learning spaces or schools rehabilitated.
About 1,875 (979 girls and 896 boys) will have access to new gender-sensitive WASH facilities in like sex-segregated pit latrine and rehabilitated boreholes and protected wells line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls). To support WASH response, all the targeted children will be trained on how to use Ash for washing hands after visiting pit-latrines and sanitation clubs will be established in the schools to promote sanitation while water committees established to manage the water points.
Additionally, in the project, about 3,019 (1,904 girls and 1,115 boys) children including 50 children with disability (30 girls, 20 boys) and youth (6-17 years) will benefit from recreational material supplies and children (3-5 years) benefit from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits including being provided with teaching and learning materials. About 250 pupils (150 girls and 100 boys) with a third being persons with disability aged from 14-17 years will also benefit from life-skills and basic vocational training.
The adult direct beneficiaries who are teachers, PTA/SMS and community education committee members will be trained and mentored on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies, targeting 85 of the stakeholders (32 females, 53 males). They will also be trained on PSS, CP, PSEA, disaster preparedness, and protection referral mechanisms. PSEA reporting mechanism will be established in all the benefiting schools including neighboring schools with PSEA and CP help desks also established in the schools. A PSEA free environment will be established in all these schools.
IHA will ensure that the Education community mobilisers work hand-in-hand with the school stakeholders to identify malnourished children giving special focus to refugees and returnees children including IDPs children so that they are referred to nutrition/health facilities for care. To make this possible, a teacher will be selected in all these schools and trained on basic response to malnutrition including identification of children, school & community response to malnutrition, referral methods, school kitchen gardening, among others to help fight malnutrition. These stakeholders will also be closely engaged and used as part of accountability to the affected community.
“Back-to-learning” campaigns will be organized to increase and retain enrolment in schools targeting 100 (60 females, 40 female) key stakeholders. To encourage girls who had dropped-out back to school, the project will also distribute 3,000 reusable sanitary pads to vulnerable girls with sensitization conducted on girl child education.
The project will establish “peace clubs” in all the schools to promote peace-building. These peace clubs will nurture children/pupils in peace building so that they grow up as a peace-loving generation which is a key tool in conflict resolution. This peace building by the peace clubs will help in reducing the decades of ongoing inter-clan or communal conflicts between Dinka Ngok of Abyei and Dinka Twic of Warrap including frequent conflicts between Misseriya Arabs from Northern Abyei and the Ngok Dinkas.
Integrated Humanitarian Aid (IHA) has active presence in Abyei Region implementation a comprehensive multi-year CP and GBV project funded by UNICEF. IHA also in the past partnered with UNICEF for Education in emergency project in Abyei which targeted conflict affected communities.

1. Needs

Inter-Agency need assessment conducted in Abyei from the 23rd to 27th September 2024 by OCHA, IOM, IHA, SCI, ACEA, HCO, Goal, WFP and FAO indicates that 30,000 individuals have been affected by floods in Abyei particularly in Central and Southern regions of Abyei. The flooding of rivers Nyimora and Kiir due to heavy rains from mid-June 2024 destroyed eight (8) out of the thirty-seven (37) functional schools including their WASH facilities affecting the education of more than 3,300 pupils in Abyei region extending to Aneet (Agok). As the floods reduce, WASH related diseases of cholera, diarrhea, hepatitis A including intestinal worm infections have been feared calling for humanitarian interventions to support with Education access programs including WASH interventions.The boundaries of Abyei have been contested since South Sudan became independent in 2011, and lacks a formally recognized governance system. Due to the lack of government oversight in the area, the development of infrastructure and key services has been limited, impacting greatly on the education sector. Without a recognized government in Abyei, services including education for the vulnerable community is entirely rendered by humanitarian actors including UN agencies like UNISFA. According to OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2024, there are nearly 250,000 people with humanitarian needs in Abyei, which represents more than 80% of the estimated population. Humanitarian needs in disputed Abyei is from a long-standing dispute over control of the area, dating back to 1905 when nine Ngok Dinka chiefdoms in the Abyei area were administered as part of Kordofan in northern Sudan instead of southern Sudan. Violence affected the area during the first Sudanese civil war (1955-1972), as relations between the Ngok Dinka and the Misseriya leadership broke down with tensions continuing to date. The Misseriya-Ngok Dinka conflict has been worsened by communal conflict between the Ngok Dinkas of Abyei and Twic Dinkas of Warrap over Aneet (Agok) land which has lasted for 2 years with last attack reported in February 2024 killing 12 people injuring several displacing thousands of people with majoring women and children who joined the list of more than 70,000 displaced since the conflict erupted in 2022. Records from Abyei Ministry of general education and instruction (AAAMoGEI) shows that the conflict at Aneet has affected the education of more than 10,000 children as out of the 15 functional schools in Aneet, only Agok Episcopal church primary school and Ganga primary school are operational as learning centers. The closure of schools in Abyei due to floods and continued conflicts is impacting on protection as many drop-out children are opting for child marriage with several cases of teenage pregnancies reported by Abyei GBV AoR and CP AoR.This project is designed to specifically respond to the above education needs created by the floods and conflicts that impacted created on the education sector.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers Activity 2: Establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms Activity 3: Rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms Activity 4: Establish gender segregated latrines, water points Activity 5: Rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points Activity 6: Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers Activity 7: Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials Activity 8: Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education Activity 9: Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children Activity 10: Train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition Activity 11: Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents Activity 12: Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change Activity 13: Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) Activity 14: Support conducting back to learning campaigns Activity 15: Support technical and institutional capacities of local actors like CBOS, youth groups, etc through coordination, mobilization and empowerment
