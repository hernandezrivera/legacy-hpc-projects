1 - Needs

The people in the North-East region of Nigeria have been suffering under the insurgency conflict driven by various Boko Haram factions since 2009. Despite the efforts by the Governments of Nigeria and development partners, the crisis continues and even deteriorate. In the three most affected states in the region, namely Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, population of 2.2 million are displaced as internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 8.4 million people needs urgent support including 4.4 million girls and women.The insecurity in the region leads to unemployment and low economic participation. Unemployment rate in the region is higher than the national average: 43.25% in Borno, 54.89% in Adamawa, and 52.57% in Yobe, while the national rate as of Q4 2020 is 33.28%. Poverty Headcount in Adamawa is 75.4% and that in Yobe is 72.3%. Large percentage of agricultural lands have been destroyed and are inaccessible due to military restrictions. Prior to the insurgency, the north-east Nigeria did more trade across national borders than it did with the rest of Nigeria. Border closures, insecure transport routes and the destruction of marketplaces have had a significant dampening effect on trade. UNDP will address this challenge through implementing activities that boost livelihood and economic opportunities.This conflict has triggered massive destruction of basic infrastructure, health and educational facilities, commercial buildings, private houses and agricultural assets. In the three target States of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe, the total damage is estimated at USD 8.93 billion with the bulk of the losses (79%) attributed to agriculture (USD 3.7 billion) and private housing (USD 3.32 billion). Damage to private enterprises is also significant at USD 0.9 billion or 10 percent of total damages. UNDP will address this challenge through implementing activities that reconstruct and rehabilitate public infrastructures. In the region, local governance system in the level of state, local government and community is restricted with various difficulties. The restrictive factors include the insufficiency of equipment and facilities damaged under the conflict, lack of information management system, insufficient training opportunities of the personnel, and knowledge on experience of recovery in other countries. UNDP will address this through implementing activities that improve the emergence of an effective and accountable local governance.The deterioration of the social and economic situation makes especially strong impacts on women and children in the region. One in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children constitute 83% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. The gendered dimensions of displacement are having a significant impact on the status of women and girls within the context of fragmentation of households, demoralization and rampant Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) including denial of access to services, physical violence, and survival sex. UNDP will address this challenge through the implementation of activities that rebuilds social cohesion and re-establish community security.Furthermore, resilience needs to be strengthened at the state/local governments and community levels to avoid dependency on humanitarian assistance. UNDP will work closely with stakeholders at different levels, such as the governments, NGOs and community organisations, to promote humanitarian-development-peace nexus.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project will contribute to Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, complementing and creating synergy with the humanitarian assistance ongoing in the target areas which will lead to the development and the peace building of the communities. Resilience and recovery of these communities with durable solutions will reduce the needs of humanitarian assistance such as food assistance and prevent recurrence of displacement which could eventually become basis for further peace building. UNDP Nigeria will correspond and coordinate with WHO and UNICEF for assurance of quality of services to be provided with the essential infrastructure to be rehabilitated under the proposed project. Additionally, UNDP will maintain the existing strong ties with the humanitarian agencies and actors including IOM, WFP and UNHCR concerning the needs of communities.
