Project Summary

Access to education continues to be inadequate, with only 30 % of children aged six-13 years are enrolled in primary education and only 26 % of children aged 14-17 years are enrolled in secondary education. According to education cluster needs analysis 2021 an estimated 1.4 million school-aged children (45% girls) need humanitarian assistance, out of these children,1,139,632 are children from non-displaced families and 300,398 are children from displaced families. According to UNHCR PRMN, an estimated 893,000 people have been displaced in Somalia between January and August 2020, of which 357,200 (40%) are school-aged children. According to Education Management Information System (EMIS) 2018/2019 systems across the country (FGS, Puntland & Somaliland), one million children are enrolled in schools which is 21% of school age children.
In addition, Somalia is also home to 35,884 refugee returnees’ school-aged children who need education support in 2021. This number excludes another 9,927 refugee returnees projected to return to Somalia in 2021. Due to limited livelihood opportunities for families of these children, parents cannot afford to meet the education cost; hence concerned children miss the opportunity to access quality primary and secondary education. There is a need to explore approaches to expand the reintegration of refugee returnees into the public schools. There are limited intermediate/ secondary education opportunities for IDPs and returnees. Tradition, culture and religion make it difficult to reach the grass-root level, and females are constrained from expressing their ideas and needs.
In addressing the key gaps and needs identified in the Cluster Needs Analysis and other assessments outlined above, UNHCR will assist IDPs, poor host community and returnees in accessing education through provision of education grants and scholastic materials to the parents of each returnee child enrolled in school, parents will receive a cash grant for the school going child through mobile money transfer, The support shall be based on school term modality, whereby parents received cash grant of $ 30 per child. This will be paid in one or two installments.

In addition, UNHCR will support existing schools in areas of return to increase their absorption capacity, including; provision of scholastic materials, teaching and learning materials, and rehabilitation of school infrastructure (classrooms, construction of gender specific latrines, playgrounds and provision of sporting facilities/items, water points/hand washing sinks, etc.). UNHCR will also conduct teachers' training and awareness raising for Community Education Committees on child protection and mitigation of COVID-19 impacts. In coordination with the Ministry of Education and other relevant actors, UNHCR will also support interventions aimed at greater school attendance and retention of students, in particular of girls, including through providing sanitary and hygiene items to girls attending school.
Inclusion will be key to UNHCR’s approach; as such, UNHCR will support the Ministry of Education in integrating returnee children into the national public-school system in order to prevent the continuation of NGO-managed parallel systems. Overall, UNHCR will work closely with the Ministry of Education and the Education Cluster, adopting a local area-based approach, where the emphasis is placed on supporting existing systems to increase capacities. UNHCR will support 46,034 students of returnees ( 25,319 boys,20,715 girls ) of which 4,603 (2,531 boys, 2,072 girls) are verified as living with various disability and they have difficulties in accessing education including lack of disability-friendly services in the schools, lack of qualified teachers and infrastructural challenges like ramps, UNHCR will work with partners to provide friendly services to children who are living with specials needs like training teachers on brails and providing assistive devices.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

UNHCR will assist returning refugees mainly from Kenya, Yemen and Djibouti in accessing education through provision of education grants and scholastic materials (books, uniforms, stationery, etc.) to the parents of each refugee returnee child enrolled in school, parents will receive a cash grant for the school going child through mobile money transfer, The support shall be based on school term modality, whereby parents received cash grant of $ 30 per child. This will be paid in one or two instalments.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The aim of using cash is to restore and maintain access to safe and quality education, in particular supporting out of school children to quickly enter or return to quality learning opportunities. Given the magnitude of needs in Somalia, these objectives will also help to minimize the impacts of crises on the right to education and children’s learning, in addition, this will also support interventions aimed at greater school attendance and retention of students, in particular of girls.

Mitigation Measures

Impact: beneficiaries will be exposed to protection risks and will fail to benefit from protection and assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will Ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is mainstreamed across the whole project cycles and that partners and community leadership structures are also aware of their obligation in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and to meet the policy of zero tolerance Impact: those in need might not receive assistance and protection Mitigation measure: UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries. All interventions will be guided by age gender and diversity mainstreaming approach that ensures that all groups in the community are consulted and their capacities and needs are identified. UNHCR will existence of a zero-tolerance policy to fraud with the framework of the project and across all stakeholder of the project. Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms will be strengthened and communities will be made fully aware of these mechanisms. Impact: Lack of existing and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms will result beneficiaries not able to report diversion of assistance/fraud and beneficiaries not able to access assistance. Mitigation: UNHCR will strengthen and establish multiple complaint and feedback mechanisms including, a hot line or toll-free phone number, suggestion boxes, community structures that allows for community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms, designated emails, UNHCR field and partner staff. In addition to this, regular beneficiary contact monitoring through post distribution monitoring through third partner will further strengthen the complaint and feedback mechanism process. UNHCR will distribute IEC materials with information on existing complaint and feedback mechanisms.

Please provide further explanation

UNHCR aims to enroll returnee students in the existing public schools with the aim of integration and ensuring continuity of their education after the end of the one-year education grant. UNHCR will endeavor to sign an MoU with MoE in FGS and FMS for smooth transition of the returnee children into the public schools, which UNHCR will support with rehabilitation and construction of classrooms and wash facilities. UNHCR also aims to design and implement programmes and to pursue partnerships that feed into wider longer-term solutions objectives, while also ensuring a focus on immediate needs. The education activities planned for 2021 are strongly aimed at sustainability and handover to the authorities. Ministry of Education at the Federal and the State levels are actively involved in the activities as UNHCR aims to mainstream education activities and phase out parallel education structures that were put in place to ensure access to education for beneficiaries. The construction and rehabilitation projects are implemented in areas where security allows. The grants provided to returnees are not impacted by the security situation.
