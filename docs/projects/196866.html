Project Summary

TGH’s intervention is closely in line with the 2022-2023 WASH HRP Objectives 1, 2 and 4, as TGH aims to contribute to life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems by addressing the needs of people affected by the conflict in Syria, coupled with the deteriorating context due to the COVID-19 pandemic and worsened by hyperinflationary economic crisis, including the new rise of AWD/Cholera outbreak in Syria.
According the draft WASH 2023 HNO report that 13.55 million require WASH assistance in Syria with 52% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water with at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, overall WASH in School needs are growing to 56% of students complained to their parents about WASH-related issues - with poor hygiene conditions affecting overall enrollment and especially girls’ attendance.
The intervention will be integrated intervention in covering WASH, Education and Shelter activities with protection, gender, and environmental issues mainstreamed. This intervention will be focused on 4 areas: South Idlib, Hama rural areas (Sub Districts: Eastern Bariy, As-Saan), Rural Damascus and Dar’a rural areas. The targeted areas were selected based on their accessibility, the severity of their needs varying between 3 and 5 as per the draft WaSH 2022 HNO, TGH field assessments during 2021 and consultations with Water authorities, SARC, local authorities and municipalities.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on TGH 4-year experience in implementing WASH projects (with a specific emphasis on WASH-in-school interventions) in Syria. TGH follows a multi-sectorial programming approach that relies on complimentary multi-donor funding to ensure comprehensive coverage, addressing a broad range of critical humanitarian needs that exist in Syria, including WASH, Shelter and education. The intervention will target 260,984 individuals (129,044 female and 131,940 male, 101,784 aged 0-17, 146,151 aged 18-64, 13,049 aged +65). 20% of the budget is already secured for the planned WASH activities in 2023.
In line with Objective 1, activities 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3, TGH will propose intervention that will include:
• In 2023, TGH will commence rehab 8 boreholes equipped with solar system, rehab 3 water networks, support Water quality test for targeted water supply systems, capacity of building to water authorities technical staff, distribution of 500 water tanks 1m3 at household’s level, and rehabilitation of 3 Sewage network.
As part of Objective 2, activity 2.1, 2.2, and Objective 4, activity 4.1, and in line with Education Sector objectives, TGH will propose intervention will include:
• In 2023, TGH will commence rehab WASH facilities in 7 schools in order to secure the access to water for drinking and hygiene purposes and ensure excreta evacuation to increase measures against infection, prevention and control both at school and at home in Idlib, Hama, Rural Damascus, and Dar’a . The intervention will be part of an integrated approach with Education HRP.
• In 2023 TGH will target 7 schools to distribute of hygiene kits and soap to students.
• Provision of emergency WASH services, including response to COVID19 and Cholera outbreak according to need in 2023.
As part of its activities, TGH puts a specific emphasis on abiding by protection principles through providing gender and disabled friendly WASH facilities in schools taking into account menstrual needs, including doors lockable from the inside and lighting. TGH will also ensure to provide WASH facilities accessible for children with disabilities.

3 - Needs

TGH conducted complemented multi-sectoral assessments (November 2022) with focusing on WaSH and education needs in Rural Damascus, Hama, Dar’a and Idleb governorates. TGH noted that 70% of water systems (boreholes and network) are not functional (damage, no maintenance, poor water resource management and electricity shortages). Many communities are completely dependent on WT to cope with their basic water needs, where WT is handled by communities at a high cost barely affordable. The overall water scarcity leads people to resort to harmful practices (i.e. reducing water consumption for hygiene purpose). Destruction of sanitation infrastructure and lack of services have increased the risk of water contamination. TGH assessed the areas of Tassil, Izra', Smad, Tafas, Abtaa, Al Shariah, Balaliyeh, and Haran Al awameed in Dar’a, Hama, Rural Damascus governorates and identified big gaps in terms of sanitation, with no functional sewage networks (high risk of fecal contamination of the piped water to other areas in sub-districs of As-Suqaylabiyah and As-Salamiyeh and access to safe water below SPHERE standards). In a context of COVID-19 and Cholera outbreak in Syria, it is a priority to implement WaSH interventions in social institutions, such as schools, to reduce the virus propagation among the entire community. The identification of the WASH needs in schools is done in collaboration with DoE, taking into account the severity scale.

4 - Implementation of activities

TGH will coordinate with the relevant local authorities such as water authorities, municipalities, SARC and WASH Sector to identify damaged facilities to be rehabilitated and avoid any overlapping or duplication with other organizations. A detailed assessment of the facilities will be conducted by qualified TGH engineers to identify the specific works to be carried out by selected local contractors. Contractor selection will be based on TGH procurement and policies procedures ensuring transparency and accountability all along the selection process. TGH WASH engineers will be in charge of monitoring the works to ensure delivered as per standards. All the works will be handed to over to local authorities. TGH will also ensure to provide training on how to maintain facilities if specific needs are identified in order to ensure the sustainability of the intervention. As part of kit distributions, TGH will rely on SARC volunteers to conduct the distributions & hygiene awareness sessions under the constant supervision of TGH staff. M&E will be carried out by TGH MEAL team through baseline & end line household surveys aiming at evaluating the impact of the activities on living conditions. To support M&E activities and ensure accountability towards beneficiaries, TGH also set up a strong Complaints & Feedback mechanism through hotline, complaint boxes & regular consultations of the community in order to allow beneficiaries of all age, gender and background to reach the organization.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The hygiene kits to be distributed are not gender oriented and all the items can be used by both genders. - Toilets safety and risk of GBV: TGH ensures that when working in toilet blocks in schools, these are separated by gender, TGH will ensure that include locks for doors and lighting in the facilities. - Abuse and exploitation: All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. Additionally, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue, TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along the contract. - Exclusion of beneficiaries: TGH ensures that all the people with disabilities have access to the facilities built. In the schools the rehabilitations always include accessibility features such as ramps and handles. - Risk of theft of WaSH rehabilitation material: TGH agrees that all the safety of the material while doing the rehabilitation is responsibility of the contractor. Once the works are finished, everything is handed over to the local authorities. We ensure that the authorities and local population takes responsibility and ownership. - Exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO): TGH ensures that the area of work is safe and has no explosive devises. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

- Abuse and exploitation (sexual exploitation and abuse): All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. And, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue. For external contractors, TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along the contract. All PSEA focal points receive specific trainings on how to deal with SEA complaint and their roles and responsibilities are defined in Terms of References. - Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits.
