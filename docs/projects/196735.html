Project Summary

As part of SYCAC protection Program in 2023.
SYCAC plans to respond to the growing needs of conflict-affected and most vulnerable families (women, adolescent girls, men, adolescent boys, and people with disability) within northwest Syria by providing GBV services projects.
The projects' activities will respond to the Gender-Based Violent (GBV) survivors by providing free GBV services within women and girls safe space (WGSS) Centers in Aleppo and Idlib Cities, which SYCAC will select two areas to work in, one center in each area. The response services will target (women, adolescent girls, and women and girls with disabilities) GBV survivors. The WGSS centers will provide GBV case management, as well as multi protection support activities, such as recreational activities, psychosocial support, life skills, and awareness sessions for the center beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the centers will allocate specific activities to target young mothers (age under 18). The activities will aim to raise young mothers' awareness of GBV risks, by using the young mother club curriculum developed by UNFPA.
Moreover, the GBV outreach’s teams will work on GBV prevention activities by conducting raising awareness sessions and GBV campaigns for men, and adolescent boys, in the community, to increase their role in preventing GBV in the community.
Also, the outreach teams will use the awareness raising toolkits; which developed by GBV sub-cluster by targeting men and adolescent boys in GBV awareness raising sessions.
The GBV program teams will ensure in delivering the protection information to survivors in an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access, using the GBV sub-cluster SOPs.

SYCAC's protection program will work on increasing the capacity of humanitarian actors, as well as the protection workers, in GBV and protection topics. The GBV capacity building component in the program will include GBV, PSS, and PFA training.

3 - Needs

Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained and recruited by parties to the conflict, while girls are increasingly at risk of being recruited and used by parties to the conflict in some locations. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. As is the case globally, women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of gender-based violence in Syria, in both the public and private spheres. In addition, women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. Men are particularly affected by restrictions of freedom of movement and forms of violence directly associated to the conduct of hostilities.The shame and stigma surrounding sexual violence mean that survivors often do not talk about violence when it happens. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with women and girls’ safe spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training).

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SYCAC will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. SYCAC understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about SYCAC position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and involved in the discussions in the community of intervention,


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Changing the security situation that interferes with the implementation of the project. Likelihood of Risk Occurring: Medium. Effect on Expected Results: high. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results. and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA . Stopping the activities for a short period. - changing in the attitudes or priorities of the population that will affect their willingness to benefit from the project activities. Likelihood of Risk Occurring: medium. Effect on expected Results: Very High. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: SYCAC will work on awareness-raising throughout the project period and will get the feedback from the beneficiaries about the services and adapt accordingly. - Local authorities decide to ban implementing the project or part of it. Mitigation: ongoing coordination and sensitization with the local councils and explain to them the importance of the project. - the shift in power balance and increased airstrikes and compromising access and implementation. Mitigation: Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. Set response plan and designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity
