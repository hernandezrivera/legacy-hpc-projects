Project Summary

The project (approved 2019 HRP HPSE19-SHL-150500-1) will target vulnerable herding and Bedouin families in Area C living in inadequate shelters due to the challenging environment – including restrictions on movement, settler violence, absence of a Master Plan and lack of building permits. The proposed shelter renovation includes the rehabilitation and insulation of substandard shelters and winterization for vulnerable communities. The project aims to protect herder and Bedouin families from harsh weather conditions, reduce the consequences of the challenging environment leading to forced displacement, and strengthen the resilience of target beneﬁciaries. Living standards will be improved through rehabilitation of the structurally unsound and inadequate shelters in order to provide adequate protection from adverse weather conditions during the summer and winter and decrease recurrent assistance by providing more durable and sustainable materials.
Secondly, the project will target vulnerable groups in Hebron city’s H2 area, those living in inadequate shelters close to Israeli settlements and military points. These families face a challenging coercive environment which includes restrictions on movement and shelter rehabilitation, settler violence and military attacks. The proposed shelter renovations include the supply and installation of protective materials (fences, gates, steel window nets, yard and alley covers) as well as renovation inputs (treat dampness, improve WASH connections and facilities, treat leaky roofs and walls, replace doors and windows). Thus, the proposed action for this area will strengthen the beneﬁciaries’ resilience by (a) protecting them from harsh weather conditions, particularly during the winter when they are exposed to ceiling leaks and wind; (b) mitigate the risks associated with Israeli military and settler violence in particular during clashes; and (c) generally improve their living standards by rehabilitating their structurally unsound and inadequate shelters.
The proposed intervention matches the allocation strategy and priority as the project intervention directly supports HRP 2020 Strategic Objective #1 “The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL”, Strategic Objective #2 ‘The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected ed persons under IHL, and Strategic Objective # 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The proposal also addresses Shelter Cluster Objective #2: " Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”.
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment. Ultimately, both proposed interventions aim to reduce the beneﬁciaries’ exposure to forced displacement by mitigating the effects of the abovementioned factors.

1 - Needs

The 2018 HNO identiﬁed the protection needs of more than 30,000 Palestinian families living in Area C related to accommodation (threat of demolition, poor living and sanitation standards, health associated risks and lack of privacy). Water inﬁltration, insuﬃcient ventilation and lack of insulation present a constant health hazard for Area C’s inhabitants, particularly for women, children and the elderly, as they spend the most time in the shelters. The common 1 room shelter fails to meet privacy and safety minimum standards. Families also have limited access to basic health and sanitation facilities. The prolonged conﬂict restricts future development opportunities. The ﬁnancial vulnerability of herders has increased due to increasing fodder costs, inﬂated water prices, restricted access to grazing land and low availability of credit. Consequently, they are gradually being forced out of business, unable to pay off debts and are forced to sell off their animal ﬂocks and assets. As part of its relocation plan for the Bedouin population, the Israeli government has increased the pressure on communities in the eastern Jerusalem Governorate, Masafer Yatta’s Firing Zone 918 and the north Jordan Valley, by issuing eviction orders as well as by demolishing their homes, livelihood structures and community assets. 2019 has already seen an increase in structure demolished and people displaced compared to the previous year. Data from OCHA (indicates that the Israeli administration have demolished more than 458 structures in the West Bank, forcing 604 people to move. This situation is likely to worsen with the Amendments to the 1965 Planning and Building Law, coming into effect on October 2019 in East Jerusalem, where it is expected to expedite demolitions and limit access to legal recourse. In August and September 2019, AAH updated its needs assessment and protection analysis for 80 communities in Area C with a particular focus on community participation and engagement of women, men, girls and boys. The assessment indicated that communities in the E1 zone, Firing Zone 918, Jordan Valley and Road 317 clusters are the most affected by Israeli restriction policies. According to the 2020 HNO on Shelter and NFI, 3,450 shelters are in poor condition and in need of urgent interventions. Early 2019, AAH and RCSD assessed more than 120 shelters in the H2 Area as part of the implementation of the HPF Shelter project (2018 2nd standard allocation). The assessment showed that female-headed and households with PWDs are the ones who suffer the most from inadequate and unfunctional shelter spaces. Based on these reports and using a community based approach, the proposed project prioritizes 25 communities at risk of forced displacement due to (1)dire socio-economic conditions (high unemployment rate, low income, total dependency on herding, limited health care services, limited social capital including education); (2)sensitivity of their location to environmental and anthropic threats (recurrent natural disasters, threat of demolition, eviction or conﬁscation orders, land conﬁscation, military and settler violence); (3)shelter status. AAH strives to reduce beneﬁciaries’ exposure in all ways possible, mitigation measures such as direct beneﬁciary implementation also imply that said beneﬁciaries have the ability and willingness to engage in the project, as well as understand the risks inherent to it. The project design was developed together with the community representatives, with particular attention to the needs of women and children. Taking safety and dignity into account, safe and weather-proofed shelters with sanitation facilities which provide suﬃcient privacy is an essential improvement for women and children’s wellbeing and protection. In July-August 2019 AAH and RCSD conducted a detailed shelter technical assessment in the proposed 25 communities to evaluate the conditions of the targeted shelters and recommended necessary improvements.

2 - Activities

The proposed activities aim to protect vulnerable families through an appropriate response to their immediate needs, thus increasing their protection and resilience to manmade and natural shocks and integrating risk reduction and coping strategies to improve the provided emergency assistance. Said activities will respond to the critical identiﬁed needs of women, men, girls and boys, to improve their living conditions by upgrading their shelters to the minimum standards. Safe access to better sanitation facilities will improve women’s health and provide increased privacy through internal partitions. The project will improve the households’ capacity to sustain their livelihood, by (1) increasing their resistance to manmade and natural risks; (2) decreasing cases of displacement and family separation as a result of inappropriate shelters and; (3) strengthening their capacity to rehabilitate and upgrade their shelters by supplying them with the needed materials and technical advice. The project will capitalize on AAH’s extensive technical experience in implementing shelter activities and RCSD’s experience in assessing and implementing humanitarian activities in the South West Bank. Activity 1: AAH and RCSD will rehabilitate and/or replace 80 substandard shelters, considering technical, cultural and protection considerations, diverse needs of women, men, boys and girls, the elderly as well as people with disabilities (based on FGDs). In order to do no harm, where the security situation requires it, direct beneﬁciary implementation will be adopted to avoid exposure and to mitigate protection risks. Technical solutions will be adapted accordingly. Activity 2: Rehabilitation and/or installation of 40 latrines based on initial survey of current hygiene practices, existing facilities, social and cultural habits and analysis of the economic conditions, considering key protection concerns and ensuring inclusive participation. This activity will complement Activity 1, as in some cases HHs receiving shelter upgrades/rehabilitation will require latrine installation as well. Activity 3: Provision of 50 winterization kits and NFIs, including plastic cover sheets, for Bedouin and herder dwellings, to mitigate winter-related hazards; appropriate kitchen kits will also be distributed. Activity 4: Supply and installation of protective measures for 25 shelters in the communities most affected by military attacks and settler violations. These measures will include the supply and installation of fences, protection materials for windows and doors, improving the shelter’s safety, for the particular beneﬁt of women and children. Legal awareness sessions and consultation with relevant agencies, such as NRC, will be conducted. Existing protection committees, Mukhtars and women and men of targeted HHs will be involved in the design, implementation and monitoring of the activities through different meetings and FGDs targeting each group separately. Advocacy campaigns will be prepared to address SWOs/DOs. RCSD staff will be trained on monitoring direct implementation activities, their ﬁeld oﬃcers will work directly with AAH technical staff and the targeted groups to ensure the quality of implementation. The implementation monitoring of the targeted shelters will be divided between AAH and RCSD staff. The latter will also be involved in the ﬁnal handover of the rehabilitated shelters. This will ensure that RCSD ﬁeld staff improve their capacities in designing, implementing and monitoring activities in sensitive areas. It will also enable them to establish direct communication with the related community bodies such as the local councils and CBOs.

3 - Indicators

A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted by RCSD and AAH to identify target families and conduct a gender and age analysis including sex and age disaggregated data. This survey will facilitate the supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Women, men, girls, boys, elderly people and people with disabilities will be involved in the design, monitoring and evaluation of the activities to ensure full participation of all members of the communities. This will be done through regular follow up in order to collect beneficiary feedback and suggestions for improvement, both at individual level and through FGDs, ensuring safe spaces where women, men, girls and boys are given the opportunity to express their needs and opinions. An end line and KAP survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the satisfaction and impacts of the activities on each of these categories, including an evaluation on the allocation of additional disposable income derived from savings on annual maintenance of shelters, responsibilities of men and women at household level and improved access to adequate shelter and sanitation services. Project activities will be monitored through (1) working field staff; (2) community representatives; (3) beneficiary lists and donation certificates; (4) baseline and endline reports; (5) donor and cluster monitoring visits; (6) project progress and impact reports. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through a beneficiary feedback mechanism that will be carefully monitored. The protection committees and village councils will support AAH and RCSD to identify and liaise with beneficiaries, provide ongoing field monitoring of the rehabilitated shelters, and advise on the protection concerns as well as the ongoing security situation to determine the optimal timing of field operations. The expected objective verifiable indicators from the project are:- 80 shelters rehabilitated or reconstructed - 50 winterization kits distributed - 40 latrines improved or installed - 25 shelters protective materials supplied and installed - The project outcomes in line with the cluster expected outcomes (2018-2020) - 80 assisted HH (376 individuals: 133 women, 121 men and 122 children) living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standards - 422 assisted (121 women, 113 men and 188 children (90 HHs) used NFIs as intended to meet basic household needs - 25 assisted HH improved their access to basic WASH facilities.To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” is added and will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. As a specific protection mainstreaming component AAH is in the process of developing its own protection mainstreaming toolkit and to start working with its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this also through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures and as a consequence to increase complementary humanitarian action. This intervention intends to do no harm by contributing to increasing the protection and resilience of the targeted households, ensuring that shelters have been designed and built with adequate consideration to environmental and legal threats, and avoiding causing tensions or conflicts by assessing households vulnerability using consistent criteria. In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH continuously works on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, in spring 2019, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Even more, most of AAH staff have been gone through protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV. In terms of dignity, the household members will themselves participate in the rehabilitation of their shelter, therefore ensuring their ownership of the works and contribution to the improvement of their own space.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. AAH has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. They are designed to increase beneficiaries’ protection and comply with sensitive data management guidelines to uphold the principle of ‘Do No Harm’. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which AAH and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report complaints through the AAH beneficiary feedback mechanisms which are accessible by phone, email, confidential complaint box or in person (AAH or partners field staff or base members), according to AAH protocols. A service phone number will be distributed to the population in the proposed target communities, enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation or AAH employees’ behavior. AAH will process and treat confidentially all information collected with ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustments or modifications if needed. A monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and community leaders relating to project development and challenges.With regard to access in this specific project, AAH tries to achieve equal and impartial access through for instance prioritizing people and groups on the basis of needs, using the same standards for prioritisation and for construction. AAH also aims to consult with everyone, ensuring access to vulnerable people and people with specific needs to ensure that all needs are met. The project will also provide both women and men with the same benefits for their input and their work in construction, preventing discrimination to female headed household in particular.
