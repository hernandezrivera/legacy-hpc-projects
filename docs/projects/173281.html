1 - Identified Needs

Decades of systematic discrimination, oppression and violence have forced Rohingya people from Rakhine state of Myanmar to take refuge to various places. A significant amount of them came to Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. As of September 30, 2020, approximately 1.3 million people were in need of various support (e.g. food, shelter, healthcare, education etc.) including more than 861,545 Rohingya refugees and 440,000 members of the local host community. Health is one of the most critical as well as essential area of operation that should reflect the exact needs of the people. There are several types of healthcare facilities covering ranges of medical conditions providing services for this population. However, quality remains a major issue. Quality and safe healthcare services are not the result of proper diagnosis and treatment only, but also dependent upon many other factors. Uninterrupted access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and proper medical waste management and cleaning in healthcare facilities is a key to ensure quality care and achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC). Despite of the significant roles, WAHS services in different HCFs around the world fall short of both WHO and national standards. According to the Joint Monitoring Programme for Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene (JMP), globally 1 in 4 healthcare facilities lack basic water, 1 in 5 have no sanitation, 42% lack hand hygiene at point of care and 40% lack systems to segregate waste.Unimproved WASH services affect women and girls disproportionately as they are the main users of health services and the primary caregivers for family members in most of the societies. Pregnant women, who are increasingly giving birth in health care facilities, and their newborns, are especially vulnerable to the consequences of poor WASH services. According to the available data, health care associated infections are responsible for between 4% and 56% of all causes of neonatal death among hospital-born babies in developing countries. In addition, women consist the majority of frontline healthcare workers. Lack of adequate WASH services at their facilities position them at increased risk of healthcare-associated infections and other infections, therefore undermine their safety and motivation. For maternity and other primary care settings, WASH services are often absent, although considered essential and support to ensure universal healthcare aspects related to dignity, equity and quality care. According to the report published by WHO and UNICEF, globally 2.8 million pregnant women and children die every year (1 one every 11 seconds) mostly from preventable diseases. A systematic review in LMIC contexts found that, more than one million deaths every year are associated with unclean birth, while infections account for 26% of neonatal deaths and 11% of maternal mortality. Improved WASH services in healthcare facilities with special attention to women needs and vulnerability can contribute to sustainable improvements in the quality of healthcare services. The proposed project aims to improve infection prevention and control as well as the hygiene situation in health facilities, to protect staff and patients from infections, allowing to maintain health facilities operational. This health facility focused activities will cover all the camps and entire host communities as defined by the WASH Sector and include all the functional government and NGO health facilities. In total the project will cover a total of approximately 160 health facilities in all 34 camps and 50 government health facilities from host communities of Cox’s Bazar. Affected population from camps and host communities will be the indirect beneficiaries as the project will enhance the capacity of healthcare facilities and service providers. As a result, the total population living in the mentioned areas will be benefited from the project.

2 - Activities or outputs

There are six different components of the proposed project. First, staff of Health facilities will be trained and counselled on the job (supportive supervision) to ensure hygiene and infection control measures in their workplace. Second, a participatory assessment will be conducted in each health facility and a WASH action plan is developed. Third, WASH facilities and infrastructures including tube-well and water sources of different healthcare facilities will be repaired and maintained. Fourth, water sources and hand washing stations will be constructed throughout the camps and host communities based on the needs. Fifth, small scale incinerator and medical waste management system will be construction in both camps and host communities. Sixth, disability inclusive toilets for both male and female with menstrual hygiene management system inside female toilets will be built. In addition to the primary activities, healthcare facilities and trained healthcare professionals will be provided on-job coaching and supportive supervision.
