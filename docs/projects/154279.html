Project Summary

In 2018, WFP operations in Iraq provided life-saving food assistance to IDPs in camp settings on a monthly basis, reaching up to 585,000 people with monthly food assistance. As of 1 November 2018, there are still more than 1.8 million displaced Iraqis throughout the country. Families facing protracted and secondary displacements due to continued insecurity, lack of job and livelihood opportunities, destroyed houses and infrastructure, or economic hardship will continue to rely on assistance from WFP to meet their basic food needs. As the situation remains precarious, WFP anticipates that protracted and secondary displacements will require the continuation of existing relief activities (in-kind, mobile money and e-vouchers transfers) that are critical to ensure access to life-saving and nutritious food throughout 2019. In 2019, WFP plans to assist about 400,000 vulnerable displaced people with monthly in-kind assistance or cash-based transfers, in coordination with government and food security cluster partners.

Needs

After two years of occupation by ISIL and large military operations to retake control over its territory, 4 million IDPs have begun their journey back home. However, families are reporting secondary displacements as they are forced to return back to camps because of difficult security conditions, and lack of job and livelihood opportunities in their areas of origin. Furthermore, in an attempt by the authorities to increase control over displaced populations, in some governorates out-of-camp IDPs are instructed to move into camps once displaced families return to their areas of origin and plots inside the camps become available. Additionally, families that cannot go back because of insecurity, destroyed houses and infrastructure or economic hardship are facing protracted displacement and will rely on assistance from WFP and the humanitarian community to meet their basic needs. As of 1 November 2018, there are still more than 1.87 million displaced Iraqis throughout the country. The protracted crisis continues to bear an enormous cost on refugees and host communities. According to the World Bank (Iraq – Reconstruction and Investment, Damage and Needs Assessment of Affected Governorates, World Bank Group, January 2018), the standard of living has deteriorated and a noticeable share of the population has fallen into poverty or is extremely vulnerable to falling into poverty. Multidimensional poverty (Non-monetary poverty measure of household welfare, based on: 1) child school enrolment; 2) living conditions (quality of a household’s walls, floor, and ceiling); and 3) access to services (electricity and clean drinking water). Each of these categories is weighted equally at a third. (World Bank, Iraq – Reconstruction and Investment, Damage and Needs Assessment of Affected Governorates, January 2018) has reached 23 percent amongst IDPs, while asset poverty has reached 70 percent. Unsurprisingly, the direct impact of economic, social and security disruptions has been harsher in conflict-affected areas. Food and rent prices are increasing, especially in the northern governorates that are hosting a large share of Syrian refugees and Iraqi Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). IDPs and IDP-hosting households have been hardest hit by loss of livelihoods and displacement. Food shocks reached 40% of IDPs in affected governorates and close to 20% of non-IDP households. Asset poverty fell from 13% in pre-crisis 2014 to 28% in 2017. This was driven primarily by displacement. Only 24 percent of residents were asset poor in 2017, but 70 percent of IDPs were (Iraq – Reconstruction and Investment, Damage and Needs Assessment of Affected Governorates, World Bank Group, January 2018). The Government of Iraq and WFP presented the 2016 Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment (CFSVA) in April 2017. The CFSVA’s results showed that 2.1 percent of the national population and 5.6 percent of the IDP population are food insecure. Based on this information, in July 2017 WFP implemented a prioritization strategy to ensure that the most vulnerable receive the assistance they need. Consequently, WFP is only providing assistance to all IDPs settled in camps. WFP no longer provides assistance to people living outside camps.According to the CFSVA, there were only marginal differences in the food security status between resident households headed by men or women, though there were slightly higher rates among IDP households headed by women in comparison to those headed by men. Notably, IDP households, headed by women, had the highest food insecurity rates overall (9 percent), while the most food secure households, headed by either men or women, were in the resident sample (headed by men 44 percent; headed by women 43 percent). When comparing urban and rural areas, rural-based households headed by women in the resident sample faced more food security risk than households headed by men, while there were only minor differences between urban and rural areas in the IDP sample. Yet, as shown in the 2016 CFSVA, households headed by women were four to five times more likely to be economically inactive.WFP’s efforts in 2018 continued to focus on the provision of life-saving assistance to displaced families. Despite the efforts of the Government of Iraq to encourage returns of people to their place of origin, protracted and secondary displacements remain an issue as many of the 1.87 million currently displaced people are unlikely to return to their homes in the near future, with the highest number of IDPs in Ninewa, Dohuk, Erbil, Salah al-Din and Sulaymaniyah governorates. They will continue to remain heavily reliant on international assistance in the coming months, and reductions of support in the past have resulted in the adoption of negative coping strategies, including decreased food consumption and food quality, and increased exposure to protection risks.

Activities

In 2019, WFP intends to continue filling gaps in government assistance by supporting about 400,000 vulnerable displaced people in camps with in-kind assistance or cash-based transfers. As returns of displaced population to their areas of origin continues, the number of targeted beneficiaries for food assistance in camps may change during the year. Though protracted displacements continue to require provision of assistance, WFP is planning to implement in the first quarter of 2019 a vulnerability assessment to assess vulnerability amongst the Iraqi displaced population in camps, to investigate the food security status of Iraqi IDPs in camps and determine food-targeting criteria that would allow programme adjustments based on needs. This will ensure that the most vulnerable households (households headed by women or children, households with members with disabilities, etc) continue to receive the food assistance they require. Key activities will be:- Distribution of monthly food assistance through Family Food Rations (FFRs), Mobile Money Transfers (MMT) or electronic food vouchers (E-vouchers) to families experiencing protracted displacement in camps. Modality of assistance depends on thorough assessments of beneficiaries’ preference, markets availability, humanitarian access and protection considerations. The FFRs are made up of basic food essentials and are nutritionally designed to provide a family of five with 80 per cent of their daily caloric intake requirements for 30 days, at around 1,800 kcal per person per day. The ration’s composition is planned to meet the cultural preferences of Iraqi families and is regularly reviewed to reflect beneficiary feedback.Cash-based transfers are provided in secure urban areas with functioning markets, giving families the choice to decide what to eat based on their dietary needs and preferences. In areas where local markets are not well-established, WFP provides assistance through e-vouchers. These can be used in certain shops in the area and encourage local businesses to stock particular goods. - During the project implementation, WFP will continue to mainstream crosscutting issues, including gender equality and women’s empowerment, protection and accountability to affected populations. - WFP also intends to continue promoting a more systematic use of cash-based transfers (CBT) through SCOPE (WFP’s own beneficiary and transfer management platform) where markets are available, accessible and functioning.

Mitigation Measures

- WFP ensures that due diligence and capacity assessment is properly implemented for all implementing partners and vendors. - WFP field monitors are present at distribution sites to monitor and ensure that adequate implementation of the assistance and that it reaches intended beneficiaries. - WFP coordinates with authorities at central, provincial, and field levels and advocate for the humanitarian nature of its assistance.- WFP complies with UNDSS operational standards and coordinates the movement of its personnel and goods with relevant authorities through official channels. - WFP addresses access issues directly with both central and local authorities and collectively with logistics cluster and OCHA’s humanitarian access unit and focal persons. - WFP ensures sufficient partners’ capacity in place, including third party monitoring, where direct access is not feasible.- WFP and its partners ensure crowd control measures are in place.WFP and its partners consult with communities on safest transfer modality and consider adjusting accordingly- WFP create awareness in targeted communities and households on targeting criteria, involving communities – recipients and non recipients – on defining vulnerability and targeting criteria. Furthermore, it consults with men and women on identification of most appropriate household entitlement holdersWFP consults community leaders and cooperating partners to identify and arrange distributions for people unable to travel to distribution points, including new mothers, elderly, people with disabilities, and ill people. Distribution points are accessible for people with disabilities, preferential lines are created for extremely vulnerable individuals.WFP and its partners ensure assistance for people unable to transport rations to reduce reliance on childrenWFP staff, as well as implementing partners, are trained on PSEA and are encouraged to report suspected cases of SEA through established channelsComplaint and feedback mechanisms are established for affected communities, capable of handling complaints of SEA. Affected communities are informed of their rights and of the available complaints mechanisms.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The project is in line with WFP Iraq Gender Action Plan (GAP), in turn aligned with WFP’s 2017-2021 Strategic Plan, the 2015-2020 Gender Policy, the associated Gender Action Plan, and the Regional Gender Strategy. Through implementation of this Gender Action Plan, WFP Iraq contributes to achieving WFP’s goal of integrating “gender equality and women’s empowerment into all of its work and activities, and to ensure that the different food security and nutrition needs of women, men, girls and boys are addressed.”Following the principles of Accountability to the Affected Populations, WFP ensures that beneficiaries are well informed about their rights, entitlements, and have access to confidential and functional complaint and feedback mechanisms. Community consultations with female-headed households are undertaken as part of Communicating with Communities (CwC) programming, to ensure women have the opportunity to provide feedback on the activities and the assistance received, which will be used to modify and refine WFP’s approach. Consultations and Complaints & Feedback Mechanisms (CFM), including information and complaints desks and suggestion boxes, have been specifically designed to be safely accessible for women and girls.On behalf of the UN Humanitarian Country Team, UNOPS implements a national toll-free hotline, the Iraq IDP Information Centre, allowing callers to access information on humanitarian services, provide feedback, and lodge complaints. A referral system through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group ensures that complaints specific to sectors are forwarded to partners and lead agencies. Beside the IDP call centre, WFP rerequires its partners to establish complementary CFMs. Beneficiary contact monitoring at distributions is conducted with monitors gathering information on beneficiary satisfaction and other issues, adding another component to WFP’s feedback mechanisms.WFP involves beneficiaries in different steps of project planning and implementation. Beneficiaries are consulted on various indicators such as the household’s food consumption, coping strategy usage, food expenditure share, and accountability/protection aspects. In addition to quantitative household level surveys, WFP food monitors conduct focus group discussions to gather qualitative information on beneficiaries needs preferences, problems and complaints.WFP employs a "no harm" policy and will ensure at every stage that gender and protection concerns are built-in into programme design and implementation. If feasible, a complaint-mechanism will be set up at distribution points to ensure that any concerns be addressed and/or channelled to the appropriate institutions. To the extent possible, WFP and its cooperating partner will conduct monitoring and post-distribution monitoring in addition to organizing focus-group discussions which will serve as a channel for women and young girls to voice their concerns regarding WFP's assistance. Moreover, assessments will be designed with gender and protection concerns as a central element, to ensure that the needs of women and young girls are appropriately identified.WFP is an active member of the Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) network, established in July 2016 to provide guidance to humanitarian agencies and trained humanitarian actors, including WFP staff members, on how to collectively advocate to implement zero tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) in Iraq. It further reinforced collective accountability for prevention from and response to SEA, respecting and honouring the dignity and integrity of communities throughout Iraq. WFP food monitors are trained to recognize issues of protection and security. Referral pathways to address protection issues, particularly affecting women and girls, have been identified in coordination with protection, GBV and Child Protections actors.
