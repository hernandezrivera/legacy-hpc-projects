Project Summary

As stimulating in the Health Cluster framework/objectives and situation analysis, the project will focus on the three areas, to Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, improve access and scale-up responsiveness to essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations and increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders by
focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality through the establishment of health facilities in Yirol East and Rumbek East. The project will target 25,000 (15,000 children- 7300 boys/7700 girls), 2600 men, 4400 women, and 3000 elderly & people with disabilities. The intervention will focus on screening, prevention, and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation, inpatients care and mobile outreaches, preventive health promotion campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery in facilities, and community through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, mental health, and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, clinical management of rape (SGBV services), regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunization services and strengthening referral pathways. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with the CHD and in coherence with BHI. More female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure that women’s health needs are addressed. The project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilization and sensitization will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout the project life cycle to ensure program ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunization, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI, and case management. This project will complement the HPF project in Lakes State and Warrap in the management of communicable diseases, immunization, save motherhood (PNC, focused ANC and deliveries), disease surveillance, management of SGBV, and Mental health problems among others which are part of HPF projects activities. Covariate climatic, conflict, and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, for example, gender-based violence (GBV) and poor water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. There are 13 counties with extreme levels of food insecurity in 2022, compared to 6 in 2021. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. In this project, SSUDDHAS will engage communities during the selection of Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMUs equipment such as Metallic trunk for keeping drugs and registers, table, chairs, mats and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. MMUs and health facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs) will be equipped with medical equipment. In addition, MMUs will store essential drugs and test kits. Home Health Promoters (HHPs) will mobilize the village ahead of the MMU team visiting the village for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. It's expected that this funding will complement the ongoing health activities being implemented by SSUDDHAS Lakes State.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face flagging humanitarian settings as the situation is deteriorated by prevalent violence, conflict, access constraints and operative nosiness, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19, and climatic tremors resulting in the twofold occurrences of unexpected flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services, (UNOCHA, 2022). In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021, (UNOCHA, 2022). Inter-communal fighting, cattle raiding, and revenge attacks between armed youth and farmers groups in Central Equatoria, Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA), Warrap states (Abyie and Warrap) resulted in hundreds of deaths and injuries, displaced hundreds of thousands and led to the suspension of humanitarian services in some areas (HRW, 2021). The project will target 25,000 (15,000 children- 7300 boys/7700 girls), 2600 men, 4400 women, and 3000 elderly & people with disabilities, including SGBV survivors, and people with mental health. Women and children are disproportionally affected by the crisis, representing 85% of the IDP population, facing heightened risk of SGBV with mental health and SRH consequences. South Sudan’s MMR is one of the highest in the world estimated at 789 per100,000 live births, whereas neonatal and under-five mortality rates are 39.3 and 99.2 per 1,000 live births respectively. Existing services are extremely limited and challenging to access (WHO 2021). Only 22% of health facilities are fully functional, and especially lack basic maternal, newborn, and child health (MNCH), obstetric, and SRH services, often resulting in maternal and infant mortality (MoH 2021). Women, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) are particularly vulnerable, exposing them to risks of exploitation. The IPC classified Yirol East, Rumbek East in category 4 (emergency). The rainy season, when it effectively sets in, is likely to result in increased incidences of human diseases such as malaria and acute watery diarrhea that will affect productivity and deplete savings in the form of increased medical expenses–this will further contribute to household vulnerability and reduce resources available for purchasing food. In Greater Yirol and Rumbek East, regardless of the presence of CUAMM, the main HPF partner, and other health partners, including SSUDDHAS, health conditions are still very dire. Women and children travel 4 hours to access health services in risky and insecure environments where women and young girls are exposed to the risk of sexual violence, rape, and abduction of children or attack by wild animals. This is exacerbated by a lack of qualified medical personnel, weak institutional policy, inadequate supplies of medical equipment and drugs, paralyzed infrastructure, poor road network, and security within the region. Women and children under 15 years are affected including lactating women and the elderly. The ongoing humanitarian catastrophe has put high pressures on the host communities and stretched the resources of the local health system. Immunization coverage remains low in the above counties. In addition, in these counties, there are limited preventive and curative services for SRH, MNCH, counseling for MHPSS especially amongst survivors of SGBV, and communicable diseases like HIV/AIDS, TB, malaria, acute respiratory infections, and AWD, among others. SSUDDHAS is one of the strong emerging women-led NGOs working in Yirol supporting health, protection, and livelihood activities. With this funding, SSUDDHAS will continue to support health intervention in Yirol East and Rumbek East with the integration of nutrition and WASH at the community and facility level.

2 - Project activities

SSUDDHAS’ proposed response plan addresses the above needs, aiming to improve and expand access to integrated emergency lifesaving primary care and essential public health through static (PHCCs, PHCUs) facilities and mobile clinic services empowering the most vulnerable and community populations in Yirol East, and Rumbek East targeting 25,000 persons (15,000 children, 7000 & women & 3000 others. Interventions will complement SSUDDHAS ongoing intervention focusing on screening, prevention, and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through outpatient consultation, mobile outreach, preventive health campaigns, injury management, SRH and MNCH services (incl. ANC and PNC), safe delivery through skilled birth attendants/mid-wives, MHPSS services, clinical management of rape, regular and routine disease surveillance, reporting and prompt response to outbreaks, routine and outreach immunization services. Protection will be mainstreamed across interventions, with special attention to the needs and rights of persons with disabilities, survivors of SGBV, MHPSS needs, case management, and strengthened referral systems. Health services will be provided through close collaboration with CHDs and in coherence with BHI. Local capacities will be empowered to ensure sustainably and continued service provision. Female staff and volunteers will be recruited to ensure women’s health needs are addressed. Community engagement will be ensured through the life of the project and include regular consultations, information provision with community elders, women, youth groups, religious leaders, and beneficiary representatives reaching to the grassroots community leaders such as women representatives, youth representatives, local chiefs, and local authorities and CHDs, and SMOH. In addition, the project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming in order to ensure positive and sustainable health outcomes. Community mobilization and sensitization will be strengthened on health-related messages, through integrated messaging that includes WASH and nutrition messages. The proposed project will ensure early detection and treatment of Tuberculosis and HIV cases in the community and management of SAM with medical complications. Communities in the targeted counties will be engaged throughout the project life cycle to ensure program ownership and sustainability. Health worker capacities will be enhanced in immunization, disease surveillance, BEmoNC, MPHSS, SGBV/CMR, IMCI, and case management. SSUDDHAS will engage communities during the selection of MMU locations, identifying storage spaces for essential drugs and MMU equipment such as Metallic trunks for keeping drugs and registers, tables, chairs, mats, and contributing with space and locally available material for establishing the MMUs. Selected MMUs and health facilities will be equipped with medical equipment, essential drugs, and test kits, such as MRDT, rapid tests for quality service provision. All selected MMUs will be staffed with health workers (Nurses, Community Health workers, midwives, vaccinations, and community mobilizers). All staff will be recruited locally, and their capacities built. Staff in each MMU will have a work plan integrating community mobilization, consultations, vaccinations, home visits for specific cases, weekly visits to all reachable villages, and weekly reporting. The community mobilizer will mobilize the Boma ahead of the MMU team visiting the Boma for providing consultations and other services responding to community needs. Integrated service provision, that includes WASH and nutrition services required for positive health outcomes, will specifically address protection concerns of the most vulnerable groups in the community. Beneficiary feedback will be encouraged and captured. Monitoring visits will be conducted by SSUDDHAS, CHDs, and community representatives/ local authorities to ensure needs and rights-based service delivery.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Gender sensitive awareness building sessions will be conducted with male community members to build trust and confidence on potential activities and related benefits for women and families. The key drivers of female, especially maternal and child, morbidity and mortality will be discussed in order to identify life-saving interventions in SRH and MNCH. Potential gender-responsive interventions and direct impact on lives, safety and dignity of women will be discussed before interacting directly with women of the community. Female staff will be assigned to implement community mobilization, health awareness building and provide SRH, MHPSS and MNCH consultations, counselling and service provision. Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example). Project activities include establishment and strengthening of referral pathways for survivors of protection incidents. SSUDDHAS will work with other local actors, including protection, health, WASH and nutrition actors as well as local authorities to design and ensure the functionality of the referral pathways. SSUDDHAS will follow up on the ground and advocate with OCHA, donor community, local authorities, clusters and the NGO forum. Humanitarian assistance and service provision will be brought closer to target communities (for ex. Mobile Medical Units and weekly visits to each target boma/village for vaccinations, medicine provision, supporting referrals, ensuring safe distribution points) and sound feedback mechanisms will be put in place to ensure that accessibility constraints and distance from service delivery points do not expose women and girls to risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Awareness will be raised amongst community members on the value, importance and benefit of the feedback mechanisms.
