Project Summary

The project aims to support humanitarian information management in Libya via sustained implementation of IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) system and via capacity building of local and national Libyan counterparts. DTM supports the humanitarian community in Libya by provision of data, information and analysis on the population mobility and its impact on the affected populations; DTM Libya also co-chairs the Assessment Working Group (AWG). The information and analysis provided by DTM in Libya covers internal displacement as well as international migration. Under this project DTM will continue to monitor internal displacement via emergency tracking, assess the priority needs of the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) via rapid assessments, supporting multi-sectoral assessments coordinated by the AWG and by continued implementation of Mobility Tracking publish bimonthly information packages on IDPs and returnees in Libya. Similarly, DTM will continue to monitor migration trends and dynamics, and conduct assessments of migrant vulnerability and humanitarian needs in Libya. Information products such as flash updates, detailed analytical reports and public datasets in addition to bilateral sharing of operational information with OCHA and partners of Rapid Response Mechanism (IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF, and WFP) will facilitate enhanced humanitarian information management. Furthermore, DTM will undertake capacity building activities aimed towards supporting local and national Libyan counterparts in the implementation of improved humanitarian information management.

1 - Needs

In 2020, Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) programme in Libya published over 30 information products, including analytical reports on displacement and migration in Libya. The assessments conducted and the subsequent reports published were crucial in identifying the vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of IDPs, Returnees (returning IDPs), and migrants in Libya. DTM initiated emergency tracking of displacement in the context of armed conflict in Western Libya and regularly published flash updates within 72 hours of sudden displacement. Furthermore, DTM also remains critical in providing information to the Rapid Response Mechanism (an interagency initiative with IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF, and WFP participating) via timely verification of the displaced populations in need, and by supporting on the ground coordination of the subsequent response. DTM in Libya is the primary source of data on displacement and migration, including the population figures used in the Libya Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNOs), and figures used for targeting humanitarian response under the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Under the working assumption for Libya HRP 2021, the humanitarian context related to the migration flows to Libya and, the political crisis including the associated armed conflict is expected to remain challenging. Neither is an increase in the national or local capacity for information management is projected to the extent to fulfill the data and analysis needs of the humanitarian community. Therefore, the need for data, information and analysis provided by DTM towards the aim of enhancing humanitarian information management, and for evidence based operational and strategic planning purposes will remain. Furthermore, the 2020 HNO process indicated a clear need for more granular and reliable data, including sex and age disaggregated information, related to multi-sectoral needs assessment to inform the humanitarian programme cycle (HPC) which DTM Libya aims to support in close coordination with other actors in the AWG.

2 - Activities

DTM will implement the following set of activities under this project: Activities aimed at provision of data, information, and analysis: 1. Emergency Tracking of Displacement with Rapid Needs Assessments to determine needs of displaced and affected populations; 2.Implementation of DTM Mobility Tracking to update baseline IDP and returnee population figures, and multisectoral needs data; 3. Migrant Stock Population Baseline Assessments; 4. Migrant Flow Monitoring and Profiling including Intention Surveys (Durable Solutions); 5. Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) Surveys to support HPC process All of DTM’s planned data collections will include age- and sex-aggregated breakdowns to facilitate tailored responses by humanitarian actors to the needs of boys, girls, men and women. Activities aimed at capacity building of local and national counterparts: 5. Joint workshops on narrowing down specific needs and areas of focused collaboration. 6. Training sessions via field experts covering the identified focused areas of collaboration. 7. Joint and collaborative field activities aimed at building capacity for data collection. 8. Workshops on joint data analysis and information management as part of local and national counterparts' capacity building.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

IOM in the overall implementation of its Displacement Tracking Matrix programme in Libya ensures strict adherence to the Do No Harm principle. In specific the following key protection risks are identified, that DTM programming aims to reduce, mitigate, and remedy: 1. Risk of protection harm emanating from gathering of personal information from respondents (key informants, migrants, and head of displaced households); 2. Risk of protection harm to DTM enumerators and implementing partners (humanitarians) via their engagement in data collection activities linked to humanitarian relief / aid provision.4.1: IOM encourages use and awareness raising of the Common Feedback Mechanisms and agency hotline across its programmes4.3: Data collection is done taking into account conflict sensitivity and ethnic/tribal relations4.4: All data collection will be done in compliance with WHO public health guidelines4.6: Project is in full compliance with IOM’s Data Protection Principles and does not collect any directly identifiable information from respondents (such as names or phone numbers)4.9: The project is coordinated with other humanitarian stakeholders, but data collection itself, including selection of respondents based on methodological frameworks, is done independently4.12: Project aims to include various minority groups in its data collection and analysis 4.15: IOM encourages use and awareness raising of CfM and agency hotline across its programmes4.20: Only remote data collection in areas with known UXO/ERW presence4.23: Project includes capacity building activities for local authorities in area of intervention

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

On one hand, accountability is ensured through the utilization of relevant inter-agency mechanism, such as the Common Feedback Mechanism. On the other hand, DTM’s data collection – which also entails qualitative data collection such as focus group discussion – gives affected populations a voice to raise their concerns and articulate their needs in order for humanitarian responder to put them at the center of their evidence-based programming. Their answers, inputs and perspectives are reflected in DTM’s Assessment Reports.
