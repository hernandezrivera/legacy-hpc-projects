Project Summary

This project, improving the protection of returnees, IDPs and host communities in South Sudan aims at improving the protective environment, protection assistance, and safe access to protective services including Gender Based Violence (GBV) services to crisis affected women, men, girls and boys among the returnees, IDPs, and host communities in South Sudan. The project targets a total of 200 000 individuals (returnees, IDPs, and host communities) inclusive of beneficiaries reached through information dissemination and awareness raising. But direct total beneficiaries to be reached is 32 000 individuals at an estimated cost of $800 000USD (60% direct costs, 33% indirect, and 7%ICR). This implies that the cost per beneficiary in this case is 25$. The gender marker is 4M, implying that the project will contribute towards gender equality. The targeted locations in Tier 1 is Juba; in Tier 2 of severity level 4 are: Akobo East, Akobo West, Wau, Jur River, Kajokeji, Mundri East, Pibor, Nyirol, Rumbek East, Rumbek Nornth, Wulu, Yirol East and Yirol West; In tier 3 locations of severity 4 and 3: Mvolo, Uror, Rumbek Centre, Mundri West, and Cuibet. Oxfam South Sudan has been working in these locations through the Rapid Response Missions (RRMs), Emergency Preparedeness and Response (EP&R) and static presence. Oxfam intends to apply the same operandi mondus given the gravity, need, and logical challenges for serving hard to reach locations. Currently, Oxfam has static basis in Nyirol, Akobo, sub office in Uror, Juba, Rumbek, and Wau. In order to achieve the desired objective, Oxfam South Sudan will consider the following protection activities: protection assessments and monitoring; strengthening community-based protection for protection risk reduction as well as promoting peace and security for women and youths; information dissemination and awareness raising on rights, services, Protection from Sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms, entitlements, and on peace; Individual Protection Assistance through either Cash for Protection and or NFIs for Protection guided by existing tools and notes and finally; supporting safe programming (protection mainstreaming) in other sectors of Oxfam South Sudan such as WASH, RRMs, EP&R, Education, EFSVL, Governance and Gender. The project’s outputs and activities are directly linked and contribute to the HRP Protection Cluster and HRP strategic objectives. By collaborating and coordinating with other agencies, line ministries, community-based structures (women, youths, disability groups, chiefs etc), and disseminating information on rights and safe entitlements, the project will sustain continuity of the activities as well as linking to resilience in targeted locations.

1 - Needs

• South Sudan has experienced wide and deep humanitarian and protection crisis since December 2013. According to OCHA (2019) the Revitalized- Agreement on the Resolution on Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in August 2018, offers optimism for the women, men, girls and boy of South Sudan. But unmet pre-transitional arrangements for the formation of Government of Unity challenges the optimism. The civilian population and authorities needs to be more aware of the rights and peace.• The humanitarian and protection crisis are characterized by continued instability, restricted humanitarian space and access, legacy of conflict, violence, and abuse, destroyed livelihoods and eroded coping capacity, insufficient basic services, limited access to assistance and protection (OCHA). • 7.5 million people are in dire need of humanitarian and protection assistance (OCHA, October 2019). 1.5 Million are internally displaced and 2.28 million South Sudanese are refugees (ibid.). Facilitated and spontaneous returnees are reported but facing challenges. 81% of the displaced households in South Sudan are female headed and ‘there are no simple solutions in a complex context of needs, threats and political uncertainty’ (Oxfam, CARE, et el, 2019). • An estimated 6.35million (54%) of South Sudanese are reported food insecure, yet nearly 2 million are malnourished (352,000 women and 1,300,000 children, OCHA September 2019). Nearly a million are severely affected by flooding in South Sudan and exposed to multiple risks (Oxfam, 2019, and OCHA October 2019). by September 2019. • More than 60% of the flood affected counties are currently classified as facing extreme levels of acute malnutrition, others are in crisis levels according to IPC, and subjecting affected civilians to protection risks (Oxfam 2019, OCHA 2019). • All these challenges increase the burden and negative coping mechanisms for women, men, girls and boys in South Sudan. Some internal reports indicate increased early and forced marriages of girls, reduced or challenged access to basic services like education, heightened tension, violence and fear of violence including intra and inter communal and tribal conflict. Those returning are faced with need for principled solutions. Yet despite ongoing efforts by the government, there are gaps due to challenging economic and political crisis. • Specifically, these needs are: o The need to improve the protective environment and decisive humanitarian and protection preparedness and response o The need to provide individual protection assistance either through cash or Non-Food Items for protection outcomes, o The need to strengthen already existing community-based protection structures for risk reduction and promoting local peace and security for women and youths, o The need to continuously raise awareness on rights, entitlements, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, available service, Peace and even Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms, o The need to support ‘safe programming’ in other sectors (and programming linking the triple nexus) towards mainstreaming protection through risk reduction approaches, conflict sensitive approaches and Do No Harmo The need for protection advocacy and coordination to improve responseUsing the modality of static, integrated preparedness and response, and rapid response missions, Oxfam in 2020 will prioritise the following areas: Central Equatoria (Yei, Juba, Kajokeji, Lainya, Morobo, Terekeka,) in Jonglei (Bor South, Ayod, Akobo, Duk, Nyirol, Pibor, Uror, Twich East), Lakes (Awerial, Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Wulu, Cueibet, Yirol East and Yirol West), Western Equatoria (Mundri East, Mundri West, Mvolo, Nzara), Western Barh el Garzal (Jur River, Wau), and Upper Nile (Longochuk, Nasir, Laukpiny, Ulang, Malakal)In response, Oxfam aims at improving the protection of civilian IDPs, Returnees, and Host Communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project locations are already experiencing spontaneous and facilitated returns and relocations of IDPs and refugees. Therefore, systematic assessment and response is also required. According to the Buchanan (2019, in 'No simple solutions: women displacement and durable solutions in South Sudan', - 'Conversations around returns and relocations of internally displaced people and refugees in South Sudan and the future of the UN Protection of Civilians sites are often framed by clear-cut distinctions between single push and pull factors. This framing – often based on perceptions of international actors of what internally displaced people or refugees do or should think – ignores the fact that decisions to stay or to move are made based on complex motivations, in contexts of high uncertainty and, especially for women, limited information. This report aims to bring the voices and experiences of South Sudanese women to the fore, exploring the motivations and intentions of returning and displaced women in the country. The report argues that current movement trends are complex, and returns are often open-ended and non-linear. Given these dynamics, and as women and girls face distinct threats during displacement and return, the report advocates for a more gender-sensitive and durable solutions approach to population movements in South Sudan' available at https://oxfamilibrary.openrepository.com/bitstream/handle/10546/620857/bp-no-simple-solutions-women-displacement-south-sudan-030919-en.pdf . Therefore, any solutions discourse, assessments and response in South Sudan requires to be thus far principled. And those women, men, girls and boys who have relocated (spontaneous or assisted) will still further need assistance. The Protection nexus in South Sudan requires a deliberate link and contribution among humanitarian, resilience, and development. Whilst Oxfam is already doing resilience programming, then certain new shocks will be assisted through this project in design
