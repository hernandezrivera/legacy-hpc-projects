Project Summary

Mercy Corps will target newly displaced IDP and vulnerable conflict affected HHs affected by the ongoing conflict in Khartoum, South Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Gedaref, Kassala, Central Darfur and Red Sea States that are experiencing an inability to meet their immediate needs for survival due to acute food insecurity and poor access to basic services and practices as a result of the severe crisis. Displacement and conflict results in both the immediate loss of households’ livelihoods and an inability to meet their needs; it also affects their ability to regain their livelihood and access income. Economic decline across the country and high food prices result in conflict affected households having little cash to cover their food and other needs.
Mercy Corps will provide multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to IDP and CAP households to improve their abilities to meet immediate basic needs and reduce use of negative coping mechanisms. A Mercy Corps market assessment conducted in January 2024 along with secondary market assessments demonstrate that although local markets are heavily disrupted and face price inflation for specific items, in the target states are still functioning and able to provide basic food commodities and essential items.
The proposed MPCA transfer value reflects 100% of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) approved by the Cash Working Group in recognition of (1) the gap left by the pause in food security activities led by the World Food Programme (WFP) and (2) the inflationary impacts since the start of the crisis. CAPs are either sheltering close to home or have fled the fighting with little or no possessions. MPCA will provide a flexible and dignified approach to enable them to meet basic needs during the immediate months following the start of the crisis. This approach will also bolster continued market functionality through the crisis. Where possible, Mercy Corps will layer the household level MPCA interventions proposed alongside market systems strengthening interventions at the vendor level supported through other donors.
In Sudan, conflict affected households are facing the immediate loss of livelihoods and an inability to meet their needs in both displacement and hosting locations and in locations where households have remained and sheltered. Food security remains a critical concern in the target areas and is exacerbated by the current conflict in the country. Increases in food and other commodity prices, a reduced harvest, and the continued conflict indicate that acute food insecurity in Sudan continues to worsen. Multipurpose cash interventions play a crucial role in addressing immediate food needs by enabling beneficiaries to purchase nutritious food items from local markets. Given challenges with in-kind distributions and the setting up of commodity supply routes - we propose cash and market support as the core modality to address food insecurity and access to basic non-food items.
Moreover, cash transfers can be utilized in different ways to meet the needs of those who are displaced by the current crisis such as using the money to pay for transportation to safer locations, communicating with family members, and reducing the potential use of negative coping strategies. Multipurpose cash interventions provide the necessary flexibility to address the unique circumstances and priorities of households and individuals in these regions. By allowing beneficiaries to decide how best to allocate the cash, this approach recognizes and respects the diverse needs, preferences, and cultural contexts within these communities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mercy Corps' protection mainstreaming approach in the MPCA program includes several key components that include conducting thorough risk and vulnerability assessments to identify specific protection risks and designing interventions that address them. Engaging with affected communities, including women, youth, and marginalized groups, will ensure their active participation and empower them to contribute to decision-making processes. The program will also adopt a gender-sensitive and socially inclusive approach, addressing gender inequality, GBV, and ensuring equal access to MPCA services for all individuals. In addition, Mercy Corps will collaborate with local authorities, community leaders, and relevant stakeholders to strengthen existing protection mechanisms and referral pathways. By fostering coordination among service providers, local capacities to prevent and respond to protection incidents will be enhanced. Robust monitoring and accountability mechanisms will be established to track protection outcomes and address any concerns that may arise during the program implementation. Feedback mechanisms will be in place to allow beneficiaries to report protection-related issues and ensure their voices are heard. Mercy Corps acknowledges the importance of coordination and partnerships in effective protection mainstreaming. We will actively engage with relevant protection actors, including UN agencies, local civil society organizations, and other humanitarian partners, to ensure a comprehensive and coordinated response to the protection needs of crisis-affected populations. The proposed MPCA program in South Darfur, South Kordofan, North Kordofan, and Khartoum (if the situation allows) places a strong emphasis on protection mainstreaming. By integrating protection considerations throughout the program cycle and addressing the specific risks and challenges in each area, Mercy Corps aims to ensure the safety, dignity, and well-being of crisis-affected populations.

Through our CARM and Safeguarding practices, Mercy Corps sensitizes participating communities on three feedback mechanisms - our integrity hotline, feedback box and a third which is designed directly with the community. Our CARM system will also include provisions for people with disabilities to effectively share feedback throughout the program cycle to ensure that their voices are heard, their needs are considered, and that they benefit from assistance.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps is aware that the distribution of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to a targeted group can create tensions in the local community. Considering this, Mercy Corps will target both IDPs and vulnerable host communities to avoid creating tensions between these two groups. Mercy Corps has previous experience implementing cash-based interventions in South Kordofan State and has found that there is a high level of humanitarian need there, with many households wanting to participate.
