Project Summary

projet There is a direct link in Cameroon between access to education and child labour, and they mutually influence each other. In the NW regions, child labour is an immediate risk resulting from absences from school . According to the latest available data, 39% of children between five and 17 years old are involved in child labour in Cameroon (40% of Cameroonian boys and 38% of Cameroonian girls) . Children and adolescents are mainly supporting their families by working as domestic workers or are involved in agricultural activities. With North west inclusive, child labour has been reported. In this region, girls separated from their families – sometimes as a result of displacement – are likely to work as salespeople, tailors, or domestic workers . Children have an increased risk of adopting negative coping strategies to sustain their livelihoods, such as cross-border trade, kidnapping, theft, and prostitution. According to OCHA Situation Report of June 2021, 2.2 million people are affected by the armed conflict in NWSW. The Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) data, states that 12,719 persons were displaced in June alone, 2021. The month of June 2021 recorded 890 protection incidents. This is almost double the figures of May where about 458 incidents were reported. Most protection cases relate to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), early marriage, early and unplanned pregnancies, sexual abuse, and CAAFAG all affecting children & adolescents as a direct outcome of heightened protection risks due to the crisis.
