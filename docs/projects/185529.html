Project Summary

International Medical Corps (IMC) will work alongside the International Rescue Committee to implement an integrated protection intervention focusing on mitigating and responding to child protection and gender-based violence risks in the Far North region. The project will aim at providing a safe and secure environment for women and children among Nigerian refugees and vulnerable households from host communities to both reinforce the community protective environment and strengthen access to basic services for the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals. Building on IMC & IRC’s child protection experience, the two organizations will focus on two components. The first component will aim at strengthening the protective environment for children, through building local capacities of key actors including government actors, community members and caregivers on protection including mental and psychosocial impact, to facilitate behaviour change in communities and with caregivers. Considering that behaviour change can only be achieved through a sustained intervention. Capacity building will be scaled up throughout the project with increased support to local structures (government and community-based actors) to lead and coordinate activities. Youth empowerment activities for vulnerable youth will be integrated into the protection response aiming to strengthen their resilience and facilitate the development of life skills. The second component will focus on emergency response to child protection risks faced by vulnerable and at-risk children affected by the conflict and will be implemented through case management, development of safe learning and healing spaces and support for civil documentation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) remains widespread and one of the main rights violations affecting women and girls in the Far North region due to the crisis as well as the patriarchal system and cultural barriers. Between January and December 2020, 2,027 cases of GBV were recorded in the GBVIMS by UNFPA in the Far North, with 1,023 (50.4%) reported by IRC and 536 (26.4%) cases reported by IMC. 99% of the reported cases are women and girls, and 24% of the cases involved children under 18, including unaccompanied or separated children. Intimate partner violence (IPV) represents 78%, or 806 of cases reported to IRC (from January to December 2020). This situation has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic due to restrictive measures such as confinement and the closure of schools, leading young girls and women to have reduced access to protective services. According to 90% of the community focal points interviewed during a security audit conducted by IRC in May 2020, there has been an increase in violence within their communities. Adolescent girls aged 10 to 17 are particularly at risk and can face multiple protection violations simultaneously including GBV, exploitation, and early or forced marriage. The coordination mechanisms for a holistic approach for case management of GBV survivors through security and legal services remains weak. Moreover, despite the support from IMC, IRC, and other partners, community-based organizations still lack the full capacity to engage and lead in prevention and response activities, and need additional capacity building, specifically in behaviour change curriculums and management skills. The sustained and high number of GBV cases show the clear need to further involve men/husbands in accountability practices and promotion of gender equality in homes and with religious and community leaders.Nigerian refugee children and other vulnerable children including IDPs and returnees continue to be exposed to the risk of exploitation, abuse, violence and neglect. In 2020, approximately 342,000 children were displaced, representing 61% of the total displaced population (DTM OIM Round 21 December 2020). These new displacements have exacerbated pre-existing challenges in the Far North related to access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of socio-economic opportunities for adolescents and young people needed to realize their full potential. According to UNHCR, 12,434 (7,736 F and 4,698 M) children and young adults from Minawao camp and its surrounding areas need qualified training for socio-professional integration. Family separations due to sudden population displacements continue to occur, increasing exposure to protection violations among the most vulnerable, including women and children. Children who do not have a birth certificate, including those who have lost them during displacement, face multi-faceted challenges in accessing education and protection services due to the lack of civil documentation which is required for school diplomas. According to the Ministry of Basic Education in January 2021, out of 950,574 children registered in primary schools (from grade I to grade VI); 400,979 do not have birth certificates and are exposed to the risk of statelessness.As of January 2021, the COVID-19 situation in Cameroon has drastically deteriorated. Since January 1, 2021, there have been 53,639 confirmed COVID-19 cases reported, representing 67% of the total cases recorded to date. From January 1st to April 30th , 2021 in Cameroon, the case fatality rate for COVID-19 also rose from 1.5% to 1.9%2. Of key concern in the current pandemic is the Far North region, which hosts over 115,000 Nigerian refugees and 1,032,000 internally displaced persons.
