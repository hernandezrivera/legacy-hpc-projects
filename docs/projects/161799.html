Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. An operational base was opened in Kukawa city, Kukawa LGA in 2018 to deploy health, nutrition and protection activities in Kukawa LGA, and more recently in Monguno. This presence enhanced PUI capacity to provide a rapid health and nutrition response following the influxes of IDPs which have occurred in late December.
PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
Given the persisting humanitarian needs within Borno State, and the increased security threats towards the civilian resulting in afflux of IDPs in Maiduguri, Monguno and other perceived safe LGA, the proposed project aims to improve access to basic services through an integrated health and nutrition response for conflict affected populations, based on a community approach.
PUI has been supporting a 24/7 reproductive health care services in two PHCC and ANC/PNC in three outreach sites in Bolori II MMC. PUI has plans to start sexual reproductive health in Monguno in 2020. Beside the 24/7 reproductive health care, PUI is also running ANC, PNC and under-five years immunization in the 5 health facilities in Bolori II in MMC.
PUI rolled out MHPSS activities in December 2018 in MMC and in February 2019 in Monguno. This was after PUI identified that IDPs seen at its supported health facilities are among the most vulnerable group to mental health issues. Over 62% of all consultations were delivered to IDP populations presenting with symptoms like somatic pains, sleeping difficulties and low mood.
Following the high burden of malnutrition in Bolori II, PUI is providing life-saving nutrition interventions since 2016 integrated with Primary Healthcare; Food Security and Livelihoods; Protection and Community Outreach; and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). PUI implemented OTP activities in collaboration with MoH at the PHCC Kukawa from August to December 2018. Following attacks and instability in Kukawa with health facility hosting Outpatient Treatment Program for SAM cases affected, PUI nutrition activities were relocated to Monguno. In Monguno, nutrition activities are and will be limited to Infant and Young Child Feeding intervention because there are several partners currently implementing nutrition activities including 5 OTP and 1 stabilization centre.

1 - Needs

SMART (Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions) survey conducted by PUI (November, 2017) showed prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at 12.4%, Stunting at 49.3% and underweight at 38.8%. According to the WHO standards, the above levels of malnutrition are classified as ‘Serious’ for GAM and ‘very high prevalence’ for stunting and underweight respectively. Similarly, the routine surveillance data collected from PUI’s active and passive MUAC screenings by Community Nutrition Mobilisers (CNMs) and health workers respectively indicate high proportion of malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months. Whereas humanitarian CMAM interventions in Bolori II majorly address Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) among children 6-59 still remains unaddressed. From the active MUAC screening data collected by PUI between May 2018 and January 2019, over a quarter (30%) of children aged 6-59 are moderately malnourished yet there is no intervention so far for MAMIYCF KAP surveys conducted by PUI in 2018 reflect that overall, IYCF practices within the community are sub-optimal. Especially on complementary feeding practices. Less than half (48%) of the surveyed population met the minimum dietary diversity requirement (PUI KAP Survey, 2018). An Urban and Socio-Economic Study conducted by PUI (June, 2018) shows that more than half (58.5%) of the population in Bolori District still have to travel long distances (more than 5km) to access health and nutrition.Women and children represent up to 87% of the newly displaced population (North-East Nigeria: Humanitarian Situation Update - Progress on key activities from the 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, January 2019 edition (covering 1 - 31 January 2019). Displacement has caused additional vulnerability, including risk of sexual violence and other forms of GBV, separation of families and children and psychosocial trauma. The separation of families, the lack of basic living necessities (e.g., Food, shelter, blankets, clothes, and water) and the accompanying public health and protection issues have placed the displaced populations in a heightened vulnerable state with risks of further violence and crime, including gender-based violence.Protection mainstreaming and Inclusion of person with Disabilities and older people The Nutrition program, especially through the IYCF component directly empowers women and mothers in the community through: Identification of mothers to take part in the Mother Care Groups activities to raise awareness on IYCF key messages. Through community consultation at the beneficiary selection phase, PUI will ensure that the community is aware of – and in support of – the beneficiary selection criteria to reduce risk of tension arising from the assistance and that the most vulnerable populations are selected for the intervention; - Creating strong community linkages between women and mothers in the community through MCGs - Promoting the election of Lead Mothers within each MCGs - Through lessons learnt from previous programming, targeted populations have provided valuable feedback into this component. Among those are creating additional MCGs as many women within the community had growing interest, better integration into PUI protection and Food Security programming, and the need for identification and treatment of MAM. Moreover, all Nutrition outreach workers are provided with protection mainstreaming trainings in addition of induction sessions on the safe identification and referral of protection issues to relevant protection officers internally, in addition to training on PUI’s code of conduct and humanitarian principles.

2 - Activities

Nutrition activities will be implemented closely with other inter-related sectors such as Health, Food Security and Protection, and focus on prevention, detection and treatment for the most vulnerable groups. In addition to IYCF-E activities, PUI will also manage acute malnutrition cases through a CMAM program. Children aged 6-59 months identified with SAM will be enrolled in OTP for treatment with Ready to Use Therapeutic foods (RUTF). While Children aged 6-23 months identified with MAM for treatment with Micro-Nutrient Powder. Nutrition teams monitor anthropometric measurements such as MUAC and Weight for Height ratio on a weekly basis, and distribute therapeutic food until treatment is complete. To ensure follow up and reduce defaulter rates in the community, CNMs support by tracking defaulter children and sensitizing caretaker on the importance of maintaining treatment. Once children with MAM are enrolled, MNP distribution will take place in PUI managed health facilities, in the OTP departments, targeting children between 6 and 23 months. According to UNICEF and other partners’ practices, each child will receive a one-year support. Each child will receive two packets of MNPs per visit for a total of three visits in an interval of four months between each visits. The primary aim for giving MNPs is to improve nutrient intake from complementary foods by children and thus help preventing from micro-nutrient deficiencies and deterioration of MAM cases in to SAM. Children with SAM who develop medical complications, will be referred to a PUI supported Stabilization Center (SC) within the existing PHCC compound of N’garanam in Bolori II in Maiduguri. Cases will be referred from PUI supported OTP and from OTPs run by other partners in Maiduguri to the stabilization center. The referral focal point informs the SC to anticipate arrival. Nutrition staff accompany the patient and confirm the admission to the SC. PUI will have IYCF interventions with focus on preventive Social Behavior Change Communication through a network of community outreach volunteers and Mother Care Groups. Within PUI facilities, dedicated staff will be trained on IYCF modules (Trainings of CNMs and Lead Mothers will follow the MoH/UNICEF training curricula) and nutrition education sessions will be conducted on a weekly basis for caregivers. IYCF outreach activities will be conducted within the targeted areas through multiple levels such as Community Nutrition Mobilisers (CNMs) - in collaboration with PUI’s Protection Community Outreach Workers (COWs) - will disseminate key messages. CNMs will be trained on IYCF modules, counselling and MUAC screening and will be supervised by PUI Nutrition team. CNMs roles and responsibilities will include; Coverage area of approximately 200 HHs, Training and supervision of Lead Mothers, including on-the-job mentoring, monitoring sessions and information refresher sessions (every 2 weeks); MUAC screening and referral of SAM and MAM children U5 to OTP/SC for treatment; and monitoring and information sharing on population influx, trends and observations. MCGs will be established through CNMs following a dialogue with the concerned communities. The beneficiaries of IYCF will be pregnant and lactating women as well as caregiver of children aged of 0-24 months. Separate sessions for men will be organized in Monguno following cultural norms

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through:(1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community and a toll-free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff
