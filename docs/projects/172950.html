Project Summary

The purpose of the intervention is to provide vulnerable populations with equitable access to safe water supplies, dignified and safe latrines and bathing facilities and to provide information on critical hygiene promotion information and training with the aim of changing behaviour away from potentially harmful practices. The provision of the services will focus on inclusive access for all and will consider especially the needs of women, children, the elderly and people living with disabilities. The target population are 243,151 Rohingya refugees living in 8 refugee camps and 100,000 Bangladeshi people living in Cox's Bazar district who are affected by the refugee influx.

1 - Identified Needs

Improved Water Services: 50% of refugees in UNICEF camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks which are being constructed under the Sector’s Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tubewells fitted with handpumps. There is a need to complete the Sector Water Network plan and ensure all refugees are accessing safe, chlorinated water from the piped networks and to reduce the reliance on tubewells and handpumps which have a risk of contamination. Access to Sanitation: 99% of households report using pit latrines in the camps however functionality is recorded at 78%. There is a need to improve operation & maintenance including community engagement for cleaning & safe operation. Just under half of the refugees state facing challenges of accessing toilests due to overcrowding or distance from households. There is a need to ensure inclusive use of all latrines by women, children, people living with disabilities and the elderly and that all latrines are appropriate for safe and dignified use by all. There is still a significant need for further construction of sanitation and hygiene infrastructure to bring coverage up to minimum Sector standards and ensure equitable distribution. There is a need to increase coverage of FSM sites to ensure all latrines are being emptied regularly and the sewage safely treated and disposed of. Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge in the camps, with only 33% of the refugee households reporting using household bins or shared garbage bins Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours remain low with for example, rates of households reporting washing their hands after handling children’s faeces at 32% , and appropriate safe water handling at household level where there are high levels contamination of stored water. Overall, 26% of households reported presence of human faeces in their areas , likely due to the lack of safe disposal of faeces of children under 5 years old, who are also reported as practicing open defecation regularly (46%). A total of 91% of households reported having soap, with 94% of men and 93% of women reporting washing their hands after using the latrine. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management, 24% of women and girls were reported as not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months. In addition, 20% of women and girls were reported as practicing safe disposal of MHM materials. There is a need to maintain covid 19 infection, prevention and control measures through communuity enagement and behaviour change. Cox’s Bazar District has some of the worst water and sanitation indicators in the country according to the 2019 WASH Mapping undertaken by the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE) with support from UNICEF and the International Centre for Diarrhoeal Disease Research, Bangladesh. The sector lacks adequate national budget allocations while the district is regularly affected by cyclones and floods. Seventy-six per cent of households have a basic water supply but only 18 per cent have access to safely managed water . Twenty-nine per cent of households use safely managed latrines and 47 per cent of households have handwashing facilities in their premises, where only 33 per cent households practice handwashing and 28 per cent households dispose of faeces from children under 5 properly. Women also reported difficulties accessing MHM materials and 39 per cent of women are able to take up proper menstrual hygiene practices .

2 - Activities or outputs

Provision of appropriate quantities safe water to refugees and host community through construction, rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water supply schemes, water quality monitoring, water safety planning, capacity development of partners and communities to safely manage sustainable water supplies. WASH in schools and health centres. Provision of improved access to safe sanitation through the construction, rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of latrines, bathing spaces, sewage treatment facilities and solid waste management facilities. Capacity development of partners and communities to safely manage sanitation services. Community Led Total Sanitation and Sanitation Marketing activities. Provision of hygiene services through participatory behaviour change approaches, community engagement and mobilisation, provision of essential hygiene supply items. Covid 19 infection prevention and control activities. Capacity development of partners and communities to safely manage hygiene activities. WASH Sector Coordination. WASH Assessments, monitoring and evaluation activities, Knowledge management.
