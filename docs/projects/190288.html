Project Summary

The ongoing conflicts in western part of Ethiopia left hounded of thousands people internally displaced in Benishangule Gumize and west wellega. According to assessment conducted in Feb 2022, Benishangule Gumize region hosts 410,000 internally displaced people. Data obtained from the region RDM indicated that Bullen woreds alone hoste74511 IDP’s. There are about 10,198 male HHs (37,184 IDPs) and 2,066 female HHs (29,623 IDPs) recorded in West Wollega zone needing urgent humanitarian response. The number of IDP’s in Mendi and Nejo town west welega zone are 5153 and 2405 respectively.In addition to these 566 people in Mendi and 282 in Nejo towns also require assistance.
The IDPs are dispersed in the host community and most of them are living outside in sub-standard makeshift shelters or rental houses which they struggle to afford. Many IDPs needs access to shelter and are forced to sleep in overcrowded spaces including emergency shelters or communal sites. Majority of the displaced communities left without basic humanitarian aid and are living in camp like sites in sub-standard shelters. It’s clear that the IDPs do not have access to adequate and standard shelters at this time. Homes of the non displaced and returnee are either fully or partially damaged. Besides shelter needs the IDPs are reported to lack basic household items such as cooking materials, water reservoirs and bedding materials which obliges them to share with others.

The project has been designed based on the existing plans; priorities and approaches set out by the government of Ethiopia and respective region-zone-district stake holder analysis, and directly linked to the 2022 draft Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). The proposed project has direct linkage with the the Emergency Shelter and NFI cluster, i.e., ensure contextualized access to lifesaving shelters and NFIs for displacement affected populations with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignity and Contribute to improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions. Therefore, this project will contribute a lot in meeting already identified strategic objectives under 2022 draft Ethiopia HRP. Ensure contextualized access to lifesaving shelter and NFIs for displacement affected population with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignified, accountable and equitable access to critical and essential.
The project considers active participation of both men and women throughout the implementation decision-making process starting from beneficiary selection up to distribution and PDM survey.
During project staff selection and recruitment, UCD will promote gender balance and upper management will encourage the hiring of female project staff.
In every step of the planning and implementation process, the project will target the most vulnerable groups, including pregnant women, children under five, the elderly, marginalized adolescent girls and boys, HIV-affected groups, and persons living with a disability. Continuation of this practice will be ensured by proper targeting and beneficiary selection.

1. - Projects activities

1.Conduct introductory meetings to share clear communication about the project and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process. UCD will held preliminary meeting with representatives of the affected communities and Woreda and Kebele officials. During the meeting UCD field staffs will explain about the purpose of the assistance, its tagrate group and other relevant information. 2.In consultation with the woreda and kebele administrations, CCCM ,DRM representatives, UCD will setup beneficiaries registration committee, in which women, elderly, disable person and other vulnerable groups would be represented.3. UCD will establish complain and feedback mechanism where beneficiaries have access to complain or report any feed backs from the beneficiary targeting, registration and verification process.4.UCD will work with communities and other relevant stake holders to identify and register beneficiaries.After completion of the joint registration of beneficiaries, UCD will conduct beneficiary verification independently. The verification will be carried out at the household level by conducting a damage assessment. Groups with specific needs that will be prioritized for assistance, in accordance with the Shelter/NFI Cluster endorsed vulnerability criteria, include girls and boys at risk, including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with serious health conditions, persons with special legal or physical protection needs, single women, women-headed households, older persons, persons with disabilities. To allow the public verification on the targeted household’s the final list will be posted at the Kebele administration office and other visible areas. Assigned focal staff from UCD in collaboration with IDP representative will address complain regarding identification, registration and verification of beneficiaries. Every complain or feedback will be recorded, reported and responded. 5.UCD conduct procurement and transportation of NFI, ESNFI Eergency shelter and Emergency shelter repair kits to the project areas. Kits are in-line with ES/NFI Cluster standards and include disable and inclusion kits.6.UCD undertake market assessment and mapping of the potential emergency shelter, Shelter repair kits, ESNFI and NFI kit suppliers and banks in the intervention areas, to establish market functionality and availability the items and banks to ensure the smooth transfer of the cash assistance and to link beneficiaries with the market. 7. UCD will conduct in kind distribution of NFI, ESNFI Emergency shelter and Emergency shelter repair kits. Before and during distributions, UCD will explain to beneficiaries what the distributed items are and how to use them properly (especially hygiene items and plastic sheets). This can be done using pictures, banners, posters, or boards posted in public locations that are accessible to all. UCD will deliver the items to shelters of vulnerable beneficiaries like people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, and elders. If they come to distribution site, they will be prioritized and receive their entitlement first and help them to leave the distribution points early and ensure their safely and dignity. 8.UCD will distribute cash to cash based intervention beneficiary households in alignment with Shelter/NFI Cluster and Cash Working Group guidance, cash will be provided equivalent to the cost of the standardized kits for each HH considering the existing market situation through bank.9.UCD will establish complaint hearing committees comprised of men and women beneficiaries, put suggestion boxes to receive feedback, and post the phone numbers of UCD staff in visible locations. The project staff will ensure the system is in place to deal with serious complaints, such as an allegation of sexual abuse, fraud, and other sensitive issues. The beneficiaries will be informed of their rights to make reasonable feedback and complaints and their right to receive responses within a certain time

Appropriate channels will be put in place to enable communities send feedback including community focal points, face-to-face feedback, or help desk where applicable. Confidential feedback systems will be developed using confidential phone numbers or community preferred persons as appropriate for the different groups. The project staffs integrate GBV cases tracing and referral into Shelter activities and incorporate protection and GBV themes.
