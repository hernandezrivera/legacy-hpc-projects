Project Summary

The proposed action aims at contributing towards the reduction of mortalities and morbidities associated with Malnutrition among under-five children and pregnant and lactating women by providing preventive and curative nutrition services and enhancing accessibility to nutrition services in Puntland, Somaliland and South Central. The proposed project is geared towards reducing the vulnerability within the fragile contexts of Somalia/Somaliland. The Project will be integrated within the other health care services to enhance the provision of life-saving basic and standard Health Nutrition and WASH services at community level, health facilities and IDP camps in urban areas. WVSOM through the proposed action plans to strengthen preventive and curative services. Continued community mobilization and sensitization will be crucial for awareness creation on the available services thus leading to increased up-take of Nutrition services and the other integrated Health and WASH services at community level. Community engagement and participation will also be crucial throughout the project cycle to ensure ownership and sustainability of the project activities. WVSOM will work with community health workers at community level to support in the implementation of the project. To enhance effective implementation of the projects, WVSOM will actively participate in nutrition cluster coordination mechanisms both at national and Zonal level. The proposed action plans to support early warning mechanisms, alert and response systems/surveillance with close linkages to the respective sectors to ensure early response to prevent the situation from worsening. WV Som will also work towards building the capacity of MOH to spearhead quality service provision to the target communities through support health care workers, trainings, supportive supervisions and through provision of essential drugs, and essential supplies to the target health care facilities.

Needs

Recurrent drought and subsequent famine risk have become a devastating and increasingly unsustainable cycle in Somalia in recent decades. The impact of drought on the Somali people is compounded by an interrelated set of factors that include the environment, governance, conflict, displacement and poverty. Food insecurity and scarcity of drinking water, coupled with displacement, have contributed to a stark rise in malnutrition and water-borne disease. According to the nutrition services delivery sites Geotagging exercise conducted by Forcier to assess the capacity of health facilities and Nutrition sites across Somaliland, Puntland and central and southern Somalia, emerges that some regions lack basic services, supplies and infrastructure at fixed nutrition sites. Across the three zones, mobile teams play a key role in delivering nutrition services in remote and isolated areas. Unfortunately, limited personnel and resources affect routine mobile services while the fixed sites are hard to reach for most isolated rural communities with key strain on available facilities during emergencies. The results highlight the need to focus on the quality of nutrition services provided, specifically training personnel to provide nutrition services in an emergency. According to the nutrition situation summary for Food Security and Nutrition (FSNAU) SOMALIA - GU 2018, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence were considered Critical (15-30%) with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) also considered Critical (≥4.0-5.6%) within the target regions. Morbidity rates are at least 20 percent or higher in more than half of the surveyed populations, contributing to the reported high levels of acute malnutrition in most of these populations.Currently, in Somalia it is estimated that 294,200 children under the age of five are acutely malnourished, including 55,200 children under the age of five who are severely malnourished and face increased risk of morbidity and death.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

WVSOM will work towards building the capacity of the communities and households in Somalia to anticipate, adapt to, and recover from the effects of hazards (natural disasters, economic stability, conflict) in a way that protects livelihoods, accelerates and sustains recovery, and supports economic and social development. The proposed action will strengthen the health care system to deliver resilient outcomes, and to prevent and counter the effects of shocks and stress. WVSOM while working in collaboration with the Government will capacity build the target populations so that they can be able to anticipate, adapt and recover from shocks without malnutrition spikes or damaging coping strategies. WVSOM will implement evidence based nutrition programs considering the lessons learnt over time at the same time increase levels of technical assistance and support to the Health care system and the MOH as it is only through building robust and sustainable systems that deliver on resilience that hunger and malnutrition can be ultimately addressed. The project will ensure effective linkage and referral pathways that ensure comprehensive service provision while addressing underlying causes of malnutrition at the same time enhancing continuity and sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

WVSOM will ensure equal access for women and girls to integrated Health and Nutrition services, Health and Nutrition promotion activities, training programs and equal chance to linkage to other livelihood programs. The program will include both host communities, IDP and refugees so as to mitigate discrimination and resentment. WVSOM will put in place regular monitoring and feedback mechanisms to ensure that women and Girls are not violated and taken advantage for them to access services. The project will also work towards providing a platform and Negotiating for the participation of women and youth in leadership and decision-making both at community and household level on factors affecting their health and nutrition status. During project implementation WVSOM will also advocate for the linkage and inclusion of displaced and locally integrating populations to prevent discrimination.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender inequalities in economic participation and opportunity. Despite high labour force participation by men and women, women are more disproportionately represented in unpaid subsistence and domestic work (reproductive gender roles), which is not accounted for in Labour Force Participation. Gender inequalities in economic opportunity are also reflected in high wage inequality for similar work. Women occupy less technical and professional positions than men. These economic factors when aggregated make women earn considerably lower annual incomes compared with men. Disability limits physical access to health care, education, employment, and justice. It also leads to economic, political, and social exclusion of vulnerable groups. Women and girls with disabilities face double discrimination and are more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion. WVSOM will work towards ensuring universal inclusion of the vulnerable populations within the project.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WVSOM will focus on women and youth – to encourage interventions that are targeted to their particular needs, while acknowledging the crucial roles in household nutrition. The design and implementation of services will be based on a thorough analysis of local conflict dynamics so as to ensure that interventions, at a minimum, “do no harm”. WVSOM during the project implementation will ensure there is an inclusive community dialogue and start-up workshop including all community members and stakeholders aimed at enhancing ownership and programme effectiveness, thereby contributing to conflict mitigation. By ensuring equitable access to program services at the same time respecting the fundamental rights of the government and community members, and social protection will be used as a powerful tool in maintaining calm. WVSOM aims during the project implementation period to build the capacity the MOH and community to be able to participate in the program activities hence being used as influencers in achieving the Do No Harm principle. World Vision Somalia through its implementation and collaboration with MOH will work towards safeguarding the vulnerable groups and invest in social dialogue during the implementation of the project to understand key issues to address that contribute to conflicts hence have clear feedback mechanisms in place.
