Project Summary

The current humanitarian situation in Gaza under 13 years of closure, internal political divide, and most recently, COVID-19 outbreak is marked by increased deprivations of basic rights of around two million Palestinians (a half of whom are children) living in Gaza, including their access to adequate water and sanitation.
Over the last years, there has been a constant lack of investment in Gaza’s basic infrastructure, due to recurring escalations of the conflict as well as inability to bring in repair materials. As a result, the supply of basic services has been unable to keep up with the increasing demand caused by Gaza’s population growth, whilst the conditions of the infrastructure have been deteriorated because of general decay over time and damages inflicted during the periodic hostilities.
This project will improve the vulnerable communities’ access to safe drinking and domestic water, and safe access to sanitation and hygiene services, and will support the preparedness and response measures to protect the vulnerable communities living in high flood prone areas from the risk of displacement, damage of property, lack of access to public services, and public health impact. It will strengthen the response to routine and sudden essential WASH services and increase the preparedness for winter season in order to avoid flooding and shortage of critical equipment and spare parts. In addition, it will mitigate the health risks related to the COVID 19 epidemic as it will ensure that WASH facilities continue to function in case of further deterioration of the situation.
Through this project, UNICEF aims at providing WASH preparedness and response to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza in order to avoid the discontinuity of WASH facilities operation and maintenance which would have an adverse public health impact.
The planned activities consist in the provision of critical spare parts and specialized services for repairs and refurbishment of water and wastewater facilities to reduce and eliminate the risks of public health deterioration and environmental pollution through:
1) Providing essential operation and maintenance materials and tools for the critical water and wastewater facilities to ensure public WASH facilities are safe and well equipped to respond to floods; and
2) Rehabilitating storm water drainage systems and sanitation networks, including installing storm water, wastewater pipes, manholes, repairing drainage systems, removing solid waste and sludge from the valley streams and the collapsed sand dunes from storm water collection/infiltration ponds to enhance infiltration capacity.

1 - Needs

Water and sanitation infrastructure and delivery have also been impacted by recurrent hostilities, over exploitation of resources and restrictions on the import of “dual use” WASH items.At the heart of this deterioration is an aggravation of Gaza’s longstanding electricity crisis. The long power outages had severe consequences on the operation of water treatment facilities, wastewater pumping stations, and drinkable/piped water supply. The increase in the electricity supply due to funding from the Government of Qatar – from 6.6 hours per day in 2018 to 12.1 hours in 2019 and 2020 on average – has improved slightly the operation of water facilities, but this support is not guaranteed for the future, and could be interrupted at any time. Furthermore, as a result of the global COVID-19 pandemic, movement restrictions have been introduced and applied in the Gaza Strip to prevent the contagion. This situation has resulted in a decrease in the collection of water bills, which is in turn affecting the service providers capacity to respond to the essential operation and maintenance of critical WASH infrastructure, including sewage treatment and provision of clean water.This intervention is designed to protect the most vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip from stormwater floods and mitigate the public health hazards and the environment from contamination or mixing of the stormwater with the wastewater flood from cesspits. Detailed needs assessments were done in coordination with the WASH Cluster and relevant authorities, as well as on the WASH responders’ reported needs and priorities. The findings extrapolated from those assessments were triangulated with the WASH cluster vulnerability map to ensure that this project will prioritize the most vulnerable communities. The project will support to the capacity building of public WASH service providers to react to routine and sudden needs, including preparedness for winter season, and shortage of critical operational and maintenance equipment. In addition, this intervention will contribute to enhance the WASH service providers’ capacity to mitigate the health risks related to the COVID-19 pandemic, and to ensure that WASH facilities continue to function in case of further deterioration of the crisis. This project considers and prioritizes the impact of potential shortages in public services of water, wastewater, and solid waste collection on vulnerable communities, especially those lacking the resilience to cope with those shocks.Based on consultations with the WASH cluster and in coordination relevant authorities, Norwegian Refugee Council conducted rapid needs assessment between 13th and17th September 2020. The assessment covered both the staff and WASH facilities' needs, including the classification of WASH facilities, and type of work of each staff. In addition, the assessment collected the maintenance requirements related to the operational WASH facilities, whose needs were reported by the focal points in each facility. In addition, needs assessment was conducted with all 25 Gaza municipalities between July and Sept 2019 to update a winter flood risk matrix. This tool registers all the public responders’ needs in order to boost their preparedness for the winter season and was conducted in cooperation with the WASH cluster and relevant authorities. The assessment was designed to list the localities subject to flooding risk during the winter season for each municipality, showing the most affected communities from potential flooding and number of affected persons, potential impact of the flooding on their properties, and their ability to access essential services during flood. The assessment specified the reason of flooding, the required intervention, and the estimated cost. Further assessment conducted by Action Against Hunger in September 2020 shows that the selected areas are prone to stormwater flood every winter and the households, markets, private and public assets have suffered from water leaking to their places and losses, where 12% of the households live in the area are not connected to wastewater networks and depend totally on cesspits to discharge their wastewater that have negative impacts during winter as they filled with stormwater and flood to the streets, and 6.5% of the households in the targeted areas were flooded with stormwater 1-3 time at least in the winter seasons.

2 - Activities

This project will improve vulnerable communities’ access to safe water and adequate sanitation through support WASH facilities in the Gaza Strip with essential operation and maintenance materials and tools. In addition, this project will mitigate flooding risks for vulnerable communities living in high flood prone areas through support the preparedness and response measures, and thus will eliminate displacement risk, damage of property, lack of access to public services, and public health impact. Activities location were selected based on the conducted need assessment in coordination with relevant authorities, and based on the WASH cluster vulnerability mapping. The planned activities consist in the provision of critical spare parts and specialized services for repairs and refurbishment of water and wastewater facilities to reduce and eliminate the risks of public health deterioration and environmental pollution through: 1) Protecting vulnerable communities from seasonal flooding in Khan Younis, Gaza and North Gaza through:1.1 Rehabilitation of stormwater systems including installing storm water, wastewater pipes, manholes, repairing drainage systems, removing solid waste and sludge from the valley streams to ensure people protected from seasonal flooding1.2 Provision of stormwater facilities with critical operation and maintenance materials and tools to ensure public stormwater facilities are safe and well equipped to respond to floods.2) Improving access to water and sanitation services in North Gaza, Gaza, Deir ALBalah, Khan Younis, and Rafah through: 2.1 The installation/ rehabilitation of wastewater pumping/ treatment unit to ensure improved access to sanitation 2.2 Provision of water facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and tools to ensure public water facilities are safe and well equipped2.3 Provision of wastewater facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and tools to ensure public wastewater facilities are safe and well equipped2.4 The rehabilitation or replacement of sewer and wastewater collection network to ensure improved access to sanitation. The communities who have interrupted WASH services face bad environment and health, particularly for women and children. Vulnerable children are the most exposed to waterborne diseases such as diarrhoea and skin infections, and without proper access to water and sanitation services, their basic hygiene needs cannot be covered, which have adverse impact on their health status. In addition, women face negative impact and additional stress as they are unable to carry out daily house chores - such as house cleaning, cooking and bathing children. The assessment was conducted to take into account the specific gender needs of men, women, boys and girls with the collection of disaggregated data. Furthermore, the water service providers will have increased awareness of gender- differentiated and disabilities related water needs, gaps, and restrictions to water access through project implementation. UNICEF considers that lives and dignity of all female and male beneficiaries including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly must be protected in all phases of the project. UNICEF will analyse and assesses relationships between women, men ,girls and boys, reviewing their respective roles, access to resources, control of resources, and constraints each group faces relative to one another. Gender along with other factors (such as age, gender identity, ethnicity, disability, education, employment, and location) will be considered to avoid further compound challenges in emergency responseUNICEF and its partners will carefully consider the special and persistent needs of women, children, the elderly and PWDs. These vulnerable groups will be consulted during the planning, design, and implementation of the interventions. In so doing, UNICEF and its partners will ensure responding to various needs of these groups in consideration of different social and traditional characteristics of the targeted communities.

3 - Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will monitor the implementation of the project in line with its standard monitoring procedures on a daily basis to ensure timely and effective implementation. Women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. The monitoring procedures include regular programme visits to the field where implementation progress, achievements of results and means of verification are systematically checked against the agreed framework including the projects indicators. Recommendations in response to the field visit findings, including bottlenecks are documented and systematically followed up on. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation, and the results will be communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism. The project will have a positive impact on both men and women, as the health risks will be significantly mitigated for the most vulnerable households benefitting from this project. As the risk of public health illness will also be reduced, this will alleviate the burden especially of women avoiding additional house chores and attending for sick children associated with meeting their personal hygiene needs, undertaking basic domestic chores, managing household water needs, and securing the needs of children, people with disabilities, the elderly and the chronically ill. For men who are usually the breadwinners, the burden on livelihood assets will be eased with the risks of health hazards being mitigated. The following results will be achieved: 1) 32,497 people (8,449 men, 8,124 women, 7,800 boys, 7,799 girls and 325 persons with disability) will be protected from seasonal flooding through:Indicator 1.1: # storm water systems installed/ rehabilitatedIndicator 1.2: # of stormwater facilities provided with operation and maintenance materials and tools 2) 94,335 people (24,527 men, 23,584 women, 22,670 boys, 22,654 girls and 900 persons with disability) will benefit from improved access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services through:Indicator 2.1: # of wastewater facilities rehabilitated Indicator 2.2: # of water facilities provided with operation and maintenance materials and tools Indicator 2.3: # of wastewater facilities provided with operation and maintenance materials and tools Indicator 2.4: # of meter length of sewer network rehabilitated

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF is leading the child protection working group, and is member of the protection cluster, applying the principles of child protection to different projects. Through the whole project cycle, UNICEF and its partners ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles considering safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment. As a specific protection mainstreaming component, UNICEF and its partners wherever applicable will coordinate with other protection and human rights agencies to ensure their engagement in the project activities as needed. Hence, this HRP project is designed in a manner that avoids harm and risks to the targeted communities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This HRP project is designed in a manner that enables equal and impartial access to assistance and services to the targeted communities. It was developed through need assessments to strengthen WASH systems capacity and improve water and sanitation access for all age, gender and diversity groups. Interviews target community representatives from men and women and include them in all project stages with regards to priority setting and the design of monitoring, evaluation and reporting systems.
