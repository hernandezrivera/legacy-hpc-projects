Project Summary

Considering the needs and in line with the first pillar of No Lost Generation Initiative,the context analysis and the current demographic trends, the intervention aims at re-establishing basic education community services in the targeted areas.The intervention is tailored to the different operational setting,adapting activities to rural and urban contexts.Areas of intervention are return areas,where population is increasing and services will have to cope with the additional stress of the population growth. Therefore, GVC opts for a community support reducing to the minimum individual assistance.This approach is relevant to returnees’ contexts,preparing the ground for sustainable intervention to benefit boys and girls of 5-17 years old affected by the crises and improving resilience of the reached communities. The intervention aims at increasing access to safe,equitable and quality education for children and adolescents affected by the crisis in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor Governorates, relying on GVC expertise in NFE(solid reputational basis and knowledge of the selected areas. First,to increase access GVC will implement a package of tailored activities, targeting out of school children(OOSC) through a spread Back-to-Learning(BTL)campaign (door to door visits, awareness and information campaigns, dialogue sessions, interactive theatre events to involve OOSC) aimed at encouraging them and their families to re-enrol in school, in cooperation with local partners such as DoE and SARC volunteers. In addition,access to school will be enhanced by providing Remedial classes to children at risk of dropping out, in line with standards provided by Ministry of Education and Education Sector. In line with the same goal, GVC will involve OOSC in Self-Learning programs(SLP)aimed at filling the gaps of those who lost 6 months or more of attendance. Children and teachers attending Remedial classes and SLP will also receive school bags and stationery. Finally, students and OOSC will be involved in edu-entertainment activities which will be implemented both as a part of each one of the over-mentioned initiatives and as ad hoc events aimed at improving life-skills and KSA (Knowledge Skills Attitude) and supporting the psycho-social development of vulnerable boys and girls. More specifically, each event will focus on a different topic, ability or life learning, which may range from stressing the importance of education, promoting hygiene practices to highlighting the relevance of ambition and aspirations to achieve personal goals. Secondly, safe and inclusive education will be guaranteed through rehabilitation and equipment of school buildings, which will include solutions to enable access to students with special needs,and gender and disability-sensitive WASH facilities.The rehabilitation of classes will increase the number of available safe learning places and directly have a positive effect in enrolment rate.In addition, furniture of teaching and learning material will be delivered to schools in need.Finally,the intervention will improve quality education through training courses for teachers in Active Learning/Psycho-social Support (ALT/PSS) and in Risk Education (MRE-Mine Risk Education). This will improve teachers’ skills, contributing to better quality education for girls and boys. The courses will also include training in Self-Learning strategies, so that GVC can rely on trained teachers in ALT when selecting – in cooperation with the DoE– the trainers to provide Self-Learning programs. GVC already implements these “assessing practices” as a successful mean to ensure children are reached by quality information and knowledge.Finally, training in Risk Education will be included in the package and will raise teachers’ awareness of the behavioural changes needed to prevent harm from explosive devices; teachers will share the information and material received with children in their schools,thus contributing to reduce protection risks.

Needs

Through this proposal GVC intends to reduce the vulnerability of the People in Need(PIN),including IDPs or returnees,living in Aleppo Governorate(main focus of assessment:Menbij,al-Khafsa and Al-Hader sub-districts) and Deir-Ez-Zor city.Areas have been selected considering:sub-district severity scale(from 4 to 5 as per HNO 2019),returnees’ flows and GVC long-lasting field presence.The action has been designed using data from different sources:Historical information on the Education system (sources: Directorate of Education, key informants’ interviews, literature study);Analysis of secondary data (source: DoE);Analysis of qualitative data by other agencies (source: Reach, INSO on children);Infrastructure analysis on site by GVC Engineers.Main problems identified: 1. Insufficient safe and protective space for educational activities: original spaces of the school buildings are not enough to accommodate safely all the students in the areas.- Deir-Ez-Zor the access to education is granted to 27222 children (13010 boys and 14212 girls), compared to 84121 children (43600 boys,40521 girls) enrolled in the school year 2011/2012 with a decrease of 67%;currently only the 34,45% of the schools are operating. Schools building due to the conflict and the occupation by the IDPs do not guarantee conditions for students and teachers to recover the normal activities. Moreover, buildings are not disability sensitive, preventing the education access to students with special needs. Rehabilitation of schools remains one of the most urgent need (7 out of 19 neighbourhoods do not have any school available).- Rural Aleppo: the Education situation shows similar vulnerability, although the rural setting renders the needs more scattered. In several villages, education facilities are closed (e.g. in Hader only 11 out of 31 schools are operational), and students are hosted in inadequate spaces.2. High rate of school dropout: it has different causes: the limited number of functioning schools in the resident areas, the active participation of children in the familiar income, the high number of students per classroom and the low quality of education provided. - Deir-Ez-Zor city: since the ISIL control of the city prevented access to school to children for a long period of time, OOSC number is very high, estimated at 20000 individuals, and it is likely to raise due to flows of returnees. -Rural Aleppo: despite movement of return, enrolment in school is low.In Hader there is a -70% of students enrolled (against 2011). In the 5 areas of Al Khafsa considered, enrolment rates vary, but anyway are low (-27% in Tal Aswad, -25% in Al Janoubi, -50% in Mamoura). To properly determine the number of OOSC, door by door census and awareness on the importance of re-enrollment are needed. In both targeted areas, support services to OOSC children and students at risk of dropping out must be boosted.3. Lack of gender and disability sensitive water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities: this is a serious barrier to children’s access to learning, and a source of discrimination for female students and a risk of GBV. 4. Lack of teaching and learning materials and furniture: because of the conflict and the occupation of the school buildings by the IDPs or the opposition groups, in the schools there is scarcity or lack of adequate furniture and didactic material. Schools need to be equipped with teaching materials, while the vulnerable families don't succeed in facing the purchase of the didactic material. 5.Poor quality of education: Formal and Non formal education provision is weak and of poor quality both in Rural Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor.The quality of the teaching dropped-down as many teachers fled and/or were forced to quit their work. Teachers and facilitators have not the proper skills to deal with children who lost school years or are traumatized.There is high need to improve skills and update didactical approaches to allow them to serve vulnerable children.

Implementation of activities

1.1Conduct Back-to-Learning(BTL)campaigns: GVC will implement mass campaigns(1 in Rural Aleppo and 1 in Deir-Ez-Zor)for sensitizing OOSC,parents and community members.BTL will include communication activities as posters in key locations,flyers distribution and bus and water tankers’decoration showing messages on the importance of education;edu-entertainment activities for OOSC and families informing about the available non-formal and accelerated programs;door by door outreach initiative.The OOSC able to enrol will be accompanied to an available school;children unable to enrol but willing for a reinsertion will be oriented to an available Self Learning Program(SLP)or other non-formal education service available.1.2 Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs:The OOSC willing to be reinserted in the education path but with no condition to access directly to curriculum A/B will benefit from SLP that will be activated during the week-ends,second shift or holidays,in line with the guidelines of the Sector working group in NFE.SLP will be integrated with recreational activities. Activation of Remedial classes:OOSC directly reinserted in school and students at risk of drop out will need a period to catch up the level.Remedial classes to ensure retention will be established during week-ends and/or after school hours.Teachers and educators will be selected in collaboration with the DoE both for SLP and Remedial. 1.3Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms:this will ensure the opening of at least 10 schools in Rural Aleppo and 2 schools in Deir-Ez- Zor.The choice of schools follows criteria of cost efficiency and the urgent need of learning space in the communities;rehabilitation is in line with INEE Standards assuring access to people with special needs.1.4Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities:Rehabilitation of schools will comply with the gender and disability inclusiveness approach also for the rehabilitation of WASH facilities,in line with GVC expertise and INEE standards.This will decrease the risk of drop-out of girls and GBV.Rehabilitations will be managed by engineers of GVC staff,in coordination with technicians from DoE.1.5 Provide students with learning materials in formal and non-formal settings:To schools rehabilitated.Kits ‘composition respects the requirement of the cluster and GVC standard. 2.1.Provide professional development to teaching staff:GVC will provide training to teachers to improve their capacity to deal with OOSC in SLP, vulnerable boys and girls in formal education and students of Remedial courses:at least 450 educators will be trained on ALT,PSS curriculum and Risk Education.The reinsertion of OOSC and support of students at risk of dropping out requires educators to improve their capacity to relate with students,increasing the quality of education.PSS and ActiveLearning Training(ALT) will last 7 days.Teachers will acquire the right methodologies(e.g.use of alternative calendar)to deal with vulnerable boys and girls and create a supportive environment.The training includes Risk Education and deliver teachers the proper knowledge to address risks related to mines with their students. 2.2Provide teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in formal and non-formal settings:Teachers will be provided with guides and kits during training.2.4 Provide children with life skills and citizenship education programs in formal and non-formal settings.Edu-entertainment activities will be implemented as part of Remedial Classes and SLP and as ah hoc events for the development of life-skills.2.5Provide Protection Risk Analysis

a.Provide support to formal and non-formal education. Protection risk: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Likelihood: medium Impact: medium/high Mitigating Measure: Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education; Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed; If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform the emergency coordinator on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack.Protection risk: Attacks on schools Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating Measure: Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed; If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform the emergency coordinator on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. b.Provide support to non-formal education. Protection risk: The host community (including traditional members) rejects the presence of partners or the Project because the host communities feel unequally treated or threatened/stigmatized, disapprove of activities implemented, or other reasons.Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigating measure: Disseminate information about projects within the community and will also organize community events to build acceptance and provide services, in coordination with local authorities c.Construct new classrooms; establish classrooms in tents, pre-fabs or rented rooms; rehabilitate classrooms; provide schools with school furniture (desk, chairs, blackboards, etc.)Protection Risk: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigating Measure: Sector/cluster agreed minimum standards with members and local authorities commitmentProtection Risk: Risk of children being out of school due to unavailability of school furniture upon completion of school rehabilitation and construction of prefabricated classrooms Likelihood: mediumImpact: medium Mitigating Measure: Include school furniture in school rehabilitation plan or sign an MoU with authorities to ensure provision of school furniture by them upon completion of school rehabilitation d.Train teachers and education personnel on psychosocial supportProtection Risk: Increased risk of adding harm to children if knowledge is not applied appropriatelyLikelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigating Measure: Ensure that teachers are trained properly with pre and post assessment for validation.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: a. Security context in Deir-Ez-Zor and Rural Aleppo remains stable.b. Access to target areas is safe for staff and enterprises to conduct the foreseen rehabilitation and activities.c. The Ministry of Education maintain the planned priorities in the areas and the emergency strategy adopted.d. If schools assessed are not accessible anymore because of security reasons, Directorate of Education will select new schools in need of rehabilitatione. Schools and public buildings are accessible for the Self Learning Programs and remedial classes and beneficiaries can easily attend. f. The Syrian Ministry of Foreign Affairs/ Ministry of Education allows the use of public building and school for SLP and Remedial classes, g. Local communities and other agencies/actors on the zones of intervention will be positive in relation to the services made available h. Market prices are not subject to greater variations than those already foreseen and the material remains available on local marketsi. There is no high turnover among the implementing staff of the intervention, especially local partners' staff. The implementation of the intervention is challenged by some major risks. a. Security situation may deteriorate to the point to prevent the implementation of some of the activities. b. Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some intervention area due to military operation. c. Major change in demographic in the area of intervention due to population movements requires modification in the strategy.d. Major change in attitude of authorities and local partners (in particular Ministry of Education) toward the planned interventions. e. Diversion of aid from staff and volunteers of the local partner. Mitigation measures (linked to risks a. to e.):a. GVC ensures a continuous coordination with local partners (SARC) and international specialized agencies (UNDSS) in order to monitor the situation. In addition, GVC adopted a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident.b. Policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, Working Groups and Coordination Mechanisms.c. GVC monitors the demography in the area and liaise with other agencies in the area.d. GVC ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of the planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working. Plans of intervention (rehabilitation) are shared and discussed in order to increase acceptance.e. GVC ensures directly and constantly the monitoring and evaluation of the activities
