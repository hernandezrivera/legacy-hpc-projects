Project Summary

Rationale:
Based on WASH assessments in, 10.7 million people in Syria are estimated require WASH assistance, 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need, in particular displaced people living in camps/shelters or informal settlements, host communities and returnees. Before the crisis, only 65 per cent of the population had access to 42 built-up sewage treatment plants. This number has significantly decreased because many of essential facilities are no longer operational due to damage and/or unavailability of indispensable essential operational supplies. This makes community vulnerable to significant health risks. The basic level of services regarding garbage collection is primarily provided by municipalities/local authorities in most parts of the country. However, the lack of comprehensive disposal strategies and operational challenges in specific rural and urban areas makes it an urgent requirement by the communicates to strengthen and upgrade the quality and regularity of solid waste management.
Objectives:
UN-HABITAT proposes an area-based-approach project which target vulnerable communities in specific areas and/or neighborhoods through rehabilitation of essential infrastructure and basic services. Selected areas have been prioritized based on identified needs and vulnerabilities. The overall objective of the project is ensuring:
• Sufficient provision of safe water;
• Enhanced water quality assurance, through supporting the rehabilitation of water system, its operation and maintenance; And
• Rehabilitation of sewage systems, including distribution - discharge networks and repair of wastewater treatment plants.
The project aims to reach 575,000 beneficiaries (direct and indirect) in (Daraa, Deir-Ezzor,Homs, Rural Damascus, Hama and Aleppo ):
- Aleppo 200,000 beneficiaries;
- Deir-ez-Zor 60,000 beneficiaries;
- Daraa 65,000 beneficiaries).
- Rural Damascus 125,000 beneficiaries
- Homs 50,000 beneficiaries
- Hama 75,000 beneficiaries
Project Components:
In each location, the project consists of Four main components as follows:
1. Repair, rehabilitation and efficiency increment of water systems;
2. Supporting sewage and solid waste management systems;
3. Distribution of essential WASH (NFIs) and Hygiene promotion
4. Capacity building through trainings, orientations, learning visits/exposure… etc.

4 - Needs

WASH sector assessments underpin that, despite some investment in water supply- and wastewater treatment- systems, provision of water disinfectants and other interventions, severe WASH needs across Syria remain.Up to 55 per cent of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. General shortage of drinking water also has implications on economy and resilience: families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10 per cent of their income on water, with 3 per cent of the population spending more than 20 per cent, in extreme cases as much as 40 per cent of their income to purchase water. Regarding sanitation, more than 99 per cent of the surveyed population has access to a functioning toilet. Governorates and sub-districts targeted through this program are outlined below. Besides the criteria for projects to be included in HRP, the selection of targeted locations is based on • Being in line with the Shelter Sector and HRP 2020 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs, • Being priority locations identified through detailed field assessments and consultants with local experts and municipalities (MTOS programme)• Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-HABITAT resources and donor commitments • High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations. • High complementarity with area-based interventions in WASH, Protection, and ERLUN Habitat in coordination with the WASH sector will ensure a “Do No Harm” principle, and has carried out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its sector strategy development. Potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Unequal distribution of servicesinadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organize joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.Protection risk: Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration. Make sure that clear instruction for use is available for chlorine products primarily for household use.Protection risk: Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to raw sewage and gases. Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating measures: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with raw sewage wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors. Within communities in Syria, the demographic profile showed that 9% of HHs were female-headed and on average their households were smaller, with four household members, compared to five in male-headed households (MHHs). The average number of children per household was the same, irrespective of head of household gender.On average, the income per household member in FHHs was lower than that of MHHs; $28 per person as compared to $42 per person, respectively. This reduced income likely increases the vulnerability of FHHs and their ability to access services, including WASH services.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/Assumption: Non-consideration of WASH support projects on grounds of national/local spatial development plansMitigation:• Municipality-led, stakeholder inclusive, transparent processes for project identification;• Capacitate municipalities and technical departments to lead assistance and recovery projects in their constituencies, through project- management-oriented capacity building programs, provision of adequate workspace, IT and technical equipment• Align assistance activities on the basis of requirements, situational circumstances and municipalities’ spatial development plans through a collaborative, consultative process• Advocate for intermediate solutions for affected communities in areas unlikely to be developed on short term. Risk/Assumption: Delayed assistance to vulnerable and people in need due to cumbersome processes to approve programs, projects, locationsMitigation:• Close coordination with the specific ministries and other related public departments to expedite processes• Diligence and due consideration of time aspects in project planning processes, incl. anticipation of possible refutation• Selection of project locations with high concentration of vulnerable / at-risk groups but with little likelihood of being contested Risk/Assumption: Incomplete, resp. partial assistance to vulnerable and people in need due to authorities' curtailing of comprehensive programmatic approachesMitigation:• Close coordination with the resp. bodies in the ministries and other related public departments to identify and design projects based on municipal priorities and action plans• Capacitate municipalities and technical departments to lead assistance and recovery projects in their constituencies, through project- management-oriented capacity building programs
