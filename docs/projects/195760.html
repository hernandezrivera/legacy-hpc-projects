Project Summary

This proposed project aims to enhance the response and meet the need for nutrition services for caretakers including PLWs and children under five years in Ariha, Jisr shughur, and Afrin areas.
The targeted communities are 39 with a total population of 129,902 across 7 sub-districts (Ariha, Ehsem, Mhambal, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Sharan, Jindiries, and Afrin) divided as follows:
Bab Ellah 3282 ,Nahleh 2061, Motaram 6682, Orm Eljoz 6559, Nahliya 5740, Thaheriya 1032, Korin 10398, Rami (Ehsem)6748, Marayan 2848, Joseph 2389, Ora Qabli - Edwan 7586, Hila Dam 896, Kafrmid 1079, Mhambal 5255, Northern Laj 2398, Alyeh (Mhambal) 766, Bsanqul 2247, Kniseh (Mhambal) 6585, Al Iraqiyah 820, Hlul 1410, Anb 1720, Ein Elsoda (Jisr-Ash-Shugur) 2781, Ein Elhamra 4068, Al Marj Al Akhdar 420, Balmis 3937, Eastern Marj Akhdar 10942, Bzeit 2320, Maalaqa - Bishlamon 3888, Kniset Nakhleh 2834, Al Areen - Kastanah Fawqan 1945, Sokkariyeh (Jisr-Ash-Shugur)1810, Basuta 3,376, Jalma 4,774, Kafr Janna 559 , Boz Ghaz 2,664, Meraf Elshalaf 3,895, Jiman 482, Big Hallubi 210, Bafelyun 130, Ali Bazan 220, Qatireh 145.
In those communities, IHR implemented nutrition projects. While there is a need in it according to the cluster update and HNO 2022.
IHR will hire 10 RRTs, each team consisting of two nutritionists, two CNWs, and one data entry. 3 RRTs will cover the ten communities in Afrin area with total population 16,455, while 7 RRTs will cover 31 communities with total population o 113,447.
Those 10 IHR RRTs will provide nutrition services to 54,558 individuals of people in need, including (16,887 PLWs, 31,176 CU5, and 6,495 caretakers) by screening, referral, treatment, and following up with CU5 6-59, PLWs who are suffering from malnutrition.
The RRTs' members will be hired from females and males. Then, before launching the project activities, they will attend training sessions about providing nutrition services according to updated RRT SOP. They will receive training about GBV, PSEA, safeguarding for children, and dealing with PWDs.
To address the previously mentioned maternal and women and to meet the nutritional aspects needs of the targeted communities, IHR is going to provide integrated lifesaving maternal and child nutrition services as following:
Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) programme
• Screen PLWs and primary caretakers of children below 2 years for lactation and feeding difficulties.
• Provide emergency skilled IYCF counseling to caretakers of children aged 0-24 months including PLWs
• Nutrition education to malnourished PLWs and caretakers of severely and moderately malnourished children.
• Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices.
• Monitor and report distribution and use of breast milk substitutes.
• Support health/nutrition/WASH education and promotion.
• Provide micro-nutrient supplements to CUS and PLWS to accelerate anaemia reduction.
Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) program:
• Screening 31,176 children 6-59 months, and 16,887 PLWs who are severely and moderately malnourished.
• Treatment of 1247 children with SAM without medical complications using sector agreed standards.
• Referral the MAM cases to WFP health centers to receive the required treatment and supplies.
• Follow up the malnutrition cases to ensure they are recovering as per acceptable standards.
• Train project staff on CMAM and IYCF according to UNICEF and sector standards.
Integration with Health
• Screen, referral CU5 with dangers signs and medical problems in PHCs.
• Screen, referral PLWs with medical problems in ANCs/PNCs.
• Referral of medical cases to nearby health facilities.
Integration with WASH.
• Improve the social awareness among the target population in the served locations through a comprehensive COVID-19 and cholera awareness plan. staff will attend training about COVID-19, Cholera and means of protection. They will use a developed SOP to avoid any harm for people during the field visits

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, an estimated 5.5 million people including mothers and children aged 0-59 months in need of nutrition assistance. Of these, over 4.6 million are in acute need (severity 4 and 5). According to the nutrition sector analysis, the number of children (0-59 months) in need was approximately 3,770,000 while the number of PLW in need was approximately 1,732,000.Additionally, disability rates in children are higher in Syria than the global average of 15 percent. chronic malnutrition or stunting increased by 4%, Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases (increased by 29%), MAM cases (increased by 44.7%), for PLWs, the number of PLWs in need for malnutrition treatment was approximately 265,000. About 156,000 pregnant mothers are suffering with iron-deficiency anaemia while almost 1 million children will need supplementation for the treatment or prevention of iron-deficiency anaemia. According to surveillance report Oct 2022, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19% to 24.5 % between May 2019 and June 2021 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. The prevalence of GAM increased from 0.9 percent to 4.7 percent over the same period. the survey showed that anemia rate was 53.8% among targeted WRA women of reproductive age (15 -49 years) and 46% among children 6 – 59 months.GAM rate by WHZ increased from 2.5% to 3.3%. and SAM rate also increased from 0.4% to 0.9%. While SAM rate by MUAC increased 0.8% to 1.3%

4 - Implementation of activities

IHR will coordinate with local authorities in those 39 targeted communities and sign MoUs. IHR will hire 10 RRTs, 3 in Afrin area and 7 in Ariha and Jisr Shughur areas. They will attend training about IYCF-E, CMAM. Each RRT will cover several communities and is expected to reach 30-70 children and mothers per day. RRTs members will screen the PLWs and children U 5 years as follows: Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition cases through screening of 31,176 children 6-59 for acute malnutrition Ensuring adequate coverage of treatment of Acute Malnutrition for children through providing Micronutrient supplies.Determining the SAM cases and referring them to health centersDistribution of micronutrient supplements for preventing anemia and malnutrition for children 6-59 months and PLWs.Screening malnutrition of 16,887 PLWs, early identification, referral nutritional health centres, and follow-up severe and moderate malnutrition cases until the stage of recovery is reached.Providing and disseminating community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO-recommended IYCF and maternal nutrition practices for 23,382 caretakers including PLWs.Support hygiene promotion through rising WASH, COVID-19, cholera awareness messagesTraining caregivers on family MUAC approachSubmitting the monthly update to the cluster and UNICEF through monthly 4Ws. Submitting the monthly and quarterly reports to UNICEF. Monitoring activities by MEAL team and checking on the collected data

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of COVID-19 transmission to beneficiaries from staff and vice versa.Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: Awareness raising: ensure social distancing, ventilation, limited number or participants, PPE. Build capacity of staff. Risk of Cholera transmission among beneficiaries.Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigating Measure: Provide awareness messages to mothers about hygiene and using clean water from safe sources. train staff with IPC protocols as per the approved health cluster standards.Risk of mechanisms may not be widely known by beneficiaries and community.Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: Training for staff on complaints feedback mechanismUse multiple methods of communications – complaints boxes, dedicated email address, website link, telephone hotline, posters.Use remote methods to advertise complaints feedback mechanism e.g. WhatsApp learning groups, text messages.Protection risk 2: Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: Availability of a wheelchair for PWD at the reception point, trained staff to deal with PWDs, priority for the PWDs, facilitate reaching the PWDs to the service centers for receiving support, or health services.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on IHR PSEA policy and before starting project interventions, IHR will conduct a SEA risk assessment to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while providing support to BNFs. 2- BNFs do not report SEA cases for IHR staff, resulting in the IHR being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance. Mitigation: After an in-depth assessment of these risks, IHR through collaboration with PSEA focal point will be designed the mitigation plan for all these risks as follows Mitigation measures: 1- IHRwill ensure that all its staff had signed a code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by itsstaff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs.2- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware of: what SEA is, what their rights are in regard to SEA, and what they can do if SEA occurs.3- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g. complaints box/telephone hotline to deal with these cases).
