Project Summary

UNFPA & its partners will focus on quality life-saving SRH interventions targeting 250,000 vulnerable, Lebanese, Palestinians & migrant women, men, boys & adolescent girls. Migrants and people with disabilities will be targeted in all interventions. Most vulnerable women will be reached through mobile teams well versed and engaged at community level. UNFPA will ensure technical support, guidance and monitoring of the following proposed interventions
A-Promoting SRH service delivery by:
-Enhancing quality of maternal care through a series of training sessions to 1500 health
care providers on SRH topics including emergency obstetric care & high risk pregnancies. This is essential considering the recent migration of health workforce
-Procurement of RH drugs & commodities to 130,000 men, women and adolescent girls accessing MOPH PHC network & dispensaries located in remote & poor areas in order to avail RH commodities to the increasing number of vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinians,and migrants accessing these heath facilities. Based on MOPH PHC data for 2022 there has been an increase of 24% in SRH services with a total number of around 350,000 beneficiaries. MSNA findings also expose the shift of patients from private to public sector with more than 50% of surveyed people reported not being able to pay the cost of medication
-Community mobilization and awareness raising on health integrated messages with focus on SRH through engagement of 80 midwives & outreach workers who will promote health seeking behavior among 80,000 women and adolescent girls with referral to care as needed. 25000 dignity kits will be distributed during awareness sessions. The MSNA findings reveal that one quarter of surveyed pregnant women/ mothers did not receive appropriate ANC; and MOPH PHC data shows that only around 10% of new mothers receive Post natal care. The limited access to RH cancer preventive services is alarming and requires prompt action to mitigate the risk of increased incidence
-Provision of SRH Services namely 1) midwifery care for vulnerable women with high risk pregnancies. Data collected about midwifery home care conducted in 2022 shows that 50% of women in need for support presents in their third trimester and the most encountered cases are hypertension, pre-eclampsia, threat of premature birth, diabetes and anemia. Prompt support to these cases would avert maternal and neonatal mortalities and morbidities. 2) Medical consultation through Tele-health with referral to additional services as needed. The increased transportation costs hinder access to care hence availing medical consultation via tele-health present an opportunity for enhanced access to care for the most vulnerable groups. 3) Coverage of advanced diagnostics to high risk pregnancies which will guide the care plan and help reducing maternal morbidities
B-Enhancing rapid response for PLWs through:
-Engaging a medical team of OBGYN trained specialists to ensure 1) technical support for the community midwives and primary care level health care providers as needed; 2) facilitation and support referral for hospitalization/ emergency OBGYN care for vulnerable women under various projects; 3) Provision of medical consultations through tele-health to vulnerable women and critical cases
-Engaging midwives to ensure 1) awareness raising on SRH including ANC/PNC, Family planning, covid19 vaccination etc; 2) identification and support to high risk pregnancies including home care visits & referral to further care as needed 3) linkages with the OBGYN specialists response team to ensure early response as needed, support transfer of cases to health care facilities as needed
C-Mainstreaming protection for the most vulnerable women and adolescent girls through:
-Capacity development on GBV safe identification and referral to health care providers, sensitization training on clinical management of rape for health &GBV actors and training on GBV/SRH integration to care providers.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 1: -Enhancing quality of maternal care through a series of training sessions to health care providers on SRH topics- Procurement of RH drugs and commodities to be dispensed in MOPH PHC network and dispensaries located in remote and poor areas across LebanonObjective 3:-Engaging a medical team of trained OBGYN specialists practicing in the COVID19 appointed hospitals to ensure technical support to community midwives, facilitate referral to secondary care and provide tele-health medical consultations to vulnerable women, referred critical cases and high risk pregnancies -Engaging a network of midwives and health care providers at community level for SRH midwifery care across Lebanon. The midwifery network will be linked to the OBGYN specialists. Objective 4:-Awareness raising on health care with focus on SRH through engagement of midwives and outreach workers who will promote health seeking behavior among women and adolescent girls at the community level all over Lebanon,-Provision of SRH services for women with high risk pregnancies ad those presenting with SRH-GBV conditions, -Capacity development on GBV safe identification and referral to health care providers from the MOPH PHC network -Provision of Integrated GBV-SRH services to vulnerable women and adolescent girls -Provision of Dignity kits to women and girls combined with the awareness raising on SRH and GBV. Also the project will advance the CMR work in country with trainings and support to referral.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of women & girls of different age, nationalities, cultural background, sexual & gender orientation. Needs of women & girls with disabilities will be factored in. Safety audits & community consultations will be held.Key messages about what constitutes PSEA and how to report allegations will be mainstreamed. Monitoring activities will include a section on protection risks & PSEA.UNFPA health partners will be supported to ensure a properly functional mechanism for feedback& complaints that allows beneficiaries to report their concerns/inputs. UNFPA will ensure that all health staff & front line workers engaged, have completed their PSEA Training. UNFPA will ensure close follow up & monitoring through 1) regular follow up with the implementing partners, 2) field visits 3) review of data making sure it is disaggregated by age, gender, and disability. UNFPA will also ensure capacity development of partners on monitoring and reporting.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNFPA operates according to the 5 Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (Leadership and governance, Transparency, Feedback and complaints, Participation, Design, monitoring and evaluation). Women and girls are key actors in their own protection, they are active partners in identifying protection risks and solutions. UNFPA will facilitate their active involvement in the design, implementation and monitoring of interventions, by consulting with them on GBV risks, barriers for access to GBV and SRH services. UNFPA will work with women and girls to identify those most marginalized and at high risk, and design approaches to ensure their effective participation. UNFPA developed a set of data collection tools and conduct training for IPs to ensure accurate and complete qualitative & quantitative data submitted on regular basis. UNFPA conducts regular monitoring field visits, provides technical supervision as part of periodic on-site reviews to assess program implementation.
