Project Summary

According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Syria, the country's protracted crisis continues to severely affect the agriculture and agri-food sectors, with an estimated 12.4 million people facing acute food insecurity. The report also highlights the following challenges facing the agriculture sector in Syria:
Drought: Syria is experiencing a prolonged drought, which is impacting crop yields and livestock production.
Water scarcity: Water scarcity is a major challenge for farmers in Syria, as many areas do not have access to reliable irrigation systems.
Conflict: The ongoing conflict in Syria has damaged agricultural infrastructure and disrupted supply chains.
Economic crisis: The economic crisis in Syria has made it difficult for farmers to access inputs such as seeds, fertilizers, and fuel.
This project aims to support 3,750 farmers and 17,500 livestock breeders in Al-Bab, Jarablus, Afrin, A'zaz, Idleb, and Harim districts as follows:
*Supporting 1,000 wheat farmers with agriculture inputs and cash for irrigation and harvesting to plant one hectare for each farmer.
*Supporting 500 potato farmers with agriculture inputs, and cash for irrigation and harvesting to planting one donum for each farmer.
*Supporting 1,000 HHs who usually grow summer and autumn vegetables with Smart Climate Agriculture kits to plant one donum of vegetables and minimize climate change effects on land productivity. This activity is part of Modern Agricultural Systems and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) to address climate change.
*Supporting 100 IDP HHs with all necessary inputs to produce vegetables using a hydroponic agriculture system, and 100 Female HHH with oyster mushroom seeds. IYD will support them with technical training and follow up all production stages. This activity is part of Modern Agricultural Systems and Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) to address climate change.
*Supporting 1,000 olive farmers with agriculture services, inputs, and CFW activities based on value chain assessments. The harvesting will be conducted as a CFW modality (1,000 workers).
*Supporting 50 tree nurseries owners with needed tools and production inputs: agricultural bags, organic fertilizers, soil, and 100 unskilled workers. These unskilled workers will work for 2 months and receive a salary for daily work.
*Rehabilitating the irrigation canals of Jarablus and Al-Ra'ee by recruiting 200 workers as Cash for Work (CFW) to clean and maintain the canal edges.
*Supporting 500 livestock breeders with all veteran inputs and tools to plant and produce forage crops (Azolla and Sudan grass) to feed their livestock. This new approach has been adopted by IYD in NWS to produce fodder to feed animals, tackle the high price of fodder, and minimize the effects of climate change.
*Supporting 2,000 breeders (500 cow breeders/1,500 sheep breeders) owning 1,000 cows and 30,000 sheep with pellet fodder distribution for four months in the winter season. Each cow and sheep will be supported with 10 and 1 kg, respectively, on a daily basis.
* Providing health and reproductive treatment and vaccines for the livestock owned by breeders in the targeted areas to enhance the animals' productivity and reproductivity. 20 mobile clinics will run to provide these services, aiming to enhance productivity and reproductivity, which will lead to more profit for the breeders. Approximately 15,000 participants will be supported during the one-year project duration.
* Conducting new castle vaccination campaign targeting 5,000,000 chicks.
This project is aligned with the HNO's strategic objectives to:
Increase food production and improve access to nutritious food
Strengthen the resilience of agriculture and food systems
Improve livelihoods and economic opportunities in rural areas
The project will contribute to the HNO's overall goal of reducing food insecurity and malnutrition in Syria.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project aims to bolster the resilience of internally displaced persons (IDP) households forced to flee their homes due to escalating security threats. Additionally, it targets vulnerable host communities identified through specific criteria set by IYD. Needs: The severity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria remains significant, as a result of the continued hostilities in localized areas, protracted displacement, harsh weather conditions, and recently the earthquake which hit Syria and Turkey. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview Summary published on 21 December 2023, the number of people in need increased by 1.4 million reaching 16.7 million compared to 2023, 5.5 million of them are internally displaced people. Whilst there has been a decrease of hostilities in Idleb governorate over the current year, the number of people in need had increased significantly, the impact of hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria. Implementation of Activities: Implementation of the project activities will be comprehensive, involving registration and verification of beneficiaries, contracting of service providers, conducting market assessments, and coordinating with local authorities for distribution and monitoring of activities. Regular post-distribution monitoring will ensure adherence to project and donor policies Protection Risk Analysis: Several risks have been identified, including exclusion and discrimination in service provision and potential harm to vulnerable groups during project activities. Mitigation measures include clarifying selection criteria, establishing robust feedback mechanisms, and conducting constant assessments to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy: IYD has adopted a comprehensive PSEA strategy, including adherence to the inter-agency Code of Conduct, active participation in PSEA networks, and establishment of internal reporting and investigative mechanisms. Completion of PSEA checklists and ongoing monitoring will ensure adherence to standards and prompt action against violations. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Various risks, including institutional, operational, social, and security risks, have been identified. Mitigation measures involve maintaining strong collaboration with stakeholders, ensuring service provider commitment, addressing social concerns, and coordinating with local leaders and security teams to mitigate security risks. Regular monitoring and updates will be conducted to ensure the safety and security of project implementation.
