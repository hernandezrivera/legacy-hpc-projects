Project Summary

This project will assist internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities in addressing their psychosocial needs. It will do so by targeting communities affected by the internal and communal conflicts and identifying victims of conflict-related incidents.
In coordination with members and leaders of the target communities, outreach teams will be established and trained in identifying vulnerable cases, provide immediate protection assistance and refer vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and repatriates to specialized institutions.
Direct assistance, as per beneficiaries’ needs will be provided. This will include but not limited to income generating activities that will contribute to the resilience of households.
According to the protection cluster, about 5,800,000 persons are affected of which 755,355 are in need of assistance and 509,345 are targeted for assistance. Under this project, 25,000 persons needing psychosocial assistance will be considered. They will comprise of women and men including young girls and boys.
The project will be carried out in the regions of Gao, Mopti, Tombouctou, and Kayes over a period of twelve (12) months.

Needs

The conflict in Mali since 2012 led to the displacement of over 500,000 persons. Of this number, more than 77,000 remain displaced according to the September 2018 report from the Commission on Movement of Population (CMP) of the Ministry of Solidarity and Humanitarian Action. The crisis has a considerable impact on the traditional resilience and coping mechanisms of communities and individuals. Many of the IDPs and returnees now lack the basic socio-economic resources and community support that would usually enable them to cope with unexpected stress factors. Security incidents, inter or intra community conflicts, illness, the loss of families’ breadwinners will, and often drastically, impact the future wellness of entire households.Given the nature of conflict in Mali, IDPs have been emotionally affected by the events, and a number of individuals need special attention and targeted support through specialized services and referral mechanisms to advanced and established medical centers.While return trends are regularly confirmed and the return intentions among those still displaced remains high, many of the most vulnerable IDPs often lack the resources to organize their journey home and restart their lives. In the case of displaced women and especially those associated with armed groups, the vulnerabilities are particularly exacerbated by the lack of specific protection services based on their needs, or the lack of knowledge on existing facilities, especially concerning the referral structures on protection and gender-based violence (GBV), or the protection mechanisms and assistance to children in areas of the country affected by the conflict.In these conditions, it is of crucial importance that Protection services, including direct assistance, remain available throughout the country for the most vulnerable people, whether IDPs, returnees, or affected communities.A comprehensive approach, including identification, counselling and orientation, referral and direct assistance, which is available for the most vulnerable, independent of their status (IDPs, returnees, or host communities) should be set up throughout the country in order to accompany returnees, IDPs, and newly displaced people, while taking into consideration the needs of affected host communities in the north of the country and contributing to rebuilding the resilience capacities of communities and individuals in Mali. This approach includes support for socio-economic reintegration as well as training and awareness sessions related to conflict management and reinforcement of self-protection capabilities and host populations in high displacement and return locations.

Activities

- Provide quality psychosocial assistance to conflict-affected individuals including women, men, and children and those associated with armed groups.- Provide holistic assistance (medical assistance, psychosocial, socio-economic reintegration, schooling and accommodation) to victims of gender-based violence affected during the conflict.- Reinforce and/or put in place psychosocial services for women and girls affected by the conflict.- Sensitize the war-affected persons and host communities on conflict management relating to access to goods and natural resources.- Put in place favorable conditions for the reintegration of displaced persons, returnees, and repatriates, to provide initial support to vulnerable individuals. Wherever and however possible beneficiaries will be referred to partner Agencies for focused individual and group support services, when the need arises. Beneficiaries will be provided information and transportation for them and their caretakers. IOM will also provide holistic assistance to the most vulnerable, including children and GBV, and socio-economic rehabilitation and reintegration activities that promote resilience. These reintegration activities will include income generating activities, for which IOM will provide directly the necessary and guidance for the beneficiaries to develop their own projects (this is not a cash-based intervention).- Conduct training and awareness raising sessions on conflict management and self-protection.
