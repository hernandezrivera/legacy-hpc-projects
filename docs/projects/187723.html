Project Summary

The project is a continuation of last year's response as QC is widening its intervention for the next two years to include most of the SNFI cluster activities in an effort to address part of the needs and complement the overall response in NWS. The project goal is to support the IDPs to cope with the severe conditions the area is going through from the beginning of the conflict through improving their shelter conditions and access to NFIs. The project includes multi-activity response to ensure full coverage of people’s needs and access to basic services. The project will be targeting both IDPs and host communities to alleviate the pressure on community resources. alongside different gender and age needs including women children elderly and people with special needs will be taken into consideration while designing the project.
The intervention for 2022 and 2023 will be done through in-kind and cash modalities preferably bought from the local market ensuring support to the local economy. Allocation of items in areas will be done through multi-sectoral needs and technical assessments to capture all aspects of IDPs’ and their hosts’ conditions, to ensure appropriate intervention. 4 - Needs

As per the HNO 2021 the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria has increased by 21% compared to 2020, from 11.1M to 13.4M. While in 2020, 42% of overall PiN were in severe and catastrophic need, this proportion has risen to 45% (6M) in 2021. Out of all people in humanitarian need, 6.7M are IDPs who exhibit a wide range of needs, 4.9M in Aleppo and Idlib only, while 1.9M IDPs in sites face elevated levels of life and health-threatening needs; 3.2K are Syrians who have returned to their places of origin in 2020, often to a very harsh context, and frequently require support to render returns sustainable and to re-integrate into their communities; and 6.4M are residents who have seen dramatic economic impoverishment throughout 2020 and are now live in areas where basic services delivery is particularly dysfunctional. The shelter and NFI PiN in Aleppo and Idlib are 1.7M and 2.7M respectively this is nearly 10% up from last year’s for this sector. Across the country, funding restrictions are limiting the humanitarian response. Adding the ongoing economic crisis compounded by COVID-19 will continue to negatively impact household resilience through all areas, further reducing families’ ability to meet their basic needs, potentially causing more households to move to IDP sites. This would further exacerbate the need for shelter and NFI support. Emergency shelter, NFI support along with improving shelter conditions will continue to be vital.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

QC has a clear strategy to mitigate the risks of PSEA, the strategy includes direct and indirect beneficiaries of our programming, adults in wider communities in which we work in. It also includes QC’s staff, partners, suppliers sub-contractors and any other individual engaged by QC's programs. Particular attention is needed for vulnerable groups, including women and children, and those most at risk of discrimination based on disability, gender identity and sexual orientation, age, and religion. Our approach to PSEA is through the following:1- Ensure that all staff, representatives and third parties are aware of the high standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect individuals of SEA. 2- Having an organizational culture based on accountability where there is zero tolerance for PSEA. 3- Have an endorsed reporting mechanism for staff, beneficiaries and partners to feel safe to report violations, and then 4- ensure that reports are handled in a manner that respects due process, and swift and credible investigation for any violations. And actively promote fair investigation and sanctions. 5- mitigating risks in programming by identifying the risks and design the program accordingly. Starting from needs assessments through project design (shelter layouts, kits components) to the implementation to closing phases. 6- Comprehensive operational, HR and mgmt. measures are also required to PSEA. By conducting relative vetting and checks of staff, suppliers ..


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Outbreak of conﬂict prevents access to project locations. Mitigation: Experience from previous operations found that while conﬂict occurred, a cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Further, the project design ﬂexibly targets at a unit level of “sub-district.” By using this target instead of speciﬁc communities, the project can respond district-wide, wherever non-conﬂict areas with access allow. Risk 2: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC activities or directly interferes with activities threatening humanitarian imperative Mitigation: staff/partners keep in close contact with the various local councils to be able to address any issues at an early stage. QC is transparent throughout all actions and communications with all stakeholders to ensure our status as impartial, neutral, and independent is unquestionable. QC will suspend operations in one location while continuing in another until delivery of aid is assured to be unimpeded. Risk 3: Bad weather conditions. Mitigation: By starting all Shelter activities during spring until the end of summer to avoid bad weather conditions which will result in a delay in implementing the activities, and cut access to targeted locations. Risks: 4 Overcrowding and/or integrating groups that do not have social histories together has the potential to increase risks of exposure to different forms of GBV. Mitigation: Use the GBV speciﬁc assess.
