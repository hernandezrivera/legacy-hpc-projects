Project Summary

Through this project, Nile Hope will be able to continue providing nutrition interventions in Pigi, Leer, Akobo, Nassir and Fangak counties. The project will seek to screen 87,525 children below five years and 47,776 PLWs and reach 10,090 children with SAM treatment services, 15,223 under fives with MAM treatment, 5,624 PLW with treatment for acute malnutrition, 48,827 caregivers and pregnant women with MIYCN messaging and counseling. The project will provide SAM management to children between 6-59 months, MAM management to under five children as well as to pregnant and lactating women as well as MIYCN and a strong component of community mobilization and sensitization. In order to offer the said services, there will be a strong component of surveillance through continuous program monitoring that will also include SMART surveys/Mass MUAC screening/KAP surveys. Routine community mobilization and sensitization, active case finding, referrals and follow ups at the household level will be done to strengthen all the components. There will also be a strong linkage with health facilities and other existing programs particularly livelihood programs, WASH and GBV/child protection. The project will rely fully on log cluster to deliver supplies to the project locations. There is are existing agreements with WFP for supply support in managing MAM cases and with UNICEF to support SAM cases in both Pigi/Canal and Leer counties. Both WFP and UNICEF agreements support Nile Hope with supplies. WFP also supports some CNVs. There will be static sites offering integrated management of SAM and MAM in both locations. Community mobilization strategy and basic monitoring and evaluation paying attention to data collection, reporting and supply chain management.

Needs

With GAM rate of 12.2% based on WFH in the most recent SMART survey conducted in Leer by Medair in April 2018, the county remains a concern more so because immediately after the assessment fighting broke out again resulting in loss of lives and livelihoods, displacements and disruption of humanitarian services. The county suffered Famine in early 2017 and remains fragile due to poor harvest this year in addition to other factors such as insecurity and access constraint. The islands where most people hide have poor hygiene and sanitation and morbidity is a going concern. Culturally, women are the 'beasts of burden' and are pre-disposed to malnutrition because of spending most of the time trying to fend for the family including chasing food distributions even in the neighboring counties if it delays. FangakFangak county SMART survey done in April 2018 had a GAM of 16.1% and hence above the emergency threshold. It is an IPC 4 zone and poor harvest this year does not help the situation. Fangak is prone to outbreaks of cholera due to a very fragile WASH infrastructure where toilets are sunk shallowly to avoid water contamination and most people still bath and drink the same water as their animals. Accessibility is a major problem too. The market infrastructure is weak as most goods have initially come through the river from Malakal and through the river from Juba. The trade routes occasionally experience blockage by armed elements hence contributing to hunger and shortage of basics and hence food insecurity. Currently, hunger is prevalent and most people are fully reliant on food distribution rations. Culturally, Fangak like all the other locations in South Sudan are male dominated. The position of the women folk is disadvantaged by the power equation and predisposes women to the harsh realities of food scarcity, economic upheavals and gender-based violence. Akobo and Nassir The year 2018 has witnessed a lot of turmoil in Akobo west leading to the suspension of services in Walgak earlier on in the year and migration to Wechjal as a temporary hub for most humanitarian actors. Armed battle characterized early 2018 and disrupted provision of aid. Hunger, lack of trade channels and morbidity have remained high in the greater Akobo and accessibility is also a challenge since some routes have armed elements making it hard to move from one side to the other to provide aid. Akobo is almost tipping into IPC 4 according to the latest IPC report projections for up to July 2018. The project will seek to bridge the gender gap by providing nutrition care to both girls and boys as an equalizer and a foundation for good health before the next phase of life. GAM rates remain at 16.1% in Akobo (IMC, June 2-11, 2018. Pigi/CanalPigi/Canal county has its own unique challenges. Currently, the county appears to be divided into two with different factions holding on to each side. Pigi was classified as IPC 4 according to the latest IPC report. Further, the southern part where CP has most of its facilities has witnessed a leadership wrangle as was witnessed in the first quarter of 2018 resulting in suspension of nutrition services for 2 weeks. The WASH infrastructure is dire as seen where the entire southern part spanning Korwai, Alam, Diel and the surrounding areas don’t have even one borehole. The masses rely heavily on swamp water also used by their animals and which also dries off on several occasions forcing people to relocate elsewhere during dry season. According to the IPC results released on 28th September 2018, there is a small population of people in Leer in Phase 5 (catastrophe) in the month of September 2018 and the same is projected to happen through to December until between Jan to March when a few Pigi population are expected to also enter into Phase 5. A significant population in the two counties are however in Phase 4 (Emergency). Therefore a majority of the population in Nile Hope operational areas are in IPC phase 3, 4 and 5.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

At the onset of the project, Community members will be involved in the design stage. They will be briefed on all the details including what the project is all about, their roles and the roles of the organization. Project duration and donor and safety issues will also be highlighted and discussed. Over the implementation period, there will be consultative meetings and monitoring visits where program details will be discussed in focused group discussions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The messages disseminated to the affected population are context specific. The strategy of implementation takes into account the security, infrastructural and cultural issues of the specific locations. Nutrition education on eating patterns will pay attention to the locally available foods in the various locations.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Direct staff implementing the project on the ground will be taken through PSEA training. The project design will seek to employ both men and women while reserving specific positions to Women to address gender disparity. Toilets and sitting spaces in the facility will be gender segregated to respect the cultural practices of the affected populations. There will be referral linkages between nutrition sites and professional protection service providers.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

A stakeholder workshop that will include local leaders, interest groups such as mother support groups, disabled persons, leaders from the various payams and villages as well as youth groups will be organized and project introduced to them; this will be done per location (county) and if possible, several groups depending on context such as access and security situation. Open fora discussions will be used to unpack and discuss the project design, roles and responsibilities, risks and mitigation measures as well as the project constraints i.e. cost, performance thresholds, time and scope.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

There are suggestion boxes in most of the OTP facilities already. There are also the monitoring teams and there will be similar teams including the Community Nutrition Volunteers who interact closely with affected persons in their private home setting and directly ask for feedback on the nature of services provided. The CNVs are local people in their settings and are likely to be trusted more with discrete information, same as the local staff. There are also quarterly monitoring plans where a team selects sample households from the various target groups and engages them through focused group discussions to identify their concerns. The concerns are tabulated in the organization's feedback matrix and addressed urgently where possible or referred to relevant organs within the humanitarian platform in South Sudan.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities are active partners in the project. They will continue to be recruited to provide both skilled and unskilled labor, they are part of the steering committee that looks into the overall running of the project and are a party to major decisions including locations of service points and provision of an ample environment where project implementation is possible. The relationship is symbiotic and the community continuously feeds partners with security updates from the ground to better plan movement and community mobilization. The project will reserve a central role for the community to enhance ownership and ensure that they are part of the project. The community will be asked to make contributions to the project by helping to rehabilitate OTP facilities and TSFP centers. The Mother support group members will also play active roles after training to counsel fellow mothers in the community and hence help to implement the MIYCN package by doing it themselves.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to take services closer to the people so that security and distance constraints don't hinder access to critical and life saving services. In some locations where two communities hostile to each other occupy the same county, services are and will be offered in both locations. At the OTP at the OTP and TSFP centers, attention is given to cultural issues that may prohibit consumption of the services on offer by providing gender segregated sitting areas, toilets and locating OTPs in locations away from locations where men often converge. The locations are also situated far away from military barracks or housing of authorities. The project activities are undertaken by local staff who are able to speak in the local dialect and who reside closer to the beneficiaries and the beneficiaries can open up to with their private matters. While giving MIYCN messages, the role of the male figures in the process will also be reinforced to ensure that they can relate with the messages without feeling left out and without an active role to play in improving child health.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address exploitation of young women by the powerful male figures in the community by offering referrals to professional GBV services through the critical referral pathway explained to the facility staff. This is done when victims of sexual exploitation and abuse volunteer information and seek help.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Specific positions such as MIYCN promoters are specifically left for women to encourage their entry and sustain them in the job market and address gender disparity. Project staff are not allowed to engage in sexual relations or to exercise their power in undue manner while working with beneficiaries. All facilities will have guards to deal with security threats. MIYCN practices will directly target women to ensure that they have a special platform to be empowered and to make their voices heard on the issues affecting them.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

No

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes. The project ensures that waste products in the treatment centers are disposed in safe places such as incinerators or burnt in dug and protected pits away from the facility to also guarantee environmental safety. Expired products are disposed and not given to beneficiaries. Only ratified products distributed through the core pipelines are used. The rest must be verified as safe for use.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes. The project is designed to assess if the beneficiaries are being given safe products i.e. unexpired products. It is also designed to ensure that all beneficiaries get the correct amount of their rations according to the treatment protocol. The beneficiaries are also empowered to understand that accessing the services is their right and not a favor from the project staff.
