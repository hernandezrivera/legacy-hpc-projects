Project Summary

SDH project aims to improve the living conditions of vulnerable people (IDPs) in northern Syria Through the activation of child protection monitoring focal points in the various locations of the IDPs in Idlib countryside and Aleppo countryside, and educating the target communities on the importance of protecting children and what this reflects on the society in terms of positives,
Insurance of participation these communities in creating sustainable projects and activities that guarantee the provision of the necessary PSS support to all segments of society, especially children and people with disabilities.
In addition to providing awareness sessions about child protection, support the establishment child protection committee to involve in identifying the need for child emergency cases and to be aware of how to study each case separately and all previous activities
The project team will engage boys and girls in structured and sustainable child protection programs,
Including psychosocial support,
In addition to raising awareness of child protection among all segments of society, through individual and group awareness sessions and,
Activating training and rehabilitation courses for community members on child protection in a manner that guarantees strengthen household and communal coping strategies in IDP sites and,
Develop exit strategies,
The project aims to Securing individual protection assistance to achieve protection outcomes through prevention material and financial assistance and response to vulnerable families and individuals.
SDH project has been planned to provide individual protection assistance to achieve protection results through
prevention, financial assistance, activating case study, and provision of psychological support to vulnerable individuals of children and disabilities people those who cannot live independently like other normal.
Those activities will ensure the the Well-being of the children to fill the gap caused by the long years of the war.
The total number of children will be targeted by the project: 18,825, by the following activities:
Girls and boys and caregivers that benefit from psychosocial support service and menthal hearlth services.
Women and men will be reached by parents' programmes

supporting to ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues
specialised child protection services through case management
Those centers will be under running of SDH and MEAL team will conduct daily visit, and periodic satisfaction surveys, follow up sessions, KII, FGD, as well as Face to face interviews.
SDH will coordinate with CP sub cluster team, protection cluster, LC, and other partner for case referral and specialized case management. SDH will explain project activities at the beginning of the project and adjust the activates according to need and consultation of BNFs as well as inclusion of PwDs in each step of the project from the 1st step of adjusting till closure phase.
Provide PwDs with needed facilities to inclusion them as facilitating them in the centers.

3 - Needs

The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many people over the past 12 years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control, in addition to the lack of job opportunities and poor income compared to the rise in living, all of the above has a clear negative impact on societal groups, where the responsibilities of parents (male and female) exceed their capabilities and energies, this was the reason for the increase in early marriages of girls and the remarkable increase in child labor and their recruitment, as well as the decreased opportunity to enroll in school. All of this was mentioned in the Syrian 2023 HNO which the statistics indicated that the disproportionate impact of negative coping mechanisms on women in children is evident when interfaced with the fact that early marriage is prevalent in 62 percent of assessed communities and child labor prevents school attendance in 67 percent of assessed communities. The SDH team has actually seen these effects during the RNA and awareness campaigns about COVID-19 that the SDH team has carried out in the areas where our medical centers are located In Many Areas around NWS. the RNA has conducted by the SDH field team indicated those most vulnerable individuals to be targeted in this response especially in the camps around: Azzaz, Maree (Total 4483, Disabilities 70, Boy 1440, Girl 1243) Dana camps: (Total 9727, Disabilities 89) in total.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of sexual exploitation: All MHPSS staff will receive PSEA/CP/Protection mainstreaming training and will know how and when to report suspected cases of exploitation. Each PSS team will employ an equal or similar number of male and female staff, so that beneficiaries will have a choice of the gender of case manager, PSW, CSW that they feel more comfortable receiving services from. If beneficiaries are making completing a referral with another service provider they may ask their case manager to accompany them. All PSS staff will be trained on the internal Risk of violence/harm toward staff: Staff never works alone, either inside the clinic or in the community. Security guards will be employed at each PHC/PSS Center. Staff providing outreach, emergency outreach, and community activities and events will be participated in by pairs and/or full teams. Each PSS staff member will have a mobile phone with them also and check in with their team leader regularly. Risk of work-related stress, burnout, and vicarious trauma: Staff Wellbeing is provided by the organization in the form of activities, staff welfare meetings, training on stress reduction, positive coping, provided by the staff wellbeing officer, as well as referral to more advanced services should they be required.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SDH field staff have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over vulnerable communities/beneficiaries that hold PSEA risks. SDH has institutionalized policies on PSEA, Whistle-blower, Code of Conduct, Gender, Safeguarding and Complaint, and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM). Proposed activities have been designed and are implemented with a standardized and cohesive approach through Standard Operational Procedures guided by the principles of “Do No Harm”, confidentiality, impartiality/non-discrimination, the best interest of the child. Gender, age, and diversity are sensitized Mitigating measures: 1) Effective and comprehensive mechanisms established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel; continuous training on the topic provided by dedicated staff members. 2) effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff is ensured. 3) Ensuring regular monitoring and evaluation through impartial assessments of needs and risks, vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting, and bene¦ciary/staff feedback mechanisms. 4) Ensuring the provision of information to communities and people affected by a crisis about SDH; its principles, how it expects its staff to behave, the programs it is implementing, and what they intend to deliver as well as how to report concerns and complaints. 5) Effective community-based CFM, including victim assistance is established and monitored for all persons (gender, age, and diversity).


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CP facilities could be attacked, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a CP workers, direct attack on workers or children, children or military use of the centers. Mitigating Measures CP facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UNOCHA. All attacks will be reported to CP cub cluster. CP facilities will have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Resources may include walkies-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flash lights, exit signs, etc. Caregivers should know CP facilities’s safety protocols and what will happen in case of a security event. Preparedness training such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills, should be regularly practiced. These processes and procedures will be appropriate for age of the involved children. Where relevant, children, CP personnel will be trained on unexplored ordinance risk awareness. children, their caregivers, workers and other CP personnel will be provided PSS. Those services will have capacity, ability and resources to address basic needs. For needs that are beyond what will be reasonably expected from CP services there will be a specialized protection services. Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by CP Workers, CP personnel, organization staff or related entities. Mitigating Measure Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place.
