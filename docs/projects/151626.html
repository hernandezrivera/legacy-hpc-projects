Project Summary

This is an emergency nutrition project that will be implemented both Badhadhe and Afmadow district. The project will target 2800 beneficiaries consisting of under 5 children, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished. The emergency nutrition project will focus on scaling up nutrition services to outreach sites to reach more vulnerable affected under-fives and PLWs. The project will intensify active case finding and mass screening to achieve its target and improve access to quality nutrition services. Continuous monitoring of project activities will enhance proper implementation of project activities and reach more vulnerable. Support supervision will be done by the Nutrition Manager and other senior management team to provide support and strengthen the staff capacity as well ensure effective project implementation. The project will be implemented in two fixed sites Kutur village in Dhobley and Hosingo town while outreach sites will be Tulaburwaqa and Dardaar locations in Dhobley.

Needs

Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. Between April and June, food security continued to improve due to average to above-average rainfall beneficial to seasonal food and income sources and market conditions combined with sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance. Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2018 indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia has improved due to better food security conditions, reduced outbreak of disease, and sustained humanitarian interventions. The national level of acute malnutrition has improved to Serious (10–14.9 percent Global Acute Malnutrition-GAM) with a median GAM of 14.0 percent, compared to a Critical prevalence (15–29.9 percent GAM) in the 2017 Gu, which recorded a median GAM of 17.4 percent. Despite this improvement, a high level of acute malnutrition persists in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes. In 12 of the 33 populations surveyed, GAM prevalence was Critical. Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence was Critical (≥4 - 5.6 percent). Based on these indicators, urgent treatment and nutrition support is required for an estimated 294 200 children will likely face acute malnutrition through December 2018, including 55 200 who are likely to be severely malnourished (total acute malnutrition burden). Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements in acute malnutrition prevalence and to prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation. WASDA proposes urgent treatment and nutrition support for approximately 2,800 acutely malnourished children, including 300 who are severely malnourished between now and December 2018. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements

Activities

1.1: Screening will be done at the community level by WASDA staff together with Community nutrition workers. 1.2: All children referred will be subjected to further screening at the fixed sites and those found to be acutely malnourished will be admitted for treatment. Complicated cases will be referred to the stabilization center in Dobley. 2.1: All children admitted into IMAM program will be treated as per the IMAM protocol guideline for different components. MTMSGs will be established within the existing OTP/TSFP centers, screening using Mother Muac approach and follow up. Since most of the deliveries are conducted at home by TBAs,it will be very crucial to include them in the support group. MtMSG members will be trained on Mother Muac approach for early identification of acutely malnourished children at community level. 2.3: Together with the local leadership community health workers, (both male and female) will be identified and trained on IMAM Guideline. 3.1: Technical staff (clinical officer, Nurses and Nutritionist) will support nutrition staff by training them on the job at the OTP/TSFP sites. 3.2: Community mobilizers will be trained and in turn support the training of community workers to actively screen and detect malnutrition cases at the community level. 3.3: Conducting OTP/TSFP outreach programme in remote rural sites across two districts.3.4:Training (new and refresher) of health facility staff on management of acute malnutrition. Participating cluster coordination meetings. The project will integrate with WASH and livelihood funded projects in the area.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

WASDA will put protection needs of project beneficiaries at the heart of its programming. WASDA Strive to internally integrate its own project and that of other actors working in the same location while Using diverse participatory approaches that will foster integration between the host communities, IDPs and returnees. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The proposed project is targeting areas and communities living in insecure and conflict sensitive environments where abuses and rights violations of women, youth, and children, the disabled and elderly often occur but sometimes unreported. Mitigation measures: WASDA will ensure that all its project activities are guided by the humanitarian principles of ensuring non-discrimination and the safety, dignity and integrity of assisted groups especially displaced populations. WASDA will seek to limit harm to vulnerable community members by putting systems in place that enable a close monitoring of the situation of target beneficiaries and communities, and the effective management of complaints. WASDA staff will regularly attend and contribute to the inter agency and cluster meetings including those called by the local administrations, project target communities and more so specifically the protection cluster and agencies to share and learn lessons and best practices as well as to share reported critical protection issues. WASDA will ensure protection monitoring and community complaints mechanisms (CCM) are established and include processes for effectively handling incidents of gender based violence, corruption and sexual exploitation and abuse and/or referring cases on to competent authorities or colleague agencies.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will be implemented with strong consideration to gender mainstreaming to ensure it largely benefits women, men, boys and girls that are most vulnerable and have the highest humanitarian needs. Women, girls, men, boys and the elderly will be given representation at all levels to ensure participation and joint decision making. WASDA will collect gender and age dis-aggregated data while carrying out monitoring of project activities. In order to ensure that the most vulnerable are reached, WASDA will employ community based targeting methodology. Selection criteria will be jointly established with the community leaders and local authorities. Key cross cutting issues such as gender, age and vulnerability will be considered during beneficiary selection.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The proposed project is targeting areas and communities living in insecure and conflict sensitive environments where abuses and rights violations of women, youth, and children, the disabled and elderly often occur but sometimes unreported. In order to mitigate the effeccts of conflict and violence on civilians, WASDA will strongly cordinate with all humanitarian and state actors so as to ensure civilian protection remains a top priority. WASDA will also ensure that its project activities will not undermine the safety and security of project beneficiaries.
