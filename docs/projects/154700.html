Needs

An estimated 2.3 million people across Iraq remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to safe and appropriate WASH services, of which females account for 52 per cent, children for 46.7 per cent and the elderly for 4 per cent. Approximately 1.4 million people including IDPs from Ninewa, Kirkuk, Salah Al-Din and Anbar governorates residing in 33 camps will continue to require some level of specialized WASH support. The poor WASH situation is affecting various categories of people throughout Iraq, with the highest needs remaining among the IDP population in camps. Camps in Anbar are in particularly poor condition and in need of support to improve hygiene practices, WASH-related infrastructure and sanitation facilities. As highlighted by the WASH Cluster Gap Analysis from September 2018, 22 IDP camps are still below the SPHERE minimum standards, with more than 20 people sharing one latrine. The poor conditions or poor design of sanitation facilities, coupled with inadequate management of wastewater is placing 482,085 people in camps and surrounding areas at risk of water-borne diseases as a result of discharge of untreated wastewater into surrounding surface water, thereby polluting drinking water sources. IOM is the supporting camp management agency in Amiryat Al-Falluja and Bizabiz (BZBZ) camps in Anbar. Both locations have inadequate WASH services and in critical need of support for liquid and solid waste management, consistent water provision and maintenance (O&M) of water supply systems, and support to improve sanitation practices. In these locations WASH needs are not covered fully covered. Both locations are not receiving a comprehensive WASH response that address the minimum humanitarian needs according to SPHERE, including but not limited to hygiene promotion and sanitation activities.

Activities

In response to the severe WASH gaps identified in the IDP camps in Anbar, IOM proposes to address the most urgent WASH-related needs, while integrating activities aimed at supporting healthy hygiene and sanitation practices in camps with the camp management support of IOM. The main objective of the project is to continue to operate, improve, upgrade and sustain quality and standards of water and sanitation services, and provide an enabling environment for good hygiene practices in protracted displacement (in camps). IOM will contribute towards the overall objective through the promotion of good hygiene practices, provision of safe drinking water, reduction of environmental health risks, and improved conditions that allow people to live with good health, dignity, comfort and security. Activities will be carried out in AAF and BZBZ camp in Anbar. Systematic and participative planning approaches will be integrated to implement effective hygiene promotion campaigns that promote positive hygiene practices in the household, food and personal hygiene and facilitate meeting the SPHERE Minimum Standards. Systematically planned and participatory hygiene promotion campaigns will reduce waterborne diseases in camps and are hence pivotal to the success of overall WASH interventions in this setting. Community hygiene promoters will be selected from the IDP community, ensuring gender-balanced teams, and trained to carry out hygiene promotion campaigns. IOM will prioritize community engagement and mobilisation to the extent possible, also engaging the IDP community in cash-for-work. WASH committees in the camps will encourage and support IDP populations to take an active role in operations and maintenance of sanitation facilities, which helps to safeguard good hygiene in the longer term. ActivitiesThe below activities will be carried out in coordination with the camp level WASH committees, IDP communities and the local authorities and in line with the recommendations of the WASH Cluster: 1.1. Carry out a comprehensive WASH needs assessment in the targeted camps, including Knowledge, Attitude and Practice (KAP) surveys to measure impact and gather lessons learned;1.2. Develop and carry out systematic promotion of hygiene through the training and mobilization of community hygiene promoters that will conduct awareness campaigns, house to house visits, focus group discussion, integrating innovate approaches (ie. participatory theatre, environmental campaigns, child-friendly IEC materials) with the aim to improve hygiene practices;1.3. Procure and distribute hygiene non-food item kits, including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and carry out targeted information sessions on the proper use of materials; 1.4. Establish/support WASH committees in the targeted camps and provide capacity building sessions to ensure adequate management, maintenance and operation of WASH-related infrastructure;1.5. Ensure sustained and improved WASH services in the targeted camps, implementing activities through Cash for Work as possible, including:i. Desludging of latrines and safe disposal in coordination with the local authorities; ii. Construct, rehabilitate and/or redesign latrines and bathing stations to ensure proper function and design in line with cultural norms;iii. Construct/rehabilitate/maintain waste water and rain water management systems, including drainage canals and flood mitigation; iv. Ensure the provision of safe water through the construction/rehabilitation and sustained operation and maintenance (O&M) of water supply systems;v. Carry out regular garbage disposal collections, including large-scale cleaning campaigns, to ensure proper waste management. IOM will make sure WASH-related activities, equipment and infrastructure is in line with cultural norms and protection principles, including community participation into the assessment, design and implementation of activities. The inclusive consultation process will especially ensure that women and girls are consulted on the location and design features of water points, latrines and bathing facilities.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Activities not implemented in a culturally appropriate manner resulting in the non-usage of WASH-related infrastructure and NFI;2. Landowners unhappy with the disposal of garbage collected;3. Actors covering some areas of the camps but not others, resulting in areas that are underserved and under assisted;4. Camp closure.

Mitigation Measures

1. Engagement with the local communities regarding preferences of materials, design and NFIs to provide culturally appropriate WASH infrastructure and households items;2. Coordination with local muktars, host communities and hiring of staff from the host communities to implement activities;3. Creation of WASH committees, or coordination with WASH committees in the targeted camps as well as the WASH Cluster to ensure adequate coverage and needs addressed for the camp populations;4. Coordination with the Government of Iraq on the camp consolidation and closure plans and targeting camps that will remain open for a longer period of time.
