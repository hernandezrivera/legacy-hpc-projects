Project Summary

Decades of conflict, recurrent climate shocks, disease outbreaks and increasing poverty are devastating the people of Somalia. Despite progress in recent years. In 2021, the country faced heightened political tensions, at times associated violence, in the context of a delayed electoral process and power struggles at the leadership level. In southern and central Somalia, conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. There is a serious risk that the combined impact of consecutive seasons of below-average to poor rainfall can develop into a major drought by early to mid-2022. At the same time, large parts of Somalia remain prone to severe riverine and flash flooding. Somalia was among the countries hardest hit by the worst desert locust upsurge in decades that started in 2019. Although infestation levels declined in 2021 as a result of intensive control operations and low rainfall, a risk to rural livelihoods across the country remains. COVID-19, as well as cholera, measles, polio and acute watery diarrhea (AWD) has also impacted the country’s fragile health systems, with a resurgence of cases and documented infections in the first quarter of 2021.
An estimated 6.6 million people, about 42 per cent of the Somali population are in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and the elderly for 4 per cent. This presents an increase of 43 per cent from 2021, in which 4.6 million people were estimated to be in need of WASH services across Somalia. Severe drought condition, seasonal flood, diseases outbreak and conflict related displacement are contributing to the increase in the number of people in need. Rural areas and hard to reach districts have shown a significant increase in the number of people in need compared to last year this also attributed to increasing vulnerability due to limited access to water and sanitation among rural areas and IDP sites.
International Medical Corps has been providing integrated Health, Nutrition, WASH and GBV services to the IDPs and Host communities of Jowhar, Galkacyo South, Baidoa and Mogadishu/Wadajir districts. IMC is proposing to continue provision of water, sanitation and Hygiene services through improved access to safe water and sanitation thereby reducing the impact of the cyclical droughts and mitigating the recurring outbreaks of AWD/cholera, besides continuation of addressing the emergency face of COVID-19 pandemic in the above named areas. The estimated beneficiary targeted in the proposed locations are 14,000 (Host 6,087 and IDPs 7,913).
Coronavirus response activities will be integrated in all our WASH programming. As new clusters of local transmissions of COVID-19 reported daily, IMC WASH priorities focus on strengthening health care service delivery at the facility level by establishing safe water supplies, adequate sanitation infrastructure, accessible handwashing facilities, and rigorous training of healthcare staff in infection prevention and control. Moreover, IMC has considered a comprehensive approach of integrating the software and hardware WASH approach of review, acknowledging, and delivering the response activities to support the WASH/IPC thematic areas about the outbreak response. IMC works with the beneficiaries to meet their WASH needs in emergency and development contexts by providing improved access to WASH infrastructure at community and institutional facilities. To reduce the risk of violence against WASH facility users, IMC will ensure water and sanitation are constructed close to dwellings and that toilets are lockable. A universal design will be adopted to ensure water and sanitation facilities are accessible to all, including persons with disabilities.

1. - Projects activities

Sub-sector 1: Water Supply Activity 1a. IMC proposes the rehabilitation of 7 shallow wells across the four regions of operation. The expected number of beneficiaries per hand pump shallow well is estimated to be 500 people based on flow rate of 16 l/m as per the Somalia WASH cluster guideline and SPHERE standards. IMC WASH engineer will assess the technical aspects of the rehabilitation works to be undertaken in line with the Somalia WASH cluster guidelines. This includes verifying the water yield, depth of the well, water static depth, possible source of contamination, mode of abstraction of water and many others. Activity 1b. IMC will install solar powered pumps to 3 high yielding shallow wells with sufficient and sustained water yield of more than 5m³/hour. The water will be delivered through gravity from an elevated 10m³ water reservoir to 6 tap stands strategically located around the water source Activity 2. Emergency water trucking (EWT) - IMC proposes to undertake short term water distribution through water vouchers to 5 most affected Baidoa IDP settlements (Seef-dheer-330HH, Jubada-dhexe-250HH, Banow-85HH Qansaxdheere-300HH, and Haluul & Harqaan-155HH,) serving a population of 1,120 households (6720 individuals) Activity 3. IMC proposes rehabilitation of 2 strategic boreholes in the hard-to-reach nomadic villages of Galkacyo South district. Activities include: replacement of generator spare parts; replacement of submersible pump spare parts; repair and replacement of borehole rising pipes; and rehabilitation of water storage facilities and animal troughs. Activity 4. WASH team will conduct routine water quality monitoring and treatment of the water storage facilities with chlorine to maintain acceptable Free Residual Chlorine (FRC). Batch chlorination by trained staff ensures minimum levels of FRC is met before distribution. Dosing rates at the storage tanks will yield sufficient residual chlorine at the point of consumption. Sub-sector 2: Sanitation IMC proposes construction of 80 shared household latrines, bathing spaces in targeted IDP settlements in Baidoa, Jowhar, Mogadishu (Wadajir) and Galkacyo south. Each latrine will be shared by an estimated 3 households (at most 20 individuals) as per SPHERE standards. Sub-sector 3: Hygiene Promotion - IMC proposes to carry out routine and continuous hygiene promotion activities at four levels: communities, schools, water points, and health facilities. Health education and hygiene promotion will target school children as the entry point and change vehicle for their families and communities. At the community level, the program will undertake massive health and hygiene education and promotion campaigns. Community-based hygiene and sanitation resource persons called Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs). Sub-sector 4: WASH NFIs - IMC is proposing the procurement and distribution of 2,000 hygiene kits to targeted IDP camps in the 4 targeted areas. IMC integrates WASH activities with health, nutrition and protection. Sub-sector 5: Environmental Health - Improved environmental health service is closely linked to improved public health and nutrition. Addressing potential causes of waterborne disease and malnutrition is intended to contribute to reduction of the public health risks associated with poor hygiene, water, poor sanitation and hygiene practices. IMC will support 8 IDPs (@2each for Baidoa, Galkacyo, Jowhar & Wadajir). through established & trained 80 solid waste management volunteers @20 for each districts. The volunteers will mobilize the community and participate in community clean up campaigns.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

On the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.), IMC will enhance protection mainstreaming in all its WASH services. IMC through its Community Hygiene promoters (CHPs), and Community Liaison focal person will engage community stake holders on its available services and as well enhance community education, mobilization and awareness on available Protection services in its areas of operation. IMC as well via its other funding sources will ensure referral pathways is available for protection survivors. On the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities; IMC will engage WASH committee, community elders, local authority and other relevant stakeholder’s in project design, implementation and closure as well as in response to emergencies. For beneficiaries with disabilities and or with limited mobility, IMC will ensure its rehabilitated/constructed water supply and sanitation infrastructure are disability friendly to have ramp/easy access paths to accommodate persons with impaired mobility. IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Nutrition/WASH/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. Standby Emergency Rapid Response Teams (ERRTs) comprising of key staff will be maintained to enable quick respond to trauma emergency/outbreaks including cholera, AWD, measles when a threshold is attained. Beneficiaries seeking services regardless of age, sex, clan affiliation, workplace and household will be treated equally. Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific needs and designs. On staff recruitment particularly community Hygiene promoters will be recruited from beneficiary communities and will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, marginalized ethnic groups). IMC will form gender and ethnic group balanced Community WASH Committees and community solid waste management volunteers to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes, promoting a sense of ownership and improving the relevance and sustainability of the WASH services. On the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, IMC maintains a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained and logged. Throughout the project cycle, IMC will ensure that it communicates with affected populations in formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate.
