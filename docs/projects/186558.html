Project Summary

The project seeks to innovatively scale up and improve access to quality integrated GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services adapted to both the changing context of protracted crisis in the midst of government camp closures, resettlements/secondary displacement and lingering Covid-19 pandemic across the BAY states of northeast Nigeria.
The targeted interventions will includ provision of lifesaving multi-sectorial and multi-level services that will address the medical, psychosocial, legal and safety needs of GBV survivors in IDP camps, host communities and communities of return as well strengthening inclusive community protection and dispute resolution mechanisms to mitigate and prevent GBV both as the sub-sector lead and as the provider of last resort.
The project will not only focus on expanding and maintain services in safe spaces/shelters and one stop-centres (OSC), but improving referrals to medical and other services while ensuring enhanced capacity for quality delivery of clinical management of rape (CMR), case management, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), legal and protection services, procurement and distribution of menstrual/dignity kits, cultural appropriate clothing, field coordination including referral pathways and ethical data management. The intervention will also seek to engage men and boys as allies and agent of change through the introduction of evidence based interventions like EMAP to address the root causes and harmful gender norms.

Other complementary activities will look at strengthening community resilience and promoting GBV/SEA risk mitigation mechanism in humanitarian setting. Our target is to reach 55% (750,000) of the sectors people in need (1,362,447) in the BAY States. Special emphasis will be put on strengthening joint inter-agency coordination, M&E, capacity building, assessment and partnership that will translate to better outcome.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, the contextual factors and the humanitarian impacts have caused a total of 7.63 million people to face adverse humanitarian conditions in areas accessible to aid workers in the BAY states.1 (Estimates indicate a further 730,000 people in need in accessible areas.) Out of the 61 LGAs that the JIAF classified using MSNA and other data, 22 are estimated at ‘extreme’ level in terms of severity of needs; another 20 at ‘severe’ level. This means that more than 4.4 million people face an extreme degree of needs and more than 2.9 million have severe needs in the BAY states. The people in need are divided into three groups—IDPs, returnees, and host communities—all with different types of need according to severity levels. Overall, 65% of the IDP households have reported at least one kind of vulnerability: having a female-headed household, a family member with a mental or physical disability, a pregnant girl or women in the household; a child separated from them; or being or having a married child. Out of the 2.22 million IDPs in need, 1.82 million reside in the forty-two LGAs whose inter-sectoral needs rate as 'extreme’ or ‘severe’ (around 1.17 million and 657,000IDPs respectively). Among those IDPs living having extreme severity of needs 81% are in Borno state. Some 38% % of the women are currently breastfeeding or pregnant. Moreover, almost 10% when using the Washington short set of questions to identify disability, 16% of the IDPs households report that they have a family member with a physical disability. At least 1.1% of IDP households report having a child who had married before the age of 17, and while this seems a small proportion, it equates to some 5,000 married children. A notably higher proportion of in-camp IDP respondents had suffered safety or security incidents: an armed attack (65%, vs. 57% among out-of-camp IDP respondents), physical violence (39% vs. 23%), or the killing of a household member (24% vs. 11%.) Few coping mechanisms are available to IDPs aside from heavy reliance on humanitarian aid. This dependence is much higher for IDPs who live in camps because of movement restrictions and paucity of livelihood opportunities. For returnees, out of the 1.5 million returnees in need, 97% reside in LGAs that are classified as having extreme or severe inter-sectoral humanitarian needs. In general, more than 83% of the returnees face extreme or severe inter-sectoral challenges. According to DTM, of the 2 million IDPs in the BAY states, only 341,000 or 17% have been displaced just once; 51% have been displaced twice, 27% three times, and 5% more than three times. The conflict has left 30% of returnee families with family member vulnerabilities, 18% being female-headed. About 20% of returnee households have a family member with chronic illness, and 36% have a family member currently pregnant or lactating. The lack of access to basic services, livelihoods opportunities and security impinge on the lives of returnees. The rehabilitation or reconstruction of essential infrastructure and the re-establishment of basic services continue to suffer delays, and many of the return locations are still affected by insecurity. This highlights the importance of mobile integrated GBV service provisions in these areas. On the other hand, the host-community as the third group are also suffering from similar challenges. Conflict, security and overall lack of basic services are the chief factors that impair the host communities’ physical and mental well-being. 2% of host-community households have suffered safety and security incidents affecting household members in the past three months. Protection remains a great concern for many families and individuals, especially for girls and women who are at higher risk of gender-based violence in many of the camps, camp-like settings and even host communities. Lack of secure sanitation facilities, safe firewood collection, and overall protective systems in some camps.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project ensures the affected persons and the beneficiaries are involved in planning, implementing and monitoring of the programs. The survivor centered approach is an integral component of this project were the survivors drive the aid delivery process toward personalized need. The production of contents of the dignity kits will be purely managed by women themselves who are beneficiaries of the project. The packaging and distribution of the kits will be managed by women-led organizations. As part of the accountability to affected population the following steps will be taken:• Consultation with the affected population, especially women and girls on culturally appropriately contents• Community Engagement on menstrual hygienic education and awareness• Engaging women in key decision making process like the vulnerability assessment • Conduct Post distribution monitoring to guide the next batch of production • Use of locally source materials and engaging women and girls in production process
