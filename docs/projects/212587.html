Project Summary

The objective of this intervention is to save lives and agricultural livelihoods of 25,000 families residing in the Dry Corridor, who were affected by the effects of El Niño in 2023 and who face the threat again in 2024.
In 2023, not only the lag at the beginning of the rains was observed but also the erratic distribution and intense (concentrated) rains, which caused losses in the Primera 2023 harvest and damage to the Postrera because the water did not arrive at the time the plant needed it. As a result of this, household food reserves have been compromised, which could become a nationwide critical situation given that the next Primera 2024 harvest would not be ready until August 2024. This means an extra extended lean season.
The above is supported by data generated by FAO-DIEM in 2023. It has been possible to analyze the results of the survey carried out between April-May 2023 and November-December 2023, that means, before and after the rainy season. The DIEM data show that 48% of those surveyed stated that they had had difficulties planting corn in Primera due to insufficient water for their crops (irrigation or rain). Farmers who planted in May lost the sowing seed or the corn in its first stages. The vast majority had to move the planting date to June when the rains returned, however, 61% of those who planted reported having had losses in corn and 53% in beans.
The above suggests that households were not able to produce enough to save reserves, sell and pay loans. 83% of households report not having sufficient corn reserves for 2023 and that what they have could last 1-3 months. Only 21% of households say they have reserves for 6 months or more.
Regarding crops funding, 86% report that they did it with their own resources and with the result of loss of the harvest they have been left without those resources; 11% reported that they took out a loan (with banks, cooperatives, savings associations) that they are paying, while 2% had credit and have managed to pay recently.
The FAO - FIES indicator (Food Insecurity Experience Scale) as of December 2023 is reported at 24.5% at the national level, having had an increase of 1.6% between the beginning and the end of the rainy season influenced by the El Niño Phenomenon. However, when observing the FIES data at the departmental level, the attention is taken to the department of Usulután reports 39.3% of the population in moderate to severe hunger, the department of Morazán with 36.6% and San Vicente with 32.1%. At least six departments exceed the FIES national average. Curiously, in three FAO - DIEM surveys carried out in El Salvador, the four departments in the east of the country, which are part of the Dry Corridor, reports higher FIES data compared to the other areas of the country.
The data cited allows us to identify some actions that must be strengthened or implemented by the government, producers, cooperation, humanitarian and development organizations, to counteract the impacts on families, for example, under the scheme of anticipatory actions they can be promoted and implemented drought-tolerant seed banks, the use of renewable energy, switching to an early harvest and good post-harvest management, making more efficient use of water by avoiding compromising water for consumption, improving the monitoring and management of pests and diseases, and others adaptive practices. It is clear that all of the above must be done being duly informed and aware of the conditions of the phenomena, threats and possible impacts for which the monitoring carried out by the DOA-MARN is key.

People affected by the drought will participate in the rehabilitation of their agricultural livelihoods and food production, but also by providing information at the baseline and monitoring the execution of actions in their communities. Female-led households will be prioritized for establishing home gardens or caring for backyard birds, other participants will be involved in broiler chicken production; Those households in which they have cattle will be provided with multi-vitamin cubes to nutritionally supplement them, and households that grow basic grains for subsistence will be provided with inputs for the planting of Primera 2024.The scope of the intervention and the mechanisms that FAO has for feedback and complaints will be shared with all participants and beneficiaries so that they can consult, dispel doubts, report problems or inappropriate situations. FAO El Salvador has a Complaints Resolution Mechanism.
