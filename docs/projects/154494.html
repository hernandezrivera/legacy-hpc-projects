Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide, Iraq (IRW-Iraq) aims to provide children and adolescents affected by the conflict access to safe and protective learning spaces inclusive of psychosocial activities. This project will target 6000 children (age 6-17 of which 50% are girls) among the displaced families in Ninewa governorate Camps (Qayarra Air-strip camp, Jedaha Camps, and Hamam Al-Alil Camp).
This project is a continuation of the same intervention in the already established Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) in the camp. The previous project has ended on 30th October 2018 due to lack of funding. The education sub-cluster in Mosul has requested IRW to continue working in the TLSs providing non-formal education to the children in the targeted camp due to the high need of supporting the education of the children in the camp. The project will mobilize the same teachers (If they are available) else, will identify teachers with a special focus on female teachers among the families in the camp in coordination with the Department of Education, distribute student and teachers kits to camp children and selected teachers respectively.
The established TLSs has been provided with students and teachers kits, recreational kits, and schools box which will be used under this project, any required additional supplies and repair of the established caravans and tents will be provided and maintained under this project.
The proposed one year project will offer the targeted children with non-formal education program at the established Temporary Learning Space (TLS), conduct PSS sessions, operate the Child Friendly spaces, and provide improvement training to 80 teacher (50 women and 30 men).
The project will also establish child club for Psycho-social support and recreational activities where the focus will be on the most vulnerable boys and girls. The project team will form community education council to raise awareness among parents/ caregivers on providing good parenting, emotional support, and basic child protection concepts.
Prior to the completion of the project, IRW will closely coordinate with DoE and Unicef to ensure that children have access to formal education. For families who will be returning to their home of origin during 2019, the project team will communicate their children names to the Doe. IRW will also monitor the situation in the Qayarra air-strip camp which currentlu host 7334 HHs, if displaced families will continue living in the camp, then IRW will mobilize fund where possible to ensure that the non-formal education is provided to children in camp setting.

Needs

According to the UNOCHA report on internally displaced people by governorate (as of 31 August 2018), there are 1,920,456 internally displaced people dispersed across 105 districts and 3,325 distinct locations in Iraq of which 598,578 people are in Ninewa, Similarly a total of 72,084 people are considered as Returnees by governorate. Ninewa governorate rank the first place of returnees with an estimated number of 1,548,108 people reutring to their origins in Ninewa. One year after ISIS’s defeat in Mosul, Ninewa governorate continues to record the highest number of returnees and displaced people in Iraq. Displaced people continue to return home across the country. As of 15 Oct 2018, 3.9 million displaced Iraqis have returned to their areas of origin, whereby the highest number of returnees are located in Ninewa governorate (1.48 million), followed by Anbar governorate (1.26 million) and Salah al-Din governorate (543,000). Ninewa governorate is also home to the largest number of displaced people with 614,790 of the almost 2 million displaced people residing mainly in Mosul’s Jad’ah, Qayyarah Airstrip and Hamam Al Alil camps. The Kurdistan Region is also hosting more than half a million Iraqis who were displaced from Ninewa governorate, mostly from Mosul. These displaced people are residing in camps and host communities in the neighbouring neighboring governorates of Dahuk, Erbil and SulaymaniyahAccording to the Qayarrah Air-strip Camp management, the camp is currently hosting 7350 families, equivalent to 41160 individuals, Similarly Jeddah camps host more than 13,648 families, equivalent to 81,888 individuals. The camp lacks a formal school to accommodate the children and provide education to the children. The nearest school is located in a different camp which is considered to be far distance for children to travel on daily basis. According to the IRW's team observation and recorded feedback in the camp, 75% of the camp families are reluctant to send their children to the schools located out of the camp especially children aged 6-15 due to lack of security and long distance.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk:1. The use of child labour obviously is a serious child protection risk and leaves children vulnerable to a number of different risks due to the unequal power dynamics and child labour involved. 2. Security Situation deteriorated in the targeted Camp3. Forced eviction of the families out of Camp to return to their origins

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation mechanism:1. The project team will sensitize families through regular meeting on the use of child labour and importance of child education for their future and the impact on their families. In addition, the project team will ensure that the formed parents council will paly a vital role of the contribution to convey the massages on mitigating the child labour. 2. Regular security update will be maintained by the project team and security focal persons involving the clusters, INSO, and the local authorities. if security situation get deteriorated in the targeted project area due any reason, Islamic Relief Iraq will avoid areas under conflict and will concentrate on comparatively safer areas for project activities implementation and might propose change the targeted area in case of inaccessibility.3. The recent reports from the MoDM shows that the capms of Jad’ah, Qayyarah Airstrip and Hamam Al Alil will remain open as they host a high number of IDPs. IRW will keep regular coordination and update on this situation an will continue runing the TLSs until end of the project.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Islamic Relief Worldwide is committed to ensure that its program interventions are appropriate and designed in a way which positively contributes in lives of children and communities. To abide by this commitment, Islamic Relief has a Monitoring, Evaluation and Accountability (MEA) system which aims at improving quality of our interventions and document key learning, at the same time MEA provide timely information to program staff and senior management to make critical and strategic decisions regarding our program interventions.The project Coordinator will take the lead role in conducting a series of community mobilization sessions at targeted locations in form of public gatherings where project goal, objectives and funded activities will clearly be outlined. The project M&E Coordinator and Project Coordinator will also be responsible for complains-feedback mechanisms whereby project beneficiaries, local authorities and other key community key people will freely contact them (through a shared and displaced phone number and complaint box ). All complains will be registered, classified (based on the seriousness of the complaint), relevant departments/sectors consulted and timely feedback relayed back to the complainant/community leaders. The project will also provide opportunities for the communities to engage directly in the project implementation thus stakeholders will be encouraged to raise their concerns.
