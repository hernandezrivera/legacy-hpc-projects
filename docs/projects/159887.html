Project Summary

In response to the Letters from local Administrations of the Donetsk Oblast (Manhush and Volnovaha Raion Administrations, Pavlopil and Talakivka Village Administrations) addressed to the NGO “Ukrainian Deminers Association” (hereafter also – Association), experts of the Association has been developed the project that will provide for an integrated approach to humanitarian demining and MRE on the territory of above mentioned local administrations of the Donetsk Oblast. The project developed in accordance with the new Law on Mine Action in Ukraine and based on international standards (IMAS) and the national standard of mine action (8820:2018).
This project includes Mine Risk Education (MRE) and different stages of humanitarian demining: Non-Technical Survey (NTS), Marking Potential Dangerous Zone, Technical Survey (TS) and Battle Area Clearance (BAC), Demining and Land transfer to Customers.
For MRE is the territory of Pavlopil and Talakivka Village Administrations, Manhush and Volnovaha Administrations.
Mine risk education with local population based on IMAS 12.10:2013 and marking of potential danger zones based on IMAS 08.40:2013 and the New Ukrainian Regulation on marking mines. All tasks of this project will be carried out taking into account the needs of the local administrations of the region, which are indicated in letters to the NGO “Ukrainian Deminers Association”.
The aim of MRE is to help reduce the risk from mines and ERW to a level where people can live safely in the presence of ERW hazards.
Complaint mechanisms and engagement with the affected population
There is guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback mechanisms (complaints and response mechanisms).
Local communities were consulted as to how they would best like to submit complaints.
At the beginning of the project, we will hold meetings in each community to inform about feedback mechanisms and channels.
The necessary tools, qualitative and quantitative indicators are determined.
The local population is informed about the activities for the implementation of the project - on an ongoing basis.
Means:
• Media,
• brochures and flyers,
• regular meetings and consultations (2 times a month),
• telephone hotline for receiving complaints and suggestions from the public,
• focus groups (2 times a month),
• participation of representatives of local communities and local authorities in meetings on project planning and implementation;
• public audit of the quality of the project (at the end of the project).
Staff is trained and well informed as to how the complaints and response mechanism works.
There are clear records of what complaints have been received and how they were responded to (Indicator - number of records of what complaints have been received and how they were responded to (Indicator - number of records).
There is a policy on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff received appropriate training.
The person responsible for this direction of activity has been identified.
The organization works with partner agencies and within the clusters to establish a joint complaints mechanism that offers a single entry point for communities – on an ongoing basis.
As for humanitarian demining, we will conduct double quality control inspection before land transfer: internal and external. Internal will be provided by the quality control engineer of the operator and external will provided by the External Quality Control inspection of the Demining Centre of MoD (Kamianets-Podilskyi). This will be carrying out as a part of the mechanisms for ensuring accountability.
Generally, the project has two main aims:
to help reduce the risk from mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) to a level for the local people in the territory of the Donetsk Oblast including the strategic area near the Entry-Exit Checkpoint (EECP) Gnutovo;
to adapt to new Law on Mine Action in Ukraine and New Ukrainian Regulations on Mine Action.

1 - Needs

Mine and unexploded ordnance (UXOs) contamination affect the lives of two million people in the eastern part of Ukraine, putting vital civilian infrastructure facilities at risk, inhibiting freedom of movement, access to livelihoods and limiting recreational activities for children. Ukraine ranks amongst the most severely affected places in the world for casualties; as a result, of landmines and other ERW. According to the last annual report "Landmine Monitor 2018" published by the Geneva International Centre for Humanitarian Demining (GICHD) placed, Ukraine placed 3d in the world for a number of casualties. On a monthly basis, the number of civilians injured or killed by these hazards regularly outstrips those as a direct result of active conflict. Landmines, ERWs and UXOs were the leading cause of child casualties in 2017-2018, accounting for about two-thirds of all recorded deaths and injuries that left many children with lifelong disabilities. A recent GICHD’s report Ukraine as number one in the world, ahead of both Afghanistan and Syria, for anti-vehicle mine casualties. These statistics come as an immediate result of the ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine, which has seen significant areas of land contaminated by landmines and UXO in both government-controlled (GCA) and non-government controlled areas (NGCA). Concerted efforts have been mounted in GCA to tackle the issue of explosive hazard contamination; both by the Government of Ukraine and the international community, but critical challenges remain. In NGCA, little to no coordinated action has been taken, whilst the situation is understood to be acute.The territories of the Pavlopil and Talakivka Village Administrations is a strategic area for Ukraine due to presence there the Entry-Exit Checkpoint Gnutovo. Every day EECP Gnutovo crossed by a thousand people who, in turn, are faced by the danger of mines and ERW.As a result of hostilities in the territory of the Pavlopil and Talakivka Village Administrations, there was an urgent need for humanitarian demining activities. In the period from 2015 to 2018, 8 accidents occurred on the territory of the Pavlopil Village Administration and 4 accidents occurred on the territory of the Talakivka Village Administration caused by EOD, which resulted in victimization among the local population, farm animals, damage to equipment. At the same time, by means of measures to inform the local population of the village community, 12 possible similar incidents were prevented.Moreover, MRE on the territory of the Manhush and Volnovaha Administrations and Talakivka Village Administration will help to reduce the risk from mines and explosive remnants of war.Our Organization will consider OHCHR research concerning casualties as a result of EO. According to GAM the most vulnerable category is male (31-46 ages) that is around 47%, boys (12-17 ages) – 17%, and female (30-39 ages) – 14%. Moreover, the statistics show us that many women are victims of improper handling of male EO.

This will be achieved as follows: NTS about 170 hectares;Marking about 170 hectares;TS about 170 hectares;BAC about 170 hectares. Target 2 - Provide MRE in the Mangush and Volnovaha Raion Administration, and Talakivka Village Administration for: children around 8500 more than 120 school-teachers, psychologists and volunteers; around 590 medical personnel. Target 3 - Preparing MRE trainers among teachers of educational institutions (schools, colleges, vocational schools) Target indicators: The number of teachers (women and men) who have been trained and received certificates more than 120. The number of conducted ToT trainings - 5. The number of distributed workbooks to students.Target 5 – During conducting of MRE drop attention to the most vulnerable category: beforehand male (31-46 ages) that is around 47%, and also boys (12-17 ages) – 17%, and female (30-39 ages) – 14%.
