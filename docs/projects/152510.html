Project Summary

Medair has worked in South Sudan since 1992. Currently its operations consist of the static projects in Renk (former Upper Nile State), Aweil Centre (former Northern Bahr el Ghazal State) and Leer and Mayendit (former Unity State) with the main objective of decreasing preventable morbidity and mortality due to acute malnutrition by treating children and PLWs with GAM and underlying illnesses, building local nutritional treatment and prevention capacity, and increasing preventative infant and young child feeding practices. In all the sites, Medair’s main activity will be implementing the management of acute malnutrition, according to the national CMAM guidelines, targeting the vulnerable 5 and pregnant and lactating women. This will include facility and community level promotion of IYCF-E in line with national MIYCN guidelines as well as training, capacity building and regular supportive supervision at supported Medair sites. In total, the target for 2019 will be of 6,602 SAM new admissions, 17,374 MAM new admissions and 9,529 PLWs.
In Renk, Medair remains the only long-term nutrition partner and one of the only health actors to respond to the nutritional emergency. In May 2018, Medair conducted a SMART survey in Renk County that revealed GAM and SAM rates above the emergency threshold, with the critical level of 22.8% and 6.0% among IDPs and 26.9% and 6.4% among the host community, respectively.
Medair performed a SMART survey in Leer County in April 2018 showing a GAM rate of 12.2% (SAM 3.0%). Soon after the completion of the survey, violence erupted in southern Unity resulting in displacement of the population and interruption of the services. A mass MUAC screening in July 2018 (during the OCV mass vaccination campaign) revealed a ‘proxy GAM’ of 19%, corresponding to very critical levels of acute malnutrition (IPC 5 for Acute Malnutrition). The subsequent FSNMS round 23 (done in early August 2018) showed a GAM (by WFH) of 13.7% (however, it has to be noted that the exercise captured children from 0-59 months and not the classic category of 6-59 months as in the standard SMART survey)
In Aweil Centre, the results from the SMART survey of July 2018 revealed the prevalence of a GAM rate of 6.3% (including a SAM prevalence of 1.3%), which is much lower than the GAM rate of 23.3% measured by the SMART survey of July 2017.
The IPC classification of September 2018 indicates that Aweil Centre is IPC 3 (Serious) and would likely deteriorate at least one IPC Phase without continued humanitarian assistance.
In all of these project sites, Medair will ensure the minimum WASH package of safe water and latrine access, including waste management is present and functional. Systematic malaria RDT is conducted on admission for SAM children, and treatment of malnourished children suffering from malaria will be implemented. Moreover, treatment of co-existing illness and measles vaccination will be ensured. Where possible, linkage with FSL partner will be encouraged, while child friendly spaces and early child development activities will be fostered in order to allow the full neuro-cognitive development of the child and promote the importance of mother-child relationship.
The Emergency Response Team (ERT) responds to nutrition emergencies in South Sudan.
About 64% of the 33 SMART surveys conducted between January and August 2018, reported critical nutrition situations with GAM prevalence of 15% and above - the WHO emergency threshold. Moreover, according to the Nutrition Information Working Group (NIWG) of South Sudan, about 76% of the 21 repeated SMART surveys conducted in same period and in the same locations in 2017 and 2018 confirmed the critical levels of acute malnutrition.
The 2018 FSNMS SMART survey results (Round 23 performed in August 2018) depicted a concerning nutrition situation in most parts of the country: about 61% of the 46 counties reported serious or critical levels of acute malnutrition.

Needs

Following the results from the mass MUAC screening performed in August 2018, showing a very low coverage (less than 50%) of the nutrition services in both the rural and urban area (Renk town), Medair will increase its support from 12 nutrition sites to up to 14 (up 5 static and 9 mobile locations, including a Stabilization Centre, which is the only one operating in the entire County).In particular, one more static site will be opened in Renk town. There will be also an expansion of the Care Groups to a further 6 locations (mobile sites). In Leer, Medair currently runs nutrition services in 3 sites (Meer islands, Padeah, Din Din), which have been recently re-opened after the cessation of violence and hostilities. The plan in 2019, If security permits, would be to re-establish the previous sites of , Gandor, Guat, Adok (outreach of Meer) and Luol (Mayendith) and open the new site of Bow. In Aweil Centre, Medair operates in up to 18 nutrition sites, an increase from 10 since June 2018, following the results of the mass MUAC screening of March 2018 done alongside the mass measles campaign vaccination. The coverage of the nutrition services resulted to be poor in Aweil Centre rural area, with values less than the minimum acceptable SPHERE standards of 50%. The activities will continue in 2019 and will be constantly tailored to address the uncovered nutrition needs in the county,. Gender has a prominent role in the evolution of food and nutrition insecurity. Poverty and poor access to resources among women result in widespread food access constraints. The region is characterised by the significant presence of female-headed households, with significantly poorer access to resources and services. For this reason, Medair plans to create mother-to-mother support groups under the form of Care Groups to improve heathy behaviors such as strengthened infant and young child feeding practices and increase resilience of the mothers and the households. Care Groups will be developed in 2019 in Aweil town and in further specific rural locations in Aweil Centre county. Medair’s project purpose is to continue to provide that humanitarian nutritional assistance to prevent deterioration of the situation while building future local nutritional capacity. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will be deployed for a period of up to 3 months to response to nutritional emergencies - through helping integrate nutrition services in health facilities, building nutrition capacity of local partners or scaling up mobile responses in remote areas with nutrition service coverage gaps.

Activities

Infant Young Child Feeding and Behaviour Change In Renk, Leer, and Aweil, Medair will use the National MIYCN Guidelines to govern the dissemination of IYCF messaging. This will be integrated into each level of contact beneficiaries have with staff throughout the OTP and TSFP programmes to provide sufficient opportunities for knowledge acquisition and to promote behaviour change. Child friendly spaces will be used as an opportunity to support mothers, in particular in the violence-affected areas of Leer County. Medair is actively monitoring and, in case, willing to report to the competent MoH authorities about the detrimental distribution of breast-milk substitute, other milk products, bottles and teats in the areas where it works, in such a way adhering to the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes and following the directives of the MIYCN Guidelines of South Sudan. Management of Acute Malnutrition Medair will continue treating children under 5 years for SAM and MAM, through the provision of the outpatient CMAM programme in up to 40 (up to 14 in Renk, up to 18 in Aweil, up to in 8 Leer) locations across Aweil Centre, Leer, Mayendit, and Renk counties. Medair will use the 2016 National Ministry of Health approved CMAM Guidelines to govern implementation of the Nutrition Programme. As per standard protocol, all children with complicated severe acute malnutrition will be referred to a SC run by Medair (i.e. Abayok, Renk) or another implementing agency. OTP and TSFP will either be integrated into existing Medair emergency health services (as is the case in Wunthou and Abayok in Renk), established alongside health activities being implemented by other actors (as is the case across all sites in Leer and Mayendit), or integrated into the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services in Health Pooled Fund/MoH-run health facilities in Aweil Centre. The community mobilisation activities will rely on the development of networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers/Workers (CNVs and CNWs). Medair closely follows the CMAM and MIYCN guidelines for all nutrition programming in South Sudan for both SAM and MAM cases. Integration As explained above, energy and resources will be employed to ensure that minimal WASH package, health package (RDT and measles vaccination; provision of amoxicillin, and deworming according to the CMAM protocol, ACT for the treatment of malaria when need; management of co-existing illnesses; referral to the stabilisation center according to the CMAM action protocol for OTP); FSL linkage and ECD activities will be implemented in the best patient’s interest and according to the directives of the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will aim to provide the full CMAM package in all emergency interventions if needed. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the needs assessment. New sites when fully established will aim to be in secure and accessible areas, while also ensuring privacy for inpatient caregivers, where possible. Medair will endeavour to recruit qualified staff or mentor existing partner staff to run nutrition activities at the targeted nutrition sites. Emergency nutrition intervention will be rolled out in the following cluster rapid response phased model depending on security and accessibility of target areas• Phase 1: High insecurity location with limited accessibility, day trips: MUAC screening at a central location and BP5 distribution • Phase 2: High GAM/immediate need to respond, limited access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: MUAC screening, provision of 2-week ration for MAM and SAM with deworming, key MIYCN/HHP messaging and follow-up visits as security permits• Phase 3: Secure Location with access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: establish full CMAM programme, identify or capacity building of long-term partners for handover

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered from the Care Groups . In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions.Through a network of community nutrition volunteers/workers who do house-to-house outreach, communities are regularly informed about existing services. When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment as well as the fact that the assessment does not automatically result in an intervention. The same message is consistently shared during the assessment at HH level, as well as during FGDs and other meetings held. Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. In case an emergency intervention will be conducted, continuous messaging through a mixture of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed—including through the use of CNVs going door-to-door.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback gathered through the Care Groups. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to elicit their opinions and advice regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, creating a network of local contacts from which community feedback is sought from both men and women. Additionally, Medair conducts regular ‘knowledge, practice and coverage’ evaluations, using local staff to conduct these exercises which elicit information from communities regarding health needs and behaviours. Local staff is also used for SMART surveys. Barrier analysis is utilised to engage community participation in overcoming obstacles to accessing services and practicing healthy behaviours.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Medair conducts coverage monitoring for nutrition services to ensure that the needs are covered adequately including sex disaggregation. Where this is not the case, Medair will strengthen the community outreach component of the programme, as well as use focus group discussions and community meetings to understand, address and mitigate access barriers.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Medair’s nutrition programmes are almost always integrated with health services including SGBV. So Nutrition teams are informed of safety issues that might arise for beneficiaries through the other sectors as well as directly through local implementing staff when needed. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of safety through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity is one of Medair’s values. As such, it is an organisational priority in all interventions and staff are trained to understand what this means in how to treat beneficiaries, treating them with respect and to recognise their value in designing and implementing projects. Medair monitors beneficiary perception of dignity through use of a specialised protection mainstreaming questionnaire in all emergency interventions.
