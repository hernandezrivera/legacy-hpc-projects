Project Summary

The project objective is to respond to the Health and nutrition cluster objective to ensure the availability,
accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza including through health
system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building
This project aims to provide urgent response to immediate health needs by ensuring access to health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable
patients (pregnant, lactating women, neonates and children under age of five; boys and girls) through the provision of medical supplies, targeted outreach postnatal home visiting services and solarization of 2 Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICUs).
The project will respond to the key components of the Health and Nutrition cluster emergency response, namely
procurement of essential medical supplies to increase the availability of drugs, disposables, and medical equipment to ensure accurate diagnostics and treatment for the high risk pregnant and lactating women including victims of or at risk of GBV, newborns and children under age of
five (boys and girls) across the 5 governorates in Gaza Strip. Furthermore, access to the quality newborn and child health and nutrition care
will be improved through solairzation of 2 NICUs (European Gaza and NPH), further enhancement of health workers’(doctors and nurses; females and males) capacity on lifesaving health and nutrition interventions, including essential newborn care, Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) and provision of early intervention services to children with delays and disabilities and strengthening community’s preparedness, response and resilience, especially during crises. After the completion of joint UNICEF, Save the Children and WFP ongoing Nutrition needs assessment in Gaza, interventions will be informed and response will be amplified to decrease risks of acute malnutrition among young children, pregnant and lactating women.
The total number of women and children that will benefit from the HRP funding will be 60,695 across the 5 Gaza governorates
over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that 25,000 high-risk women and caregivers and 35,595 children under age of five (18,153 boys and 17,442 girls) and 100 health professionals(doctors and nurses; female 60 percent and 40 percent males) will benefit from urgently
needed lifesaving health and nutrition interventions.

Needs

Of the total population in need in Gaza 677,485 (55 percent), are female, and over 583,814 are children. The nearly depleted health sector has been overreached to respond to high numbers of casualties from the incidents at the fence which has led to an estimated 22,500 cases of not being able to access essential healthcare. This includes response to the health needs of high-risk pregnant and lactating women and young children under five. The situation with maternal and infant mortality in Gaza improved, still significant proportion of child deaths that occurs during the first year of life (the neonatal period), particularly in the first 28 days can be managed through quality maternal and neonatal health care services. Per the MoH, of the total number of annual pregnancies in Gaza, 27,600 are at high risk, with 23 percent of preterm births and around 10,000 neonates (MoH Gaza Birth Report, 2017) need transfers to neonatal intensive care units for immediate health care and early intervention. Even those maternity and neonatal wards which are adequately equipped are overstretched due to their limited numbers. This leads to the early discharge of mothers and their babies, within 2-3 hours after birth and with no access to post-natal home visiting (PNHV), which reduces opportunities for detection of medical complications and provision of lifesaving interventions. Recent findings of the External evaluation of the UNICEF’s supported PNHV programme (UNICEF, PNHV Evaluation Report,2018) provided strong arguments for the relevance and efficiency of the PNHV targeted interventions as the lifesaving interventions particularly for the most vulnerable and hard to reach pregnant, lactating women and new-borns.Gaza’s Central Drug Store noted in August, 47 percent out of a total of 516 essential medicines were at less than one month’s stock while 40 per were completely depleted and 30 percent of the 853 essential disposables were at less than one month’s supply. (HNO, 2019)The access to quality maternal and newborn health care is further compromised by increased electricity a day, that are undermining basic health care services which are already depleted by the blockade, recurrent hostilities, and restrictions on the import of medical supplies. Therefore, the life of 10,000 newborns which require immediate neonatal healthcare interventions might be severely jeopardized with intermittent and insufficient electricity supply. Further, an estimated 72,000 children under five (female: 35,280; male: 36,720) in Gaza, including 41,280 children suffering from anemia, are acutely vulnerable and are at high risk of life-threatening conditions and developmental delays (UNICEF, SitAn 2017). This situation is similar among pregnant (38 percent) and lactating women (28 percent).The current situation in Gaza affected nutritional status amongst the children under five, pregnant and lactating women, and increased their vulnerabilities and nutrition risks. (SC, H&N scoping mission in Gaza; June 2018). Furthermore, it deteriorated infant feeding practices and established a strong link between sub-optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, acute malnutrition and disease prevalence. UNICEF jointly with Save the children and WFP is undertaking the Nutrition needs assessment among young children, pregnant lactating women in Gaza. The assessment results will inform nutrition intervention and will amplify response focusing on the risks of acute malnutrition and impaired physical children’s growth and cognitive development. Gender equity in Gaza is already under tremendous pressure. As reported by the Health Cluster, the already fragile situation of the health system in Gaza will negatively impact health response and will affect women.This project is designed to contribute significantly to the gender equality and -sensitive programming focusing on the needs of gender-specific vulnerabilities among high risk pregnant lactating women and newborns.

Activities

UNICEF will partner with the MoH, NGOs and Health and Nutrition Cluster agencies (WHO, UNRWA, UNFPA, MAP UK) to address identified needs and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and child health and nutrition interventions through the implementation of a comprehensive package of services in the selected 8 maternity and neonatal hospitals, clinics and communities in 5 Gaza Governorates. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with the implementation of the WHO’s and UNFPA’s HRP 2019 projects, and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the Gaza Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. In order to reach the 2019 HRP objective “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities defined under the Health and Nutrition Cluster(HNC) will be implemented: HNC Output 1: Provide access to health care for the most vulnerable children under the age of 5, neonates and pregnant and lactating women through life-sustaining interventions at the facility and community level and Minimal Initial Service Package (MISP) (Remedial) Activity1.1.Provision of 10,000 targeted outreach health care services to families with high risk pregnant and lactating women, newborns and children under age of five(boys and girls), including newborn babies with malnutrition and disabilities. The health, nutritional and development status of mothers and children will be assessed, monitored and followed. Targeted home visiting services will be provided to 8,000 high risk pregnant and lactating women and newborns immediately after the birth within 24 hours after delivery. Treatment, including distribution of nutritional supplements, will be delivered by trained home visitors and community workers. Activity 1.2.Delivery of training for 100 doctors and nurses (female 60 per cent and 40 per cent males) from North Gaza, Rafah and Khan Younis on lifesaving health and nutrition interventions, including essential newborn care, Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) and provision of early intervention services to children with delays and disabilities (including real-time consultation and counselling) Activity 1.3. Conduct of 230 awareness-raising sessions on nutrition (‘demonstration kitchen’) at the community level for 7,000 beneficiaries and distribution of printed materials on IYCF nutrition to mothers and families. Activity 1.4.Engagement and mobilization of 3 most vulnerable communities (North Gaza, Khan Younis, Rafah) to support the delivery of quality health and nutrition services for high risk pregnant lactating women and to strengthen community’s preparedness, response, and resilience, especially during crises. HNC Output 2: Respond to the needs of the most vulnerable people by ensuring that they have access to essential secondary level health care in Gaza. (Responsive) Activity 2.1.Provision of basic essential lifesaving drugs, consumables and micronutrient supplements for neonates, pregnant lactating women including victims of or at risk of GBV, and children under the age of 5 (boys and girls), Activity 2.2.Provision of medical equipment for 3 maternity wards (Rafah Emirati, Al Aqsa, Shifa), Nasser pediatric hospital, and 4 NICUs (Gaza European, Al Aqsa, Shifa, and Nasser) (e.g. the list of required equipment available); Activity 2.3.Solarization of the 2 NICUs (European Gaza and NPH )
