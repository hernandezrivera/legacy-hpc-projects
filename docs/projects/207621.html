Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address the protracted Rohingya crisis by supporting the vulnerable Rohingya community and host communities through long term impact and the promotion of sustainable livelihoods, particularly emphasizing skill development. The project will focus on developing livelihood options for women, and youths of Rohingya camp and marginalized host communities to enhance their access to sustainable income sources.
Oxfam will provide support through targeted skill enhancement interventions that are to continue organizing 120 hours long non-formal skill development training support to the vulnerable women, youth boys and girls of the selected camp and host communities. This non-formal intervention includes handicrafts production, block-boutique printing in cloth items, reusable sanitary napkin production, carpentry, etc. In addition, this project will introduce 360 hours long TVET interventions following the sector recommended guidelines and especially on Sewing Machine Operation (Dressmaking), Solar (PV) System Installation and Maintenance in Rohingya camps and Solar (PV) System Installation and Maintenance, Agricultural Crops Production and Sewing Machine Operation in host communities. These skills will create job opening in camps and host community and these acquired knowledge and skills will help the refugees to sustainably repatriate and reintegration in Myanmar or any other places.
After completing on the job training the refugees will be involved with follow-up activities like provided input tools, linked in different camp shops, and also will be linked with other sector and agencies through volunteer engagement based on their skills and competency level. For instance, the certified 40 refugees with solar maintenance training will be linked with Protection sector through referrals or engaged by Oxfam’s Protection team. In addition, after successful completion of skill development training, all project participants in host community also will get input/tools support to start up their jobs/business. For quick promotion of products made by the host community women, market linkage to be established coordinating by organizing linkage workshop with different level of stakeholders like market actors (input seller and output buyers), chamber of commerce, representative of Govt. line department like Youth development, social welfare, Women, and child affairs), private sectors, e-commerce platforms and local and financial institutes etc. A development fair will also be arranged with close coordination with LSDS Sector for linkage building and connecting the participants with buyers or broader scale customers at host community.
The proposed project will also include some activities in the activity group 2 in objective 03, like as research and study on the current gaps, market and value chain assessment related to livelihood sector. These will result in developing common understanding, networking, and advocacy for a sustainable business/ livelihood strategy development for both the Rohingya and host community producer groups (women, men, person with disabilities and youths).
To achieve all the result in an integrated approach with close coordination (with ISCG, sectors, partners, NGOs, Govt. body etc.) and avoiding duplications the project will cover refugee camps (04, 12, 19, 22) including the host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilla (Whykong and Hnilla) where Oxfam is already working. The project will contribute and prioritize the person with disabilities as the participants of the skills training and promote the disability friendly livelihood opportunities for them.
The project will also include a small contingency fund with cash or in-kind support to respond to any emergencies considering the context and geographical risks of the region.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As the Rohingya crisis evolves and becomes more protracted, needs are increasingly becoming diverse and complex. As per MSNA conducted in 2023, 71% of refugee households reported facing barriers to income opportunities. However, out of the remaining 29%, only 5% reported volunteer engagement as their source of household income, and 14% reported income from small-business and own production. Skills need assessments by UNHCR and UCEP in 2022 found that over 76% of Rohingya have not received skill development training, with men receiving more training than women. The assessment found barriers to female participation in productive engagement due to stereotypical ideas about women's roles, low self-esteem due to low educational attainment, polygamy, unintended pregnancy, lack of opportunities and information, and a lack of family decision-making provisions. Entrepreneurship development and context-specific skills training were recommended as the two most important aspects of skill training. MSNA 2023 confirmed a high unemployment rate remaining among the workforce in the host communities (32%), particularly youth and women. The Livelihood and Skill Development Sector is supporting activities focused on developing sustainable employment and entrepreneurship-oriented skills. According to REVA-6 The highest deprivation in income was faced by female-headed households in the Rohingya camps followed by Rohingya male-headed households and male and female-headed households in the Bangladeshi community. The long-term humanitarian responses need to call for a review of the response and market linkage modalities currently in place and a rethink of humanitarian strategies for sustainable job market for Rohingya and host community. Greater emphasis should be placed on market linkage and strengthening linkages between sectors with a drive towards an integrated approach to enable meeting the essential needs of affected populations.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will strongly adhere to all Rationalization Principles, emphasizing the adoption of an integrated approach, predictability and continuity. Oxfam will implement this initiative by closely coordinating with its Protection, Gender, and other relevant sectors, promoting a comprehensive response to the protracted Rohingya crises. By supporting LSDS sector and aligning all activities within the same camps and regions, Oxfam will ensure an integrated approach between various activities and cover refugee camps (04, 12, 19, and 22) including the Ukhiya and Teknaf host community. This strategic coordination will allow Oxfam and other agencies in collaboration effectively with different stakeholders avoiding duplication of efforts. Furthermore, the project will adopt a consortium approach to enhance its effectiveness. Oxfam will Coordinate with key partners and stakeholders including Sector, NGOs, Government of Bangladesh, line departments, Chamber of commerce, Disaster Management and Relief, NGO Affairs Bureau, RRRC (Rohingya Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner), Deputy Commissioner, and UNO (Upazila Nirbahi Officer). Oxfam will actively participate in regular meetings and engage in 5W response, Sector meeting, CiC meetings, RRRC meetings, and DC office coordination meetings etc. These collaborative efforts will enhance the overall predictability and continuity of the project, through engagements with stakeholders, including market stakeholders, government representatives, and financial institutions, Oxfam aims to ensure the quick promotion of products fostering economic opportunities for project participants. Moreover, the project will actively engage in advocacy initiatives and evidenced based assessment at various levels, collaborating with, UN, NGOs, and government entities. This advocacy work will influence the policies and decisions that impact the livelihoods of the Rohingya community and host populations positively.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will prioritise Age and Disability Inclusion to follow OXFAM’s core value “Inclusiveness”. The project will make sure everyone, regardless of age or ability, can easily access the facilities, activities, and communication materials. One key part is gathering and analyzing segregated data on disabilities and age groups, will help the planning and targeted interventions. Staffs will also be trained to communicate inclusively and adapt programs for different ages and abilities. Ensuring at least 10% of the total participants to be of PwD groups, Oxfam will ensure that everyone's voice, no matter their background, influences and shapes our efforts. Advocating for the rights of individuals with disabilities and diverse age groups is central, Oxfam will coordinate actively work with specialized organizations and build alliances for a stronger impact OXFAM will also work with other groups to make sure the rights of people with disabilities and different age groups are supported.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

From inception throughout the project cycle, rights holders will be involved in the planning, design, implementation, and monitoring of the project activities. All rights holders and the wider community will be enabled to provide regular feedback to all activities through perception collection which will then be included in further programming, applying a newly proposed approach that has been piloted by Oxfam. Oxfam has piloted an approach that captures the learning and programs are adapted based on the learning and sharing from the community. It is termed the Community Perception Tracker (CPT) approach. The CPT approach will be introduced across the camps and host community locations to support program teams in responding to community perceptions through program adaptation measures. Oxfam piloted the approach in 5 countries including Bangladesh where the field situation is volatile and the wider context keeps changing. It is an effortless tool used by field staff for continued context analysis and program adaptation accordingly. Through the CPT, as part of the accountability mechanism, the mapping of perceptions is not only analyzed by locality but also by context (the difference between urban/rural/ IDP site). The CPT approach will be implemented as the project-specific AAP Framework (aligning with the steps as per IASC’s AAP guidelines). A complaint-handling committee will be established. Communication has been proven to be a challenge for both the refugee community and humanitarian organisations since the beginning of the emergency where humanitarian organisations have relied on translations by speakers of Chittagonian. The project team will work with this challenge, by ensuring all documents are translated to Rohingya, ensuring CwC principles, using visual communication for people with low levels of literacy, ensuring volunteers speak the Rohingya language and providing language training for local staff.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project includes a small contingency budget for any emergencies considering the context and geographical risks. This will enable the team to respond and adapt to potential future hazards especially over the coming monsoon season. This fund will support approximately affected by natural disasters (e.g. cyclone,fires, flood, earthquake etc.) with any emergency items selected through coordination with Sector and need analysis. This is extremely important after the immediate outset of a disaster when food or other needs can be difficult to obtain for the most vulnerable refugee and host community members, especially female-headed households, the elderly and persons with disabilities.
