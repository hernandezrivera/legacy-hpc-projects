1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project responds to urgent needs of particularly girls and boys and adolescents and will reach at least 30,650 affected people and provide gender and age appropriate protection services through access to CFS activities, provision of MPHSS, case management, CP information dissemination, MHM support and strengthening CP referral systems, community-based CP mechanisms. The project also provides services including identification, documentation and case management support to UASC, provision of family-based alternative care and referral for FTR services. PLAN will establish and reinforce internal reporting and feedback mechanisms by establishing age, gender, and culturally appropriate mechanisms suggested by children, adolescents and community members. The project key target is 30,650 conflict affected children, adolescent boys and girls, women nd men in crisis affected communities including IDPs and host community members. The tragets are divided by districts including Mueda-9,000, Montepuez-8,000, Meconta-4,650 and Chiure-9,000. Key target groups include:2,650 Children (3-5 years)-female 1,590, male 1,0608,000 children (6-9 years)-female 4,800, male 3,20010,000 Adolescents (10-17 years)-female 6,000, male 4,0002,000 young adults (18-24 years)-female 1,200, male 8008,000 adults (25 years above)-female 4,800, male 3,200.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Chiure, Montepeuz, Meuda and Meconta districts in IDP and host communities shall have similar activities:• Provision mental health and psychosocial activities for children in CFS and other safe spaces. Project will establish and equip CFS in target communities to ensure girls and boys are provided psychosocial interventions. CFS animators will be identified and trained from the community to manage age- and gender-appropriate activities in the spaces. • Provision of quality case management and support UASC; including identification, documentation and FTR, provided by trained Caseworkers to vulnerable children including UASC, CAAFAG and child survivors of SGBV. Alternative care will be provided through identification and training of foster families and Caregivers. • Establishment of and strengthening community-based CP mechanisms. The project will map existing community support structures in target communities and where necessary establish and build capacity of community-based child protection committees in collaboration with relevant government departments. The committes will be supported to led on prevention of and response to child protection concerns. • Strengthening capacity of formal CP systems to effectively prevent and respond to child protection concerns and needs. The project will work with the SEDEMAS and CP District Reference Groups to ensure capacity strengthening of Social workers in CPiE. • Dissemination of protection information to children, adolescents, parents and other community members on CP concerns implemented through community awareness sessions, meetings, during CFS activities with children, child’s clubs in schools and community radio programming. • Provision of MHM support to vulnerable adolescent girls and young women in IDP settlements and host communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project aims to strengthen protective environment and promote access to supported services for children and adolescents (girls and boys) affected by the crisis, prevent SGBV and provide appropriate gender and age support to child survivors of SGBV, neglect and exploitation. Girls and boys become vulnerable particularly when in humanitarian crisis like the risk of GBV, SEA, violence and child labour is exacerbated. To prevent these, the project will include a series of child protection activities, ensuring that crisis affected girls and boys will access psycho-social support and referral responses, including gender-based violence and comprehensive CP case management in order to prevent and respond to protection risks in the affected population Furthermore, the project will conduct a child-centred baseline risk mapping and ensure child protection concerns are seeing from children’s perspectives and ensure meaningful child participation according with the CP Principles and CPMS. The mapping will also take into consideration existing CP structures and community resources that support and addres children’s issues. Results will feel into the project to improve implementation and performance. Capacity development of children’s groups and adolescents will help enhace their meaningful participation in their own protection and ensure they lead on protection activities in their respective communities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

PLAN, informed by the IASC Commitments on Accountability to Affected People and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, will ensure array of measures to ensure accountability. PLAN is committed to involvement of beneficiaries in the design and the implementation of the response, starting with the rapid assessments at the onset of the response, PLAN will initiate an AAP assessment to ensure accountability and feedback mechanisms are in place and relevant for the response. A key component of the accountability strategy will be timely, accessible and accurate information-sharing.This will be done face-to-face, through flyers and banners, at all project sites. Information will be shared about topics such as the planning of activities and distributions, the content of the support, but also the commitments of the organisation, staff behaviour and the rights of people affected. The selection and verification of beneficiaries will be conducted in consultation with men and women in target locations PLAN will ensure the establishment of community level feedback mechanisms that are easily assessible, appropriate, child and community-friendly, through consultations and participation of cross-section of community members including children. All feedbacks will be analyzed and results felt back to the community to help to improve project implementation and performance.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

PLAN is committed to disability inclusion in all its programming. This project is also designed to include all children including child with disabilities. PLAN makes explicit the need to include children with disabilities and promotes children with disabilities to be afforded equal access to protection activities; upholding the principles of non-discrimination, participation and inclusion rights. PLAN will work to include children with disabilities in baseline mapping, project activities design, implementation and monitoring and will seek to identifiy and address the barriers children with disabilities facing to participate in project activities in order to increase access to activities and services. Project staff will be provided orientation and training on disability inclusion so that they consciously adopt inclusive principles at outset of project implementation, so that their personal attitudes and practices are more aligned with humanitarian principles, inclusion principles and child protection standards. Consultation with children with disabilities will be critical and the project team will adopt activities that tend to empower and increase children with disabilities inclusion in project activities, i.e. attention to access issues-CFS locations, training venues, sites for PSS support activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The majority of the project will be a person to person intervention with minimum environmental impact. To ensure there is limited waste produced by the project, reusable supplies will be purchased whenever possible and limited consumables, particularly single use plastic goods, will be purchased throughout the activities. For the construction of child friendly spaces, environment assessment will be contucted to ensure that materials used for CFS construction do not result into contributing to deforestation of local communities. Basic communmtiy friendly materials shall be use in construction child friendly spaces. Most staff of the project and volunteers will be recruited locally and in proximity communities in which they will work, to minimize the number of times vehicles are plying in IDP settlement centres and host communities.
