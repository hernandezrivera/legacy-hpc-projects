Project Summary

This proposal comes within the under of WASH Sector according to the following WASH cluster HRP objectives:
Objectives: Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians in area C in Zatara second phase and Bait Ta’amar in Bethlehem has access to essential services by improving access to WASH services by providing an appropriate domestic water services response for vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in fragile, and critically underserved communities by ensuring resilience and access to clean drinking water in Zatara and beit Tamir in east Bethlehem Governorate.
Country Strategic Objectives: S.O.2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
WASH Cluster Objective 1: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank

Sector Activity: Provision of domestic water services.
It is about an extension of an 8-inch carrier water line, 3900 meters from the point of Janata water, to the beginning of Za'tara municipality, serving six residential communities (Zatara, Ras Al-Wad, Beit Tamir, Jib Al-Theb, Al Aranda, Al aqban). the old line was installed before more than 50 years and suffers a loss of up to 45%, and this project is a complementary of the project to rehabilitate internal lines in Zatara.

Needs

Zatara and Beit Tamir villages are located east Bethlehem in the south of West Bank, 640 - 650 m above sea level with annual rainfall is about 324 mm; therefore residents are raising livestock as main source of income. more than one third of the two communities land is located in area C.Both communities are considered as marginalized in terms of basic needs and services, especially the water and sewage facilities. They suffer from an old water network established in the sixties, with no maintenance, hence water loses in the old network is very high and reach up to 46%, and water supply capacity become very weak, in many cases people receive water each 45 days, and during summer, 30% of the households do not receive water at all.The residents of the two communities are supplied with water by the West Bank Water Service through the public water network. The network is about 65 kilometers long.The percentage of residential units connected to the network is 80% of the total housing units in the town, while 1.5 % of the housing units rely on the water of rainwater harvesting wells for their animal use, (Note that there is no sewage network and they depend on the absorption hole). 7% rely on the purchase of water tanks, while others rely on the network and tanking.In theory, the average water supply per capita is average estimated at 40 liters per day (Zatara/ Beit Tamir Municipality/ Village council, 2018). It should be noted here this amount of water does not reach the capita due to high losses of water, in fact, only 40 liters per day per capita reached the community, that opposite the World Health Organization (WHO) recommended the minimum is 100 liters per person per day, the received amount of water is no sufficient for human use, while animal need is doubled the problem, as it increase the demand, and thus people goes for tankaring, which increase the cost of water.There are about 6500 sheep and goats in the area, sheep owners benefit from sheep milk in the form of cheese or Jamid. The owners buy feed at the summer time and graze their sheep in the eastern slopes in winter and spring to decrease the cost of production due to the high fodder prices. However, this has increase the cost of the water tanking as the area is far from the town center. The eastern slope which calcified as area C, eastern side (Malha and Musahb), western side (Ras El wad / Evangelical College) and the southern side of the village (Al Jaroush area), these areas are extremely suffering from the lack of water network. While current net water network need serious maintenance to enhance water supply more efficient.The main problems are:1- The carrier line from Jnata to Zatara and Beit Tamir is too old, over than 5y 2- High percentage of water losses, the daily losses along the carrier line is estimated to be 250- 350 cubic meters per day from Zatara village alone. This equal to 10000 cubic meters losses of water each month, which means 30000 NIS per month.3- Neither Zatra nor Beit Tamir municipality and village council and the Palestinian Water Authority PWA are able to maintain the carrier line due to the fact that the carrier line is along the by-pass road, where the Israelis does not allow anyone to do any work there. 4. Due to the bad condition of the carrier line in the by-pass road, physical contamination affected the water, like soil and small stones which affected the sub-lines capacities.This project is coming on WASH Master Plan need assessment in area C- March,2017- (PWA, ACF and GVC) and coming on the list of PWAs' priority recommended Projects for Bethlehem and Hebron (2018), thus, AAA have conducted with the PWA several meetings with the communities, the meetings covered water supply and needs in 6 communities and Bedouin groups. Participants agreed replacing the outdated carrier line with new one goes through Janata and avoid the by-pass road will solve the problem in the area, furthermore, the PWA, agrees to build a reservoir

Activities

It’s evident that replace the water carrier line between Zatara and Beit Tamir from Janata will enhance water supply for human and animal use, and will decrease the community debts. This will decrease the water lost, cost, time, effort, and water related diseases, this will also guarantee better quality water. The project come on the list of PWAs' priority recommended Projects for Bethlehem and Hebron for 2018, hence AAA conducted several meetings with relevant stakholders, in September 2018, covered water supply and needs in 6 communities around zatara (Zatara, Beit Tamir, Ras al-Wad, Jeb al -Theb, Al A'randala,al Oqban) improve water supply and rehabilitation water networks. The project is a complementary step to the ongoing AAA WASH project in Zatara, is the ongoing project is decreasing water losses to around 50% in the two villages, which still consider high, this project will decrease the losses more and save money and efforts, and will improve living conditions for human and their animals in all communities in entire Zarara and Beit Tamir because the transition water pipeline will start before the first point on the Zatara and Beit Tamir water network, that means it will serve the people who live in area B &C. The people in area C are 5130 capita (Zatara: 3630, Beit Tamir: 1500) and 5719 capita in area B (Zatara: 4219, Beit Tamir: 1500).Activity: Install new main water carrier line between Zatara and Beit Tamir, with length 3900 meters.to serve and supply clean drinking water to Beit Tamir, Zatara and all the nearby communities through Janata and avoid the by-pass road. AAA will build a control station in Zatara to manage the water pressure in the line.Output 1: 10849 individuals will benefit from access to safe and affordable drinking water through pipeline in an entire the communities in the Zatara and Beit Tamir.Output 2: No water losses in the carrier line between the two communities.Output 3: Decrease water losses to 50% in Zatara and Bait Tamir water networks.Output 4: Individual daily water allocation will increase by 35% at least, this mean they will be close to the international human’s water need daily, which will help people enjoy water, and will have sufficient water for their livestock. Output 5: The income of the two communities councils will increase by 40000 NIS/ month, this mean less debt and better services for people.Output: continues and regular maintenance from the PWA as the line is passing Palestinian area, classified as area B.Output 6: No water physical contamination, thus high water pressure in the pipelines, so better water supply mainly to remote areas like Bidouns.Output 7: Zatara municipality, Beit Tamir and nearby communities will decrease their water bill, and will generate more profit as more water will reach up to the community and more money will come to the municipality/village council.

Indicators

Monitoring and evaluation:• A baseline survey will be conducted at the initiation of the project, which will be updated until the final evaluation. Information will be collected through semi structured interviews, household visits, and field observations. Regular monitoring will allow AAA to document the impact of the activities, document challenges and constraints on monthly basis, and suggest the right solutions. Attention will be paid to the quality of implementation and will ensure close liaison with PWA as well as zatara and Beit Tamir municipality and village council, community leaders, and local protections committees. AAA will consult prior to the implementation with the protection cluster and NRC to ensure legal advice, second legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen. Accountability will be ensured through ongoing follow up by AAA with field teams and beneficiaries.The expected indicators and targets of the project are: A total of 10849 direct (disaggregated by gender) beneficiaries will benefit from increased access to drinking clear water. Expenditure on drinking water will decreased by 25%. Reduced time and effort required by women and men by 50%. Reduced the wasted water in the pipeline to 50%. Decrease the community municipality water bill deficit by 20%. Gender aspects in the project:The identified indicators will be disaggregated by gender to ensure inclusion of women, and other groups as well as involve men, girls and girls at schools, youth, Bedouin, PwDs and elderly. The M&E system of AAA plus the complaint system will ensure the inputs of men and women and all others are equally registered and respected. This implies the input of Bedouin women will be highly valued and analyzed. Women and girls have considerable responsibilities for water bringing and use at home, as well as ensure adequate hygiene is maintained for children. The intervention will ensure this role is respected and enhanced by reducing the pressure on women in physical labor of bringing water on daily basis.The implemented activities will take into consideration the needs of diverse groups of women and girls such as the hygienic aspects that comes more essential with better availability of drinking water that is close to the community, and that bringing water will be of much less burden on women who usually do this responsibility of work. Reports gathered on women roles will be capitalized and used in AAA women empowerment as well as used to support local AAA initiatives on policy change towards marginalized areas. M&E Indicators will focus on 2 categories: 1 Women Health (women Access and Utilization of water, Disease and Prevention, Environmental Health, water and fertility, Health Care Management, Health Expenditures, Maternal and Infant Health, Mental Health and Risk Behaviors, Nutrition, water and violence against Women), 2 Economic Contribution (Market Participation, Resource Equity) related to water availability.
