Project Summary

Rationale:
The rationale of this area-based project is derived from experience gained from previous pilots and shelter projects, as well as evaluation of Shelter Sector interventions’ challenges and impact during the last years, concluding that
• Targeting individual housing units scattered across cities, reduces the desired impact of emergency response to enhance stability and social cohesion in areas,
• Restoration of services and infrastructure is a key factor in reviving the affected neighborhoods and improving stability,
• Community (and municipality) involvement in project identification and planning processes is a prominent element in emergency assistance, as it serves to mitigate possible tensions arising from perceived or real discrimination in selection processes. It is however essential to manage expectations of beneficiary populations, thus to boost positive impact of outcomes of delivered assistance,
Objectives:
Through the area-based approach, UN-Habitat aims to contribute in securing safe access for returnees to their destroyed homes by restoration of essential infrastructure and access to cadastral services to displaced-, host- and returnee- populations in selected neighborhoods in Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Homs, Rural Damascus and Aleppo. The holistic notion to serve entire conflict-affected, vulnerable communities through a comprehensive package of essential infrastructure and related services, under the objective to revive neighborhoods, is at the core of this project.
Project Components:
Based on the municipalities’ neighborhood action plans, developed through the municipality-led MTOS program (Municipal Technical Office Support) the project will cover the
• Connection of residential buildings to water- and sewage mains,
• Light rehabilitation of roads in the residential areas including sidewalk, street lighting, and repair the damaged Asphalt layers.
• Support to local cadastral departments to restore its functions and to provide services in regards of ownership and tenure documentation to all citizens.

Needs

Despite the reduction in the level of hostilities in certain parts of the country, notably through De-Escalation Agreements and some local agreements, along with the efforts of humanitarian actors and the Government of Syria over the past seven years, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain deep and far-reaching. An estimated of 6.2 million people remained internally displaced, and 4.7 million people are in need for shelter assistance. The shelter response required is of such scale that it goes beyond humanitarian response and the shelter sector members will not be able to meet all the shelter needs.On the other hand, a significant number of IDPs were able or would like to return to their communities of origin, increasing the need to restore basics services and repair infrastructure to move toward more sustainable solutions for those able to return. At the same time, a variety of Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues, e.g. destroyed premises of responsible institutions, lack of equipment, damaged, lost or incomplete records, absence of human resources, etc., present further challenges to the shelter and housing situation in Syria. On the legal and regulative side, repair and rehabilitation of housing is only pursued following confirmation of HLP- relevant documentary evidence, including ownership and lawful tenure in instances where repair and rehabilitation of partially damaged houses could positively contribute to alleviate dire needs for living space. Availability of documentation is a pervasive issue, therefore it is essential to restore processes and infrastructure to formalize or recuperate documents along with shelter response. Some 1 million displaced people have spontaneously returned to locations in Syria in 2018. It is expected that between 1.2 million to 1.5 million IDPs will return throughout 2019, in addition to returning refugees from mainly neighboring countries. Needs will be addressed through comprehensive area-specific programs comprising of activities in line with the Shelter Sector objective in regards of improving shelter-related community infrastructure. Implementation of activities

Implementation of the project will adopt a process sequence as follows: • Develop the Municipality’s action plan identifying the priority neighborhoods/areas of intervention through a stakeholder-inclusive consultative process (MTOS methodology).• Form a community committee to guide through project implementation.• Debris removal in target areas.• Connecting residential buildings to the main sewage, water and electricity networks.• Light rehabilitation of roads in the residential areas including sidewalk, street lighting, and repair the damaged Asphalt layers.• Support the Cadastral Department to restore basic cadastral services in the target areas
