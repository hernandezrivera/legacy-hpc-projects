Project Summary

Food insecurity in the Upper Nile region is driven by climatic shocks, insecurity, population displacements, crop deficits and limited access to basic services with the cumulative effects of years of asset depletion eroding households coping capacities and loss of livelihoods.
The area was already at IPC 4 levels of food insecurity, when in November fresh fighting erupted between armed forces in Fashoda County, resulting in close to 40,000 people being displaced. An inter-agency mission (OCHA and UNMISS) has estimated 21,000 people have gathered in Kodok and near the local UNMISS compound. The people managed to escape with livestock, however 2600 animals have recently died leaving them with no alternative source of food or income. The affected population, including the elderly, disabled and large numbers of unaccompanied children. It is estimated that 75% of the IDP’s are women and children. Newly displaced persons continue to arrive. They have no access to food or cash, are traumatized and have lost most of their assets. They come from an already compromised food security and nutrition position. The markets in the area are however operational and food items are available. A lack of sanitation facilities is resulting in widespread open defecation with women reportedly walking long distances to relieve themselves.
This project intends to complement a START funded emergency response, coordinated by CA and launched by multiple partners. (The project has recently concluded). CA and ADA provided cash assistance for food security and 3 agencies provided safe water (World Vision, Solidarite and Cord-Aid). This met the Sphere standards. Health care and SGBV services was provided by Mercy Corps, while UNICEF supported child protection and reunification of children with their families. Bearing in mind the resource level available under the HEF fund and in line with the needs assessment and community consultation the immediate priority needs are cash/food security and sanitation facilities.
Outcome 1: To address the food access crisis for the new arrivals, the project will provide multipurpose cash to 681HH (4,086people). Targeting mainly women, the elderly and child headed HHs. 20% of the cash assistance will target the host community who are equally vulnerable. This is to promote inclusion and apply conflict sensitivity to the project design. The cash value of £82 per household has been set by the National Cash Working Group.
Outcome 2: The project will provide 6 pit latrines of 5 holes using temporary materials. In this instance corrugated iron sheets will be used and plastic slabs. This design is proposed to ensure privacy and safety for users. Hygiene awareness will be conducted through the 15 hygiene promoters identified and trained from both the displaced population and host community. IEC materials will be developed to disseminate health and hygiene information, feedback mechanisms and safeguarding and accountability messages. This outcome is being proposed to prevent disease outbreaks and provide dignified sanitation facilities for the displaced population especially women and girls. These latrines will not meet Sphere standards of 1:20 but will compliment ongoing sanitation provision efforts while CA and other partners fundraise to scale up the response.

1. Needs

The WASH situation in this proposed County is high due to heavy flooding, which has caused further disruption to livelihoods, displacement, and damage to WASH infrastructure while limiting humanitarian access. Limited sustainability of the WASH response in the IDP sites due to funding shortage will increase the likelihood of water borne disease and outbreaks, and the area categorized under IPC 4 levels of food insecurity, this is further exacerbated by the influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan increasing the dare need for WASH.
