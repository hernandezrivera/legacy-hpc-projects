Project Summary

Arabouna village is one of Jenin villages which located close to green line. The total area of the village is 6.87 km2, around 93.9% of its land classified as area C, while the rest are classified as area B. The separation wall confiscated 11.8% of its land.
Araboneh supply and hygiene promotion project aims to improve the health situation of 1300 people in Araboneh region. The community in Araboneh are currently suffering from chronic health problems due to lack of access to clean and adequate water and hygiene education that in turn, resulted in low economic status. The project will address these essential needs which form the basis of all development through participatory, inclusive and effective means to bring about the following life-changing project purposes:
• The safe water supply coverage of the districts increased.
• Elapsed time to fetch water for rural women and girls reduced after project implementation,
• The project district population with access to and use of sanitation facilities increased.
Moreover, the implementation of this project will also contribute in achieving the millennium development goals (specifically to Ensure Environmental Sustainability, Reduce Child mortality, Achieve Universal / Primary education and Eradicate Extreme Poverty and Hunger) of the nation.
The project will have primary beneficiaries of 1300 people with access to safe and adequate water and hygiene education, of this figure around 500 are students that will benefit from the constructed water facilities in schools.
Union of Agriculture Work Committee will implement the project with the Village Council and the target beneficiaries.
The expected outputs from the project are summarized as follows.
Construction of transmission pipelines and simple water networks
• Construction 500 m3 R.C Elevated Water Tanks
• Construction network (1.5 km Transmission line 4" Diameter, 1 km 3" distribution line and 5.4 km 2" distribution line)
• Installation of Chlorination Plan

1 - Needs

A needs assessment conducted in May 2020 reveals that Araboneh community in the Jenin Governorate are in dire need of improved access to water services. The needs assessment reveals that, in this village, there is no water network available, and the families take the water by water tanker from many unsafe and unreliable sources. The magnitude of the problem is illustrated by an increase in water demand in the summer, where families cannot access water more than five times per month. Water is the primary source of life. Water is an essential condition for life, which is indispensable for development, ensuring nutrition, providing health and environmental services, Water sources at Araboneh are limited to rainwater collected in domestic wells as well as water tankers from unreliable water.The results of the survey carried out for a number of residents of the region, and the council showed the following. 1. High population growth.2. Diseases caused by the use of pool water and water tanks are considered the biggest pathogen among residents of the area because of polluted collection water and portable tanks that bring water from unreliable sources and because of the erosion of these tanks, which results in increased spending on treatment.3. The per capita range water supply in Araboneh from 15 - 20 litres per day while the minimum recommended by the World Health Organization is 100 litres per day.4. The cost of one Cubic meter of water increases up to NIS 15 at the peak period in summer.5. Lack of water in schools and public and health facilities in the village due to lack of water availability. • There is a shortage of drinking water in the target area in quantity and quality.• Lack of a mechanism to link the reliable and safe water source with the resident's area.• Spread of health diseases and damage caused by pollution and lack of drinking water.• Lack of healthy water in schools, clinics and medical centers.• Increasing the number of complaints submitted by citizens and calling for finding appropriate solutions to provide safe and clean water for them and their children.• High water costs.• Women do not organize their time due to fluctuating water availability. Project Objectives:• To provide clean and healthy water for drinking within the standards and specifications of the World Health Organization.• Link the water source with the beneficiaries to cover the water deficit and reduce the cost.• Contributing to reducing the spread of health diseases caused by pollution and water shortage.• Improving the overall health and environmental situation, especially in schools.• Contribute to the provision of clean water to help children progress in the educational process.• Contribute to the provision of clean water to patients in medical centers.• Participate and activate the local community, and improve the relationship between citizens and municipalities by solving their problems and their contribution to decision-making. The expected outputs from the project are summarized as follows.Construction of transmission pipelines and simple water networks• Construction 500 m3 R.C Elevated Water Tanks• Construction network (1.5 km Transmission line 4" Diameter, 1 km 3" distribution line and 5.4 km 2" distribution line)• Installation of Chlorination Plan Promoting good hygiene practices:• Awareness raising sessions for both men and women will be organized separately to promote good hygiene practices (focusing on hand washing with soap, self-cleaning, etc), saving and recycling of water will be provided to women and men at household and community level.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

UAWC adopts different mechanisms of stakeholders’ feedback. They are: verbal feedback to the project team in the field or over the phone, filling out the Complaint Cards, and finally through the UAWC field monitoring visits by UAWC MEAL Officer. The feedback is treated seriously and confidentially, and then taking the proper action, either preventive or corrective actions. The feedback may be received by direct beneficiaries, official institutions such as local municipalities, or from the out-sourced service providers such as contractors, or other stakeholders. By UAWC experience, the complaining parties contact the coordinator of the field team directly, either by face to face while being in the field or over the phone. In this case, the coordinator takes the right action and may inform/consult with the UAWC higher decision-making level if necessary. Even the stakeholders are informed about the possibility to share their feedback via Complain Card - CC or over the official phone of the institution; complaining parties use the CC in a few cases. On a regular monthly basis, and with attendance of the head of the UAWC Operational & Development Head of Department, MEAL Officer and Program & Project Section Head, UAWC Project Coordinators meet and discuss the project progress and issues including mainly the received complains. The main purpose of this monthly meeting is to share experience on how to react with all project issues and discussing the lessons learned and best practices. On the other hands, and to ensure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with the project deliverable and team (internal or external team), the MEAL officer conducts desk review of the project reports and field visits to meet with a random sample of direct beneficiaries as well as with official institution representatives. Before each field visit, the MEAL Officer customized the M&E form, considering the type of interventions, provided service/product and direct beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Based on Human Righ Approach (international human rights law and international humanitarian law), UAWC ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved. In this project, strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities are taken, particularly concerning designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, UAWC analyzes protection risks and possible consequences that may arise and finally are reported to the right concerned agencies.In the field, UAWC conducts regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are considered throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for vulnerable groups including mainly women, children. UAWC is highly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For that, UAWC staff are made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in UAWC’S Code of Conduct and related to other policies including Child Protection; and others. On the other hand and to avoid negative impacts and maximize the positive impact of the project interventions, UAWC adopts different principles to avoid any attempts of any individuals/groups/political parties to control and use aid resources to support their side. These principles are mainly the transparency, professionalism and considering some cross-cutting issues, mainly gender equality and giving priority for the most vulnerable groups, including small farmers, women-headed families, people with disability. To ensure Do-No-Harm, UAWC involves and consult with different stakeholders in the project design, beneficiary selection criteria, project implementation mechanisms. It should be noted that the UAWC Team is aware of Palestinian context, and interaction between the different intervention and this context, so that avoid negative impacts and maximize positive impacts on the conflict.the project ensure access and benefit form project activities for all ages and sex groups in the community, in the community the number of total females is 622 and 678 males, also 601 children and 650 adult and 49 elderly.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Since its establishment, Union of Agricultural Work Committees - UAWC has credible local agricultural committees in all mostly Palestinian locations, through them, the information on assistance are disseminated. To ensure all targeted beneficiaries, specifically the most vulnerable group such as small farmers, women-headed families, and people with disabilities, are fully informed of the assistance available under the project, UAWC field team ensures that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages. In general, UAWC adopts a different way of dissemination about the (Direct beneficiary groups, list of the beneficiary of high priority, and beneficiary selection criteria, etc..). These ways are mainly through UAWC local agricultural committees and their representatives in all Palestinian locations, using announcement board in the local official entities such as the local municipality, the cooperative, local CBO. For some intervention such as land rehabilitation or agricultural road, beneficiaries are requested to fill-out applications form that is studied with high transparency by a committee formed by the UAWC team and a representative from the local official entity and local agricultural committee focal point. To ensure equal and impartial access to aid, UAWC M&E Officer carry out a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process, and all applicants are notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process.the project ensure access and benefit form project activities for all ages and sex groups in the community, in the community the number of total females is 622 and 678 males, also 601 children and 650 adult and 49 elderly.
