Project Summary

UNHAS started operation in Cameroon in November 2015, following a request from the Humanitarian Coordinator as the provision of assistance by aid organizations was hampered by vast distances, insecurity and poor road infrastructure. In the years that followed, the security situation further deteriorated and made access to northern parts of the country, particularly the Far North extremely difficult and hardly reachable via roads and unreliable flight connections. The introduction of UNHAS flights has therefore been vital in enabling humanitarian organizations to scale up their response and reach more people in need. WFP-led aviation services being a key component for the whole humanitarian response in Cameroon, a country torn by three distinct geographical crises, connecting Yaoundé to two cities in the northern part of the country, all the way to N’djamena and beyond to other cities of Chad, therefore UNHAS route is critical for humanitarian actors to reach this remote affected part of the country
UNHAS aims:
1. To provide NGOs, UN agencies, donor organizations and diplomatic missions in Cameroon with safe, effective and efficient access to beneficiaries and project implementation sites;
2. To transport light cargo, such as medical supplies, information and communication technology (ICT) equipment
3. To provide adequate capacity for medical and security evacuations of humanitarian staff. UNHAS supported 100 % all requests, a total of 5 medical evacuations as of November 2019.
Target for 2020: 9,600 Passengers; Cargo (24 MT); 60 Organisations; 8 Locations served and 100% Medevac

Besoins / Needs

Far North: Poor road conditions and long distances delay the provision of humanitarian aid. In addition, insecurity in the northern parts of Cameroon makes road travel along some key routes dangerous, further constraining humanitarian access and service delivery. For example, it takes one day driving from the capital to the north location of Ngaoundéré, and to reach Garoua it would take two-days. Thus, UNHAS services remain essential to ensure a reliable link in-country between Cameroon's capital and locations in the Far North, North and Adamawa regions and the crisis- affected western part of the country, where the highly volatile security situation and state of roads prevent humanitarian actors to easily reached centers of assistance where regular “ghost-town” days and lock-downs impede movement of actors and ensuring continuity for the humanitarian response.The situation in Cameroon remains fragile and prone to extreme deterioration with little or no fore warning. In such an event, humanitarian actors may have to be temporarily moved to parts of the country with lower risk levels until appropriate or improvements observed. If this occurs, there is currently limited means to safely move staff quickly. The health infrastructure in both affected regions has limited capacity and UNHAS medical evacuation is therefore a crucial service to the humanitarian community. The deployment of UNHAS would thus enhance the ability to relocate or evacuate staff quickly and safely. Humanitarian actors have in the past complained of poor reliability and regular cancellations of the commercial airline, CAMAIR-CO, that operates in the same areas. This affects activities’ performance and ability to timely implement humanitarian and development Programmes in these locations. Results of access satisfaction survey conducted end of November 2018 early January 2019, indicated that UNHAS users rely solely on UNHAS services for air transport to reach populations in need of assistance. Therefore, UNHAS services will remain essential for the humanitarian community to reach beneficiaries and their project implementation sites in 2019.SWNW:In 2019, UNHAS successfully opened a new route, flying to the crisis- affected western part of the country, where the highly volatile security situation and state of roads prevent humanitarian actors to easily reached centers of assistance. The establishment of this new route, serving 3 airports from Yaoundé namely Bamenda, Tiko (Buea) and Mamfe would help increase the operationality of humanitarian assistance in the area and offers a safe option to easily flight out from the area where regular “ghost-town” days and lockdowns impede movement of actors. Following security incidents at one particular airport (bamebda) and dangerousness of flying there, UNHAS showed its flexibility and reactivity and proposed a potential new airport to be served close-by (Bafoussam), ensuring continuity for the humanitarian response.Since its inception, UNHAS transported 29,983 passengers, 83.53 MT of light cargo and served almost 60 organizations. In 2019 from January to November, UNHAS supported 60 organizations registered in Cameroon, transporting 9258 passengers and 22 MT of light cargo. Since the launch of the new routes, UNHAS has transported 341 passengers in SWNW. For 2019, the Global Humanitarian Overview (GHO) identified 4.3 million people in Cameroon in need of humanitarian assistance (a 30% increase compared to 2018), majority of whom being women and children. In order to support the planned humanitarian response and enable access to the populations in need and project implementation sites.
