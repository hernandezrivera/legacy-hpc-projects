Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different interventions at the individual and community-level. At the individual level, emergency responses will be delivered through distribution of in-kind food baskets and Ready to Eat Rations. Longer-term support will also be provided, delivering crop farmers a voucher to redeem seeds, fertilizers and tools and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. Furthermore, kitchen gardens and polytunnels will be distributed to enhance individuals' food security. With regards to the livestock activity, fodder-voucher and veterinary support will be provided. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate communal agricultural assets, including bakeries, mills, but also rehabilitate irrigation network. These interventions will benefit both farmers (who will have increased access to water for irrigation through network rehabilitation) and also communities (who will be able to access good quality bread through bakeries rehabilitation, for instance). All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, ACTED will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. Through training on kitchen gardens, or community greenhouses, or vocational and business training, for example, ACTED will help build to develop skills that can be used to improve their livelihood opportunities long after the project has ended. This project is estimated to benefit 53,148 individuals.

4 - Needs

As the Syrian crisis enters its ninth year, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are still food insecure, and an additional 2.5 million are at risk of food insecurity. The ongoing hostilities, both in NWS and NES, continue causing new waves of displacement, leaving a high number of individuals without access to food. In the aftermath of displacements, providing food baskets/vouchers and ready to eat rations become, therefore, essential to support newly displaced individuals in meeting their basic needs. In addition, across the country, high production costs, the lack of quality inputs and damaged or destroyed infrastructure have been identified as the main constraints to the recovery of the Syrian agricultural sector, who was once one the breadbaskets in the region. Indeed, prior to the crisis, the country had 144 grain-collection centres where the Government purchased grain from farmers at controlled prices but only 35 are estimated to be still functioning today. Additionally, production levels for staple crops have further been impacted by climate change and erratic weather, with Aleppo governorate being one of the five most affected seed-producing governorates by the 2018 droughts Livestock activities, a sector which once accounted for between 35 to 40% of the country’s total agricultural production, have also seen production levels drop drastically. Therefore, investing in agricultural businesses, in the rehabilitation of communal agricultural assets, and guaranteeing support to farmers and livestock holders, through the provision of inputs, is crucial to enhance Syrians resilience and early recovery. Lastly, as large influxes of IDPs have put strain on shared natural resources and livelihood opportunities in more stable areas, resulting in an increase in the food prices, improve households’ access to food, through the provision of home and kitchen garden and polytunnels, is fundamental to ensure increased food security at the household level.

5 - Implementation of activities

This intervention aims at covering immediate HHs’ food needs as well as strengthening the early recovery and enhance resilience.• Distribution of in-kind food basket and vouchers,Food basket: HHs with access to sufficient cooking facilities, but lacking food and access to the market in NWSFood vouchers: HHs with access to markets but no access to income• Distribution of Ready to Eat RationsTarget: extremely vulnerable HHs on a one-off basis to cover a HH’s food needs for 2 weeks, following new displacement in NWS.• Provision of conditional value vouchers to mid-size crop farmers (seeds, tools and fertilizers); and to livestock holders (fodder) in NWS who will receive veterinary support.Farmers vouchers selection criteria: up to 50 dunums of owned land, not receiving any agricultural assistance, having regular access to water for irrigation and usable agricultural land, experience in agricultural work, agriculture as the main source of income. Standard vulnerability criteria and risk factors will also apply.Fodder vouchers: No of vouchers received will be dependent on the No of livestock held by the farmer. Each voucher will cover the fodder for two months• Provision of home and kitchen garden kits and polytunnelsKGs, HGs, and polytunnels selection criteria: HH ability to benefit from the project and its willingness to take part in it activity, low household income, with priority given to households having one or less members generating an income, the household did not receive similar assistance in the past month, having regular access to water for irrigation.• Support to agricultural-based businesses Gaps identified by farmers, livestock holders and/or consumers in a value chain assessment conducted in NWS. Selection criteria: technical skills demonstrated through relevant professional experience or diploma needed to run the proposed agri-business.• Rehabilitation of communal agricultural assets and maintenance and rehabilitation of irrigation networks

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies.Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.
