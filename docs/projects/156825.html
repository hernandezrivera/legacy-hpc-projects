Project Summary

Takaful Al-Sham will aim to support the food security of the most vulnerable IDPs and part of the host community in Idleb and Aleppo governorate through three main
interventions:
First: providing vulnerable IDPs and HCs standard food baskets on monthly basis to meet their basic food needs, targeting 4975 HHs in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, where Takaful will either receive food baskets from the donor, or purchase them from the markets according to Takaful policies, only 1,975 HHs with secured fund until June 2019, so Takaful needs to sustain supporting those 1,975 HHs (not secured) to meet the minimum standard in providing food assistance to them which is eight rounds at least and appeals for 5000 HHs (not secured) that will get regular monthly food assistance for eight months in Al Bab and Afrin subdistricts. The distribution will be conducted in food distribution points (FDPs), safe FDPs will be chosen. For beneficiaries who have difficulties to reach distribution points such as HHs headed by child or disabled persons, the baskets will be transferred to their location. The HHs who live in camps will receive baked bread instead of flour bags.
Third: this intervention aims to increase and improve agricultural production through the application of crop rotation, and thus to promote food security in northern Syria through supporting 600 farmers HHs (males and females), where we will target male farmers and part of female farmers, where 3,600 individuals will benefit from this intervention (774 men – 878 women – 954 boys 994 girls), then distributing two types of agricultural baskets which are enough to plant 1 up to 2 hectares with different high quality seeds (each basket will be designed to meet the farmers need from the seeds, pesticides and fertilizer according to his\her land size), the seeds to be distributed is one of the following two packages (wheat) or legumes (lentil – broad beans – chickpeas – beans - …etc), the type of seeds to be distributed to the farmer depends on his/her previous season agriculture rotation, targeted sub-district was selected because they include large numbers of IDPs according to the last CCCM reports.
These 600 HHs who will be targeted with agriculture inputs will be from the same beneficiaries of regular food basket.
At the beginning of the project Takaful will conduct a meeting with local community to ensure their participation in the project, explain the project goals and take their
suggestions and notes to develop the project activities and implementation.
Beneficiaries will be selected among the most valuable IDPs and HCs (male and female). Takaful will coordinate with food security cluster and with other organizations who work in the same sector to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication. The project will ensure equal access for both genders with the priority for women, children headed household, disabled person, sick, PLW and elderly in addition the project staff will include female and male members trained on the protection principles and gender equality.

Needs

An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Compared to HNO 2017, there is 4 percent decrease in the proportion of People in Need at the national level. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. The main drivers of food insecurity in Syria are a set of interrelated factors related to the deteriorating economic environment as well as food insecurity stemming from massive displacements. Other key drivers include loss of livelihoods and reduced production capacity, limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation, reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations.The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficits of the most vulnerable population as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria. The most affected population who can be targeted by Takaful Alsham are the IDPs and returnees who are at a greater degree of vulnerability. By residence status from the assessed households in MSNA 2019 33.4 percent IDPs and 41.2 percent of returnees are food insecure. Additionally, 29 percent of returnees are adopting emergency coping strategy as compared to 18.7 percent reported across all groups at a national level.The households headed by Persons with Disabilities and the older people are at a greater degree of vulnerability due to the stress on their capacities to earn their living and afford their basic needs. Whereas the same source 600,000 farming households are suffering from the lack of agricultural inputs (cereals, vegetables, fertilizers, irrigation kits, etc.) to support their own production. Probability: Low Impact: High Mitigation strategies (MS): Our team will take in consideration IDPs tracking tables from CCCM and after that will cross-check the data from local relief oces in the targeted location in addition to site visits to samples of supposed. Assumption 2: The project will not cause a tension between IDPs and host community Risk: Tension between host community and IDPs due to lack of context/needs understanding or poor communication and/or tension between included and excluded IDPs might lead to conict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian organizations (MS): 1. Clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the results of the verification/selection exercise 2. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process Assumption 3: The access of all beneficiaries especially women and elders are secured.
