Project Summary

ADAPT Phase II/THRIVE Shelter activities will focus on providing the most appropriate shelter solution to meet the various needs of newly displaced at reception centers and/or at transition sites, existing IDPs in over-congested camps and the IDPs living within host communities, returnees and the host communities themselves. Mercy Corps will ensure to harmonize the assistance packages aligned to shelter CCCM sector guidelines in all the location of the intervention LGA. Extensive technical assistance will be an integral component to all the assistance packages that will ensure appropriate utilization of the assistance provided. HH level technical assistance certainly demands additional human resources, for this purpose Mercy Corps will mobilize skilled construction supervisors in Jere LGA. Similarly, unemployed youth with relevant education or skills (engineering, vocational, experience) from the locality will be identified to provide necessary training and aboard them as daily workers. Mercy Corps will ensure to harmonize the assistance packages aligned to shelter CCCM sector guidelines in these locations. Assistance packages will include a) A customized new arrival kit comprising basic emergency shelter materials and S&S NFIs for 325HHs. Materials will include, tarpaulin, timber poles, rope, nails and fixtures) paired with household utensils such as bedding, blankets, floor mat, mosquito net, kitchen set, jerry cans, buckets, basin, solar torch). A communal tool kit – shared by up to five households – will also be provided, including necessary items like shovels, claw hammers, and saws. b) A tailor-made NFIs replenishment assistance for 325 HHs c) Mercy Corps will also provide tailor-made assistance for vulnerable households in camp settings to access the needed shelter materials such as timber poles, tarpaulin sheets, rope, and nails to maintain their deteriorated shelters. d) To assist existing IDPs and returnees living in host communities, a shelter repair package will be provided to 700 HHs (IDPs, returnees, and host community) living in poor sheltering conditions (i.e. unfinished, abandoned, or damaged buildings). 1 - Needs

The Shelter CCCM sector for HRP 2021 aimed to target 1,587,664 people in need, among them 66% are IDP, 1% are returnees and 33% are host families. For the HRP2020 funding target, the Financial Tracking System (FTS) shows that the emergency shelter & NFIs sector is significantly underfunded that will lead to subsequent deterioration in the shelter situation of the people already living in poor conditions. As of November 2020, only 4% funding has been received against 72M USD the total funding target for the emergency shelter & NFIs sector. Humanitarian needs in Borno state remain high and concerning as 388,353 households are in need of shelter, while another 229,694 households are in need of NFI assistance. Further scale up urgently needed given the increasing multiple displacements prompted by the deteriorating security environment and other socioeconomic factors for new and existing displaced people. Most in need among them are those who are sleeping in the open, without shelter, living in extremely damaged shelters or sharing shelters with other households resulting in over-congestion that leads to potential SGBV, and protection risks and also expose to COVID-19 outbreak. There is an urgent need to construct more shelters to accommodate 305,560 IDPs who are sleeping outside or sharing spaces in Dikwa, Bama, Konduga, Monguno, Ngala, Gwoza and Damboa LGAs. Shelter/NFIs needs are continuously generated by forced displacement due to continuously deteriorating security situation, heavy rains and poor living conditions in the North-East Nigeria. Shelter CCCM sector reports that the majority of the affected populations in need are Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps, IDPs in host community and returnees who are living in makeshift shelters or shelters that are either partially or completely damaged and affected by heavy rains and waterlogging in the garrison towns. Most of the IDPs with no access to shelters has left no option other than sleeping in open and constrained to use inadequate materials to construct provisional shelters. These HHs also have limited access to NFIs and fully rely on humanitarian assistance to survive. LGA level damage assessment has been carried out in the camp settings across Brono state by the CCCM partners (DTM round 38) to assess shelters repairs needs, results show that Gwoza LGA remains among the most in need of decongestion and shelter maintenence/up-gradation assistance, with 16,984 are sharing shelters, while 4,749 HHs are living in makeshift shelters. In Monguno LGA alone there are 11,626 HHs in need of urgent assistance sharing shelters, in Ngala, 2,240 HHs, Konduga LGA, 8,979 HHs, Damboa, 12,931 HHs, Dikwa 3,083 HHs and 6,612 HHs in Bama. Damasak witnessed an increase in the influx of returnees resulting in the congestion of the temporary camps where over 4,749HH reside in makeshift shelters while over 1,468 HH remain without shelter, hence sleeping in the open. In the camps, sporadic fire incidents since January 2020 to date has destroyed more shelters across camps in Muna Garage, Ngala, Mafa and Monguno

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Gender and protection mainstreaming will remain at the heart of the shelter &settlement assistance. During the targeting, priority will be given to families shelteringseparated/unaccompanied children (below 18 years old), household headed by awoman - priority to be given to widows who lost their husband due to recent conflict,households headed by children (below 18 years old) . To help improve the privacy andsafety of the women and girls, lockable doors and windows will be incorporated in thedesign of the shelters. The scope of the repairs will consider the safety of the children,accessibility needs of the people with special needs and privacy and safety of thewomen and girls. Female headed HHs will be provided with the fuel-efficient stovesthat will minimize the SGBV risks associated with fetching firewood from the bush.Privacy screens will be provided to overcrowded families that will also minimize therisk of SGBV. Elderly, women and children headed HHs will be prioritized throughoutand receive additional unskilled labor assistance when others are supposed tocontribute. Furthermore, field supervisors, daily hire workers and the field staff will betrained on DNH principles. Mercy Corps shelter interventions indeed focus on ensuringthe privacy, security, and dignity of all household members, however, an additionalcaseload for the families has access to a liveable shelter yet lacks privacy for womenand girls. These partitions will also help in COVID-19 pandemic context forovercrowded families.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As described above, Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community ReliefCommittees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, withrepresentatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups,including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard andaddressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members,helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter andservices. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) willbe continued in the ADAPT and THRIVE project to enable a safe and effectivefeedback/complaint system for bene\0ciaries and non-bene\0ciaries. The CARMincludes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps inGombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate bene\0ciaries) to givefeedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered throughdesignated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by thefocal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of allcomplaints within a speci\0ed timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also beused to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under theProtection-sector speci\0c programming described below; that information will beused to reduce existing and newly identi\0ed risks. The program will monitor thesecurity situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situationdeteriorates
