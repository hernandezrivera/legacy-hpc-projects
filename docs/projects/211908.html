Project Summary

Now in its fourteenth year, the conflict in northeast Nigeria continues to disrupt the lives and livelihoods of millions of people across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. Violent attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and military counter-operations have displaced men, women, and children, disrupted farming and other livelihoods, and limited the functionality of markets and other basic services.
The UNICEF Child Protection project will continue to focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive child protection services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers in the conflict affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). The emergency response will prioritize the most vulnerable including at-risk children and their families. UNICEF’s efforts to address child protection and gender-based violence in emergencies is part of its over-all strategy on prevention and response to all forms of violence against children (VAC) through adopting a nexus approach that addresses the urgent protection concerns facing children affected by crises; reinforce existing local and national capacities and systems for children’s protection; transitional justice and promoting peaceful coexistence including a focus on climate change mitigation where possible.
The present project module is part of ongoing efforts to provide a comprehensive, multi-year child protection and gender-based violence programme that aims to support the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict affected children and caregivers, quality case management services for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and survivors GBV violence and community-based reintegration of children and adolescents formerly associated with Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) within their communities. Girls, boys and women survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), including conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) will also have access to safe spaces and benefit from case management, information and referral systems to specialized services.
In addition, project actions will further focus on child protection system strengthening through the roll out of the child protection law in the BAY states, registering under-5 child births, and enhancing the capacity of a professional social service workforce to improve the quality, coordination and sustaining the response. The UNICEF child protection response will be integrated with the work of other sectors, such as education, WASH, health and nutrition, for increased and comprehensive delivery of services to conflict affected children and caregivers.
Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children including family tracing and reunification for UASC; delivering quality and sustainable socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with armed groups as well as other vulnerable children and their families. In addition, conflict-affected children will have access to community-based mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) including structured recreational activities and adolescents’ life skills education. The project will also contribute to strengthening capacity of service delivery to GBV survivors. Child and adult survivors of GBV will in addition have access to comprehensive minimum lifesaving services including medical, psychosocial, legal and safety. Children and caregivers living in or returning to locations contaminated by unexploded remnant of war (UXO) will be provided with explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) in schools, child friendly spaces, transit and displace camps s with the support of Mine Action sub sector. Grave violations against children affected by conflict will be monitored and reporting through the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM).

1 - Needs

 According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2.13 million people (59% children, 22% women) need child protection. Child protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military operations and the high influx of people, inflation, high poverty rates and the relocation of IDPs. In 2022, the United Nations verified 524 grave violations against 307 children (135 boys, 172 girls) in north-east Nigeria. This is over twice the cases verified in 2021. Most violations of recruitment and use by Boko Haram affiliated or splinter groups, the Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). Armed groups and security forces both continue to commit crimes under international humanitarian law and international human rights law and have a severe impact on the protection of children. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence and forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. In 2022, about 2,200 cases of GBV survivors were supported through case management in the north-east. Forced displacement and returns impose high risk on separated and unaccompanied children. Boys and adolescent male risk forcible recruitment by armed groups, or suspicion of association with armed groups by authorities. Civilians continue to suffer death and injury from explosive ordnance, including the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Most children have been exposed to one or more of the following: violence, loss of friends, family members and neighbours, accumulated stress, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, increased military presence, divisions in societies, and lack of access to services. They can also hamper their ability to access other services, such as education, further damaging their life chances.  As of 31 December 2023, a total of 3,083 individuals (55% women and children) were living in three transit camps (Hajj, Shokari and Bulumkutu) in Maiduguri. Sustaining this momentum of exits presents challenges to effective process of handling and treatment of the individuals led by government authorities and ensuring their reintegration into communities amidst an ongoing conflict, an internal displacement situation.  The effective reintegration of children is hindered by the impact of armed conflict on societies, such as the absence of livelihood opportunities, weak economies and markets, and a breakdown of social infrastructure, including schools and health facilities.  Community resilience to protect children has been weakened, resulting in children and families adopting negative coping mechanisms.  According to UNMAS, incidents and EORE activities report, there was a 69% increase in children’s casualties in 2023 (49) when compared to 2022 (29). Bama (49), Damboa (47) and Ngala (41) all LGAs in Borno State, account for the highest number of casualties.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

UNICEF will engage with the target population at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, in particular vulnerable and marginalized groups, women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities, old age, women headed HH, child headed HH and pregnant, lactating and mothers married girl children. All implementing partner staff, will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working in the program. This is to both help prevent GBV and PSEA but also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities. UNICEF and its partners will establish safe and accountable GBV reporting and feedback mechanism in their targeted communities. UNICEF and its partner will collaborate with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, displaced populations, and children at risk. The proposed Programme is focused on mainstreaming protection. UNICEF will deploy the principle of “Do no harm” and employ appropriate measures to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of project actions and ensure that the environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people to further hazards, violence or human rights abuses or violations. UNICEF/partner will manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized, UNICEF/partner will ensure that the affected population have access to humanitarian assistance against any form of direct or indirect discrimination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF has established several safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms in targeted communities to report against PSEA and other violations. UNICEF also uses its short text messages (sms) based U-Report tool to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback or complaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports, and feedback from communities, analyzes the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and its partners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to act. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
