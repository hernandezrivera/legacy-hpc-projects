Project Summary

The project aims at Increasing protection of children, especially Unaccompanied Minors, Separated, orphaned, abused/neglected children in Borno state.
UNHCR intervention will strengthen the child protection environment by; -
1. Establishing and strengthening community-based child protection systems/structures.
2. Promoting participation and psychosocial wellbeing of children and their caregivers.
3. Promoting advocacy and capacity of duty bearers to prevent and respond to abuse, exploitation, and Violence against Children.
4. Providing support to the reintegration process for returning children and their caregivers

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the ongoing crisis in 2009, more than 2.1 million men, women and children have been forced to flee their homes in North-East Nigeria. Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States account for 2 million of these internally displaced people (IDPs), 55 % of whom are children. Majority of displaced children are in Borno state, the epicentre of 12-year-old Boko haram Insurgency. The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States has been marked by mass human rights violations, brutal attacks, abduction, rampant sexual and gender-based violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, family separation, disappearances, and forced displacement. Children and their caregivers face heightened protection risks which has resulted to increase in female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys, and girls among other vulnerable populations. Recovery efforts are largely hampered by continued cycle of displacement induced by incessant NSAG attacks and clearance operations by the military. There is lack of basic infrastructures, limited availability of basic services, and limited presence of the relevant national actors and key humanitarian partners in some locations, all of which exacerbate protection risks for the most vulnerable populations (especially children and women). The community based- child protection systems are weak or broken and the capacity of duty bearers to respond to child protection concerns is weak, thus complicating the process of recovery. Limited access to education, dilapidated education infrastructure, abductions, lack of basic needs (among other needs) discourages affected children from enrolling or staying in school. High numbers of unaccompanied and separated children, orphans and child-headed households have critical protection needs and many children have had to resort to hawking/begging as means of survival. There has been an increase of instances of attacks, harassment, abduction and GBV when women and adolescent girls venture outside sites' security perimeter to search for firewood and food. There is increased vulnerability to sexual violence, sale, and exchange of sex for food or money and forced/early marriages for adolescent girls due to pre-dominant gender inequality, culture and religious norms in the north east, lack of sanitary materials and basic necessities. The challenges for reintegration facing boys who were recruited and used by Boko Haram and currently surrendering, are critical. The absence of civil authorities in newly accessible areas and civil authorities’ functions undertaken by military has undermined the humanitarian and civilian character of IDP sites. There is a critical need to continue to scale up efforts to prevent, refer and respond to cases with identified child protection risks with effective child protection interventions. Strengthened engagement, collaboration, and coordination with all stakeholders to find durable solutions for children is highly required

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles:• Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need.• To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls.• If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks.• In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR will strengthen community-based feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanism, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of child protection-based materials. UNHCR and child protection partner will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, child safeguarding and child protection policy. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through Participatory Approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict and insurgency
