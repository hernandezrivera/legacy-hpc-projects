Project Summary

The proposed project, which is a basically a Protection project will be base in both Formal (primary schools) and non0formal education (TLSs)with the objective: to improve conflict-affected children’s learning outcomes by improving their access to safe, quality, protective, gender-friendly, inclusive learning spaces will be implemented in Gwoza and Jere LGAs. The project will contribute to the Education Sector’s outcome area: Conflict affected children and adolescents have improved access to inclusive quality basic education within a safe learning environment; and with the GBV AoR SO3: Strengthened community resilience and systems/institutions that prevent and respond to gender-based violence including harmful practices. The project is based on the recognition of the key role that education can and plays in the protection of the children. Education is a powerful tool to prevent VAC including GBV by promoting gender equality, challenging harmful gender norms and providing child friendly spaces for children and adolescents/youth. In addition, schools can serve as a platform for raising awareness about protection concerns/GBV, providing support for children who are survivors or at-risk and building community resilience. The project will support 5,000 (3000 girls; 2,000 boys)(2,000 NFE; 3,000 FE) conflict-affected children to access protective, gender-friendly education through a holistic approach that empowers the individual child girls whilst also working with teachers and other community education structures to build safer, more inclusive learning spaces in the target LGAs towards zero tolerance to GBV in the schools and TLSs.
Within the component of improving access to quality, inclusive education, the project will carry out these key activities: leverage on existing schools and TLSs, renovating them to create an environment that is gender and disability friendly. including toilets disaggregated by sex and ramps. The project will work with Community Education Committee, which together with the School Based Management Committee (SBMCs), shall be trained on school management, advocacy, development and oversight functions of the school administrators and to ensure a GBV zero tolerant schools. To mitigate menstruation-related absenteesm, girls shall be provided with dignity kits including menstrual pads to improve retention and learning outcome for girls.
The project shall mainstream VAC/GBV prevention and response into the education systems through the following key activities: Training of 80 teachers and 40 VTs on girls rights, SGBV prevention, response, safe guarding codes of conduct , to ensure teachers are equipped with safeguarding and essential child protection skills that enable them to recognize and respond to distress, and refer children in need to appropriate service providers; Train and resource 4 SBMCs and 4 CECs/CPCs to increase knowledge of girls’ rights and develop clear safeguarding policies for teachers and VTs respectively; establishment of CFS to provide ongoing psychosocial support and facilitate access to comprehensive GBV case management for cases identified through the CFS, Child Help Desks, CEC and CPC members; awareness raising on GBV forms, consequences and activism among children through establishment of anti-GBV clubs and activities (including life skills training, SRH, GBV, Advocacy, etc) supervised by trained Club mentors; EMAP/Boys and Girl Shine Session for Girls and EMAP for Men and Women’s Sessions to change beliefs, negative social norms to become agents of change in GBV prevention and response in the schools and communities.

1 - Needs

The Nigeria HNO and HRP (2023) estimated that 8.3m people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023. Education which is a fundamental human rights has been greatly affected by the crisis; with over 1,400 schools have been destroyed and 2,295 teachers killed leaving 1.1m children in need of education in 2023. According to the JENA report (2021), half the schools – mainly in Borno (61 per cent) – have been partially destroyed or rendered inoperable by conflict and lack of investment in rehabilitation limiting the number of intakes and overstretching those that are functional with an average class population of 124 students per classroom. Also, only 46 per cent of schools have access to adequate safe drinking water. Most schools have no functioning handwashing facilities, water or soap. One latrine serves 264 students, on average; only 6 per cent of schools in the BAY states have adapted their premises to improve access for children with physical disabilities; only 60 per cent of teachers had some sort of qualification (JENA Report 2021). The risks of GBV faced by women and girls in the BAY states are expected to increase in 2024 due to ongoing and protracted conflict, climate change induced events such as floods, seasonal fire outbreaks within IDP camps, camp closures, cholera outbreaks and influx of refugees from Cameroon and Niger. 0.8m children are in need of GBV response assistance. Key GBV needs include strengthening of identification, reporting, and response mechanisms especially in educational institutions. Needs include PSS increased access to mental health and psychosocial support services, psychosocial first aid, inadequate services, paucity of knowledge on Child rights, reporting channels and referral pathways. In the schools, there is lack of knowledge and skills on the part of teachers, school administrators, SBMC and other education support structures to carry out oversight functions in the schools and TLSs as well as that of child protection, GBV prevention and response in the schools to identify, report, and refer cases in the school. Most projects have either targeted education needs of protection needs. This project is innovative as instead of submitting two projects; one on education and one on protection, this integrates the protection into education for greater synergy of outcome in the recognition that protection responses such as mental health, PSS, capacity for children to report are keys to students and teachers ensuring a conducive learning environment.

2 - Activities

The activities are highlighted under two key output:Output 1: Creating a conducive environment in the education the formal and NFS for successful integration of GBV prevention and response. The activities under this output include the following:Activity 1: Rehabilitate/Renovation of 4 target TLSs and 4 primary schools. make it both gender- and disability-friendly including toilets disaggregated by sex.Activity 2: Training 4 SBMCs and 4 CECs/CPCs on school management, advocacy, development and oversight functions of the school administrators and to ensure a GBV zero tolerant schools..Activity 3: Support of adolescent girls with dignity kits including menstrual pads to reduce menstruation related absentism and to improve attendance and learning outcomes for girls Output 2: Violence against children/GBV prevention and response integrated into activities of formal and non-formal schools.The activities here include:Activity 1: Training of 80 teachers and 40 VTs on girls rights, SGBV prevention, response, safe guarding codes of conduct, to ensure teachers are equipped with safeguarding and essential child protection skills that enable them to recognize and respond to distress, and refer children in need to appropriate service providersActivity 2: Training of 80 teachers and 40 VTs on case management in the schoolsActivity 3: Training 4 SBMCs and 4 CECs/CPCs to increase knowledge of girls’ rights and develop clear safeguarding policies and code of conducts for teachers and VTs respectively Activity 4: Establishment of Child Help Desk in the school to identify, counsel and publicise reporting channels and referrals. The child help desk shall encourage and support survivors/victims to report cases of abuseActivity 5: Establishment of CFS to provide ongoing psychosocial support and facilitate access to comprehensive GBV case management for cases identified through the CFS, Child Help Desks, CEC and CPC membersActivity 6: Awareness raising on GBV, forms, consequences and activism among children through during school assembly, use of IEC/BCC materials and animated video clipsActivity 7: Formation of anti-GBV clubs and activities (including life skills training, SRH, GBV, Advocacy, etc) supervised by trained Club mentors;Activity 8: Training of EMAP officers, supervisors and facilitators for EMAP/Boys and Girl Shine Session for Girls in target schools and EMAP for men and Women Sessions to change beliefs, negative social norms to become agents of change in GBV prevention and response in the schools and communities.Activity 9: Support for EMAP sessions (27 sessions) and Girls Shine Sessions (12)Activity 10: Support for Bi-Weekly activities of Boys and Girls clubsActivity 11: Support for monthly activities of SBMC/CEC/CPCActivity 12: Community outreaches by CPC members
