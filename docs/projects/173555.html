Project Summary

The intervention intends to support school age boys and girls gain access to timely, safe, protective quality education through addressing multiple barriers that otherwise hinder their access to education. This will be done through improving school infrastructure; both classroom and WASH facilities – by rehabilitation and/or construction of new facilities on a needs basis, provision of appropriate teaching and learning materials to students, teachers and schools. The intervention will also support teachers’ and schools’ capacity development in provision of psychosocial support (PSS) and implementation of COVID-19 protocols for safe re-opening of schools. In addition, the intervention will support capacity building and the work of community-based education support structures like PTAs through development and implementation of School Improvement Plans (SIPs). The intervention targets school age boys and girls from IDP, returnee and vulnerable host communities for this support, including the out of school children (OOSC) and those who have dropped out.

1 - Needs

Majority of the schools in target areas have dilapidated structures owing to lack of regular maintenance over the years, while others have been destroyed by environmental causes such as floods. The displacement situation in some areas has increased pressure on schools in communities hosting displaced populations exponentially increasing student numbers in schools that are already overcrowded and struggling with abnormally high teacher-student ratios. The prevailing economic hardships- sky rocketing inflation, currency devaluation and general loss/lack of livelihood sources has made it hard for families to afford costs associated to education of their children such as scholastic materials, uniforms among others. There is need to improve structural integrity of school buildings to address safety, increase classroom space to accommodate enrolment of more students. There is also need to support schools, teachers and students with their various specific teaching/learning supplies and capacity development to ensure improved access to safe, quality education for all children including those out of school and those that had dropped out.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Rehabilitation/ construction of school infrastructure: NRC engineers will jointly work with MoE technical teams to carry out technical assessment of school buildings (including WASH facilities) to ascertain their structural integrity, sufficiency and agree on the upgrades/works needed to either restore or ensure sufficiency. In some cases, additional classrooms or temporary learning spaces (TLSs) will be established to provide more classroom space, if deemed necessary. Gender segregated latrines will be a key focus in this activity to address the protection concerns (GBV) as well as provide the culturally required privacy for students per gender. Implementation of the works will be through the School Improvement Plan (SIP) modality led by the PTAs while MoE and NRC technical teams supervise for quality assurance and compliance to agreed upon standards. School supplies: School level assessments will be carried out to find out the student and teacher population of each targeted school, the specific school supplies needed for students, teachers and the school in general for better performance. The materials will include scholastic materials, school uniforms, furniture, hygiene and COVID-19 prevention materials (PPE), dignity kits for teenage girls (menstrual health management) as well as recreational materials. NRC will procure the materials and deliver them directly to the beneficiaries, with MoE’s participation.Capacity Building: Key stakeholders in the school system such as teachers in the targeted schools will be trained in various relevant packages such as Teacher Preparedness and Training Package (TPTP), Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Safeguarding, Psychosocial support (PSS) among others as will be guided by the Education Sector leadership and MoE. A keen focus will be put on increasing awareness about COVID-19 prevention and general child protection perspectives in education. PTAs will also be supported to develop and implement School Improvement Plans (SIPs) for their schools, to increase their active participation and ownership in the schools in their communitiesTeacher incentive payment: to address the issues of teacher scarcity, abnormally high teacher-student ratios in some schools with high populations, additional qualified teachers will be jointly selected with MoE from the community and engaged to support in teaching the additional classrooms established. A monthly incentive will be paid to these teachers to facilitate them in their work. The rate will be guided by the Education Sector approved standards for the same.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection mainstreaming is at the core of the intervention; right from establishment of gender segregated latrines in schools to provide the gender-required privacy of both boys and girls and to prevent SGBV in schools, to improvement of structural integrity of school buildings to ensure safety, construction of more classrooms and handwashing stations in schools to reduce over crowding and support better hand hygiene especially in light of COVID-19 prevention measures in school to training teachers on PSEA, child safe guarding and PSS.
