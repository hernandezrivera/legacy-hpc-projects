Project Summary

During clashes between SAF and RSF which started in April 2023 , more than 3000 where killed in Khartoum including infants and children and 17000 people injured need humanitarian assistance, among which 14M need emergency assistance. Moreover, a high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). Of these, around 5.5 million people are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), while about 1.8 million are critically food insecure classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An increase in localised conflicts triggered population displacement, which, combined with the deterioration of the economy, led to higher than usual levels of acute food insecurity. As a result, the highest prevalence of population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse are observed in several states, including Central Darfur state ranging from 17-18%. both sides of the conflict (SAF and RSF) began recruting children to engage in military operations ,
According to Sudan 2023 HNO, The United Nations verified 292 grave violations against 274 children (143 boys, 131 girls); including killing, maiming, sexual violence, recruitment and use by armed groups and forces, abduction, and denial of humanitarian assistance mostly in Khartoum North. The economic crisis and increasing deprivation continue to fuel harmful coping mechanisms, particularly among adolescent girls and boys. This highly affects adolescent boys to be more likely to be killed and injured, detained, recruited, or exploited, while adolescent are particularly at risk of child military conscription, harassment, exploitation, and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. with no access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services for both girls and boys.
Khartoum State (7 Localities) has one of the highest range of protection insecure situation among most populations in the refugees and the urban poorest most affected population exacerbated by the political situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, economic deterioration which is not expected to improve and the most vulnerable will not be able to meet their basic needs,
BPWO designed this project aligned to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2023 under emergency for six months , Strategic Objective 3 (Mitigate protection risks and respond to protection needs through humanitarian action). This project also aligned with the child protection activities targeting children and adolescents among them IDPs, and vulnerable children from the host communities. All activities aim to enhance care and protective environment for children through strengthening community-based protection safety nets; provision of direct individual case management support to the most vulnerable children, strengthening of community‐based child protection mechanisms and referral structures, provision of psychosocial and mental health services for children through MPC and mobile CFS targeting both children and caregivers, as well as strengthening the response mechanisms through linkages between formal and informal structures to work together to improve the system and establishment of new temporary care homes away from clashes zones.
This intervention contributes to cluster objective 1 and 2 and contribute to humanitarian response plan objective 3. Moreover; BPWOI will coordinate with all different INGOs, NGOs, relevant government ministries and local authorities in Khartoum State , this will be through ensuring information sharing based on need, the aim is ensured relevant and related actors are informed and maximize the use of resources and sharing of best practices.
The project targeted 6000 children in four localities:
-Establishment of 4 child-friendly inclusive Multipurpose Community Centers (MPC) targeting a total of 4,590 children (2,745 girls, 1,845 boys) and 1 mobile Child-Friendly Space targeting a total of 2,700 children specially unsupported in care homes who were been behind while most of Khartoum residents were flee from war.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this intervention, BPWO is committed safeguard Right holders from violence and discrimination and is aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals. BPWO will implement the project in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance during assessment, design of project and delivery of services. The project team will ensure all elements of accountability to affected population to be captured through extensive engagement and consultation with the community. Project is designed to inform all displaced about the availability and location of services. The project ensures safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory provision of services. The intervention will also carefully apply Do No Harm approach to avoid any negative impact that it might bring to the area or the targeted communities. Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. The assistance and protection measures will not undermine local capacities for self-protection as well as support the efforts of the affected population and local communities to find security and restore dignity. Project intends to respond to the root causes of the protection concerns in the area. To achieve this, BPWO embraces community based protection approach, for building capacity of local communities, service providers and government officials. Their capacity will be enhanced in analyzing & finding solutions to protection problems, inclusive of reducing threats& risks, reducing vulnerability of affected population and increasing their capacity to cope with protection problems following exposure to shock and disaster.
