Project Summary

CEDS proposes to provide education interventions targeting 1500 conflict, flood and drought affected children aged 3-17 years in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap states to ensure they have access to adequate, basic quality education. The project will also target to reach 304 teachers, 178 parent teacher associations (PTAs) and school management committees (SMC), parents, community members and government education representatives at state, county and payam level. To achieve this, CEDS will construct and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces in schools, organize and facilitate back to learning campaigns targeting school aged children, parents and community members, distribute education materials to schools, train Parent and Teacher Association members (PTA) and School Management Committee (SMC) members on school management, basic maintenance of school infrastructure, PSS and disaster risk reduction, strengthen capacity of local education representatives and school leadership to collect data, monitor and report school admissions, absenteeism and drop outs, train teachers on psychosocial support (PSS), child protection and referral pathways and also on Education in Emergencies (EiEs) and learner centered approach. CEDS will also distribute hygiene and sanitation materials to school going girls of reproductive age and install hand washing stations in selected schools to promote handwashing practices as a preventive means of spread of Covid-19. CEDS will further construct and rehabilitate gender-sensitive WASH facilities to ensure access to safe clean and dignified sanitation facilities and safe drinking water. To ensure that beneficiaries are informed about the project, CEDS will carry out information sharing sessions about the project, PSEA and safeguarding as well as the formal and informal complaints and feedback channels in place (CFM) through the school and student leadership and will also use this platform to pass on Covid-19 preventive messages. CEDS will also purpose to make sure that voices of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups are heard, and their feedback collected and used to shape project implementation. Lastly, CEDS will put in place concrete mitigation measures to ensure that the project does not cause harm to the beneficiaries and community members. As a result of this project, 1500 children aged 3-17 years will have adequate access to conducive learning environment and learning materials, teachers and stakeholder capacity will be built to provide quality education and the out of school children will be reached and supported to start or resume their education.
CEDS presently facilitates the storage, management and distribution of the education materials for the entire Northern Bahr el Ghazal state on behalf of UNICEF and will continue to do so in the course of this project.

1 - Needs

n 2023, 3.3 million people, a larger proportion being children are estimated to need education services in South Sudan (HNO,2023). The number of OoSC children in the age group 3–17 years old in 2020 was 2.8 million, of which 53% are girls; The situation remains the same and is anticipated to worsen in 2023. Dropout rates continue to remain at an all level high with at least 1 in 5 of enrolled children dropping out of school (24% girls compared to 21% boys) (MoGEI,2020). In general, acquisition of quality education in South Sudan is thwarted by several barriers ranging from systemic, cultural and structural challenges. Frequent floods, conflict and poor maintenance has significantly contributed to destruction of school structures. The recent IRNA (Aug-Sept,2022) findings indicate that operations in 855 schools in 9 states were affected. 4-Due to conflict, 384 flooding and 467 damaged. A total of 391 schools in Warrap and NBG were impacted. Moreover, the South Sudan HNO 2023 Education Severity Mapping categorized Warrap Tonj North and Twic counties at severity level 5 and NBG Aweil East, Aweil West, counties at severity level 3. Overall increased sectoral needs have direct or indirect negative impact to learner access to education. Majority of the operational schools are sparsely located forcing learners to move long distances to access education or have non-user-friendly latrines for CwD. This exposes children to GBV risk and contributes to absenteeism among girls and CwD. The lack of adequate, safe and clean WASH facilities and MHM also present a challenge to girl’s accessing education. Another limitation is inadequate education materials. Learners in most instances share one textbook in groups of not less than 4 learners (Education Annual census,2018). During conflict, most schools are utilized to house armed forces or in cases of floods used as temporary shelter for IDPs thus affecting school operations. Even though education in South Sudan is free in public schools, the costs associated with learning such as school contributions and examination fees lock out some of the learners from accessing education (ENA,2021). Most vulnerable households live below the poverty index and the little income obtained is prioritized for food supplies or, as a means of obtaining income, marry off young girls -halting their education. The community culture also gives more recognition to role of girls as taking care of the siblings or doing household chores while boys graze livestock and this, together with community limited perception on the value education causes most parents and caregivers to overlook their children’s education. Girls exposed to SGBV, who fall pregnant-usually due to shame, stigma and societal pressure leave school to care for infants. Teacher presence in schools still remains low with most schools continuing to be understaffed. Teacher incentives are neither paid on time nor in full and this results in high teacher turn over. This puts a strain in the available human resource and is common to find the teacher to learner ratio way above the recommended 1:50 (ENA, 2021). Furthermore, teachers are not trained to deliver inclusive education for CwD and the community is not sensitized to create enabling learning environment for CwD. High rates of school absenteeism are also observed particularly, during dry spells. Hunger is a key barrier to education resulting in poor academic performance, inconsistent attendance and eventual dropout. The IPC Acute Food Insecurity report (October,2022) indicate that 6.31 million people including children are likely to be severely food insecure in the lean season of 2023. This will consequently affect learner retention especially in schools unsupported with school meals. Lack of community engagement in school management and operations also negatively affect learners' education. This project proposes to increase learners access to basic quality education through education proposed interventions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will put in place measures to ensure that the views of most vulnerable community members are taken in account during project implementation. CEDS will make use of the routine community feedback meetings to pass on information and collect feedback about the project from the neediest community members. In order to increase meaningful participation from the at-risk groups, CEDS will purpose to hold separate feedback meetings for people with disabilities, women, girls and men. This will provide an opportunity for self-expression devoid of social and cultural beliefs that look down upon these vulnerable groups on voicing out their individual thoughts. To ensure that illiterate community members’ voices are heard, CEDS will use alternative feedback collection channels involving one-on-one personal contact such as helpdesks, project staff and through existing school committees (SMCs), associations (PTA), school recreational club membership, local education representatives and community leaders to obtain feedback. CEDS will also make efforts to ensure women and people with disability have equal representation in the established SMCs and PTAs' and relevantly participate in decision making. All feedback received will be channeled to CED's AAP focal point for action taking and a response provided to the beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEDS recognizes the significance of providing beneficiaries information about the project as well as obtaining feedback through a complaints and feedback mechanism. Guided by the organizational accountability framework, CEDS will utilize multichannel to provide information to the targeted beneficiaries and the community members about this project. To achieve this, CEDS will hold initial project meetings targeting School PTA's and SMC's members, school administration, student leaders, teachers and community members to share information about the project, beneficiaries targeted, activities entailed, stakeholder level of engagement throughout the project as well as complaints and feedback channels in place and how to access and utilize them. CEDS will also make use other platforms such as open forums with children, student club meetings and parents and community meetings to pass on information and collect community feedback. CEDS will ensure that all the community members including the hard to reach are reached with the information about this project. CEDS will incorporate the information about this project to the already existing community information sharing checklist and ensure that the project staff and other key stakeholders are provided with this information. Furthermore, prior to commencement of any activity CEDS will make it a point to share information about the project to present stakeholders.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through the already existing feedback channels, CEDS will ensure that the information about the organizations PSEA initiatives is provided to the community members and the project beneficiaries. CEDS will organize and facilitate training for this proposed project staff on PSEA and ensure that this staff are aware of and share with the community members on the organizations PSEA efforts. CEDS will also train the school pupils leadership, school PTA, SMCs members and local education representatives about PSEA and ensure that they are aware of the channels or reporting PSEA concerns. CEDS through information and feedback collection meetings, will also inform the target beneficiaries about their entitlement rights making it known to them that the project assistance is totally free of any sexual exchanges' favors or coercions and also encourage all beneficiaries and community members to report any suspected, observed or experienced PSEA incident. CEDS will also print materials containing PSEA messages to pass on information to target beneficiaries. All PSEA complaints received will be fast tracked and handled in a sensitive manner and the survivor referred for immediate medical support as the investigation is conducted. CEDS staff will also be instrumental in sharing information and collecting SEA complaints during any direct engagements with the community. CEDS will endeavor to ensure that all complaints made are confidential and that the processes are transparent and responsive. All of CEDS contracted suppliers will also mandated to commit to abide by CEDS PSEA policy and made to sign it

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CEDS will ensure that beneficiary feedback is taken into consideration throughout the project cycle. In the routine monitoring of project activities, CEDS will seek to incorporate beneficiary inputs on the project progress through focus group discussions with target beneficiaries. For example, on a monthly basis, the collected beneficiaries' feedback will be analyzed and shared with the programme team for further action. The project implementation team will develop feedback actioning monitoring tool to ensure that the feedback collected from the beneficiaries and the community members is acted upon and the project is routinely improved and adjusted based on the feedback collected. On a quarterly basis, CEDS will organize a project review meeting between the project implementation team, beneficiaries and the organization management to review how the beneficiary feedback has been used to improve the project delivery. CEDS will further conduct consultative meetings with the community leaders and vulnerable community members to get their insights about the locations they deem safe and accessible to establish temporary learning spaces and school latrines. In the course of implementation, CEDS will also organize lessons learn event for all stakeholder in which the feedback collected from the beneficiaries will be incorporate as part of the learning, documented and used not only to improve the ongoing project but as well for future programming. CEDS will utilize feedback obtained to better improve the project assistance and share learnings across other networks.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CEDS will put in place concrete mitigation measures during implementation to ensure that the project does not pose a protection risk to target beneficiaries. To mitigate the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration and distribution, CEDS will hold consultative meetings with the community members, leaders, school and student leadership to agree on beneficiary preferred distribution locations and schedules. All distributions will be conducted in open common areas deemed safe to access and concluded by 3pm to allow beneficiaries ample time to get back home in broad daylight. CEDS will also spread out the distribution dates to avoid overcrowding and long queues associated with distributions that result in frustrations and sparks conflict. To minimize the risk of women, girls, boys and people with disabilities being exposed to sexual violence including SEA, CEDS will ensure project staff are trained on SEA and uphold this commitment by signing a formal agreement. CEDS contracted suppliers who will come into direct contact with target beneficiaries will also be made to sign the agreement. Prior to provision of any assistance, CEDS will make the beneficiaries and community members aware about SEA and also inform them of their entitlements to assistance without having to trade any sexual favours, exchanges or being coerced to do something in return. They will also be made aware of and encouraged to use the SEA reporting channels to report any suspected, witnessed or experience SEA. Additionally, to ensure that the project has sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, CEDS will make sure all staff are well conversant with organizational child safeguarding and PSEA policies and the referral pathways. To counter the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents, CEDS will map out which organizations offering protection response and through information meetings with the beneficiaries create awareness on accessing protection services available in the area. Protection-related cases will be referred to the GBV, mine action and child protection partners. During the implementation, CEDS will hold consultative meetings with community members including the marginalized groups to ensure they have equal opportunity to participate in project discussions and their inputs are used to inform decision making. To guarantee that beneficiaries will be accessing and using the CFM in place, CEDS from the onset of the project will share information about CFM, its importance to the community members and how it works. CEDS will avail feedback channels that are accessible by both literate and illiterate community members i.e., use of suggestion boxes, walk in help desks and use of school-based committees (SMCs), associations (PTAs) and student club leads. Feedback collected will be utilized to adapt project assistance provided. To lessen external interference with the beneficiary selections, CEDS in the project initiation meeting will clearly outline the roles of stakeholders in the project implementation. The project implementation team-lead will be informed of any misdeeds relating to beneficiary selection and distribution to ensure fairness and transparency in the process. Lastly, CEDS will use its existing Data Protection policy to guide in data collection and staff will be required to adhere to this policy making sure that all beneficiary data is kept confidential and stored digitally in the database and only accessible to authorized personnel.
