Project Summary

This project seeks to strengthen the protective environment for vulnerable, conflict affected persons in Libya by improving the living conditions for IDPs and migrants through tailored shelter solutions, material assistance and capacity building in site management.
Shelter solutions: Access to safe shelter arrangements can be extremely fraught for both IDPs and migrants, due to inflation, increasing rent prices and landlords exploiting the war economy, especially in densely populated areas such as Tripoli. Many are forced into paying high rental prices for low standard or overcrowded housing. Unfinished buildings, construction sites and warehouses/industrial buildings have all been used for shelter. Lack of privacy, exposure to elements and dangerous building conditions all impact the lives of vulnerable inhabitants. Therefore, working on a case by case, or site by site basis, DRC will provide tailored shelter solutions to those in need. This will involve the selection of appropriate interventions, ranging from shelter repairs or shelter sealing (through either contracted services or sealing off kits) to the running and management of collective shelters for temporary accommodation, to the provision of cash for rent or placement into a host family. The inclusion of those we assist will be paramount, with discussions on designing and implementing shelter solutions throughout the intervention. Both unskilled and skilled labour will be sought from the communities themselves. DRC will continue to monitor their shelter arrangements through site management activities where appropriate, ensuring longevity of assistance and creating community driven durable solutions.
Material Assistance: In addition, many vulnerable households lack basic items, either due to their loss or destruction. Many migrants, on leaving detention centres or returning from the sea have little to no material possession. As such DRC will provide either individual items, or kits of items to households, based on an understanding of their needs. Kits and items will range from lights, kitchen sets, cooking sets, to beds and clothing. The items are intended to fill the immediate needs of the vulnerable households. Through regular community and household discussion DRC can further tailor items and kits to the needs of vulnerable households.
Capacity Building: Finally, many vulnerable households are reliant or semi-reliant on either community focal points or municipality leaders for the management of their shelter arrangements. Semi-formal or informal shelter arrangements are often run by self-appointed or community elected focal points or municipality leaders. Through workshops and training sessions, identified site focal points will be provided with training on protection mainstreaming, site management and shelter skills, in order to better assist the communities, they represent or manage.

1 - Needs

Libya has been adversely affected by conflict and protracted political crisis since 2011.According to the 2020 HNO, out of 6.7 million people in Libya, 1.8 (26%) million peoplehave been identified as people affected. About 50% of the affected population around897,000 are identified as people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than342,000 are identified as acutely in need of humanitarian assistance including 102,600children. The need for humanitarian assistance is due to instability, conflict andinsecurity, the absence of rule of law, disrespect of IHL by armed actors, a deterioratingpublic sector and a dysfunctional economy. The new wave of violence that erupted inApril 2019, resulted in new displacements of more than 128,000 people, an unconfirmednumber of civilian casualties, damage to infrastructure while thousands remain trappedin the conflict-affected areas. The majority of the newly displaced people are staying withfamily or in private accommodation, while thousands are sheltered in collective centers set up by local authorities. In May 2019, DRC, under the auspices of the protection sector, led an inter-agency joint assessment of the protection risks and service gaps faced by the population. The top five protection concerns reported by key informants were civilian deaths from indiscriminate attacks, killings, physical assault, torture and arbitrary detention while food, shelter (including cash for shelter) and cash assistance were identified as the top three priority needs. Given DRC's presence along the western coast and mountains, DRC replicated a protection assessment in Western Tripolitania. A similar priority ranking of needs was identified by respondents in this assessment. For shelter options, respondents also identified that the most common accommodation option was with host families (relatives or non-relatives). It was further explained that this option tends to lead to overcrowding and, therefore, limiting privacy and movement especially for women. It is also noted that collective centres usually lack appropriate management and the IDPs living in them often cite, amongst the biggest challenges, feelings of being unsafe, lack of privacy and limited representation of their needs.According to IOM reports, majority of those displaced in Tripoli have, to date, been hosted by relatives or have moved back to their city of origin and are staying in private accommodation. Over 3,900 IDPs are hosted in 29 newly established collective shelters including unfinished buildings, school, school dorms, etc. Multi-sectoral gaps (within the collective shelters or other urban areas) which require quick fixes to bring immediate relief to the affected populations have been flagged as has the need for gender appropriate solutions. IDPs living in schools are at risk of eviction if they reopen, as such, IDPs may face secondary displacement. It is also noted that although the majority of IDPs are residing with host families or are otherwise supported by host communities, the collective capacity to cope is quickly eroding as family and friends struggle to support larger households. Migrants released from detention exhibit considerable vulnerabilities, and require significant assistance. The activities listed in this project are designed to ensure as smooth as possible transition from detention into shelter in urban areas. Combined with existing projects such as DRC Libyas Protection services, DRC can offer a multi-sectoral, holistic, timely package of assistance to address the chronic needs of these newly released.

2 - Activities

The implementation strategy for shelter will, be implemented in compliance with existing global humanitarian standards. Given the lack of existing national shelter coordination mechanisms, it will be imperative to work closely with the selected municipalities to ensure that minimum standards can be achieved, as well as to safeguard shelter investments. To achieve this outcome, DRC will assess the actual hosting capacity in selected sites as well as existing physical infrastructures, using a community-based approach. This will include an assessment of the municipalities’ own coping mechanisms and ability to provide shelters as well as coping capacities of community members. It is noted that women and girls face particular threats to their safety from living in such cramped and crowded conditions. This shelter assessment will, therefore, provide the basis for identifying feasible and safe shelter options for men, women, girls and boys displaced in the collective shelter in Tripoli municipalities.Main activities will include: - Provision of shelter kits in order to weatherproof shelters, including assistance if required for installation. - Shelter servicing (such as repairs of doors and windows) where appropriate. - Conditional cash for rent for those able to support themselves in the medium and long term, but lack immediate short-term cash for the initial payments of rent- Running and management of collective shelters through urban areas of Tripoli- Distribution of material kits and items, including, but not limited to kitchen sets, bedding materials and clothing. - Provision of training courses and workshops on Protection, Site Management and Shelter skills provided to appropriate community and municipality focal points. The courses will centre of humanitarian responses to urban crisis, the management of collective and temporary shelters and the provision of protection focused, lifesaving assistance.

5 - Mitigation measures

1. DRC has established security management plans and standard operating proceduresincluding the contingency plan for hibernation/evacuation. Currently, all international staffis required to attend HEAT training before traveling to Libya. In the event of a majorincident, the Incident Management Team will be activated to address urgent situations.Safety teams are in regular bi-lateral contact with INGO’s, whilst the CD raises majorissues of concerns and discusses key security trends/issues during the INGOcoordination meetings. Security assessments are regularly undertaken in programlocations to manage risk and mitigate potential threats. In case of direct threats to staff,activities would immediately be suspended. 2. DRC will carry out stakeholder analysisand develop an engagement plan to clarify the extent of its activities and seek permissionbefore operating. 3.DRC is registered with the relevant authorities in Libya. Other mitigation measures include advocacy, through humanitarian coordination mechanisms, as well as lobbying with relevant Ministries 4. DRC will maximize the use of bank transfer mechanisms andcheques inside Tunisia and Libya and minimize the use of cash as well as the use ofalternative financial systems in accordance with donor rules and regulations. Lastly, where possible, agencies will enter into framework agreements with financial service providers if providing the cash. 5. Protection teams will monitor the protection environment for migrants and refugees, and provide ongoing advice to colleagues on shifts in the environment and ways to access populations and mitigate increased protection threats. Provision of certain services may be reduced if there is increased risk to migrants or to staff. DRC has established expertise on Libya Considerable emphasis is placed on comprehensive and regular context analysis and consultation with Libyan networks to ensure that activities and interventions are conflict-sensitive
