Project Summary

This project is going to close a big gap in the target areas. It's an ER project that will help support direct hosts and IDPs in Idlib and Aleppo.
The project aims as follows:
- Rehabilitate roads near health facilities and Rehabilitate Nursing Schools: Rehabilitation of infrastructure and improving access to Nursing Schools located in Idleb city so that 1000 students will have a good health education yearly by improving the roads leading to their schools and Rehabilitate Nursing Schools.
- Also, there will be rehabilitation activities for 10 Km of the roads leading to medical centers Afrin-Idlib and Aleppo to enhance the health services in the clinics and hospitals (SEMA centers) by improving access to these medical centers.
- Support businesses (shop owners): The project will be targeting 100 shop owners 50 men and 50 women in Idleb and Aleppo by giving them 1000$ as a grant to restart their businesses.
- Secure lighting with solar panels for the rehabilitated roads and sub roads to facilitate reaching the patients to facilities and also other indirect beneficiaries in the same area, also for the market street and sub roads that extend over a distance of about (10 km).

3 - Needs

Based on HNO, Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world.In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Despite receiving only 46 percent of the required funds for the 2021 HRP, Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in the past year (2022 humanitarian needs overview Syrian Arab republic).As socioeconomic deterioration continues, Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs are expected to continue rising. A further increase in the cost of living – due to, among other factors, the reduction of subsidies, general price increases, volatility of the exchange rate, and the global economic outlook affecting remittances and return rates –, is likely and will result in a further deterioration in terms of real income and purchasing power across all population groups. The sector foresees the income/expenditure gap to continue increasing or remain high in the coming year across all population groups. High percentage of PWD among school children from both sex in need to teaching, increase their access by schools' rehabilitation and road fixing.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups through adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle “Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries, and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedback and complaint mechanism, and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness on their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In light of the armed groups taking over more and more regions and the unfortunate consequence of reputable INGOs closing their activities, developing security strategies is a critical step in ensuring that before committing staff, resources and the organization’s reputation to a response, SEMA has taken all reasonable steps to minimize the risk. SEMA will take preventative measures to make sure we let the local authorities know our projects in the area and how they affect the community at large, so as to reduce the probability of any incidents with them. We will also contact OCHA, specifically the Access and Security Working Group to get the latest information on security issues (if any) and keep up with the current’s events.Both our Turkey staff and field staff have been trained in our SOPs when these incidents happen, and they got the required awareness about COVID-19 and the cholera Pandemic.Risk 1: Conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, the advancement of hostile actors.probability: the average Response: if needed we can move easily to a new location after taking the required coordination and approvals, also we will work on a security and safety plan in the first few days of the project.if any unseen circumstances happened, we will take the following actions in coordination with OCHA:Risk2: PandemicsProbability: highResponse: Postponing the services if there is no pandemic for a short time until safety is back or relocation.*Comment /gender risk
