Project Summary

The armed conflict in the two English Regions of Cameroon has been going on for four years since 2016. From the 30th of September to the 4th of October 2019, the Cameroon Government organised a National Dialogue to discuss a way out of the conflict. But the exercise is futile as only the Government side and some opposition parties were represented at the forum. The real leaders of the armed groups fighting on the ground could not attend sighting security and other concerns. Hence the resolutions from this Dialogue have been largely ignored by these Separatist Leaders who keep putting pressure on the Government to yield to genuine dialogue by strengthening their Armed Groups, fighting to liberate the two English Regions of Cameroon into a new State.
This is due to the fact that, the Cameroon Government has ignored to address the root causes of this conflict, which is to revisit the incomplete decolonization process of the British Southern Cameroon.
As a result of the ongoing conflict, the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) report, completed in September 2019 by OCHA, assessed displacement in the SW/NW at 465,909. This is in addition to 200,189 IDPs from the SW/NW displaced in the Littoral and West Regions - and 44,247 refugees from the SW/NW registered by UNHCR in Nigeria.
The total level of displacement from the NW/SW is therefore over 700,000 from a population prior to the conflict of just over 4 million people. Based on the OCHA situational report of 2019, 1.3 million persons are in need, 820 thousand targeted for assistance, 376 thousand members of host communities in need, 330 thousand other persons in need, 315 thousand people in extreme food insecurity need.
In the domain of education, in the NW/SW regions, over 4,100 public primary schools and 744 secondary schools remain closed or non-operational. This represents 90 % of public primary schools and 77 % of public secondary schools.
For these children, not only have their inalienable rights to education denied, but their educational development compromised, as a bargain to resolving the ongoing conflict. Going to school or having access to a Safe Learning Spaces, will provide them, a degree of stability and normalcy in their already traumatized lives as well as can be a critical source of psychosocial support, and to help reduce children’s exposure to other threats including child trafficking, sexual exploitation, early marriage, rape, child abuse, labour, servitude, physical attack, radicalization and recruitment into armed groups.
This project therefore aims at promoting access to quality education for Internally Displaced Children and war orphans, within the Fako and Kupe Muanenguba Divisions of the South West Region of Cameroon.
We target these children in the Government Primary, Secondary Schools and the Lay Private Schools as well as those out of school by promoting Safe Learning Spaces in communities or localities where the environment is safe for children to learn.
We hope to assist at least 2000 of these children, 75% will be within Schools and Safe Learning Spaces in Fako Division, and 25% in Safe Learning Spaces within KUPE Muanenguba Division.
Based on the understanding of these issues, LINK-UP activated the project to Educate and Give Hope to Internally Displaced Children (EGHIDC) 2018/2019 in Government Primary and Nursery Schools Great Soppo Buea, where 93 needy IDP children were supported with the PTA Levy (Fees) and a Christmas party as part of the psychosocial support.
We would like to scale up this project to assist at least 2000 War Orphans and Vulnerable Internally Displaced Children for 2020 school year in Fako and KUPE Munanenguba Divisions of the South West Region.

Besoins / Needs

The needs of vulnerable war orphans and IDP children at school and in the bushes, needs to be addressed. The needs of teachers of these children will be supported. There is the need for remedial catch-up lessons on basic reading and mathematics, as most of these IDP children have missed several years of schooling and lagging behind. E.g. In class six of Government Primary School Group 2 Likoko – Buea and out of 67 pupils, less than 10 could read giving a percentage of 85% of children who could not read. The need for capitation grants to mitigate the barriers that prevent children from attending school. This will include the payment of the school, PTA, exam fees, books, uniforms. The project will engage strategies in Social Mobilization, to enables parents participation via Self Help Support Groups (SHSG) for IDPs. This will enable the parents to provide their consent on how best to operate the Safe Learning Spaces without attracting the wrath of the warring forces.Main Objective: To provide Access to Education and Remedial/Catch-up classes to at least 2,000 Internally Displaced Children in Fako and KUPE MUANENGUBA Divisions of the South West Region; Objective1 To mobilize IDPS into 12 Self Help Support Groups (SHSGs) with at least 20 members each to be able to have a community platform to support the education process of IDP children. Activities: 1) Identify IDPs either through their children at schools, Quarter Heads, churches, or other humanitarian actors carrying out distribution exercises. 2) Identify a venue for their meetings and provide orientations. Output: 12 SHSG mobilized with at least 20 members each. Outcome: IDP children in need of education will have a community support system to Support them. Impact: 1) A community support system will be available for the education of IDP children. Objective 2: To carry out a baseline survey and needs assessment of the Government and lay private schools operational in Fako Division to understand the challenges faced by Internally displaced children.Activities: 1) In consultation with the Regional and Divisional Delegations of Education, Education Secretaries of Lay private schools, organize a workshop, which will facilitate the production of a baseline report of operational schools in Fako.1) Visit to shortlisted schools to assess the needs of the internally displaced children and teachers. Output: 1) A baseline document will be available on the functional schools in Fako Division 2) The needs of children and teacher identified for further intervention.Outcome: 1) Information available for intervention based on expressed needs.Impact : 1) Children in these institutions will be able to have a better environment to learn.Objective3: To organize 3 training workshops for teachers, on participatory teaching methods, crisis management, psychosocial support, trauma counseling, classroom management and sensitive teaching in crisis situations.Activity 1: To organize 3 workshops within 3 subdivisions of Fako Division with at least 20 teachers each and educational authourities. Output: At least 60 teachers from across Fako Division, trained on crisis management etc. Outcome: the teachers will become sensitive to the plight of these IDP childrenImpact: The school will become a better environment for children in crisis to learn. Objective4: Provision for didactic materials, teaching aids and Libraries in boxes to facilitate learning and teachingObjective5: To bridge the barriers that prevents such children from getting to school through capitation grants (assist at least 580 children with school fees and writing material).Objective 6To support the operations of at least 30 Safe Learning Spaces for at least 390 children for five months.Objective 7: To Promote Remedial Follow-up and Catch-up lessons by 15 teachers for at least 450 children.
