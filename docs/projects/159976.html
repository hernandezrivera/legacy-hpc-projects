Project Summary

This project will support vulnerable persons from IDPs, returnees and host community in Panyijiar, Koch, Mayendit and Leer Counties in Unity State. The project will target 4,167 beneficiaries to support them with lifesaving emergency health care services through 8 primary health care units (PHCUs) and 4 mobile clinics in the targeted counties. The planned activities are in line with the Health cluster objectives bellow.
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases
2. Improve timely access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and monitoring health insecurities
The emergence health care services will be provided through static PHCU and PHCC and mobile clinics across the targeted locations to ensure vulnerable persons are given adequate health care services.
The weak health system in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit and Koch Counties is being overwhelmed by the high number of IDPs and returnees from POC in Juba and Bentiu , Sudan, Uganda, Kenya and other neighbouring countries after the signing of the R-ARCISS in September 2018. Massive internal movement from Jonglei, Malakal and Juba are already overstretching the existing health system. Because of the relative peace in Unity State since 2015, it has become a hosting site for IDPS from the neighbouring counties.
The justification for emergence health care services is supported by a number of situation analysis conducted in South Sudan since most targeted locations have the same health related challenges.
According to HNO August 2019, “7 million people are in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection, 4.2million people to ﬂee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. About 1.5 million people live in areas facing high levels of access constraints – places where armed hostilities, violence against aid workers and assets, and other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely restricted”
HRP August 2019 reports that 7million people are in need of food, while 5 million women and girls need protection. 3.56 are in need of health care services. The severity of health care is conﬁrmed by the recent IPC reports where more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million populations in needs if health services are in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation.
Furthermore, HNO August 2019 indictors confirm that currently, one primary health Centre serves an average of 50,000 people and only 40 % of nutrition treatment centres have access to safe water,4% is a gap that puts more vulnerable people, particularly women, boys and girls, at risk of malnutrition and disease. Only about one in five childbirths involves a skilled health care worker and the maternal mortality ratio is estimated at 789 per 100,000 live births. In addition, HNO September 20919 also indicates that 3.57m people and in need of health services. This is very alarming and therefore there is need for intervention on the health services.
CH teams both in static PHCUs, PHCC and mobile outreach will strengthen the existing surveillance system to pick the first case and raise the alert to the cluster and other implementing partners in timely manner. The project also will build capacity of the community health workers to report outbreaks timely and provide lifesaving emergency vaccination campaigns such as, EPI, case management, rapid response, risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit and Koch.
This program will increase health services accessibility in remote and unreachable parts of targeted locations.

1 - Needs

Findings from Coalition for Humanity (CH) assessment conducted in October, 2019 on health specific needs in Leer and Panyijiar counties confirm that over 50% of the population is in dire need of health services. The major health gaps are in maternal health care due to high rate of malaria cases in the area and mother child healthcare. This intervention will provide an estimated population of about 70% in the targeted locations in Unity State. According to the recent IPC August 2019 report, more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million people in needs are in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation. This information is higher than the projection for 2019. The data above has been confirmed by various internal assessments conducted by Coalition for Humanity (CH) and partners in the counties of Koch, Leer, Panyinjiar, and Mayendit. These reports confirm dire humanitarian needs and urgent support to the most affected population with limited humanitarian assistance especially for those outside the POCs. Some of these locations are not easily accessible further jeopardizing the lives of innocent children and women.WHO estimates for 2019 suggest that nearly 260 000 children will suffer from severe acute malnutrition, affecting more than 11% of children younger than 5 in South Sudan. The country has been experiencing a deteriorating nutrition situation in the last five years (2014-2018). The burden of acute malnutrition remains significantly high, with over 1million children under five acutely malnourished in 2018. Results from the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) May –July 2019 analysis show that 59% of population is expected to face acute food insecurity. This indicates that acute malnutrition remains a major public health challenge in South SudanWith internal and external insecurity especially in cattle camps, limited road access and close down of most functional markets, these counties remain constrained in terms of access to humanitarian services. As a result, health related risks have been experienced due to inadequate health facilities, as well as limited access to nutrition services. This is because of the massive need and gaps identified in various reports such as the OCHA humanitarian snapshot reports, IPC, Reach regular displacement reports, NAWG and HRP2020 documents. A Coalition for Humanity rapid needs Assessment report conducted in October 2019 in those locations confirms this worrying trend. In those reports, it is recorded that the locations continue to receive high number of returnees and IDPs either internally within Unity State or from neighbouring states such as Jonglei, Upper Nile and Khartoum where there was political stalemate. This project is therefore, designed to contribute and reduce these huge needs and gaps as identified by many partners during the assessment missions. It is estimated that these figures will be worse in the first quarter of 2020.This project will address community cross cutting issues which include; Protection of the vulnerable and internally displaced persons to provide them with health care services through mobile clinics. It is anticipated that this project will respond to GBV related cases such as rape. This project will provide emergence service to any rape case by providing immediate critical management of PREP to prevent HIV infection, children with high malnutrition will be provided with MAM and SAM to boost their immunity. This intervention shall be environmentally sensitive through adhering to best practices of waste disposal by ensuring that medical waste is strictly disposed to predetermined safe locations out of reach to none medical staff and avoiding pollution of the environment particularly the air and water consumed by beneficiaries in the health facility
