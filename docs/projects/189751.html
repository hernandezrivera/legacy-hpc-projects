Project Summary

I-WIN proposes 12months humanitarian response plan for the year 2022 to continue and expand its integrated Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services to the vulnerable population in Budi County of Eastern Equatoria. The project will provide timely equitable access to safe clean water, adequate sanitation, and improved hygiene behaviors through an integrated approach with WASH-nutrition in line with WC objective one: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition. And WC cluster objective 2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. I-WIN will conduct its response in a holistic manner through coordination and collaboration with nutrition, health and GBV partners in the respective project locations. This will contribute to mitigation and reduction of high levels of malnutrition, WASH related diseases and Covid-19, gender based violence (GBV) issues as well as lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices.
I-WIN will focus on rehabilitation and building water sources in institutions including nutrition sites, health facilities and those prioritized by community members. Sanitation facilities will be provided on institutional level and hygiene promotion combined with protection, health, nutrition elements, with cross-sectoral topics adjusted to the needs of the groups will also be provided to vulnerable communities. The response aims to at reducing levels of chronic malnutrition especially amongst children, pregnant and lactating women and mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH. Persistence of food insecurity, signalled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in Nutrition services, through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP mainly for mother/caretaker of malnourished children. Health will be integrated to address water related disease outbreaks.
The numerous WASH gaps identified ranges from the shortage of water for drinking and domestic use that requires drilling of new wells in the raised/ dry land settlement areas far from the water sources, rehabilitation of the non-functional motorized systems, rehabilitation of broken-down wells, and shock chlorination of wells submerged by the floodwater, construction of shared household latrines, de-commissioning of latrines, distribution of latrine construction materials to family/households willing to construct their pits, hygiene awareness sessions and WASH NFI’s distributions.
This response plan is an accompaniment to I-WIN’s ongoing health project in Kapoeta South and Budi of Eastern Equatoria State. The proposed intervention will reach a target of 30,000 vulnerable people (5437men, 5752women, 6013boys and 6234 girls) among internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities including 3000 disabled people and people with special needs.
The activities will include:- Construction of new water points after GBV risk analysis; rehabilitation of water points in nutrition centres and health facilities and community level after GBV risk analysis; construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in nutrition centres and health facilities and community level after GBV risk analysis; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing in order to reduce water and sanitation related diseases and Covid-19; hygiene promotion education sessions water protection and environmental hygiene through door to door visits, distribution of standard hygiene kits to mothers or care takers of children admitted for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) treatment at outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)/SC and sessions on menstrual hygiene management and provision of menstrual hygiene kits to schools girls and young women.

1 - Needs

according to HNO 2022, An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The five years of conflict, economic crisis and recurrent flooding in South Sudan has negatively impacted on the delivery of WASH services hence WASH continues to be a great challenge in the Country. It is anticipated that the living standard and well-being of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation (MWRI) states that 30-50 percent of water facilities are non-functional at any point in time due to the lack of spare part supply chains, weak maintenance capacity, poor management and/or inappropriate choice of technology. Forty-one per cent of South Sudanese households access their water from an unimproved water sources. For those using an improved water source, 34 percent travel less than 30 minutes to and from the source while the remaining travel more than 30 minutes. The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria. The (Inter-Agency 29th to 30th January 2022 assessment report) indicated that most of the population draw their water from the river, swamps and ponds and few get their water from boreholes which are contaminated by the riverine flood. Furthermore, only 13 percent of the population has access to adequate sanitation facilities, random open defecation is the order of the day in Kapoeta South and Budi Counties with feces along the roads, and water sources. Beyond limited access to improved water and sanitation there is low knowledge and practice of good hygiene behaviors. The lack of access to improved water and sanitation and poor hygiene behaviors is a principal cause of water-related diseases such as acute watery diarrhea, ARI, cholera etc. and contributes to malnutrition. In addition to above dilemmas, an estimated 6.35 of the population of South Sudan are facing severe acute food insecurity (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification [IPC] Phase 3 or above) with a projected 7.2 million people (60%), to face severe acute food insecurity between May and July 2021, of whom over 2.5 million are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Additionally, 20 per cent of IDPs and 28 per cent of returnees live in settlements where people feel unsafe when they go to collect water, highlighting the protection risks especially for women and girls who experience harassment, assault and sexual violence while collecting water far from their homes. Armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV. It is against this background that I-WIN proposes to remedy this situation through integrated approach to reduce and mitigate high levels of malnutrition, WASH related diseases and Covid-19, gender based violence (GBV) issues as well as lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices among the IDPs, returnees and host communities in the targeted project locations. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine. This aggregated figure however hides some extreme geographical variations. In certain states, such as Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10 per cent, with open defecation the norm. On the opposite side of the spectrum, Western Equatoria has the highest proportion of households (60 per cent) accessing a latrine.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Views and feedback will be collected from the community liaison teams (CLTs) from the start (design) of the intervention through key information interviews. The key informant interviews will be conducted with the local leaders and community leaders over the period. All stakeholders will be involved in all assessments including assessment among IDP, host community and other interest groups. I-WIN will approach the principle of participation from equity perspective and advocate for equal participation of different groups. Based on the cluster reporting system, the project will put in place and implement and monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5Ws formats developed by the cluster. Additionally, I-WIN operates complaint desks to enable community members to report concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for ll affected populations. Beneficiaries will be given opportunities to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of the services. I-WIN will also gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGD. The I-WIN gender officer will be dedicated to conduct this activity once in a week with protection colleagues to provide further support that improves dignity of females. Complaints and feedback submitted to project managers will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project in order to sort remedial action.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be set up and made functional and accessible to the targeted community. The CFRM will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. The project will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls). Access barriers could be at following level i.e. within IDP, refugee returnees and host communities and age and sex. I-WIN has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to report concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services, whereby the hygiene promoters and water point attendants will gather information daily and report to the Project Manager. Additionally, I-WIN will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGD. The I-WIN gender focal person will be dedicated to conduct this activity once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. Through its activities, I-WIN will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the project managers will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project in order sort remedial action.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The gender focal person for I-WIN will be dedicated to discus with women and girls on the GBV issues affecting them once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of females. The project will target both sexes (male and female) with gender and age disaggregation. Our commitment to beneficiary protection (including protection of sexual exploitation and abuse) and communities will be continuously consulted on the process and contents of interventions conducted; local support, including males and females, will be recruited on a temporary basis to support the project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation and ensure that the intervention is locally appropriate, sensitive and relevant. I-WIN takes into account women’s dignity and security during WASH service provision to reduce possible risks of GBV and fear of not using the facilities. Also protection of females against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) during access to WASH service will be closely monitored. The project will provide trainings and orientations to project staff on Programme, Participation, Protection, and Policy (P4) and PSEA policy. It will focus on the five working principles: 1) rights of all, 2) participation and access to decision-making, 3) non-discrimination and equal access, 4) accountability and access to rule of law and 5) transparency and access to information. The other factors such as cultural, gender norms, values and beliefs will take into account while developing behavioral change communication messages.

I-WIN will deploy beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to report concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - I-WIN has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy which includes PSEA. Ongoing orientation for new and existing staff and affiliates will be completed. I-WIN adheres to a zero tolerance policy of SEA. 8.8. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources, I-WIN will remedy this risk through through consultative and inclusive approach with both primary and secondary stakeholders. 8.9. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area - In order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, I-WIN will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, I-WIN will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated M&E mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help building their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid. 8.10. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area - Risk will be mitigated by prioritizing accreditation of EORE Teams in a timely manner (refresher trainings and ongoing capacity development). EORE Teams are equipped with mobile phones of key focal points at UNMAS and NMSS should immediate assistance be required. Further, EORE Teams carry identifying materials for themselves and communities to be able to mark any UXOs discovered.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

I-WIN team will review the social, economic and political context of the project locations and use conflict sensitivity approach to minimize the associated risks. At the start of the project, detailed community consultations including women, men, girls, boys (including elders and people with disabilities) will be conducted in order to identify the specific WASH needs of the target community according to their gender, age and vulnerability. Ongoing consultations with women and girls held with the support of Protection specialist to specifically address potential Gender Based Violence (GBV) challenges. A strong consideration will be given to protection concerns and gender dynamics that require innovative response methodologies and specific material provisions.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

I-WIN will provide equitable access to WASH services through integrated approach where women, children, elderly, disabled and people with special needs will have equal access to water supply, sanitation hygiene education sessions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Through provision of access to safe water supply, the proportion of populations faced with IPC 3,4 and 5 will be reduced as the population may no longer be spending a lot of time searching for water. The population will have more time to participate on their daily livelihoods including agriculture to reduce on economic losses hence reduced IPC 3,4 and 5 in the project targeted locations.
