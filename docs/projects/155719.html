Project Summary

NCA will address the unmet acute WASH needs of 20,977 returnees in two
tehsils of Tribal District North Waziristan (TDNW). NCA, and its implementing partner PADO, will focus
on addressing WASH needs through rehabilitation of community and institutional WASH services. The
Action will implement WASH interventions in 11 villages of 4 tehsils in TDNW in communities and
institutions. The action incorporates an integrated approach with the inclusion of communities and
institutions (schools and health facilities) for maximum impact. The action will rehabilitate water supply
schemes in 4 villages, 4 schools and 2 health facilities. NCA and PADO will assist communities in
rehabilitation of 1260 (1100 normal latrines and 160 PWD friendly) sanitation facilities at households and 6 twin pour flush latrines at institutional level. Based on the significant drop in practice of positive hygiene behavior, the action will sensitize and mobilize targeted communities through awareness raising campaigns with the help of local media outlets. In addition, Lady Health Workers will receive capacity building on MHM. Female students and female school staff will be provided with MHM kits. Standard hygiene kits will also be distributed in schools to 1200 students and staff.
Action's interventions are based on the needs identified in the rapid needs assessment carried out in
November in TDNW. Tribal Districts witnessed displacement of more than 5 million when conflict
ensued between non-state armed actors and security forces. TDNW was one of the most affected areas
during the conflict. So far, more than 190,219 families have returned after prolonged displacement for
many years. The action will target returnee families with a special emphasis on vulnerable households
(HH) i.e. female/child/elderly headed HH, HH having Person with disabilities (PWDs). Equitable
community involvement for ownership and sustainable returns shall also be prioritized.

Needs

RNA conducted in November 2018 reveals that there is not much difference from the last year’sassessment/validation study that was conducted by NCA in Nov-Dec 2017. Out of 100 HHsurveyed, 25 HH questionnaires were filled with females gauging their special needs related to MHM,privacy and protection. The secondary data was gathered from OCHA/FDMA and MDTF informationfrom 2017 and presents similar findings to that of the RNA. However, the detailed multi methods-basedRNA has allowed for more nuanced analysis of the needs of target population, missing from last year’sassessment. Despite majority of respondents having access to water, there is a need for rehabilitation ofdrinking water schemes and for awareness raising to treat water before usage. Majority of returnees insurveyed areas have pit latrines that they built themselves, without technical assistance. Moreover, localcultural values do not allow for shared use of sanitation facilities between families. Pit latrines athousehold level are of poor quality and fail to meet DRR and protection standards, according to surveyfindings and direct observation from enumerators. Those without access to any type of latrine practiceopen defecation. However, most worrying trend according to this RNA is a substantial decrease innumber of respondents not washing their hands at critical times and instances of health-related issuesin the households. RNA paints a picture of acute WASH and health needs for selected areas that are inalignment with ECHO analysis and strategy in country as spelled out in HIP. A detailed explanation ofWASH needs from RNA is listed below.The assessment indicates 72% of the households have access to protected water sources likehand pump, protected well, and tap water, whereas remaining 28% of the households use unprotectedwater sources such as canals, rivers, open well, and ponds. Observation from survey enumerators showmajority of water schemes are either in poor condition or in disrepair. Moreover, 55% of the householdshave access to drinking water at the premises reflecting an increase of 4% from last year, whileremaining 45% fetch water from outside the periphery of house. 13% of the households take more than30 minutes by walk to fetch drinking water and 60% of water fetching burden falls on the shoulder ofwomen and girls. 93% of the household surveyed are not treating their drinking water. 65% of thepopulation is satisfied with the water quality. Of 70% who have latrines in their house, 55% of thehouses have traditional pit latrines, while 23% have pour flush with septic tanks, 21% have pour flushwith open drain and 1% have other type of latrine. Returnee families informed that they have mostlyconstructed latrines on their own and enumerators’ observation of latrines reveal substandard andnon-DRR compliant construction of such latrines. While remaining 30% have no latrines at all and optfor open defecation. The presence of unexploded land mines was also mentioned as one the threats ofusing fields for defecation. Usage of pit latrines by 70% of the household reflects the risks ofunderground water contamination as well as non-sustainability of these latrines.Only 23 % of the households shared that they have soaps available in latrines which is a 9% drop fromlast year. This decrease is due to a) very limited to no access for humanitarian actors in the arealeading to disruption of hygiene promotion activities for the entire last year, and b) poor financialsituation of the families. 80% households report not washing hands at critical times. 30% of the familiesreported having a patient with diarrhea in the last four weeks prior to the assessment. During theFGDs, returnees mentioned it is more common in the children, and in monsoon and summer seasons.

Activities

NCA and PADO will support the local communities in the establishment of WASH committees throughtrainings to operate and maintain water supply schemes at community level and pressurized pumps atinstitutional levels. These committees will be formed in a transparent mechanism by the local communitiesthemselves. The committees will extend their support in planning and managing the water facilitiesincluding use, control, cleanliness and functionality. They will also play a role in identification of work force(skilled and unskilled) needed for rehabilitation of the schemes.NCA and PADO's technical staff has identified drinking water supply schemes after consultation withlocal communities and PHEDs. Based on technical work involved for the repair of the WSS rehabilitation,NCA/PADO will sub-contract the repair work of water schemes to the selected vendor after a competitiveprocess. More importantly, local community will be engaged by the vendor as skilled and unskilled laboursin excavation and repair work for distribution pipe networks and the masonry work for the reservoirs.Maximum distance between water sources and settlements will be well below 500 m. Considering the localwomen are responsible for water carriage, water collection points will be established at accessible andvisible locations. These locations shall also be disabled-friendly and DRR compliant. To ensure theadequate water quality and quantity, verification mechanism will be carried out such as water qualitytesting and verification from PHED. Capacity building of the communities will be carried out throughknowledge transfer on various methods of water treatment at household level such as filtration, boiling andchlorination. Locally formed WASH committees will be involved in quality assurance and timely completionof DWSS work. Additionally, visits from NCA Islamabad office will be carriedout to provide technical advice to the project field team.NCA will provide PADO with DelAgua kits for water quality testing. This will be followed by detailed trainingon its best utilization. DelAgua kits will be placed in project office in TNWD or Peshawar. For pre-testing14 samples will be collected from 4 schools, 2 health facilities and 4 from DWSS. Tests will be conductedon Biological and physical parameters of Water. NCA and PADO will assist the local communities through distribution of sanitation kits to 630 households.These households will be given technical assistance by NCA and PADO for quality and timely constructionof sanitation facilities. Specific attention will be given to ensure sanitations facilities are disaster resilientand women-friendly. Out of 630 latrines, 130 will be disabled-friendly through installation of hand-rails,easier access and appropriate space.- Inclusion of WASH committees will be ensured during the distribution of sanitation kits and duringrehabilitation work of latrines for quality assurance and timely completion.- Targeted households will be sensitised to carry out on timely cleanliness of septic tanks, and safedisposal of excreta in accordance with SPHERE Standards.- For CommunitiesThere will be 200 hygiene promotion sessions in selected villages. Each session will comprise of 30 to 50participants. Each session will focus on personal, domestic and environmental hygiene following PHASTapproach.- School Hygiene Campaigns8 hygiene campaigns shall be carried out in schools in close collaboration with School ManagementCommittees (SMCs) and Education department. In these sessions, children will be motivated to act asagents of change to bring about sustainable positive hygiene behaviors.
