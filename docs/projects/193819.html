Project Summary

The project will provide integrated WASH, FSL, CP, GBV and NFI support to South Sudanese and Ethiopian Refugees in White Nile and Gedaref States. The project will address the immediate needs of the refugees with the following interventions:
Awareness raising on CP and supporting Child protection networks
Capacity Building on CP and GBV mechanisms
Providing Household NFIs
Providing Households with clean and sustainable energy
Construction of latrines and providing hygiene promotion services

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan has significantly worsened since the last update on humanitarian needs was released in November 20221 . Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF has killed hundreds of people, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict. The number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase. An estimated 736,200 people have been displaced within Sudan, of which 368,000 are children.5 Approximately 68 per cent of the internally displaced population is from Khartoum state, with some fleeing to neighboring states and others seeking refuge within the state.6 The highest numbers of internally displaced people are in West Darfur, White Nile and Northern states. These new displacements are in addition to the almost 3.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)7 , primarily concentrated in Darfur, prior to the new fighting. Vulnerable population groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks with immediate as well as longerterm harm to their physical and mental wellbeing. This includes an increased number of women, girls and at-risk groups, who are exposed to risks of genderbased violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). The deteriorating security situation is also limiting access to lifesaving GBV services, including GBV case management, clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support and referrals. Safe spaces for women and girls, including to access lifesaving services and obtain referrals, also limit options for support. The destruction of critical infrastructure, including water systems and communications networks, is having devasting impacts across Sudan. Water shortages have been reported across Khartoum state, as urban water treatment plants remain non-functional. Water authorities are unable to repair damaged infrastructure due to access and security obstacles. As a result, more than a million people rely on unsafe drinking water from the River Nile, increasing the risk of water-borne disease and the need to undertake awareness raising around water quality and water safety practices

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CAFOD acknowledges that different groups face different kinds of vulnerabilities. Therefore, CAFOD and its local partner will conduct conflict analysis through collecting primary data and triangulating with secondary data to understand the conflict dynamics of the area. CAFOD has a policy of Do no Harm, impartiality, neutrality, identifying the conflict sensitivity that could potentially harm our communities, and staff, ensure that safety measures are put in place. Moreover, this project will recognize the specific challenges faced by women and girls in the targeted villages. The project will ensure women are integrated into all decision-making processes of water taps construction sites, selection of beneficiaries, and all training for the HPs, WASH Committee, volunteers, and other program participants. Women and young girls will particularly be encouraged to participate in the provision of extension services as HPs and the WASH Committee. Staff with conflict-sensitive are deployed in their respective places of origin by supporting local recruitment plans to gain community acceptance. Communities in conflict with one another are served separately on the bases of their needs. During project implementation, conflict mitigation, prevention, and response approaches such as community conflict awareness, training, and community dialogues will be integrated into program outcomes. We shall also work with the communities to identify dividers and connectors as strategies to initiate activities that could potentially encourage peaceful co-existence among communities. Moreover, women and girls are in general more exposed to different types of violence such as GBV and abuse; boys are more exposed to forced recruitment; especially in the surrounding host community children are denied education; and people with disabilities have challenges such as stigma, lack of access to services and general neglect. To mitigate threats such as these, reduce vulnerability and ensure protection and safeguarding, the project will therefore ensure equal participation and full inclusion of all community members with special attention to children, women, the elderly, and persons with disabilities and their respective needs. Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) established in the target communities/villages, will seek to enhance beneficiaries’ rights to accountability. All vulnerable groups (PWD, PLW, elderly, FHH) in the target communities will be encouraged to participate in all planning, implementation, and monitoring processes of the project to ensure that their protection, safety, and security rights are known and upheld in all processes for delivery of project services and facilities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

To guarantee accountability and full participation of the affected population including children, the community will be consulted for culturally acceptable, safe, and accessible mechanisms in terms of diversity. This consultation will be done in form of rapid needs assessment, community stake holder's meetings, and service mapping. Moreover, CAFOD and its partners will establish various community project management committees to support the intervention. Training to be conducted in the project will provide specific inputs and will be devised to enhance women, girls, PWD, and elderly capacities while ensuring all groups are adequately represented in the groups of trainees and beneficiaries of project supplies and facilities. Increased knowledge/awareness of the project and details of key contact persons will be provided on the strategically affixed signboards in the target project villages. Moreover, to further ensure inclusive participation of all crisis-affected people CAFOD will designate complaint and feedback focal points – one male and one female, in both locations to respond to traditional barriers in communication between women and men, limited access for persons with disabilities, and persons with chronic illness, and low literacy among the population in intervention. The feedback collected will be used to improve program quality as well as plan for future improvements in gaps that shall be identified. The feedback is intended for CAFOD to respond to sensitive complaints and establish a safe and confidential strategy for safeguarding communities from any forms of exploitation and abuse. The feedback/complaints out of CAFOD’s scope of work shall be used for lobbying and advocacy with partners as well as referrals.
