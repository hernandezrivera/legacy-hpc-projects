Project Summary

The Rohingya refugee crisis is one of the worst forced displacement situations and the fastest growing refugee crisis in the world. In 2017, the needs for emergency humanitarian support in Cox’s Bazar were massive, in shelter, food, health, psychosocial support and education. Now around years later, they are strived by the progress made by various actors to provide services, despite working in difficult conditions.
Children’s education is one of the major interventions for reviving their lost dreams with basic education, sports, life skills, music, and arts in hundreds of learning centers run by different organizations. The learning centers have changed the lives of the children in the camps, where they have a safe and secure space to enjoy, and opportunities to play, and socialize with other children.
Caritas Bangladesh has launched education program in camp under Education in Emergency (EiE) programming from January 2020. CB has been operating one Learning Centre and 8 Home-based Learning centres at camp 1E and camp 3. A total of 223 children enrolled in 9 learning centres. It includes center based LCs and home based LCs for the Rohingya children of 3-14 years age group. The children already enrolled and has been educated in these LCs and will continue their education in 2021. Each LC (centre-base and home-based) opens for 5 days a week, daily 2 shifts and each shift will be for 2 hours. The program is being implemented under the guidance of Education Sector, Cox’s Bazar and guided by Government of Bangladesh’s policies. The project provides all necessary reports including 5W regularly and participate coordination meeting and capacity building interventions. Providing education based on the Learning Competency Framework Approach (LCFA) under the Guidelines for Informal Education Programme (GIEP) will continue in 2021 in the camps.

1 - Identified Needs

Children’s education is one of the major interventions for reviving their lost dreams with basic education, sports, life skills, music, and arts in hundreds of learning centers run by different organizations. It is estimated that there are about 39,4169 children under the age of 3-18 living in the camps (source: GoB/UNHCR Joint Registration Exercise - as of 30-Sep-2020), with about 322,907 aged three to 14. In 2020, education partners planned to implement the Myanmar curriculum on a pilot basis, initiate middle school and progressively expand other lower levels, with a view to reintegration. But the closure of caused missing of vital learning opportunities as well as psychosocial support. In COVID 19 situation, the majority of Rohingya children are now study at home by their own, but lack of learning materials and guidance from teachers is a challenge. Moreover, in most cases no one available in family to support children education at home (J-MSNA). Lack of home learning support as well as centre-based support will likely lead to many drop out specially for girls. The current closure of LCs likely connected to the increase in early, forced and child marriages specially for adolescent girls. Potential Covid-19 programming needs additional funding to have the capacity to rapidly establish or scale up education activities as a result of established presence and programing in the camps. Limited feasible land in the camps has made it increasingly difficult to secure adequate space for learning centers. As a result, CB is committed to utilizing innovative and flexible education delivery modalities which encourage equity in access, including meeting the learning needs of vulnerable groups, especially girls, children with disabilities and child-headed households. These alternative modalities for the delivery of education include home-based education supported by roving educators and outreach tutors. Caritas Bangladesh provides support to and operates Learning Centers (LCs) and Home-based Learning Centers (HbLC). LCs are the structured dedicated absolutely as education facilities and the HbLCs are shared spaces for children’s education. Community people share a room in their shelter for children’s education for a certain period of time and day in a week in case of HbLC. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) believes that, continuation of education has a key protection benefit - keeping children safe by attending regularly in safe spaces which can reduce the prospect of child labor or exploitative work as well as gender-based violence. The socializing aspect of education provides particular benefits to children and adolescents, where sites of learning can promote positive social networks. Schooling is vital to allowing children and their families to improve their lives. Education leading to hope, adverse children and adolescent to be tempted into crime or extremism, drug dealing, human trafficking and other contra-actions. Social emotional learning can mitigate risks and enhance efficacy of educational programs, demonstrating significant positive effects on academic outcomes, school retention and attendance rates, lower stress and anxiety, reduce negative student behavior like bullying, violence, SEA and juvenile crime, and improve health outcomes such as reduced drug use. The interventions strengthen teachers’ own social-emotional learning and mindfulness, as a way to support teachers to face the daily stressors of their own lives, serve as an additional incentive to keep them engaged in the classroom, and to help promote positive classroom environments and strong teacher-student relationships. Realizing the situation and necessity Caritas Bangladesh intends to continue it education program for Rohingya children and adolescent in 2021 with the support of development partners and the guideline from the education sector and RRRC.
