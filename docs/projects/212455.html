Project Summary

Needs:
The proposed action aims to address the WASH needs of the people affected by the humanitarian crisis in northern Syria, based on needs assessments and stakeholder consultations. The action also includes longer-term solutions to improve WASH conditions and resilience in the region. HNO for 2024 shows that most people in northwest Syria need humanitarian and WASH assistance, due to critical water issues, hygiene challenges, and the impact of the 2023 earthquake.
Implementation and intervention activities:
•For Aleppo and Idleb Governorates, the organization will provide 30-40 liters of water per person per day to 97,942 individuals through water pumping.
•Provide durable solutions for water supply with a local partner. the organization will rehabilitate and extend water networks in Aleppo and Idleb, benefiting 62,526 individuals. the organization and local partner will also construct a total of 11 solar-powered water stations in Aleppo and Idleb, covering the needs of 97,034 individuals. Lastly, the organization will organize 2 workshops on solar water pumping systems for technical and humanitarian actors in Idleb and A’zaz.
•The organization will conduct solid waste removal activities in Aleppo and Idleb for 87,322 individuals and rehabilitate one dumpsite to mitigate the spread of diseases such as Leishmaniasis.
•The organization will conduct light maintenance operations and desludging on sanitation facilities, reaching 78,834 individuals across Aleppo and Idleb. the organization will rehabilitate and extend the sewage networks in two of these locations (Mukhawamah and Dar Al-Soror respectively).
•The organization will install one DWWTP by completing the second phase of the ABR in Kelly village in Harim district, targeting 42,000 individuals.
•The organization will distribute soap to a total of 14,858 individuals in camps located in Dana sub-districts, which has a high-severity risk for waterborne diseases. Following epidemiological and emergency data from the WASH Cluster, the organization will also make 200 emergency kits available to respond to emergency outbreaks including floods and diseases.
Risk and assumption:
Access: The key risks identified are potential access restrictions due to administrative barriers set up by the authorities, border closures and security restrictions in Syria or Turkey.
Escalation of conflict (including ground fighting, airstrikes / shelling) affects the project activities: Military escalation and opening of new frontlines; Kidnapping, injury or killing of humanitarian workers due to conflicts in operational areas; Presence of unexploded ordnances and mines.
Protection Risk:
There is a risk of diversion WASH aids due to the presence of authorities, potentially impacting the intended beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium
Mitigating measures: 1. Establish and maintain effective coordination mechanisms between NGOs and local stakeholders to ensure constant communication and collaboration. 2.Implement acceptance strategies to foster positive relationships with authorities and reduce the likelihood of aid diversion. 3.Strengthen FCRM.
Protection Risk: There is a risk of exploitation of vulnerable groups, including women, girls, elders, and PwD, by authorities during the project implementation. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High
Mitigating measures:
1. Implement a robust Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCRM) to provide beneficiaries with a channel to report any concerns or instances of exploitation promptly. 2.Strengthen coordination with authorities by engaging in dialogue about humanitarian principles and the importance of protecting vulnerable populations.
PESA strategy:
1.Wide dissemination of the PSEA hotline to ensure beneficiaries have access to reporting mechanisms.
2.During all MEAL surveys, interviewees are asked if they would like to report a complaint.
3.Wide dissemination of the PSEA hotline to ensure beneficiaries have access to reporting mechanisms

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Contingency measures taken to mitigate the risks:Access: Approval for work from local councils; Active member of NGO working groups and clusters; Defined SOP for remote management. Escalation of conflict: Temporary closure or reduction in activities; Temporary management of activities through daily workers, suppliers, and camp management; Daily follow up on security situation and briefing to field staff; Close coordination with donors to adjust the interventions; Part of INSO round table to obtain security updates; All travel movements are subject to security approvals. Protection risk Analys:Protection Risk: There is a protection risk related to the distance to communal latrines, particularly for men. This poses a potential threat of unsafety and/or injury while accessing communal latrines in camps, especially as women, children, elders, and people with disabilities are using household latrines. Likelihood: High. Impact: LowMitigating measures: 1.Engage in advocacy and coordination with the Camp Coordination and Management for installing lights for latrines to enhance safety. 2.Advocate for the improvement of pathways leading to the latrines to reduce the risk of injury during travel.Protection Risk: The identified protection risk involves violence, armed clashes, injuries, and death during the general project phase. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1. Ongoing monitoring of the security context to stay informed about potential threats and changes in the environment. 2.Coordination with active agencies and referral to protection actors and clusters.Protection Risk: In Northwest Syria, the lack of doors for bathrooms/latrines at the household level cases a protection risk, particularly impacting women, and girls. This absence of privacy at HH level, including latrines and bathrooms, can lead to a medium-severity issue during the project phase. Likelihood: High. Impact: MediumMitigating measures: 1. Coordinate with shelter actors to provide Non-Food Items, such as curtains, to split the rooms and enhance privacy at the HH level. 2. Collaborate with shelter actors for shelter improvement initiatives, including the installation of doors, to address the identified protection risks and ensure a medium-risk level.3. Integrate comprehensive planning and implementation strategies that prioritize the privacy needs of women and girls, reducing the likelihood of the identified risk trend during the project phase. PSEA strategy:1. Feedback received from beneficiaries is used to revise project elements and ensure maximum impact for affected individuals.2. Proactive communication with communities to ensure all members have the same level of information if a significant number of complaints is received on the same topic or if the FCM is underutilized.3. The organization emphasizes the importance of adhering to The Do No Harm principle and promoting equal representation of all age groups and genders as part of the PSEA strategy.
