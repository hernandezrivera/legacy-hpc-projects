Project Summary

The country is turned to a police state where unknown gunmen, arbitrary arrest, eliminations enhanced by economic collapse are the order of the day but cannot be spoken off as required or one risk being call anti-government. As the country yearn to return to stability, Canal (Pigi), Fangak, Maiwut, Nasir, and Uror counties will reverse gear to become the hubs of return and repatriation routes from the refugees and IDPs camps as they have served the exit routes to refugee and reception camps.
While the guns might be silent countrywide and despite the country becoming independent in 2011, the issues of tribal violence mostly perpetrated by youth have not been address well, especially between the communities. Exacerbated by dismal function of lack of law enforcement institutions, coupled with unruly armed youth, and the impeded traditional leadership and customary law, communal revenges over past killings, cattle rustling between communities, clan feuds, etc remained potential threats to many lives and a genuine concern to the at risk communities. There is therefore a need for an increased effort to reverse this practice because these challenges threaten peaceful co-existence and create fearful living among communities and so are daring concerned to the returning and IDPs communities. Now that the countdown to the implementation of the signed peace agreement began with the colorful celebration yesterday here in Juba, the next question comes forth. The need surely will be enormous with regard to the return, repatriation and reintegration of those in and outside of the country and the vulnerable host community.
Currently South Sudan government and the humanitarian agencies will engaged in a concerted return, repatriation and resettlement of 4 million refugees and 1.76 million internally displaced person back home to start a new life. This brings the total of conflict displaced persons to over 5.7 million. Humanitarian reports indicate that women and girls are the most vulnerable and at risk of SGBV, according to the protection trends as of April 2017, therefore, gender-based violence (GBV) and its related effect remained the most serious and prevalent protection threats to women and girls in South Sudan. Other forms of sexual violence, abductions and possible sexual slavery, forced and early marriage and harmful traditional practices are major concerns still haunting communities amid strides and strings.
A partner in NFIs though outside of S/NFI cluster, Nile Hope has been distributing NFIs since 2016 and is added shelters construction for returned IDPs in 2018. It has since served 9,000 Households from Akobo, Fangak, Pochalla and Uror counties with NFIs in the course of its partnership with UNHCR. This year too, Nile Hope engaged in shelter construction for the 251 individual IDPs UNHCR voluntarily repatriated from Bor PoC to New Fangak.
Partnering with S/NFIs cluster, Nile Hope will further services provisions to the most IDPs, returnees and the host community of Canal, Nasir and Maiwut Counties. And through partnership with S/NFIs cluster, Nile Hope intends to complement its UNHCR supported services Uror County mostly the southern part: Yuai, Weikol, Pamai, Padiek, Panyok and Pajut. In Fangak, Nile Hope will extends its services to the South, Central and western part of the county including Old Fangak, Toch and Juaibor areas. This will avoid overlaps and duplication of services mostly in New Fangak areas to the north and also in areas of Pieri, Motot, Pulchuol, Pathay and Karam of Uror counties. Nile Hope therefore, applies to the cluster for both Shelter (cash) and NFIs (in-kind) to support live saving programming and promote resilience of the 20,000 needy communities across the toward total resettlement and support of the IDPs, returnees, refugees and the vulnerable at risk.

Needs

Why do Nile Hope target Canal, Fangak, Maiwut, Nasir and Uror Counties in these proposed S/NFIs life-saving intervention? Here are some reasons justifying why Nile Hope choose these location. This proposed shelter and NFIs intervention will support 18,000 individuals (4,500 per a county) of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees the vulnerable host communities. Canal (Pigi). It is severely affected by the recent conflict, whose populations are displaced into Malakal, Atar, Kurwai and New Fangak. Currently, 11,500 of Canal community lives in Atar, Kurwai and New Fangak according to RRM mission in July 2018 while many more lives in Malakal town. These displaced populations desire to return to their conflict destroyed homes they left 4 or 5 years ago. They have no belongings save their lives. Nile Hope has since vacated Canal in 2014 and instead based in Kurwai to continue with the provision of lifesaving services in healthy, wash, protection, and Nutrition. Fangak: Currently Greater Fangak hosts a great number of IDPs estimated to 13,000 individuals by Relief Organization office in New Fangak. ROSS officials said these IDPs are mostly from Juba, Malakal, Bor, Pigi and Bentiu. This estimate number excludes the 251 individual IDPs UNHCR help transport from Bor early this year. Fangak is also a gateway to refugees seeking refuges in Sudan. Nile Hope NFIs provision mostly concentrated in New Fangak and the surrounded areas with little or no outreach to Old Fangak, Juaibor and Toch areas. As such, Nile applies to extends its Shelter and NFIs services provision to these unreached locations to provide equal life-savings to the most vulnerable and in need IDPs. Maiwut: It hosts over 18,000 IDPs from Longechuk, Nasir, Malakal and Maban according to Relief Organization for South Sudan reports captured in join Assessment (RRM) in Urieng, February 2018. Maiwut remains a hub of displaced persons seeking refuge in Ethiopia as well as those IDPs seeking safety and security. Maiwut hosts a UNHCR constructed waystation, a transit camp to the returning refugees in 2007/2008. This center will also service as a hub for the expected return of those in refugee camps in Ethiopia. Nasir has been a transit route to Ethiopia via Burebiey border town in Ethiopia. It has been multiply affected and mass displacement by recent conflict taking into account the present of government forces in Nasir town. Last year Nyatong fighting displaced many persons into Mandeang, Jikmiir and into Ethiopia. Among the refugees in all the Ethiopia camps, Nasir and Ulang counties account for slightly over a quarter of the population after Greater Maiwut and greater Akobo, whose return will not only strain the food basket but equally challenge the living conditions and possess health risk through congestions of homes. Fear for a return to conflict is still high; deep seated mistrust and hatred swallow the spirit of nationhood. Even the IDPs from Greater Akobo will eventually passed through Maiwut and Nasir before they reach their destinations. Nile Hope has only been doing protection programming mainly GBV, Wash, Nutrition, FLS and Health in Nasir. The proposed Shelter and NFIs intervention will extends Nile Hope services and cover the gap left void including support to identified vulnerable and the expected return of near 107,000 IDPs from Ethiopia refugee camps Uror is a densely populated county with IDPs from within and out of the county. Multiple displacements in parts of Uror County notably from Pajut, Padiek, Panyok, Yuai, Motot, Pathay and Pieri complicated the already fragile and unstable living condition of the most affected communities. This is in addition to the displaced communities from Juba and Bor. Uror South (Yuai, Panyok, Padiek and Pajut are in controlled as such our UNHCR NFIs intervention rarely reached these locations. Activities

Activity 1: Improve access to emergency shelter to protect displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks. Nile Hope will construct new emergency shelter for the most vulnerable IDPs in the proposed locations and will repair the already existing shelters for the most affected IDPs and the Host community members. Nile Hope will use the local available materials for the construction of these emergency shelters that are more available and affordable. Nile Hope will consider the cluster guidelines for the selection of the vulnerable people to avoid conflict of interest between the host community and the IDPs. The construction will be done based on the level of vulnerability; the most vulnerable people will have their shelters constructed fully, and those who can manage their construction will be given in-kind assistance such as constructions materials Activity 2: Improve access to emergency shelter to enhance access to other basic services such as food, WASH, protection and livelihood. Nile Hope will register the affected vulnerable person with specific needs, screen them and provide them with Nonfood items.Activity 3: Provide life-saving NFIs that contribute to better health of IDPs, enhance nutrition, protection and better hygiene; particularly through multi-sectoral Survival Kit distribution Activity 4: Improves living conditions, safety and dignity of the IDPs Activity 5: Enhances the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and build on existing skills. Nile Hope will engage the beneficiaries on resilience and income generating activities and vocational training activities that will empower them economically.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A stakeholder workshop and community group discussions will be conducted, beneficiaries are the most target and will be given an inform understanding about the project, the activities and what will benefit them

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Nile Hope will develop and disseminate key GBV messages and shared with the communities, before dissemination, a consultation has to be done with the GBV sub cluster for guidance. These messages are developed with consideration of the GBV guiding principles where Respect, Confidentiality, safety and security plus no discrimination of the survivor are observed and respected

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

As part of Nile Hope's policies, we have a reporting desk for the issues relating to protection from sexual exploitation. Nile Hope have been supporting CBCM system which is both culturally and gender-sensitivity both formal and informal community structures, where individuals are able and encouraged to safely report incidents of SEA. Local communities are involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is Community based complaint mechanism

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Conduct community dialogues where community representatives are involved and share their views with the project team. Carry out community safety audits to assess the challenges affecting the community members especially women and girls and design a good respond plan to address the issues raised by the community members

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint mechanisms feedback will be collected through Focus group discussions and KII to have the view of the beneficiaries.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities are active partners in the project. They are recruited to provide both skilled and unskilled labor, they are part of the steering committee that looks into the overall running of the project and are a party to major decisions including locations of service points and provision of an ample environment where project implementation is possible. The relationship is relevant and the community continuously feeds partners with security updates from the ground to better plan movement and community mobilization.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

Protection sector proposed a shift from a “mainstreaming” approach to a strategy that promotes and supports “Do No Harm” programming across the response, and increases opportunities for a multi-sectoral approach to addressing protection threats and risks experienced by affected communities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to take services closer to the people so that security and distance constraints don't hinder access to critical and life saving services. In some locations where two communities hostile to each other occupy the same county, services are and will be offered in both locations.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address exploitation of young women by the powerful male figures in the community by offering referrals to professional GBV services through the critical referral pathway explained to the facility staff. This is done when victims of sexual exploitation and abuse volunteer information and seek help; a well-informed team that will be trained on Protection from sexual exploitation will be deployed and will handle cases related to inequalities with in the project locations

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed with its activities addressing the protection threats, the project will target a number of trainings where community members will participate and learn how these protection threats are minimized and addressed.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will measure access to beneficiaries through its indicators, the formulated indicators will show the numbers and percentages of how, when and where the target population access the benefits of the interventions, these data will then be used to generate information that will eventually be inject for project control purposes and to ensure that uptake is improved, increased or maintained.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of beneficiaries is central to the project hence the design phase has ensured that the project will quantitatively measure the number of beneficiaries that have been exposed to any form of violation or harm ranging from SGBV/GBV, exposure to UXO, and conflict; mitigation measures will be put in place to enure that the cases reported are addressed accordingly.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

During the periodic joint monitoring of the project, the steering committee will come up with specific indicators that will not only measure the satisfaction levels of the beneficiaries, but also determine the level at which there dignity was upheld during the execution of various activities within the project.
