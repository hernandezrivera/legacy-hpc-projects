Project Summary

This project is designed in alignment with the Food Security Cluster (FSL) Response Plan for South Sudan for 2024-2026. It seeks to contribute to the three main objectives of the FSL Cluster including (CO1) “provides food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC4 and IPC5 ”; (CO2) “enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihoods and fishing and livestock supports” and (CO3) “Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks”.
In 2024, the project plans to extend food and livelihood assistance for people facing severe food insecurity in 9 counties across five states of South Sudan. The project targets a total of 227,776 people expected to face severe food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) specifically 8,134 people in Central Equatoria, 60,000 in Jonglei, 100,828 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, 15,567 in Unity, and 43,247 people in Warrap State. Of these about 63,600 people will receive livelihood inputs to boost emergency food production. Further, the project will provide a fortified rice food (also known as Mannapack) to support over 3,400 returnees.
The project will be premised on two major interventions which augment and reinforce each other’s. The pathway to food security starts with the direct delivery of food assistance to affected people which is designed to strengthen food security in the immediate term, in particular, during lean seasons. This will be complemented by interventions targeted at restoring local food production through the provision of key emergency livelihood inputs and resilience building measures. In doing so, the project will stabilize households in the short run by safeguarding livelihoods and remaining assets and lay the foundation for long term recovery.
Building on the consumption stability achieved through food assistance, the project will extend appropriate emergency livelihood inputs and skills as well as support community participation in asset creation actions which will lead to recovery of local food production and the development of durable and productive assets. This will in turn enhance food security, restore livelihoods, and build resilience to ongoing and future shocks and stressors.
Specific interventions include:
a) General food distribution GFD and Asset Creation and Livelihood (ACL): These are meant to provide access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food to address ongoing hunger among the most vulnerable people (IPC4 &5). The GFD assistance will be used to engage beneficiaries in value-added activities (GFD+) to facilitate shift in their mindset from ‘free food’ to self-reliance. While the ACL intervention encourages beneficiaries to get engaged in complementary asset creation activities such as restoration of crop lands which will in turn improve farmers’ productivity and resilience to shocks.
b) Providing emergency livelihood assistance including the distribution of crop and vegetable seeds, agricultural tools and fishing kits coupled with skill enhancement measures to stimulate local food production. Vegetable demo plots and kitchen gardens will be promoted to showcase best practices for improving production. To strengthen linkage with nutrition measures, kitchen gardens will be promoted as simple interventions that can promote improved nutrition at household level especially for families with malnourished children. The adoption of better farming and fishing methods will improve food self-sufficiency and reduce dependency on external food assistance in the long run.
The project will set up feedback mechanisms through which targeted community members can voice their needs, preferences and demand inclusive processes. It will also ensure conflict sensitive approach so as to safeguard the safety and security of target community and staff.

1. Needs

High level of food insecurity continued to persist in South Sudan, with over 60 percent of the population expected to be acutely food insecure between October and January 2024. The widespread loss of productive assets due to years of conflict and floods continues to undermine households’ capacity to cope with shocks and rebuild their livelihoods. With insufficient means of producing or purchasing food, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes will be widespread at the peak of the lean season. Household stocks depleted earlier than usual in many areas income-generating opportunities remain limited, and staple food prices are atypically high, exacerbated by disrupted trade flows and increased demand related to the conflict in Sudan and influx of refugees and returnees. During the post-harvest projection period of December 2023 to March 2024, an estimated 5.78 million people (46% of the population) will likely face crisis (IPC Phase 3) of acute food insecurity. This is projected to increase to 7.10 million people during the projection period of April-July 2024 (IPC Snapshot, Sep2023-July 2024). Of the most food insecure States between Sep-Nov 2023, with high levels of acute food insecurity are Jonglie (61%), Unity (57.9%), NBeG (46.4%), and Warrap (37%). Factors including endemic conflict, deep rooted intercommunal violence, extreme climatic conditions resulting in drought and localized flooding as well as chronic economic constraints and Sudan crisis all converge to form a complex humanitarian context. Moreover, the outlook for malnutrition is expected to be worse Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children un¬der five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition (IPC Sep 2023). Acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. With 95 percent of livelihoods dependent on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry, climate shocks are likely to lead to greater food insecurity and vulnerability. Drought and flooding often lead to livestock mortality and a decline in the amount and viability of available land for cultivation. This will also lead to disruption of traditional seasonal migration routes of pastoralists and limited grazing land for cattle which may increase the risk of inter-community tensions in affected areas. Climate change driven heavy rains and floods are now impacting about one million people in South Sudan every year, aggravating an already precarious food insecurity situation. Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years, resulting in widespread asset and livelihood (crops and livestock) loss and displacement. According to a recent report by FEWS-NET (Sep, 2022), average to above-average rainfall is forecast to continue through October 2022, likely leading to atypically extensive flooding for the fourth consecutive year and an increase in flood-related humanitarian needs in 2022 The project will target 9 counties all facing acute food insecurity namely Juba county from Central Equatoria State; Pibor, Bor South and Twic East Counties from Jonglei State; Aweil Center, Aweil South and Aweil West Counties from NBEG; Rubkona from Unity state; and Gogrial West and Twic from Warrap state. Due to the multiple impacts of shocks, a significant number of the farming families in the proposed counties missed the previous year’s agricultural season, remaining without access to food and livelihood opportunities to meet immediate household needs.
