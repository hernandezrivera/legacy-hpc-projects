Project Summary

IRC will implement a project that will aim at 1- Reinforce the centrality of Protection in Cameroon through support to the Protection Cluster and Humanitarian actors to mainstream protection, support coordination of specialized protection services, and deliver programs that mitigate and respond to protection risks and incidents in the Far North and 2-responding to GBV priority needs in the Far North region of Cameroon through prevention and response activities. Protection mainstreaming is the first layer needed to achieve the Centrality of Protection, ensuring that a beneficiary in any humanitarian sector can safely access humanitarian assistance without being at risk of harm (outcome 1). In addition to being responsible for placing protection at the center of the humanitarian response in Cameroon, along with the Humanitarian Coordinator, Humanitarian Country Team, and Cluster Coordinators, the Protection Cluster is further responsible to ensure that humanitarian actors develop protection strategies, mainstream protection in their interventions (across sectors), and coordinate specialized protection services for affected populations (outcome 1).
The Centrality of protection is also ensured through the delivery of programs that are based on analysis, identifying who are the most at-need and why, and through joint efforts (and ideally integrated interventions), to respond to those needs while also ensuring the safety and well-being of people (outcome 2).

1 - Besoins / Needs

Three distinct humanitarian crises in Cameroon continue to drive displacements of civilian populations and needs for humanitarian assistance. This includes the protracted conflict in the Lake Chad Basin which has led displacement of an estimated 560,000 individuals within the Far North of Cameroon (HNO, 2021 – September 2020 figures), while active violence in the North-West and South-West regions has seen an estimated 1.1 million displaced from the area (HNO, 2021). Humanitarian needs, which are compounded by structural under-development, continue to be wide-ranging for an estimated 1.2 million people in need in the Far North alone. In the Far North, the protective environment has been eroded significantly as a result of ongoing violence perpetrated by Boko-Haram and other armed actors, which combines with pre-existing vulnerabilities, endemic poverty, and cultural/traditional norms, to present significant risks to civilian populations. This is evidenced by ongoing reported incidents of violence, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and other human rights violations (e.g., theft, physical violence, kidnapping, looting of properties), and state of extreme deprivation caused by forced displacements. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains widespread and one of the main rights violations affecting women and girls in the Far North region. This is due to violent conflict, negative traditional and cultural barriers, in addition to existing patriarchal systems. Between January and December 2020, 2,027 cases of GBV were recorded in the GBVIMS by UNFPA in the Far North, with 1,023 (50.4%) reported by IRC. 99% of the reported cases are women and girls, and 24% of the cases involved children under 18, including unaccompanied or separated children. Approximately 58% of GBV incidents are perpetrated by intimate partners, with the roots of violence existing in widespread social and cultural norms, as well as shifting gender roles and tensions arising from disruption to livelihoods and social status. Displacement conditions also increase the vulnerability of women and girls to sexual exploitation and abuse, which make up 13% of cases (10% of cases being sexual abuse of children).
