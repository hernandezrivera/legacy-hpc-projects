Project Summary

According to the Health cluster strategy for 2021, it is estimated that 4,940,938 people in South Sudan will require humanitarian healthcare services in 2021. However, the health cluster will target 3,008,345 people with various health care services in different parts of the country.
The health cluster intends to reach this target population using three main objectives:
1) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities; 2) Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations; and 3) Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Objectives 2 and 3 are in alignment with UNFPA’s country programme and especially the output on ; Crisis affected populations, particularly women and adolescent girls, have increased access to information and services for maternal health, family planning, gender-based violence and HIV prevention in emergency and fragile contexts.
The Reproductive Health situation, including the availability of commodities and supplies for the population affected by the crisis, remains dire. The implementation of the Minimum Initial Services Package (MISP) for SRH varies in scale and quality as you move across the country. Overall access to SRH services is limited due to poor health infrastructure and road network, limited availability of SRH commodities, limited skilled staff and COVID-19 pandemic, impacting several areas of health care interventions. Antenatal visits and skilled delivery remain a signiﬁcant challenge, with only 17% of pregnant women attending four visits and a chronic shortage of basic emergency obstetrics and neonatal services. The unmet need for family planning remains high at 24%. The needs for clinical management of rape and psychosocial services for survivors of sexual violence remains high. Simultaneously, the mix of frequent population movements due to conflicts and flooding, lack of awareness, and providing a conducive environment for GBV and new HIV infections due to the destruction of existing health services/systems or disruption in the social structure of the community.
Through this project, UNFPA seeks to serve two categories of affected populations, namely: 722,003 women of reproductive age group (WRA) of whom we expect 104,693 deliveries (15,704 deliveries out of the 104,693 will take place in health facilities), 51,262 WRA attempt to use modern contraceptives, 43,168 people seeking care for STI syndromes and about 14,440 cases of sexual violence who will seek care. On the other hand, UNFPA will seek to reach 691,919 girls and boys with HIV prevention services and information, adolescent sexual and reproductive health services, and information. Overall, UNFPA seeks to reach 1,413,922 affected people with various forms of SRH services and information. The targets will be achieved through a well-coordinated mechanism for SRH at the national, state and local levels by the SRH technical working groups.
The main interventions to achieve the project purpose include, among others:
a) Ensuring availability of skilled staff and good quality infrastructure for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches.
b) Conducting community dialogues and health education for women, men, families and the community to increase awareness on beneﬁts of safe sex, HIV prevention, family planning, ANC, PNC and delivery in a health facility by skilled birth attendants.
c) Procuring, prepositioning, and distributing a whole range of RH Kits and Non-Kit RH/maternal medicines and sundries essential for delivering RH services at the community and health facilities, including RH equipment for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others).

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is exacerbated by a collapsed health system that was already weak before the conflicts the eruption of conflicts in 2013 and 2016. The ongoing flooding poses a challenge to protecting and improving women and girls' health. The country continues to experience frequent and unexpected acute localized crises amidst the major protracted crisis. This stretches available resources considerably. Like several countries in the region, South Sudan has also been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. The South Sudan maternal mortality ratio still ranks as one of the highest in the world (789 deaths per 100.000 live births) due to lack of access and limited availability of RH care services across the country. Pregnant mothers continued to die while giving life, and through this project, UNFPA will avert 948 maternal deaths. The UNFPA assessment on implementing the MISP for RH services conducted in 2018 highlighted the signiﬁcant breakdown of functioning health facilities. The local health systems in remote rural areas, which have also the worst of the crisis, are overwhelmed and collapsed. 80% of health service provision is delivered through non-governmental agencies. Focus group discussions and key informants’ interviews highlighted a high demand for specialised RH care services, including FP, PAC, GBV services, etc. Access to comprehensive obstetric surgical care in many parts of the country, the limited number of skilled birth attendants and the lack of ambulance services and access to essential medicines and medical supplies are still critical in the humanitarian protection of civilians sites and government facilities. Since 2018 the situation is no better, and instead, the needs for services had increased. This project will address the immediate needs of pregnant women who want to access antenatal, perinatal and postnatal care, including post-partum family planning and services for a complicated pregnancy, PAC and delivery by ensuring that they beneﬁt from basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care. GBV risks will be mitigated by ensuring that women, girls and boys have access to services through community awareness sessions and protection networks, mainly to engage them in HIV prevention and combating gender-based violence. Youth-friendly service, including recreational spaces, for young people (both male and female) to be able to get some positive outlets for their energies and to tap into them as a positive source of role models and peer educators within their The HNO 2021 for South Sudan estimated there are 5.2 million in need of humanitarian assistance. The health cluster estimated 4,940,938 people in need of health services. However, the health cluster is targetting 2.4 million people in need to be reached with health services and information. This target includes 722,003 women of reproductive age and 691,919 adolescent girls and boys in need of health care in 2021. However, UNFPA estimates that only a third of these women and adolescents will access quality sexual and reproductive health. Therefore UNFPA will reach 240,668 women of reproductive health age group and 230,640 adolescents with a total of 471,308 beneﬁciaries.

2 - Project activities

This project is intended to realize one of the health cluster's core strategies of ensuring the availability of life-saving maternal health services, including commodities and supplies in all service delivery points among affected populations. Therefore, the activities below are intended to ensure that RH services are available and accessible in all the affected population locations. These are; 1. Identify and deploy skilled staff for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches. 2. Maintain, renovate, and whenever possible, re-open infrastructure for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches. 3. Conduct community dialogues and health education for women, men, families and the community to increase awareness on beneﬁts of safe sex, HIV prevention, family planning, ANC, PNC, PAC and delivery in a health facility by skilled birth attendants. 4. Procure, preposition, and distributes a whole range of RH Kits and Non-Kit RH/maternal medicines and sundries essential for the delivery of RH services at the community, and health facilities, including RH equipment for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others) 5. Train service providers and SRH focal persons on essential lifesaving service areas like the last-mile assurance of RH commodities, rational use of RH kits, SRH Care in Emergencies, 2020 Version, the orientation of SPs on IARH Kits 6th Edition 2019, MISP, CMR, PAC, and universal precautions, and other lifesaving interventions including provision of RH/GBV services amidst COVID-19 pandemic.6. Produce and disseminate IEC materials on SRH related topics and Covid-19. 7. Support a 24/7 referral system of complicated obstetric cases and GBV cases. 8. Engage men and boys in awareness sessions and protection networks for HIV and gender-based violence prevention; 9. Monitor, maintain and scale up data collection and reporting on SRH services10. Support coordination mechanisms and regular field monitoring visit to SRH/GBV service provision sites

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will take into consideration the protection of beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse issues. This is done through joint planning with protections actors on mitigating barriers to health care for integrated SGBV survivors. Client-oriented service provision-orientation of health care workers on the needs and services of SGBV survivors.The mechanism will record and handle Sexual Exploitation, and Abuse (SEA)-related complaints, including aspects of confidentiality, accessibility and follow-up; UNFPA has a dedicated PSEA focal person who works with partners to raise awareness duty inform/notify /report SEA allegations. Allegations are reported in a safe, effective, and culturally-appropriate manner. All received allegations are sent to the PSEA focal person, while the non-identifiable information is shared with the PSEA Task Force Chairs, who notify the Organization responsible for initiating investigations. If the allegation involves UNFPA staff, allegations are safely reported only to UNFPA OAIS through confidential means. Feedback is provided to the victims on progress so far made through the Co-chairs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNFPA will also ensure that its IPs gather information about preferred communication channels of age and gender groups by having separate discussions with adult women and men and boys and girls in the project sites. It will also advise its IPs to hold specific consultations which engage people with disabilities and the elderly. Based on these discussions, communication and feedback channels will be tailored, with the information provided through community meetings and in separate age/gender/diversity groups. Avenues established in project sites include communicating directly through IPs staff/volunteers and proactive focus group discussions conducted by monitoring and evaluation teams. UNFPA will ensure that its IPs use pictorial information, education and communication (IEC) materials for low literacy populations and local staff/volunteers who speak native languages.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During project implementation, UNFPA ensures to get feedback from the community's most vulnerable in many ways; by conducting community consultative meetings, through safety audits, focus group discussions and key informant interviews. For example, UNFPA, in collaboration with partners, has created several communities based complaints mechanism(CBCM). The system uses a complaints box for those who can read and write and relies on trained community leaders who act as recipients of the information. The community leaders also provide feedback to the community on how complaints are being solved. The strength of the system is to ensure everyone involved is trained on confidentiality. The system is easily accessible to the community, and any information obtained through the CBCMs is handled and processed confidentially.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNFPA will account to the affected population in the following ways: Active participation of affected people (Women, girls, boys and men) inappropriate age groups by influencing decisions throughout the project; Conducting community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected communities; Evidence of using beneficiary experience to modify and plan responses; and Prepare and submit monthly, quarterly and bi-annual project report to the stakeholders. Beneficiaries’ feedback information will be used to inform decision-making, re-programming and corrective actions for the identified problems.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/ activities.- Likelihood (High) and impact (High).- Project consultations with communities will be broad and representative, beyond community leaders and usual constituencies. Women, men, boys and girls across all social setups will be engaged in the consultation. So far, UNFPA consulted women, men, boys and girls through focus group discussions and a key informant interview. The team will also engage community leaders and religious leaders including special groups in the community like women group, Youth group and people living with a disability so that the project will be designed to meet their specific needs. Therefore, not consulting women and girls would pause a signiﬁcant risk to the project. Women and girls input into the project design would enhance ownership and improve service uptake. 2. Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.- Likelihood (High) and impact (High).- Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation during access to humanitarian assistance would have a devastating impact on the project. The women and girls would be gravely affected physically and psychologically, among others, while the image of the humanitarian system as a whole would be tainted. The consequences of sexual violence and exploitation are life long and can severely impact the community as a whole. 3. Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA- Likelihood (Medium) and impact (High).- UNFPA and its IPs have a dedicated PSEA focal person who works with partners to raise awareness on duty to inform/notify /report SEA allegations. Allegations are reported in a safe, effective, and culturally-appropriate manner. All received allegations are sent to the PSEA focal person, while the non-identifiable information is shared with the PSEA Task Force Chairs, who notify the Organization responsible for initiating investigations.4. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries- Likelihood (Medium) and impact (High).- Unsafe and unethical data management, especially in the provision of health services, can result in severe consequences. In some situations, health workers have been attacked and injured in the past due to it, while beneﬁciaries have shunned services due to such practices. Therefore, data management is an important part of project management, and all those involved in the implementation of the project have to commit to managing data safely and ethically.
